# The Pakistan Movement — from Congress to the Muslim League

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From Congress in 1885 to the midnight of 14 August 1947, the Muslims of India organise, bargain and finally demand a country of their own — province by province, founder by founder — until Pakistan is born. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The provinces create Pakistan

After the failed war of 1857 the Muslims of India organise; the Lahore Resolution of 23 March 1940 demands a separate homeland; and each province plays its part — Punjab passes the resolution's spirit at the Minar-e-Pakistan's ground, Sindh, the land where Islam first entered with Muhammad bin Qasim in 712 and which the book calls Bab-ul-Islam, becomes the first province to vote for Pakistan in 1943, Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa join by their own decisions and referendum, until 14 August 1947.

## Sub lesson 3 — The organisers and the name-giver

Begum Rana Liaquat Ali Khan builds the women's movement and founds APWA in 1949; Begum Jahan Ara Shahnawaz organises the Punjab women's Muslim League from 1937; Chaudhry Rahmat Ali, a student at Cambridge, coins the very name "Pakistan" in his 1933 pamphlet Now or Never; and Sir Abdullah Haroon carries the demand in Sindh, whose assembly votes to join Pakistan on 26 June 1947.
