# Lesson plan — The Pakistan Movement — from Congress to the Muslim League

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Pakistan Movement — from Congress to the Muslim League
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Pakistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From Congress in 1885 to the midnight of 14 August 1947, the Muslims of India organise, bargain and finally demand a country of their own — province by province, founder by founder — until Pakistan is born.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The provinces create Pakistan** — After the failed war of 1857 the Muslims of India organise; the Lahore Resolution of 23 March 1940 demands a separate homeland; and each province plays its part — Punjab passes the resolution's spirit at the Minar-e-Pakistan's ground, Sindh, the land where Islam first entered with Muhammad bin Qasim in 712 and which the book calls Bab-ul-Islam, becomes the first province to vote for Pakistan in 1943, Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa join by their own decisions and referendum, until 14 August 1947

2. **Minorities share in the making** — The new country is not built by Muslims alone — minority citizens, from the Christians, Hindus, Sikhs and Parsis who chose Pakistan to servants of the sick like Dr Ruth Pfau, share in its making, and the chapter closes with the heroes decorated with the Nishan-e-Haider

3. **The organisers and the name-giver** — Begum Rana Liaquat Ali Khan builds the women's movement and founds APWA in 1949; Begum Jahan Ara Shahnawaz organises the Punjab women's Muslim League from 1937; Chaudhry Rahmat Ali, a student at Cambridge, coins the very name "Pakistan" in his 1933 pamphlet Now or Never; and Sir Abdullah Haroon carries the demand in Sindh, whose assembly votes to join Pakistan on 26 June 1947

4. **The provinces complete the map** — The map is completed piece by piece — Punjab, the movement's centre under leaders like Sir Mian Muhammad Shafi, is divided by the 3 June 1947 plan; Khyber Pakhtunkhwa votes in its referendum; Balochistan's Shahi Jirga and Quetta municipality decide for Pakistan; Mirza Hasan Khan's Gilgit scouts raise Pakistan's flag in Gilgit-Baltistan in November 1947; and Sardar Ibrahim's Azad Jammu and Kashmir joins its future to Pakistan

5. **Congress is founded, 1885** — A retired British official creates India's first national political platform, which Muslims soon find speaks mainly for Hindus

6. **Bengal divided and the Simla Deputation** — Bengal's division shows Muslims the power of a Muslim-majority province, and Agha Khan's deputation turns that lesson into a constitutional demand

7. **The Muslim League is founded, 1906** — Muslims decide they need a party of their own, and the League that will demand Pakistan is born

8. **The Lucknow Pact, 1916** — For once Hindu and Muslim leaders sign the same paper, the high point of cooperation before the split

9. **The Khilafat Movement, 1919** — Indian Muslims march for a caliph in Istanbul, and the movement collapses when its cause disappears

10. **The Nehru Report, 1928** — A Congress-written constitution leaves Muslims unprotected, and the League learns to bargain as a separate nation

11. **Iqbal's Allahabad Address, 1930** — A poet-philosopher names the destination while London conferences prove Hindus and Muslims cannot agree on the road

12. **The 1935 Act and the Congress ministries** — Two years of Congress government become the League's best recruiting argument for a separate homeland

13. **The Lahore Resolution, 1940** — At Minto Park the League stops asking for safeguards and demands a country of its own

14. **Women and students carry the movement** — Purdah-era women and college students become organisers and couriers of a national movement

15. **Cripps, Quit India and Direct Action Day** — Wartime offers satisfy nobody, and politics moves from conference tables to the street

16. **Independence — 14 August 1947** — An empire ends at midnight and a new state begins with a refugee crisis already at its door
