# Lesson plan — Bahamas — pirates, wreckers and Woodes Rogers

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas — pirates, wreckers and Woodes Rogers
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Bahamas, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pirates infest The Bahamas until Woodes Rogers, first Royal Governor, pardons some and hangs the rest, and wreckers make a living luring and stripping wrecked ships — until an organised colony with its first parliament begins in 1729
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pirates and Buccaneers: from boucan fires to Blackbeard** — shipwrecked English, French and Dutch hunters cure meat over a boucan fire on Hispaniola until Spaniards chase them to sea as pirates; Blackbeard, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, Calico Jack, Stede Bonnet, Henry Morgan and Charles Vane infest The Bahamas until Woodes Rogers pardons some and hangs the rest (Pirates and Buccaneers: from boucan fires to Blackbeard)
2. **Wrecking: the false-light trade** — in the mid-1600s wrecking is big business — wreckers lure ships onto cays and reefs, sometimes with false lights, strip the goods and sell them, and many Bahamians make a living from the wrecks (Wrecking: the false-light trade)
3. **Early Connections: pirates, Woodes Rogers and the first parliament** — pirates seize the islands in the 1600s and ruin their reputation until Woodes Rogers, first Royal Governor, clears them out; under him the islands organise their first parliament in 1729, with John Colebrook as first Speaker and 24 members (Early Connections: pirates, Woodes Rogers and the first parliament)
