# Bahamas — pirates, wreckers and Woodes Rogers

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Pirates infest The Bahamas until Woodes Rogers, first Royal Governor, pardons some and hangs the rest, and wreckers make a living luring and stripping wrecked ships — until an organised colony with its first parliament begins in 1729. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pirates and Buccaneers: from boucan fires to Blackbeard

shipwrecked English, French and Dutch hunters cure meat over a boucan fire on Hispaniola until Spaniards chase them to sea as pirates; Blackbeard, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, Calico Jack, Stede Bonnet, Henry Morgan and Charles Vane infest The Bahamas until Woodes Rogers pardons some and hangs the rest (Pirates and Buccaneers: from boucan fires to Blackbeard)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this lesson's story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wrecking: the false-light trade

in the mid-1600s wrecking is big business — wreckers lure ships onto cays and reefs, sometimes with false lights, strip the goods and sell them, and many Bahamians make a living from the wrecks (Wrecking: the false-light trade)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this lesson's story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Early Connections: pirates, Woodes Rogers and the first parliament

pirates seize the islands in the 1600s and ruin their reputation until Woodes Rogers, first Royal Governor, clears them out; under him the islands organise their first parliament in 1729, with John Colebrook as first Speaker and 24 members (Early Connections: pirates, Woodes Rogers and the first parliament)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this lesson's story. Name two things that changed in the story.
