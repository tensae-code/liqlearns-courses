# Bahamas — pirates, wreckers and Woodes Rogers — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pirates and Buccaneers: from boucan fires to Blackbeard
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: shipwrecked English, French and Dutch hunters cure meat over a boucan fire on Hispaniola until Spaniards chase them to sea as pirates; Blackbeard, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, Calico Jack, Stede Bonnet, Henry Morgan and Charles Vane infest The Bahamas until Woodes Rogers pardons some and hangs the rest

## Sub lesson 2 — Wrecking: the false-light trade
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: in the mid-1600s wrecking is big business — wreckers lure ships onto cays and reefs, sometimes with false lights, strip the goods and sell them, and many Bahamians make a living from the wrecks

## Sub lesson 3 — Early Connections: pirates, Woodes Rogers and the first parliament
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: pirates seize the islands in the 1600s and ruin their reputation until Woodes Rogers, first Royal Governor, clears them out; under him the islands organise their first parliament in 1729, with John Colebrook as first Speaker and 24 members
