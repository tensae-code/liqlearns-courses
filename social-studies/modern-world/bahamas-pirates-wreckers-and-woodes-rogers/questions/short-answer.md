# Bahamas — pirates, wreckers and Woodes Rogers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Pirates and Buccaneers: from boucan fires to Blackbeard.
**Answer: shipwrecked English, French and Dutch hunters cure meat over a boucan fire on Hispaniola until Spaniards chase them to sea as pirates; Blackbeard, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, Calico Jack, Stede Bonnet, Henry Morgan and Charles Vane infest The Bahamas until Woodes Rogers pardons some and hangs the rest (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Wrecking: the false-light trade.
**Answer: in the mid-1600s wrecking is big business — wreckers lure ships onto cays and reefs, sometimes with false lights, strip the goods and sell them, and many Bahamians make a living from the wrecks (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Early Connections: pirates, Woodes Rogers and the first parliament.
**Answer: pirates seize the islands in the 1600s and ruin their reputation until Woodes Rogers, first Royal Governor, clears them out; under him the islands organise their first parliament in 1729, with John Colebrook as first Speaker and 24 members (any close wording).**
