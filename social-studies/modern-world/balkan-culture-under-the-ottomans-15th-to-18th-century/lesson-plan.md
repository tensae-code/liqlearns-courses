# Lesson plan — Balkan Culture Under The Ottomans 15th To 18th Century

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Balkan Culture Under The Ottomans 15th To 18th Century
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (North Macedonia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** monasteries at Lesnovo, Treskavec and Slepče keep Slavic letters alive in scriptoria; damaskini retell saints' lives in near-vernacular; printing happens abroad — Jakov of Kamena Reka in Venice, Žefarović's Stemmatographia (Vienna 1741) with its Macedonian coat of arms, Buzuku's Albanian Meshari (1555); cell schools and medreses (Skopje's Isa-beg) teach the two faiths; hidden cave churches ring Lake Ohrid while Ottoman towns take their lasting shape — čaršija and mahala, türbe, hamam, han and bezisten
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
