# Micronesia — German rule and the Sokehs Rebellion

## Do it first

A government builds roads and plants crops — and calls it development. The people it rules call it something else, and rise. Before you read on, decide: can a reform be both useful and unjust at the same time? Hold your answer; Sokehs will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Germany buys the Carolines and pushes development

*Over Seas and Time* calls the chapter German Attempts at Development. In 1899 Germany bought the Carolines from Spain for just over four million dollars — an entire island world bought and sold between empires.

From district offices on Saipan, Yap and Pohnpei, Germany pushed development: roads, cash crops, and land and labour reforms that reached into how islanders held land and owed work.

In your mind's eye: a new German road being built on Pohnpei, with island workers, a district officer, and the Sokehs peninsula across the water. The student points to the road and the peninsula and says what each came to mean.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Sokehs Rebellion and stormy weather

In 1910 the people of Sokehs, on Pohnpei, rebelled against the strict new reforms and killed the German district officer. The Grade 11 book adds the other side of the same years: German rule worked through and against traditional leaders — roads built with Yapese chiefs on one island, soldiers sent against the Sokehs rebels on Pohnpei in 1911 on another.

The same years brought storms: typhoons drove outer islanders onto the high islands, resettling communities, while the Catholic Church expanded into Chuuk. Rebellion, weather and faith all reshaped the islands under German rule.

## Sub lesson 3 — Kapingamarangi's German time, 1899–1914

The Kapingamarangi reader dates its German section exactly: The Time of the Germans, 1899–1914. German administration followed the purchase, bringing new officials, new rules and new trade to the atoll.

It ended from outside: the First World War closed Germany's Pacific rule in 1914, and a new foreign power — Japan — took the islands. On the atoll, one dated era simply gives way to the next.
