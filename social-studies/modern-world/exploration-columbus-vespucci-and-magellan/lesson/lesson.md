# Exploration — Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

## Sub lesson 1 — Columbus and the New World

Portugal's rival was **Spain**. Its rulers, **Ferdinand and Isabella**, made exploration a national agenda too, and they funded an Italian sailor, **Christopher Columbus**. Columbus crossed the **Atlantic** and reached the **Caribbean islands** and the lands Europeans called the **New World** — **North and South America**. Thinking he had reached Asia's Indies, he announced the lands as the '**West Indies**'. He was wrong about where he was — and the proof came from **Amerigo Vespucci**, who showed that these lands were **not the Indies** but a different continent pair: **the Americas**. The Americas carry his name to this day: America comes from Amerigo.

## Sub lesson 2 — Magellan — the first voyage around the world

**Ferdinand Magellan** was Portuguese-born (1480), but he sailed for Spain. His idea was bold: **sailing west must reach the Far East**. He won the backing of **Charles V of Spain** and **five small ships**, and set out in **August 1519**. He crossed the Atlantic, and in **October 1520** he found and sailed the **Pacific** — the first to sail that ocean. He reached the **Philippines** — and there Magellan **died**. Four of his five ships were lost to various causes. But the **one surviving ship** kept going, and on **September 9, 1522** (as printed in the book) it returned to Spain. One ship had gone **right around the world**: the first circumnavigation, and living proof that the oceans join into one great ring around the Earth.
