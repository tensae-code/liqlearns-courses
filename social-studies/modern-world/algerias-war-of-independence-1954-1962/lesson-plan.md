# Lesson plan — Algeria's war of independence, 1954–1962

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Algeria's war of independence, 1954–1962
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The massacre of 8 May 1945 teaches Algeria that only arms will free it — the revolution of 1 November 1954 wins independence on 5 July 1962, at a cost of over a million martyrs.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Algeria's war of independence, 1954–1962** — The Constantine massacre of 8 May 1945, in which 45,000 Algerians were martyred, taught the national movement that only arms would free the country; the Great Algerian Revolution opened on 1 November 1954 from the Aurès, Jamila Bouhired among its fighters, a provisional government formed in Cairo in 1958, De Gaulle failed to crush it, and the Evian accords brought independence on 5 July 1962 after a struggle that cost over a million martyrs.
