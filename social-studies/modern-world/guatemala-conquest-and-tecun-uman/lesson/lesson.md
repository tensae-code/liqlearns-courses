# Guatemala — conquest and Tecún Umán

## Do it first

Before you read on, think about this lesson's one thing: Pedro de Alvarado's army and smallpox advance together against highland kingdoms that fail to unite; Tecún Umán falls at El Pinar, Q'umarkaj burns, Santiago is founded at Iximche', and the betrayed Kaqchikel rise in the second stage, painted by their Quauhquechollan allies on the Lienzo. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — First stage — Alvarado, smallpox and El Pinar

Guatemala's Grade 4 book tells the conquest in two stages. Warned by Moctezuma's envoys, the highland lords still fail to unite. Pedro de Alvarado's army advances — and smallpox advances with it. Tecún Umán falls at El Pinar, Q'umarkaj burns, and Santiago is founded at Iximche'. Division, disease and Spanish arms together break the highland kingdoms.

[Picture slot — a picture of the highland road to Q'umarkaj in 1524, with the Lienzo de Quauhquechollan cloth painting unrolled beside it showing the campaign — the student names Tecún Umán and the Lienzo.]

## Sub lesson 2 — Second stage — the Kaqchikel rise and the Lienzo

In the second stage, the Kaqchikel — betrayed as allies — rise against the Spanish. Jorge de Alvarado retraces his brother's route with Quauhquechollan allies, whose weavers paint the campaign on cloth: the Lienzo de Quauhquechollan. The book sets this source-work beside Jorge's own letter, so pupils read the conquest in both a painted cloth and a written letter.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 2 beat(s): First stage — Alvarado, smallpox and El Pinar; Second stage — the Kaqchikel rise and the Lienzo. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
