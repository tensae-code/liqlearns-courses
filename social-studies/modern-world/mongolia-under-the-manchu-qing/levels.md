# Mongolia under the Manchu Qing — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. I.3: Mongolia (mid-17th–18th c.)
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Khalkha splits into Tüsheet, Sechen and Jasagtu khanates, the Oirats rise in the west under Galdan, and Mongol-on-Mongol war opens the door to the Manchu
## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. I.5: Mongolia loses its independence
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Ligdan, the last great khan, is defeated by the Manchu and dies in flight in 1634; Inner Mongolia submits in 1636, and in 1691 the Khalkha nobles at Dolon Nor accept Qing rule
## Sub lesson 3 — Part III, Ch. I.1: How the Mongols fought for freedom and independence
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — under Qing rule the resistance flares in Chingünjav's 1756 uprising in Khotgoid and Amarsanaa's Jungar war — both crushed, both remembered
## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. I.2: The Manchu Qing policy that ruled Mongolia
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Qing bind Mongolia with banners and hereditary jasag princes, forbid Mongol-Chinese mixing and movement, and rule through the Lifan Yuan
## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. I.6: Manchu Qing policy toward Mongolia changes
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the late Qing "new policy" opens Mongolia to Chinese farming colonists and firms like the Mongolor gold company (1900) — and the pressure pushes the nobles toward the 1911 break
## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. I.1 The Mongolian state falls under Manchu domination
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — divided Mongols fall in sequence — Inner Mongolia 1636, Khalkha at Dolon Nuur 1691, the Khoshud in Tibet, and finally the Jungar state destroyed 1755-58
## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. I.3 The causes of Mongolia's loss of independence
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the book weighs internal division, the khans' rivalries and Ligdan's failed unification against Ming decline, Russian advance and Manchu statecraft
