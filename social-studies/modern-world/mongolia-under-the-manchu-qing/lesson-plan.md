# Lesson plan — Mongolia under the Manchu Qing

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Mongolia under the Manchu Qing
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Mongolia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Khalkha splits into Tüsheet, Sechen and Jasagtu khanates, the Oirats rise in the west under Galdan, and Mongol-on-Mongol war opens the door to the Manchu
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ch. I.3: Mongolia (mid-17th–18th c.)** — Khalkha splits into Tüsheet, Sechen and Jasagtu khanates, the Oirats rise in the west under Galdan, and Mongol-on-Mongol war opens the door to the Manchu
2. **Ch. I.5: Mongolia loses its independence** — Ligdan, the last great khan, is defeated by the Manchu and dies in flight in 1634; Inner Mongolia submits in 1636, and in 1691 the Khalkha nobles at Dolon Nor accept Qing rule
3. **Part III, Ch. I.1: How the Mongols fought for freedom and independence** — under Qing rule the resistance flares in Chingünjav's 1756 uprising in Khotgoid and Amarsanaa's Jungar war — both crushed, both remembered
4. **Ch. I.2: The Manchu Qing policy that ruled Mongolia** — the Qing bind Mongolia with banners and hereditary jasag princes, forbid Mongol-Chinese mixing and movement, and rule through the Lifan Yuan
5. **Ch. I.6: Manchu Qing policy toward Mongolia changes** — the late Qing "new policy" opens Mongolia to Chinese farming colonists and firms like the Mongolor gold company (1900) — and the pressure pushes the nobles toward the 1911 break
6. **Ch. I.1 The Mongolian state falls under Manchu domination** — divided Mongols fall in sequence — Inner Mongolia 1636, Khalkha at Dolon Nuur 1691, the Khoshud in Tibet, and finally the Jungar state destroyed 1755-58
7. **Ch. I.3 The causes of Mongolia's loss of independence** — the book weighs internal division, the khans' rivalries and Ligdan's failed unification against Ming decline, Russian advance and Manchu statecraft
