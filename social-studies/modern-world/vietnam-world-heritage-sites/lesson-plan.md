# Lesson plan — Vietnam World Heritage Sites

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam World Heritage Sites
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the tangible timeline of special national relics and UNESCO inscriptions runs from the Huế complex (1993) through Hội An and Mỹ Sơn (1999), the Đền Hùng temples, the Independence Palace and the Điện Biên Phủ battlefield (2009), the Thăng Long centre (2009–10) and the Hồ Citadel (2011–12) to the Ngọc Lũ bronze drum, the Óc Eo–Ba Thê site and Côn Đảo prison (2012); Mỹ Sơn is a complex of over 70 Chăm temple-towers built from the 4th to the 13th century under Hindu influence, where every Chăm king worshipped after his accession — its brick walls, bonded by an adhesive still unidentified, have stood a thousand years; Hội An grows from a Chăm-pa port into Đàng Trong's busiest harbour town (15th–16th centuries), where the Nguyễn lords let Chinese and Japanese merchants settle in their own streets — the Japanese-built Chùa Cầu of about the 17th century still stands; the Thăng Long Imperial Citadel centre layers foundations from the Chinese-rule age through the Lý, Trần, Lê, Mạc and Nguyễn dynasties, excavated at 18 Hoàng Diệu beside the Kính Thiên Palace steps, Đoan Môn gate and the D67 bunker; the natural and mixed table adds Hạ Long Bay (1994, 2000 — with nearly 20 archaeological sites 3,500–14,000 years old proving it a cradle of the ancient Viet), Phong Nha–Kẻ Bàng (2003, 2015 — a 400-million-year "giant geology museum" whose boundary UNESCO approved extending in 2025 to Laos's Hin Nam Nô, the first transboundary World Heritage to carry the Phong Nha–Kẻ Bàng name), the Đồng Văn karst plateau geopark (2010 — Sơn Vi culture tools 10,000–30,000 years old, home of 17 ethnic groups) and Tràng An (mixed World Heritage 2014, with over 30 sites spanning 30,000 years of human adaptation)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
