# Papua New Guinea — early explorers, missionaries, miners and traders arrive — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Four kinds of outsiders reach the island of New Guinea one after another — navigators charting coasts, missionaries carrying the word of God, then miners and traders after wealth — children name them, date them and trace their routes on the map.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Early Explorers / Missionaries / Miners and Traders

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Social Science Teacher Guide — Primary Grade 7, Standards Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Early Explorers / Missionaries / Miners and Traders

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Five European groups build colonial power — administrators (the Company, then Governor Hahl), gold miners who open Sudest, Misima and Wau-Bulolo, settlers like Queen Emma of Rabaul (welcomed by some, resisted by the Binandere), and mission churches whose local catechists and pastors carry schools and health centres.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Roles of Europeans in Papua and New Guinea

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Social Science Junior High Grade 9 Teacher Guide, Standards-Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Roles of Europeans in Papua and New Guinea

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The Spice Islands and a rumoured golden southern continent pull Europeans in; mapping creeps up the rivers from the coast until the 1930 patrols walk into a Highlands world Europe did not know existed.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Early explorers and economic activities

**Answer: False** — it is about Early explorers and economic activities
