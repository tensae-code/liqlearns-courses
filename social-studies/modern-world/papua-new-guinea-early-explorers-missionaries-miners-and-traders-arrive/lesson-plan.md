# Lesson plan — Papua New Guinea — early explorers, missionaries, miners and traders arrive

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Papua New Guinea — early explorers, missionaries, miners and traders arrive
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Papua New Guinea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Four kinds of outsiders reach the island of New Guinea one after another — navigators charting coasts, missionaries carrying the word of God, then miners and traders after wealth — children name them, date them and trace their routes on the map
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Early Explorers / Missionaries / Miners and Traders** — four kinds of outsiders reach the island of New Guinea one after another — navigators charting coasts, missionaries carrying the word of God, then miners and traders after wealth — children name them, date them and trace their routes on the map
2. **Roles of Europeans in Papua and New Guinea** — five European groups build colonial power — administrators (the Company, then Governor Hahl), gold miners who open Sudest, Misima and Wau-Bulolo, settlers like Queen Emma of Rabaul (welcomed by some, resisted by the Binandere), and mission churches whose local catechists and pastors carry schools and health centres
3. **Early explorers and economic activities** — the Spice Islands and a rumoured golden southern continent pull Europeans in; mapping creeps up the rivers from the coast until the 1930 patrols walk into a Highlands world Europe did not know existed
4. **Unit 11.2 case-study assessment: one planter, administrator, missionary, trader or miner in Papua and New Guinea, from primary sources** — empire gets a face — one foreigner's life, impact and legacy reconstructed from the evidence he left behind

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
