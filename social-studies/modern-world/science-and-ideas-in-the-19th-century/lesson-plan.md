**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Science And Ideas In The 19th Century
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Greece, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Darwin, Pasteur, Marx and Freud remake how the world explains nature, society and the mind
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§24 Sciences and thought in the 19th-century world** — Darwin, Pasteur, Marx and Freud remake how the world explains nature, society and the mind
2. **Science and thought in the 19th-century world** — Darwin rewrites man's place in nature, Marx rewrites society's, and laboratories change daily life
3. **The 19th-century world scene: the triumph of science** — steam gives way to electricity, germs are found, evolution is argued — science becomes the age's authority
4. **The movement of ideas: science and thought of the 19th century** — positivism, Darwin, Marx and the new social sciences remake how the century explains nature and society
