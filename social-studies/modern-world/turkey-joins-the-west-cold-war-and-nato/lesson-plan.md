# Lesson plan — Turkey joins the West — Cold War and NATO

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Turkey joins the West — Cold War and NATO
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 6, Turkey and the World after the Second World War (Turkey part): the 1945–1950 passage to multi-party life and the Democrat Party's 1950 victory; the Council of Europe (1949), the Korean brigade and NATO membership; the Turkey of the 1950s.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: the republic votes its founders' party out, and anchors itself to the West in the same five years. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the home face of the turn: the passage to multi-party life and the Democrat Party's 1950 victory.
2. Name the abroad face: Council of Europe (1949), the Korean brigade, NATO membership.
3. Say what the 1950 election proves: the founders' party can be voted out, peacefully.
4. Explain, in their own words, why the book treats the two faces as one turn.
