# Turkey joins the West — Cold War and NATO

## Do it first

In five years, a one-party republic does two things its founder never saw: it votes its founders' own party out of power, and it anchors the country to the West — a council in Europe, soldiers in Korea, a seat in NATO. Before you read on, decide whether those two moves pull together or apart. Hold your answer; the book treats them as one turn.

## Sub lesson 1 — Multi-party life, the Democrat victory, Korea and NATO

Turkey's Grade 12 book gives the post-war turn two faces, and dates them close. At home, the 1945–1950 passage to multi-party life ends in the Democrat Party's victory of 1950 — the republic votes its founders' party out, peacefully, at the ballot box.

Abroad, the same years anchor Turkey to the West: the Council of Europe in 1949, the Turkish brigade fighting in Korea, and NATO membership sealing the choice. The book's beat holds the two faces together: the republic votes its founders' party out, and anchors itself to the West in the same five years — democracy at home and alliance abroad as one turn, into the Turkey of the 1950s.

In your mind's eye: 1950s Turkey at the crossroads — voters queueing at a ballot box in a village square while, beyond, Turkish soldiers board a ship for Korea under the UN flag. The student points to the ballot box and the ship and says what single turn joins them.
