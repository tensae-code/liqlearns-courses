# Lesson plan — The Long Road To Human Rights

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Long Road To Human Rights
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Armenia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a timeline runs from Cyrus's clay cylinder (539 BC) through Magna Carta (1215) to America's 1776 and France's 1789 declarations and the UN's 1948 charter — with Armenia's own Shahamir Shahamirian printing his «Vorogayt Parats» constitution draft in Madras in 1773
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§6.3 The history of human rights and freedoms** — a timeline runs from Cyrus's clay cylinder (539 BC) through Magna Carta (1215) to America's 1776 and France's 1789 declarations and the UN's 1948 charter — with Armenia's own Shahamir Shahamirian printing his «Vorogayt Parats» constitution draft in Madras in 1773
2. **Lesson plan for §6.3, the history of human rights** — teachers are advised to present rights as a long struggle won step by step, not a gift handed down
3. **§2.1 The components and generations of human rights** — rights arrive in generations — civil and political rights won in the age of revolutions, social rights in the industrial age, collective rights after 1945
