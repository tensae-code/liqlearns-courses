# Lesson plan — The European geographical discoveries

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The European geographical discoveries
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Yemen, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** armed with Arab navigation science, the compass, the astrolabe and Islamic maps, and hungry to reach India and China without Muslim middlemen and their prices
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The European geographical discoveries** — armed with Arab navigation science, the compass, the astrolabe and Islamic maps, and hungry to reach India and China without Muslim middlemen and their prices, Portugal and Spain sail out — Henry the Navigator feeling down Africa's coast, Dias rounding the Cape of Storms, da Gama reaching Calicut, Columbus crossing to a world he dies believing is India, Vespucci's name given to the Americas, Magellan's men completing the circle of the earth the Muslims had already proved on paper — followed by Cabot, Cook and the French and Dutch, until discovery hardens into colonisation, the slave trade, and the wiping out of whole peoples
