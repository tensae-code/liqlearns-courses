# Honduras Morazan And The Federation — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Morazán, born in Tegucigalpa in 1792, wins the five-hour Battle of La Trinidad (11 November 1827) against Justo Milla, retakes the country, presides over the Federation, abolishes the tithe and sets civil marriage — then Rafael Carrera brings him down and he is shot in San José on 15 September 1842.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lección 26 Nuestro país después de la independencia: Morazán

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Libro para Estudiantes, Ciencias Sociales, Tercer grado* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lección 26 Nuestro país después de la independencia: Morazán

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Fearing invasion, the provinces annex to Iturbide's Mexico; on 1 July 1823 they declare absolute independence and form the Central American Federation under Arce, liberals against conservatives; in 1838 the Federation dissolves and Honduras becomes a State with its first constitution.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lección 26: anexión, Federación y Estado

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Libro para Estudiantes, Ciencias Sociales, Tercer grado* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lección 26: anexión, Federación y Estado

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The five provinces united with free press and curbed church power, but fiscal deficit, regionalism, church boycott and Guatemala's rivalry killed it; Morazán, proclaimed liberator of Costa Rica, was shot in San José on 15 September 1842, and thirty years of conservative reaction and caudillismo followed.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in the Federal Republic 1825–1839

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Libro para Estudiantes, Ciencias Sociales — Séptimo Grado de Educación Básica* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about the Federal Republic 1825–1839
