# The US Constitution and republican governments, 1776–1790

## Do it first

First a pamphlet killed a king in print. Then thirteen arguing states tried to build a republic — and had to build it twice. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Common Sense kills the king in print

Paine's Common Sense attacked the very idea of kingship in plain language any reader could follow. Before independence was declared, it was argued — and the argument killed the king in print.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — The states experiment with democracy

The new states experimented with democracy, writing their own governments and arguing over how much change the Revolution really made. Under the Articles the union stayed loose — and the looseness began to fail.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Convention of 1787 builds a federal republic

The Constitutional Convention of 1787 built a federal republic strong enough to replace the failing Articles: power divided between the union and the states, and strong enough to hold both. The republic had its frame.
