## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Majority Rule – Independence Part 1: the Burma Road Riot, 1942** — pay varies by sex and race — four shillings for men, two for women — and Bay Street merchants force an American company to keep Bahamian wages unequal; workers march from Oakes Field to Bay Street, the Riot Act is read, and five days later they return with a wage increase won; in 1953 the PLP is founded, the first party to represent the Black majority (Majority Rule – Independence Part 1: the Burma Road Riot, 1942)
2. **Majority Rule – Independence Part 2: suffrage, Black Tuesday, independence** — Mary Ingraham and Georgianna Symonette lead the Women's Suffrage Movement to victory in 1962, Ruby Ann Cooper Darling first to register; on Black Tuesday, 27 April 1965, Pindling throws the Speaker's mace out of the House window to the crowd and Butler follows with the hourglass; in 1969 the Premier becomes Prime Minister, and in 1973 independence arrives with Butler as Governor General and Pindling as Prime Minister (Majority Rule – Independence Part 2: suffrage, Black Tuesday, independence)
3. **Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 2: Burma Road on the timeline** — on the road-to-independence timeline the June 1942 Burma Road Riot is the first dated step — unequal pay by sex, race and nationality, the new Federation of Labour, the march on Bay Street, the Riot Act, and a wage increase five days later (Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 2: Burma Road on the timeline)
4. **Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 3: 1953 to 1973, the new political era** — the PLP (1953) demands one man one vote; women win the vote in 1962; Black Tuesday (1965) shows the House's power lies with the people; in 1967 the PLP wins and Pindling becomes the first Black Premier — still a colony — until independence in 1973 makes him Prime Minister (Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 3: 1953 to 1973, the new political era)
