# Video script — Bahamas — Majority Rule and the road to independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bahamas books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

pay varies by sex and race — four shillings for men, two for women — and Bay Street merchants force an American company to keep Bahamian wages unequal; workers march from Oakes Field to Bay Street, the Riot Act is read, and five days later they return with a wage increase won; in 1953 the PLP is founded, the first party to represent the Black majority; Mary Ingraham and Georgianna Symonette lead the Women's Suffrage Movement to victory in 1962, Ruby Ann Cooper Darling first to register; on Black Tuesday, 27 April 1965, Pindling throws the Speaker's mace out of the House window to the crowd and Butler follows with the hourglass; in 1969 the Premier becomes Prime Minister, and in 1973 independence arrives with Butler as Governor General and Pindling as Prime Minister; on the road-to-independence timeline the June 1942 Burma Road Riot is the first dated step — unequal pay by sex, race and nationality, the new Federation of Labour, the march on Bay Street, the Riot Act, and a wage increase five days later; the PLP (1953) demands one man one vote; women win the vote in 1962; Black Tuesday (1965) shows the House's power lies with the people; in 1967 the PLP wins and Pindling becomes the first Black Premier — still a colony — until independence in 1973 makes him Prime Minister

Hold that story: From the Burma Road Riot of 1942, through the PLP's founding in 1953, women's suffrage in 1962 and Black Tuesday in 1965, the Black majority wins Majority Rule in 1967 — and the road ends at independence in 1973
