# Bahamas — Majority Rule and the road to independence — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Majority Rule – Independence Part 1: the Burma Road Riot, 1942
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: pay varies by sex and race — four shillings for men, two for women — and Bay Street merchants force an American company to keep Bahamian wages unequal; workers march from Oakes Field to Bay Street, the Riot Act is read, and five days later they return with a wage increase won; in 1953 the PLP is founded, the first party to represent the Black majority

## Sub lesson 2 — Majority Rule – Independence Part 2: suffrage, Black Tuesday, independence
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Mary Ingraham and Georgianna Symonette lead the Women's Suffrage Movement to victory in 1962, Ruby Ann Cooper Darling first to register; on Black Tuesday, 27 April 1965, Pindling throws the Speaker's mace out of the House window to the crowd and Butler follows with the hourglass; in 1969 the Premier becomes Prime Minister, and in 1973 independence arrives with Butler as Governor General and Pindling as Prime Minister

## Sub lesson 3 — Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 2: Burma Road on the timeline
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: on the road-to-independence timeline the June 1942 Burma Road Riot is the first dated step — unequal pay by sex, race and nationality, the new Federation of Labour, the march on Bay Street, the Riot Act, and a wage increase five days later

## Sub lesson 4 — Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 3: 1953 to 1973, the new political era
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the PLP (1953) demands one man one vote; women win the vote in 1962; Black Tuesday (1965) shows the House's power lies with the people; in 1967 the PLP wins and Pindling becomes the first Black Premier — still a colony — until independence in 1973 makes him Prime Minister
