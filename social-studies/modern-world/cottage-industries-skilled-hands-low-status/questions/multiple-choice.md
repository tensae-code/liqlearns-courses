# Cottage industries — skilled hands, low status — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Which list is all cottage industries named in the lesson?
a) Metalworking, pottery, weaving, basket-making  b) Shipbuilding, printing, mining, banking  c) Railways, telegraph, factories, mills  d) Only weaving
**Answer: a) Metalworking, pottery, weaving, basket-making** — Metalworking, pottery, tannery, carpentry, masonry, weaving, jewellery and basket-making are the named crafts.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Blacksmiths in Ethiopia's cottage industries chiefly manufactured —
a) Guns for export only  b) A wide variety of agricultural implements  c) Church bells only  d) Coinage
**Answer: b) A wide variety of agricultural implements** — Agricultural implements — the tools farming lived by.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Ethiopia's cottage industries are described as —
a) New imports from Europe  b) Immemorial — practised from ancient times  c) A 19th-century invention  d) Royal monopolies
**Answer: b) Immemorial — practised from ancient times** — The book calls them immemorial.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The general public's attitude to artisans was —
a) Honour and high rank  b) Disdain — artisans were marginalised  c) Indifference  d) Fear of their armies
**Answer: b) Disdain — artisans were marginalised** — The public disdained and marginalised them.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** The consequence of the artisans' low status was —
a) Implements and furniture showed little improvement or sophistication  b) Crafts disappeared entirely  c) Artisans became rulers  d) Imports were banned
**Answer: a) Implements and furniture showed little improvement or sophistication** — Stigma held back improvement and sophistication.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** The lesson's comparison with Europe's factory age is that —
a) Ethiopia had more factories  b) In Ethiopia the makers stayed at the bottom, and their tools stayed simple  c) Europe despised all makers equally  d) There is no comparison
**Answer: b) In Ethiopia the makers stayed at the bottom, and their tools stayed simple** — Skill without honour does not grow — the makers stayed low, the tools simple.
