# Cottage industries — skilled hands, low status

## Sub lesson 1 — The crafts that clothed and fed a country

Before factories, Ethiopia made its world by hand — and had done so from time immemorial. Its **cottage industries** covered the needs of life: **metalworking, pottery, tannery, carpentry, masonry, weaving, jewellery and basket-making**. At the forge stood the **blacksmith**, manufacturing a wide variety of **agricultural implements** — the ploughshares and tools that the whole farming country ate by. ## Sub lesson 2 — Held in low status — and what it cost

Now the lesson's sharp point. The hands were skilled — but the people who owned them were not honoured. The **general public disdained and marginalised artisans**, looking down on the very crafts everyone depended on. And stigma has a price: **consequently, locally produced implements and household furniture showed little improvement or sophistication**. A technology held in contempt does not grow. Compare the capitalism lesson: in Europe, the factory age raised machine-makers to power; in Ethiopia, the makers stayed at the bottom — and their tools stayed simple.
