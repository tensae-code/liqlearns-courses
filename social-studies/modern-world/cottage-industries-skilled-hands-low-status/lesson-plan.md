# Lesson plan — Cottage industries — skilled hands, low status

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Cottage industries — skilled hands, low status
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Social, Economic and Political Developments in Ethiopia, Mid-19th C. to 1941 (Grade 10 History); built in the Modern World title beside the capitalism lesson, whose factory system is this lesson's mirror.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's **cottage industries** are immemorial — metalworking, pottery, tannery, carpentry, masonry, weaving, jewellery and basket-making — yet the public **disdained and marginalised the artisans**, and that stigma held back the improvement of the very implements the country lived by.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands beside the capitalism lesson in the Modern World title — the same world of hand production, seen from Ethiopia, before the factory age arrives. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The crafts that clothed and fed a country** — List Ethiopia's cottage industries and say what blacksmiths in particular made: a wide variety of agricultural implements.
2. **Held in low status — and what it cost** — Explain that the general public disdained and marginalised artisans, and that consequently locally produced implements and household furniture showed little improvement or sophistication.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List Ethiopia's cottage industries.
2. Say what blacksmiths chiefly made.
3. Explain how the artisans' low status held back the improvement of implements and furniture.
