# The world after 1945 — losses, division and rebuilding

## Sub lesson 1 — The aftermath inventory — losses, and a divided continent

The Grade 12 book opens its post-1945 unit not with celebration but with an **inventory** — count what the war left before you judge what followed.

   First, the **human and material losses**: tens of millions dead across Europe and Asia, cities flattened from Warsaw to Berlin, factories, railways, bridges and farms destroyed, millions homeless or displaced, whole economies in ruins. No earlier war's bill had ever been presented to a whole continent at once.

   Second, the **division**. **Germany was divided** — a western part under the Western Allies and an eastern part under the Soviet Union — and **Europe divided with it**, an ideological frontier running through the continent's middle. Out of the ruins stood **two superpowers**, the United States and the Soviet Union, and a world **demanding a new organisation** to keep the peace the League had failed to keep — the demand the United Nations was built to answer.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rebuilding the West — the Marshall Plan and the welfare state

Recovery, where it came, was organised. The **Marshall Plan** poured American money into Western Europe's rebuilding — factories restarted, trade revived, and the western half of the continent rose with startling speed. The socialist bloc rebuilt under its own plans, on its own side of the divide.

   And inside the recovering West, governments changed their promise. The **welfare state** spread: states took responsibility for health, housing, education and support in unemployment and old age — the lesson their peoples drew from depression and war was that a state must catch its citizens when they fall.

## Sub lesson 3 — The new order's banks — the World Bank and the IMF

Two institutions were built to run the new economic order, and the Grade 12 book names them as part of this placement's strand: the **World Bank**, to lend for reconstruction and development, and the **International Monetary Fund (IMF)**, to steady currencies and trade between nations.

   Stand back and see the unit's shape as the book builds it: losses counted, a continent divided, recovery organised by plan and by welfare state, and money itself given new world institutions. That inventory is the ground on which the United Nations, the Cold War and everything after 1945 in this course is built.
