# Lesson plan — Gilded Age politics and populism, 1870–1900

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Gilded Age politics and populism, 1870–1900
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (United States, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Gilded means gold paint over cheap metal. Politics ran on corruption and favours, the parties split over tariffs and gold — and indebted farmers revolted.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else first.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Corruption and patronage dominate** — Corruption and patronage dominated postbellum politics: jobs and contracts handed to supporters, machines running the cities and much of the parties.
2. **The tariff and gold questions divide the parties** — Two questions divided the parties for a generation: the tariff — taxing imports to protect industry — and gold: whether money should be gold alone or silver too.
3. **Indebted farmers revolt — Populism** — Indebted farmers revolted in the Populist movement — organising against railroads, banks and the gold standard that squeezed them.
