# Video script — Gilded Age politics and populism, 1870–1900

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told straight, in the order it happened. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Gilded means gold paint over cheap metal. Politics ran on corruption and favours, the parties split over tariffs and gold — and indebted farmers revolted.

Corruption and patronage dominated postbellum politics: jobs and contracts handed to supporters, machines running the cities and much of the parties. The age glittered; the book keeps asking what the glitter covered.

Two questions divided the parties for a generation: the tariff — taxing imports to protect industry — and gold: whether money should be gold alone or silver too. Behind both stood a real fight over who the economy was run for.

Indebted farmers revolted in the Populist movement — organising against railroads, banks and the gold standard that squeezed them. The 1890s closed in strikes and social unrest: the gilding was cracking.
