# Video script — The Marianas and Carolines — first Catholic missions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as Micronesia's books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The first Catholic mission in these islands was planted on Guam by Fr. Sanvitores. A mission meant a church, a priest who stayed, and teaching and baptism offered to the islanders — a permanent Spanish presence of a new kind: not a passing ship, but a settlement of faith.

With the mission came an empire's name: the islands the Spanish had called the Ladrones were renamed the Marianas. And the welcome did not hold. Spanish soldiers turned welcome into conflict, and the mission's story on Guam became a story of fighting as well as faith — the pattern the book sets down for the first missions.

A generation later, the mission story moved south. Fr. Cantova carried word of the Catholic faith from the Marianas into the Carolines — the island groups that would become Micronesia. The Carolines' long church history, Catholic and later Protestant, begins with journeys like his.
