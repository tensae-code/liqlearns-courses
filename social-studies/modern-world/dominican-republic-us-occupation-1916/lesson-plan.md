# Lesson plan — Dominican Republic — the US occupation, 1916–1924

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Dominican Republic — the US occupation, 1916–1924
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Dominican Republic, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Dictators borrow and default until the United States steps in, first with customs, then with marines.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unidad 7 Dictaduras de Heureaux (Lilís) y Cáceres; hegemonía de los Estados Unidos y primera intervención** — dictators borrow and default until the United States steps in, first with customs, then with marines
2. **Unidad 7 Historia dominicana (1916–1963): Ocupación norteamericana (1916–1924)** — US marines occupy the country for eight years and build the constabulary that will produce Trujillo
3. **Unidad 1 Ocupación estadounidense: causas iniciales, planes y acciones, apogeo azucarero** — in 1916 the marines land, claiming to end caudillo chaos, while sugar booms under occupation
4. **Unidad 1 Resistencia a la Ocupación y hacia la Desocupación** — Dominican patriots fight at La Barranquita and resist until the occupation winds down in 1924
