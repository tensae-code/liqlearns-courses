# Brazil — from slavery to free labour

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Brazil and the United States ended slavery in two different ways — Brazil by gradual laws to abolition in 1888 without land or rights for the freed, the United States through civil war — and in both countries the freed were left to compete as wage workers. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two slave societies

Brazil and the United States were both slave societies. In both, enslaved people did the hardest work of the land — and in both, slavery would one day end. But the book teaches that the two countries ended it very differently, and that difference shaped everything that came after.

## Sub lesson 2 — Brazil — gradual laws to 1888

In Brazil, slavery ended step by step, through gradual laws, until abolition came in 1888. The freed received no land and no rights to start their new lives with. Freedom arrived — but empty-handed.

## Sub lesson 3 — The United States — civil war, and the same hard landing

In the United States, slavery ended through civil war. Yet the book holds the two endings side by side and lands them in the same place: in both countries, the freed were left to compete as wage workers — free to sell their labour, with nothing else to live on. That is how slavery's end connects to the story of wage labour itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of Brazil — from slavery to free labour in your own words, part by part, using the sub lesson headings as your beats. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

2. Say how the United States ended slavery, and how that differed from Brazil's path.
