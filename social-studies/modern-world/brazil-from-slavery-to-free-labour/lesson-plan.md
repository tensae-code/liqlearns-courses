# Lesson plan — Brazil — from slavery to free labour

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Brazil — from slavery to free labour
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Brazil, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Brazil and the United States ended slavery in two different ways — Brazil by gradual laws to abolition in 1888 without land or rights for the freed, the United States through civil war — and in both countries the freed were left to compete as wage workers.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Two slave societies** — Brazil and the United States were both slave societies. In both, enslaved people did the hardest work of the land — and in both, slavery would one day end. But the book teaches that the two countries ended it very differently, and that difference shaped everything that came after.
2. **Brazil — gradual laws to 1888** — In Brazil, slavery ended step by step, through gradual laws, until abolition came in 1888. The freed received no land and no rights to start their new lives with. Freedom arrived — but empty-handed.
3. **The United States — civil war, and the same hard landing** — In the United States, slavery ended through civil war. Yet the book holds the two endings side by side and lands them in the same place: in both countries, the freed were left to compete as wage workers — free to sell their labour, with nothing else to live on. That is how slavery's end connects to the story of wage labour itself.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Capitalism — private ownership, factories, new classes; The American Civil War — union against slavery. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how Brazil ended slavery — gradual laws to abolition in 1888 — and what the freed did not receive.
2. Say how the United States ended slavery, and how that differed from Brazil's path.
3. Say what the freed in both countries were left to do, and why the book teaches the two endings together.
