## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **3.1 Reasons for economic recovery** — Stresemann's Rentenmark, the Dawes Plan's American loans and restarted reparations steady the economy from 1924 — a recovery built on borrowed money
2. **3.2 Stresemann's policies abroad** — Stresemann wins Locarno, League of Nations entry (1926) and the Young Plan, restoring Germany's standing abroad; his death in October 1929 removes the republic's most trusted leader weeks before the Crash
3. **4.1 The standard of living** — Wages and welfare rise in the 'golden years', yet farmers, the Mittelstand and those ruined in 1923 never share the recovery and feel ignored by the republic
4. **4.2 The position of women** — Weimar women vote, work and adopt new fashions and freedoms in the cities, while much of Germany expects them back in the home — a freedom the Nazis later promise to reverse
5. **4.3 Cultural changes** — Bauhaus design, cabaret, cinema and art flourish in a republic without censorship; the same openness lets opponents call the culture decadent
