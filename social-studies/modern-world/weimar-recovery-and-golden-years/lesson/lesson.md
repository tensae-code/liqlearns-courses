# Weimar recovery and golden years

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From 1924, Stresemann steadies Weimar — recovery, diplomacy and golden years built on borrowed money. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — 3.1 Reasons for economic recovery

Stresemann's Rentenmark, the Dawes Plan's American loans and restarted reparations steady the economy from 1924 — a recovery built on borrowed money.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — 3.2 Stresemann's policies abroad

Stresemann wins Locarno, League of Nations entry (1926) and the Young Plan, restoring Germany's standing abroad; his death in October 1929 removes the republic's most trusted leader weeks before the Crash.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — 4.1 The standard of living

Wages and welfare rise in the 'golden years', yet farmers, the Mittelstand and those ruined in 1923 never share the recovery and feel ignored by the republic.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — 4.2 The position of women

Weimar women vote, work and adopt new fashions and freedoms in the cities, while much of Germany expects them back in the home — a freedom the Nazis later promise to reverse.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 5 — 4.3 Cultural changes

Bauhaus design, cabaret, cinema and art flourish in a republic without censorship; the same openness lets opponents call the culture decadent.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: 3.1 Reasons for economic recovery; 3.2 Stresemann's policies abroad; 4.1 The standard of living; 4.2 The position of women; 4.3 Cultural changes. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
