# Weimar recovery and golden years — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — 3.1 Reasons for economic recovery
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Stresemann's Rentenmark, the Dawes Plan's American loans and restarted reparations steady the economy from 1924 — a recovery built on borrowed money

## Sub lesson 2 — 3.2 Stresemann's policies abroad
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Stresemann wins Locarno, League of Nations entry (1926) and the Young Plan, restoring Germany's standing abroad; his death in October 1929 removes the republic's most trusted leader weeks before the Crash

## Sub lesson 3 — 4.1 The standard of living
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Wages and welfare rise in the 'golden years', yet farmers, the Mittelstand and those ruined in 1923 never share the recovery and feel ignored by the republic

## Sub lesson 4 — 4.2 The position of women
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Weimar women vote, work and adopt new fashions and freedoms in the cities, while much of Germany expects them back in the home — a freedom the Nazis later promise to reverse

## Sub lesson 5 — 4.3 Cultural changes
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Bauhaus design, cabaret, cinema and art flourish in a republic without censorship; the same openness lets opponents call the culture decadent
