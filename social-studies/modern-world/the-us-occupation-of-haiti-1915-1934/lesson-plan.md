# Lesson plan — The US occupation of Haiti, 1915–1934

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The US occupation of Haiti, 1915–1934
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Haiti, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** US Marines land in 1915 and stay until 1934 — customs, finances and a gendarmerie pass under Washington's hand, Caco guerrillas and writers resist, and pupils must weigh roads and sanitation against humiliation, deaths and a militarised state.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Preludes to the occupation** — presidents fall one after another, debts press in, and President Vilbrun Guillaume Sam is assassinated on 28 July 1915 — US Marines land the same day (Séance 1, Le contexte externe et interne ou les préludes à l'occupation)
2. **The Haitian state placed under tutelage** — an imposed convention hands Washington Haiti's customs, finances and a US-officered gendarmerie — sovereignty survives on paper only (Séance 2, La mise sous tutelle de l'État haïtien ?)
3. **The occupation and institutionalisation** — the occupation builds roads with forced corvée labour and a centralised armed force — the machinery pupils are asked to link to Haiti's later authoritarian model (Séance 3, L'occupation et le processus d'institutionnalisation)
4. **Resistances to the occupation** — Caco guerrillas under leaders like Charlemagne Péralte fight from the hills while writers and nationalists resist in print (Séance 4, Les résistances à l'occupation)
5. **Collaboration with the occupier** — through the era's newspapers, pupils watch elites who served, hedged or turned against the occupier as the years changed the bargain (Séance 5, La collaboration avec l'occupant, des relations complexes et changeantes)
6. **Towards a balance sheet of the occupation** — the Marines leave in 1934; pupils weigh roads and sanitation against humiliation, deaths and a militarised state, and deliver a verdict (Séance 6, Vers un bilan de l'occupation américaine ?)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
