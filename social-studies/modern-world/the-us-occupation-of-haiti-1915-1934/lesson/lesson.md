# The US occupation of Haiti, 1915–1934

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: US Marines land in 1915 and stay until 1934 — customs, finances and a gendarmerie pass under Washington's hand, Caco guerrillas and writers resist, and pupils must weigh roads and sanitation against humiliation, deaths and a militarised state. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Preludes to the occupation

Presidents fall one after another, debts press in, and president vilbrun guillaume sam is assassinated on 28 july 1915 — us marines land the same day (Séance 1, Le contexte externe et interne ou les préludes à l'occupation)

In your mind's eye: charlemagne Péralte and the Caco resistance in the hills. Name one way Haitians resisted the occupation.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Haitian state placed under tutelage

An imposed convention hands washington haiti's customs, finances and a us-officered gendarmerie — sovereignty survives on paper only (Séance 2, La mise sous tutelle de l'État haïtien ?)

## Sub lesson 3 — The occupation and institutionalisation

The occupation builds roads with forced corvée labour and a centralised armed force — the machinery pupils are asked to link to haiti's later authoritarian model (Séance 3, L'occupation et le processus d'institutionnalisation)

## Sub lesson 4 — Resistances to the occupation

Caco guerrillas under leaders like charlemagne péralte fight from the hills while writers and nationalists resist in print (Séance 4, Les résistances à l'occupation)

## Sub lesson 5 — Collaboration with the occupier

Through the era's newspapers, pupils watch elites who served, hedged or turned against the occupier as the years changed the bargain (Séance 5, La collaboration avec l'occupant, des relations complexes et changeantes)

## Sub lesson 6 — Towards a balance sheet of the occupation

The marines leave in 1934; pupils weigh roads and sanitation against humiliation, deaths and a militarised state, and deliver a verdict (Séance 6, Vers un bilan de l'occupation américaine ?)
