# The US occupation of Haiti, 1915–1934 — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Preludes to the occupation.
**Answer: Presidents fall one after another, debts press in, and president vilbrun guillaume sam is assassinated on 28 july 1915 — us marines land the same day (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The Haitian state placed under tutelage.
**Answer: An imposed convention hands washington haiti's customs, finances and a us-officered gendarmerie — sovereignty survives on paper only (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The occupation and institutionalisation.
**Answer: The occupation builds roads with forced corvée labour and a centralised armed force — the machinery pupils are asked to link to haiti's later authoritarian model (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Resistances to the occupation.
**Answer: Caco guerrillas under leaders like charlemagne péralte fight from the hills while writers and nationalists resist in print (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Collaboration with the occupier.
**Answer: Through the era's newspapers, pupils watch elites who served, hedged or turned against the occupier as the years changed the bargain (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Towards a balance sheet of the occupation.
**Answer: The marines leave in 1934; pupils weigh roads and sanitation against humiliation, deaths and a militarised state, and deliver a verdict (any close wording).**
