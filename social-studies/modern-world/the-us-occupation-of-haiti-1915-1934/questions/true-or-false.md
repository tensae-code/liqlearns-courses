# The US occupation of Haiti, 1915–1934 — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Presidents fall one after another, debts press in, and president vilbrun guillaume sam is assassinated on 28 july 1915 — us marines land the same day
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 1, Le contexte externe et interne ou les préludes à l'occupation).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** An imposed convention hands washington haiti's customs, finances and a us-officered gendarmerie — sovereignty survives on paper only
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 2, La mise sous tutelle de l'État haïtien ?).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** The occupation builds roads with forced corvée labour and a centralised armed force — the machinery pupils are asked to link to haiti's later authoritarian model
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 3, L'occupation et le processus d'institutionnalisation).

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Caco guerrillas under leaders like charlemagne péralte fight from the hills while writers and nationalists resist in print
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 4, Les résistances à l'occupation).

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Through the era's newspapers, pupils watch elites who served, hedged or turned against the occupier as the years changed the bargain
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 5, La collaboration avec l'occupant, des relations complexes et changeantes).

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** The marines leave in 1934; pupils weigh roads and sanitation against humiliation, deaths and a militarised state, and deliver a verdict
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 6, Vers un bilan de l'occupation américaine ?).
