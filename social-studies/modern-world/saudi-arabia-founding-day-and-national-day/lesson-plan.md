# Lesson plan — Saudi Arabia — Founding Day and National Day

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Saudi Arabia — Founding Day and National Day
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Saudi Arabia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one state begins in a small town in 1727, when Imam Muhammad bin Saud founds the Saudi state, and is united into a kingdom two centuries later, in 1932, by King Abdulaziz — and Saudi children mark both days every year, Founding Day and National Day, taught in the Grade 4 book inside the Citizenship unit's lesson on national symbols, with Flag Day beside them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: two birthdays for one country — 1727, when the state was founded, and 1932, when it was united into a kingdom — more than two hundred years apart. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Founding Day, 1727** — Imam Muhammad bin Saud founds the Saudi state in 1727 (1139هـ) — roots more than 300 years deep.
2. **National Day, 1932** — King Abdulaziz unifies the country in 1932 (1351هـ) — the kingdom children celebrate on National Day.
3. **Flag Day** — Abdulaziz approved the flag's form — the third national day the lesson names, kept beside the other two.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what Founding Day remembers — Imam Muhammad bin Saud founding the Saudi state in 1727.
2. Say what National Day remembers — King Abdulaziz unifying the country in 1932.
3. Say what Flag Day remembers — Abdulaziz approving the flag's form.
4. Put 1727 and 1932 in order on a line and explain, in their own words, why one country keeps both days.
