# Saudi Arabia — Founding Day and National Day

## Do it first

Can a country have two birthdays? Saudi children celebrate two: one for the year their state was founded, and one for the year, more than two hundred years later, when it was united into one kingdom. Before you read on, decide: if your country had a founding day and a uniting day, which would feel older, deeper, more yours? Hold your answer; the two dates will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Founding Day, 1727

The Saudi Arabia Grade 4 book teaches the national days inside Unit 1, the Citizenship unit, in its Lesson 4 on national symbols — the days are taught as symbols a citizen carries, not only as dates.

Founding Day remembers the beginning: Imam Muhammad bin Saud founds the Saudi state in 1727 — 1139هـ in the Hijri count the book gives beside it. The book stresses the depth of it: roots more than 300 years deep. The state a Saudi child belongs to did not begin with the modern kingdom; it began in 1727.

[Picture slot — a picture of two flags flying over a Saudi town, one above an old mud-brick street dated 1727 and one above a modern city dated 1932 — the student points to each flag and says which day it stands for and what happened in that year.]

## Sub lesson 2 — National Day, 1932

National Day remembers the uniting: King Abdulaziz unifies the country in 1932 — 1351هـ.

Between the two days lie more than two hundred years. In 1727 a state is founded; in 1932 that inheritance is gathered into one united kingdom. Saudi children mark both days every year — the founding and the uniting are both theirs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Flag Day

Beside the two days, the lesson names a third: Flag Day, when Abdulaziz approved the flag's form.

A flag, a founding and a uniting — the lesson's three symbols say one thing three ways: this country has a beginning, a maker of its unity, and a sign under which both are remembered.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson as three beats: Founding Day remembers Imam Muhammad bin Saud founding the Saudi state in 1727; National Day remembers King Abdulaziz unifying the country in 1932; and Flag Day remembers Abdulaziz approving the flag's form. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory, the two years — 1727 and 1932 — and what each one remembers.
2. Write who founded the Saudi state, and who unified the country.
3. Write two sentences explaining why one country keeps both a Founding Day and a National Day.
