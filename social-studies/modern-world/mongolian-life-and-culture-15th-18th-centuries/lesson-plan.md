**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ch. I.6: Life and culture of the Mongols in the 15th–18th c.** — herding life goes on under the khans; Erdene Zuu rises on Karakorum's ruins, Zanabazar casts his Buddhas, and in 1662 Sagan Setsen writes the Erdeniin Tovch chronicle
2. **Ch. I.4: Life of the Mongols in the 18th–early 20th c.** — taxes, corvée and debt to Chinese firms grind the herders while the banner princes and monasteries hold the land and herds
3. **Ch. I.5: Mongolian culture in the 18th–early 20th c.** — chronicles, medicine, printing and temple art keep a national culture alive under foreign rule
4. **Ch. 4.3 Culture and economy (14th–17th c.)** — Buddhism's second coming, Altan Khan and the Dalai Lama title, Erdene Zuu's founding — faith remakes Mongol culture before the Qing remakes its politics
5. **Ch. I.4 Mongolian society and culture in the Manchu domination era** — khoshuu banners freeze herders to their lords' land; alba taxes, Chinese merchant debt and great monasteries reshape life, while learning, law codes and chronicles quietly flourish
