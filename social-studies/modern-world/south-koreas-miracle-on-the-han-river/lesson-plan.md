# Lesson plan — South Korea's miracle on the Han River

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** South Korea's miracle on the Han River
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Cold War splits Korea in 1945; the North invades in 1950; from the ashes — and from 80% illiteracy in 1945, after Japanese rule forbade Koreans their own Hangul letters — Seoul plans, borrows and builds for three decades into the "Miracle on the Han River"; Jordan's ties date to 1962, a Korean embassy opens in Amman in 1975, and King Abdullah II's 2015 visit seals more than twenty-four agreements
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
