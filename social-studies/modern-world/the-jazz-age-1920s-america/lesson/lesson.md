# The Jazz Age — 1920s America

## Do it first

Cars, radio and movies mass-produced a new way of life. A young generation flouted the old rules — and Prohibition, a revived Klan and the Scopes trial answered with backlash. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Prosperity mass-produces a new life

Prosperity mass-produced cars, radio and movies — the same songs, films and advertisements reaching a whole nation at once. The 1920s invented mass culture while Republican ascendancy governed the boom.

[Picture slot — a picture of the world of this lesson as the book shows it: point to the picture and say what changed in this part of the story.]

## Sub lesson 2 — A new generation flouts the old rules

A new generation flouted the old rules — in dress, music, dancing and manners. Jazz gave the age its name and its soundtrack: America was redefining itself, loudly.

## Sub lesson 3 — Backlash: Prohibition, the Klan, Scopes

The backlash answered: Prohibition banned drink and bred speakeasies and crime; the Klan revived, marching against the new America; and the Scopes trial put evolution and the old faith on trial together. Boom and backlash are the same decade.
