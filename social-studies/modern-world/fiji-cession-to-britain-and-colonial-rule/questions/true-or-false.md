# Fiji — cession to Britain and colonial rule — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Barter gives way to money, mission schools spread, Cakobau the Tui Viti cedes Fiji to Great Britain, and a colonial government at Levuka brings schools, hospitals, police and law.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Early iTaukei and Europeans / Fiji Today

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Year 5 Social Studies (title from contents/front matter; PDF opens at the Acknowledgement page)* is about Colonisation in the Pacific — New Zealand, Australia and the islands.
**Answer: False** — it is about Early iTaukei and Europeans / Fiji Today

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** US Consul Williams's looted stores and a US warship's demand leave Cakobau owing debts he cannot pay (US$38,531); Thurston carries his offer to London and on 10 October 1874 Cakobau, Ma'afu and senior chiefs sign Fiji over to Britain.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Year 6 Social Science Textbook (revised) (title from contents/front matter; PDF opens at the copyright page)* is about Colonisation in the Pacific — New Zealand, Australia and the islands.
**Answer: False** — it is about 2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Chaotic Fiji, a collapsing Cakobau government and thriving blackbirding force Gladstone's Britain to send Goodenough and Layard (1873); the commissioners go beyond their brief, win the chiefs round, and Sir Hercules Robinson takes an unconditional cession signed at Levuka on 10 October 1874, Cakobau pensioned as Vunivalu and Ma'afu recognised as Tui Lau.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Chapter 2: Significance of Cession; the Commission; the Deed of Cession

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Social Science Year 10 — Global Perspectives, Local Interaction* is about Colonisation in the Pacific — New Zealand, Australia and the islands.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** The transition from traditional chiefdom to democratic government runs through the 1800s and materialises in 1874, an interim government forming on 10 March 1874 on the road to cession.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Democratic government in Fiji: timeline from chiefdom to 1874
