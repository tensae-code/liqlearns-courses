# Fiji — cession to Britain and colonial rule — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Early iTaukei and Europeans / Fiji Today
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — barter gives way to money, mission schools spread, Cakobau the Tui Viti cedes Fiji to Great Britain, and a colonial government at Levuka brings schools, hospitals, police and law

## Sub lesson 2 — 2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — US Consul Williams's looted stores and a US warship's demand leave Cakobau owing debts he cannot pay (US$38,531); Thurston carries his offer to London and on 10 October 1874 Cakobau, Ma'afu and senior chiefs sign Fiji over to Britain

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 2: Significance of Cession; the Commission; the Deed of Cession
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — chaotic Fiji, a collapsing Cakobau government and thriving blackbirding force Gladstone's Britain to send Goodenough and Layard (1873); the commissioners go beyond their brief, win the chiefs round, and Sir Hercules Robinson takes an unconditional cession signed at Levuka on 10 October 1874, Cakobau pensioned as Vunivalu and Ma'afu recognised as Tui Lau

## Sub lesson 4 — Democratic government in Fiji: timeline from chiefdom to 1874
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the transition from traditional chiefdom to democratic government runs through the 1800s and materialises in 1874, an interim government forming on 10 March 1874 on the road to cession
