## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Early iTaukei and Europeans / Fiji Today** — barter gives way to money, mission schools spread, Cakobau the Tui Viti cedes Fiji to Great Britain, and a colonial government at Levuka brings schools, hospitals, police and law
2. **2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession** — US Consul Williams's looted stores and a US warship's demand leave Cakobau owing debts he cannot pay (US$38,531); Thurston carries his offer to London and on 10 October 1874 Cakobau, Ma'afu and senior chiefs sign Fiji over to Britain
3. **Chapter 2: Significance of Cession; the Commission; the Deed of Cession** — chaotic Fiji, a collapsing Cakobau government and thriving blackbirding force Gladstone's Britain to send Goodenough and Layard (1873); the commissioners go beyond their brief, win the chiefs round, and Sir Hercules Robinson takes an unconditional cession signed at Levuka on 10 October 1874, Cakobau pensioned as Vunivalu and Ma'afu recognised as Tui Lau
4. **Democratic government in Fiji: timeline from chiefdom to 1874** — the transition from traditional chiefdom to democratic government runs through the 1800s and materialises in 1874, an interim government forming on 10 March 1874 on the road to cession
