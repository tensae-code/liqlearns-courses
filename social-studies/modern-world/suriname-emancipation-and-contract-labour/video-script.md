# Video script — Suriname — emancipation and contract labour

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Suriname's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

After protests and the 1804 English ban era, slavery ends 1 July 1863 but the freed face ten years of state supervision; planters import contract labourers instead — Chinese in 1853, British Indians from 1873 (Lalla Rookh), Javanese from 1890 — whose descendants together form the Surinamese people.

Ketikoti, 1 July 1863: the book traces abolition of the trans-Atlantic trade, the effect of "Uncle Tom's Cabin" (1852) and the slave registers, to emancipation day itself.

Owners and traders resist abolition; the freed live under state supervision (staatstoezicht) with newspaper notices of release, and the Maroons' freedom is recognised too.

Following British Guiana's example, the Dutch open a Calcutta emigration office and recruit Indian contract workers, then Javanese — remembered by the Mariënburg monument to the first Javanese immigrants.
