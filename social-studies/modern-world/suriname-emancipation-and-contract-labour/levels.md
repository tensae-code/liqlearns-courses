# Suriname — emancipation and contract labour — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Emancipatie, Staatstoezicht en contractarbeid
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — After protests and the 1804 English ban era, slavery ends 1 July 1863 but the freed face ten years of state supervision; planters import contract labourers instead — Chinese in 1853, British Indians from 1873 (Lalla Rookh), Javanese from 1890 — whose descendants together form the Surinamese people.

## Sub lesson 2 — Afschaffing van de slavernij in ons land
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Ketikoti, 1 July 1863: the book traces abolition of the trans-Atlantic trade, the effect of "Uncle Tom's Cabin" (1852) and the slave registers, to emancipation day itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — Na de afschaffing van de slavernij
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Owners and traders resist abolition; the freed live under state supervision (staatstoezicht) with newspaper notices of release, and the Maroons' freedom is recognised too.

## Sub lesson 4 — De komst van Hindostaanse en Javaanse contractarbeiders
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Following British Guiana's example, the Dutch open a Calcutta emigration office and recruit Indian contract workers, then Javanese — remembered by the Mariënburg monument to the first Javanese immigrants.
