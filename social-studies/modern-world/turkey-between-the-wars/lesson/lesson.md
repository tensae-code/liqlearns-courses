# Turkey between the wars

## Do it first

'Peace at home, peace in the world' — the founder's motto is easy to say and hard to practise when every border carries an old quarrel: Mosul, the Straits, Hatay, the Aegean. Between the wars, Turkey practises it border by border, treaty by treaty — while at home the single party tries, twice, to allow an opposition, and fails. Before you read on, guess which is harder for a new state: peace abroad, or pluralism at home. Hold your guess; 1938 ends the founding age.

## Sub lesson 1 — Parties at home, peace abroad — to Atatürk's death, 1938

Turkey's Grade 12 book runs the interwar years on two tracks. At home: the early parties and the failed multi-party tries — the young republic twice attempts an opposition and twice draws back — shadowed by the 1926 assassination attempt against Atatürk. Abroad: a patient diplomacy settles the inheritance of empire — questions with Greece, France, and Britain over Mosul; friendship with the Soviets; entry into the League of Nations in 1932.

Then the pacts that arm peace with signatures: the Balkan Entente of 1934, the Montreux Convention of 1936 restoring the Straits, the Sadabad Pact of 1937 — and Hatay joining the homeland. The age closes on 10 November 1938, with Atatürk's death. The book's beat: 'peace at home, peace in the world' is practised border by border while the founder's chair empties.

In your mind's eye: ankara in the 1930s — the new capital's broad avenue, flags of a Balkan Entente conference flying. The student points to the flags and says what policy they stand for.
