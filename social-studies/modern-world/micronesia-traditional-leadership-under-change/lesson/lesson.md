# Micronesia — traditional leadership under change

## Do it first

A chief's son holds power — but not for the old reasons. Before you read on, decide: when the world around a tradition changes completely, does the tradition survive by staying the same, or by finding new roots? Hold your answer; Henry Nanpei will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Warfare ends, population collapses

The Grade 11 book's fifth chapter turns from the three leadership models to their fate. Foreign contact ended warfare between island groups — one old ground of chiefly power — and population collapse shook the communities the chiefs led. The old order's foundations were moving.

In your mind's eye: henry Nanpei standing between a chief's meetinghouse and a church, holding a land paper. The student points to the three things in the scene and says which gave him rank, and which gave him power.

## Sub lesson 2 — Churches and German land reforms move authority

Two new powers handed out authority of their own. The churches gave office — a man could rise as a church leader. And German land reforms re-granted land, the deepest source of standing in the islands. Authority now flowed from church and reform as well as from birth.

## Sub lesson 3 — Henry Nanpei: power from church and land

The book's example is Henry Nanpei of Pohnpei. His father was a Nahnken — high traditional rank. But Henry Nanpei's own power, the book teaches, came from church office and from land granted under the reforms — the new grounds of authority, held by a chiefly son.

That is the chapter's lesson: traditional leadership did not simply die under foreign rule. It survived by standing on new ground — which also meant it was no longer quite the old thing.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as these beats: say that warfare ended and population collapsed, shaking the old bases of chiefly authority; say that churches and German land reforms moved authority to new holders and new grounds; say that Henry Nanpei's power came from church office and reform-granted land, not only from his Nahnken father. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
