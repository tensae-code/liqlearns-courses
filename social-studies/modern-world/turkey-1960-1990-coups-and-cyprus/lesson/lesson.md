# Turkey 1960–1990 — coups and Cyprus

## Do it first

Three times in thirty years, soldiers step into Turkey's politics. Once, an island ninety kilometres off its southern coast pulls Ankara into its gravest foreign crisis, ending in 1974. And underneath the crises, the country itself is being remade — villages emptying into cities, markets opening to the world. Before you read on, guess which changes a nation more: its crises, or its quiet remaking. Hold your guess; the book weighs both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Foreign policy from the 1960s — Cyprus, Greece, the Armenian question

Turkey's Grade 12 book takes the republic's foreign policy from the 1960s as a story of standing questions. The Cyprus problem stands first: the island's fate pulls Ankara and Athens against each other until the crisis ends in the intervention of 1974. Relations with Greece carry the Aegean's quarrels alongside it, and the Armenian question presses from abroad.

The beat the book gives the foreign story is the island's: one island crisis that ends in 1974 defines the age abroad.

In your mind's eye: the changing Turkey of these decades — villagers with bundles boarding a bus for the city, new apartment blocks rising behind them. The student points to the bus and the blocks and says what quiet remaking is happening under the crises.

## Sub lesson 2 — Turkey 1960–1990 — the country remade

At home, the book's thirty years hold two stories at once. The political story is broken three times by military interventions — soldiers stepping into the republic's politics in 1960 and after. The deeper story is quieter: migration empties the villages into the cities, markets open, and the country's economy and culture are remade from below.

The book's full beat weighs both together: three military interventions, one island crisis that ends in 1974, and a country remade by migration and markets.
