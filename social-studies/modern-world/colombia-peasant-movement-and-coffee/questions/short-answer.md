# Colombia — the peasant movement and coffee — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Guía 3: The Colombian peasant movement.
**Answer: Peasant families work the coffee harvests together, organise, and are driven off the land; rural statistics and the question of whether their organisations still exist close the guide (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Colombia's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Ciencias Sociales — Primera Cartilla, Grado Quinto, Escuela Nueva* (Colombia), Guía 3: The Colombian peasant movement, printed p. 40.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Módulo 4 / Guía 10: Migrations and conflicts of our land.
**Answer: Colonos travel to baldío lands, peasants answer the agrarian conflict with land invasions and the first peasant leagues, and López Pumarejo's thought is set against Laureano Gómez's discourse (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Colombia's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Ciencias Sociales — Grado 8, Postprimaria Rural (Sociales)* (Colombia), Módulo 4 / Guía 10: Migrations and conflicts of our land, printed p. 137.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: Módulo 4 / Guías 10–12: Migrations and displacements in our territory / Peasant communities in defence of their territory.
**Answer: By the late 1800s and early 1900s western Colombia becomes the economic centre as colonists suffer the "illness of the cold lands" and tertian fevers; today peasant women and leaders demand territoriality and an end to forced displacement (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: Coffee production jumps 62% by 1933, the Federación de Cafeteros organises a fund and becomes decisive for industrial development itself (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
