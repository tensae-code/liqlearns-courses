# Micronesia — Spanish colonial rule

## Do it first

An empire claims your islands on paper for centuries — then one day its officials actually arrive to rule. Before you read on, decide: what changes on the day paper rule becomes real rule? Hold your answer; Pohnpei's three months will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Spain becomes first coloniser: Yap and Pohnpei

*Over Seas and Time* calls the chapter Initial Rule by the Spain. Spain had explored the Carolines first, centuries before; now it became the first coloniser, setting up administrations on Yap and Pohnpei.

Real rule was resisted at once: Pohnpeians attacked only three months in, and Spain fought to hold its new colony. Paper claims were old; governing was new, and it began in conflict.

[Picture slot — a picture of a Spanish administration building on Pohnpei with a flag above it, and far away a tiny atoll with a single distant flag — the student points to each and says how Spanish rule felt different in the two places.]

## Sub lesson 2 — Quarrels with the missionaries: Reverend Doane

Spain was Catholic; the missions already on Pohnpei were American and Protestant. The colonial government quarrelled with the missionaries — Reverend Doane among them — over whose authority the islanders should obey. Two foreign powers, church and state, fought their quarrel on island ground.

## Sub lesson 3 — Kapingamarangi's Spanish era, to 1899

The Kapingamarangi reader gives the Spanish era its own dated section: The Time of the Spanish, to 1899. The atoll lived its Spanish era at the edge of an empire that ruled the Carolines from far away — Spanish rule there was distant and thin.

It ended not with a battle but a sale: in 1899 Spain sold the islands, and Kapingamarangi's Spanish time closed with a signature in Europe.
