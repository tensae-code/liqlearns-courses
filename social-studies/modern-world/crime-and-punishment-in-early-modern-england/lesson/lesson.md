# Crime and punishment in early modern England

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Between 1500 and 1700, new crimes are named, towns outgrow old law enforcement, and punishments grow harsher. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — 2.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1500–c1700

After Luther's 1517 protest splits Europe's religion, heresy, treason, vagabondage and witchcraft are newly criminalised and 1650s laws ban traditional entertainments.

## Sub lesson 2 — 2.2 Law enforcement and punishment, c1500–c1700

Growing towns outgrow the hue and cry, so town constables and the night watch appear, the death penalty spreads and transportation is introduced.

## Sub lesson 3 — 2.3 Case study: the crimes and punishment of the Gunpowder Plotters, 1605

Guy Fawkes and fellow Catholic conspirators plot to blow up James I and Parliament on 5 November 1605; caught, they are executed with exemplary harshness as a warning.

## Sub lesson 4 — 2.4 Witchcraft and the law, c1500–c1700

A whole society believes witches pact with the devil; in the 1640s Matthew Hopkins hunts them, then belief fades and witchcraft stops being prosecuted as a serious crime by c1700.
