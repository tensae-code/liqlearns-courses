## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **2.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1500–c1700** — After Luther's 1517 protest splits Europe's religion, heresy, treason, vagabondage and witchcraft are newly criminalised and 1650s laws ban traditional entertainments
2. **2.2 Law enforcement and punishment, c1500–c1700** — Growing towns outgrow the hue and cry, so town constables and the night watch appear, the death penalty spreads and transportation is introduced
3. **2.3 Case study: the crimes and punishment of the Gunpowder Plotters, 1605** — Guy Fawkes and fellow Catholic conspirators plot to blow up James I and Parliament on 5 November 1605; caught, they are executed with exemplary harshness as a warning
4. **2.4 Witchcraft and the law, c1500–c1700** — A whole society believes witches pact with the devil; in the 1640s Matthew Hopkins hunts them, then belief fades and witchcraft stops being prosecuted as a serious crime by c1700
