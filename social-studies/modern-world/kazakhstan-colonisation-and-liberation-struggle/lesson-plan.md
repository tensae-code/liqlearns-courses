# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan Colonisation And Liberation Struggle

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan Colonisation And Liberation Struggle
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** land seizure and administrative control are named as colonial aims
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§§19–20 The Russian Empire's colonial policy: goals and methods** — land seizure and administrative control are named as colonial aims
2. **§§21–22 The national-liberation movement led by Syrym Datov** — the Junior Zhuz rises under Syrym against khan and empire alike
3. **§23 The reforms of 1822–1824 and Kazakh society** — Speransky's and the Orenburg charters abolish khan rule in two zhuzes
4. **§24 The Kazakh people's national-liberation movement in the first half of the 19th century** — Zholaman and Isatai keep armed resistance alive
5. **§§25–26 The uprising of the Bukey Horde Kazakhs, 1836–1838** — Isatai and Makhambet lead the Inner Horde's revolt
6. **§§27–28 The national-liberation movement led by Kenesary Kasymov** — Kenesary restores the khanate and wars on two empires for a decade
7. **§§29–30 The Kazakhs' uprising against the Central Asian khanates' invasions** — steppe towns resist Kokand's and Khiva's tax collectors
8. **§§31–32 The joining of Kazakhstan's southern regions to the Russian Empire** — fort by fort, the south is annexed by the 1860s
9. **§§17–18 Kazakhstan's colonisation begins** — administrative control replaces alliance as empire advances
10. **§§19–20 Syrym Datuly's national-liberation uprising (1783–1797)** — the Junior Zhuz wars on puppet khans and border forts
11. **§§21–22 The tsarist reforms of 1822–1824** — charters dissolve khan power in the Middle and Junior zhuzes
12. **§§23–24 The uprising in the Bukey Horde (1836–1838)** — Isatai Taimanuly and Makhambet rise against Jangir Khan
13. **§§25–26 Kenesary Kasymuly's national-liberation uprising (1837–1847)** — the last khan restores the state and falls fighting in the Kyrgyz mountains
14. **§§27–28 The Kazakhs in the 1840s–60s; relations with the Central Asian khanates** — Kokand's forts tax the southern auls until revolts spread
15. **§§29–30 The completion of Kazakhstan's joining of the Russian Empire** — the Great Horde and the south are absorbed by 1864
