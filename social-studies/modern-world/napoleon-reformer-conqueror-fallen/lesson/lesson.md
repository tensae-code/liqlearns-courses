# Napoleon — reformer, conqueror, fallen

## Do it first

He was a young artillery officer from a poor island family. By thirty he ruled France; by forty he had marched farther than Alexander's fame in Europe's memory — and he wrote laws that outlived his empire. Reformer or conqueror? This lesson holds both, because he was both.

## Sub lesson 1 — The road up — Italy, Egypt, power (teacher guide — evidence)

His name was made in Italy. In 1796 the young general Napoleon Bonaparte defeated Austria in Italy, and France learned to say his name the way it had once said kings'. A short-lived conquest of Egypt followed in 1798–1801 — glory and science marching together, even where the campaign itself could not hold. When he returned, the revolutionary republic was tired, corrupt and governable by whoever dared. He dared, and took power.

As ruler, the guide records, he made employment and promotion in government depend on ability, not birth — the revolution's promise kept in the civil service. And he expanded education, opening state secondary schools and universities: a ladder of merit, built in classrooms, by the soldier who had climbed by talent himself.

## Sub lesson 2 — The builder — tax, bank, schools, the Code

The France Napoleon governed was rebuilt piece by piece. The tax system was made regular and collectable, so the state could plan instead of lurching from crisis to crisis. A national bank steadied the currency and credit. Schools and universities under the state trained the administrators and officers the new France needed — the guide's merit ladder, given bricks and timetables.

Above all stands the Code: the Napoleonic Code gathered the revolution's legal gains — equality before the law, property secured, careers open — into one clear book of law for the whole country. Empires of territory fell with him; the Code marched on without him, and its descendants still govern daily life far beyond France.

## Sub lesson 3 — The conqueror falls; Vienna restores; the verdict (teacher guide — evidence)

The same energy that rebuilt France redrew Europe. Napoleon's armies carried the Code and the revolution's forms across the continent, toppling old thrones and merging petty states into fewer, larger ones. The map swelled to its height — then overreach, endless war and united enemies shrank it, campaign by campaign, until the fall came and exile closed the career that Italy had opened.

The victors met at the Congress of Vienna and restored, as far as they could, the order and borders of before 1790. But they could not restore the world before the ideas. The Grade 11 teacher guide's answer key gives the build's verdict, and the Grade 9 guide's effects list supports it: Napoleon was, in the guide's words, an enlightened despot — his reforms answered the Enlightenment's questions even as his rule denied its freedoms; and the revolution he closed had already lessened religion's old importance, popularised modern nationalism, spread liberalism, and ignited an Age of Revolutions no congress could put out.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion both Napoleons: the builder with his four reforms, and the conqueror whose map swelled and shrank. End with the guides' verdict — an enlightened despot — and one sentence saying what it means.
