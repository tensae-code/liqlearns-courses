# Lesson plan — The Hijaz Railway and Jordan's train story

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Hijaz Railway and Jordan's train story
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Faisal visits Amman's Hijaz railway station with his family and his grandfather tells how the old train once carried goods and travellers and now runs tourist trips through Jordan's stations
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 4 Lesson 2: development of trade in my homeland, opening story** — Faisal visits Amman's Hijaz railway station with his family and his grandfather tells how the old train once carried goods and travellers and now runs tourist trips through Jordan's stations
2. **Unit 2 Lesson 5: Jordan under Ottoman rule** — East Jordan stays in Damascus's orbit from first to last; Abdülhamid II drives the Hijaz Railway from Damascus toward Madinah across Jordanian stations, and the Shami Hajj road makes the land an economic lifeline
3. **Unit 3 Lesson 2, the railway part** — the class traces the Hijaz Railway on the map through Jordan's stations — Damascus, Daraa, Mafraq, Zarqa, Amman, Qatrana, Ma'an, Batn al-Ghul, Aqaba's branch, al-Mudawara, Tabuk, Mada'in Salih, al-Ula, Medina — the iron road that replaced the forty-day Hajj caravan
4. **Unit 3 Lesson 2, the railway part** — the Hijaz Railway map is traced through Jordan as in the vocational book — Qatrana, Ma'an, Batn al-Ghul and the Aqaba branch among its stations — the line along which the Circassian and Chechen villages of the previous lesson were planted
