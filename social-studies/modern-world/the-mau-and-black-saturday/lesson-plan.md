# Lesson plan — The Mau and Black Saturday

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Mau and Black Saturday
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Samoa, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Talune brings Spanish influenza on 7 November 1918 and a fifth to a quarter of Western Samoa dies; grievances over interference with the fa'a Samoa, taxes and exclusion from government form the Mau in 1926; on Black Saturday, 28 December 1929, police fire on an unarmed Apia march, killing eleven including Tupua Tamasese, whose dying words plead for peace; Samoan delegations then press the UN until independence
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2, Samoa history timeline and "The Mau Movement, 1926"** — the Talune brings Spanish influenza on 7 November 1918 and a fifth to a quarter of Western Samoa dies; grievances over interference with the fa'a Samoa, taxes and exclusion from government form the Mau in 1926; on Black Saturday, 28 December 1929, police fire on an unarmed Apia march, killing eleven including Tupua Tamasese, whose dying words plead for peace; Samoan delegations then press the UN until independence
2. **Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's diary" (diary story, plot summary only)** — a Vaimoso-area mother watches her husband join the secret Mau meetings of 1926, agonises over the tax, registration and school boycott, sews purple uniforms for the Women's Mau led by the wives of Tamasese, Tuimaliifano, Nelson and Faumuina, marches with a thousand women — and buries her husband after police machine-gun the unarmed Beach Road march on Black Saturday, the day Tamasese is buried at Lepea
3. **Part 2, Unit 4, second half** — New Zealand seizes Sāmoa unresisted on 31 August 1914; Colonel Logan's rule, the 1918 influenza disaster and paternalist racism build the Mau under Olaf Nelson, A. G. Smythe and Tupua Tamasese Lealofi III; leaders are deported, and on Black Saturday (dated 28 December 1928 in this unit, 1929 elsewhere in the book) police kill eleven marchers including Tamasese — whereupon the Women's Mau takes over the struggle
