# The Mau and Black Saturday — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** the Talune brings Spanish influenza on 7 November 1918 and a fifth to a quarter of Western Samoa dies; grievances over interference with the fa'a Samoa, taxes and exclusion from government form the Mau in 1926; on Black Saturday, 28 December 1929, police fire on an unarmed Apia march, killing eleven including Tupua Tamasese, whose dying words plead for peace; Samoan delegations then press the UN until independence.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 2, Samoa history timeline and "The Mau Movement, 1926"

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Social Studies, Year 10 Book One* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 2, Samoa history timeline and "The Mau Movement, 1926"

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** a Vaimoso-area mother watches her husband join the secret Mau meetings of 1926, agonises over the tax, registration and school boycott, sews purple uniforms for the Women's Mau led by the wives of Tamasese, Tuimaliifano, Nelson and Faumuina, marches with a thousand women — and buries her husband after police machine-gun the unarmed Beach Road march on Black Saturday, the day Tamasese is buried at Lepea.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's diary" (diary story, plot summary only)

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Social Studies, Year 11 Book One* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's diary" (diary story, plot summary only)

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** New Zealand seizes Sāmoa unresisted on 31 August 1914; Colonel Logan's rule, the 1918 influenza disaster and paternalist racism build the Mau under Olaf Nelson, A. G. Smythe and Tupua Tamasese Lealofi III; leaders are deported, and on Black Saturday (dated 28 December 1928 in this unit, 1929 elsewhere in the book) police kill eleven marchers including Tamasese — whereupon the Women's Mau takes over the struggle.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Part 2, Unit 4, second half

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *History, Year 12* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Part 2, Unit 4, second half
