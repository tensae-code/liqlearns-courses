# Lesson plan — Switzerland — Uschi Waser and the Yenish Children of the Road

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Switzerland — Uschi Waser and the Yenish Children of the Road
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Switzerland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ursula Waser, a Yenish baby, was taken by police at seven months old for a scheme that meant to erase her people's travelling life by taking its children; she was moved 22 times in 13 years, and the files written about her decided even the court case she brought as a girl.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Taken at seven months** — Baby Ursula — Uschi — is a Yenish child. At seven months the police take her for Pro Juventute's "Children of the Road" scheme. In 13 years she is moved 22 times. When she accuses her abusive stepfather, the court does not believe her. Only as an adult does she read the files that betrayed her.
2. **The scheme that took 586 children** — The companion names the machine behind her fate: Alfred Siegfried's scheme, running 1926–1973, takes 586 Yenish children — 294 of them from Graubünden — to erase a travelling culture. Parents appeal in vain. Journalist Hans Caprez's 1972 articles force the scheme to close, and in 1986/87 the federal president and Pro Juventute apologise. (Teacher guide — evidence.)
3. **The scheme in its own words** — At the upper level pupils meet the scheme's own paper: Siegfried writes in 1943 that the travelling people's family bond must be torn apart, and his office files Ursula's whole childhood on green index cards. Pupils weigh that record against the Nazi persecution of the Sinti and Roma and against the charge of cultural genocide. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell Uschi Waser's story: taken at seven months, moved 22 times in 13 years, not believed in court, reading her files as an adult.
2. Say what the Children of the Road scheme was for, how many children it took, and what finally closed it.
3. Say what Siegfried wrote in 1943, and what charge pupils must weigh the scheme against.
