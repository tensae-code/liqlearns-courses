# Bengal — Sultanate, Mughals and the Baro Bhuiyans

## Do it first

An emperor's army can win a battle and still not win a river land. Bengal's zamindars fought the Mughals from boats and flooded fields for years after their sultan fell. Before you read on, guess why rivers make a land hard to conquer. Hold your guess; Isa Khan is coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sultanate and Mughal Bengal — Ilyas Shah, Akbar and the Baro Bhuiyans

The Grade 9 book's middle chapters give Bengal two opposite fortunes. First, independence: Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah founds an independent Bengal Sultanate and takes the title 'Shah-i Bangalah' — King of Bengal. Centuries later comes conquest: Akbar's armies kill Daud Karrani in 1576, and Bengal falls under the Mughals. But the story refuses to end there: the Baro Bhuiyan zamindars, under Isa Khan, keep resisting the Mughal subadars river by river. A sultanate founded, an empire resisted — Bengal's pattern of ruling itself, then fighting to do so again.
