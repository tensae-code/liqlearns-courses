# Germany unifies — Bismarck, Blood and Iron, Versailles 1871

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Blood and Iron — Prussia clears the road

Germany's road ran through one strong state: **Prussia**. Its rival for leadership of the Germans was **Austria**, and Prussia's chancellor, **Bismarck**, announced the method in advance — **'Blood and Iron'**: unification would be won by armies, not speeches. The ground had been prepared without a shot: the **Zollverein**, a customs union, had already tied the German states together economically, so trade flowed across borders that armies would soon erase.

Then came Prussia's **three wars** — the book's exercise fixes **Sadowa** among them, the victory that shut Austria out of German affairs. The last war turned on France. Each war removed one obstacle, until no power stood between Prussia and a German empire.

## Sub lesson 2 — Versailles 1871 — an empire on French soil

The ending was staged to be remembered. By the **Treaty of Frankfurt, 10 May 1871**, defeated France **ceded coal- and iron-rich Alsace and Lorraine** and paid **huge indemnities**. And in **1871**, in the **Palace of Versailles** — the palace of France's own kings — the **German Empire was proclaimed**, with **King William of Prussia as Kaiser**. Unification was announced, pointedly, **on French soil**: Germany arrived among the great powers with a message no one in Paris would forget.
