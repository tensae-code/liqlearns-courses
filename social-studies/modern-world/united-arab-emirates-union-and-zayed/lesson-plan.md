# Lesson plan — United Arab Emirates — the union and Sheikh Zayed

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** United Arab Emirates — the union and Sheikh Zayed
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (United Arab Emirates, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The United Arab Emirates is a young country made of seven emirates united under its founding president, Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan; a Grade 1 child meets that story through a family visit to the Etihad Museum in Dubai — the house of the union — through the map of the seven emirates and the country's landmarks old and new, through Sheikh Zayed's sayings under the heading "Zayed taught me", and through the story of how schooling itself began in the Emirates, from the Mutawa'a's circle to the first regular school, Al Qasimiyah School in Sharjah, and modern formal education from 1953.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own country feeling: a country younger than the children's grandparents, introduced through the museum that keeps its birth certificate and the words of the man who united it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Emirates is my country** — a family visits the Etihad Museum in Dubai, the house of the union, and the child meets the map of the seven emirates that together make one country.
2. **Landmarks old and new, and "Zayed taught me"** — Louvre Abu Dhabi, Burj Khalifa, Al Maqta Fort in Umm Al Quwain and Ajman Museum stand side by side, and through the whole book Sheikh Zayed's sayings teach the child, under the heading "Zayed taught me".
3. **How schooling began in the Emirates** — teaching first rested on the Mutawa'a, who taught children Qur'an, reading, writing and arithmetic; the first regular school was Al Qasimiyah School in Sharjah; modern formal education began in 1953.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nationalism — the feeling that made nation states. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the Etihad Museum in Dubai is — the house of the union — and why a family visit there opens the country's story.
2. Say that the United Arab Emirates is made of seven emirates, shown together on one map.
3. Name landmarks old and new from the book: Louvre Abu Dhabi, Burj Khalifa, Al Maqta Fort and Ajman Museum.
4. Say who Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan was — the founding president who united the country — and what "Zayed taught me" is in the book.
5. Tell how schooling began: the Mutawa'a, Al Qasimiyah School in Sharjah as the first regular school, and modern formal education from 1953.
