# Video script — United Arab Emirates — the union and Sheikh Zayed

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: a family visits the Etihad Museum in Dubai, as the Grade 1 book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A family walks into a museum in Dubai. This is not a museum of very old things. This is the Etihad Museum — the house of the union, the place that keeps the story of how this country began.

Inside, the child sees a map. Seven emirates, side by side — and together, one country: the United Arab Emirates. The country is younger than the child's grandparents, and its story starts here.

Then the landmarks rise, old and new together: the tall Burj Khalifa, the Louvre Abu Dhabi, and the old stones of Al Maqta Fort and Ajman Museum. A young country, holding its old places and its new ones in the same hand.

And everywhere in the book, one man's words guide the child, under the words "Zayed taught me": Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the founding president, the man who united the seven emirates.

Last, the story turns to school itself. Long ago there were no school buildings: one teacher, the Mutawa'a, taught the children Qur'an, reading, writing and arithmetic. Then came the first regular school — Al Qasimiyah School in Sharjah — and in 1953, modern schooling began. Within one lifetime, the country went from one teacher's circle to schools for every child.
