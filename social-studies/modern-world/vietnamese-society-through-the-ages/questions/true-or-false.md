# Vietnamese Society Through The Ages — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** in Văn Lang–Âu Lạc society already divides into king, lạc hầu and lạc tướng aristocracy, commoners and a few slaves, organised in village communities (làng, chiềng, chạ) that remain the base unit through the Chinese-rule centuries, when a Han landlord-official class confronts the Vietnamese while the villages keep wide autonomy; the independent monarchies raise cities — Thăng Long the capital, the ports Vân Đồn, Phố Hiến, Thanh Hà, Hội An — where a "market" quarter grows beside the citadel, officials are chosen by examination beside the hereditary aristocracy, and residents fall into the four groups: scholars, farmers, artisans, merchants; colonial rule breaks the frame — great landlords and landless tenant farmers in Nam Kì, French officials, workers, bourgeoisie and petty bourgeoisie appear, and most Vietnamese are pushed down to "slaves who have lost their country"; the modern ages follow — the working people's new society of 1945–54, the divided decades, the unified subsidy years sliding into crisis, and Đổi mới's diversified society of entrepreneurs and foreign residents.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Chuyên đề 1, II.3, Vietnamese society from Văn Lang to Đổi mới
