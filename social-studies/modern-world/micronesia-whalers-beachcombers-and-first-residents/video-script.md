# Video script — Micronesia — whalers, beachcombers and first residents

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as Micronesia's books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The chapter is called Early Guests Who Stayed. Whaling ships and traders hunting beche-de-mer — sea cucumber for the China market — crowded into the eastern Carolines. The islands now had regular foreign visitors with goods, guns and demands, long before any foreign flag ruled them.

Some guests never left. Deserting sailors became beachcombers: foreigners who lived on the islands under the chiefs, marrying in, translating, trading — and sometimes stirring trouble. They were the first resident foreigners, and they lived by island law because there was no other.

The meetings could end in blood: the crews of two ships, the Waverly and the Harriet, were massacred. And a quieter killer came with the ships — disease. The pox cut island populations, weakening communities before any colonial government arrived. The foreign age began with trade, violence and sickness together.
