# Micronesia — whalers, beachcombers and first residents

## Do it first

The first foreigners who stay are not governors or priests — they are sailors who jump ship and will not leave. Before you read on, decide: under whose law does such a man live, and what does his staying do to the island? Hold your answer; the beachcombers' story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Whalers and beche-de-mer traders crowd the eastern Carolines

The chapter is called Early Guests Who Stayed. Whaling ships and traders hunting beche-de-mer — sea cucumber for the China market — crowded into the eastern Carolines. The islands now had regular foreign visitors with goods, guns and demands, long before any foreign flag ruled them.

[Picture slot — a picture of a whaling ship anchored off an eastern Caroline island, with traders on the beach and a lone sailor walking inland toward a chief's house — the student points to the sailor and says who he is and under whose law he will live.]

## Sub lesson 2 — Beachcombers: deserters living under chiefs

Some guests never left. Deserting sailors became beachcombers: foreigners who lived on the islands under the chiefs, marrying in, translating, trading — and sometimes stirring trouble. They were the first resident foreigners, and they lived by island law because there was no other.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Waverly and Harriet massacres — and the pox

The meetings could end in blood: the crews of two ships, the Waverly and the Harriet, were massacred. And a quieter killer came with the ships — disease. The pox cut island populations, weakening communities before any colonial government arrived. The foreign age began with trade, violence and sickness together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as these beats: say that whalers and beche-de-mer traders crowded the eastern Carolines; say that deserting sailors became beachcombers living under chiefs; say that the crews of the Waverly and Harriet were massacred, and that disease — the pox — cut island populations. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
