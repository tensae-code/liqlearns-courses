# Sāmoa — from German rule to independence, 1962 — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Britain asks New Zealand to seize German Samoa at the start of the First World War; troops land unresisted at Apia in August 1914; Samoa passes through League of Nations mandate (1919) and UN trusteeship (1945), with New Zealand charged to prepare it for self-government, until in 1962 Samoa becomes the first Pacific island country to win independence.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 1, Topic 1 "Government Development And Organisation"

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Social Studies, Year 10 Book One* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 1, Topic 1 "Government Development And Organisation"

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** learners return to the Year 10 Book 1 history timeline (printed pp. 46–47 of that book) and choose which events on it — takeover, influenza, Mau, independence — did the work of forming Samoa's national identity.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 1, Activity 6 "Samoa's National Identity"

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Social Studies, Year 10 Book Two* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 1, Activity 6 "Samoa's National Identity"

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** when the First World War begins, New Zealand takes Samoa from Germany and tears German men from their Samoan wives and families, imprisoning them until the war ends.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 1, Topic 2 key-words box, "Discrimination" example

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Social Studies, Year 11 Book One* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 1, Topic 2 key-words box, "Discrimination" example
