# Video script — Micronesia — the road to self-government

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as Micronesia's books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The Grade 11 book opens the independence story inside the meetinghouse. In 1965, Congress of Micronesia member Andon Amaraich told the octopus story: independence was killed and scattered like the octopus's arms — but its blood waited in the rafters, ready to come back together.

It is self-rule explained in island words: what foreign rule had cut apart was not dead, only waiting. The story gave the Congress's work a meaning every islander could hold.

*Over Seas and Time* follows the negotiations: the Congress of Micronesia led the status talks with the United States, arguing the islands' future face to face with the administering power.

Unity did not hold to the end: the Marshalls and Palau chose to go their own way as separate nations. Four districts stayed together and approved a constitution creating a federation — the Federated States of Micronesia.

The government book tells the fight's length: the young leaders of the Congress of Micronesia spent years pressing the United States — and, when America stalled, appealing over its head to the United Nations — until the Trust Territory won independence.

It closes with short lives of the leaders who carried the fight: the men the new nation names its founding fathers. A country's road to self-government, in this book, is walked by named people, not by forces.
