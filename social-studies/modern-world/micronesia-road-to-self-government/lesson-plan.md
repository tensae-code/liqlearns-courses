# Lesson plan — Micronesia — the road to self-government

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Micronesia — the road to self-government
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Micronesia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Congress of Micronesia led status negotiations with the United States — its member Andon Amaraich telling the octopus story of an independence scattered but waiting to come back together — the young leaders pressed America for years and appealed to the United Nations when America stalled, the Marshalls and Palau chose to go their own way, and four districts approved a constitution creating a federation, the Federated States of Micronesia, carried by the men the nation names its founding fathers.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told in order, part by part, as Micronesia's books tell it. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The octopus in the meetinghouse** — say that in 1965 Congress of Micronesia member Andon Amaraich told the octopus story — independence killed and scattered like the octopus's arms, its blood waiting in the rafters to come back together — to explain self-rule in island words.
2. **Negotiations — and the Marshalls and Palau go their own way** — say that the Congress of Micronesia led status negotiations with the United States, and the Marshalls and Palau chose to go their own way, while four districts approved a constitution creating the FSM federation.
3. **Pressing America, appealing to the UN — and the founding fathers** — say that the Congress's young leaders spent years pressing the United States and appealing to the United Nations when America stalled, until the Trust Territory won independence, and that the book honours the leaders as the FSM's founding fathers.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
