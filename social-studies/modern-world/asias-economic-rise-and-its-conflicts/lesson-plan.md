# Lesson plan — Asia's economic rise — and its conflicts

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Asia's economic rise — and its conflicts
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Papua New Guinea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Since the 1960s Asia grows richer faster than any region — East Asian extreme poverty falls from 56% to 12% — yet subnational conflicts multiply against the theory that prosperity brings peace; China's turn from export industrialisation remakes its economy and its environment, and millions migrate for work
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Economic systems in Asia / Impact of economic decisions on the people and environment / Economic influence on movement of people / Economic influence on conflicts in Asia** — since the 1960s Asia grows richer faster than any region — East Asian extreme poverty falls from 56% to 12% — yet subnational conflicts multiply against the theory that prosperity brings peace; China's turn from export industrialisation remakes its economy and its environment, and millions migrate for work
2. **Asia's development: past and present / Regional and global development influences** — the four Asian Tigers industrialise between the 1960s and 1990s; China and India's rise shifts world power from West to East and raises the fear of a clash with US dominance
3. **Governments and change in Asia and the world / Impact of different governments' actions / Government reactions and contributions to change / Sustainability or change policies and plans of Asian governments (incl. investigating China's Belt and Road)** — Asian governments bet on western capitalism and win — agricultural exporters become industrial powers in twenty years; Malaysia and Singapore steer with industrial policy, Deng Xiaoping blends Marx with markets as "capitalism with Chinese characteristics", and a 56%-middle-class Asia plans its next stage
4. **Unit 12.2 global-issues essay task** — students collect media evidence and write on the influence of China or India in the 21st century — the region's new giants weighed from PNG's side of the fence

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
