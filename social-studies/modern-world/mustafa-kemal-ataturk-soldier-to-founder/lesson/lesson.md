# Mustafa Kemal Atatürk — soldier to founder

## Do it first

Before he is the founder on the banknotes, he is a schoolboy: a teacher gives him his second name, Kemal, for his excellence; military schools shape him; postings carry him from Damascus to Sofia. Turkey's final-year book opens the republic's whole story with one man's schooling. Before you read on, decide why a nation would start its history with a boy's education. Hold your answer; the book's way is deliberate.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mustafa Kemal's education and military life

Turkey's Grade 12 İnkılap Tarihi opens the republic's biography with a schoolboy. Mustafa Kemal's education runs from the Şemsi Efendi school through the military schools of Salonica and Monastir, to the War College and the Academy in Istanbul — a ladder of classrooms, each one forming the officer.

Then come the postings that season him: service from Damascus to Sofia, an empire's worth of frontiers and peoples passing under one officer's eyes. The book's beat is its order of telling: the republic's founder is introduced the Turkish way, as a schoolboy formed by teachers before he is a general — the classrooms come first, because the book wants its readers to see that the founder was made, not born.

In your mind's eye: the young Mustafa Kemal as a military-school cadet in Salonica, books under his arm, the school gate behind him. The student points to the books and says what formed the founder before any battle did.
