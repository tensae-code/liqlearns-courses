# America in the twenty-first century

## Do it first

The survey that began before 1492 ends in our own century: terror attacks and two wars, a financial crisis, old disputes that never closed — and an election built on hope and change. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — 9/11 and the war on terror

9/11 launched the war on terror and wars in Afghanistan and Iraq. The new century opened for America as the postwar one had — with war — but this time the attack had come at home, on a single morning.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — The financial crisis

At home the financial crisis shook the prosperity the century began with — banks, houses and jobs falling together, and the government rescuing the system that had fallen. The New Deal's old question returned: what is Washington for in a collapse?

## Sub lesson 3 — Old disputes — and the hope-and-change election of Obama

Old disputes over race and rights ran through the century's first decade unresolved. The survey closes with the hope-and-change election of Barack Obama — the first African American president, elected on a promise that the old story could turn.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats: 9/11 and the war on terror; The financial crisis; Old disputes — and the hope-and-change election of Obama. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
