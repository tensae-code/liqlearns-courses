# Lesson plan — Nepal — unification and the Anglo-Nepalese War heroes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Nepal — unification and the Anglo-Nepalese War heroes
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Nepal, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bal Bhadra Kunwar holds Nalapani with women and children fighting beside him, Amar Singh Thapa guards the western forts, 70-year-old Bhakti Thapa dies defending Malaun, and Queen Rajendra Laxmi rules eight years annexing small states to complete Prithvi Narayan Shah's unification
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Our Heroes and Heroines (Grade 3)** — Bal Bhadra Kunwar holds Nalapani with women and children fighting beside him, Amar Singh Thapa guards the western forts, 70-year-old Bhakti Thapa dies defending Malaun, and Queen Rajendra Laxmi rules…
2. **Our National Heroes — Prithvi Narayan, Balabhadra and Amar Singh (Grade 5)** — in a dream-dialogue Prithvi Narayan explains his raised finger — all Nepalis as one — Balabhadra tells of holding Nalapani on little food and water without surrender, and Amar Singh refuses British…
3. **The Unification of Nepal — People's Participation (Grade 6)** — a Grade 6 class research report shows unification was not the king alone — astrologer Kulananda Dhakal's counsel times the Nuwakot attack, commander Kalu Pande falls at Kirtipur, and ordinary citizen…
4. **Rajendra Laxmi and Bahadur Shah in the Unification (Grade 6)** — after Prithvi Narayan's death his widowed daughter-in-law Rajendra Laxmi rules as regent and pushes the armies west, quarrels with her brother-in-law Bahadur Shah, has him exiled, and on her death in…
5. **The Nepal–British War (Grade 6)** — the East India Company's trade ambition turns to war in November 1814; Balbhadra at Nalapani, Bhakti Thapa at Deuthal and Ranveer Singh at Jitgadhi win fame even in defeat, Colonel Ochterlony breaks…
6. **National Luminary Prithvi Narayan Shah (Grade 7)** — born at Gorkha on 27 Poush 1779 BS, king at 20, he vows to weld the scattered states into one Nepal, lays unification's foundation by 1826 BS, and his deathbed Divya Upadesh still teaches: Nepal is a…
7. **Our National Luminaries (later figures) (Grade 9)** — the gallery continues with Ram Shah of Gorkha and his famed justice reforms, Prithvi Narayan Shah the unifier, Bhimsen Thapa the first prime minister, the war heroes Amar Singh Thapa and Balbhadra…
8. **The Unification of Nepal — Causes and Effects (Grade 9)** — the campaign is told fort by fort — Nuwakot won, lost and retaken; Kirtipur taken at the third assault after Kalu Pande falls; Makwanpur; the blockade of the valley; Kathmandu seized during…
9. **The Contribution of the Heroes in the Nepal–British War (Grade 9)** — the war's heroes are honoured one by one — Amar Singh Thapa in the west, Balbhadra Kunwar at Nalapani, Bhakti Thapa at Malaun — the commanders whose stands made the British respect Nepali arms even…
10. **The Modern Period (Grade 12)** — the modern age opens with Prithvi Narayan Shah's unification from 1825 BS — the valley conquered, the kingdom expanded east and west, and the Anglo-Nepalese war testing the new state
