# Video script — Nepal — unification and the Anglo-Nepalese War heroes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Nepal's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bal Bhadra Kunwar holds Nalapani with women and children fighting beside him, Amar Singh Thapa guards the western forts, 70-year-old Bhakti Thapa dies defending Malaun, and Queen Rajendra Laxmi rules eight years annexing small states to complete Prithvi Narayan Shah's unification

---

in a dream-dialogue Prithvi Narayan explains his raised finger — all Nepalis as one — Balabhadra tells of holding Nalapani on little food and water without surrender, and Amar Singh refuses British gold and rank as a traitor's price

---

a Grade 6 class research report shows unification was not the king alone — astrologer Kulananda Dhakal's counsel times the Nuwakot attack, commander Kalu Pande falls at Kirtipur, and ordinary citizen Bise Nagarchi raises the war fund from 12,000 Gorkha households while youths of 16–30 march

---

after Prithvi Narayan's death his widowed daughter-in-law Rajendra Laxmi rules as regent and pushes the armies west, quarrels with her brother-in-law Bahadur Shah, has him exiled, and on her death in 1842 BS Bahadur Shah returns as regent and carries unification to the Mahakali and beyond

---

the East India Company's trade ambition turns to war in November 1814; Balbhadra at Nalapani, Bhakti Thapa at Deuthal and Ranveer Singh at Jitgadhi win fame even in defeat, Colonel Ochterlony breaks the west, and the Sugauli Treaty (March 1816) ends the war with Nepal losing a third of its land

---

born at Gorkha on 27 Poush 1779 BS, king at 20, he vows to weld the scattered states into one Nepal, lays unification's foundation by 1826 BS, and his deathbed Divya Upadesh still teaches: Nepal is a garden of four varnas and thirty-six castes, a yam between two stones, take no bribes and give none, and let the people's chosen man be Kaji

---

the gallery continues with Ram Shah of Gorkha and his famed justice reforms, Prithvi Narayan Shah the unifier, Bhimsen Thapa the first prime minister, the war heroes Amar Singh Thapa and Balbhadra Kunwar, and Bhanubhakta Acharya, who gave Nepal its Ramayana in Nepali

---

the campaign is told fort by fort — Nuwakot won, lost and retaken; Kirtipur taken at the third assault after Kalu Pande falls; Makwanpur; the blockade of the valley; Kathmandu seized during Indrajatra while Jayaprakash Malla feasts; Lalitpur and Bhaktapur follow — until the scattered kingdoms stand as one Nepal

---

the war's heroes are honoured one by one — Amar Singh Thapa in the west, Balbhadra Kunwar at Nalapani, Bhakti Thapa at Malaun — the commanders whose stands made the British respect Nepali arms even in defeat

---

the modern age opens with Prithvi Narayan Shah's unification from 1825 BS — the valley conquered, the kingdom expanded east and west, and the Anglo-Nepalese war testing the new state
