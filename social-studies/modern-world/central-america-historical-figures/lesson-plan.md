# Lesson plan — Central America Historical Figures

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Central America Historical Figures
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Honduras, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Asturias wins the 1967 Nobel for a literature built on Indigenous heritage and invents magical realism against dictatorship in El Señor Presidente; Monseñor Romero marks El Salvador; Honduras's Clementina Suárez, feminist poet against Carías, Roberto Sosa and Sor María Rosa stand beside Nicaragua's Chamorro, martyr of public liberties against the Somozas
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
