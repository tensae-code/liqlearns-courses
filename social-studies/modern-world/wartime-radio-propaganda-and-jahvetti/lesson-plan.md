## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Johdanto: the Jahvetin kirjelaatikko programme and its archive** — through the war years some 100,000 Finns write their troubles to a radio letterbox, and over 70,000 of the letters survive on 18 shelf-metres in the National Archives, anonymised for pupils to read, hear and question
2. **Jahvetin kirjelaatikko (the programme, 1940–1945)** — twice a week the beloved voice "Jahvetti" — secretly teacher and writer Yrjö Kilpeläinen — answers citizens on air while his office, part of the State Information Office, sounds the nation's mood and fights rumours and Soviet propaganda, a one-man front in Finland's spiritual national defence
3. **Task 4 mood-regulation question** — the letter box belonged to the State Information Office; pupils find its mood-managing aim inside the official reply
4. **introduction: radio in 1940s Finland and the letter-box programme** — one channel, 500,000 licences by 1945, communal listening; the state builds Jahvetti to hear and steer the nation's mood while Soviet agitator Armas Äikiä jams the Lahti wavelength
5. **Task 1, dugout letter 1942** — front soldiers ('the 5418 lads') write that the more Äikiä shouts over the broadcast, the less anyone at the front believes him
6. **Task 2, radio speech 4 July 1944 (part)** — at the evacuation summer's height Jahvetti preaches duty and a fair welcome for the displaced on their new shores
7. **Task 3, radio speech 7 March 1944 (part)** — Jahvetti calls on battery-radio owners; donated receivers must reach the front and be kept working — the set itself is war materiel
8. **Task 4, the 4 July 1944 speech in full (upper secondary)** — pupils dissect Jahvetti's personal rhetoric and ask why his anonymity helped the programme work
9. **Task 5, Humppila letter Oct 1944** — an 'old mother' whose sons were sent north again notes her village walks two kilometres to hear the radio together — listening as a community act
