# Wartime radio, propaganda and Jahvetti — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Johdanto: the Jahvetin kirjelaatikko programme and its archive
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — through the war years some 100,000 Finns write their troubles to a radio letterbox, and over 70,000 of the letters survive on 18 shelf-metres in the National Archives, anonymised for pupils to read, hear and question

## Sub lesson 2 — Jahvetin kirjelaatikko (the programme, 1940–1945)
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — twice a week the beloved voice "Jahvetti" — secretly teacher and writer Yrjö Kilpeläinen — answers citizens on air while his office, part of the State Information Office, sounds the nation's mood and fights rumours and Soviet propaganda, a one-man front in Finland's spiritual national defence

## Sub lesson 3 — Task 4 mood-regulation question
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the letter box belonged to the State Information Office; pupils find its mood-managing aim inside the official reply

## Sub lesson 4 — introduction: radio in 1940s Finland and the letter-box programme
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — one channel, 500,000 licences by 1945, communal listening; the state builds Jahvetti to hear and steer the nation's mood while Soviet agitator Armas Äikiä jams the Lahti wavelength

## Sub lesson 5 — Task 1, dugout letter 1942
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — front soldiers ('the 5418 lads') write that the more Äikiä shouts over the broadcast, the less anyone at the front believes him

## Sub lesson 6 — Task 2, radio speech 4 July 1944 (part)
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — at the evacuation summer's height Jahvetti preaches duty and a fair welcome for the displaced on their new shores

## Sub lesson 7 — Task 3, radio speech 7 March 1944 (part)
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Jahvetti calls on battery-radio owners; donated receivers must reach the front and be kept working — the set itself is war materiel

## Sub lesson 8 — Task 4, the 4 July 1944 speech in full (upper secondary)
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — pupils dissect Jahvetti's personal rhetoric and ask why his anonymity helped the programme work

## Sub lesson 9 — Task 5, Humppila letter Oct 1944
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — an 'old mother' whose sons were sent north again notes her village walks two kilometres to hear the radio together — listening as a community act
