# Lesson plan — Building the Haitian state, 1804–1843

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Building the Haitian state, 1804–1843
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Haiti, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** On the ruins of Saint-Domingue a free state is proclaimed in 1804 — then rival blueprints collide: Dessalines' empire, Christophe's kingdom, Pétion's republic, reunification under Boyer, and the Rural Code of 1826.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The state, society and the Declaration of Independence** — on the ruins of Saint-Domingue, Black and mixed-race leaders proclaim a free state no one in 1804 thought possible — and argue over what it should be (Séance 1, L'État, la société et la déclaration d'indépendance (1er janvier 1804))
2. **An authoritarian state — the Dessalinian empire** — Dessalines crowns himself emperor and rules by the sword; his assassination in 1806 leaves the young state without an agreed direction (Séance 2, Un État autoritaire, l'empire dessalinien et la crise d'orientation)
3. **Rupture in the unitary strategy** — the 1806 constitution fight breaks the founding unity — two rival models of organising the state and the community face off (Séance 3, Rupture dans la stratégie unitaire de construction de l'État)
4. **One territory, two states (1) — Christophe's kingdom** — in the north Henri Christophe builds a kingdom — palace, code and court — betting that discipline and grandeur will earn respect (Séance 4, Un territoire et deux États (1): l'État d'Haïti et le royaume de Christophe)
5. **One territory, two states (2) — Pétion's republic** — in the west and south Pétion's republic takes the opposite bet — land to soldiers, a senate, and help for Bolívar's independence fight (Séance 5, Un territoire et deux États (2): l'État de l'ouest et du sud, la République d'Haïti sous Pétion)
6. **The reunification of the Republic of Haiti** — after Christophe's fall in 1820, Boyer reunites north and republic — one Haiti again, soon stretched over the whole island (1822) (Séance 6, La réunification de la République d'Haïti)
7. **The Rural Code of 1826 and the authoritarian state** — Boyer's Rural Code tries to chain peasants back to plantation work — the state wins on paper, the countryside resists in fact (Séance 8, Le code rural de 1826 et la consolidation de l'État autoritaire)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
