# Building the Haitian state, 1804–1843 — short answer

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The state, society and the Declaration of Independence.
**Answer: On the ruins of saint-domingue, black and mixed-race leaders proclaim a free state no one in 1804 thought possible — and argue over what it should be (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: An authoritarian state — the Dessalinian empire.
**Answer: Dessalines crowns himself emperor and rules by the sword; his assassination in 1806 leaves the young state without an agreed direction (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Rupture in the unitary strategy.
**Answer: The 1806 constitution fight breaks the founding unity — two rival models of organising the state and the community face off (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: One territory, two states (1) — Christophe's kingdom.
**Answer: In the north henri christophe builds a kingdom — palace, code and court — betting that discipline and grandeur will earn respect (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: One territory, two states (2) — Pétion's republic.
**Answer: In the west and south pétion's republic takes the opposite bet — land to soldiers, a senate, and help for bolívar's independence fight (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The reunification of the Republic of Haiti.
**Answer: After christophe's fall in 1820, boyer reunites north and republic — one haiti again, soon stretched over the whole island (1822) (any close wording).**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The Rural Code of 1826 and the authoritarian state.
**Answer: Boyer's rural code tries to chain peasants back to plantation work — the state wins on paper, the countryside resists in fact (any close wording).**
