# The East India Company — Plassey and Buxar

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: A trading company turns political — Clive's bargain with Mir Jafar at Plassey and the victory at Buxar make it master of northern India, Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse swallows state after state, and after the failed rising of 1857 the Crown rules directly. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Crown takes over after 1857

After the failed rising of 1857 the East India Company's rule passes to the Crown; the book closes the chapter with the challenges the Muslims of the Subcontinent then faced — political eclipse, economic ruin and an education gap.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plassey and Buxar — a company becomes a ruler

A trading company turns political, Clive's bargain with Mir Jafar hands it Bengal, and Buxar makes it master of northern India.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Doctrine of Lapse

A legal rule becomes a land-grab, Indian rulers learn adoption no longer protects their thrones, and resentment builds toward 1857.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: The Crown takes over after 1857; Plassey and Buxar — a company becomes a ruler; The Doctrine of Lapse. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
