# Lesson plan — Vietnam Can Vuong And Ham Nghi

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Can Vuong And Ham Nghi
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Nguyễn court surrenders to France, but in 1885 King Hàm Nghi issues the Cần Vương edict and the Bru–Vân Kiều, Tà Ôi and Cơ Tu peoples of Tân Sở feed, house and hide the king so well the French never find him
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 4, story box: ethnic unity in the Cần Vương movement** — the Nguyễn court surrenders to France, but in 1885 King Hàm Nghi issues the Cần Vương edict and the Bru–Vân Kiều, Tà Ôi and Cơ Tu peoples of Tân Sở feed, house and hide the king so well the French never find him
2. **Bài 13, the Cần Vương movement against the French** — after the 1884 treaty recognises the French protectorate, the war faction around young King Hàm Nghi and Tôn Thất Thuyết proclaims the Cần Vương edict in 1885 and risings spread nationwide
3. **Bài 18, the Cần Vương movement breaks out, 1885** — after the Patenôtre treaty a war party still stands in the Huế court around Tôn Thất Thuyết, Minister of War, who builds forces, stockpiles food and weapons and puts the boy Hàm Nghi on the throne; when his counterattack on the French in the capital fails on 5 July 1885 he carries the king to Tân Sở in Quảng Trị and, in Hàm Nghi's name, issues the Cần Vương edict — the Appeal to Aid the King — calling literati and people across the country to rise; the movement burns to the century's end
