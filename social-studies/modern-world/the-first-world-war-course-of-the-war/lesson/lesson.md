# The First World War — course of the war

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — sarajevo and the cascade of declarations is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sarajevo and the cascade of declarations

The spark: the Austro-Hungarian heir **Franz Ferdinand and his wife Sophia were assassinated** on a visit to **Sarajevo, capital of Bosnia, on June 28, 1914**, by the Serbian **Gavrilo Princip** (as printed in the book). **Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on July 28, 1914.** Then the blocs fired like a row of falling stones. **Russia moved to help Serbia. Germany declared war on Russia on August 2 and on France on August 3**, using **neutral Belgium as its bridge into France**. **Britain declared war on Germany on August 4.** **Turkey and Bulgaria joined the Central Powers**, and **about thirty countries** lined up behind the Entente. One assassination, one month, and the armed camps of the last lesson were at war.

## Sub lesson 2 — Three fronts — and the end in 1918

The war ran on **three fronts**. **The west:** Germany fought **France, Britain and America** — where **aircraft, tanks, artillery and poison gas were first used in battle**. **The east:** **Russia was heavily defeated** by Germany and Austria-Hungary and **left the war after the 1917 socialist revolution**. **The south (Balkan) front:** by **1917** Germany and Austria-Hungary held **most of the Balkans**. Then the turn: in **1918** the Allies' attack **freed part of the Balkans**; **Austria-Hungary left defeated**; **Turkey and Bulgaria had already left**; and **Germany accepted defeat and agreed to stop firing in 1918**. The Central Powers fell away one by one until only Germany remained — and Germany gave in.
