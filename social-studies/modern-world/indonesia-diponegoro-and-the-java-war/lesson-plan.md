# Lesson plan — Indonesia — Diponegoro and the Java War

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Indonesia — Diponegoro and the Java War
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Indonesia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Prince Diponegoro, raised outside the court, leads Java's five-year war against the Dutch — and Raden Saleh's painting of his capture is read as a source on how it ended
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A.2, change-over-time section** — Raden Saleh's painting of the capture of Diponegoro is read as a source on the end of the Java War and on how the defeated prince, raised outside the court, became its leader
2. **Bab I.B** — Raden Saleh's canvas of Diponegoro's capture closes the five-year Java War, the last great princely resistance before the colonial state hardens
3. **Bab 2.B.4 Perang Diponegoro** — the prince's five-year guerrilla war bleeds Java and the colonial treasury before his capture under a flag of negotiation

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
