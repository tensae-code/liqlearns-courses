# Lesson plan — The Durrani Empire after Ahmad Shah

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Durrani Empire after Ahmad Shah
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Afghanistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the founder's son holds the empire for two decades and moves the seat of rule toward Kabul
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lesson 7: Did you know Timur Shah reigned twenty years?** — the founder's son holds the empire for two decades and moves the seat of rule toward Kabul
2. **Lesson 8: Do you know Shah Zaman's reign programmes?** — Shah Zaman plans to hold the empire together as its provinces begin to pull away
3. **Lesson 9: How did Shah Mahmud first come to power?** — the Durrani princes' rivalries open the throne to Mahmud's first rise
4. **Lesson 10: The course of Shah Shuja's first reign** — Shuja's first reign plays out amid the princes' contest for the Durrani throne
5. **Lesson 11: The course of Shah Mahmud's second reign** — Mahmud returns to a throne the empire can no longer hold together
6. **Lesson 4: Ahmad Shah Baba's successors — the reign of Timur Shah (with Shah Zaman 1793–1801 and Shah Mahmud Abdali 1801–1804)** — the empire passes to Timur Shah for twenty years, then to Zaman and the first Mahmud as the princes' rivalries begin
7. **Lesson 5: The first reign of Shah Shuja (1804–1809) and the second reign of Shah Mahmud (1809–1818)** — Shuja and Mahmud trade the throne while the empire breaks
8. **Lesson 6: The renewal of rivalries and disputes** — Durrani and Barakzai contenders multiply and the state's centre cannot hold
9. **Lesson 7: Transfer of power and interventions (1818–1834)** — power shifts among the Barakzai brothers as Persia and Britain begin their interventions

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
