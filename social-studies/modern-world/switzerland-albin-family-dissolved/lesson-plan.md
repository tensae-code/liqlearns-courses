# Lesson plan — Switzerland — The Albin family dissolved

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Switzerland — The Albin family dissolved
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Switzerland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Josef and Sophia Albin had eight children and almost nothing else; the authorities declared them incapable, took the children away in stages between 1949 and 1953, blocked all contact — and the family never came together again.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Eight children and no family any more** — Josef and Sophia Albin have eight children in twelve years of extreme poverty. The authorities declare the parents incapable of raising them. Between 1949 and 1953 the children are taken away in stages, all contact is blocked, and both parents die without the family ever reuniting.
2. **Measured against the model family** — The companion explains the measure used on the Albins: the 1912 Civil Code makes the husband head of the family, and "neglect" is a stretchable word. Measured against that patriarchal model, the Albins fail. Other poor families, the companion notes, got beds, furniture and a "family rescue" instead — the Albins got dissolution. (Teacher guide — evidence.)
3. **The appeal that failed** — At the upper level pupils read the fight itself: lawyer Gaudenz Canova appeals in full against taking the last four children, and the Grisons government answers him point by point — and rejects him. Pupils must rebuild 1947–1953 from both files and deliver their own verdict. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Switzerland — Compulsory welfare measures: care or coercion?. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the Albin story in order: eight children, declared incapable, taken in stages 1949–1953, contact blocked, never reunited.
2. Say what model the Albins were measured against, and what other poor families received instead of dissolution.
3. Say who appealed for the Albins, how the government answered, and what pupils must do with the two files.
