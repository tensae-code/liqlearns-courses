# Switzerland — The Albin family dissolved

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Josef and Sophia Albin had eight children and almost nothing else; the authorities declared them incapable, took the children away in stages between 1949 and 1953, blocked all contact — and the family never came together again. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Eight children and no family any more

Josef and Sophia Albin have eight children in twelve years of extreme poverty. The authorities declare the parents incapable of raising them. Between 1949 and 1953 the children are taken away in stages, all contact is blocked, and both parents die without the family ever reuniting.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measured against the model family

The companion explains the measure used on the Albins: the 1912 Civil Code makes the husband head of the family, and "neglect" is a stretchable word. Measured against that patriarchal model, the Albins fail. Other poor families, the companion notes, got beds, furniture and a "family rescue" instead — the Albins got dissolution. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## Sub lesson 3 — The appeal that failed

At the upper level pupils read the fight itself: lawyer Gaudenz Canova appeals in full against taking the last four children, and the Grisons government answers him point by point — and rejects him. Pupils must rebuild 1947–1953 from both files and deliver their own verdict. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of Switzerland — The Albin family dissolved in your own words, part by part, using the sub lesson headings as your beats. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Tell the Albin story in order: eight children, declared incapable, taken in stages 1949–1953, contact blocked, never reunited.
2. Say what model the Albins were measured against, and what other poor families received instead of dissolution.
3. Say who appealed for the Albins, how the government answered, and what pupils must do with the two files.
