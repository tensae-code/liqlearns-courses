# Whaling today — protecting the whales

## Do it first

The story so far: a community lived from the whale for ages; then ships from across the sea took so many that the whales grew few. Before you read on, say in your mind: what would a good ending look like — for the whales, and for the community that still needs them? Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## The story

The Nunavut reader's history of whaling ends in our own time — and the ending holds two things together.

First, the whales are now protected and counted. After the years of depletion, the whales are watched over: people count them, so their numbers are known and their safety can be guarded. The whale that the big ships hunted almost to scarcity is now a creature under protection.

Second, Inuit whaling continues today. It did not vanish with the old days. It goes on as a small, community-run hunt, under shared rules — the community itself runs it, and the rules are the community's shared agreement about how it is done. The Elders' knowledge and the crew's teamwork that carried the old hunt carry this one too — now inside limits that protect the whales.

That is the balance the story ends on: protection for the whales, counting to prove they are safe, and the community's own hunt carried on — small, shared and ruled — into the present day.
