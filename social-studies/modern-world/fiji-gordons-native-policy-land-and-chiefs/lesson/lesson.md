# Fiji — Gordon's native policy: land and chiefs

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Gordon arrives in 1875 to a measles epidemic that killed about a quarter of Fiji and an uprising in the Sigatoka valley; the "champion of native rights" freezes land sales, proves only 164,000 ha fairly bought, locks the rest as inalienable native land, and under the 1876 Native Affairs Ordinance rules iTaukei through their own ranks — Governor, Council of Chiefs, Roko, Buli, Turaga-ni-Koro — a system lasting to the 1970s As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sir Arthur Gordon: land policies, taxation and native administration

Gordon arrives in 1875 to a measles epidemic that killed about a quarter of Fiji and an uprising in the Sigatoka valley; the "champion of native rights" freezes land sales, proves only 164,000 ha fairly bought, locks the rest as inalienable native land, and under the 1876 Native Affairs Ordinance rules iTaukei through their own ranks — Governor, Council of Chiefs, Roko, Buli, Turaga-ni-Koro — a system lasting to the 1970s.

## Sub lesson 2 — The development of the Native Administration since 1876

From 10 October 1874 Fiji is home to peoples of different cultures; Gordon builds a separate Fijian Administration from 1876, province by province, expressly to preserve iTaukei society — pupils do a personality study of its makers.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Co-existence of the two administration systems; native administration after independence to 2006

Gordon's system does not end at independence in 1970 — diagrams trace the Fijian Administration running beside the national parliament and councils right up to 2006.
