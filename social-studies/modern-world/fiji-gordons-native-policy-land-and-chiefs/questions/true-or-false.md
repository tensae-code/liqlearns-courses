# Fiji — Gordon's native policy: land and chiefs — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Gordon arrives in 1875 to a measles epidemic that killed about a quarter of Fiji and an uprising in the Sigatoka valley; the "champion of native rights" freezes land sales, proves only 164,000 ha fairly bought, locks the rest as inalienable native land, and under the 1876 Native Affairs Ordinance rules iTaukei through their own ranks — Governor, Council of Chiefs, Roko, Buli, Turaga-ni-Koro — a system lasting to the 1970s.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Sir Arthur Gordon: land policies, taxation and native administration

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Social Science Year 10 — Global Perspectives, Local Interaction* is about Rotuma — settlement, contact and the mission war.
**Answer: False** — it is about Sir Arthur Gordon: land policies, taxation and native administration

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** From 10 October 1874 Fiji is home to peoples of different cultures; Gordon builds a separate Fijian Administration from 1876, province by province, expressly to preserve iTaukei society — pupils do a personality study of its makers.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The development of the Native Administration since 1876

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *History Year 11 — Government and Governance, Unit 2: Administration* is about Rotuma — settlement, contact and the mission war.
**Answer: False** — it is about The development of the Native Administration since 1876

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Gordon's system does not end at independence in 1970 — diagrams trace the Fijian Administration running beside the national parliament and councils right up to 2006.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Co-existence of the two administration systems; native administration after independence to 2006

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *History Year 11 — Government and Governance, Unit 2: Administration* is about Rotuma — settlement, contact and the mission war.
