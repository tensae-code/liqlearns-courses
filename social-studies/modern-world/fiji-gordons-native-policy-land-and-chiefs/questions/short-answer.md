# Fiji — Gordon's native policy: land and chiefs — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Sir Arthur Gordon: land policies, taxation and native administration.
**Answer: Gordon arrives in 1875 to a measles epidemic that killed about a quarter of Fiji and an uprising in the Sigatoka valley; the "champion of native rights" freezes land sales, proves only 164,000 ha fairly bought, locks the rest as inalienable native land, and under the 1876 Native Affairs Ordinance rules iTaukei through their own ranks — Governor, Council of Chiefs, Roko, Buli, Turaga-ni-Koro — a system lasting to the 1970s (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: The development of the Native Administration since 1876.
**Answer: from 10 October 1874 Fiji is home to peoples of different cultures; Gordon builds a separate Fijian Administration from 1876, province by province, expressly to preserve iTaukei society — pupils do a personality study of its makers (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: Gordon's system does not end at independence in 1970 — diagrams trace the Fijian Administration running beside the national parliament and councils right up to 2006 (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
