# Fiji — Gordon's native policy: land and chiefs — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sir Arthur Gordon: land policies, taxation and native administration
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Gordon arrives in 1875 to a measles epidemic that killed about a quarter of Fiji and an uprising in the Sigatoka valley; the "champion of native rights" freezes land sales, proves only 164,000 ha fairly bought, locks the rest as inalienable native land, and under the 1876 Native Affairs Ordinance rules iTaukei through their own ranks — Governor, Council of Chiefs, Roko, Buli, Turaga-ni-Koro — a system lasting to the 1970s

## Sub lesson 2 — The development of the Native Administration since 1876
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — from 10 October 1874 Fiji is home to peoples of different cultures; Gordon builds a separate Fijian Administration from 1876, province by province, expressly to preserve iTaukei society — pupils do a personality study of its makers

## Sub lesson 3 — Co-existence of the two administration systems; native administration after independence to 2006
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Gordon's system does not end at independence in 1970 — diagrams trace the Fijian Administration running beside the national parliament and councils right up to 2006
