# Turkey's National Struggle begins

## Do it first

The war is lost, the capital occupied, the army ordered to disarm — and the peace being prepared in Paris would carve the homeland itself into pieces. In Anatolia, officers and towns begin to organise a second state in the shadow of the first. Before you read on, decide what comes first in a resistance: an army, or a decision. Hold your answer; Amasya's circular is a decision on paper.

## Sub lesson 1 — Armistice, resistance and congresses — Mondros to the Grand National Assembly

Turkey's Grade 12 book tells the National Struggle's preparation as a chain of answers to defeat. The Mondros armistice disarms the empire and the occupations follow; the first answer is the Kuvâ-yi Milliye, the national forces — local resistance rising where the state cannot. Then the movement gives itself words and shape: the Amasya Circular declares that the nation's independence will be saved by the nation's own determination; the Erzurum and Sivas congresses organise that determination across Anatolia.

The chain ends in two opposite papers. In Ankara, the Grand National Assembly opens — a second state standing up inside the occupied empire. In Paris, the Treaty of Sèvres is laid before the capital — the empire's death warrant, partitioning the homeland. The book's beat holds both: an occupied, disarmed empire's officers and towns organise a second state in Anatolia while the capital signs its death warrant.
