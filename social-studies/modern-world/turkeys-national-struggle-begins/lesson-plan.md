# Lesson plan — Turkey's National Struggle begins

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Turkey's National Struggle begins
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 2, The National Struggle (preparation part): the Mondros armistice and occupations, the Kuvâ-yi Milliye resistance, the Amasya Circular, the Erzurum and Sivas congresses, the opening of the Grand National Assembly and the Treaty of Sèvres.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: an occupied, disarmed empire's officers and towns organise a second state in Anatolia while the capital signs its death warrant. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the preparation chain in order: Mondros armistice and occupations; Kuvâ-yi Milliye resistance; Amasya Circular; Erzurum and Sivas congresses; the Grand National Assembly opens.
2. Say what the Amasya Circular declares: the nation's independence will be saved by the nation's own determination.
3. Say what the Treaty of Sèvres would have done: partitioned the homeland — the empire's death warrant.
4. Explain, in their own words, what 'a second state in Anatolia' means.
