# Lesson plan — Northeast Asia Since 1991

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Northeast Asia Since 1991
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the World Bank figures in the opener give China, Japan and Korea alone a fifth of world GDP and a quarter of its people; Japan stagnates through alternating recession and weak growth yet stays a great economic-financial centre (GDP $3,580 billion in 1991, $5,010 billion in 2021; life expectancy near 85) until China passes it in 2010 after four decades as the world's second economy; South Korea, one of the four Asian "dragons", shifts decisively to high-tech industry — Samsung, LG, Hyundai — multiplying GDP some 5.5 times (1991–2021) to a top-ten ranking with per-capita income near $35,000; China sets the goal of a "socialist market economy", grows for thirty years at the world's highest rate to $17,820 billion (about 18% of world GDP in 2021), builds growth-pole regions from Pudong to Chengdu, and from 2013 pursues the Belt and Road initiative — though the book notes low growth quality, pollution and a wide rich–poor gap
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
