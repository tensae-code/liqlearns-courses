# Bahamas independence, 1973 — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Leaders: the first leaders of the independent Bahamas
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: after independence the new country has its own first Prime Minister, Sir Lynden Pindling, and first Governor General, Sir Milo Butler, pictured as the faces of the new nation

## Sub lesson 2 — The Bahamas, My Country: Independence Day and national symbols
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: on 10 July 1973 The Bahamas stops being ruled by Britain and can make its own laws; the gold, black and aquamarine flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms give the new country its own identity

## Sub lesson 3 — The Bahamas, My Country: independence and the symbols of the new nation
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: on 10 July 1973 The Bahamas becomes free to govern itself; Timothy Gibson's anthem "March on, Bahamaland", Rev. Phillip Rahming's pledge, the gold-black-aquamarine flag and a coat of arms carrying Columbus's Santa Maria give the new country its identity

## Sub lesson 4 — Bahamian History: We Celebrate Our History — the holidays that remember the past
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the old Discovery Day becomes National Heroes' Day; Emancipation Day remembers the slaves' freedom, Majority Rule Day (10 January) and Independence Day (10 July) mark the road to self-government

## Sub lesson 5 — Leaders and Nation Builders
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sir Milo Butler, first Governor General (1973–1979), Sir Lynden Pindling, first Prime Minister (1973–1992), and Hubert Ingraham, second Prime Minister, lead the roll of nation builders — with Timothy Gibson, Doris L. Johnson, Cecil V. Bethel, Mabel Walker, Dame Bertha Isaacs and others whose names schools now carry

## Sub lesson 6 — Leaders Part 1: twelve Governors General since independence
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: since 1973 twelve Governors General have represented the monarch — Sir John Paul, first Bahamian Governor General Sir Milo Butler, Sir Gerald Cash, Sir Henry Taylor, Sir Clifford Darling, Sir Orville Turnquest, first female Governor General Dame Ivy Dumont, Paul Adderley, Arthur Hanna, Sir Arthur Foulkes, Dame Marguerite Pindling and Sir Cornelius Smith

## Sub lesson 7 — Leaders Part 2: the Prime Ministers since 1973
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: five Prime Ministers lead the independent country — Sir Lynden Pindling for five consecutive terms, Hubert Ingraham, Perry Christie, Hubert Minnis, and the present Prime Minister Philip Davis

## Sub lesson 8 — Nation Builders Part 1: builders in education and religion
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Governor General Sir Gerald Cash (1979–1986), educator C. W. Sawyer who taught for 57 years and has a school named for him, A. F. Adderley, Michael Eldon, first Bahamian Anglican Bishop of Nassau, and Archdeacon William Thompson, rector of St. Agnes for 29 years, shot in his rectory in 2000

## Sub lesson 9 — Nation Builders Part 2: builders in sport, art and music
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: swimmer Andy Knowles races at the 1976 Olympics, Arianna Vanderpool-Wallace sets national records, painter Alton Lowe of Green Turtle Cay paints Bahamian life, and musicians Joseph Spence and Blind Blake (Alphonso Higgs) carry Bahamian music to the world

## Sub lesson 10 — Celebrations: the historical holidays
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the calendar itself remembers — Majority Rule Day (10 January), Randol Fawkes Labour Day for the "father" of the labour movement, Emancipation Day, National Heroes' Day (formerly Discovery Day, 12 October) and Independence Day (10 July)

## Sub lesson 11 — National Pride: to be Bahamian is to know Bahamian history
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: citizens protect the Bahamian identity; knowing the country's history, pictured at Independence Day, Clifford Park, 1973, is named first among the ways pride is shown

## Sub lesson 12 — National Symbols: flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the black-gold-aquamarine flag stands for a united people's strength and their sun and sea; the coat of arms puts Columbus's Santa Maria on the shield under the motto "Forward, Upward, Onward, Together"

## Sub lesson 13 — Citizenship in Colonial Rule: British subjects before independence
Sub lesson 13 questions from the type files. Pass mark: before independence Bahamians are ruled by Britain, carry British passports, honour British symbols and pledge loyalty to England — citizenship in their own country is what independence will change

## Sub lesson 14 — Nation Builders Part 1: politicians and pastors
Sub lesson 14 questions from the type files. Pass mark: S. C. McPherson (1885–1972), elected in 1923, and lawyer-politician A. F. Adderley (Cambridge 1912, called to the Bar 1919) build the political nation; pastors Charles Saunders, who founds a Baptist school and college, and H. W. Brown of Bethel Baptist, honoured in 1967 as a hero of the Majority Rule struggle, build its churches and schools

## Sub lesson 15 — Nation Builders Part 2: the sailors
Sub lesson 15 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sir Durward Knowles becomes the first Bahamian world sailing champion, winning Olympic bronze at Melbourne 1956 and gold at Tokyo 1964 with Cecil Cooke; Eleazar Johnson, the "Sailing Barber", starts the St. Valentine's Day regatta, and Captain Rolly Gray, self-taught from age ten, becomes the "Grand Master" of sloop racing

## Sub lesson 16 — Nation Builders Part 3: the musicians
Sub lesson 16 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Phil Stubbs, Patrick Rahming and Region Bells write the songs — goombay beats and gospel — by which Bahamians recognise themselves, and the country honours its builders with buildings, streets and events named for them

## Sub lesson 17 — National Symbols – An Independent Nation Part 1: from the Queen's song to our own flag
Sub lesson 17 questions from the type files. Pass mark: before 1973 the colony's symbols are British and children sing "God Save Our Gracious Queen"; independence brings a Bahamian flag and Timothy Gibson's "March on, Bahamaland" as the anthem of a sovereign nation

## Sub lesson 18 — National Symbols Part 2: the coat of arms remembers Columbus
Sub lesson 18 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the crest of the coat of arms shows Columbus's landfall and a blazing sun, the crown keeps the monarch link, the motto is written by Hervis Bain, and the independent Bahamas stays in the Commonwealth of Nations

## Sub lesson 19 — The Constitution / Citizenship: the 1972 constitution
Sub lesson 19 questions from the type files. Pass mark: in 1972 the British and Bahamian governments agree the constitution that becomes the basis of all Bahamian law, sets out who is a Bahamian, and protects citizens' rights and freedoms

## Sub lesson 20 — National Builders: heroes of the nation
Sub lesson 20 questions from the type files. Pass mark: two men are both called "Father of the Nation" — Sir Lynden Pindling and Randol Fawkes; tennis pioneer Mark Knowles, the Golden Girls and Golden Knights, Shaunae Miller-Uibo and Steven Gardiner, songwriter Eddie Minnis, artist Stanley Burnside, Rev. Dr. Myles Munroe and Cat Island's Father Jerome, architect of the Hermitage, fill the national honour roll
