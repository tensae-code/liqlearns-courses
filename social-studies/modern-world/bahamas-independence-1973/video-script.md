# Video script — Bahamas independence, 1973

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bahamas books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

after independence the new country has its own first Prime Minister, Sir Lynden Pindling, and first Governor General, Sir Milo Butler, pictured as the faces of the new nation; on 10 July 1973 The Bahamas stops being ruled by Britain and can make its own laws; the gold, black and aquamarine flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms give the new country its own identity; on 10 July 1973 The Bahamas becomes free to govern itself; Timothy Gibson's anthem "March on, Bahamaland", Rev. Phillip Rahming's pledge, the gold-black-aquamarine flag and a coat of arms carrying Columbus's Santa Maria give the new country its identity; the old Discovery Day becomes National Heroes' Day; Emancipation Day remembers the slaves' freedom, Majority Rule Day (10 January) and Independence Day (10 July) mark the road to self-government; Sir Milo Butler, first Governor General (1973–1979), Sir Lynden Pindling, first Prime Minister (1973–1992), and Hubert Ingraham, second Prime Minister, lead the roll of nation builders — with Timothy Gibson, Doris L. Johnson, Cecil V. Bethel, Mabel Walker, Dame Bertha Isaacs and others whose names schools now carry; since 1973 twelve Governors General have represented the monarch — Sir John Paul, first Bahamian Governor General Sir Milo Butler, Sir Gerald Cash, Sir Henry Taylor, Sir Clifford Darling, Sir Orville Turnquest, first female Governor General Dame Ivy Dumont, Paul Adderley, Arthur Hanna, Sir Arthur Foulkes, Dame Marguerite Pindling and Sir Cornelius Smith; five Prime Ministers lead the independent country — Sir Lynden Pindling for five consecutive terms, Hubert Ingraham, Perry Christie, Hubert Minnis, and the present Prime Minister Philip Davis; Governor General Sir Gerald Cash (1979–1986), educator C. W. Sawyer who taught for 57 years and has a school named for him, A. F. Adderley, Michael Eldon, first Bahamian Anglican Bishop of Nassau, and Archdeacon William Thompson, rector of St. Agnes for 29 years, shot in his rectory in 2000; swimmer Andy Knowles races at the 1976 Olympics, Arianna Vanderpool-Wallace sets national records, painter Alton Lowe of Green Turtle Cay paints Bahamian life, and musicians Joseph Spence and Blind Blake (Alphonso Higgs) carry Bahamian music to the world; the calendar itself remembers — Majority Rule Day (10 January), Randol Fawkes Labour Day for the "father" of the labour movement, Emancipation Day, National Heroes' Day (formerly Discovery Day, 12 October) and Independence Day (10 July); citizens protect the Bahamian identity; knowing the country's history, pictured at Independence Day, Clifford Park, 1973, is named first among the ways pride is shown; the black-gold-aquamarine flag stands for a united people's strength and their sun and sea; the coat of arms puts Columbus's Santa Maria on the shield under the motto "Forward, Upward, Onward, Together"; before independence Bahamians are ruled by Britain, carry British passports, honour British symbols and pledge loyalty to England — citizenship in their own country is what independence will change; S. C. McPherson (1885–1972), elected in 1923, and lawyer-politician A. F. Adderley (Cambridge 1912, called to the Bar 1919) build the political nation; pastors Charles Saunders, who founds a Baptist school and college, and H. W. Brown of Bethel Baptist, honoured in 1967 as a hero of the Majority Rule struggle, build its churches and schools; Sir Durward Knowles becomes the first Bahamian world sailing champion, winning Olympic bronze at Melbourne 1956 and gold at Tokyo 1964 with Cecil Cooke; Eleazar Johnson, the "Sailing Barber", starts the St. Valentine's Day regatta, and Captain Rolly Gray, self-taught from age ten, becomes the "Grand Master" of sloop racing; Phil Stubbs, Patrick Rahming and Region Bells write the songs — goombay beats and gospel — by which Bahamians recognise themselves, and the country honours its builders with buildings, streets and events named for them; before 1973 the colony's symbols are British and children sing "God Save Our Gracious Queen"; independence brings a Bahamian flag and Timothy Gibson's "March on, Bahamaland" as the anthem of a sovereign nation; the crest of the coat of arms shows Columbus's landfall and a blazing sun, the crown keeps the monarch link, the motto is written by Hervis Bain, and the independent Bahamas stays in the Commonwealth of Nations; in 1972 the British and Bahamian governments agree the constitution that becomes the basis of all Bahamian law, sets out who is a Bahamian, and protects citizens' rights and freedoms; two men are both called "Father of the Nation" — Sir Lynden Pindling and Randol Fawkes; tennis pioneer Mark Knowles, the Golden Girls and Golden Knights, Shaunae Miller-Uibo and Steven Gardiner, songwriter Eddie Minnis, artist Stanley Burnside, Rev. Dr. Myles Munroe and Cat Island's Father Jerome, architect of the Hermitage, fill the national honour roll
