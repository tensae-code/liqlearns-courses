# Bahamas independence, 1973 — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Leaders: the first leaders of the independent Bahamas.
**Answer: after independence the new country has its own first Prime Minister, Sir Lynden Pindling, and first Governor General, Sir Milo Butler, pictured as the faces of the new nation (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The Bahamas, My Country: Independence Day and national symbols.
**Answer: on 10 July 1973 The Bahamas stops being ruled by Britain and can make its own laws; the gold, black and aquamarine flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms give the new country its own identity (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The Bahamas, My Country: independence and the symbols of the new nation.
**Answer: on 10 July 1973 The Bahamas becomes free to govern itself; Timothy Gibson's anthem "March on, Bahamaland", Rev. Phillip Rahming's pledge, the gold-black-aquamarine flag and a coat of arms carrying Columbus's Santa Maria give the new country its identity (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Bahamian History: We Celebrate Our History — the holidays that remember the past.
**Answer: the old Discovery Day becomes National Heroes' Day; Emancipation Day remembers the slaves' freedom, Majority Rule Day (10 January) and Independence Day (10 July) mark the road to self-government (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Leaders and Nation Builders.
**Answer: Sir Milo Butler, first Governor General (1973–1979), Sir Lynden Pindling, first Prime Minister (1973–1992), and Hubert Ingraham, second Prime Minister, lead the roll of nation builders — with Timothy Gibson, Doris L. Johnson, Cecil V. Bethel, Mabel Walker, Dame Bertha Isaacs and others whose names schools now carry (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Leaders Part 1: twelve Governors General since independence.
**Answer: since 1973 twelve Governors General have represented the monarch — Sir John Paul, first Bahamian Governor General Sir Milo Butler, Sir Gerald Cash, Sir Henry Taylor, Sir Clifford Darling, Sir Orville Turnquest, first female Governor General Dame Ivy Dumont, Paul Adderley, Arthur Hanna, Sir Arthur Foulkes, Dame Marguerite Pindling and Sir Cornelius Smith (any close wording).**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Leaders Part 2: the Prime Ministers since 1973.
**Answer: five Prime Ministers lead the independent country — Sir Lynden Pindling for five consecutive terms, Hubert Ingraham, Perry Christie, Hubert Minnis, and the present Prime Minister Philip Davis (any close wording).**

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Nation Builders Part 1: builders in education and religion.
**Answer: Governor General Sir Gerald Cash (1979–1986), educator C. W. Sawyer who taught for 57 years and has a school named for him, A. F. Adderley, Michael Eldon, first Bahamian Anglican Bishop of Nassau, and Archdeacon William Thompson, rector of St. Agnes for 29 years, shot in his rectory in 2000 (any close wording).**

**9. (Sub lesson 9)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Nation Builders Part 2: builders in sport, art and music.
**Answer: swimmer Andy Knowles races at the 1976 Olympics, Arianna Vanderpool-Wallace sets national records, painter Alton Lowe of Green Turtle Cay paints Bahamian life, and musicians Joseph Spence and Blind Blake (Alphonso Higgs) carry Bahamian music to the world (any close wording).**

**10. (Sub lesson 10)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Celebrations: the historical holidays.
**Answer: the calendar itself remembers — Majority Rule Day (10 January), Randol Fawkes Labour Day for the "father" of the labour movement, Emancipation Day, National Heroes' Day (formerly Discovery Day, 12 October) and Independence Day (10 July) (any close wording).**

**11. (Sub lesson 11)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: National Pride: to be Bahamian is to know Bahamian history.
**Answer: citizens protect the Bahamian identity; knowing the country's history, pictured at Independence Day, Clifford Park, 1973, is named first among the ways pride is shown (any close wording).**

**12. (Sub lesson 12)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: National Symbols: flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms.
**Answer: the black-gold-aquamarine flag stands for a united people's strength and their sun and sea; the coat of arms puts Columbus's Santa Maria on the shield under the motto "Forward, Upward, Onward, Together" (any close wording).**

**13. (Sub lesson 13)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Citizenship in Colonial Rule: British subjects before independence.
**Answer: before independence Bahamians are ruled by Britain, carry British passports, honour British symbols and pledge loyalty to England — citizenship in their own country is what independence will change (any close wording).**

**14. (Sub lesson 14)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Nation Builders Part 1: politicians and pastors.
**Answer: S. C. McPherson (1885–1972), elected in 1923, and lawyer-politician A. F. Adderley (Cambridge 1912, called to the Bar 1919) build the political nation; pastors Charles Saunders, who founds a Baptist school and college, and H. W. Brown of Bethel Baptist, honoured in 1967 as a hero of the Majority Rule struggle, build its churches and schools (any close wording).**

**15. (Sub lesson 15)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Nation Builders Part 2: the sailors.
**Answer: Sir Durward Knowles becomes the first Bahamian world sailing champion, winning Olympic bronze at Melbourne 1956 and gold at Tokyo 1964 with Cecil Cooke; Eleazar Johnson, the "Sailing Barber", starts the St. Valentine's Day regatta, and Captain Rolly Gray, self-taught from age ten, becomes the "Grand Master" of sloop racing (any close wording).**

**16. (Sub lesson 16)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: Nation Builders Part 3: the musicians.
**Answer: Phil Stubbs, Patrick Rahming and Region Bells write the songs — goombay beats and gospel — by which Bahamians recognise themselves, and the country honours its builders with buildings, streets and events named for them (any close wording).**

**17. (Sub lesson 17)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: National Symbols – An Independent Nation Part 1: from the Queen's song to our own flag.
**Answer: before 1973 the colony's symbols are British and children sing "God Save Our Gracious Queen"; independence brings a Bahamian flag and Timothy Gibson's "March on, Bahamaland" as the anthem of a sovereign nation (any close wording).**

**18. (Sub lesson 18)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: National Symbols Part 2: the coat of arms remembers Columbus.
**Answer: the crest of the coat of arms shows Columbus's landfall and a blazing sun, the crown keeps the monarch link, the motto is written by Hervis Bain, and the independent Bahamas stays in the Commonwealth of Nations (any close wording).**

**19. (Sub lesson 19)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: The Constitution / Citizenship: the 1972 constitution.
**Answer: in 1972 the British and Bahamian governments agree the constitution that becomes the basis of all Bahamian law, sets out who is a Bahamian, and protects citizens' rights and freedoms (any close wording).**

**20. (Sub lesson 20)** Tell this part of the story in your own words: National Builders: heroes of the nation.
**Answer: two men are both called "Father of the Nation" — Sir Lynden Pindling and Randol Fawkes; tennis pioneer Mark Knowles, the Golden Girls and Golden Knights, Shaunae Miller-Uibo and Steven Gardiner, songwriter Eddie Minnis, artist Stanley Burnside, Rev. Dr. Myles Munroe and Cat Island's Father Jerome, architect of the Hermitage, fill the national honour roll (any close wording).**
