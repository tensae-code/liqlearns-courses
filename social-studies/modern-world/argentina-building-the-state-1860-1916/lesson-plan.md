# Lesson plan — Argentina — building the state, 1860–1916

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Argentina — building the state, 1860–1916
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Argentina, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Between 1860 and 1916 Argentina's leaders built one national state out of a loose set of provinces — army, laws, schools and railways — and foreign capital tied the new country's export wealth to markets across the ocean; Argentina's Grade 6 teacher guide teaches that building of the state as one big case, worked from stories, images, laws and statistics.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the idea of a state itself — what has to be built before a set of provinces becomes one country — and on reading photographs and accounts as evidence of how fast a country can change. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Thinking about the state** — after decades of division, Argentina's leaders build one national state — army, laws, schools, railways — and turn a loose set of provinces into a single country.
2. **Stories and images of a country changing fast** — photographs and accounts show Buenos Aires and the pampas transformed in one generation: ports, trains and new towns remake daily life.
3. **The state and foreign investment** — British and other foreign capital builds the railways and meat-packing plants of the export boom, tying Argentina's new wealth to markets across the ocean.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nationalism — the feeling that made nation states; Germany unifies — Bismarck, blood and iron; Italy unifies — Cavour and the patriots. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what Argentina's leaders built between 1860 and 1916, and name the tools of that building: army, laws, schools, railways.
2. Say what photographs and accounts show about Buenos Aires and the pampas in this period.
3. Say where much of the money for the railways and meat-packing plants came from, and what that tied Argentina's wealth to.
4. Say why the book calls this a complex, controversial period, and where that question is taken up (the companion lessons on immigration and on the conquest of indigenous territories).

## If the learner is stuck
If the learner loses the thread, return to the starting picture: provinces that were divided becoming one country — and ask what a single country needs that provinces on their own do not have: one army, one set of laws, schools, railways joining them together.
