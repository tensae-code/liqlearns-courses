# Video script — Argentina — building the state, 1860–1916

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: a loose set of provinces becomes one country, as Argentina's Grade 6 teacher guide tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

For a long time, Argentina was not one country pulling together. It was a loose set of provinces, divided, each going its own way.

Then, between 1860 and 1916, its leaders set out to build a state. One army. One set of laws. Schools for the country's children. Railways running across the land, joining province to province. Piece by piece, the provinces became a single country.

And the change was fast enough to see. Photographs and accounts from the time show Buenos Aires and the pampas transformed in one generation — new ports, trains crossing the plains, new towns where there had been open country. Daily life itself was remade.

But building all this took money, and much of that money came from across the ocean. British and other foreign capital built the railways and the meat-packing plants of the export boom — so Argentina's new wealth was tied, from the start, to markets far away.
