# Lesson plan — Sarawak and Sabah — local governments before Brooke and North Borneo

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Sarawak and Sabah — local governments before Brooke and North Borneo
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Malaysia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Before Brooke and the Chartered Company, Sarawak and Sabah are governed from the rivers — Brunei's wakil raja system, Sulu's trade, free chiefs, and the bumiputera peoples ordering their lives by the rivers
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sarawak and Sabah** — Brunei's sultanate rises and draws Sarawak's trade to itself while the Sulu Sultanate (15th century) lifts north Sabah's trade in sea and forest produce — bird's nests for China; government runs through the wakil raja system inherited from Majapahit and Sambas and used by Brunei, and in Sabah through free chiefs (ketua bebas) like Syarif Osman at Marudu and Datu Kurunding at Tungku; the bumiputera peoples — Orang Ulu (Kayan, Kenyah, Kelabit, Penan, Punan), Bidayuh, Melanau, Kadazandusun, Bajau, Suluk and the Murut, "hill people" in Brunei's naming — order their lives by the rivers (Bab 10: Sarawak and Sabah)
