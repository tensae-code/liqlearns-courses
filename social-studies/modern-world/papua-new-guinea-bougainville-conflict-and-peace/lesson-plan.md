# Lesson plan — Papua New Guinea — the Bougainville conflict and peace

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Papua New Guinea — the Bougainville conflict and peace
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Papua New Guinea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Oceania's biggest conflict — ten years of civil war between the PNG Government and the Bougainville Revolutionary Army, an estimated 15,000–20,000 dead by 1999, and a slow reconciliation still being woven
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Military force and change in PNG — Case Study: The Bougainville Conflict** — Oceania's biggest conflict — ten years of civil war between the PNG Government and the Bougainville Revolutionary Army, an estimated 15,000–20,000 dead by 1999, and a slow reconciliation still being woven
2. **Impact of historical events on societies in PNG** — 1988–1998, government against the BRA; 15,000–20,000 Bougainvillean lives; the 1998 Peace Agreement trades the guns for an Autonomous Bougainville Government — students analyse causes, effects and the conflict's mark on community and nation
3. **Papua New Guinea as a Nation** — a report on the Crisis's significant events and what they did to every stakeholder and to the national economy — the nation's deepest wound studied as current history

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
