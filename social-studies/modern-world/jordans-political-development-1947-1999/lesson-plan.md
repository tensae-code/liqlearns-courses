# Lesson plan — Jordan's political development, 1947–1999

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Jordan's political development, 1947–1999
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** independence leads into the 1948 war, in which the Arabs lose some 78% of historic Palestine, then the Unity of the Two Banks (1950); Abdullah I is assassinated at al-Aqsa during Friday prayer on 20 July 1951; Talal follows, then Hussein — born in Amman on 14 November 1935, schooled at Victoria College, Alexandria — is proclaimed in 1952 as Talal's health fails; the army's command is arabised, and the first party government (Suleiman al-Nabulsi, 1956–57) rises and falls; June 1967 costs Jordan 696 martyrs, 421 wounded, 520 prisoners and 350,000 displaced — but at Karameh on 21 March 1968 the Arab Army, losing 86 martyrs, breaks the myth of Israel's invincibility; Jordanian armour fights on the Golan in October 1973; parliament sleeps until 1984, then the 1989 elections, the 1991 National Charter, the end of martial law and the 1992 Parties Law reopen political life; Hussein dies on 7 February 1999 and the world fills Amman for "the funeral of the century"
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
