# Lesson plan — Bahamas — Loyalists and the plantation islands

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas — Loyalists and the plantation islands
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Bahamas, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** When America wins independence in the 1780s, Loyalists who stayed loyal to the English king flee to still-British Bahamas, plant cotton with enslaved labour, rake salt in the off-season, and build the colony's churches, schools, newspaper and law and order
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Loyalists: the king's men who remade the islands** — when America wins independence in the 1780s, Loyalists who stayed loyal to the English king flee to still-British Bahamas, plant cotton with enslaved labour, rake salt in the off-season, and build Christ Church Cathedral and St. Matthew's, start schools, and John Wells starts a newspaper (Loyalists: the king's men who remade the islands)
2. **Early Connections: the Loyalists build the colony's institutions** — Loyalists from the United States build Christ Church Cathedral, establish the first newspaper, the Gazette, and organise law and order in the community (Early Connections: the Loyalists build the colony's institutions)
