# Lesson plan — The Industrial Revolution — what changed and why England first

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Industrial Revolution — what changed and why England first
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 13 — The Modern World (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From about 1750, machines replaced and aided hand labour, and production moved from homes to factories — beginning in England, because England had the wealth, the markets, the coal and the iron.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has followed the early-modern and modern world in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Hand to machine, home to factory** — The revolution's two moves: machines do the work; factories gather the workers.
2. **Why England — and steam** — Wealth, markets, coal and iron — and a new power that works anywhere: steam.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the Industrial Revolution: date, machines replacing/aiding hand labour, home to factory.
2. Give England's three advantages: wealthy machine-buyers, favourable markets, coal and iron ore.
3. Explain steam power — boiled water, compressed steam, usable anywhere — and name Savery and Newcomen with their dates as printed.
