# Nicaragua Independence And San Jacinto — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Efemérides: birth of General José Dolores Estrada
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Estrada, a soldier from Nandaime, grows up to command the patriots at the Battle of San Jacinto

## Sub lesson 2 — Celebraciones del 14 y 15 de septiembre
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — on 14 Sept Estrada's poorly armed patriots beat Walker's filibusters at San Jacinto, Andrés Castro felling an invader with a stone; on 15 Sept the country celebrates Central American independence in blue and white

## Sub lesson 3 — Efemérides: anniversary of General José Dolores Estrada's death
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — pupils visit/read the inscription honouring "the illustrious General José Dolores Estrada", closing the San Jacinto story begun at his birth anniversary

## Sub lesson 4 — Celebraciones de hechos históricos de mi departamento (unit introduction)
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — historical events are kept alive by celebrating them; the unit frames discovery, resistance, autonomy, independence and heroes as one patriotic calendar

## Sub lesson 5 — Las fiestas patrias: Independencia de Centroamérica y Nicaragua
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the September patriotic festivals retell how Central America became independent and Nicaragua kept that freedom

## Sub lesson 6 — La Batalla de San Jacinto
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — liberal-conservative civil wars invite William Walker's filibusters; at the Acta de Matagalpa (20 Apr 1856) Nicaraguans unite across parties, and at dawn 14 Sept 1856 some 120 patriots under Estrada defeat 300 filibusters at Hacienda San Jacinto, Andrés Castro killing one with a stone

## Sub lesson 7 — Personajes históricos: Gesta de Enmanuel Mongalo
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — teacher Enmanuel Mongalo runs with a burning torch under enemy fire to set the filibusters' position alight, a schoolmaster turned hero of the Walker war

## Sub lesson 8 — Antecedentes de la Guerra Nacional: agresión inglesa y disputa EE.UU.–Inglaterra por Nicaragua
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Britain seizes Caribbean positions and the US and Britain rival over a canal route through Nicaragua, making the small country a prize

## Sub lesson 9 — Guerra civil: causas
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — liberals and conservatives fight for power and their ambitions open the door to the foreign invader

## Sub lesson 10 — Los filibusteros en Nicaragua: William Walker
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Walker arrives as a hired filibuster, makes himself president and re-legalises slavery, turning civil war into national war

## Sub lesson 11 — Desarrollo de la Guerra Nacional
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Central American armies unite against Walker and drive him out battle by battle

## Sub lesson 12 — La Batalla de San Jacinto
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — at dawn on 14 Sept 1856 Estrada's outnumbered patriots hold Hacienda San Jacinto; Andrés Castro's stone becomes the war's most famous shot

## Sub lesson 13 — Consecuencias de la Guerra Nacional y tratado Jerez-Cañas
Sub lesson 13 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the war leaves social, economic and political costs, settled partly by the Jerez-Cañas treaty fixing the Costa Rica border

## Sub lesson 14 — La unidad centroamericana frente al filibusterismo
Sub lesson 14 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Walker's filibusters, agents of Manifest Destiny aiming to "americanise" the continent, provoke the opposite — a defensive Central American unity and a shared Latin identity in the 1856-57 Campaña Nacional

## Sub lesson 15 — Participación de Centroamérica en la Guerra Nacional de Nicaragua
Sub lesson 15 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — troops from the sister republics march into Nicaragua's National War, making San Jacinto an isthmian victory

## Sub lesson 16 — La Guerra Nacional contra Walker
Sub lesson 16 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the 1854 León-Granada civil war brings Walker's filibusters; teacher Enmanuel Mongalo burns their Rivas stronghold, Matagalpa archers join the allied Central American armies, and of 2,000 filibusters barely 250 survive — Walker is shot at Trujillo in 1860

## Sub lesson 17 — Guerra Nacional: causas y la guerra civil de 1854
Sub lesson 17 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — León's exiled liberals, unable to beat Granada's conservatives, hire Byron Cole to bring in Walker's filibusters — and a civil war becomes a national one

## Sub lesson 18 — La Batalla de San Jacinto y el fin de la Guerra Nacional
Sub lesson 18 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Estrada fortifies the San Jacinto hacienda with 160 men and 66 Matagalpa indigenous archers; Andrés Castro wins his fame there, and on 1 May 1857 Walker surrenders, ending a war the book insists was fought for all Central America
