## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Efemérides: birth of General José Dolores Estrada** — Estrada, a soldier from Nandaime, grows up to command the patriots at the Battle of San Jacinto
2. **Celebraciones del 14 y 15 de septiembre** — on 14 Sept Estrada's poorly armed patriots beat Walker's filibusters at San Jacinto, Andrés Castro felling an invader with a stone; on 15 Sept the country celebrates Central American independence in blue and white
3. **Efemérides: anniversary of General José Dolores Estrada's death** — pupils visit/read the inscription honouring "the illustrious General José Dolores Estrada", closing the San Jacinto story begun at his birth anniversary
4. **Celebraciones de hechos históricos de mi departamento (unit introduction)** — historical events are kept alive by celebrating them; the unit frames discovery, resistance, autonomy, independence and heroes as one patriotic calendar
5. **Las fiestas patrias: Independencia de Centroamérica y Nicaragua** — the September patriotic festivals retell how Central America became independent and Nicaragua kept that freedom
6. **La Batalla de San Jacinto** — liberal-conservative civil wars invite William Walker's filibusters; at the Acta de Matagalpa (20 Apr 1856) Nicaraguans unite across parties, and at dawn 14 Sept 1856 some 120 patriots under Estrada defeat 300 filibusters at Hacienda San Jacinto, Andrés Castro killing one with a stone
7. **Personajes históricos: Gesta de Enmanuel Mongalo** — teacher Enmanuel Mongalo runs with a burning torch under enemy fire to set the filibusters' position alight, a schoolmaster turned hero of the Walker war
8. **Antecedentes de la Guerra Nacional: agresión inglesa y disputa EE.UU.–Inglaterra por Nicaragua** — Britain seizes Caribbean positions and the US and Britain rival over a canal route through Nicaragua, making the small country a prize
9. **Guerra civil: causas** — liberals and conservatives fight for power and their ambitions open the door to the foreign invader
10. **Los filibusteros en Nicaragua: William Walker** — Walker arrives as a hired filibuster, makes himself president and re-legalises slavery, turning civil war into national war
11. **Desarrollo de la Guerra Nacional** — Central American armies unite against Walker and drive him out battle by battle
12. **La Batalla de San Jacinto** — at dawn on 14 Sept 1856 Estrada's outnumbered patriots hold Hacienda San Jacinto; Andrés Castro's stone becomes the war's most famous shot
13. **Consecuencias de la Guerra Nacional y tratado Jerez-Cañas** — the war leaves social, economic and political costs, settled partly by the Jerez-Cañas treaty fixing the Costa Rica border
14. **La unidad centroamericana frente al filibusterismo** — Walker's filibusters, agents of Manifest Destiny aiming to "americanise" the continent, provoke the opposite — a defensive Central American unity and a shared Latin identity in the 1856-57 Campaña Nacional
15. **Participación de Centroamérica en la Guerra Nacional de Nicaragua** — troops from the sister republics march into Nicaragua's National War, making San Jacinto an isthmian victory
16. **La Guerra Nacional contra Walker** — the 1854 León-Granada civil war brings Walker's filibusters; teacher Enmanuel Mongalo burns their Rivas stronghold, Matagalpa archers join the allied Central American armies, and of 2,000 filibusters barely 250 survive — Walker is shot at Trujillo in 1860
17. **Guerra Nacional: causas y la guerra civil de 1854** — León's exiled liberals, unable to beat Granada's conservatives, hire Byron Cole to bring in Walker's filibusters — and a civil war becomes a national one
18. **La Batalla de San Jacinto y el fin de la Guerra Nacional** — Estrada fortifies the San Jacinto hacienda with 160 men and 66 Matagalpa indigenous archers; Andrés Castro wins his fame there, and on 1 May 1857 Walker surrenders, ending a war the book insists was fought for all Central America
