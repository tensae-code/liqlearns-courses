## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 13, the national great-unity bloc in Vietnamese history
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — President Hồ Chí Minh's teaching opens the lesson: "Our history teaches us this lesson: whenever our people unite as one, our country is independent and free; conversely, whenever our people are not united, the country is invaded by foreigners — so now we must know how to unite, unite quickly, unite firmly, more and more"; the bloc forms early, from the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc founding era, out of the need to unite for water control and irrigation serving agricultural production and to gather forces against foreign invasion, and the literature box reads the legend Con Rồng cháu Tiên (Children of the Dragon, Grandchildren of the Immortals) as its charter — all the ethnic groups on Vietnam's territory share one father and one mother, one stock, proof of the unity bloc from the dawn of history; it is consolidated through more than a thousand years of persistent struggle against northern feudal domination and assimilation, regaining independence, and in the autonomous feudal era the dynasties care for the bloc between the Kinh and the minority peoples through concrete policies, notably enfeoffing minority chiefs and marrying princesses to them in the border regions — the quoted chronicle entry (Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư, vol. I) records that in Giáp Tý [1144] the king marries Princess Thiều Dung to Dương Tự Minh, a Tày chief in Thái Nguyên, enfeoffs him as Phò mã lang, and in the fifth month makes Mâu Du Đô Thái sư, charged with patrolling the passes along the whole border — though when necessary the central government also uses firm measures against separatism to keep the country's unity and territorial integrity; since the Communist Party of Vietnam is founded in 1930 the bloc is consolidated, expanded and developed into one of the decisive factors of the national liberation cause and of today's building, development and defence of the Fatherland, expressed concentratedly in the Vietnam National United Front, founded on 18 November 1930 as the Hội Phản đế Đồng minh (Anti-Imperialist League) and now the Vietnam Fatherland Front (the box adds that 18 November is kept yearly as the Front's founding anniversary and the National Great Unity Festival Day); its role in building and defending the country is decisive throughout: it helps form Vietnam's first state, the Văn Lang state of the Hùng Kings, and decides the success of the resistance against the Qin invasion under Thục Phán – An Dương Vương (late 3rd–early 2nd century BCE) that forms the Âu Lạc state; the quoted research (Phan Huy Lê) follows the Hai Bà Trưng uprising — following the sisters' flag of righteousness the people of the districts and counties rise as one "smashing the châu and quận", and when the Trưng sisters take Giao Chỉ commandery, the Man Lý people of Cửu Chân, Nhật Nam and Hợp Phố all respond and 65 citadels are captured (Hậu Hán thư) — and the line runs through the resistances against the Southern Han (938), the Song (1075–1077), the Mongol–Yuan (1258, 1285, 1287–1288) and the Lam Sơn uprising against the Ming (1418–1427), the book summing up that one cause of victory is the strength of the national great-unity bloc; in the 20th-century struggle against French colonialism and US imperialism under the Party's leadership the bloc rises to a new height, contributing to national liberation and unification (Hồ Chí Minh's quoted words: "Unity is an invincible force — the force of unity helped the August Revolution succeed, the force of unity helped the resistance win..."), and the photograph shows Saigon's people rallying to welcome the City Military Management Committee on 7 May 1975; today the bloc founds the building and defence of the socialist Fatherland, mobilises the whole nation's strength for economic and cultural development and social stability, and ensures national defence and security — it is the important basis on which Vietnam overcomes the difficulties and crisis of the decade after liberation and unification, carries out Đổi mới and rises to an upper-middle-income country (the quoted figures: GDP from 346 billion USD in 2020, 37th in the world, to 510 billion USD in 2025, 32nd in the world and 4th in ASEAN, with GDP per capita up 1.4 times from 3,552 to about 5,000 USD), and the basis for the victories of the two border defence wars, in the southwest (1978) and the north (1979), for completing land-border demarcation with China and basically with Cambodia, and for protecting sovereignty and Vietnam's basic interests in the East Sea, while inter-ethnic solidarity shows itself whenever natural disasters and epidemics strike (ethnic people together supporting the Central provinces after storm no. 9 in 2020 are pictured); the lesson closes with the Party's and State's ethnic policy today — three consistent principles, Unity, Equality, and Mutual support for common development, written into the constitutions themselves (the 1946 Constitution's "Unity of the whole people without distinction of race, sex, class or religion", and Article 5 of the 2013 Constitution, quoted: the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is the unified state of all the ethnic groups living together on Vietnam's territory, equal, united, respecting and helping one another to develop together, every act of ethnic discrimination and division strictly forbidden) — and its basic contents run comprehensively over economy (developing the mountainous and minority regions and closing the gaps between regions and groups), culture (building an advanced Vietnamese culture imbued with national identity, including the values and identities of all 54 ethnic groups), society, and defence and security in the strategic areas
