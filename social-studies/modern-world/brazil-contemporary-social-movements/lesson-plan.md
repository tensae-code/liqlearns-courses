# Lesson plan — Brazil — contemporary social movements

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Brazil — contemporary social movements
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Brazil, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From 1968's slogan 'is it forbidden to forbid?' to Brazil's own movements, students, workers, women and Black and landless movements contest power in the pupils' own lifetime.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1968 — 'is it forbidden to forbid?'** — The book opens in 1968, with the slogan of a generation: 'is it forbidden to forbid?' Students in the streets questioned every authority that told them what they could not do, say or be. The chapter carries that question into the pupils' own lifetime.
2. **Brazil's own movements** — Then the story comes home. In Brazil, social, political and cultural movements contest power in the pupils' own lifetime: students, workers and women — and Black movements and landless movements, each pressing its own claim on the country's future.
3. **Movements as power contested** — The book teaches movements as power being contested from below: ordinary people organising together to challenge who decides. The chapter closes the book's long arc — work, power and culture — with power still being argued over, in the streets of the pupils' own country and time.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Domination and resistance in the modern West; State power and violence. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the 1968 slogan the chapter opens with, and what it questioned.
2. Name the movements the book follows in Brazil — students, workers, women, and Black and landless movements.
3. Say what movements do, in the book's teaching — how they contest power.
