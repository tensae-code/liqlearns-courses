# Brazil — contemporary social movements

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From 1968's slogan 'is it forbidden to forbid?' to Brazil's own movements, students, workers, women and Black and landless movements contest power in the pupils' own lifetime. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1968 — 'is it forbidden to forbid?'

The book opens in 1968, with the slogan of a generation: 'is it forbidden to forbid?' Students in the streets questioned every authority that told them what they could not do, say or be. The chapter carries that question into the pupils' own lifetime.

## Sub lesson 2 — Brazil's own movements

Then the story comes home. In Brazil, social, political and cultural movements contest power in the pupils' own lifetime: students, workers and women — and Black movements and landless movements, each pressing its own claim on the country's future.

## Sub lesson 3 — Movements as power contested

The book teaches movements as power being contested from below: ordinary people organising together to challenge who decides. The chapter closes the book's long arc — work, power and culture — with power still being argued over, in the streets of the pupils' own country and time.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of Brazil — contemporary social movements in your own words, part by part, using the sub lesson headings as your beats. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
