# Lesson plan — Life in Nazi Germany, 1933 to 1939

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Life in Nazi Germany, 1933 to 1939
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (United Kingdom, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Life in Nazi Germany reshapes women, the young, work and living standards to fit the regime.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **10.1 The Nazi view of the role of women** — Kinder, Küche, Kirche — the regime's ideal woman marries early, bears many 'Aryan' children and leaves work and politics to men
2. **10.2 The changing role of women** — Marriage loans and the Mother's Cross push the birth rate up, yet by the late 1930s labour shortage quietly draws women back into work the ideology said was not theirs
3. **11.1 Control of the young through education** — Rust's ministry Nazifies schools — race science and maths problems about 'aliens', Napola and Adolf Hitler Schools for the elite, teachers sworn to the regime
4. **11.2 The Hitler Youth** — All rival youth groups are banned and membership becomes compulsory; seven million boys drill for soldiering and girls of the League of German Maidens train for motherhood — though many quietly stop turning up
5. **12.1 Policies to reduce unemployment** — The Reich Labour Service, autobahn building and rearmament erase the six million unemployed — with Jews and women struck off the count and conscription absorbing the young
6. **12.2 Changes in the standard of living** — Strength through Joy holidays and the promised Volkswagen sweeten workers' lives while wages are frozen, unions gone and hours lengthen — better off is judged both ways
