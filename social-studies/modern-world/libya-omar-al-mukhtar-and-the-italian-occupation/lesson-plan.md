# Lesson plan — Libya — Omar al-Mukhtar and the Italian occupation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Libya — Omar al-Mukhtar and the Italian occupation
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Omar al-Mukhtar's guerrilla war holds the Jabal al-Akhdar against Italy until 1931; Libya wins independence in 1951, falls to Gaddafi in 1969, and rises in February 2011.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Libya — Omar al-Mukhtar and the Italian occupation** — Italy invaded in 1911 and the Ottoman state ceded Libya at Ouchy in 1912; the Libyans formed the Tripoli government under Sulayman al-Baruni, Ahmad al-Sharif al-Senussi beat the Italians at Derna in 1913, and Italy recognised the Tripolitanian Republic in 1918; from 1921 Omar al-Mukhtar led the guerrilla war from the Jabal al-Akhdar until Italy walled the Egyptian border, captured him and executed him in 1931; the United Nations granted independence in 1949, Idris became king in 1951, Gaddafi's coup of 1969 brought a tyranny of prisons and the Abu Salim massacre, and the popular revolution of February 2011 ended his rule and his life.
