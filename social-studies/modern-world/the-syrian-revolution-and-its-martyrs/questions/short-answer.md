# The Syrian revolution and its martyrs — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: The martyrs of the Syrian revolution.
**Answer: Syria declared its independence from French colonialism in 1946 and stayed free until Hafez al-Assad's military coup of 1970 brought plunder, prisons, killing and displacement on its people; the lesson studies a photograph of the funeral of the martyrs of the Khalidiya massacre in Homs, and the class documents the Syrian revolution's martyrs and the regime's violations against children, women, neighbourhoods and schools in a report for the United Nations and the Red Cross. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Syria's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *الدراسات الاجتماعية — الصف الرابع (Social Studies, Grade 4)* (Grade 4), Unit 5 المواطنة, Lesson الشهداء (The martyrs), printed p. 107.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Fifty years of rule — and the revolution of 2011.
**Answer: Hafez al-Assad's coup of 16 November 1970 began some thirty years of despotism — the massacres of Jisr al-Shughur, Aleppo and Hama in 1982, where some 40,000 were killed, and tens of thousands disappeared in the prisons; the rule was inherited by his son Bashar in 2000; when the children of Daraa were arrested and tortured for writing freedom slogans, the revolution of March 2011 rose, the regime answered with every weapon including chemical weapons, and the story ends with the liberation of all Syria's land and the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime on 8 December 2024. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Syria's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *التاريخ — الصف التاسع (History, Grade 9)* (Grade 9), Unit 1, Lesson سورية منذ الاستقلال وحتى الوقت الحاضر (Syria from independence to the present), printed pp. 30–32.**
