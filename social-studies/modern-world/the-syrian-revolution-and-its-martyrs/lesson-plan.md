# Lesson plan — The Syrian revolution and its martyrs

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Syrian revolution and its martyrs
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From Hafez al-Assad's 1970 coup through the massacres and prisons to the revolution of March 2011 — Syria's story ends, in these books, with the regime's fall on 8 December 2024.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The martyrs of the Syrian revolution** — Syria declared its independence from French colonialism in 1946 and stayed free until Hafez al-Assad's military coup of 1970 brought plunder, prisons, killing and displacement on its people; the lesson studies a photograph of the funeral of the martyrs of the Khalidiya massacre in Homs, and the class documents the Syrian revolution's martyrs and the regime's violations against children, women, neighbourhoods and schools in a report for the United Nations and the Red Cross.

2. **Fifty years of rule — and the revolution of 2011** — Hafez al-Assad's coup of 16 November 1970 began some thirty years of despotism — the massacres of Jisr al-Shughur, Aleppo and Hama in 1982, where some 40,000 were killed, and tens of thousands disappeared in the prisons; the rule was inherited by his son Bashar in 2000; when the children of Daraa were arrested and tortured for writing freedom slogans, the revolution of March 2011 rose, the regime answered with every weapon including chemical weapons, and the story ends with the liberation of all Syria's land and the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime on 8 December 2024.
