# The Syrian revolution and its martyrs

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From Hafez al-Assad's 1970 coup through the massacres and prisons to the revolution of March 2011 — Syria's story ends, in these books, with the regime's fall on 8 December 2024. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The martyrs of the Syrian revolution

Syria declared its independence from French colonialism in 1946 and stayed free until Hafez al-Assad's military coup of 1970 brought plunder, prisons, killing and displacement on its people; the lesson studies a photograph of the funeral of the martyrs of the Khalidiya massacre in Homs, and the class documents the Syrian revolution's martyrs and the regime's violations against children, women, neighbourhoods and schools in a report for the United Nations and the Red Cross.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fifty years of rule — and the revolution of 2011

Hafez al-Assad's coup of 16 November 1970 began some thirty years of despotism — the massacres of Jisr al-Shughur, Aleppo and Hama in 1982, where some 40,000 were killed, and tens of thousands disappeared in the prisons; the rule was inherited by his son Bashar in 2000; when the children of Daraa were arrested and tortured for writing freedom slogans, the revolution of March 2011 rose, the regime answered with every weapon including chemical weapons, and the story ends with the liberation of all Syria's land and the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime on 8 December 2024.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: The martyrs of the Syrian revolution; Fifty years of rule — and the revolution of 2011. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
