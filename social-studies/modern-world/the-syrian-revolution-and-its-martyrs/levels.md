# The Syrian revolution and its martyrs — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — The martyrs of the Syrian revolution
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Syria declared its independence from French colonialism in 1946 and stayed free until Hafez al-Assad's military coup of 1970 brought plunder, prisons, killing and displacement on its people; the lesson studies a photograph of the funeral of the martyrs of the Khalidiya massacre in Homs, and the class documents the Syrian revolution's martyrs and the regime's violations against children, women, neighbourhoods and schools in a report for the United Nations and the Red Cross.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fifty years of rule — and the revolution of 2011
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Hafez al-Assad's coup of 16 November 1970 began some thirty years of despotism — the massacres of Jisr al-Shughur, Aleppo and Hama in 1982, where some 40,000 were killed, and tens of thousands disappeared in the prisons; the rule was inherited by his son Bashar in 2000; when the children of Daraa were arrested and tortured for writing freedom slogans, the revolution of March 2011 rose, the regime answered with every weapon including chemical weapons, and the story ends with the liberation of all Syria's land and the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime on 8 December 2024.
