# Lesson plan — The Cyprus Question

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Cyprus Question
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Greece, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Cyprus's Greeks struggle for enosis through EOKA's campaign, win a fragile independence in 1960, and face invasion and division in 1974
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit E, ch. 11 The Cyprus question** — Cyprus's Greeks struggle for enosis through EOKA's campaign, win a fragile independence in 1960, and face invasion and division in 1974
2. **§54b The Cyprus struggle (1955–1960)** — EOKA's campaign against Britain ends in Zurich-London independence — a republic neither enosis nor partition intended
3. **The Cypriot struggle (1955–1960)** — Greek Cypriots fight British rule for enosis and settle for an independence nobody designed to last
4. **The Cyprus Problem** — the EOKA struggle, the 1960 republic, intercommunal collapse, and the 1974 coup and Turkish invasion that divide the island to this day
