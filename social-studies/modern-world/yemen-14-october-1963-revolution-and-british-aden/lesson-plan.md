# Lesson plan — Revolution of 14 October

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Revolution of 14 October
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Yemen, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Britain seizes Aden on 19 January 1839 and splits the south into sultanates and protectorates to rule it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Revolution of 14 October** — britain seizes Aden on 19 January 1839 and splits the south into sultanates and protectorates to rule it, until the first spark flies from the Radfan mountains on 14 October 1963 under Sheikh Rajeh bin Ghaleb Labouza, martyred that first day, and the struggle drives the British out on 30 November 1967
2. **British ambitions in Yemen** — britain, holding India — the jewel of its crown, taken from the French after the Seven Years War — needs the road to it guarded, and Yemen commands that road: Bab al-Mandab, revived by the Suez Canal in 1869, the watchtower islands of Perim, Kamaran and Socotra, and Aden, the finest servicing port on the way east, where the first British ship called in 1609; fearing Napoleon's drive from Egypt toward India, Britain seizes Perim in 1799, signs with the Sultan of Lahj in 1802, and in 1839, on the pretext of a looted Indian ship, the Daria Dawlat, occupies Aden itself and spreads over the southern protectorates
3. **The Yemeni Revolution: the South (Unit 2 Lesson 4)** — against Britain's divide-and-rule in Aden, the first spark of armed revolution rises from the Radfan mountains on 14 October 1963, joining armed struggle, workers' and students' marches and diplomacy until the evacuation of 30 November 1967 raises the republic over more than 23 sultanates and sheikhdoms Britain had kept divided
