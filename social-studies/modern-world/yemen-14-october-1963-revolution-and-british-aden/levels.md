# Revolution of 14 October — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Revolution of 14 October
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — britain seizes Aden on 19 January 1839 and splits the south into sultanates and protectorates to rule it, until the first spark flies from the Radfan mountains on 14 October 1963 under Sheikh Rajeh bin Ghaleb Labouza, martyred that first day, and the struggle drives the British out on 30 November 1967

## Sub lesson 2 — British ambitions in Yemen
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — britain, holding India — the jewel of its crown, taken from the French after the Seven Years War — needs the road to it guarded, and Yemen commands that road: Bab al-Mandab, revived by the Suez Canal in 1869, the watchtower islands of Perim, Kamaran and Socotra, and Aden, the finest servicing port on the way east, where the first British ship called in 1609; fearing Napoleon's drive from Egypt toward India, Britain seizes Perim in 1799, signs with the Sultan of Lahj in 1802, and in 1839, on the pretext of a looted Indian ship, the Daria Dawlat, occupies Aden itself and spreads over the southern protectorates

## Sub lesson 3 — The Yemeni Revolution: the South (Unit 2 Lesson 4)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — against Britain's divide-and-rule in Aden, the first spark of armed revolution rises from the Radfan mountains on 14 October 1963, joining armed struggle, workers' and students' marches and diplomacy until the evacuation of 30 November 1967 raises the republic over more than 23 sultanates and sheikhdoms Britain had kept divided
