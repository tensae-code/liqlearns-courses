# The Renaissance — what it was

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — from dark age to rebirth is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — From Dark Age to rebirth

From the **5th to the 14th century**, ancient civilisation **declined in Europe**: the book calls this the **Dark Age**, an age **without civilisation or social change**. Then the turn. From the **early 14th century**, the civilisations of **ancient Greece and Rome**, **with new values added**, began to **revive**. That revival is the **Renaissance** — a rebirth. It was not a movement in one field only: it brought **great change to literature, art, politics, science, and social and economic thought**. And its deepest shift was in what scholars studied. Medieval scholars had tied their thinking to **religion and divine things**. Renaissance scholars made **human nature and the created, visible world** their field of study — people, and the world you can see and test, moved to the centre.

## Sub lesson 2 — Italy first — then Europe

The Renaissance appeared **first in Italy** — and the book gives the reason: Italy was the **centre of ancient Roman civilisation** and lay **close to Greece**, so its **remains stirred emulation** — people living among Rome's ruins wanted to match what Rome had done. From Italy it **spread to France, Germany, England, Holland and elsewhere**. Its fruits show how wide it reached: scholars' **research participation grew across politics, sport, music, art and science**; research turned to **people's daily challenges** rather than religious and divine ideas; and the **vernacular languages** — the languages people actually spoke — **grew into literary languages**, fit for books, not only Latin.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grade 11 depth: The Renaissance in depth — humanism, art and the press

Italy's rebirth receives the Grade 11 depth: humanism returning to classical learning and putting human capability at the centre; art reborn in Leonardo and Michelangelo; and Gutenberg's printing press multiplying every book — and every idea — a hundredfold. From Italy the movement spreads north, carrying its questions into schools, churches and courts across Europe.
