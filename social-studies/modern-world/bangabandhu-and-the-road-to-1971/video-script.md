# Video script — Bangabandhu and the road to 1971

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bangladesh book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Grade 7's Bangabandhu travels first: Mujib journeys Bengal's waterways to reach its people.

Then the milestones: the 6-Point movement of 1966, the mass uprising of 1969, the election victory of 1970.

And on 7 March, the declaration — the struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation.

Grade 8 starts the road in Tungipara, 1920, where Sheikh Mujib is born.

As a student leader he fights the Urdu-only policy from 1948 — and is expelled and jailed for it.

In February 1952, in prison, he hunger-strikes as the Language Movement ignites. The road to 1971 begins with language.

Grade 9 asks what Mujib stood for. Its answer: love for all people, regardless of language, religion or caste — and nation before self.

The book roots those ideals in Bengal's own thousand-year humanitarian tradition.

Pupils meet them as case studies at the Bangabandhu Mela — a fair of stories about the man and the spirit he carried.

Grade 9 ends its political history at the road's opening. Pakistan tries to impose Urdu on a Bengali-speaking people.

Dhaka University students protest from December 1947, and Dhirendranath Dutta defies the Constituent Assembly.

The book's verdict: this language protest opens the road that ends in 1971.
