# Lesson plan — Bangabandhu and the road to 1971

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Bangabandhu and the road to 1971
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Bangladesh, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one man's road carries a nation's: from the Urdu-only fight of 1948 and the hunger strike of February 1952, through the 6-Point movement of 1966, the mass uprising of 1969 and the 1970 election victory, to the 7 March declaration — 'the struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation'.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman — the 6 Points, 1969, 1970 and 7 March** — Mujib, who travelled Bengal's waterways to reach its people, leads the 6-Point movement of 1966, the mass uprising of 1969 and the 1970 election victory, and declares on 7 March that 'the struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation'
2. **Mujib's early struggle — Urdu-only, jail and the hunger strike of 1952** — born in Tungipara in 1920, student leader Sheikh Mujib fights the Urdu-only policy from 1948, is expelled and jailed, and hunger-strikes in prison in February 1952 as the Language Movement ignites
3. **Humanitarian ideals and secular spirit** — Mujib's love for all people regardless of language, religion or caste, and his rule of nation before self, are traced to Bengal's own thousand-year humanitarian tradition and presented in pupil case studies at the Bangabandhu Mela
4. **From Pakistan to the Language Movement** — the new state of Pakistan tries to impose Urdu; Dhaka University students protest from December 1947 and Dhirendranath Dutta defies the Constituent Assembly, opening the road that ends in 1971

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the road's milestones: 6-Point movement 1966, mass uprising 1969, election victory 1970, 7 March declaration.
2. Say how Mujib reached Bengal's people: travelling its waterways.
3. Say what the 7 March declaration announced: the struggle for emancipation.
4. Say where and when Mujib was born: Tungipara, 1920.
5. Say what he fought from 1948: the Urdu-only policy.
6. Say what he did in prison in February 1952: hunger-struck, as the Language Movement ignited.
7. Name Mujib's ideals as the book gives them: love for all people regardless of language, religion or caste; nation before self.
8. Say where the book roots those ideals: Bengal's thousand-year humanitarian tradition.
9. Say how pupils meet them: case studies at the Bangabandhu Mela.
10. Say when Dhaka University students began protesting: December 1947.
11. Name who defied the Constituent Assembly: Dhirendranath Dutta.
12. Explain the book's verdict: the Language Movement opens the road that ends in 1971.
