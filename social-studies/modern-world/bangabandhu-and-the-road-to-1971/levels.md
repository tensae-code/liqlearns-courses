# Bangabandhu and the road to 1971 — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman — the 6 Points, 1969, 1970 and 7 March
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Name the road's milestones: 6-Point movement 1966, mass uprising 1969, election victory 1970, 7 March declaration. Say how Mujib reached Bengal's people: travelling its waterways. Say what the 7 March declaration announced: the struggle for emancipation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mujib's early struggle — Urdu-only, jail and the hunger strike of 1952
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Say where and when Mujib was born: Tungipara, 1920. Say what he fought from 1948: the Urdu-only policy. Say what he did in prison in February 1952: hunger-struck, as the Language Movement ignited.

## Sub lesson 3 — Humanitarian ideals and secular spirit
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Name Mujib's ideals as the book gives them: love for all people regardless of language, religion or caste; nation before self. Say where the book roots those ideals: Bengal's thousand-year humanitarian tradition. Say how pupils meet them: case studies at the Bangabandhu Mela.

## Sub lesson 4 — From Pakistan to the Language Movement
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Say when Dhaka University students began protesting: December 1947. Name who defied the Constituent Assembly: Dhirendranath Dutta. Explain the book's verdict: the Language Movement opens the road that ends in 1971.
