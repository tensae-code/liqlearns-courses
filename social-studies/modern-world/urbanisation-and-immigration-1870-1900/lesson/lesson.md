# Urbanisation and immigration, 1870–1900

## Do it first

Cities swelled faster than they could govern themselves. Migrants and immigrants remade them, reformers fought the chaos — and a new America learned to see itself. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cities swell faster than they can govern themselves

Cities swelled faster than they could govern themselves — housing, water, disease and corruption all outrunning city hall. The book calls them growing pains: the pain was real, and so was the growth.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Great Migration and new immigration remake the cities

Two great movements remade the cities: the African American Great Migration out of the South, and new European immigration across the Atlantic. New languages, faiths and foods — and new tensions — filled the swelling streets.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reformers fight the chaos; thinkers reflect a changed America

Reformers fought the chaos — cleaning, housing, governing. And a new generation of writers and thinkers reflected a changed America back to itself, asking what the city was doing to the nation's character.
