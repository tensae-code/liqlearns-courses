# Whalers from across the sea

## Do it first

Picture the Arctic shore your community has always hunted from. One season, a sail appears that no one recognises — a ship bigger than anything your camp has ever built, from a country across the sea. Before you read on, say in your mind: why would such a ship come all this way to your cold waters — and what could its coming change? Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## The story

The Nunavut reader's history of whaling turns a page here. For long ages the hunters in Arctic waters were Inuit crews, taking whales the old way, for the community that shared each catch.

Then foreign commercial whaling ships arrived in Arctic waters. These whalers had crossed the sea to hunt whales in large numbers — not to feed one camp, but for trade: for whale oil, and for baleen, the great flexible plates in the whale's mouth, both valuable goods in their home countries far away.

Inuit life was changed by what followed. The ships brought trade — new goods, new dealings, a new presence in Inuit waters and Inuit life. And the hunt at that scale brought depletion: with so many whales taken, the whales grew few. A people whose food, light and tools had always come from the whale now faced a sea with far fewer whales in it.

That is the turn in the story: the whale did not change, and the Inuit did not change their need of it — what changed was who else was hunting, how many they took, and what the taking left behind.
In your mind's eye: a tall commercial whaling ship in Arctic waters. Point to the ship and say how it differs from an open hunting boat.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story in order: foreign ships arrive in Arctic waters; they hunt whales in large numbers for oil and baleen; Inuit life is changed by the trade — and by the depletion, as the whales grow few. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Say who the whalers from across the sea were, and the two things they hunted whales for.
2. Write the contrast in one sentence each: the old Inuit hunt, and the ships' hunt.
3. Name the two ways Inuit life was changed: the trade, and the depletion.
