# Lesson plan — Whalers from across the sea

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Whalers from across the sea
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Canada, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Foreign commercial whaling ships arrived in Arctic waters and hunted whales in large numbers for oil and baleen, and Inuit life was changed by the trade and the depletion that followed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lesson before it in this story — the traditional Inuit hunt — but it also stands alone: new ships arrive from far away, hunt far more, and the whales grow few. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Inuit whaling — the traditional hunt. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say who the whalers from across the sea were and why they came.
2. Say what they hunted whales for — oil and baleen — and how their hunt differed from the old Inuit hunt.
3. Say how Inuit life was changed: by the trade, and by the depletion of the whales.

## If the learner is stuck
If the learner loses the thread, return to the contrast: the old hunt took a whale for the camp that shared it; the ships took whales in large numbers for goods to sell far away — and the whales grew few.
