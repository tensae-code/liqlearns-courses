# Hands-on — European whaling in the Pacific

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Two ways of meeting a whale
Draw two panels. In the first, draw a stranded whale on a shore with islanders taking its teeth for a chief's necklace. In the second, draw a whaling fleet's ship hunting a mother whale and her calf. Under each panel, write one sentence: what did people take, and what happened to the whales?

## A century on a line
Draw a long line. Mark one end "for centuries — stranded whales only" and the other "one century — hunting mothers and calves". Put a whale that grows smaller along the line, then, past a mark labelled "legal protection", a whale slowly growing again. Say out loud what changed at each mark.

## The chief's necklace
Make a necklace from paper teeth. While you make it, say where the teeth in a real chief's necklace came from — a stranded whale — and why that way of taking left the living whales safe.
