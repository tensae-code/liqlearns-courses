# Lesson plan — European whaling in the Pacific

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** European whaling in the Pacific
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Tuvalu, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pacific people used only stranded whales, taking teeth for chiefs' necklaces, until North American whaling fleets reached Tonga in the 1890s and hunted mothers and calves — an animal the islands had lived beside safely for centuries was driven near extinction in one century of commercial hunting, then slowly recovered under legal protection.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: for centuries the islands took only what the sea stranded, and the whales lived; in one century of hunting for sale, mothers and calves were taken and the whales nearly vanished. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Whales before the fleets** — Pacific people used only stranded whales, taking teeth for chiefs' necklaces.
2. **The whaling fleets come** — North American whaling fleets reached Tonga in the 1890s and hunted mothers and calves.
3. **Near extinction, then protection** — commercial hunting drove the whales near extinction in one century; under legal protection they slowly recover.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Pacific voyaging and island exchange, and Heritage — what it is and why it matters. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how Pacific people used whales before the fleets came — only stranded whales, teeth for chiefs' necklaces.
2. Say who the new hunters were and when they reached Tonga — North American whaling fleets, in the 1890s.
3. Say what the fleets hunted — mothers and calves.
4. Explain, in their own words, how an animal lived beside safely for centuries was driven near extinction in one century, and what let it slowly recover — legal protection.
