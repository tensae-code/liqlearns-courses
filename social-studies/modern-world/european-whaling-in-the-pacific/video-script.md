# Video script — European whaling in the Pacific

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the Pacific whales, told as the Tuvalu reader tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

For centuries, the peoples of the Pacific lived beside the whales — and the whales were safe. If a whale was stranded, washed up on the shore, people used it. Its teeth were precious: they were made into necklaces for chiefs. But no one hunted the living whales in the sea.

Then, in the 1890s, big whaling fleets from North America reached Tonga. These hunters did not wait for stranded whales. They hunted the whales themselves — even mothers swimming with their calves.

In about one century of hunting like that — hunting to sell — the whales were driven near extinction. An animal that had lived safely beside the islands for centuries nearly disappeared.

Only when laws protected the whales did the story turn. Slowly, under legal protection, the whales are recovering.
