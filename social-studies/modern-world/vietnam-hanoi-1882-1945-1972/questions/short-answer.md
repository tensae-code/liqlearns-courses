# Vietnam Hanoi 1882 1945 1972 — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 12, three days that made modern Hanoi.
**Answer: governor Hoàng Diệu dies defending the citadel from the French in 1882; Hồ Chí Minh reads the Declaration of Independence at Ba Đình in 1945; Hanoi's people shoot down US bombers in the twelve days of December 1972, the "Điện Biên Phủ in the air" (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Vietnam's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Lịch sử và Địa lí 4 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* (Vietnam), Bài 12, three days that made modern Hanoi, printed p. 57.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 17, the two French descents on Hanoi and the Cầu Giấy victories, 1873–1884.
**Answer: in November 1873 Francis Garnier storms Hanoi; Governor Nguyễn Tri Phương, wounded and captured, starves himself to death to keep his honour; on 21 December the court army and Lưu Vĩnh Phúc's Black Flags ambush the French at Cầu Giấy and kill Garnier; the Treaty of Giáp Tuất (1874) gives France all six southern provinces — and, the quoted history warns, the bases from which it will return; in April 1882 Henri Rivière takes Hanoi after Governor Hoàng Diệu's suicide in the Võ Miếu garden, spreads over the delta, and on 19 May 1883 is ambushed and killed at Cầu Giấy in his turn; France answers by storming Thuận An at Huế's sea gate and imposing the Harmand treaty (1883) and the Patenôtre treaty of 6 June 1884 — the protectorate (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Vietnam's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Lịch sử và Địa lí 8 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* (Vietnam), Bài 17, the two French descents on Hanoi and the Cầu Giấy victories, 1873–1884, printed p. 80.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 17, the North under the air war.
**Answer: on the Gulf of Tonkin pretext (5 August 1964) and the "retaliation" for Plây-ku (7 February 1965) America opens its first destruction war on the North; in over four years the North shoots down 3,243 aircraft including 6 B-52s and sinks 143 warships while keeping transport moving — the book pauses at Đồng Lộc junction, where ten young women volunteers died keeping the Hồ Chí Minh Trail's blood vessel open (the site a national relic since 1989); America halts the bombing on 1 November 1968, resumes a second destruction war on 16 April 1972, and loses its twelve-day B-52 campaign against Hanoi and Hải Phòng (18–29 December 1972) — "Điện Biên Phủ in the air" — which forces it back to sign the Paris Agreement (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
