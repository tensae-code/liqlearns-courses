# Lesson plan — Venezuela The 1902 Blockade And Drago Doctrine

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Venezuela The 1902 Blockade And Drago Doctrine
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Venezuela, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** European fleets blockade the ports to collect debts by force; the Drago Doctrine answers that public debt can never justify armed intervention, settled at Washington in 1903
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The blockade of Venezuelan ports, 1902–1903** — European fleets blockade the ports to collect debts by force; the Drago Doctrine answers that public debt can never justify armed intervention, settled at Washington in 1903
2. **A December of cannon fire: 1902** — in December 1902 foreign warships blockade Venezuela violently over its debts, and a child of the time hears the guns
3. **The blockade of Venezuelan coasts, 1902** — British, German and Italian warships blockade the coasts to collect debts by force, the crisis that produces the Drago answer
