# Venezuela The 1902 Blockade And Drago Doctrine — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: The blockade of Venezuelan ports, 1902–1903.
**Answer: European fleets blockade the ports to collect debts by force; the Drago Doctrine answers that public debt can never justify armed intervention, settled at Washington in 1903 (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Venezuela's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Historia de Venezuela Contemporánea — Ciencias Sociales, 4to año* (Venezuela), The blockade of Venezuelan ports, 1902–1903, printed pp. 51–52.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: A December of cannon fire: 1902.
**Answer: in December 1902 foreign warships blockade Venezuela violently over its debts, and a child of the time hears the guns (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Venezuela's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Viva Venezuela — Ciencias Sociales, 3er grado* (Venezuela), A December of cannon fire: 1902, printed p. 90.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: The blockade of Venezuelan coasts, 1902.
**Answer: British, German and Italian warships blockade the coasts to collect debts by force, the crisis that produces the Drago answer (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** In Venezuela's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Nuestra Historia Republicana — Ciencias Sociales, 2do año* (Venezuela), The blockade of Venezuelan coasts, 1902, printed pp. 64–66.**
