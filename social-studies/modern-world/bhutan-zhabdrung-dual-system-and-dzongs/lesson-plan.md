# Lesson plan — Bhutan — the Zhabdrung's dual system and dzongs

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Bhutan — the Zhabdrung's dual system and dzongs
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Bhutan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the unifier codifies a distinct Bhutanese Drukpa Kagyu order, national dress and driglam namzha, laws, taxes and the great dzongs, then in 1651 sets up the Chhoe-sid dual system — Je Khenpo for religion, Desi for temporal rule — that outlives his death that same year; in his new home the exile builds the nation state Palden Druk, raising fortified dzongs region by region as joint seats of monk body and administration under the dual system; proclaimed supreme ruler around 1627, the Zhabdrung pairs the spiritual law 'like a silken knot' that tightens with bad deeds with the secular law 'like a golden yoke' that grows heavier with wrongs, and in 1651 publishes the Kathrim code — Je Khenpo for religion, Desi for politics, three Ponlops and a Lhengye Tshog council below
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
