# Rights and Duties at School and in the Community — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: 1.6 Let us do our duties and accept our responsibilities while enjoying our rights — a pupil's rights in school (education, developing one's talents, security, equality, expressing ideas, association, safeguarding one's identity), the free provision that upholds them, and the duties that go with each right — including treating a diverse school community with brotherhood (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: 1.7 Implementing duties between teachers and students — the two-way duties of the classroom — pupils' duties by their teachers (commitment, obedience, respect) and teachers' duties by their pupils (a good education, safety, guidance, spotting and sharpening talents), and pupils' responsibilities to their peers (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: 2.3 Duties of the institutions that provide us with services, and duties of us who receive services — the two sides of public service — providers must serve properly, efficiently, fairly and without bias; receivers must pay taxes correctly, protect the institutions' property and not obtain services by improper means (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way — civics starts inside the child's own school — its history, its people and their duties, its rules, symbols and the pupil's own rights — then widens to the region (the Grama Niladhari ladder up to the province, the officers and councils that serve it) and closes with the good citizen's virtues: polite speech, listening, discipline, telling good from bad, using resources frugally, guarding public property and preserving the local identity with pride. (any close wording).**
