# Video script — Rights and Duties at School and in the Community

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Sri Lanka Grade 6 Civic Education (printed p. 30), teaches: A pupil's rights in school (education, developing one's talents, security, equality, expressing ideas, association, safeguarding one's identity), the free provision that upholds them, and the duties that go with each right — including treating a diverse school community with brotherhood.

Sri Lanka Grade 6 Civic Education (printed p. 32), teaches: The two-way duties of the classroom — pupils' duties by their teachers (commitment, obedience, respect) and teachers' duties by their pupils (a good education, safety, guidance, spotting and sharpening talents), and pupils' responsibilities to their peers.

Sri Lanka Grade 6 Civic Education (printed p. 58), teaches: The two sides of public service — providers must serve properly, efficiently, fairly and without bias; receivers must pay taxes correctly, protect the institutions' property and not obtain services by improper means.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 Civic Education): civics starts inside the child's own school — its history, its people and their duties, its rules, symbols and the pupil's own rights — then widens to the region (the Grama Niladhari ladder up to the province, the officers and councils that serve it) and closes with the good citizen's virtues: polite speech, listening, discipline, telling good from bad, using resources frugally, guarding public property and preserving the local identity with pride.
