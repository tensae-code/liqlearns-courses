# Hands-on — Rights and Duties at School and in the Community

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## 1.6 Let us do our duties and accept our responsibilities while enjoying our rights
Work through this part with your hands: a pupil's rights in school (education, developing one's talents, security, equality, expressing ideas, association, safeguarding one's identity), the free provision that upholds them, and the duties that go with each right — including treating a diverse school community with brotherhood. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## 1.7 Implementing duties between teachers and students
Work through this part with your hands: the two-way duties of the classroom — pupils' duties by their teachers (commitment, obedience, respect) and teachers' duties by their pupils (a good education, safety, guidance, spotting and sharpening talents), and pupils' responsibilities to their peers. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## 2.3 Duties of the institutions that provide us with services, and duties of us who receive services
Work through this part with your hands: the two sides of public service — providers must serve properly, efficiently, fairly and without bias; receivers must pay taxes correctly, protect the institutions' property and not obtain services by improper means. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: civics starts inside the child's own school — its history, its people and their duties, its rules, symbols and the pupil's own rights — then widens to the region (the Grama Niladhari ladder up to the province, the officers and councils that serve it) and closes with the good citizen's virtues: polite speech, listening, discipline, telling good from bad, using resources frugally, guarding public property and preserving the local identity with pride.. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
