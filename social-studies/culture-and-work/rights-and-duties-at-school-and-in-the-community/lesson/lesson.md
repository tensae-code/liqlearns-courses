# Rights and Duties at School and in the Community

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a pupil's rights in school (education, developing one's talents, security, equality, expressing ideas, association, safeguarding one's identity), the free provision that upholds them, and the duties that go with each right — including treating a diverse school community with brotherhood — as Sri Lanka's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1.6 Let us do our duties and accept our responsibilities while enjoying our rights

Sri Lanka Grade 6 Civic Education, printed p. 30, teaches: A pupil's rights in school (education, developing one's talents, security, equality, expressing ideas, association, safeguarding one's identity), the free provision that upholds them, and the duties that go with each right — including treating a diverse school community with brotherhood. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 1.7 Implementing duties between teachers and students

Sri Lanka Grade 6 Civic Education, printed p. 32, teaches: The two-way duties of the classroom — pupils' duties by their teachers (commitment, obedience, respect) and teachers' duties by their pupils (a good education, safety, guidance, spotting and sharpening talents), and pupils' responsibilities to their peers. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — 2.3 Duties of the institutions that provide us with services, and duties of us who receive services

Sri Lanka Grade 6 Civic Education, printed p. 58, teaches: The two sides of public service — providers must serve properly, efficiently, fairly and without bias; receivers must pay taxes correctly, protect the institutions' property and not obtain services by improper means. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way

Sri Lanka's own way: civics starts inside the child's own school — its history, its people and their duties, its rules, symbols and the pupil's own rights — then widens to the region (the Grama Niladhari ladder up to the province, the officers and councils that serve it) and closes with the good citizen's virtues: polite speech, listening, discipline, telling good from bad, using resources frugally, guarding public property and preserving the local identity with pride. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
