# Video script — Features of Pakistani Society and Culture

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Pakistan's Grade 10 PS10 (printed pp. 114–116); Grade 12 PS12-2014 (printed pp. 85–87); Grade 10 PS10-KPK (printed pp. 102–106), teaches: Ch. 6 Salient Features of Pakistani Society and Culture, Ch. 6 Salient Features of Pakistani Culture: Mixed Culture; Religious Uniformity, Ch. 4 Basic Features of Pakistani Society and the Major Social Problems — teaches: the thirteen features the book itself lists — Islamic culture's colours, the joint family system, customs and traditions, religious harmony, festivals, dress and food, mixed culture, urs and fairs, sports, minority rights, hospitality, architecture and painting, poetry and literature; Pakistan's culture is a mixture — peoples from Iran, Arabia, Turkey, Central Asia, Greece and Europe each left traditions, food and ways of life — held together by one dominant Islamic colour and a living religious cooperation in which minorities lead safe and honorable lives; the society's features stated plainly, then its problems named without flinching — social stratification by creed and caste, feudalism, the curse of dowry, drug trafficking and smuggling, terrorism, and child labour, "not just a problem but an evil".

Pakistan's own way: at Class 10 the state tells its story whole — ideology first, then the society it made: thirteen named features of Pakistani culture, minorities' rights written into the culture chapter, and a full closing chapter on women's rights, women nation-builders and the laws that protect them. the older book teaches the same state from the other end — culture defined before it is described, languages given a whole chapter with Urdu named the language of national integration, and national integration itself defined, factored and argued like a civics concept, not a slogan. KPK's Class 10 book ends where the PCTB book's culture chapter begins — society first (its features and its named evils: feudalism, the curse of dowry, child labour), then culture defined as a people's inherited way of life, province by province, with the minorities' role and Jinnah's 11 August 1947 speech as the closing civic ground.
