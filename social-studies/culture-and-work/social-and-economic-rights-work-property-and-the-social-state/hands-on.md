# Hands-on — Social and Economic Rights Work Property and the Social State

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Social-economic rights as the conditions of a citizen's individual freedom — historically property was the main condition of freedom and the state kept only a minimal role, guarding citizens' property; with the growth of large capitalist production the state was forced to strengthen its social-economic role and became the social state (from the mid-19th century), fixing social-economic rights in constitutions and international documents (the UN's 1987 meeting of experts on the International Covenant); the rights themselves, as written in Articles 41–48 of the Constitution of the Republic of Belarus — the right to work (with the International Labour Organization and the fight against unemployment) and the right to property (Article 44; private, joint-stock and collective forms growing beside state property, and the wide spread of self-employment in Belarus), with rest, social security, housing, health protection and education. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
