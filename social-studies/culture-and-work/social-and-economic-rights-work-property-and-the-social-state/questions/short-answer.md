# Social and Economic Rights Work Property and the Social State — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Social-economic rights (Сацыяльна-эканамічныя правы) — Grade 9 — social-economic rights as the conditions of a citizen's individual freedom — historically property was the main condition of freedom and the state kept only a minimal role, guarding citizens' property; with the growth of large capitalist production the state was forced to strengthen its social-economic role and became the social state (from the mid-19th century), fixing social-economic rights in constitutions and international documents (the UN's 1987 meeting of experts on the International Covenant); the rights themselves, as written in Articles 41–48 of the Constitution of the Republic of Belarus — the right to work (with the International Labour Organization and the fight against unemployment) and the right to property (Article 44; private, joint-stock and collective forms growing beside state property, and the wide spread of self-employment in Belarus), with rest, social security, housing, health protection and education (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way — the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state. (any close wording).**
