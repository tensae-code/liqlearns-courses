Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies (printed p. 142), teaches: Respect is one of the basic pillars for building sound social relations and their continuity among society's members, for it expresses appreciation and love between two or more parties; and mutual respect means people's dealing with one another in a good way, such that each individual or group accepts the others' ideas and opinions, regardless of their sex, colour or lineage, and respects difference of opinion with others, for it is the key to relations' success; and among the most important examples of mutual respect: mutual respect for religious beliefs, mutual respect for national and local occasions, and mutual respect for others' rights.

Iraq's own way: values are defined first as a system — standards that steer behaviour and spread peace "regardless of sex, religion, nationality, language or race" — and citizenship is cashed out in named rights (education, work, candidacy, the vote, freedom of opinion and belief), while the internet chapter warns in plain terms that the open network "is governed by no restrictions or conditions".
