# Video script — Setting a Goal and Working Until I Reach It

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Syria Grade 1 Social Studies (Syria Grade 1 Social Studies G1, printed p. 15), teaches: Reaching a goal in steps — set the goal, organise your time, practise, and try again if you fail — told through Julia learning the accordion and the bear who keeps trying for the honey despite the bee stings

Syria Grade 3 Social Studies (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies G3, printed p. 13), teaches: Shared goals against personal goals as the class plans its Evacuation Day celebration — agree the programme, share out the roles, and Razan's written plan for her acting role: the goal, one week to reach it, and a step for every day (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1)

Syria Grade 2 Social Studies (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2, printed p. 14), teaches: A goal with its how — Sally's goal is protecting her teeth, so she cuts the sweets; Raed's is helping his mother, so he tidies his toys and books; Jana's is her family's happiness, so she makes them greeting cards; every one of us has a goal they strive for (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1)

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 1): the whole primary series opens with widening rings — Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland — taught almost entirely through picture scenes with a bear mascot, one "I can…" outcome per spread; rights arrive at age six as lived scenes (the child shut out of play has lost a right; the state gives every child the vaccine free).

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 3): Grade 3 is where the series turns civic — the child meets the vote inside a classroom quarrel (proposals, persuasion, a ballot box, the winner implemented), meets rights on World Children's Rights Day (20 November) as picture pairs where every right is matched by its duty, and meets the inclusive city: the society-we-want lesson is built around services for people with special needs, down to the sign-language alphabet and Braille.

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 2): Grade 2 is the honesty-and-safety year — the right-decision lesson turns on a shopkeeper who offers a boy an expensive sweet free if he tells no one, the careful lesson warns that things found on the road may be explosives, and rights arrive as a shipwreck game: children who trash a dream island invent rules, and discover the balance — "my right to enjoy nature, my duty to protect it".
