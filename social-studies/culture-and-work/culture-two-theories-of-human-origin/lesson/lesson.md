# Culture — the two theories of human origin

## Do it first

Hold a small debate: one partner defends one theory, one the other, each using only the lesson's own words for that theory. Then swap.

## Sub lesson 1 — The creationist theory

Human origin is presented through **two accepted but unfinished theories**. The first is the **creationist theory** — divine and religious: **people were created by the Creator already in their present form**, and human life, language and spiritual matters all come from above. Resting on **faith**, it is accepted by **believing, not by testing**.

[Picture 11526 — two panels: creation as received belief, evolution as a slow change line, drawn with equal weight]

## Sub lesson 2 — The evolutionary theory — and the even-handed close

The second is the **evolutionary theory** — **research-based**: people reached their present form **gradually, over time, differentiating from other animals**.

The book closes even-handedly: **both theories accept that humans are social beings**, and filling the remaining gaps of knowledge is the task of pupils and the coming generation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Tell the creationist theory in its own terms — created in present form, resting on faith. Tell the evolutionary theory in its own terms — gradual change, research-based. Say what both theories agree on.
