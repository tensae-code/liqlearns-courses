# Video script — Society and Its Shared Bonds

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iraq Grade 5 Social Studies (printed p. 82), teaches: Society is a group of individuals who live on one land and are bound by shared ties; the most important shared ties that gather the sons of Iraqi society are the homeland, the shared history, the shared language, the shared interests, and the shared customs and traditions.

Iraq Grade 6 Social Studies (printed p. 103), teaches: Cultural diversity — each nationality and sect has its own cultural heritage, and this diversity strengthened the brotherhood among the sons of Iraqi society, for they share the unity of origin, which is the homeland; customs and traditions — the intellectual and moral traits the sons of society inherited from their ancestors, stemming from Islam and the other heavenly religions, never a barrier in the way of Iraqi cultural unity; language and religion — the linguistic diversity (Arabic, Kurdish, Turkmen, Syriac and others), and the constitution stipulated respect for all the Iraqi religions and sects, showing the constitution's respect for the rights and freedoms of all spectra of Iraqi society; the shared history — the sons of Iraqi society stand in one rank in facing the challenges and dangers, as in all Iraq's spectra standing together in fighting terrorism; and tolerance and rejecting violence — seeking peaceful coexistence with others in dignity and peace, far from violence, rejecting disputes, adopting a culture of dialogue with others, and respecting the other's opinion.

Iraq's own way: (Book 3) the state is defined as institutions that serve, its pillars are named homeland, people, federal authorities and sovereignty, and citizenship is taught as a two-way street — the state's services (free education first) on one side, and on the other a good citizen whose first quality is knowing his own abilities so he can fix his duties and his rights. (Book 4) the components are named one by one with where each lives and what each built, equality is stated in the concept itself — all equal in rights and duties — and the values are taught as a civilisational inheritance, from keeping covenants "for thousands of years" to altruism as the road to social justice.
