**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Nepal).**

Nepal's own way (Book 2): Grade 2's civic sense is property and patience — our common property, protecting public property, and waiting one's turn — beside neighbourliness taught through festivals and living styles.

Nepal's own way (Book 3): Grade 3 names caste directly — "Abolish Untouchability" is a named lesson — and local government arrives as the child's own ward and what it does.

Nepal's own way (Book 4): Grade 4's civic unit is the fullest primary civics in the series — rights, child participation, traffic rules and both tiers of local government (VDC and municipality) — and its social-problems unit teaches settling a quarrel as a named skill.

Nepal's own way (Book 5): the trial Human Value Education edition re-teaches Grade 4 social studies through values first — forgiveness is a named lesson, and civic awareness opens with our security and our rights-and-duties together.

Nepal's own way (Book 6): Grade 5's civic sense reaches the district — the District Coordination Committee and its functions get two full lessons — and the state itself arrives inside the history unit: head of state against head of government, through the 2062–65 BS movement and the republic.

Nepal's own way (Book 8): Grade 6 is where the federation becomes concrete — rural municipality against municipality, each with its services listed — and where conflict itself becomes a school subject: a lesson on disputes and a lesson on resolving them close the social-problems unit.

Nepal's own way (Book 9): Grade 7 completes the state — elements of the state, democratic government, our constitution, human and women's rights, the provincial government's functions and the security agencies — then turns outward: the United Nations, the proper use of social media, and the world's current events close the book.
