# Lesson plan — The Internet a Double Edged Sword

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** The Internet a Double Edged Sword
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Iraq, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The present age witnesses a rapid development in the information revolution, and everyone now lives in an age of openness to the world without restrictions, which made the world a small village; and despite the internet's importance — the programmes and lessons it offers contribute to raising the individual's cultural level — it is a double-edged sword, for the internet and its programmes are not free of several negatives, especially as the internet network is an open, wide world governed by no restrictions or conditions: wasting time — internet use is among the greatest causes of time's loss, especially among the youth, who spend many continuous hours chatting, browsing useless sites and playing electronic games; hacking and spying — internet use made it easy to infiltrate and violate others' privacy and spy on their secrets and movements, so caution must be taken and the official authorities informed when exposed to such a situation; health problems — sitting for long hours browsing leads to problems of the eyes and the spine, pains of the joints and nerves, and weight gain and obesity diseases from little movement; promoting bad ideas — many parties and groups use it to influence individuals' culture, to mislead the youth, and to legitimise terrorist operations and encourage incorrect and strange relations; and falsifying the facts — some persons use pseudonyms and fictitious names to distort some people's reputation and blackmail them by stealing their private information, or to transmit untrue and undocumented social or political news and facts, so the security apparatuses work to protect the citizen by arresting the promoters of rumours and those who seek to blackmail the citizen — as Iraq's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
