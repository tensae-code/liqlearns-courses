# Video script — The Internet a Double Edged Sword

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies (printed pp. 156–157), teaches: The present age witnesses a rapid development in the information revolution, and everyone now lives in an age of openness to the world without restrictions, which made the world a small village; and despite the internet's importance — the programmes and lessons it offers contribute to raising the individual's cultural level — it is a double-edged sword, for the internet and its programmes are not free of several negatives, especially as the internet network is an open, wide world governed by no restrictions or conditions: wasting time — internet use is among the greatest causes of time's loss, especially among the youth, who spend many continuous hours chatting, browsing useless sites and playing electronic games; hacking and spying — internet use made it easy to infiltrate and violate others' privacy and spy on their secrets and movements, so caution must be taken and the official authorities informed when exposed to such a situation; health problems — sitting for long hours browsing leads to problems of the eyes and the spine, pains of the joints and nerves, and weight gain and obesity diseases from little movement; promoting bad ideas — many parties and groups use it to influence individuals' culture, to mislead the youth, and to legitimise terrorist operations and encourage incorrect and strange relations; and falsifying the facts — some persons use pseudonyms and fictitious names to distort some people's reputation and blackmail them by stealing their private information, or to transmit untrue and undocumented social or political news and facts, so the security apparatuses work to protect the citizen by arresting the promoters of rumours and those who seek to blackmail the citizen.

Iraq's own way: values are defined first as a system — standards that steer behaviour and spread peace "regardless of sex, religion, nationality, language or race" — and citizenship is cashed out in named rights (education, work, candidacy, the vote, freedom of opinion and belief), while the internet chapter warns in plain terms that the open network "is governed by no restrictions or conditions".
