**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Ethiopia, Ukraine, Bolivia, Bangladesh, Dominican Republic, Bahamas, India, Cambodia, Sudan, Syria, Kuwait, Iran, Yemen, Mozambique, Jordan, Iraq).**

Ukraine's own way: A "Geography: Regions and Countries" course — every region is done in the same order (position, nature/resources, population, primary/secondary/tertiary sectors) then typological country studies with rubrics (country visiting-card, capital, pride of the country, unique experience), ending with Ukraine's own place in the world; research tasks (Дослідження) embedded in chapters. That way is stocked here as the way Ukraine teaches this thing.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 12, Sexto Año): The final year opens with the census itself — the censista's work as civic-geographic practice — and later teaches geopolitics critically: Haushofer and geopolitical thought, geographic scales, and natural resources as a product of human knowledge (Sunkel), alongside Bolivia's own resource politics: the Gas War, hydrocarbon nationalisation and the urea plant that industrialises gas.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 5): Three geography units climb from the planet to the country: general geography and the American continent's location, then how climate varies across the continents — with climographs the children draw and read themselves — then the Dominican Republic's own climate, activities and population, ending with a “discovering the geography of my country” field activity.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): The Grade 6 book is civics-led (government, elections, courts, constitution, finance ministry) but opens the primary course's widest geography: the world's continents and oceans, political and physical maps, global warming and natural disasters, human resources and the job market, worldwide transport and communication through Nassau's ports of entry, and a closing cultural comparison of The Bahamas with Africa, Australia, Brazil and Japan.

India's own way (Grade 8 Resources and Development): Grade 8 is a resources year, world-first but India-anchored throughout: resources and their conservation, then land/soil/water/vegetation, minerals and power, agriculture, industries and human resources — every world pattern is paired with its Indian distribution map and an Indian case study (Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad textiles, Bangalore IT, a farm in India set against a farm in the USA).

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 12 Geography): the final Geography year is economic geography — Cambodia's population, inequalities, farm/industry/energy/trade/tourism economy and urbanisation first, then the world's economic poles studied one by one: the USA, Japan and the European Union.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 9 SD Geography 9): A single-country book: the whole of Sudan taught in three units — its physical geography, its human geography, then its environmental problems — with map-work activities on Sudan's own maps (states, roads, the Nile) and an infographic "resources of Sudan" spread; the preface warns its statistics change and are only indicators.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 6 SD Geography 6): A whole-Sudan book in three units — its natural environments (desert to rich savanna, plus the Red Sea coast), its productive resources taught environment by environment (farming, herding with the transhumance year of the Kabbabish and Kawahla, industry, mining), then its human geography: population, public services and its important cities one by one.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 7 SD Geography 7): A continents book: after a world opener (continents, oceans, relief, world environments), each continent — Africa, Asia, Europe — gets the same treatment in the same order (relief, drainage, climate, vegetation, population, economic activity, environmental problems), closing with a British Isles case study; Sudan and Africa anchor the examples throughout.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 4 G4 Social Studies): Grade 4 turns from the child's surroundings to the homeland itself — one unit on Yemen's land and people, one on its resources sector by sector (farm, animal, sea, industry, trade/tourism), before services, national occasions and historical figures.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 2): human/economic geography organised sector by sector — population first, then each economic activity taught world-then-Mozambique (agriculture, livestock, fishing, forestry, industry, commerce, tourism, transport), with Mozambique's own distribution tables, ports and exports as the local evidence.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 7): industry taught as a long history — from Palaeolithic tool-making through manufacture and mercantilism to the Industrial Revolution and its consequences — before location, types and industrial landscapes; commerce likewise from barter and the invention of coin to trade blocs and unequal world flows.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G2 SS T2): Term 2 teaches change over time (transport, housing, writing) and the child's economy (production, consumption, recycling, Jordanian-made goods), closing with the blue planet, Earth's surface shapes and a first map of Jordan.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 5 Social Studies): the whole first unit is Iraq's own geography — location first (world, region, locality, then the astronomical location), then the land itself region by region off the relief map, then people and economic activity; history and national education follow as separate units.
