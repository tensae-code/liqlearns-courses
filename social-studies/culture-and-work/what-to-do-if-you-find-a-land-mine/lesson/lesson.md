# What to Do If You Find a Land Mine

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: if you find a land mine, report it to the authorities — they have a safer way of dealing with mines, so people and animals can live without fear — as South Sudan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Activity 6 What should you do with mines found in the region

South Sudan Primary 3 P3 Social Studies SB, printed p. 48, teaches: if you find a land mine, report it to the authorities — they have a safer way of dealing with mines, so people and animals can live without fear. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 5 overview: risks of mines and unexploded ordnance

South Sudan Primary 3 P3 Social Studies TG, printed p. 43, teaches: the guide adds the risks the pupil's book leaves unstated — mines and unexploded ordnance can kill or permanently disable, and conflict led to the placing of landmines, so pupils must recognise the danger and persuade others to look out for them (the land-mine lesson is filed NEW from Book 5 in this wave). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — South Sudan's way — the book's own teaching way

South Sudan's own way: Conflict is taught from the child's own ground — its named causes are theft, refusing other people's religions and corruption; its solvers are the payam chief and the village elder; and the flag itself carries the country's peace story, its two white meanings tied to the 1972 Addis Ababa and 2005 Naivasha agreements. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
