# Video script — What to Do If You Find a Land Mine

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

South Sudan Primary 3 P3 Social Studies SB, printed p. 48, teaches: if you find a land mine, report it to the authorities — they have a safer way of dealing with mines, so people and animals can live without fear.

South Sudan Primary 3 P3 Social Studies TG, printed p. 43, teaches: the guide adds the risks the pupil's book leaves unstated — mines and unexploded ordnance can kill or permanently disable, and conflict led to the placing of landmines, so pupils must recognise the danger and persuade others to look out for them (the land-mine lesson is filed NEW from Book 5 in this wave).

South Sudan's own way: Conflict is taught from the child's own ground — its named causes are theft, refusing other people's religions and corruption; its solvers are the payam chief and the village elder; and the flag itself carries the country's peace story, its two white meanings tied to the 1972 Addis Ababa and 2005 Naivasha agreements.
