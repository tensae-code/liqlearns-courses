**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Cambodia, South Sudan).** South Sudan's teacher-guide parts are evidence only, counted nowhere in addition — South Sudan counts through its student-book parts.

South Sudan's own way (Book 11): The country's own conflict is the textbook — secession is explained by marginalisation and exploitation of the South's resources, and the constitution enters as homework: explain why South Sudan's constitution is important and name five rights it guarantees.

South Sudan's own way (Book 12) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide names the frame the pupil's book leaves implicit — interactions with neighbours are to be examined against the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and tolerance, respect and equality are added to the features a successful change needs.

South Sudan's own way (Book 15): The final primary book is a civics of unity for a country at war with itself — power sharing is taught from the 2016 Transitional Government of National Unity cabinet photograph, the constitution's Section 33 equality clause is quoted in full, and Kenya's 2007–08 crisis and Kofi Annan's mediation are the worked example of how a neighbour's unity process shapes South Sudan's own.
