# Video script — Why We Pay Taxes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Nicaragua Grade 2 Convivencia y Civismo G2 (printed p. 120), teaches: Why paying taxes matters — taxes fund the common services.

Nicaragua Grade 5 Convivencia y Civismo G5 (printed p. 153), teaches: Nicaragua's tax authority — who collects taxes and what they fund.

Nicaragua Grade 6 Convivencia y Civismo G6 (printed p. 151), teaches: Tax education — the citizen's duty and the system's purpose.

Nicaragua Grade 4 Convivencia y Civismo G4 (printed p. 112), teaches: Spending and taxes — where the money for common services comes from.

Nicaragua Grade 2 Conociendo mi Mundo G2 (printed p. 151), teaches: Taxes are a contribution every citizen pays the State (visible on the family's shopping receipts), invested in schools, health centres, roads, bridges and housing.

Nicaragua's own way: civics starts at age 7 with living-together values, then the child's own Constitution, Children's Code and national symbols, and closes on organising for the common good — the state and its duties are present from the first civics book. the upper-primary civics book teaches the state itself — Constitution, Executive and Judicial powers, the DGI tax office — and sets Nicaragua's symbols beside all six Central American neighbours'. the final primary civics book widens from Nicaragua to Central America (symbols and heroes compared across the isthmus) and from values to the machinery of rights — the Children's Convention, the CNA code, consumer rights and tax education. Grade 4 teaches cultural identity region by region (Pacific, Caribbean Coast, north and centre each with their own traditions) before the Constitution's principles and the Powers of the State. the first combined book builds the social world in rings — family, neighbours, school, homeland, municipality — and even its taxes lesson is told through the family's shopping receipts and the works taxes build in the child's own community.
