**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does this part of this lesson teach?

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Nicaragua's way — the books' own teaching way — civics starts at age 7 with living-together values, then the child's own Constitution, Children's Code and national symbols, and closes on organising for the common good — the state and its duties are present from the first civics book. the upper-primary civics book teaches the state itself — Constitution, Executive and Judicial powers, the DGI tax office — and sets Nicaragua's symbols beside all six Central American neighbours'. the final primary civics book widens from Nicaragua to Central America (symbols and heroes compared across the isthmus) and from values to the machinery of rights — the Children's Convention, the CNA code, consumer rights and tax education. Grade 4 teaches cultural identity region by region (Pacific, Caribbean Coast, north and centre each with their own traditions) before the Constitution's principles and the Powers of the State. the first combined book builds the social world in rings — family, neighbours, school, homeland, municipality — and even its taxes lesson is told through the family's shopping receipts and the works taxes build in the child's own community. (any close wording).**
