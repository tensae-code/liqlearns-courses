# Video script — Standing for Election

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Palestine Grade 9 Social Studies Part 1, printed pp. 68–69, teaches: candidacy is the process by which individuals, members of a political party, or of a government or civic institution compete to take over the post of president or membership in it; its conditions in Palestine — for the presidency: Palestinian, born to two Palestinian parents, having completed forty years of age or more on polling day, permanently resident in the Palestinian territories, and registered in the final voters' register; for the Legislative Council: Palestinian, twenty-eight years or more on polling day, name entered in the final voters' register, not convicted of a felony or a misdemeanour against honour and trust, and permanently resident; for the local councils: twenty-five years or more on polling day, name in the final register of the local body, no such conviction, not an employee of the Ministry of Local Government or of any public security body unless he has resigned and attached proof of its acceptance, resident within the body for at least a year before the elections, and not a candidate for another local body or list.

Palestine's own way: participation is taught as existence itself — "I participate, therefore I am" — and the lesson counts women's actual seats (56 of 744 in the National Council, 17 of 132 in the Legislative Council) before teaching the quota women won to change them; elections close the course with Palestine's own candidacy ages: 40 for president, 28 for the Legislative Council, 25 for local councils.
