# Gratitude to Workers

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: what the workers around us give (the night sweeper's clean street, the farmer's rice, the driver's safe ride, the doctor's care) and how gratitude is shown — greeting the cleaner, never wasting the rice others grew, thanking the repairman, helping the school cook whose cart has tipped, and honouring manual work as much as any other — as Vietnam's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Be grateful to working people

Vietnam Grade 4 Đạo đức 4, printed pp. 5–11, teaches: What the workers around us give (the night sweeper's clean street, the farmer's rice, the driver's safe ride, the doctor's care) and how gratitude is shown — greeting the cleaner, never wasting the rice others grew, thanking the repairman, helping the school cook whose cart has tipped, and honouring manual work as much as any other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vietnam's way — the book's own teaching way

Vietnam's own way: Grade 4 turns outward to society's work and property: gratitude is owed to every worker including the night street-sweeper of Tố Hữu's poem; "public property" (của công) is named as a legal-moral category of its own — what belongs to everyone is guarded by everyone — and the book closes with the child's place in law itself: the basic rights and duties of children, including a children's forum where pupils speak. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
