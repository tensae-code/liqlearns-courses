# Traditions and Customs Build Values and Morals — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does part 1 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Traditions and Customs Build Values and Morals — pre-colonial traditions taught caring for the elderly, children, people with disabilities and the poor, accountability and hard work, respect for elders, cooperation in happiness and sadness, hospitality to guests and good relationships without quarrels; elders and knowledgeable persons educated the young in peer groups, at home and at initiation; parenting ran from conception to puberty with the whole community responsible for raising children in good moral values (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does part 2 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Tanzania's way — the books' own teaching way — Tanzania's own way (Standard Seven, Social Studies Pupil's Book Standard Seven): at Standard Seven pupils judge their own heritage — traditions and customs are kept or discarded by their values: pre-colonial traditions aimed to honour good behaviour, hard work, equality, dignity, good parenting and a culture of cooperation, solidarity, brotherhood, patriotism and mutual support, with chiefs and elders as custodians; bad or outdated traditions are to be identified and eradicated. (any close wording).**
