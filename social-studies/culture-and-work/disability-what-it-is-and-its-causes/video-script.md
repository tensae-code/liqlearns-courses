The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2, printed p. 62, teaches: disability is an injury to a person — bodily, mental or psychological — that harms his bodily or mental growth or both and limits his ability to perform his natural role in life for his age, sex and social and cultural characteristics; its types include motor, sensory and mental disability, and it requires special opportunities for growth, education and training with tools and methods that respect their particularities; its causes are many — hereditary factors in the parents, the pregnant mother's health problems, some diseases, malnutrition and smoking, and serious accidents — and in the Palestinian case the occupation is added, as children and individuals are hit by bullets and explosions during aggression or beating during arrest, the clearest example being the bone-breaking policy its soldiers followed in the 1987 Intifada.

Palestine's own way: human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy.
