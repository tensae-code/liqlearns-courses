## Sub lesson 1 — Iraqi Societys Components

Iraq Grade 5 Social Studies (printed pp. 82–83), teaches: Our Iraqi society is composed of several components spread over Iraq's governorates from the far north to the far south — the Arabs, the Kurds, the Turkmen, the Assyrians, the Chaldeans, the Syriacs, the Armenians, the Sabians, the Shabak and the Yazidis — bound together on Iraq's land by rights and duties; and belonging to the homeland and pride in it is a value that must be planted in the human being from his childhood, so that he serves his country and works for its advancement. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: the state is defined as institutions that serve, its pillars are named homeland, people, federal authorities and sovereignty, and citizenship is taught as a two-way street — the state's services (free education first) on one side, and on the other a good citizen whose first quality is knowing his own abilities so he can fix his duties and his rights. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
