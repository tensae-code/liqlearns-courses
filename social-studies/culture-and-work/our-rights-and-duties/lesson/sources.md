**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Maldives, Samoa, Fiji). Albania is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Maldives's own way: belonging widens in rings — me, family, school, island, atoll, country, world — and citizenship is taught through the island itself: its councilors, its roads, its flag and its crafts.

Samoa's own way: Society is taught as a set of institutions a Samoan child already lives inside — family, church, school, economy, government — and change is taught by pairing America's civil-rights movement with Samoa's own Mau as the home example of a social movement.

Albania's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Citizenship in Grade 5 is taught as a ladder — family, group, community, then the state: democracy is first practised inside the family and in class elections, local self-government is compared with the class's own elections, and a whole chapter (Kreu 3, The Road) treats the child as pedestrian, cyclist and passenger with duties on the road.

Fiji's own way (Grade 7): Identity is taught as "what makes you who you are" — family, friends, school, church, province and culture — and valuing cultures starts from respecting your own roots.
