Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 English): geography starts from the child's own school and home — location in the Grama Niladhari → Divisional Secretariat → District → Province hierarchy, a plan of the school garden, home-made weather instruments, and care of the home surroundings — before widening to Sri Lanka's location in the world in the final chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka G10 Tamil SB): the O/L year pairs every world topic with its Sri Lankan twin — world agriculture then Sri Lanka's land use, world industries then Sri Lanka's industries — and closes with map skills and an outline-map chapter; the syllabus (units 10.1–10.13) is printed at the back.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 5 geo-5af-m): The whole book is Mauritania itself, in four "I get to know" axes: my country's location and borders, my country's climate, my society, my country's wealth. Climate is taught through the country's own three wind types and the effect of rain on where people, animals and plants live; farming includes the flood-recession farming of the Chamama plain after the Senegal River falls.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 12 geo-6as-cd): A human-geography year in three units — world population (growth, distribution, migration, structure, with an applications lesson on the age pyramid and demographic projections), urbanisation and its problems, and economic activities from traditional farming to modern industry, transport, trade, craft industry and tourism.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Social Studies G6 T1): Kuwait's intermediate Social Studies is organised as four "learning units" per term, each with its own field label (Geography / History / Civics) printed in the unit's standards page, and every unit closes with a project and a full assessment. Term 1 of Grade 6 takes the whole Arab Gulf as its region — Kuwait first among equals: Unit 1 (Geography) covers the natural features of Kuwait and the Gulf States, Unit 2 (History) the civilizational features, Unit 3 (Geography/History) population origins and migrations, Unit 4 (Geography) the old economic activities; a values unit ("Stations with Good Kuwait") and a maps appendix close the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 2 Lessons 2–5: the historical importance of the Gulf's location and the Dilmun, Tarut, Hili and Magan civilizations, printed pp. 47–50; civilizations in Kuwait (Ubaid at Al-Subiya, Hellenistic Failaka), pp. 50–52; Islamic civilization in the Gulf region, pp. 52–55; Islamic civilization in Kuwait (Kazma, Umm al-Aish, Al-Shuqaya; Dar al-Athar al-Islamiyya), pp. 55–57. History — Unit 3 Lessons 3–5: the Utub migration and the founding of Kuwait, pp. 68–70; the naming of Kuwait and the choice of Al Sabah, pp. 70–73; the founding of the other Gulf States, pp. 74–76. Values/Civics — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": social solidarity (the Failaka story), pp. 101–104; patience, pp. 104–106; visiting the sick, pp. 106–108; the gift of water (Sabil bin Duaij), pp. 108–110.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T1): Grade 7 widens the lens from the Gulf (Grade 6) to the whole Arab homeland, and each unit is fronted by a guide character — the Geographer leads Unit 1, the Narrator of the Eras Unit 2, the Historian of Time Unit 3, the Witness of the Age Unit 4. Unit 1 is the Geography unit: the Arab world treated as one region — its definition and shared traits, its relative and astronomical location, its climate and climate regions, its plant and animal life, its terrain — and then Kuwait placed inside the same natural frame. Units 2–4 are a chronological History sweep (ages and civilizations; Arab-Islamic civilization; colonialism to independence), and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 2, Kuwait and the Arab world through the ages: the rise of the eras (Ice, Stone, Bronze, Iron), printed pp. 40–42; the rise of civilizations and models of the old Arab civilizations (Pharaonic Egypt, Jarma in Libya, Babylon, Saba and its Ma'rib dam), pp. 43–44; the Nabataean civilization and Petra, pp. 45–46; the Kush civilization in Nubia and Meroë, pp. 47–48; civilization stations in Kuwait — Ubaid at Al-Subiya, Dilmun and Hellenistic Ikaros on Failaka Island, pp. 49–50. History — Unit 3, Kuwait and the Arab world in Arab-Islamic civilization: the Arab world before Islam, pp. 58–59; the appearance of Islam and its spread, pp. 60–61; Arab scholars — Maryam al-Asturlabi, Ibn al-Nafis, the navigator Ibn Majid, pp. 62–63; Ibn al-Haytham, al-Kindi, Hunayn al-Abadi, pp. 64–65; pages from Kuwait's history — Kazma in history, culture and war, pp. 66–67. History — Unit 4, between colonialism and independence: the state of the Arab world in the 18th century and European ambitions, pp. 76–77; the colonial movements and the forms and effects of colonialism, pp. 78–79; Arab resistance movements (Palestine, Libya), pp. 80–81; the independence stage in the Arab world, pp. 82–83; Kuwait from protection (1899) to independence (1961), pp. 84–85. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": generosity and giving (Sheikh Jaber "Al-Aish"), pp. 93–94; respecting the teacher, pp. 97–98; congratulations from the heart (Eid greetings), pp. 99–100.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T1): Grade 8 widens the region a second time — from the Arab homeland (Grade 7) to the whole Islamic world — and reverses the balance: two History-led units on the Islamic state's rise and civilization come first, then two full Geography units (the natural environment; its effect on economy and society), a Civics unit on law in Kuwait, and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait". Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/religion — Unit 1, a historical glimpse of the Islamic state and its civilization: the heavenly religions and the Arab world's old civilizations, printed pp. 14–15; the rise of the Islamic state and the conquests movement, pp. 16–18; the concept and foundations of Islamic civilization, pp. 19–22 (the culture definition is placed below); its characteristics — faith and work, universality, tolerance, moderation — pp. 23–24. Civics/History — Unit 2 Lesson 1, the political and administrative manifestations: the caliphate, the ministry, the governorship, the diwans and the police, pp. 30–32. History of science — Unit 2 Lesson 4, the social, cultural and scientific manifestations: society's classes by profession, the status of women, the people of the covenant in farming, industry and trade, the scientific centres (the two Holy Mosques, Al-Azhar, Baghdad's House of Wisdom) and the translation of Muslim scholars' works in Europe, pp. 40–43 (the al-Idrisi map part is placed below). Civics — Unit 5, law as the basis of stability and building society in Kuwait: the definition, rules and aims of law; law and morality; the characteristics of the legal rule, pp. 85–96. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": the founding of Al-Mubarakiya school in 1911, Kuwait's first regular school, pp. 100–101; the etiquette of replies, pp. 102–103; the fragrance of neighbourliness, pp. 104–105; "What do you say, folks?" — consultation (shura) under Sheikh Salem Al-Mubarak, pp. 106–107 (the customs part is placed below).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 9 Social Studies G9 T2): Term 2 is the citizenship-and-world book, the Civics-heaviest of the nine: global citizenship and community responsibility; the State of Kuwait and international relations; the State of Kuwait and world organisations; and Kuwait and the world facing the future's challenges, closing with the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait". Its Geography runs through culture and identity, trade and oil, food security and sustainable development rather than through landforms. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics — Unit 1 Lessons 1–2: global citizenship defined as a system of values and behaviours expressing a person's belonging to a world community beyond the homeland, its importance and goals; human values (equality, freedom, dignity, justice, cooperation, peace) and digital global citizenship, with Kuwait Vision 2035 for a safe, balanced digital society, printed pp. 14–18. Civics/law — Unit 1 Lesson 4: national belonging defined; Kuwait's COVID-19 response — repatriation flights and full care for citizens abroad, medical and financial aid to stricken states with the World Health Organization; and the National Unity Protection Law, Decree 19 of 2012, pp. 22–24. Civics/History — Unit 2 Lesson 2: political relations and their forms (diplomatic representation, treaties, conferences), with the Kuwait–Britain model from the 1899 protection agreement to independence in 1961, pp. 34–35. Civics — Unit 2 Lesson 5: Kuwait's foreign policy — diplomacy defined, its principles (respecting sovereignty, settling disputes peacefully), the international coalition that liberated Kuwait in 1991, development and humanitarian aid through the Kuwait Fund and the charities (Palestine, Syria, Darfur from 2003, Beirut after the 2020 port explosion), and the United Nations naming Kuwait a centre of humanitarian work in 2014, pp. 42–44. Civics/institutions — Unit 3: world organisations defined and typed (social-humanitarian, economic, cultural-scientific, political, environmental-health), pp. 48–49; the United Nations — founded 1945 after the League of Nations failed, charter written at San Francisco, headquarters New York, Kuwait a member from 1963, its main organs (General Assembly, Security Council of 15 with five permanent members holding the veto, the Economic and Social Council, the International Court of Justice at The Hague, the Secretariat) and its goals, pp. 50–52; the World Health Organization — founded 7 April 1948, headquarters Geneva, Kuwait a member from 1960, its Kuwait office opened 2021 and its 2025 Sheikh Sabah Al-Ahmad award to Kuwait for elderly health care, pp. 53–55; the Food and Agriculture Organization — founded 16 October 1945, headquarters Rome, World Food Day on 16 October, Kuwait a member from 1961 working with it on desert farming, food security and fish farming — and UNESCO (Paris), UNIDO (Vienna) and the ILO (Geneva), pp. 56–58. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": Ali Bu Kahil, whose warning saved Kuwait from an approaching enemy fleet, pp. 89–90; and Sheikh Misaed Al-Azmi, who learned smallpox vaccination at Al-Azhar in Cairo and vaccinated Kuwaitis through the 1932 outbreak, making the vaccine himself, pp. 96–97 (the women's-professions and coffee stations are placed below).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 1 np-g1): Geography begins as "Our Earth" at home scale — the land around the home and school, then a map of home, school and classroom; picture-led lessons ("look and talk about the pictures") with almost no text at Grade 1.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 2 np-g2): "Our Earth" grows from living place to village/town and the village in the map; economic geography is comparative — things made in the village vs things made in the city.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 3 np-g3): Grade 3 introduces Nepal's three ecological regions — Himalayan (cold, Gaurishankar), Hilly (Kathmandu valley) and Terai (flat, hot, Dhangadhi) — through three children's home places, with rivers (Tamakoshi, Narayani), landforms and climate tied to each child's village/town.

Eritrea's own way (Eritrea Grade 6 Social Studies 6): Economics enters as one unit inside integrated Social Studies — the three activity levels are taught entirely through Eritrean examples (highland wheat and barley vs lowland sorghum and sesame, Red Sea fishing, handicrafts before Italian-era modern industry), with a local research project on the pupil's own area.

Eritrea's own way (Eritrea Grade 8 Social Studies 8): the Economics is Eritrea itself — its own post-independence development story (National Charter principles, 1995 land reform, self-reliance, the Warsai-Yikaalo campaign) followed by a full survey of its farming, fishing, trade and industries with national statistics.
