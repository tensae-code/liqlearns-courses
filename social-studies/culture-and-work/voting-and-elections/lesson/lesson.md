# Voting and Elections

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the kinds of electoral systems (majoritarian in its three forms, proportional, mixed, minority-representation) and that Belarus uses the majoritarian system; who may vote and be elected (vote at 18; local Soviets from 18, Chamber of Representatives from 21, Council of the Republic from 30, President — a citizen by birth of 35+ resident 10 years); the eight stages of the electoral process from setting the date to publishing results, run by election commissions headed by the Central Commission; elections free, equal, direct and secret (Arts. 65–68); and the referendum as direct democracy whose decisions can be changed only by another referendum (Arts. 76–77) — as Belarus' book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Elections and referendums (Выборы и референдумы) — Grade 11

Belarus Grade 11 Obshchestvovedenie (printed p. 20), teaches: The kinds of electoral systems (majoritarian in its three forms, proportional, mixed, minority-representation) and that Belarus uses the majoritarian system; who may vote and be elected (vote at 18; local Soviets from 18, Chamber of Representatives from 21, Council of the Republic from 30, President — a citizen by birth of 35+ resident 10 years); the eight stages of the electoral process from setting the date to publishing results, run by election commissions headed by the Central Commission; elections free, equal, direct and secret (Arts. 65–68); and the referendum as direct democracy whose decisions can be changed only by another referendum (Arts. 76–77). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way

Belarus' own way: the whole Grade 11 course is the state describing itself — the 1994 Constitution and its 1996/2004 referendums, the President as guarantor of the Constitution, a two-chamber National Assembly and elected local Soviets of deputies under appointed executive committees, and two state languages — so its civics is placed as Belarus's own constitutional order, not as generic civics.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
