# How We Build Our Ger

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the ger is raised on a chosen place — sheltered from wind and flood, its door facing south or south-east; the order of building: crown wheel (тооно) set first, then the wall sections (хана), door, posts (багана), roof poles (унь), ropes and covers, layer upon layer by season; when the ger stands, tea is brewed and the neighbours are invited in, and taking it down runs the order backwards; the blessing verse (мялаалга) praises the new ger piece by piece — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 8 "From the customs of building a ger" (Гэр барих ёсноос)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 24, teaches: The ger is raised on a chosen place — sheltered from wind and flood, its door facing south or south-east; the order of building: crown wheel (тооно) set first, then the wall sections (хана), door, posts (багана), roof poles (унь), ropes and covers, layer upon layer by season; when the ger stands, tea is brewed and the neighbours are invited in, and taking it down runs the order backwards; the blessing verse (мялаалга) praises the new ger piece by piece. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
