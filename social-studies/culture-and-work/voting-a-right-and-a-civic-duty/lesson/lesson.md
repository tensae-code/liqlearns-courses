# Voting a Right and a Civic Duty

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the vote as the citizen's direct participation — choosing freely who will govern; under the Electoral and Political Parties Law it is a right and a civic duty, and it is universal (all citizens over 18), secret, single, personal and non-transferable — as Guatemala's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 4 "El voto"

Guatemala Grade 4 Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana, printed pp. 191–192, teaches: the vote as the citizen's direct participation — choosing freely who will govern; under the Electoral and Political Parties Law it is a right and a civic duty, and it is universal (all citizens over 18), secret, single, personal and non-transferable. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guatemala's way (Book 5) — the book's own teaching way

Guatemala's own way: citizenship is woven through every unit of the history/geography book — a fourth-grader meets the three powers, the human-rights declaration, the civil registry and the ballot box inside the same volume that tells the national story, under the unit motto "Vivamos en Armonía" (Let us live in harmony).. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
