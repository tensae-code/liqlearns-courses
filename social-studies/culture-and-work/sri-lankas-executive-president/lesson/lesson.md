# Sri Lankas Executive President

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the President elected by the whole people (Article 30) for six years and at most twice; who may stand — over thirty, qualified to sit in Parliament, never twice elected before, never removed from the office; and the election itself — one nationwide electorate marking preferences 1, 2, 3, the second preferences counted on until a candidate passes half the valid votes — as Sri Lanka's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 5 Electing the President

Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000, printed pp. 117–119, teaches: The President elected by the whole people (Article 30) for six years and at most twice; who may stand — over thirty, qualified to sit in Parliament, never twice elected before, never removed from the office; and the election itself — one nationwide electorate marking preferences 1, 2, 3, the second preferences counted on until a candidate passes half the valid votes. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 5 The President's powers

Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000, printed pp. 120–121, teaches: The executive President's reach — head of the Cabinet, appointing the Prime Minister and the ministers and giving them their subjects, declaring war and peace, and the great appointments (ambassadors, the chiefs of the services and police, the Auditor General, the Commissioner of Elections, the Chief Justice and the judges), with the pardon power and the reports that must reach him before a death sentence is touched. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way

Sri Lanka's own way: the combined book's second half is a full civics course in disguise — democracy first as an idea with its thinkers and its six conditions, then Sri Lanka's own 1978 constitution opened article by article: the rights chapter with its Ombudsman and Human Rights Commission, the vote's road from McCallum to Donoughmore, the preferential presidential election, Parliament's 225 seats, the cabinet that falls with its bill, provinces and pradeshiya sabhas, and a court ladder that ends in village mediation boards. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
