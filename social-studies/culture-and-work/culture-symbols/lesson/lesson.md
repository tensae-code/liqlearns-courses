# Culture — symbols

## Do it first

Walk your street or market and list five symbols you meet — a flag, a dress pattern, a sign, a sound. Write what each stands for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Things that stand for things

Every culture has **symbols: a known thing, sound or image that stands for something else.** Sounds, images, dress, beliefs and other human acts are all symbols.

The book's own examples: **ululation signals joy**, while **wailing cries signal grief or trouble**. You hear the sound — and you know the meaning without a word of explanation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Symbols differ — and change

**Priests, Christianity, Islam, government and nation all have their symbols.** Symbols **differ from culture to culture** and they **change over time**. Human life without symbols is hardly thinkable.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Say what a symbol is. Give the book's two sound examples with their meanings. Say how symbols relate to culture and time: they differ between cultures and change over time.

## Do It Yourself

1. Walk your street or market and list five symbols you meet — a flag, a dress pattern, a sign, a sound. Write what each stands for.
2. Ask an elder about one symbol that has changed in their lifetime. Write what it meant then and what it means now.
3. Draw one symbol of your school or your nation and write its meaning underneath in one sentence.
