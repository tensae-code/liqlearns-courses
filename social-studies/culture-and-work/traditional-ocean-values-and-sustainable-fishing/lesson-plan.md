# Lesson plan — Traditional Ocean Values and Sustainable Fishing

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Traditional Ocean Values and Sustainable Fishing
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Tonga, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Traditional uses of the reef and ocean, Tongan fishing values and Uloa and Pola — teaches: values of respect and honour guided fishing; places were set aside with no fishing so nature could regenerate, and the number, size, age and season of the harvest were controlled; faka'apa'apa (respect), lototo (humility), tauhi vaa (nurturing relationships) and mamahi'i me'a (loyalty) as the values that keep the ocean thriving; herding fish with a 1–2 km line of coconut leaves took the whole community working together for the good of all — as Tonga's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the lesson itself — values of respect and honour guiding fishing, places set aside with no fishing so nature could regenerate, the Tongan fishing values, and the whole community herding fish together with a line of coconut leaves.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.
