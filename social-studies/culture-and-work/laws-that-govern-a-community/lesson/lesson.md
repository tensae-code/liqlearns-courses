# Laws That Govern a Community

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: laws are a system of rules used to direct someone on what to do and what not to do; each community has laws that govern them, moral laws guide us on how to behave, breaking laws leads to arrest and the court, and class and school rules matter for the same reason — as South Sudan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 4: laws that govern a community

South Sudan Primary 4 P4 Social Studies SB, printed p. 65, teaches: laws are a system of rules used to direct someone on what to do and what not to do; each community has laws that govern them, moral laws guide us on how to behave, breaking laws leads to arrest and the court, and class and school rules matter for the same reason. ## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 4 Activities 5–6 guidance, notes and answers

South Sudan Primary 4 P4 Social Studies TG, printed p. 62, teaches: the guide's own law lists and answers — people are arrested for stealing, rape, corruption and murder; class rules help us concentrate, keep a clean environment, work hard and respect teachers; and the laws in the communities and the states are the same, among them taking no one's property without permission and respect for the constitution (the laws lesson is filed NEW from Book 7 in this wave). ## Sub lesson 3 — Activities 5–6 Law and order

South Sudan Primary 7 P7 Social Studies SB, printed p. 104, teaches: just as a school has rules, a country has laws; citizens who break laws are taken to court and given a chance to defend themselves; when people obey the laws there is order in a country, and the government employs police and other leaders, with the village chief's role examined, to keep law and order so people feel safe (the laws lesson is filed NEW from Book 7 in this wave). ## Sub lesson 4 — South Sudan's way — the book's own teaching way

South Sudan's own way: Law enters as the rules each community governs itself by, with arrest and the court as its pictures; the moral half of the conflict unit is a right/wrong table judged by community eyes — gossiping and not greeting elders are wrongs alongside stealing — and the unit closes on resisting negative peer pressure, with drug abuse named as the Health-course part (named here, not placed). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
