# The Four Gender Roles

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a role is the set of behaviours a person performs for a purpose, and gender roles are shaped by social conditions, not by biology — childrearing and housework are traditionally women's, yet men can do them; the book counts four roles: the reproductive role (women's from conception and birth, with child care and upbringing shared), the productive role (production and market work, where women's work is still undervalued despite distinguished results), the community role (for women an extension of the reproductive role around family and near community, for men an extension of productive work relations), and the political role (decision-making authority inside and outside the family, where Palestinian women leaders have emerged in many fields but not at the required level) — and the aim is sharing the roles, not exchanging them — as Palestine's book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 8 Social Studies Part 2): the semester's only civics unit pairs gender with violence — sex is biology but gender is society's script, and the four roles (reproductive, productive, community, political) are to be shared, not swapped; violence is then read through Palestine's own causes, the occupation first, and its legal form is discrimination written into the state's own laws.
