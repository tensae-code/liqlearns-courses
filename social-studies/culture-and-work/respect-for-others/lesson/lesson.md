## Sub lesson 1 — Respect for Others

Iraq Grade 1 Moral Education (printed pp. 31–35), teaches: Respect is a trait of the polite person, and respecting others is what makes a person remain in others' memory: Nour, a former pupil, knocks on the classroom door, greets her old teacher, apologises for interrupting the lesson, and explains why she came — and years later the teacher still remembers her by name, telling the class that they all share Nour's trait, polite pupils who respect others; I respect others and deal with them politely, and I apologise if I disturb them. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: moral education is a separate subject from Grade 1, taught entirely as stories about five named children (Muhammad, Yusuf, Zahraa, Lana, Sara) whose small school and family incidents carry each value; every lesson ends in the child's own voice — "What I learned" — and the second unit's family tree is drawn with the father's help, branch by branch, until the big family shades them all. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
