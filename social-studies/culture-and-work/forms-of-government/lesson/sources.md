**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Kazakhstan, Costa Rica, Maldives). Kiribati is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Maldives's own way: belonging widens in rings — me, family, school, island, atoll, country, world — and citizenship is taught through the island itself: its councilors, its roads, its flag and its crafts.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 8 Sakhova NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course phrases its lessons as questions and tasks — «Әр жерде неліктен уақыт әркелкі?» (Why is time different in different places?, 2.2), «Тау жыныстары қалай пайда болады?» (How do rocks form?, 3.1.5), «Өзеннің суы қалай өзгереді?» (How does a river's water change?, 3.3.4) — and adds a lesson on the culture of showing geographic names on the map (2.3).

Kiribati's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Social Science is built island-first — the maneaba (mwaneaba) meeting house is at once the culture-keeping building, the traditional parliament and the centre of village life, and the modern civics (constitution, rights, voting at 18 for the Maneaba ni Maungatabu) is taught growing out of the traditional systems pupils already live under (kaainga family rule, unimwane elders, uea chiefs). That evidence is stocked here as the way Kiribati teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Costa Rica's own way: the civics seed is planted inside the History line — the Enlightenment is taught through its political thinkers (Montesquieu's separation of powers, Voltaire's tolerance, Rousseau's general will), and the French Revolution unit closes by printing the full text of the 1789 Declaration and asking pupils to judge its meaning for Costa Rica today.
