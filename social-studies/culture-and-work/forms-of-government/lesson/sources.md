Samoa's own way: Social organisation is taught from the inside out — the groups a child belongs to first, then the aiga and its matai, then the village's own circle of groups (matai at the centre, aumaga as the matai's right hand), and only then a neighbour kingdom's political system as the worked example of government. Bhutan's own way: the primary course closes by putting the whole state in front of the child — what a government is, Bhutan's own change from monarchy to Democratic Constitutional Monarchy in 2008, its three branches told through the courts' own ladder and the kabney/patang ranks the King bestows, the Article 7 fundamental rights quoted from Bhutan's Constitution, and a social-problems chapter that names Bhutan's own helping agencies (Royal Bhutan Police, RENEW, NCWC) for every problem.

Fiji's own way (Grade 10): Government is taught from Fiji outward — Fiji's own traditional chiefly government and western-style government first, then Fiji's Parliament as the worked example of parliamentary rule, then the United Nations.
