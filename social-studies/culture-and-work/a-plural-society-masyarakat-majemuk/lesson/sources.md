Indonesia's own way (Book 1): national integration is taught as a contrast — the old integrations of Sriwijaya and Majapahit were made by conquest and stayed fragile (semu), while the modern one grew from shared fate under colonisation and is held by tolerance, with Indonesia cited by its own Foreign Ministry as a world reference for unity in diversity.

Indonesia's own way (Book 2): the guide's graduate-profile mapping names Kewargaan (citizenship) as the dimension carried by the plural-society and social-change chapters — the civics strand is labelled as such for the teacher.
