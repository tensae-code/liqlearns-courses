# The Family the First Cell of Society

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the family as society's fundamental cell; the Constitution (arts. 81, 84, 88) gives everyone the right to found a family, sets parents' duties to feed, respect and protect their children, children's duty to respect, attend and protect their parents, and the protection of older adults; the family tree; each member's responsibilities at home; equality of man and woman in housework; talking and listening with respect, and affectionate treatment of grandparents — as Cuba's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Family the First Cell of Society

Cuba Grade 5 Educación Ciudadana OM (printed pp. 5–11), teaches: The family as society's fundamental cell; the Constitution (arts. 81, 84, 88) gives everyone the right to found a family, sets parents' duties to feed, respect and protect their children, children's duty to respect, attend and protect their parents, and the protection of older adults; the family tree; each member's responsibilities at home; equality of man and woman in housework; talking and listening with respect, and affectionate treatment of grandparents. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cuba's way — the book's own teaching way

Cuba's own way: citizenship is taught through the child's own pioneer organisation and through the 2019 Constitution article by article — family duties, equality and the mass organisations are all read straight out of constitutional text, and the state is presented as the people's own power (sovereignty in the people, delegates answerable to their electors). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
