**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Mongolia, Bahamas, Rwanda, Fiji, Pakistan, Bhutan, Sri Lanka). Papua New Guinea is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class V Social Studies V): Money is taught through the ngultrum and Bhutan's own named banks (Bank of Bhutan, BNBL, BDBL, Druk PNB, Tashi Bank), with deposit/loan arithmetic in ngultrum.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grades 9–10 Business Studies TG): the old OBE Business Studies course carries its Economics inside Unit 9.1 — production and trading of PNG-made goods, shell money and pigs as traditional exchange against modern money, and why domestic trade keeps money inside the country — plus a wise-consumer unit and a Taxation option taught with real IRC forms. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 Social Studies Class 4 SNC): Economics enters at Class 4 as one named chapter of Social Studies, taught in Urdu with English concept labels, through Pakistani examples — the State Bank of Pakistan, a National Bank of Pakistan cheque, and Pakistan's own import/export goods.


Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 5 Year 5 Social Studies): A full primary money curriculum in one unit — work and wages first, then the Fiji dollar against other currencies, then a family budget, a personal saving plan and what banks do.

Bahamas' own way: banking is defined for the Grade 6 learner inside the Ministry of Finance spread — you store money safely and it grows; the bank lends your money to other people and businesses and pays you interest.

Mongolia's own way: Social Studies is a five-chapter spiral (Individual & Society, Individual & Culture, Individual & Economy, Human Rights & Law, Public Administration) continued from Grade 8; there is no physical-geography chapter — Mongolia's geography is a separate subject (Газарзүй) — so this book's Geography-wave content is its culture and economics teaching, grounded in Mongolian surveys, wage tables and heritage inventories. That way is stocked here as the way Mongolia teaches this thing. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 9 G9 Business and Accounting Studies 1996): Economics is introduced inside a Commerce book that starts from the pupil's own meals, clothes and schoolbooks, then reads Sri Lanka's whole economy off Central Bank 1990 tables (sector shares, import/export composition) before teaching trade channels, with People's Bank cheque and bill-of-exchange specimens as the money teaching.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 10 G10 Business and Accounting Studies 1998): the second-year book teaches the services that make trade possible, each one through Sri Lanka's own institutions — the Central Bank, Bank of Ceylon and People's Bank, the CTB and Air Lanka, the Insurance Corporation of Sri Lanka — with insurance first explained as 100 people each paying Rs 100 a month into one shared pot.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 10 Commerce Introductory Book 1974): a full British-style commerce course rebuilt for Ceylon — every concept is landed on Ceylon data: the 1953 census of occupations, Ceylon's trade balances 1954–59, the rice subsidy accounts, mint-par exchange worked in rupees, and Gresham's law and the trade cycle taught a decade before the later Business Studies books appear.
