South Korea's Grade 10 Cheonjae Integrated Society, printed p. 192; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society, printed p. 188; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society 2 (2022), printed p. 54, teaches: VI.03 사회 및 공간 불평등 현상과 개선 방안, VI.3 사회 및 공간 불평등 현상과 정의로운 사회, 사회정의와 불평등 3 사회 및 공간 불평등 현상과 개선 방안 — teaches: social and spatial (regional) inequality — its faces and the remedies proposed for it; social and spatial inequality, and the institutions and practical plans for a just society; social and spatial inequality and the plans that improve it.

South Korea's own way: Cheonjae's Integrated Society teaches the same nine-unit national frame as MiraeN's but ends the book with the Constitution itself and a standard labour contract (표준 근로 계약서) as appendices — pupils finish the course holding the two documents. Jihaksa splits every section into two numbered lessons, so its civics teaching is the most granular of the three publishers — human rights alone gets five lessons (meaning/change, modern society, the constitution's role, law-abiding participation, problems and solutions), and its justice unit ends on the institutions and practical plans for a just society. the 2022 curriculum splits Integrated Society into two volumes; volume 2 is the civics-heavy one — human rights and the constitution, justice and inequality, then globalisation and peace — and it closes by naming the pupil's own role outright: "life as a global citizen in the future society" (세계시민으로서의 삶). Its enrichment boxes (사회 고수 되기) add the proportionality principle in limiting basic rights, redistributive justice for the socially weak, and the death penalty debated as a human-rights question.
