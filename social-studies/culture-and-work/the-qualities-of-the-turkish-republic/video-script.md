Turkey Grade 7 Sosyal Bilgiler 2. Kitap, printed p. 12, teaches: the 1982 Constitution's first three articles cannot be changed or even proposed for change; Article 1 — Turkey is a Republic, the nation holding sovereignty and using it through deputies it elects for set terms; Article 2 — the state respects human rights, is bound to Atatürk nationalism (a citizenship-based nationalism without racial distinction), and is a democratic, secular, social and rule-of-law state; the book traces the roots in old Turkish states — beys chosen by election in the toy assembly, Kutadgu Bilig's command to rule justly, endowments aiding orphans

Turkey's own way: the course ends where citizenship becomes constitutional: thirteen-year-olds read Articles 1–3 of their own constitution (the ones that cannot even be proposed for change), separate the state's three powers, walk democracy's road from the Tanzimat edicts to the first multi-party election of 1946, and finish by naming the behaviours that wound democracy in daily life.
