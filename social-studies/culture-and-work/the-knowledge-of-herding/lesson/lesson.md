# The Knowledge of Herding

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: herding is learned from childhood — from four or five, children turn the lambs and kids, from five or six gather dung and help father and mother, and even Chinggis's envoys remarked on Mongol children riding at five or six; the herding customs children learn: water the animals without crowding or frightening them at the well, and refill the trough with clean water — it is a kindness to every creature that drinks; care for the young animals, for the herd's increase rests on them; gather dung and horse droppings as fuel, drying the wet; and every animal is named by its years — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 7 "Herding knowledge" (Мал маллах ухаан)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 27, teaches: Herding is learned from childhood — from four or five, children turn the lambs and kids, from five or six gather dung and help father and mother, and even Chinggis's envoys remarked on Mongol children riding at five or six; the herding customs children learn: water the animals without crowding or frightening them at the well, and refill the trough with clean water — it is a kindness to every creature that drinks; care for the young animals, for the herd's increase rests on them; gather dung and horse droppings as fuel, drying the wet; and every animal is named by its years. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 3 turns from the family to the whole moral order — deeds and their fruits, the moving camp, the spirits of land and water, the state's banner and emblem — and ends with the child's first political ideas: deciding together, equal rights, and a government the citizens elect and pay for.
