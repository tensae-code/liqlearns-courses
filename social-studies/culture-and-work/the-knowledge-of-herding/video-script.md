The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 27, teaches: Herding is learned from childhood — from four or five, children turn the lambs and kids, from five or six gather dung and help father and mother, and even Chinggis's envoys remarked on Mongol children riding at five or six; the herding customs children learn: water the animals without crowding or frightening them at the well, and refill the trough with clean water — it is a kindness to every creature that drinks; care for the young animals, for the herd's increase rests on them; gather dung and horse droppings as fuel, drying the wet; and every animal is named by its years.

Mongolia's own way (Link book L1): Grade 3 turns from the family to the whole moral order — deeds and their fruits, the moving camp, the spirits of land and water, the state's banner and emblem — and ends with the child's first political ideas: deciding together, equal rights, and a government the citizens elect and pay for.
