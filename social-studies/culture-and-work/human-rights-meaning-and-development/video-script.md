South Korea's Grade 10 MiraeN Integrated Society, printed p. 94; Grade 10 Cheonjae Integrated Society, printed p. 108; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society, printed p. 105; Grade 10 Bisang Integrated Society, printed p. 98; Grade 10 Donga Integrated Society, printed p. 100; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society 2 (2022), printed p. 12, teaches: IV.주제1 인권의 의미와 발전 과정, IV.01 인권의 의미와 변화 양상, IV.1-01 인권의 의미와 변화, IV.1 인권의 의미와 변화 양상, IV.01 역사 속에서 인권이 확립되어 온 과정은?, 인권 보장과 헌법 1-1 인권의 의미와 확장 과정 — teaches: the meaning of human rights and how they developed and expanded through history; the meaning of human rights and how their range has changed and widened; the meaning of human rights and how they have changed; the meaning of human rights and how their scope has changed; the process by which human rights were established through history; the meaning of human rights and the process by which they expanded.

South Korea's own way: the high-school common subject 통합사회 (Integrated Society) is built around nine key questions asked from temporal, spatial, social and ethical perspectives at once; its civics core is Unit IV (human rights guaranteed by the constitution, law-abiding spirit and citizen participation) and Unit VI (justice and inequality), and its peace unit teaches the peaceful resolution of Korea's own division and of East Asia's history conflicts as the closing civics content. Cheonjae's Integrated Society teaches the same nine-unit national frame as MiraeN's but ends the book with the Constitution itself and a standard labour contract (표준 근로 계약서) as appendices — pupils finish the course holding the two documents. Jihaksa splits every section into two numbered lessons, so its civics teaching is the most granular of the three publishers — human rights alone gets five lessons (meaning/change, modern society, the constitution's role, law-abiding participation, problems and solutions), and its justice unit ends on the institutions and practical plans for a just society. Bisang's Integrated Society binds its justice unit shut with a practice section (불평등의 해결과 정의의 실현 — solving inequality and realising justice) and its culture unit with creative succession of traditional culture; its peace unit is framed as East Asia's conflicts and international peace rather than the two Koreas alone. Donga writes every lesson as a question ("Why is civil disobedience ever needed?", "What does universal ethics do in understanding a culture?") and is the only one of the five publishers to teach civil disobedience (시민 불복종) beside the law-abiding spirit, and to split the conditions of happiness into two lessons — living environment/economic stability, then democracy's development and moral practice. the 2022 curriculum splits Integrated Society into two volumes; volume 2 is the civics-heavy one — human rights and the constitution, justice and inequality, then globalisation and peace — and it closes by naming the pupil's own role outright: "life as a global citizen in the future society" (세계시민으로서의 삶). Its enrichment boxes (사회 고수 되기) add the proportionality principle in limiting basic rights, redistributive justice for the socially weak, and the death penalty debated as a human-rights question.
