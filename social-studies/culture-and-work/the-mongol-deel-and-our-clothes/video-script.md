Mongolia Civic Education Grade 2, printed p. 23, teaches: The deel is shaped for a horse people's life — free to ride in, wide sash, closed chest against the wind, long sleeves, tall boots that guard against cold and snakes; the hat and sash are honoured as the state's own symbols, and the clothing taboos — never step over a hat or clothes, never fold the deel's collar inward, never enter a ger with the sash untied, tie an untied sash in a knot rather than lay it on the ground.

Mongolia's own way (Book 2): Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact.
