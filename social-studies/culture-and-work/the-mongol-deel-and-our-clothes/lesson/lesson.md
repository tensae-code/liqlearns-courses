# The Mongol Deel And Our Clothes

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the deel is shaped for a horse people's life — free to ride in, wide sash, closed chest against the wind, long sleeves, tall boots that guard against cold and snakes; the hat and sash are honoured as the state's own symbols, and the clothing taboos — never step over a hat or clothes, never fold the deel's collar inward, never enter a ger with the sash untied, tie an untied sash in a knot rather than lay it on the ground — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 7 "Mongolian clothing" (Монгол хувцас)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 2, printed p. 23, teaches: The deel is shaped for a horse people's life — free to ride in, wide sash, closed chest against the wind, long sleeves, tall boots that guard against cold and snakes; the hat and sash are honoured as the state's own symbols, and the clothing taboos — never step over a hat or clothes, never fold the deel's collar inward, never enter a ger with the sash untied, tie an untied sash in a knot rather than lay it on the ground. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
