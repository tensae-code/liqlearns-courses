## Sub lesson 1 — The Six Families of Civic Values

Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 1 (printed p. 85), teaches: civil society's values are varied and interlocking, covering all sides of human life: national values (love of homeland, sacrifice, struggle for its freedom and development, defending it); political values (freedom, democracy, the rule of law, justice, equality, accountability, respecting opinion and the other opinion); social values (respect for human dignity, tolerance, social solidarity, care for education, truthfulness, sincerity, honesty, cooperation, selflessness); economic values (justice in distributing wealth, no waste, respecting work practised according to law, respecting time, valuing manual work, forbidding cheating in commercial dealings, strengthening integrity and fighting corruption in all its forms); cultural values (interest in culture, literature and art, research and reading, adopting dialogue as a means of understanding, and using scientific research to develop the sides of life); and religious values (drawn from religion — faith in God, justice, equality, respect for human rights, tolerance, and rejecting racism and extremism). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 7 Social Studies Part 1): civil society is taught entirely through Palestinian institutions named one by one — the Red Crescent, Al-Haq, the Independent Commission for Human Rights, the teachers' and women's unions — and its values lesson ends in a project: mining Palestine's own Declaration of Independence and Basic Law for their values and sorting them into the book's six families. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
