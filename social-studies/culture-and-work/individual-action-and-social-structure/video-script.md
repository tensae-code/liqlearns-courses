Mongolia Grade 10 Social Studies, printed p. 5, teaches: What society and its structure are — the stable pattern of relations between society's parts; the person inside it holds statuses (байр суурь) — some ascribed at birth (sex, ethnicity, family), some achieved (profession, office) — and plays the roles (үүрэг) each status carries, one person holding many at once (pupil, daughter, friend, classmate); institutions — family, education, economy, science, the state and law, health, religion — each answering a social need, set out in one table; and the three lenses sociology reads it all through: structural functionalism (Comte, Durkheim, Parsons, Merton — every institution has manifest and latent functions, and dysfunctions), Marxism (the structure is built by those who own the means of production), and symbolic interactionism (Weber, Mead — the self is built by taking others' roles: "I becomes Me").

Mongolia's own way (Book 8): the senior book teaches society as a science and the state as a machine the citizen operates — sociology's three lenses, the law encountered as real contracts, dismissals and complaints, and democracy measured against Mongolia's own survey numbers.
