# Video script — Gender Equality in Our Community

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

South Sudan Primary 3 P3 Social Studies TG, printed p. 43, teaches: pupils should come to understand the need for gender equality in their community, defining examples and situations where this attitude is particularly relevant (guide-only teaching; the pupil's book's Unit 5 has no gender part).

South Sudan Primary 5 P5 Social Studies SB, printed p. 57, teaches: gender equality is where everyone is regarded as equal whether a boy or a girl and male or female, and the class surveys why the country should embrace human rights and gender equality (the gender-equality lesson is filed NEW from Book 6 in this wave).

South Sudan Primary 5 P5 Social Studies TG, printed p. 34, teaches: the guide adds that respect for human rights and gender equality underpins peaceful coexistence, including challenging gender stereotyping (the gender-equality lesson is filed NEW from Book 6 in this wave).

South Sudan Primary 7 P7 Social Studies SB, printed p. 83, teaches: gender is the state of being male or female and gender equality is having the same rights, status and opportunities regardless of one's gender; the story of Mr Adek, a village elder who educates all his children against his community's tradition, treats his wife as an equal partner and wills inheritance to daughters and sons alike, carries the equal division of labour in the family (the gender-equality lesson is filed NEW from Book 6 in this wave).

South Sudan Primary 7 P7 Social Studies SB, printed p. 96, teaches: equal representation with women actively involved, equal access to job opportunities and empowerment of women, improvement of democracy, and promotion of peace and reduction of violence (the gender-equality lesson is filed NEW from Book 6 in this wave).

South Sudan's own way: The guide widens the pupil's book in two places — national symbols are defined as the items that bring unity in a country (the presidency itself counted among them), and the peace unit is extended to the risks of mines and unexploded ordnance left by the conflict, and to gender equality in the community.
