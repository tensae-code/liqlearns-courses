**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (South Sudan).** South Sudan's teacher-guide parts are evidence only, counted nowhere in addition — South Sudan counts through its student-book parts.

South Sudan's own way (Book 6) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide widens the pupil's book in two places — national symbols are defined as the items that bring unity in a country (the presidency itself counted among them), and the peace unit is extended to the risks of mines and unexploded ordnance left by the conflict, and to gender equality in the community.

South Sudan's own way (Book 9): Citizenship is taught as a sung list of duties — loyalty, voting, peace, defending the country, paying taxes, obeying laws, helping in disasters — and leadership is measured against citizenship itself: to be a good leader a person must first be a good citizen, and the President is named as the example of both.

South Sudan's own way (Book 10) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide lifts the unit from the classroom to the state — good leaders and citizens support decision making in communities and at higher levels of governance, and respect for human rights and gender equality is named as what underpins peaceful coexistence.

South Sudan's own way (Book 13): Gender equality is argued from South Sudanese lives — an elder who educates his daughters against tradition, and four named women leaders — and peace itself is put in women's hands: the book prints the view that gender equality is a tool that can end South Sudan's civil war.
