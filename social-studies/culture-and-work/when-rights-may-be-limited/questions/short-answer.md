# When Rights May Be Limited — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Section 4.4 "Is it right to limit human rights?" — No right is absolute — every right carries duties, and rights may be limited only within the measure the law sets: the Universal Declaration's Article 29(2) and Mongolia's Constitution Article 19(2) say the same — limits only by law, to protect others' rights and freedoms, security, public order and morals; in a democracy limits are allowed in exactly two situations — where a person's act violates another's rights as a court decides, and in a declared emergency for national security; the worked example is quarantine: in an epidemic the movement of people in the affected place may be limited for a set time, to stop the disease spreading (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way — the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it (any close wording).**
