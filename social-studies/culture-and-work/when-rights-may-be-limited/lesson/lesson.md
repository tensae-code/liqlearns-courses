# When Rights May Be Limited

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: no right is absolute — every right carries duties, and rights may be limited only within the measure the law sets: the Universal Declaration's Article 29(2) and Mongolia's Constitution Article 19(2) say the same — limits only by law, to protect others' rights and freedoms, security, public order and morals; in a democracy limits are allowed in exactly two situations — where a person's act violates another's rights as a court decides, and in a declared emergency for national security; the worked example is quarantine: in an epidemic the movement of people in the affected place may be limited for a set time, to stop the disease spreading — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it.
