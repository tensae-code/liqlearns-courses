# Video script — When Rights May Be Limited

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies, printed p. 74, teaches: No right is absolute — every right carries duties, and rights may be limited only within the measure the law sets: the Universal Declaration's Article 29(2) and Mongolia's Constitution Article 19(2) say the same — limits only by law, to protect others' rights and freedoms, security, public order and morals; in a democracy limits are allowed in exactly two situations — where a person's act violates another's rights as a court decides, and in a declared emergency for national security; the worked example is quarantine: in an epidemic the movement of people in the affected place may be limited for a set time, to stop the disease spreading.

Mongolia's own way (Book 6): the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it.
