## Sub lesson 9 — Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way

Honduras's own way: civics begins inside the child's own circles — feelings, family norms and tasks, classroom rules and school authorities, a rehearsal student-government election with a paper ballot, and only then the community's elected authorities (alcaldesa, regidores, patronato, junta de agua). Grade 2 turns civics municipal — the child learns the Corporación Municipal's full membership, the open town meeting (cabildo abierto), a citizen transparency commission, and the country's own Citizen Participation Law with its three instruments (cabildo abierto, plebiscite, citizen initiatives). Grade 4 widens civics to Central America and to rights as law — human rights with the Constitution's own Article 60 and the habeas corpus and amparo remedies, sex versus gender taught for equality, and Honduras's real civic organisations (CODEH, COFADEH, the unions) named as civil society. the cycle closes with vocation and rights — three full lessons on the generations of human rights (with Honduras's own record: the most-violated civil right is physical integrity; Garífuna and Miskito peoples organising for third-generation rights), then the UN from Honduras's seat and the country's foreign relations told through its border awards. the final guide deepens the pupil book's rights cycle into doctrine and machinery — the UN treaty bodies and the three pillars, the ancient sources of rights from Hammurabi to the Iroquois constitution, the 1966 Covenants — and widens Bloque 4 from the pupil book's UN seat to Honduras's own regional house: the OAS, ODECA and SICA, and a foreign-relations history that ends at the 1992 Hague judgment and the 2014 Isla Conejo dispute. the Telebásica twin of Grade 9 teaches the same cycle at a distance learner's pace, each sequence paired with its television programme — and it is the more Honduras-specific of the two books on rights: CONADEH's creation decrees dated one by one, women's suffrage won by Decree 29 of 1955 with the literacy limit named, and the protective laws listed with their La Gaceta dates. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
