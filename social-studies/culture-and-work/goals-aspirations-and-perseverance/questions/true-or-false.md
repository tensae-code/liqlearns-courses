# Goals Aspirations and Perseverance — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: what I want to be when I grow up; the most important goal is becoming a better person who helps others; will, study, dedication and perseverance reach goals.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: a life plan (plan de vida) — a detailed list of what I want in the future, with questions (what do I want to be, in how long, what service will I give others); a plan needs discipline, organisation, commitment, effort, responsibility and punctuality.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: believe "sí podemos" — set personal objectives and fight for the life you want; never stop dreaming, because dreams become goals; positive attitudes and putting learning into practice bring success.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: a life project (proyecto de vida) organises the actions that reach our goals and must be realistic, accepting our potential and limits; Morazán, self-taught after only 18 months of school, is the model; parents are our first life models, and an autobiography gathers what identifies us as unique persons.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson's fifth part teaches: vocation and profession — work done with vocation is done with greater care and enjoyed; pedagogy and psychology recommend the vocation be a personal, voluntary and rational decision; choosing a career by institutional offer rather than vocation is the error the lesson warns against, and adults are the examples for the decision.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This lesson's sixth part teaches: life cannot be managed as a game of chance — a life project is the whole of what the person wants to be and what they will do, with the possibilities of achieving it; the lesson builds it with Robert Dilts's life-project tree and its formulating questions (what are our goals, what do we want to be after school and university, where are we going).
**Answer: True**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** This lesson's seventh part teaches: vocation is not a list to look up but a passion woken by doing many activities, and a person may hold several vocations at once; personal satisfaction in the work done well is self-realisation (autorrealización) — giving everything within our reach to make dreams and life projects concrete stage by stage — and the family's counsel, weighing each profession's pros and cons from its knowledge of the national occupational field, eases the choice.
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** This lesson's eighth part teaches: a professional is not only a title-holder — professionalism also means commitment, ethics and excellence in the work; beneath it stands the human vocation, an interior call, unique and unrepeatable in each person, to develop one's own potentialities in commitment to one's neighbour, the world and transcendence, not in selfish enclosure; a person holds not one vocation but many, and choosing a career weighs likings against abilities, economic and social possibilities and the participation in society the occupation allows — a choice without labour outlet ends in marginalisation and frustration, while recognition gratifies more than money.
**Answer: True**

**9. (Sub lesson 9)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: False — it also stands on Honduras's teacher guide(s), named in lesson/sources.md, as teacher-guide evidence.**
