# The Struggle That Made Human Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the idea of human rights grew through history's struggles — Hammurabi's written law, Confucius's golden rule, and above all Magna Carta (1215), where England's King John, pressed by his barons, signed away the power to tax, imprison or punish any free man except by lawful judgement — the first written limit on a ruler; then the English Bill of Rights (1689) and the American Declaration of Independence (1776); "the history of mankind is the history of the struggle for human rights", and the documents are compared in a class timeline game — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Section 4.1 "Human rights — the history the struggle created"

Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies, printed p. 63, teaches: The idea of human rights grew through history's struggles — Hammurabi's written law, Confucius's golden rule, and above all Magna Carta (1215), where England's King John, pressed by his barons, signed away the power to tax, imprison or punish any free man except by lawful judgement — the first written limit on a ruler; then the English Bill of Rights (1689) and the American Declaration of Independence (1776); "the history of mankind is the history of the struggle for human rights", and the documents are compared in a class timeline game. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it.
