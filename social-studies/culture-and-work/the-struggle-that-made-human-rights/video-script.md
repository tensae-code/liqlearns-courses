# Video script — The Struggle That Made Human Rights

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies, printed p. 63, teaches: The idea of human rights grew through history's struggles — Hammurabi's written law, Confucius's golden rule, and above all Magna Carta (1215), where England's King John, pressed by his barons, signed away the power to tax, imprison or punish any free man except by lawful judgement — the first written limit on a ruler; then the English Bill of Rights (1689) and the American Declaration of Independence (1776); "the history of mankind is the history of the struggle for human rights", and the documents are compared in a class timeline game.

Mongolia's own way (Book 6): the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it.
