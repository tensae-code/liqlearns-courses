Guyana Grade 1 Social Studies For Our Children Book 1 (printed p. 41), teaches: Diwali as a celebration Guyanese families mark.

Guyana Grade 1 Social Studies For Our Children Book 1 (printed p. 42), teaches: Christmas in the family — parents give toys and children receive gifts.

Guyana Grade 2 Social Studies For Our Children Book 2 (printed p. 40 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 1 above)), teaches: Neighbours celebrate Diwali, a happy time; making a Diwali card and a diya.

Guyana Grade 2 Social Studies For Our Children Book 2 (printed p. 41), teaches: Youman Nabi is a Muslim festival and a national holiday.

Guyana Grade 3 Social Studies For Our Children Book 3 (printed p. 39 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 1 above)), teaches: Phagwah, the Hindu spring festival of colours — abrack powder and abeer, the burning of Holika the night before showing good wins over evil, and peace among all Guyanese.

Guyana's own way: a story-led first book built around two children, Ron and Sue — family, school, rules and nation are all met through their home, street and school day, with a draw/name/complete task on almost every page. Book 2 widens Book 1's world — the same Ron and Sue now meet family types, class rules, neighbourhood shops and, for the first time, the country's leaders (President, Prime Minister, Ministers) and its national days (Republic Day, May Day, Enmore Martyrs' Day, Freedom Day). the whole book is one widening idea — a community is people living and working together who share services; three invented communities (Riverview, Mountain Gap, Runsvale) carry every lesson, and the book ends not on facts but on duties: courtesy, respect for national symbols, and protecting the community.
