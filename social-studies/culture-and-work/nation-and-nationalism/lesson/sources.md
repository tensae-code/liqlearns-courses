**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Pakistan).**

Pakistan's own way (Book 1): citizenship is taught before everything else — a child first learns what a citizen, a global citizen and a digital citizen is, then Pakistan's own 1973 Constitution, its federal and provincial organs with real seat counts, and only then history, geography and economics.

Pakistan's own way (Book 2): Class 5 deepens Class 4 — rights now change with time, the UN and the 1948 Declaration enter, freedom of speech gets its own lesson with John Stuart Mill quoted, and government is compared across federal, provincial AND local levels with a table of who does what.

Pakistan's own way (Book 7): at Class 10 the state tells its story whole — ideology first, then the society it made: thirteen named features of Pakistani culture, minorities' rights written into the culture chapter, and a full closing chapter on women's rights, women nation-builders and the laws that protect them.

Pakistan's own way (Book 8): the English-medium Class 9 book states the ideology in plain definitions, quotes Jinnah's promise that minorities "are free to go to your places of worship", and closes with the women's chapter in its sharpest form — myth-versus-fact boxes that refuse to blame victims.

Pakistan's own way (Book 9): the brand-new PECTAA book opens the final school year with the constitution as a living document — its amendments listed one by one to 2017 — and sets Pakistan's own rights charter side by side with the UN's, asking students to compare them.

Pakistan's own way (Book 10): the older book teaches the same state from the other end — culture defined before it is described, languages given a whole chapter with Urdu named the language of national integration, and national integration itself defined, factored and argued like a civics concept, not a slogan.

Pakistan's own way (Book 11): the same Single National Curriculum in the KPK board's English — citizenship taught identically in Peshawar and Lahore, with the global-citizen lesson anchored by Dr Ruth Pfau, the German doctor who gave 57 years to Pakistan's leprosy patients, and Malala Yousafzai, KPK's own daughter, as the worked examples in the pupil's copy.

Pakistan's own way (Book 13): KPK's older-curriculum book defines ideology with a philosopher's care — a nation's collective wisdom and predetermined objective — and lists seven things ideology does for a nation, from uniting it to guarding its culture against negative influences.

Pakistan's own way (Book 15): the same state taught in the nation's own language — the ideology's four components are listed in Urdu as Islam, democracy, social justice-and-equality, and fundamental human rights, and the integration chapter's virtues are named رواداری (tolerance), قوم سے وفاداری (loyalty to the nation) and دوسروں کے حقوق کا احترام (respect for the rights of others).
