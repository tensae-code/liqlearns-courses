# Saudi Systems and State Sovereignty — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's part teaches: Every state has systems that govern it and none may act against them — the state's sovereignty; a system as the general rules organising a defined aspect, prepared by the regulatory authority and enforced by the executive after its issuance stages; Saudi systems are of two kinds — basic systems (governance, state administration, the relations and competencies of the authorities, and individuals' main rights) and general systems (how state bodies perform their tasks and services and the rules of society's relations); their development under King Abdulaziz — the Basic Instructions of 1345H before unification (founding the Shura Council and the Council of Deputies, later the Council of Ministers), more than 250 administrative systems in his reign, all on the Islamic Shariah method — and the National Center for Archives and Records that documents and preserves them.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on Saudi Arabia's student book, named in lesson/sources.md.**
