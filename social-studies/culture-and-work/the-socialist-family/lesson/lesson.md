# The Socialist Family

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the ideal mother of the new society told as a story — her work, her care and her part in building the country — as Somalia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hooyo hantiwadaag ah (A Socialist Mother)

Somalia Grade 2 Barbaarinta iyo Cilmiga Beesha (printed p. 24), teaches: the ideal mother of the new society told as a story — her work, her care and her part in building the country. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Aabaha hantiwadaagga ah (The Socialist Father)

Somalia Grade 2 Barbaarinta iyo Cilmiga Beesha (printed p. 32), teaches: the ideal father told as a story — Raage the fisherman, a Guulwade who believes in socialism and works for his people and country, goes out in groups with men and women, and is decorated with medals of bravery — the family as the smallest unit of the new state. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Somalia's way — the books' own teaching way

Somalia's own way (Grade 2): Grade 2 teaches the same civic world through people, not topics — a mother, a father, a fisherman-hero — and through the child's own uniformed corps, the October Revolution Flowers, whose members are known by cleanliness, honesty, bravery and love of the flag. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
