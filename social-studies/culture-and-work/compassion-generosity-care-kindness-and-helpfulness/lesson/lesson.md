# Compassion Generosity Care Kindness and Helpfulness

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: compassion as a week of practice — generosity (the Giving Tree read whole, then judged in four everyday choices of one's own), care (the son who took his old father to dinner; my caring acts written down day by day), kindness (the Kindness Tree — a leaf for every kind act, a fruit when three are gathered) and helpfulness (Mother Teresa's words on helping anyone; helping at home, marked by parents) — as Bhutan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Compassion Generosity Care Kindness and Helpfulness

Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Student Workbook 2017, printed pp. 20–39, teaches: compassion as a week of practice — generosity (the Giving Tree read whole, then judged in four everyday choices of one's own), care (the son who took his old father to dinner; my caring acts written down day by day), kindness (the Kindness Tree — a leaf for every kind act, a fruit when three are gathered) and helpfulness (Mother Teresa's words on helping anyone; helping at home, marked by parents); and Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Teacher's Handbook 2017, printed pp. 24–41, teaches: TEACHER: compassion as understanding another's suffering and wanting to ease it — generosity (the Giving Tree, giving apples, branches, trunk and finally its stump), care (the son who calmly cleaned his old father in the restaurant and left behind "a lesson for every son and hope for every father"), kindness (thinking of others' feelings and expecting nothing in return) and helpfulness (doing part of another's work before being asked). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bhutan's way — the book's own teaching way

Bhutan's own way: the workbook turns each value into a week of doing — a sorry-card actually drawn and given, a day planned to the minute, kind acts collected as leaves on a classroom Kindness Tree until they earn a fruit, and obedience sealed with written pledges to teacher and parents; the values are Bhutan's own (Tha-Dam-Tse Ley-Judy, Tsawa Sum) carried in stories the whole world tells (the Giving Tree, Little Red Riding Hood, the Itsy Bitsy Spider). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
