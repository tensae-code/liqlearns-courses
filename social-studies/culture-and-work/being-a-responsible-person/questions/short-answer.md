# Being a Responsible Person — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: A person who does his duty — What a responsible child finishes, at home and at school (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Each one his own duty — Every person has a duty — mother, father, teacher, child — and the grateful heart that helps others in return (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: One's role and responsibility — Knowing your own duty and carrying it out — Shwe Madi as master of ceremony, and the planting team dividing its duties (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Do your duty in the place where you are — Every place lays a duty on the people in it — the water pot by the road, the bus passenger, the farmer, the restaurant staff (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does the fifth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: One who knows duty and takes responsibility — Seeing the duty yourself and taking it on without being told (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** What does the sixth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Myanmar's way — the book's own teaching way — every value is taught as a named story or picture-sequence about one child (or one lion cub) plus a question set — never as a rule list; the patriotism chapter teaches love of country as the eight ethnic peoples in their own dress holding hands around the Independence Monument. Grade 2 widens Grade 1's values from home to school — the same story-plus-pictures shape now carries school property, school rules, honest judgement of classroom incidents, friends with disabilities, and the whole country drawn as its ethnic children around the map. the Grade 3 units keep the story-and-poem shape but argue finer points: fairness is NOT equal shares (the hungry son gets bread, the full daughter a candy; the girl with glasses sits in front), trust is keeping a friend's weakness secret, and the clean-environment unit teaches Reduce–Reuse–Recycle by name. Grade 4 names children's RIGHTS as a picture inventory (food, water, a house, clothes, care, medicine, play, learning, family) and turns environmental care into two full units — preserving the earth and the natural surroundings, with drought, flood, felled forests and factory smoke as the evidence. (any close wording).**
