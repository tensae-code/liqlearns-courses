# Culture — language

## Do it first

Count the languages spoken in your own area — ask at home and at the market. Write the count and the names you can gather.

## Sub lesson 1 — The great communication tool

People must communicate to live. **Language is the great communication tool** and a pillar of human growth: it carries thought, growth and experience into use.

Language is also **the main vehicle that passes culture on** — experience, knowledge and heritage travel from one generation to the next inside it. And **writing thought down began about 5,000 years ago** — and raised civilisation steeply.

## Sub lesson 2 — Families of languages — and Ethiopia's four (teacher guide — evidence)

The world's languages are grouped into **families**, by how close they are to each other. The teacher guide's review key names Ethiopia's language families outright (teacher guide — evidence): **Semitic, Cushitic, Omotic and Nilo-Saharan**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Say why language is called culture's carrier. Say when writing began and what it changed. Name Ethiopia's four language families (teacher guide — evidence).

## Do It Yourself

1. Count the languages spoken in your own area — ask at home and at the market. Write the count and the names you can gather.
2. Take one proverb or one story told at home. Write what would be lost if it were never passed on in language.
3. Write one spoken sentence from home, then write it down. That second step is the 5,000-year-old step: writing.
