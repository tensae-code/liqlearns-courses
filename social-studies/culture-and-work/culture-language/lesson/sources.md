Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs, its activity numbers key to its own companion textbook, and whole student-book topics have no guide counterpart. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence, never as a page-key to the student book. Pupil content in this lesson comes from the Amhara student book.

The guide's review key spells Semitic as "Semetic" (guide verification block, error 10); the lesson teaches the corrected spelling, Semitic, and names the misprint here.

Guatemala's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Social Studies is taught through three long “learning experiences” rooted in the country's own peoples (Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino); Geography enters as lived territory — the Maya unity of peoples and territory, care for Mother Earth, and a linguistic map of Guatemala played as a lotería game. That evidence is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Zimbabwe's own way (ECD A) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum organised by cross-cutting topics repeated at every level (Identity, National history, Heritage, Work and leisure, Transport and communication, Shelter, Global issues…); teaching is play-, song- and role-play-based with workbook colouring/cutting tasks; local examples throughout (totems, pada hopscotch, veld fires, Zimbabwe outline map). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 2) (teacher guide — evidence): The spiral widens from family to community — ethnic groups and languages of Zimbabwe (Kalanga, Sotho and others), production in the community, shelter types in the neighbourhood (granary, silos, community hall, clinic), and global issues introduced as floods and deforestation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 5) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 5 transport is taught as a landlocked country's system — goods by rail/road/air/sea, animal transport (riding, leading, herding), and the roadports, seaports and airports Zimbabwe depends on; shelter links heritage sites (Matobo Hills) and map-drawing of Zimbabwe to building safely in disaster-prone areas. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 2 G2 CS Viva Venezuela): population education (UNESCO population-education guides reworked) woven into story form — Tío Conejo tales, grandparents' memories and the child's own school and street carry population, housing, landscape and weather-sign content.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 3 np-g3): Grade 3 introduces Nepal's three ecological regions — Himalayan (cold, Gaurishankar), Hilly (Kathmandu valley) and Terai (flat, hot, Dhangadhi) — through three children's home places, with rivers (Tamakoshi, Narayani), landforms and climate tied to each child's village/town.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 np-g4): Grade 4 "Our Earth" moves to the district (Kanchanpur read on a map), the origin and structure of the Earth, and Nepal's two neighbours India and China; resources and traffic rules sit inside the Civic Awareness unit.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 np-g4b): The newer (2078 BS) trial book reorganises the same ground by units ending in "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth): Earth as our common home, our needs from the Earth, our local level, and drawing a map / making a map of our village; economic life is taught through production, income-generating work and the haat bazaar (periodic market).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 5 np-g5): Grade 5 "Our Earth" is a first full geography of Nepal — physical features, climate and lifestyle, natural vegetation, soils, map-drawing with symbols, globe and maps, and the wider South Asian neighbours; economic geography is district-based (Sankhuwasabha's occupations mapped place by place, from yak and sheep rearing and herb collecting in the high Himalaya to paddy in the low valleys).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 5 Estudios Sociales G5): The scale widens from Nicaragua to the whole Central American isthmus: Units I–IV give the planet and map skills, then Central America's position, relief, climate, hydrography, resources, population and economies, with Nicaragua's place in the isthmus as an explicit topic; the second half is Central American History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit V first settlers, Maya and Aztec history (printed pp. 70–90, History, except the two topics placed below); Unit VI European presence (printed pp. 92–111, History); Unit VII independence process (printed pp. 114–140, History); Unit VIII Central America in the 20th century (printed pp. 142–154, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 Estudios Sociales G8 full-year): Grade 8 widens the lens to the whole American continent: five Geography units on America's territorial patrimony, physical landscape, ethnic-cultural diversity, space-and-economy and environmental protection (closing with Nicaragua's own SINAPRED disaster system), then five History units. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI American origins (printed pp. 125–138, History); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans and colonial domination (printed pp. 145–165, History); Unit VIII emancipation processes (printed pp. 167–184, History); Unit IX changes in Latin American society (printed pp. 185–194, History); Unit X America in the 20th century (printed pp. 197–222, History).
