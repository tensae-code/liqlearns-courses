# Respecting Life Yours and Others

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a life just born is the one and only precious life; the wish inside your own name, heard from your family; when we feel "I am alive"; and the tale of the elephants' graveyard — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — (1) いのちを大切に (Treasure life)

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 90), teaches: A life just born is the one and only precious life; the wish inside your own name, heard from your family; when we feel "I am alive"; and the tale of the elephants' graveyard. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 読みもの「ハムスターの赤ちゃん」("The Baby Hamsters")

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 96), teaches: Watching baby hamsters — born hairless and blind, drinking their mother's milk — grow day by day (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — 生きているってすばらしい (Being alive is wonderful)

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 100), teaches: Yanase Takashi's song "Te no hira wo taiyou ni" — we are all alive, and that is why we sing and why we are sad. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — (1) 命あるものを大切に (Treasure everything that has life)

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 90), teaches: Every living thing's life is precious; how we feel when a life is struggling to live. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 5 — 読みもの「ヒキガエルとロバ」("The Toad and the Donkey")

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 96), teaches: A story of animals' lives and the people around them, read for treasuring living things (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 100), teaches: Each life is the only one of its kind, and lives are connected to many other lives. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 98), teaches: Each life is one and irreplaceable, carried by many people's support. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 8 — 読みもの「かけがえのない命 ―命てんでんこ」("Irreplaceable Life — Inochi Tendenko")

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 108), teaches: The tsunami lesson of 11 March 2011 — run, each for yourself, so all may live (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 9 — Japan's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 10 — Japan's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 11 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
