Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 45–46), teaches: In all societies women were long given subordinate roles and their contributions to development were not recognised; the equality movement and the UN Decade for Women (1976–85) asserted equality in dignity, rights, opportunities and responsibilities; women hold four important roles — producers, home managers, mothers and community organisers — and International Women's Day, March 8, grew out of working women's protests for better wages, conditions and the vote.

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 47–48 (NEW slug first proposed in the line above)), teaches: Traditionally Guyanese women were seen in the domestic sphere — mothers, cooks and wives — their work a "labour of love", unrecognised as work; today women choose to work or stay home, most work longer hours than men, and they have moved into the informal sector, the professions and the military and para-military sectors.

Guyana's own way: the secondary book teaches the same citizenship at national scale — CARICOM as the region's own institution, the family as society's oldest institution, women's equality written into the 1980 Constitution and the Acts of the 1990s, and the state answering pollution through the Environmental Protection Agency.
