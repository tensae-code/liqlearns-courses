The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Civic Ethics Grade 6, printed p. 11, teaches: A wrong act begins in a wrong thought, is spoken, and is then done — so it is corrected at the root: the tree of wrong acts (body: taking life, stealing; speech: lying, gossip, harsh words; mind: greed, malice) against the tree of right acts; the daily practice of examining my acts morning, noon and evening, judging what I thought, said and did, and resolving the correction — with the "Who am I?" essay, in which the writer finds a second self inside who hears criticism, fears, dreams and must be known.

Mongolia's own way (Link book L2): from Grade 6 the civics subject changes its name and its method — no longer "Civic Education" but "Civic Ethics" (Иргэний ёс зүйн боловсрол), a self-observation workbook in the Buddhist-inflected Mongolian moral tradition: the pupil keeps a diary of their own acts of body, speech and mind, and ethics is practised as a daily discipline before it is ever a rule about others.
