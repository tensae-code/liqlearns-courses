# Video script — Efforts to Realise a Just Society

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

South Korea's Grade 10 MiraeN Integrated Society, printed p. 182; Grade 10 Bisang Integrated Society, printed p. 182; Grade 10 Donga Integrated Society, printed p. 176, teaches: VI.주제5 정의로운 사회 실현을 위한 노력, VI.3 불평등의 해결과 정의의 실현, VI.04 사회 불평등의 올바른 해결 방안은? — teaches: efforts to realise a just society — social safety nets and affirmative measures; solving inequality and realising justice in practice; the right ways to solve social inequality.

South Korea's own way: the high-school common subject 통합사회 (Integrated Society) is built around nine key questions asked from temporal, spatial, social and ethical perspectives at once; its civics core is Unit IV (human rights guaranteed by the constitution, law-abiding spirit and citizen participation) and Unit VI (justice and inequality), and its peace unit teaches the peaceful resolution of Korea's own division and of East Asia's history conflicts as the closing civics content. Bisang's Integrated Society binds its justice unit shut with a practice section (불평등의 해결과 정의의 실현 — solving inequality and realising justice) and its culture unit with creative succession of traditional culture; its peace unit is framed as East Asia's conflicts and international peace rather than the two Koreas alone. Donga writes every lesson as a question ("Why is civil disobedience ever needed?", "What does universal ethics do in understanding a culture?") and is the only one of the five publishers to teach civil disobedience (시민 불복종) beside the law-abiding spirit, and to split the conditions of happiness into two lessons — living environment/economic stability, then democracy's development and moral practice.
