# Human Rights How People Won Them

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: rights were won by long struggle — Magna Carta binding King John in 13th-century England, the fall of Charles I, the 1689 Bill of Rights and the Habeas Corpus Act limiting arbitrary arrest — as Sri Lanka's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 15 Protecting human rights: protecting freedom and equality

Sri Lanka Grade 10 Social Education 1998, printed pp. 142–143, teaches: Rights were won by long struggle — Magna Carta binding King John in 13th-century England, the fall of Charles I, the 1689 Bill of Rights and the Habeas Corpus Act limiting arbitrary arrest. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 15 The struggle continues

Sri Lanka Grade 10 Social Education 1998, printed pp. 143–144, teaches: The American and French revolutions and the rights declarations they issued, then the 20th-century struggles — Russia 1917, China 1911 and 1949 — and the rights struggles inside Asian countries, Sri Lanka's own among them. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way

Sri Lanka's own way: systems of government are taught as a ladder the pupil climbs from Sri Lanka's own unitary state outward — democracy defined through Lincoln and the French declaration, its three freedoms separated and worked one by one, dictatorship and monarchy set against it with Mussolini, Hitler and Ashoka as the named cases, and human rights told as a single long struggle from Magna Carta to the UN Charter and the regional charters of Europe, America and Africa. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
