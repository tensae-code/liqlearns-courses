# Hands-on — Human Rights How People Won Them

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Ch. 15 Protecting human rights: protecting freedom and equality
Work through this part with your hands: rights were won by long struggle — Magna Carta binding King John in 13th-century England, the fall of Charles I, the 1689 Bill of Rights and the Habeas Corpus Act limiting arbitrary arrest. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Ch. 15 The struggle continues
Work through this part with your hands: the American and French revolutions and the rights declarations they issued, then the 20th-century struggles — Russia 1917, China 1911 and 1949 — and the rights struggles inside Asian countries, Sri Lanka's own among them. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: systems of government are taught as a ladder the pupil climbs from Sri Lanka's own unitary state outward — democracy defined through Lincoln and the French declaration, its three freedoms separated and worked one by one, dictatorship and monarchy set against it with Mussolini, Hitler and Ashoka as the named cases, and human rights told as a single long struggle from Magna Carta to the UN Charter and the regional charters of Europe, America and Africa.. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
