**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Iran, Egypt, Philippines, Samoa, Ecuador). Albania is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 7 Social Studies): one combined Social Studies book of 24 lessons in 12 chapters. The Geography parts placed below are Ch. 5 Knowing our living environment (Lessons 9–10, printed pp. 43–56), Ch. 6 Protecting Iran's habitats (Lessons 11–12, printed pp. 57–72), Ch. 7 Population and resources (Lessons 13–14, printed pp. 73–88) and Ch. 8 Tourism in Iran (Lessons 15–16, printed pp. 89–102), plus the sectors part of Ch. 4. The other parts are named here, not placed: Civics — Ch. 1 Our rights and responsibilities (Lessons 1–2, printed pp. 1–12), Ch. 2 Law (Lessons 3–4, printed pp. 13–20), Ch. 3 Coping with incidents — empathy and insurance (Lessons 5–6, printed pp. 21–30); Economics — Ch. 4 Production, distribution, consumption (Lesson 8 Consumption, printed pp. 37–42; Lesson 7 placed above); History and Culture — Ch. 9 Our land, searching the distant past (Lessons 17–18, from printed p. 103), Ch. 10 Iran in ancient times (Lessons 19–20), Ch. 11 The coming of Islam to Iran and Islamic Iran (Lessons 21–22), and Ch. 12 Culture and art (Lessons 23–24) fill the rest of the book (printed pp. 103–178).

Egypt's own way (Egypt Grade 4 Social Studies T1): the child's first government lesson is the map of their own governorate — the state is introduced as the administration that runs the child's home province, with its own emblem and feast day, before it is ever a parliament or a president.

Philippines's own way: the whole third unit of Grade 4 is one civics arc — the national government and its three branches first, then levels down to the local government, then what government does for citizens (health, education, peace, infrastructure, rights), closing on local government working with community servants; the Bill of Rights is taught straight from Article III of the Philippine Constitution.

Samoa's own way: Social organisation is taught from the inside out — the groups a child belongs to first, then the aiga and its matai, then the village's own circle of groups (matai at the centre, aumaga as the matai's right hand), and only then a neighbour kingdom's political system as the worked example of government.


Albania's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Citizenship in Grade 5 is taught as a ladder — family, group, community, then the state: democracy is first practised inside the family and in class elections, local self-government is compared with the class's own elections, and a whole chapter (Kreu 3, The Road) treats the child as pedestrian, cyclist and passenger with duties on the road.

Ecuador's own way (Estudios Sociales 3ro EGB (SM 2016)): the SM edition teaches local government from the child's own ground up — parish, then canton — with Ecuador's real institutions (the elected junta parroquial, the teniente político, the concejo municipal and the GADs), and teaches values through the Andean minga and the Kichwa reciprocity idea randi-randi, under the book's recurring Cultura del Buen Vivir spreads. That evidence is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing; it is counted in the acceptance count above (student book).
