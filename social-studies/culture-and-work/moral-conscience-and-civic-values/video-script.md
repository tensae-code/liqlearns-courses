The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Honduras Grade 8 G8 Student CCSS, printed p. 14, teaches: Moral conscience makes us distinguish good actions from bad; moral and civic values regulate human conduct through our conscious acts under society's laws, norms and customs, and what opposes them is an antivalue; civic values strengthen respect for fundamental rights and guarantees and the fulfilment of duties, preparing responsible citizens of conviction; the class works through written moral dilemmas, and today's media also shape the young's models and values.

Honduras Grade 8 G8 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 12, teaches: Moral conscience is the knowledge we have or must have of moral norms and rules — the faculty that lets us judge whether our moral conduct is valuable or not; two positions explain its nature: the innatist (conscience is born with the person) and the empirical; from belief in the supernatural it was seen as an expression of divine law — in medieval Christianity, God gave conscience so humans could recognise the natural law — while naturalist positions defend other origins.

Honduras's own way (Book 8): Grade 8 is the moral-and-legal year — moral conscience and dilemmas, the Anti-Bullying Law read article by article, the rule of law traced to 1789, and human rights taught through the Americas' own instruments (the American Declaration, the OAS, the anti-torture and forced-disappearance conventions).
