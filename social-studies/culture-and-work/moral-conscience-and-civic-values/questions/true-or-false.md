# Moral Conscience and Civic Values — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: moral conscience makes us distinguish good actions from bad; moral and civic values regulate human conduct through our conscious acts under society's laws, norms and customs, and what opposes them is an antivalue; civic values strengthen respect for fundamental rights and guarantees and the fulfilment of duties, preparing responsible citizens of conviction; the class works through written moral dilemmas, and today's media also shape the young's models and values.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: moral conscience is the knowledge we have or must have of moral norms and rules — the faculty that lets us judge whether our moral conduct is valuable or not; two positions explain its nature: the innatist (conscience is born with the person) and the empirical; from belief in the supernatural it was seen as an expression of divine law — in medieval Christianity, God gave conscience so humans could recognise the natural law — while naturalist positions defend other origins.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: False — it also stands on Honduras's teacher guide(s), named in lesson/sources.md, as teacher-guide evidence.**
