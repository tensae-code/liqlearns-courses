# Video script — Loving My Country and Its Peoples

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Myanmar Morality and Civics, Grade 1 (printed p. 55), teaches: Myanmar is village and town together, and its eight ethnic peoples (Kachin, Kayah, Karen, Chin, Bamar, Mon, Rakhine, Shan) in their own dress, hands joined around the Independence Monument.

Myanmar Morality and Civics, Grade 2 (printed p. 57), teaches: Myanmar on the map with its ethnic children in their own dress under the flag, and the "blood-relations" song naming the peoples one by one.

Myanmar Morality and Civics Grade 3 (printed p. 56), teaches: Loving your native place and the whole union — highlanders and plains people as siblings in the "Our Union" poem, different languages, one national spirit.

Myanmar Teacher Guide — Morality and Civic — Grade 6 (printed p. 46), teaches: Love of country through the "Forever Myanmar" song and the "Our grandparents' heritage" poem.

Myanmar's own way (Book 2): every value is taught as a named story or picture-sequence about one child (or one lion cub) plus a question set — never as a rule list; the patriotism chapter teaches love of country as the eight ethnic peoples in their own dress holding hands around the Independence Monument.

Myanmar's own way (Book 5): Grade 2 widens Grade 1's values from home to school — the same story-plus-pictures shape now carries school property, school rules, honest judgement of classroom incidents, friends with disabilities, and the whole country drawn as its ethnic children around the map.

Myanmar's own way (Book 7): the Grade 3 units keep the story-and-poem shape but argue finer points: fairness is NOT equal shares (the hungry son gets bread, the full daughter a candy; the girl with glasses sits in front), trust is keeping a friend's weakness secret, and the clean-environment unit teaches Reduce–Reuse–Recycle by name.

Myanmar's own way (Book 8): Grade 6 is where the values course becomes a civics course — rights and responsibilities are named as such, the school has a council, rules are explained up to the rules of a country, and the last unit is peace: what conflict is, the bundle-of-firewood lesson, peace symbols, and rowing in unison.
