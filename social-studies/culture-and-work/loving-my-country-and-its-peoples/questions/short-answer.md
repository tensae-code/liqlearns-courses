# Loving My Country and Its Peoples — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: A person who loves the country — Myanmar is village and town together, and its eight ethnic peoples (Kachin, Kayah, Karen, Chin, Bamar, Mon, Rakhine, Shan) in their own dress, hands joined around the Independence Monument (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Our special country — Myanmar on the map with its ethnic children in their own dress under the flag, and the "blood-relations" song naming the peoples one by one (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Cherish the motherland — Loving your native place and the whole union — highlanders and plains people as siblings in the "Our Union" poem, different languages, one national spirit (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Our country that we love — Love of country through the "Forever Myanmar" song and the "Our grandparents' heritage" poem (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does the fifth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Myanmar's way — the book's own teaching way — every value is taught as a named story or picture-sequence about one child (or one lion cub) plus a question set — never as a rule list; the patriotism chapter teaches love of country as the eight ethnic peoples in their own dress holding hands around the Independence Monument. Grade 2 widens Grade 1's values from home to school — the same story-plus-pictures shape now carries school property, school rules, honest judgement of classroom incidents, friends with disabilities, and the whole country drawn as its ethnic children around the map. the Grade 3 units keep the story-and-poem shape but argue finer points: fairness is NOT equal shares (the hungry son gets bread, the full daughter a candy; the girl with glasses sits in front), trust is keeping a friend's weakness secret, and the clean-environment unit teaches Reduce–Reuse–Recycle by name. Grade 6 is where the values course becomes a civics course — rights and responsibilities are named as such, the school has a council, rules are explained up to the rules of a country, and the last unit is peace: what conflict is, the bundle-of-firewood lesson, peace symbols, and rowing in unison. (any close wording).**
