# The Herders Wisdom

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: herding is a fine knowledge handed down by work, experience and teaching: the herder reads the sky, the sun, the plants and the moon to know tomorrow's weather, works with the seasons ("in autumn race the fattening, in winter race the cold, in spring race the birthing, in summer do not race at all"), and reads the herd's behaviour — how sheep and goats graze tells their condition — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 8 "From the traditional wisdom of herding" (Мал маллах уламжлалт арга ухаанаас)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 29, teaches: Herding is a fine knowledge handed down by work, experience and teaching: the herder reads the sky, the sun, the plants and the moon to know tomorrow's weather, works with the seasons ("in autumn race the fattening, in winter race the cold, in spring race the birthing, in summer do not race at all"), and reads the herd's behaviour — how sheep and goats graze tells their condition. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
