# Lesson plan — Fairs and Festivals of Pakistan

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Fairs and Festivals of Pakistan
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Pakistan, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ch. 2 Fairs and Festivals, Ch. 2 Fairs and Festivals; Popular Events, Ch. 3 Religious Festivals in Pakistan, Ch. 6 Salient Features: Religious Festivals and Urs and Fairs — teaches: Pakistanis celebrate religious and national occasions together — Eid gatherings, urs and melas at shrines, Shandur polo, Kalam mela and Jashn-e-Baharan pictured; Eid and the urs melas bring every community together, and the nation's shared days are kept by all — Pakistan Day (23 March), Independence Day (14 August), Defence Day (6 September) and Quaid-e-Azam Day (25 December); Christmas (25 December, a public holiday in Pakistan), Eid-ul-Fitr (with fitrana charity so the poor share the joy) and Guru Nanak's birthday at Nankana Sahib are celebrated across faiths — neighbours of every religion join each other's festivals; Eid, Ramadan observances and the other faiths' festivals, plus the saints' urs gatherings and village fairs that draw crowds of every background — as Pakistan's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
