The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Saudi Arabia Grade 6 Social Studies Term 1 (printed pp. 155–159), teaches: Antiquities as the homeland's civilisational record expressing its history and heritage, found in museums and historic sites (al-Faw, al-Rabadha, Jurash, al-Hijr); preserving them is a national duty — the Heritage Commission cares for them and the antiquities law punishes offences (unlicensed digging, seizing, damaging or forging antiquities); their kinds — buildings, water installations, pottery, glass, inscriptions, coins, weapons and jewellery — and what the homeland gains from them: national belonging, culture, tourism and documented history.

Saudi Arabia's own way: the Grade 6 book ties citizenship to Saudi Vision 2030 — what a vision is, its realisation programmes and projects, the Quality of Life Programme and the Human Capability Development Programme — and then to citizenship itself, honesty and trustworthiness, guarding national antiquities, and taxes and VAT.
