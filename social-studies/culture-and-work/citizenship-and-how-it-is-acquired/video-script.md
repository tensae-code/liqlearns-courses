Uganda P5 Home-Study (Merged) (SST printed p. 68 (PDF p. 342)), teaches: Becoming a ugandan citizen by registration or by descent (parents or grandparents of the indigenous communities of 1926), and the rights and duties of citizens.

Uganda Excel P6 SST (Term-Three printed p. 46 (PDF p. 190)), teaches: Citizenship as the legal right of belonging to a country; a ugandan acquires it by birth, by descent and by registration/naturalisation.

Uganda's own way (Uganda P5 Home-Study (Merged)): the home-study civics teaches the three organs through the three cooking stones — remove one stand and the pot falls; then the constitution Uganda has had since independence, and the citizen's rights and duties.
Uganda's own way (Uganda Excel P6 SST): P6 looks outward and inward at once — the East African Community's own organs and symbols beside the member states' flags, then the citizen: what citizenship is, the child's rights, what violating rights looks like (mob justice named plainly), and how elections actually run.
