Venezuela Grade 4 G4 CS Venezuela y Su Gente, printed p. 146, teaches: Humanity's basic needs — five fundamental needs at every moment of life (to eat, dress, be housed, be cured, be entertained) plus water as a vital need and the services that supply them; and the citizen's side of the bargain: paying taxes is the duty that gives right to public services — a tax is the money contribution the nation and municipality require, income tax direct on salary or profits, VAT indirect on everything bought, vehicle and property taxes to the municipality — funding street cleaning, hospitals and schools.

Venezuela Grade 5 G5 CS Venezuela y Su Gente, printed p. 128, teaches: Satisfying basic needs is a human right — like all Earth's inhabitants, Venezuelans have indispensable needs from birth through their whole development, and meeting them is not a favour but a human right.

Venezuela's own way: the closing block builds citizenship in steps — family first (a community with its own norms, duties and rights), then the homeland's symbols, then humanity's rights (the 1948 UN Declaration read article by article), then the needs those rights protect and the taxes that pay for the services, and finally Venezuela's own 1999 Constitution — participatory democracy and its four referendums — as the nation's rulebook for living well. Grade 5's citizenship block is a complete primary civics course in one closing movement — citizenship, the school as the first community where citizens are formed (with student councils by law), needs as human rights, the constitution as fundamental law, the five public powers including Bolívar's own Citizen and Electoral powers, and the municipal power as the one closest to daily life.
