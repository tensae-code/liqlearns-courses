## Sub lesson 1 — Fulfilling Responsibilities

Tanzania Standard Six tz-civic6-en (printed p. 87), teaches: Identifying and fulfilling responsibilities at home, school, in the community and the country, leadership at family level (a leader shows the way), solving problems with others' involvement, and revealing corruption and fraud while fulfilling responsibilities. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Being Responsible

Tanzania Standard Five tz-civic5-en (printed p. 89), teaches: Responsibilities are duties that must be done voluntarily, not by force — personal responsibilities (sweeping, cleaning, watering the garden, home duties) and social responsibilities given by the community (class monitor, association secretary, leader); readiness means willingly fulfilling duties to standard, accepting advice humbly, and doing even useful things outside your timetable; being responsible connects to good governance and obeying the law. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tanzania's way — the books' own teaching way

Tanzania's own way (Standard Six, Civic and Moral Education Standard Six Pupil's Book): TIE teaches civics as a good-governance wheel (the cover itself: transparency, accountability, participation, human rights, integrity, efficiency, reconciliation, democracy, equality, rule of law around “GOOD GOVERNANCE”) and builds the state from the child's own levels — street, village, ward, district, region, nation — with the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar as the founding fact and Kiswahili, unity and humanism (utu) as named national treasures. Tanzania's own way (Standard Five, Civic and Moral Education Standard Five Pupil's Book): Standard Five civics teaches the state from the ward up — Ward Development Committee, district council and its heads of departments, regional commissioner — and the nation through its symbols in detail (the emblem's thirteen signs, the shilling, the Uhuru torch first lit 9 December 1961, national festivals from Zanzibar Revolution Day to Farmers' Day); democracy is taught with political parties' duties and political tolerance, and peace as peaceful coexistence that condemns segregation on any ground. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
