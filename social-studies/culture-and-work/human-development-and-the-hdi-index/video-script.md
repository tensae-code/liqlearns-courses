Honduras Grade 9 G9 Telebásica Student CCSS, printed p. 229, teaches: Human development is the process by which a society improves its citizens' living conditions, widening the goods that cover basic and complementary needs inside an environment where everyone's human rights are respected — it is the quantity of options a person has to be or do what they wish, and so a way of measuring quality of life and grading a country; its pillars begin with equity (equal access to opportunities) and sustainability (not compromising future generations); the UNDP's Human Development Index joins three parameters — a long and healthy life, education and a dignified standard of living — and Honduras's 2016 index, 0.625, placed it 130th, in medium human development (the sequence's remaining pages are population growth, distribution and migration — Geography, named not placed).

Honduras's own way (Book 19): the Telebásica twin of Grade 9 teaches the same cycle at a distance learner's pace, each sequence paired with its television programme — and it is the more Honduras-specific of the two books on rights: CONADEH's creation decrees dated one by one, women's suffrage won by Decree 29 of 1955 with the literacy limit named, and the protective laws listed with their La Gaceta dates.
