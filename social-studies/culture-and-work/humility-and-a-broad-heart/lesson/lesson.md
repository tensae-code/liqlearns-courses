# Humility and a Broad Heart

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: humility and breadth of heart toward others — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — (4) けんきょに、広い心をもって (Humbly, with a broad heart)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 80), teaches: Humility and breadth of heart toward others. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 読みもの「銀のしょく台」("The Silver Candlesticks")

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 82), teaches: Mercy that changes a person, from the story of Jean Valjean and the bishop (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — 読みもの「ブランコ乗りとピエロ」("The Trapeze Artist and the Clown")

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 84), teaches: A circus story read for the unit's theme of humility and a broad heart (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
