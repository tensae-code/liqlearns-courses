**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Rwanda, Uganda, Bahamas, Uzbekistan, Vietnam, Indonesia).**

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Panduan Guru Ekonomi Edisi Revisi) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide works with real Indonesian data — provincial per-capita PDRB tables, BPS poverty lines, the open unemployment rate (TPT), provincial minimum wages (UMP) — and adds online-loan (pinjol) financial-literacy teaching. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Indonesia is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Ekonomi): growth measured on Indonesia's own PDB series; trade taught through Indonesia's nickel-export case; budgets are Indonesia's real APBN/APBD with village funds (Dana Desa) as the regional-budget story.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Panduan Guru Ekonomi) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide's worked answers run on the student book's Indonesian data — PDB growth series, Indonesia's trade and balance-of-payments cases, the APBN/APBD. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Indonesia is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Bahamas' own way: public finance is taught through the Ministry of Finance and the national budget — the ministry creates and reports the Budget, and government borrows for capital works such as roads and schools.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): P6 widens the frame from the province to the country, then to East Africa and Africa — Rwanda is located by latitude and longitude (1°S–3°S, 28°E–31°E), its landlocked position is taught as the reason for regional co-operation, and every unit is built around a problem statement with the pupil's book activities answered in the guide. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Rwanda is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies SB): the P6 pupil's book is the fullest Geography book of the set — a whole unit on direction and location works Rwanda onto the East African and African maps by latitude and longitude and time zones, then physical features and climate, and a climate-change sequence (types, change, effects, overcoming) sits inside it; regional co-operation is taught bloc by bloc (COMESA, CEPGL, ECCAS, AU, NEPAD).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Primary 5 Excel Standard Social Studies): the national budget is Uganda's own — a financial year running 1 July–30 June, prepared by the Ministry of Finance — and the three kinds of budget (surplus, balanced, deficit) are taught with the causes and dangers of a deficit, at Primary Five.
