# Why Rights And Equality Matter — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Section 4.3 "Why are rights and equality important?" — 7.2 billion people differ in colour, faith, language, culture and custom, and each country's development differs too — yet rights are equal; people differ naturally (age, sex, body — төрөлхийн ялгаа) and by acquisition (education, work, wealth — төрөлхийн бус ялгаа), and difference itself is no wrong — the wrong is treating someone worse for it: discrimination (ялгаварлан гадуурхалт) is any distinction on grounds of ethnicity, colour, age, sex, language, religion, opinion, origin or property that denies equal rights; special measures that lift a disadvantaged group to equality — like the support that carried a boy with a disability through school to success — are not discrimination (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way — the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it (any close wording).**
