# Why Rights And Equality Matter

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: 7.2 billion people differ in colour, faith, language, culture and custom, and each country's development differs too — yet rights are equal; people differ naturally (age, sex, body — төрөлхийн ялгаа) and by acquisition (education, work, wealth — төрөлхийн бус ялгаа), and difference itself is no wrong — the wrong is treating someone worse for it: discrimination (ялгаварлан гадуурхалт) is any distinction on grounds of ethnicity, colour, age, sex, language, religion, opinion, origin or property that denies equal rights; special measures that lift a disadvantaged group to equality — like the support that carried a boy with a disability through school to success — are not discrimination — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Section 4.3 "Why are rights and equality important?"

Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies, printed p. 70, teaches: 7.2 billion people differ in colour, faith, language, culture and custom, and each country's development differs too — yet rights are equal; people differ naturally (age, sex, body — төрөлхийн ялгаа) and by acquisition (education, work, wealth — төрөлхийн бус ялгаа), and difference itself is no wrong — the wrong is treating someone worse for it: discrimination (ялгаварлан гадуурхалт) is any distinction on grounds of ethnicity, colour, age, sex, language, religion, opinion, origin or property that denies equal rights; special measures that lift a disadvantaged group to equality — like the support that carried a boy with a disability through school to success — are not discrimination. ## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it.
