The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 130), teaches: Working people's faces shine; everyone in town, school and home works for someone; my own week on lunch duty, wiping the serving table clean and being thanked.
Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 134), teaches: Bear delivers letters and parcels house to house through the forest, even in the snow, and the forest animals rejoice (reading).
Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 130), teaches: Why people work, the jobs done for everyone at school (cleaning duty and the rest), and workers who shine at their work, including a heart surgeon.

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood.

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out.
