**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Mozambique, Dominican Republic, Palestine, Venezuela). Iceland is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere. Bolivia is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 8 Venezuela G8 Historia de la Humanidad SB Spanish): economic systems are taught as history's engine — modes of production (feudal, capitalist, socialist) are defined and compared, and the 1929 crisis is taught with its mechanism: overproduction meeting wages that did not rise. This lesson teaches the placements' content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 6 Ciencias Sociales 6° Primaria): economic systems arrive inside world history — capitalism is introduced through the Scientific and Industrial Revolutions and Adam Smith, then set against socialism and Marx in a compare-the-systems table the pupils build themselves, opening with the question of whether their own country is capitalist or socialist.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 7 Ciencias Sociales 1° Secundaria): at secondary level the tax lesson becomes concrete Dominican fiscal practice — the ITBIS rate a pupil sees added at the shop till, its reduced rate and its exemptions for the family food basket, water, electricity, health and education.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 7): industry taught as a long history — from Palaeolithic tool-making through manufacture and mercantilism to the Industrial Revolution and its consequences — before location, types and industrial landscapes; commerce likewise from barter and the invention of coin to trade blocs and unequal world flows.

Iceland's own way: economics arrives inside 19th-century history — pupils role-play a factory meeting where wages must fall, and rank strike-decision cards, before they ever meet a textbook definition. This Iceland material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.
