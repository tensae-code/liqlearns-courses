# Video script — Gender Equity Indigenous Institutions and Law

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe Grade 7 zw-g7 PlusOne Social Science Grade 7 Teacher's Guide (printed pp. 69–71), teaches: Gender equity is fair treatment of all genders; Zimbabwe's own promoters are named — indigenous institutions, the Zimbabwe Gender Commission, Msasa Project, Women's Bank and community savings groups (VSLAs) — with Constitution excerpts, equal provision of resources and encouragement of positive behaviour in boys and girls.
Zimbabwe Grade 7 zw-ppp-g7 Best Approach to Heritage-Social Studies Teacher's Resource Book Grade 7 (printed pp. 22–32), teaches: Naming indigenous institutions that promote gender equity and explaining the importance of gender equity.
Zimbabwe Grade 6 zw-g6 PlusOne Social Science Grade 6 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 72), teaches: Gender equity is the fair treatment of men and women based on their needs, not stereotypes, giving equal access to resources, jobs and leadership; it improves fairness, teamwork and problem-solving, provides role models for boys and girls, and leads to prosperity and justice.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 9) (teacher guide — evidence): the primary cycle closes with adult law taught straight — inheritance under the Deceased Estates Succession Act with the Master of the High Court role-played in class, and gender equity taught through Zimbabwe's own institutions (Zimbabwe Gender Commission, Msasa Project, Women's Bank, community savings groups) with Constitution excerpts in the materials list.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 11) (teacher guide — evidence): the parallel Grade 7 book adds what the PlusOne guide does not teach — elections themselves (forms of elections, the electoral process, role-played class elections for class monitor) — and teaches international organisations as a table of UN agencies with their functions and years of formation.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 8) (teacher guide — evidence): the three arms of government are taught through school comparisons (executive = school head, legislature = student council, judiciary = disciplinary committee) and the full bill-to-Act path; protection law is explicit — the age of consent is 18, sex with a minor is statutory rape, and child pledging is named as a harmful past practice; community support to families still runs through nhimbe/ilima communal work parties.
