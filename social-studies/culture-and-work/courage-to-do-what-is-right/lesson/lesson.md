# Courage to Do What is Right

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the courage to do the right thing even when it is hard or laughed at — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — (3) 正しいことは勇気をもって (Do what is right, with courage)

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 30), teaches: The courage to do the right thing even when it is hard or laughed at. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 読みもの「よわむし太郎」("Taro the Coward")

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 34), teaches: A boy called a coward by everyone finds the courage to act for what is right (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Japan's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
