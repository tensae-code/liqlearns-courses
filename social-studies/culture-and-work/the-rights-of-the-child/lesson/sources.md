**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Bahrain, Bahamas, Libya, Afghanistan, Guatemala).**

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): citizenship is taught before nation history — the child first meets the law-abiding citizen, the community and their own rights, island by island.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): the fused lower-primary Social Science book carries citizenship from Grade 1 — rights, leaders, my country and being a peaceful citizen sit between the Bible topics and the science topics.

Bahrain's own way: citizenship is taught outwards from the child — homeland love and public property first, then the services of Bahrain's own government, the child's own rights under the UN Convention Bahrain ratified, choosing leaders from the class council up to the King, Crown Prince and Prime Minister, and finally Bahrain inside the Gulf Cooperation Council, its own regional family.

Searchable tags: rights of the child, UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, children's rights, Bahrain, social studies, culture-and-work, Bahamas, Libya, Afghanistan, Palestine

Libya's own way (National Education G6): Grade 6 defines the citizen by a chain of relations — rights paired with duties, freedom paired with responsibility, then one lesson per circle the child belongs to (family, school, neighbourhood) — and grounds the rights lesson in named world instruments: "human rights" as the UN defines them, the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child, and UNICEF.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 6 Social Studies): the Afghan state is taught as the child's own house — society needs a state as a family needs order; the 2004 constitution is the "mother law"; the two houses are named in Pashto (Wolesi Jirga, Meshrano Jirga) and every right (children's, women's, human dignity) is grounded first in Islam and then in the constitution's own articles.
