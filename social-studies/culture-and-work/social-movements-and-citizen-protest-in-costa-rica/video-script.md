# Video script — Social Movements and Citizen Protest in Costa Rica

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Costa Rica Grade 11 CONED Antología 11 (printed p. 111), teaches: Social movements as citizen participation since the 1980s — university students against the "combo ice" energy/telecom reform, campesino organisations demanding land and fair prices, and the new social movements, with the state's answers (demobilisation, negotiation, reconversión productiva).

Costa Rica Grade 11 Estudios Sociales 11 (Porras y Gamboa) (printed p. 153), teaches: Protest as the citizen's other vote: the 1982 medical-incentives strike (42 days), the 1994–95 millicom fight, the 2000 "combo ice" general strike that stopped the energy/telecom laws, the air controllers' strike and the 2013 ebais health strike — unions and pressure groups defending public services and vulnerable minorities against the adjustment programmes.

Costa Rica's own way: Costa Rica's civics is taught as the country's own living system, not as abstract forms — the electoral code and the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones are explained rule by rule (who votes, at what age, free and secret, parties guaranteed by the 1949 Constitution, state funding of parties), and citizenship after 1980 is taught through the protests themselves: students, campesinos and the new social movements answering the state.

Costa Rica's own way: the commercial workbook twin of the CONED 11 anthology teaches the same civics as a fixed cycle (my previous ideas, basic concepts, widening my knowledge, I apply my knowledge) — and it is blunt where the anthology is neutral: the banned Communist Party is judged "a very dangerous restriction", abstention is tabled year by year from the TSE's own figures, and the answer offered is civic education itself — student elections run with the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones.
