Palestine Grade 5 Social Studies Part 2, printed p. 66, teaches: writing is among the oldest means of communication — picture writing in Pharaonic Egypt, cuneiform in Iraq, and the Canaanites' alphabetic writing of some 4,000 years ago, its letters drawn from their environment; writing matters as a means of teaching and learning, of passing knowledge and sciences between people and peoples, of communication and expression, of recording the history and culture of nations, and of preserving people's rights from loss.

Palestine's own way: civics is taught as relations — the child's social, cultural and political relations are four faces of one human-relations unit — and the constitution is not abstract: fifth-graders read three named articles of Palestine's own Basic Law and finish by drafting a constitution for their own classroom.
