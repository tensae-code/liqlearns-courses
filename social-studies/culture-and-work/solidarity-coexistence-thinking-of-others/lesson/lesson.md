# Solidarity Coexistence Thinking of Others

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a society of harmony, not inequality and exclusion, is built on solidarity coexistence — think of others and stand in their place; all persons enjoy the same dignity, respect and opportunities, and hold rights regardless of social, economic, ethnic, religious or gender condition; work for unity, tolerance and dialogue — as Guatemala's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1 "Convivencia solidaria"

Guatemala Grade 6 Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana (2025), printed p. 50, teaches: a society of harmony, not inequality and exclusion, is built on solidarity coexistence — think of others and stand in their place; all persons enjoy the same dignity, respect and opportunities, and hold rights regardless of social, economic, ethnic, religious or gender condition; work for unity, tolerance and dialogue. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guatemala's way (Book 8) — the book's own teaching way

Guatemala's own way: the sixth-grade book widens the lens — democracy is traced from Athens to Guatemala's own authoritarian past, and citizenship ends at the world: Guatemala's Belize claim put to its own citizens in the 2018 popular consultation on the International Court of Justice.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
