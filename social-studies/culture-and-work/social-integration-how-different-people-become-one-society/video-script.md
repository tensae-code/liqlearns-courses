Indonesia's Grade 8 IPS-G8 student (printed p. 216); Grade 8 IPS-G8 student (printed p. 217); Grade 9 IPS-G9 student (printed p. 79); Grade 8 IPS-G8 teacher (printed p. 215), teaches: Bab 4 D. Integrasi Sosial, Bab 4 D.2–3 Syarat dan Faktor Integrasi Sosial, Bab 1 C.3 Terbentuknya Integrasi Nasional, Panduan Khusus Bab 4: Perdagangan Antarwilayah di Indonesia — teaches: social integration defined as different elements blending while guarding each other into peace — gotong royong, and marakka bola house-lifting in South Sulawesi, as the popular example; the conditions for integration (members filling each other's needs; shared agreement on norms and values, consistently kept) and its speed factors (group homogeneity, group size, geographic mobility, communication effectiveness); how national integration formed — Sriwijaya and Majapahit uniting by trade, religion and conquest (fragile, semu) against the modern unity born of shared colonial fate and national consciousness; guidance for the social-integration part of the trade chapter, including the marakka bola gotong royong case.

Indonesia's own way: social integration is taught through marakka bola — the South Sulawesi gotong royong tradition of literally lifting and carrying a neighbour's house to its new site, without regard to difference. national integration is taught as a contrast — the old integrations of Sriwijaya and Majapahit were made by conquest and stayed fragile (semu), while the modern one grew from shared fate under colonisation and is held by tolerance, with Indonesia cited by its own Foreign Ministry as a world reference for unity in diversity. the guide builds each chapter around an Aktivitas Kolaborasi (collaboration activity), so the norms and institutions content is taught by group work, not lecture.
