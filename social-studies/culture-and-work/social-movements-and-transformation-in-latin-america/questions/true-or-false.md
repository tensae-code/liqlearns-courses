# Social Movements and Transformation in Latin America — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: latin America's inequality, from the bad distribution of goods, breeds discontent that moves societies to transform themselves; the labour movement (in Honduras, the 1954 banana strike that won benefits for the working class), cultural movements bringing art to the people, and women's new roles drive the change; a country has development when its population's needs — food, clothing, health, housing, services, work, education — can be met.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: a social organisation is a group sharing opinions, values, worldviews and interests, planning strategies for a group's or community's benefit; Latin America shows the world's greatest economic and social inequalities, and its classic social movements formed under European (Italian and Spanish, anarchist) influence — the labour movement, the peasant movement articulating with democratic struggles, and the ethnic question in its peasant-indigenous and peasant-Black strands.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: social movements arise from the need to reach common objectives for a group sharing economic and social characteristics; the traditional movement rests on unionism — Honduran unionism has been a fundamental political balance and a brake on privatising policies — while the new social movements leave class struggle to fight for rights by sectors (feminist and climate movements named), pushing new values, identities and cultural paradigms inside modern civil society.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: social movements are collective manifestations seeking common benefits for large groups — workers, peasants, women, the environment — and each epoch has its own; only some break history open (the French Revolution broke the estate society and gave rise to democratic, republican politics), and much of today's normal life — democracy, labour rights, the division of powers — crystallised thanks to them; movements are often led by smaller groups that make new ideas known and hold values little considered, until a critical mass takes the protagonist role and wider social acceptance follows.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: False — it also stands on Honduras's teacher guide(s), named in lesson/sources.md, as teacher-guide evidence.**
