# Hands-on — Social Movements and Transformation in Latin America

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Lección 32 Transformación social en América Latina
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Latin America's inequality, from the bad distribution of goods, breeds discontent that moves societies to transform themselves; the labour movement (in Honduras, the 1954 banana strike that won benefits for the working class), cultural movements bringing art to the people, and women's new roles drive the change; a country has development when its population's needs — food, clothing, health, housing, services, work, education — can be met. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Lección 34 La organización social en América Latina
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: A social organisation is a group sharing opinions, values, worldviews and interests, planning strategies for a group's or community's benefit; Latin America shows the world's greatest economic and social inequalities, and its classic social movements formed under European (Italian and Spanish, anarchist) influence — the labour movement, the peasant movement articulating with democratic struggles, and the ethnic question in its peasant-indigenous and peasant-Black strands. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Lección 38 Los movimientos sociales
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Social movements arise from the need to reach common objectives for a group sharing economic and social characteristics; the traditional movement rests on unionism — Honduran unionism has been a fundamental political balance and a brake on privatising policies — while the new social movements leave class struggle to fight for rights by sectors (feminist and climate movements named), pushing new values, identities and cultural paradigms inside modern civil society. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Lección 38, teoría adicional
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Social movements are collective manifestations seeking common benefits for large groups — workers, peasants, women, the environment — and each epoch has its own; only some break history open (the French Revolution broke the estate society and gave rise to democratic, republican politics), and much of today's normal life — democracy, labour rights, the division of powers — crystallised thanks to them; movements are often led by smaller groups that make new ideas known and hold values little considered, until a critical mass takes the protagonist role and wider social acceptance follows. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
