# Everyones Rights to a Dignified Life

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the fundamental rights for a dignified life — work, education, health and recreation, and women's rights — enjoyed without discrimination of race or sex; human rights as a lived reality in Cuba, including the Declaration of the Rights of the Child; Cuban children's lives compared with children's in other countries — as Cuba's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Everyones Rights to a Dignified Life

Cuba Grade 5 Educación Ciudadana OM (printed pp. 50–58), teaches: The fundamental rights for a dignified life — work, education, health and recreation, and women's rights — enjoyed without discrimination of race or sex; human rights as a lived reality in Cuba, including the Declaration of the Rights of the Child; Cuban children's lives compared with children's in other countries. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cuba's way — the book's own teaching way

Cuba's own way: citizenship is taught through the child's own pioneer organisation and through the 2019 Constitution article by article — family duties, equality and the mass organisations are all read straight out of constitutional text, and the state is presented as the people's own power (sovereignty in the people, delegates answerable to their electors). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
