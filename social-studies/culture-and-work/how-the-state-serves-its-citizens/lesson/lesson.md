# How the State Serves Its Citizens

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the government the citizens elected must serve the citizens: it pays the salaries of the people who work in schools and kindergartens, builds separate schools for children with disabilities, and runs training for those who left school — and it does all this with the budget (төсөв), money gathered as tax (татвар) from the citizens and businesses; the school's own people — director, training manager, school doctor, teachers, pupils — each hold rights and duties in that service — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 15 "How does the state serve its citizens?" (Төр иргэддээ хэрхэн үйлчилдэг вэ?)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 59, teaches: The government the citizens elected must serve the citizens: it pays the salaries of the people who work in schools and kindergartens, builds separate schools for children with disabilities, and runs training for those who left school — and it does all this with the budget (төсөв), money gathered as tax (татвар) from the citizens and businesses; the school's own people — director, training manager, school doctor, teachers, pupils — each hold rights and duties in that service. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
