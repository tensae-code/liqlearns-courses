**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Samoa, Belarus).**

Samoa's own way: Society is taught as a set of institutions a Samoan child already lives inside — family, church, school, economy, government — and change is taught by pairing America's civil-rights movement with Samoa's own Mau as the home example of a social movement.

Belarus's own way (Grade 10): The Grade 10 course teaches society and politics in general concepts, but anchors them at home — the Statute-era separation of powers on Belarusian lands, Article 36's freedom of association and Article 49's right to education quoted from its own Constitution, and Belarusian parties and public associations as the living examples of the political system.

Belarus's own way (Grade 9): The civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state.
