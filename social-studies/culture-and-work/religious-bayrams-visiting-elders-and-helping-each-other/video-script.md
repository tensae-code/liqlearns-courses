# Video script — Religious Bayrams Visiting Elders and Helping Each Other

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Turkey Grade 1 Hayat Bilgisi 2. Kitap, printed p. 42, teaches: Ramazan Bayramı and Kurban Bayramı are our religious festivals, and Kadir Gecesi, Aşure Günü and the kandil nights our religious days; before a bayram we clean the home and prepare treats, at the bayram we visit our elders, phone or send cards to those far away, and stand in solidarity and mutual help, which strengthens our bonds

Turkey Grade 2 Hayat Bilgisi 2. Kitap, printed p. 38, teaches: the religious days (the kandil nights, Kadir Gecesi, Aşure Günü) and the two religious bayrams (Ramazan and Kurban) are national and spiritual values, celebrated in solidarity, mutual help, unity and togetherness — visiting elders to kiss their hands, crowded family tables — told through a Karagöz-and-Hacivat shadow-play dialogue; the home-grade version of the lesson proposed at Book 3

Turkey's own way: the second Grade 1 book turns from school and family to the homeland: the flag and the anthem are taught as the symbols of independence a six-year-old stands for at the school ceremony, the five national days are learned as one family of celebrations, and the religious bayrams are taught through visiting elders and helping each other — the state and the faith calendar side by side in one learning area.

Turkey's own way: local government is taught to seven-year-olds as the answer to a child's question — "who put the new play equipment in our park?" — and the class then visits a real muhtarlık, belediye or kaymakamlık and interviews the manager with a form it wrote itself.
