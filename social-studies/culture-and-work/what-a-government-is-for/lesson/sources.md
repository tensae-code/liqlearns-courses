Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): government is taught as a growth story — from clan and tribal administration run by appointed, removable leaders in the common interest, through class society and city-states (Athens beside Eritrea's own Adulis, Coloe and Toconda), to Eritrea's present unitary state with Regional Administration.

**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Eritrea, Iran).**

Iran's own way (Iran Social Studies Grade 5): civics is taught by analogy from the school camp trip — a group needs roles, rules and a leader, so a country needs a government — and the Islamic Republic is presented as the people's own choice, made in the Farvardin 1358 referendum and renewed in every Majlis election.

Iran's own way (Iran Social Studies Grade 9): the last middle-school civics year turns sociological — culture, identity and the family are defined as concepts (layers of culture, functions of the family) before the book returns to the state, and citizenship is finally named as a two-way bond of rights and duties between people and government.
