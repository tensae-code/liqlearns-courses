# The Peoples of Guyana and Their Ways of Life — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: The first people's way of life in Guyana today (their customs and foods within the arrival story, which is History and named not placed).
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: African Guyanese ways of life carried into Guyana — foods and dance among them (the enslavement and emancipation story is History, named not placed).
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: East Indian Guyanese customs — birth customs (the baby's head-shaving), formal dress and foods (indentureship arrival story is History, named not placed).
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: Amerindian reservations/settlements led by a Touchau or Captain, and how their houses changed from large shared houses of shingle and troolie palm to individual enclosed houses of boards, zinc and mud-blocks.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson's fifth part teaches: Amerindians still work together co-operatively, in hunting, fishing, farming, timber, mining and community services, sell craft work, and keep their culture in tales and the Mari-Mari, Baboon and Carrion Crow dances and the Mashramani merriment meeting.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This lesson's sixth part teaches: Cassava is the staple — cassava bread, farine, casareep and pepperpot — and Amerindians named Guyana's lakes, rivers and falls (Kaieteur after Old Kaie; Bartica, "Red Earth"; names ending in "tuk" mean falls).
**Answer: True**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** This lesson's seventh part teaches: Freed Africans pooled their savings and bought whole plantations, founding villages — Victoria the first, then Buxton (Kofi's village), Friendship, Beterverwagting and Plaisance.
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** This lesson's eighth part teaches: The bought villages were run as co-operative farms, and Freedom Day (1 August) is still celebrated with African songs, dancing and the Masquerade, Mother Sally and Bad-cow dances.
**Answer: True**

**9. (Sub lesson 9)** This lesson's ninth part teaches: The Portuguese left the estates to open businesses still running today, the Chinese followed them into business, and the East Indians stayed to grow rice and brought cultures still practised — Indian dishes enjoyed and festivals (Phagwah, Diwali, Eid-Ul-Azha, Eid-Ul-Fitr) taken part in by all.
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 10)** This lesson's tenth part teaches: The first people of Guyana and their way of life — their crafts, music and the foods they grow and make.
**Answer: True**

**11. (Sub lesson 11)** This lesson's eleventh part teaches: The Europeans' way of life in Guyana — their clothing, houses and building styles that still stand.
**Answer: True**

**12. (Sub lesson 12)** This lesson's twelfth part teaches: The Africans' way of life — their folk songs, music, dances and stories.
**Answer: True**

**13. (Sub lesson 13)** This lesson's thirteenth part teaches: The Portuguese in Guyana and the work and businesses they became known for.
**Answer: True**

**14. (Sub lesson 14)** This lesson's fourteenth part teaches: The East Indians' way of life — their foods, music and dress.
**Answer: True**

**15. (Sub lesson 15)** This lesson's fifteenth part teaches: The Chinese in Guyana and how they lived and worked after arriving as indentured immigrants.
**Answer: True**

**16. (Sub lesson 16)** This lesson's sixteenth part teaches: The first people of Guyana, nine groups living across the regions, using the land for food and shelter, with their songs, instruments (banjos, flutes, maracas), dances (Mari Mari, Humming Bird), foods (cassava bread, pepperpot, farine) and nine languages.
**Answer: True**

**17. (Sub lesson 17)** This lesson's seventeenth part teaches: The British, Portuguese, Dutch and Spanish and what they brought — their worship, instruments (mandolin, lyre, accordion), dances (Maypole, Waltz, samba, flamenco), foods (pastries, garlic pork) and clothes.
**Answer: True**

**18. (Sub lesson 18)** This lesson's eighteenth part teaches: The Africans, brought as slaves to the sugarcane plantations, and what they brought — folksongs, African drums and shak shak, the Cumfa dance, and foods such as conkie, cook-up rice, foo-foo and metemgee.
**Answer: True**

**19. (Sub lesson 19)** This lesson's nineteenth part teaches: The East Indians, who came as indentured labourers, and what they brought — bhajans and chowtal songs, sitar, dhantal, dholak and tassa, kathak and odissi dances, and roti, curry and dhal.
**Answer: True**

**20. (Sub lesson 20)** This lesson's twentieth part teaches: The Chinese, who also came as indentured labourers, and what they brought — flute, violin and drum, the dragon dance, and fried rice, chow mein and wanton soup.
**Answer: True**

**21. (Sub lesson 21)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on Guyana's student book, named in lesson/sources.md.**
