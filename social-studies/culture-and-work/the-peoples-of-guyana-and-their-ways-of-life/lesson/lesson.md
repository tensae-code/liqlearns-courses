## Sub lesson 21 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: Book 4 turns from "my community" to "our homeland" — Guyana is taught as six peoples who each brought a way of life, and citizenship is taught through honour (National Awards) and shared observance (Road Safety, Fire Prevention and Youth Weeks) before it is taught through rules. Book 5 widens the child from Guyana to the Caribbean Community — the peoples are taught by what each group still does today (foods, villages, festivals), neighbours are taught as people who help each other in disasters, and leadership is taught whole: Mayor, Touchau, Regional Chairman and trade-union leader side by side, all elected for a fixed time and removable. the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught. the series opens at Grade 1 inside the home — the family that cooks and gardens together, the chores shared out fairly, the groups a small child already belongs to, and rules taught as the way a home and a classroom stay in harmony. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
