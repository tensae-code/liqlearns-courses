# Hands-on — The Peoples of Guyana and Their Ways of Life

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The first people's way of life in Guyana today (their customs and foods within the arrival story, which is History and named not placed). Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: African Guyanese ways of life carried into Guyana — foods and dance among them (the enslavement and emancipation story is History, named not placed). Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: East Indian Guyanese customs — birth customs (the baby's head-shaving), formal dress and foods (indentureship arrival story is History, named not placed). Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Amerindian reservations/settlements led by a Touchau or Captain, and how their houses changed from large shared houses of shingle and troolie palm to individual enclosed houses of boards, zinc and mud-blocks. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Amerindians still work together co-operatively, in hunting, fishing, farming, timber, mining and community services, sell craft work, and keep their culture in tales and the Mari-Mari, Baboon and Carrion Crow dances and the Mashramani merriment meeting. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Cassava is the staple — cassava bread, farine, casareep and pepperpot — and Amerindians named Guyana's lakes, rivers and falls (Kaieteur after Old Kaie; Bartica, "Red Earth"; names ending in "tuk" mean falls). Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Freed Africans pooled their savings and bought whole plantations, founding villages — Victoria the first, then Buxton (Kofi's village), Friendship, Beterverwagting and Plaisance. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The bought villages were run as co-operative farms, and Freedom Day (1 August) is still celebrated with African songs, dancing and the Masquerade, Mother Sally and Bad-cow dances. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The Portuguese left the estates to open businesses still running today, the Chinese followed them into business, and the East Indians stayed to grow rice and brought cultures still practised — Indian dishes enjoyed and festivals (Phagwah, Diwali, Eid-Ul-Azha, Eid-Ul-Fitr) taken part in by all. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The first people of Guyana, nine groups living across the regions, using the land for food and shelter, with their songs, instruments (banjos, flutes, maracas), dances (Mari Mari, Humming Bird), foods (cassava bread, pepperpot, farine) and nine languages. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The British, Portuguese, Dutch and Spanish and what they brought — their worship, instruments (mandolin, lyre, accordion), dances (Maypole, Waltz, samba, flamenco), foods (pastries, garlic pork) and clothes. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The Africans, brought as slaves to the sugarcane plantations, and what they brought — folksongs, African drums and shak shak, the Cumfa dance, and foods such as conkie, cook-up rice, foo-foo and metemgee. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The East Indians, who came as indentured labourers, and what they brought — bhajans and chowtal songs, sitar, dhantal, dholak and tassa, kathak and odissi dances, and roti, curry and dhal. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The Chinese, who also came as indentured labourers, and what they brought — flute, violin and drum, the dragon dance, and fried rice, chow mein and wanton soup. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
