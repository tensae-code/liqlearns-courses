**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Micronesia, Ecuador, Colombia). Haiti is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Micronesia's own way: present-day government is taught as “Micronesia's Two Governments” — the elected FSM state/national system and the traditional leadership system running beside it — and every structure (federal levels, checks and balances, the rotating presidency) is explained through the compromises of the first constitutional convention and through village-level stories (a Yap bus bill stopped by chiefs, a fishing-fee fight between governors and Palikir, the Amnesty Bill stopped by the people).

Haiti's own way (EC2 7AF citoyenne/citoyen dans un État démocratique) (teacher guide — evidence): The democratic state is taught as resting on its citizens: rights are learned by exercising them at school (debate, a school charter of rights and duties), and Haitian texts (1987 amended Constitution, the 1801/1805 constitutions, laws on paternity and on violence against women) are read beside the international declarations. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Ecuador's own way (Estudios Sociales 8vo EGB): at Superior level the civics strand is built on Ecuador's own 2008 constitutional order — Buen Vivir (Sumak Kawsay) as the state's development idea, a plurinational and intercultural state, nature itself as a subject of rights, and children's rights taught from Ecuador's own Código de la Niñez y Adolescencia.
