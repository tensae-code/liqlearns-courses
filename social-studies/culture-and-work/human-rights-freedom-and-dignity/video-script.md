# Video script — Human Rights Freedom and Dignity

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Venezuela Grade 4 G4 CS Venezuela y Su Gente, printed p. 144, teaches: The rights of humanity — human rights are the set of rights that assure respect for people's dignity and freedom, two pillars held up by justice, honesty, solidarity, loyalty, honour and respect; dignity makes a person deserving of life and security, health and education, food and housing, work and respect, justice and peace; the lesson reads the UN's 1948 Universal Declaration itself — Articles 1, 3, 5, 7, 28 and 29, including everyone's duties to the community — through Aesop's fable of the wolf who prefers hunger with dignity to the dog's collar.

Venezuela Grade 5 G5 CS Venezuela y Su Gente, printed p. 132, teaches: Human rights and our constitution — the universal rights carried into Venezuela's own constitution, including its respect for the country's peoples and languages.

Venezuela's own way: the closing block builds citizenship in steps — family first (a community with its own norms, duties and rights), then the homeland's symbols, then humanity's rights (the 1948 UN Declaration read article by article), then the needs those rights protect and the taxes that pay for the services, and finally Venezuela's own 1999 Constitution — participatory democracy and its four referendums — as the nation's rulebook for living well. Grade 5's citizenship block is a complete primary civics course in one closing movement — citizenship, the school as the first community where citizens are formed (with student councils by law), needs as human rights, the constitution as fundamental law, the five public powers including Bolívar's own Citizen and Electoral powers, and the municipal power as the one closest to daily life.
