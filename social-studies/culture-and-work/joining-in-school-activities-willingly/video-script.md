The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Vietnam Grade 1 Đạo đức 1, printed pp. 44–45, teaches: Cleaning the classroom, planting trees, giving to poorer classmates, celebrating the teacher — taking part because the school is yours.

Vietnam Grade 1 Tự nhiên và Xã hội 1, printed pp. 32–35, teaches: The school's shared activities and the people who work in it — teacher, school doctor, librarian, cleaner, security guard and cooks, greeted and thanked politely (queueing, handling library books with care) — and the activities themselves: the arts exchange, the flag ceremony, gardening, exercise, and the collection for flood-area friends (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 1 of this file; counted here as an existing target).

Vietnam Grade 2 Tự nhiên và Xã hội 2, printed pp. 24–27, teaches: The school year's opening ceremony — the flag parade and drum team, the head teacher's speech and the opening drum, the welcome for the new Grade 1 pupils, the performances — and the pupils' own part before and after: cleaning the schoolyard and giving presents to classmates in difficult circumstances (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 1 of this file; counted here as an existing target).

Vietnam Grade 2 Tự nhiên và Xã hội 2, printed pp. 28–31, teaches: The school's reading festival — book displays and new-book shelves, telling a story to the school, presenting a favourite book, giving books to the school library, and making your own month's reading plan; 21 April is Vietnam Book Day (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 1 of this file; counted here as an existing target).

Vietnam's own way: Đạo đức (Ethics) is Vietnam's standalone primary moral-education subject; every lesson runs the same four moves — a song to open, picture stories to explore, practice pictures to judge, a role-play to apply — and closes with a two-line rhyme that states the duty; the family is taught three-generational (ông bà – cha mẹ – anh chị em) and school rules are taught as the child's first shared rules. the local community is introduced through two big picture scenes — the home village (health station, post office, market, school) against the city (hospital, supermarket, cinema, traffic) — and every job, from farmer to traffic policeman, is summed up in one sentence: any work that benefits society is precious. work is met first inside the family — the adults' occupations, paid and unpaid, with Hồ Chí Minh's saying "labour is glory" — and traffic is taught as a system of kinds (road, rail, air, water) whose signs fall into three colour-coded families every road user must obey.
