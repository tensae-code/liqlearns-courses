## Sub lesson 1 — Trustworthiness is My Trait

Iraq Grade 1 Moral Education (printed pp. 26–30), teaches: Trustworthiness (al-amana) is among the important traits of the human being and the trait of the believer: Muhammad finds a new reading book on his classroom windowsill, prettier than his own old book, yet he does not think of keeping it, because he is trustworthy — he asks his classmates, then searches the schoolyard through the breaks until he finds Sara crying by the next classroom's door for her lost book, and returns it to her, and she thanks him for his trustworthiness; I am trustworthy in all circumstances, I return things to their owners, and I do not take others' things without permission. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: moral education is a separate subject from Grade 1, taught entirely as stories about five named children (Muhammad, Yusuf, Zahraa, Lana, Sara) whose small school and family incidents carry each value; every lesson ends in the child's own voice — "What I learned" — and the second unit's family tree is drawn with the father's help, branch by branch, until the big family shades them all. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
