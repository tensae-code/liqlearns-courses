# Human Rights in the Modern World Legal Status and International Protection

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the legal status of the citizen — the person as a subject and bearer of law and a participant in legal relations, the ideal of the free personality; the principle of the formal equality of different people; the two theories that root that status — the theory of natural law and the theory of the social contract, by which people enter a civil union to secure their safety, freedom, property and happiness; and the modern system of protection — the International Bill of Human Rights (the Universal Declaration and the two 1966 Covenants), the UN's human-rights machinery and the European system (the European Convention and the European Social Charter) — human rights as "the foundations of the whole legal order" — as Belarus' book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way

Belarus' own way: the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
