Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Mexico is a pluricultural nation by its own Constitution (Article 2): 71 indigenous peoples, Afro-Mexican communities, migrants and urban groups hold identities, languages and worldviews that must be equally respected, marked each 12 October on the Day of the Pluricultural Nation — as Mexico's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mexico a Pluricultural Nation

Mexico Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 1 (secundaria) (printed pp. 71–73), teaches: Mexico is a pluricultural nation by its own Constitution (Article 2): 71 indigenous peoples, Afro-Mexican communities, migrants and urban groups hold identities, languages and worldviews that must be equally respected, marked each 12 October on the Day of the Pluricultural Nation. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mexico's way — the book's own teaching way

Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
