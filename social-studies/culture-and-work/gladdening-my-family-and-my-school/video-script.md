Mongolia Civic Ethics Grade 6, printed p. 56, teaches: Ethics becomes daily practice at home and school — the morning greeting that sets the family's day (мэндчилгээ: "Did you sleep well? Did you dream sweetly?"); planning the day ahead, since a planned day is the habit of every person who lives happily; gratitude (талархал) to everyone whose work carries us, and filial duty (ачлал) — repaying parents' kindness, the first of all duties, in Gamzatov's lines; doing the housework with a glad heart rather than a forced one, for the heart in the work is what the family sees; asking the elders life's teachings (including the tea-offering customs); the evening reckoning of the day's right and wrong acts; and at school — listening to the lesson with full attention, fulfilling and thanking the teacher's teaching, and apologising and forgiving (уучлал) — saying "Уучлаарай" is not weakness but the act that keeps every relationship whole.

Mongolia's own way (Link book L2): from Grade 6 the civics subject changes its name and its method — no longer "Civic Education" but "Civic Ethics" (Иргэний ёс зүйн боловсрол), a self-observation workbook in the Buddhist-inflected Mongolian moral tradition: the pupil keeps a diary of their own acts of body, speech and mind, and ethics is practised as a daily discipline before it is ever a rule about others.
