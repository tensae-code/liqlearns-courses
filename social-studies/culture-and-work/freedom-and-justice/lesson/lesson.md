# Freedom and Justice

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: freedom is the state in which a person is not subject to coercion or restriction and his choices and conduct follow his own will, provided he does not assault others' freedoms; it is a human right no one may deny or limit to another, and it drives the work for independence, national liberation and building the independent Palestinian state with Jerusalem as its capital; its importance is that the individual practises his capacities and develops his energies and skills, so the people share in shaping the laws and feel the state moves by their proposals, which deepens their sense of responsibility; justice is the belief that all people are equal with no discrimination, that each person has a set of rights to enjoy and responsibilities to keep, and applying it in society achieves equality between its members, security and safety for all without fear of injustice, and spreads love and familiarity among them — as Palestine's book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 7 Social Studies Part 1): civil society is taught entirely through Palestinian institutions named one by one — the Red Crescent, Al-Haq, the Independent Commission for Human Rights, the teachers' and women's unions — and its values lesson ends in a project: mining Palestine's own Declaration of Independence and Basic Law for their values and sorting them into the book's six families. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
