Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 11 Geography (Dari)): an economic-geography year in two halves — first Afghanistan (farming and livestock, energy and mines, industries and trade), then the same themes for the world (water and farming, energy, mines and industries, trade and tourism), each Afghanistan chapter closing with a "review of the situation" lesson on the country's own state.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 4 Social Studies (Dari)): primary social studies starts from the child's own world — family, village, city — and only then opens the map: directions, Afghanistan's boundaries and mountains and rivers, the continents and oceans, and finally the Earth's shape, equator, latitude/longitude and movements.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 5 Social Studies (Dari)): Grade 5 social studies is an economic tour of Afghanistan for children — public services first (water, electricity, telecom, post, transport), then farming crop by crop, livestock animal by animal, industries craft by craft and factory by factory, trade and the country's (dry) ports, ending with environment, climate and mines.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 4 G4 Social Studies): Grade 4 turns from the child's surroundings to the homeland itself — one unit on Yemen's land and people, one on its resources sector by sector (farm, animal, sea, industry, trade/tourism), before services, national occasions and historical figures.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 5 G5 Geography of Yemen): the first standalone Geography book is entirely about Yemen itself — map skills first, then Yemen's physical geography, then its population, then its economic activity sector by sector.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 9 G9 Geography of Yemen and the Arab World): Grade 9 returns to Yemen by environments (mountain, coastal-marine, desert), then Yemen's population and resources, then scales up to the Arab world's environment and water problems, population and industrial development.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 11 G11 Geography of Yemen and the Arab World): Grade 11 does Yemen in depth first (physical base, population and social development, economic resources), then the Arab world's physical geography, its people and resources, and finally its contemporary problems — water, food, poverty, illiteracy, integration.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 7 LY G7 Geography of Libya): A whole book on Libya alone, in four units — physical (location, relief, climate, soil, water, vegetation), human (population and urban centres), economic (farming, herding, fishing, minerals and oil, industry, trade), then tourism and transport, with tourism taught through Libya's historical cities (Jarma of the Garamantes) and oases (Awjila).

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 11 G11 Geography): a human geography of the world year — political map and state typology, natural resources by sphere, population and settlements, then the world economy branch by branch (agriculture, each industry, services, transport, tourism), closing with economic-geographic characterisations of 14 states on every continent (Egypt among them) written to one template, plus case studies including labour migration at local/regional/global scale and using Google Maps/Google Earth as geo-information portals.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 9 Geography): the human-geography year of one's own country, written as a citizen's handbook — it opens with the Constitution's own words on territory, weighs the advantages and disadvantages of the country's economic-geographic position (including EU candidate status, June 2022), and treats the wine industry as national patrimony with roots in the Cucuteni-Tripolie civilisation; every theme carries "current problems", case studies and debates about the pupils' own locality, and the two practical works study the home locality itself (its population; its tourist sights).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 9 np-g9): Grade 9 "Hamro Prithvi" widens from Nepal (latitude/longitude, surface form, climate, vegetation, minerals and water resources, map work) to whole continents — Asia, Europe and Australia, each in two lessons (geographical/natural environment, then economic activities and social life); the preface notes this is a model textbook tested in schools under the 2076 secondary curriculum.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 2): human/economic geography organised sector by sector — population first, then each economic activity taught world-then-Mozambique (agriculture, livestock, fishing, forestry, industry, commerce, tourism, transport), with Mozambique's own distribution tables, ports and exports as the local evidence.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 6): farming taught historically first — the Neolithic revolution, the Iron civilisation, and what the Industrial Revolution gave agriculture — then agrarian space and its natural/human factors, farming systems by development level, and the tropical systems (dryland, oasis, monsoon rice) before modern farming and livestock.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G4 SS T1): Grade 4 Term 1 grounds the child in Jordanian society and dress, then an environment-resources-population unit: agriculture, industry, energy (renewable and not, in Jordan), human impact and Jordan's wildlife.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 Geography T2): the final Geography term is climate (atmosphere, elements, classifications, change), environment (components, problems, protection, Jordanian ecotourism), and economic geography (production, agriculture, industry, services, development).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 4 Social Studies): geography enters inside "Social Studies" through the child's own surroundings — phenomena are sorted from photographs first, then the local environment, then the map of Iraq's governorates, and only then the wider geographical environments (city, countryside); history and homeland/civics chapters follow in the same book.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 5 Social Studies): the whole first unit is Iraq's own geography — location first (world, region, locality, then the astronomical location), then the land itself region by region off the relief map, then people and economic activity; history and national education follow as separate units.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 8 Social Studies): the intermediate sequence widens ring by ring — after Iraq itself (G5–G6) and the ancient world (G7), Grade 8 gives the whole Arab world as one geographical unit (its position between two continents, relief, climates, waters, people and economy), worked constantly off outline maps of the Arab world; the history of the Arab world follows as Part 2.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies): Grade 9 returns to Iraq for the fullest treatment yet — the same national geography as Grade 5 but measured: the plain is 24% and about 600 km long, the desert plateau about 55%, the desert plant belt about 70%, two-thirds of the people live on the central and southern plain; every topic is pinned to numbered maps of Iraq.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 10 Geography): the literary track opens geography as a discipline in its own right — its thought from the Sumerians to GIS, how a geographer researches, where geographical data come from and how they are drawn, and only then the fields of the science; Iraq supplies the worked examples throughout (Baghdad research problem, Iraqi topographic maps, Iraq's gas time series, the Baghdad–Basra distance exercise).
