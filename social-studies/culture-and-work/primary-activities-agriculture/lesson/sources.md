**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Ethiopia, Barbados, Nauru, Tuvalu, Niue, Costa Rica, Palestine, Bangladesh, Turkmenistan, Egypt, Dominican Republic, Colombia, India, North Macedonia, Cambodia, Fiji).**

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — land Geography is taught through Pacific farming itself: taro, pulaka and the coconut "tree of life", with Niue's own crops, exports and name-story ("behold the coconut") as anchor examples, and shifting cultivation/fallowing taught as sound science. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — and Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

Barbados's own way: this is the shared Caribbean CSEC examination textbook used in Barbados — every world process is re-taught through Caribbean case studies (the Caribbean plate, Montserrat's volcano, Jamaica's coasts and Kingston's urban problems, St Lucia's reefs, Bahamas/Jamaica/Puerto Rico karst, Guyana's rivers), map and field skills are folded into the chapters, and the course ends in a School-Based Assessment fieldwork project. That way is stocked here as the way Barbados teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Costa Rica's own way: a distance-education anthology organised around "the human footprint on geographic space" — every unit opens with a generating question and objectives, and Costa Rica is used as the home case inside world topics (tropical forest conservation, food production problems). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P2): the settlement story is told as a ladder — farming, animal domestication, the agricultural village, the Palestinian village of today, then the city located on the map — and the year closes with the road itself: what a road is, its parts, and how to cross it safely.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.

Egypt's own way (Grade 6 Term 1): primary school ends by widening from Egypt to the Arab homeland — the 22 Arab states in their Asian (12) and African (10) wings, their shared location, relief, climate and environmental challenges, then the region's economic development (agriculture with its natural and human problems and state solutions such as desalination, land reclamation, dams and modern irrigation; Egypt's New Delta and Toshka projects as worked examples).

Egypt's own way (Grade 7 Term 2): Term 2 turns from Africa's nature to its people and economy — population growth worked from real birth/death-rate charts (1970–2023) with illegal migration treated through Egypt's own 2016 anti-smuggling law, then Africa's economic activities and the practical machinery of integration (trans-African highways, the AfCFTA, chambers of commerce between Egypt and African states).

Egypt's own way (Grade 11): a whole secondary book on development geography alone, built as concept → resources → economy → people: the idea of development traced as it widened (economic, then human, then sustainable development, with its principles of comprehensiveness, integration and sustainability), resources always paired with how to manage them, and the human-development unit closing with full worked country models (Malaysia profiled in depth — position, poverty fall, education, health, electronics and car industries — and Brazil) before the future of development in the Arab homeland.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 5): Three geography units climb from the planet to the country: general geography and the American continent's location, then how climate varies across the continents — with climographs the children draw and read themselves — then the Dominican Republic's own climate, activities and population, ending with a “discovering the geography of my country” field activity.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado2_01): Escuela Nueva self-paced guides (parts A–D: explore, deepen, practise, apply) set in rural Colombia — veredas, resguardos, coffee regions; geography starts from the walk home-to-school, the classroom plan and the home municipality (Aracataca/Ciénaga worked example).

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado05_02): Grade 5's second cartilla turns from history to landscape and economy: how people transform natural into cultural landscape, the region's environmental problems, then work and money — geography taught through the child's own region (mountain/valley/savanna/near-the-sea location exercise).

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado09 Secundaria Activa): the Grade 9 Secundaria Activa module is a history course; its only land-geography thread is the expansion of Colombia's agricultural frontier inside the early-20th-century economic history.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado06 Secundaria Activa): Grade 6 Secundaria Activa starts from the universe and the Earth as a "living machine" (rocks and wrinkles, water, air), then follows humans evolving, migrating from Africa to America and adapting to each continent's environment — geography as the stage for all the history that follows.

India's own way (Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): Contemporary India-II is the human/economic book, matching Part I's physical book: India's resources, forests, water, agriculture, minerals and energy, manufacturing and the "lifelines" (transport, communication, trade) — taught throughout as development-versus-environment argument, with movements and case studies (Chipko, Narmada Bachao, rooftop rainwater harvesting in the Thar) carrying the criticism.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 10 mk-g10): the gymnasium course restarts Geography as a systems science — Theme 1 works through the four spheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) lesson by lesson, then natural disasters by sphere; Theme 2 turns to people in geographical space (world urbanisation, energy, farming, industry, transport and trade, tourism, environmental impact); Theme 3 treats the world as one global system (globalisation, transport corridors and sea straits/canals, global networks and navigation systems, international organisations, global challenges and climate-change solutions). 43 numbered lessons.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 7 Social Studies): from Grade 7 the combined Social Studies book splits into four named subject sections; its Geography section teaches people-in-environments — hot zones, deserts and oases, polar peoples, climate zones — through world case studies (Kenya farmers, Amazon, Inuit, Sahara oil) before turning to world population.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 12 Geography): the final Geography year is economic geography — Cambodia's population, inequalities, farm/industry/energy/trade/tourism economy and urbanisation first, then the world's economic poles studied one by one: the USA, Japan and the European Union.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 6 Social Science): Year 6 turns outward to the whole Fiji map — map conventions, island groups, towns, rivers and roads — and to Fiji's climate and sea resources, with conservation taught through traditional taboos and harvest seasons.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 is the foundation for senior Geography and History — Fiji's own physical geography (tectonics, relief, rivers, climate, soils) taught in full, then its industries, population and urbanisation, all with Fiji data.
