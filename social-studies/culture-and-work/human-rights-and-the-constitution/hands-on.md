# Hands-on — Human Rights And the Constitution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Topic 3.1 "Human rights and the Constitution"
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The final year's return to rights, now as constitutional machinery — the natural-rights thinkers set in a row (Rousseau, Locke, Hobbes, Bastiat, Nozick, Mill, Acton: "power tends to corrupt"); the hard lesson of history — proclaiming rights never sufficed, so a protection system had to be built, and the state itself, created to protect rights, is also their chief threat when its power goes unlimited; constitutionalism (үндсэн хуульт ёс) as the answer, with its principles listed — separation of powers, guaranteeing human rights, a democratic republican order accepted by the governed, the rule of law as foundation, a humane civil society, wider citizen participation, independent state bodies, constitutional review; the Constitutional Court (Үндсэн хуулийн цэц) at work in two real 2010 conclusions — striking down the Labour Law's rule retiring a person at sixty regardless of wish, and a Family Law provision on maintaining a spouse — each tested against the Constitution article by article; and who guards the Constitution: the Цэц alone annuls unconstitutional acts and recalls the officials who made them, from the President and the Speaker down. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
