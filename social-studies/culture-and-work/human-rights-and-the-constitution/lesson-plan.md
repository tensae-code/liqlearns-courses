# Lesson plan — Human Rights And the Constitution

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Human Rights And the Constitution
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Mongolia, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Human Rights And the Constitution — The final year's return to rights, now as constitutional machinery — the natural-rights thinkers set in a row (Rousseau, Locke, Hobbes, Bastiat, Nozick, Mill, Acton: "power tends to corrupt"); the hard lesson of history — proclaiming rights never sufficed, so a protection system had to be built, and the state itself, created to protect rights, is also their chief threat when its power goes unlimited; constitutionalism (үндсэн хуульт ёс) as the answer, with its principles listed — separation of powers, guaranteeing human rights, a democratic republican order accepted by the governed, the rule of law as foundation, a humane civil society, wider citizen participation, independent state bodies, constitutional review; the Constitutional Court (Үндсэн хуулийн цэц) at work in two real 2010 conclusions — striking down the Labour Law's rule retiring a person at sixty regardless of wish, and a Family Law provision on maintaining a spouse — each tested against the Constitution article by article; and who guards the Constitution: the Цэц alone annuls unconstitutional acts and recalls the officials who made them, from the President and the Speaker down — as Mongolia's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
