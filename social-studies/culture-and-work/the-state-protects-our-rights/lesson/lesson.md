# The State Protects Our Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the Constitution (Article 19) makes the state answerable to its citizens for creating the economic, social and legal guarantees of their rights and for fighting violations; the state does this through its administration, whose powers are fixed by the Constitution and laws and which may act only inside them — an administrative body works by law, serves the public interest, and answers for decisions that touch people's rights; Mongolia's administration stands in four levels (ministries; aimag/capital; soum/district; bag/khoroo), and its face to the citizen is the One-Stop Service centre (Нэг цэгийн үйлчилгээ), where social welfare, insurance, civil registration, property registration, land, petitions and notary services are gathered in one place — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 9 is where the spiral turns civic — communication first (how people actually reach each other), then rights as a working system of state duties and citizen duties, then the state itself: how a law is made, how a cabinet decides, how a court sits, and the seven doors through which a citizen walks into all three.
