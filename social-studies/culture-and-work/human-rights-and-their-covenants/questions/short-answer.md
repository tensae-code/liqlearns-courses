# Human Rights and Their Covenants — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 10 Ciudadanía e identidad, Los Derechos Humanos — Human rights as a topic with its international frame, including the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, and a closing classification of human rights (food, clothing, housing, medical care, maternity and childhood protection) (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 11 Educación Ciudadana, Derechos humanos y gobernanza — Human rights and governance — the fundamental rights all people possess simply for being human, including freedom of expression (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 12, Derechos humanos y Estado de derecho — Human rights as inherent to every person regardless of gender, religion, ideology, colour or economic condition, guaranteed by the Dominican Constitution and by treaties and conventions, with the 1948 Universal Declaration — and the duty of authorities and citizens to protect, respect and guarantee them (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Dominican Republic's way — the book's own teaching way — secondary civics is taught as constitutional history — Duarte's own draft constitution, the 1963 Bosch constitution and the current 2010 text read side by side — and citizenship as practice: civic education defined as learning to contribute to public processes and debate real problems. Grade 9 civics pairs the abstract frame (human rights and governance, the rule of law) with the country's named social ill — a full unit on narcotráfico, its violence and the building of a drug-free society through education. the Grade 10 citizen-education unit is built as close reading — pupils paraphrase the texts aloud in their own words: the Constitution as Carta Magna / Ley de Leyes, sovereignty residing exclusively in the people, Locke's division of powers, and democratic coexistence resting on education and the culture of peace. (any close wording).**
