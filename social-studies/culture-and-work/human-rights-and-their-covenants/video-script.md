# Video script — Human Rights and Their Covenants

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 1° Secundaria (printed p. 156), teaches: Human rights as a topic with its international frame, including the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, and a closing classification of human rights (food, clothing, housing, medical care, maternity and childhood protection).

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 3° Secundaria (printed p. 172), teaches: Human rights and governance — the fundamental rights all people possess simply for being human, including freedom of expression.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 4° Secundaria (printed p. 194), teaches: Human rights as inherent to every person regardless of gender, religion, ideology, colour or economic condition, guaranteed by the Dominican Constitution and by treaties and conventions, with the 1948 Universal Declaration — and the duty of authorities and citizens to protect, respect and guarantee them.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 11): secondary civics is taught as constitutional history — Duarte's own draft constitution, the 1963 Bosch constitution and the current 2010 text read side by side — and citizenship as practice: civic education defined as learning to contribute to public processes and debate real problems.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 13): Grade 9 civics pairs the abstract frame (human rights and governance, the rule of law) with the country's named social ill — a full unit on narcotráfico, its violence and the building of a drug-free society through education.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 14): the Grade 10 citizen-education unit is built as close reading — pupils paraphrase the texts aloud in their own words: the Constitution as Carta Magna / Ley de Leyes, sovereignty residing exclusively in the people, Locke's division of powers, and democratic coexistence resting on education and the culture of peace.
