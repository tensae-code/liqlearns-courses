# How Different Peoples See the Land

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a worldview is the way you think and feel about the things around you; the Amerindians viewed the environment as sacred and holy and took only what they needed, while the Europeans came seeking wealth — the El Dorado stories — and used the land to make their countries richer — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two worldviews of the land

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 125–127), teaches: A worldview is the way you think and feel about the things around you; the Amerindians viewed the environment as sacred and holy and took only what they needed, while the Europeans came seeking wealth — the El Dorado stories — and used the land to make their countries richer. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
