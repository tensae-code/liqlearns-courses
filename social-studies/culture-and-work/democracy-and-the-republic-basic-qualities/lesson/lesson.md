# Democracy and the Republic Basic Qualities

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: democracy's qualities are freedom, equality, the right to vote and be elected, national sovereignty (ruling power belongs to the people), pluralism (more than one party or candidate, and respect for different views whatever the result), participation (voting, petitions, civil-society work) and the rule of law; in the republic the president and deputies come by election for fixed terms and the people can change rulers they are not pleased with; democracy also lives at home and in class-president elections — as Turkey's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Turkey's way — the book's own teaching way

Turkey's own way: democracy is taught from the classroom outwards — the class first writes and votes its own rules item by item, then meets the same machinery at state scale (elections, petitions, CİMER), and an "active citizen" is defined by what she does about a dirty canteen, a faulty vacuum cleaner and a damaged historic fountain. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
