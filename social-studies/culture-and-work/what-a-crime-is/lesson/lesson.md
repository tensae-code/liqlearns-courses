# What a Crime Is

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: what a crime is, the difference between a wrong act and a violating behaviour, and the two basic elements a crime is tied to — as Kuwait's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The concept of crime and its difference from violating behaviours

Kuwait Grade 9 Term 1 (printed pp. 90–92), teaches: What a crime is, the difference between a wrong act and a violating behaviour, and the two basic elements a crime is tied to. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — The elements of crime in law

Kuwait Grade 9 Term 1 (printed pp. 93–95), teaches: A crime's three elements — the legal element (no act is a crime unless the law defines it; its purpose is protecting people from injustice), the material element and the moral element. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Kuwait's way — the book's own teaching way

Kuwait's own way: the law strand that began in Grade 8 with what law is ends in Grade 9 with crime — the pupil learns to separate a crime from a merely wrong act, the three elements every crime must have, how crimes rank by the danger they pose to society's security, and the drug crime worked end to end, from use and possession to the harm done to person and society. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
