**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ecuador, Eritrea, Syria, Venezuela). Burkina Faso is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Ecuador's own way (Estudios Sociales 8vo EGB): at Superior level the civics strand is built on Ecuador's own 2008 constitutional order — Buen Vivir (Sumak Kawsay) as the state's development idea, a plurinational and intercultural state, nature itself as a subject of rights, and children's rights taught from Ecuador's own Código de la Niñez y Adolescencia.

Burkina Faso's own way (Éducation Civique et Morale CP1 — Fiches d'aide à la préparation) (teacher guide — evidence): civic and moral education is a standalone primary subject from CP1, taught through short prepared lesson sheets built on a story situation, group exchange and a spoken resolution the class takes together; road safety, the flag ceremony and care of the schoolyard and latrines are taught as a young citizen's first duties, each fiche ending in a resolution the pupils speak aloud.

Burkina Faso's own way (Éducation Civique et Morale CP2 — Fiches d'aide à la préparation) (teacher guide — evidence): CP2 widens CP1's duties — children now elect their class leaders, run a school cooperative, and take up the fight against excision and plastic waste as citizens.

Eritrea's own way (ER G6 Citizenship Education): a standalone Citizenship Education subject built entirely from Eritrean stories, poems and dialogues — responsibility is taught through a shop-queue drama and a family Tandoor day, the state through the 1993 referendum story, and patriotism as practical service (keeping the school clean, planting trees at the new clinic) that even a child can do.

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 1): the whole primary series opens with widening rings — Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland — taught almost entirely through picture scenes with a bear mascot, one "I can…" outcome per spread; rights arrive at age six as lived scenes (the child shut out of play has lost a right; the state gives every child the vaccine free).

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 3): Grade 3 is where the series turns civic — the child meets the vote inside a classroom quarrel (proposals, persuasion, a ballot box, the winner implemented), meets rights on World Children's Rights Day (20 November) as picture pairs where every right is matched by its duty, and meets the inclusive city: the society-we-want lesson is built around services for people with special needs, down to the sign-language alphabet and Braille.

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 2): Grade 2 is the honesty-and-safety year — the right-decision lesson turns on a shopkeeper who offers a boy an expensive sweet free if he tells no one, the careful lesson warns that things found on the road may be explosives, and rights arrive as a shipwreck game: children who trash a dream island invent rules, and discover the balance — "my right to enjoy nature, my duty to protect it".
