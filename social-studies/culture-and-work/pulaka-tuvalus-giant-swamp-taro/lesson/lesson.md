## Sub lesson 1 — Pulaka — giant swamp taro in compost pits

Tuvalu's pulaka is a giant swamp taro grown in pits filled with compost — a farming method fitted to a low coral island with little fresh soil. The plant has many uses for the islanders who grow it. And it is threatened: drought, heavy rain and salt water reach the pits as the climate changes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nauru's way — one island crop, studied whole

Nauru learns pulaka inside a case-study reader: a single Pacific crop is studied whole — how it is grown, what it is used for, and what threatens it — alongside taro in Tonga and Niue and the coconut, the tree of life.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
