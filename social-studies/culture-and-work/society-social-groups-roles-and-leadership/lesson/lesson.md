# Society Social Groups Roles and Leadership

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: society is a group of people interacting in a place, sharing a culture, organised by people and institutions; social groups (family, friends, churches, firms, clubs, patronatos) have common objectives, structure, norms, values and roles; doing your entrusted role responsibly builds leadership, and a leader has six basic functions, beginning with setting the group's objectives — as Honduras's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 30 Organización social

Honduras Grade 5 G5 Student CCSS, printed p. 166, teaches: Societies organise because needs point social actors to a form of organisation that defends individual and collective rights; the forms run from the first small equal groups of nomad hunter-gatherers through horde, family, tribe, clan and village as farming settles people; workers' and peasant organisations defend their members' interests, and organisation is an integrated system of valuable people solving community problems together. ## Sub lesson 6 — Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way

Honduras's own way: Grade 4 widens civics to Central America and to rights as law — human rights with the Constitution's own Article 60 and the habeas corpus and amparo remedies, sex versus gender taught for equality, and Honduras's real civic organisations (CODEH, COFADEH, the unions) named as civil society. Grade 5 teaches democracy as a continental story — Latin America's dictatorships and transitions, integral citizenship (political, civil, social), social movements from the 1954 banana strike onward — while the pupil's own rehearsal is the student government with its annual operating plan and suggestion box. secondary civics starts from the family as a legal institution — the Family Code (Decree 76-84) enters in Lesson 1 — and builds to the State itself: its elements (population, territory, power), Montesquieu's separation, and the full legislative process ending in publication in La Gaceta. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
