# Video script — Society Social Groups Roles and Leadership

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Honduras Grade 4 G4 Student CCSS, printed p. 15, teaches: Society is a group of people interacting in a place, sharing a culture, organised by people and institutions; social groups (family, friends, churches, firms, clubs, patronatos) have common objectives, structure, norms, values and roles; doing your entrusted role responsibly builds leadership, and a leader has six basic functions, beginning with setting the group's objectives.

Honduras Grade 5 G5 Student CCSS, printed p. 166, teaches: Societies organise because needs point social actors to a form of organisation that defends individual and collective rights; the forms run from the first small equal groups of nomad hunter-gatherers through horde, family, tribe, clan and village as farming settles people; workers' and peasant organisations defend their members' interests, and organisation is an integrated system of valuable people solving community problems together.

Honduras Grade 7 G7 Student CCSS, printed p. 188, teaches: Society is a grouping of people who decide to meet and live together to improve their quality of life; it is composed of all its members and needs a set of rules and norms regulating life in society; Honduras's decentralised institutions serve it (National Printing Enterprise ENAG, the Fire Corps, the Honduran Social Investment Fund FHIS) — the lesson's national-identity part was placed by the History wave.

Honduras Grade 7 G7 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 124, teaches: The "social component" is the whole structure that builds a population — density, characteristics, composition and migratory movements; social organisation is any grouping established from shared elements, common ideas and similar ways of seeing the world; Honduran society shows sharp contrasts: high densities in San Pedro Sula, Atlántida and Tegucigalpa against a dispersed countryside.

Honduras Grade 8 G8 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 124, teaches: Latin America's social stratification, studied by sociology (Germani's three phases among them): at the top the oligarchy — groups that traditionally hold power: business, union, religious and military elites and civilian technocrats, with complex relations among them; business sectors tended to link themselves to the groups of greatest economic and social power.

Honduras's own way (Book 4): Grade 4 widens civics to Central America and to rights as law — human rights with the Constitution's own Article 60 and the habeas corpus and amparo remedies, sex versus gender taught for equality, and Honduras's real civic organisations (CODEH, COFADEH, the unions) named as civil society.

Honduras's own way (Book 5): Grade 5 teaches democracy as a continental story — Latin America's dictatorships and transitions, integral citizenship (political, civil, social), social movements from the 1954 banana strike onward — while the pupil's own rehearsal is the student government with its annual operating plan and suggestion box.

Honduras's own way (Book 7): secondary civics starts from the family as a legal institution — the Family Code (Decree 76-84) enters in Lesson 1 — and builds to the State itself: its elements (population, territory, power), Montesquieu's separation, and the full legislative process ending in publication in La Gaceta.
