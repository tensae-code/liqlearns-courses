# Society Social Groups Roles and Leadership — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 2 Vivo en una sociedad — Society is a group of people interacting in a place, sharing a culture, organised by people and institutions; social groups (family, friends, churches, firms, clubs, patronatos) have common objectives, structure, norms, values and roles; doing your entrusted role responsibly builds leadership, and a leader has six basic functions, beginning with setting the group's objectives (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 30 Organización social — Societies organise because needs point social actors to a form of organisation that defends individual and collective rights; the forms run from the first small equal groups of nomad hunter-gatherers through horde, family, tribe, clan and village as farming settles people; workers' and peasant organisations defend their members' interests, and organisation is an integrated system of valuable people solving community problems together (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 30 La construcción de la sociedad — Society is a grouping of people who decide to meet and live together to improve their quality of life; it is composed of all its members and needs a set of rules and norms regulating life in society; Honduras's decentralised institutions serve it (National Printing Enterprise ENAG, the Fire Corps, the Honduran Social Investment Fund FHIS) — the lesson's national-identity part was placed by the History wave (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 30, teoría adicional — The "social component" is the whole structure that builds a population — density, characteristics, composition and migratory movements; social organisation is any grouping established from shared elements, common ideas and similar ways of seeing the world; Honduran society shows sharp contrasts: high densities in San Pedro Sula, Atlántida and Tegucigalpa against a dispersed countryside (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does the fifth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 34, teoría adicional — Latin America's social stratification, studied by sociology (Germani's three phases among them): at the top the oligarchy — groups that traditionally hold power: business, union, religious and military elites and civilian technocrats, with complex relations among them; business sectors tended to link themselves to the groups of greatest economic and social power (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** What does the sixth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way — Grade 4 widens civics to Central America and to rights as law — human rights with the Constitution's own Article 60 and the habeas corpus and amparo remedies, sex versus gender taught for equality, and Honduras's real civic organisations (CODEH, COFADEH, the unions) named as civil society. Grade 5 teaches democracy as a continental story — Latin America's dictatorships and transitions, integral citizenship (political, civil, social), social movements from the 1954 banana strike onward — while the pupil's own rehearsal is the student government with its annual operating plan and suggestion box. secondary civics starts from the family as a legal institution — the Family Code (Decree 76-84) enters in Lesson 1 — and builds to the State itself: its elements (population, territory, power), Montesquieu's separation, and the full legislative process ending in publication in La Gaceta. (any close wording).**
