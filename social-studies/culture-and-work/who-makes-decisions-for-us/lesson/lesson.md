# Who Makes Decisions for Us

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a decision is a choice; parents decide at home, the principal at school, religious leaders in churches, mandirs and mosques, and councils, the President and the Government for the country; community leaders vote by show of hands or secretly; in Amerindian communities the Toshao and Village Council decide, the NDC Chairman for coastal villages and the Mayor and City Council for towns — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who makes decisions

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 146–150), teaches: A decision is a choice; parents decide at home, the principal at school, religious leaders in churches, mandirs and mosques, and councils, the President and the Government for the country; community leaders vote by show of hands or secretly; in Amerindian communities the Toshao and Village Council decide, the NDC Chairman for coastal villages and the Mayor and City Council for towns. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
