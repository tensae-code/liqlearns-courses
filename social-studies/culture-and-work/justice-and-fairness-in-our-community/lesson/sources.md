**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (South Sudan).** South Sudan's teacher-guide parts are evidence only, counted nowhere in addition — South Sudan counts through its student-book parts.

South Sudan's own way (Book 13): Gender equality is argued from South Sudanese lives — an elder who educates his daughters against tradition, and four named women leaders — and peace itself is put in women's hands: the book prints the view that gender equality is a tool that can end South Sudan's civil war.

South Sudan's own way (Book 14) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide's one structural addition is comparative — pupils study the laws and systems of countries where gender equity succeeded and use them to design South Sudan's own promotion of equality.

South Sudan's own way (Book 15): The final primary book is a civics of unity for a country at war with itself — power sharing is taught from the 2016 Transitional Government of National Unity cabinet photograph, the constitution's Section 33 equality clause is quoted in full, and Kenya's 2007–08 crisis and Kofi Annan's mediation are the worked example of how a neighbour's unity process shapes South Sudan's own.
