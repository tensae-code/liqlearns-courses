# Justice and Fairness in Our Community — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This part teaches: justice is the legal process by which fairness is administered and fairness is treating people in the right manner (opened by the story of a teacher who punished a whole class without asking); justice is promoted by equal distribution of resources, equal application of law, gender balance in job distribution and equal sharing of responsibilities — in South Sudan girls and boys both go to school, men and women get equal job opportunities, everyone including leaders faces the law equally, and government resources reach towns and remote areas alike.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This part teaches: using courts when aggrieved to get justice; equitable sharing and distribution of national resources (the principle of equity); and ensuring human rights are respected at all levels (the justice lesson is filed NEW from this book in this wave).
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This part teaches: the guide adds that ideas of justice and fairness underpin the legal system, that pupils should judge the effectiveness of law and order in their community, state and country, and that law and justice in the community aim to uphold human rights (the justice lesson is filed NEW from Book 13 in this wave).
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This part teaches: equal distribution means everyone has equal rights over the available resources; it stops resources being exploited by a few, keeps a sufficient flow to every section of society, eliminates the difference between rich and poor and supports sustainable development (the justice lesson is filed NEW from Book 13 in this wave).
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on South Sudan's student book(s), named in lesson/sources.md, with teacher-guide parts as evidence only.**
