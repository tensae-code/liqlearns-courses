# Video script — Workers Rights and Labour Rights Protection

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Belarus Grade 9 Person. Society. State, Book 2 (printed p. 214), teaches: The working person — his rights and duties (the §'s profit passage is the Economics wave's, placed there and not repeated here): the rights to work and to dispose freely of one's abilities, to unite in trade unions to defend one's interests, to strike as the extreme measure, and to take part in managing the enterprise through the labour collective's general meetings; the worker's rights to healthy and safe work conditions, respect for his personal dignity, timely payment, rest (breaks, days off, holidays) and social insurance and pension provision; his duties — to work conscientiously, keep safety rules, treat the employer's property carefully, carry out orders and keep the employer's commercial secret; discipline and the organisation of work — a worker who breaks his duties bears disciplinary, material, administrative or criminal responsibility; trade unions and the administration — professional solidarity, unions as voluntary formations defending their members' social-economic rights independently, subordinated only to the law; and the employer's duties, with the Labour Code's protection of minors (no employment contract under 16; from 14 only with a parent's written consent, Articles 173–174).
Belarus' own way: the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state..
