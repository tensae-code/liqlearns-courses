# State Holidays and Nauryz

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Kazakhstan's state and national holidays — Nauryz, Independence Day on 16 December, the Day of Unity of the People of Kazakhstan on 1 May — and how holidays are divided into national, state and professional — as Kazakhstan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — State Holidays and Nauryz

Kazakhstan Grade 2 Poznanie mira (970/980) (printed p. 69), teaches: Nauryz as the spring festival and start of the New Year by the eastern calendar, marked from 22 March for a month — homes and streets put in order, festive clothes and the dastarkhan, the seven-component nauryz-kozhe symbolising sufficiency, the aksakals' bata blessing, altybakan swings, national games and aitys contests of akyns. Kazakhstan Grade 2 Poznanie mira NIS (1031/1019) (printed p. 64), teaches: the meaning of the national holidays — 16 December 1991, when the country proclaimed its independence, marked every year as Independence Day beside Victory Day (9 May) and Knowledge Day (1 September); the ancestors' long dream of an independent state, and the Independence monuments of Almaty, Nur-Sultan, Taraz and Karaganda. Kazakhstan Grade 3 Poznanie mira (992/999) (printed p. 84), teaches: in Kazakhstan more than 130 ethnoses live in friendship and accord — Kazakhs, Russians, Ukrainians, Uyghurs, Tatars and others — each ethnos with its own language and distinctive culture, and all united by the Motherland, a common future and common holidays; 1 May, the Day of Unity of the People of Kazakhstan, with dances, sports contests and national cultural centres introducing each people to the others; holidays are divided into national, state and professional. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kazakhstan's way — the books' own teaching way

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 2): Grade 2 turns the Grade 1 circles outward — the child meets "society" itself, etiquette as society's rules and the golden rule of politeness, then rights and duties read straight out of the Constitution, and service to society taught through volunteers and four named Kazakh figures (Abai, Akhmet Baitursynov, Kanysh Satpaev, Bauyrzhan Momyshuly). Kazakhstan's own way (Book 3): the NIS telling names things the Atamura book leaves general — citizenship by birth with rights and duties quoted from the Constitution itself (including Article 34's duty to respect the state symbols), the Kazakh custom of asar (the whole village doing unpaid work too heavy for one person) set beside modern volunteering, and the school collective drawn as a full staff chart from director to janitor. Kazakhstan's own way (Book 4): Grade 3 gives the child the Constitution itself — its preamble read aloud ("We, the people of Kazakhstan…"), the citizen's rights drawn as a diagram and Constitution Day named — and roots the family in the Kazakh shezhire, the genealogy every Kazakh was expected to know to the seventh generation (Zheti ata). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
