# Doing My Own Work and Helping Others

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: doing your own small work and helping at home and at school instead of waiting to be served (May May's story and the kindness poem) — as Myanmar's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Do things ourselves too

Myanmar Morality and Civics, Grade 2 (printed p. 5), teaches: Doing your own small work and helping at home and at school instead of waiting to be served (May May's story and the kindness poem). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Doing things by one's own effort

Myanmar Morality and Civics, Grade 4 (printed p. 44), teaches: Packing your own bag, washing your own dishes, helping serve the meal — work done by your own hands. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Myanmar's way — the book's own teaching way

Myanmar's own way: Grade 2 widens Grade 1's values from home to school — the same story-plus-pictures shape now carries school property, school rules, honest judgement of classroom incidents, friends with disabilities, and the whole country drawn as its ethnic children around the map. Grade 4 names children's RIGHTS as a picture inventory (food, water, a house, clothes, care, medicine, play, learning, family) and turns environmental care into two full units — preserving the earth and the natural surroundings, with drought, flood, felled forests and factory smoke as the evidence. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
