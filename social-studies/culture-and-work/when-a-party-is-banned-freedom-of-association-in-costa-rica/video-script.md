Costa Rica Grade 11 Estudios Sociales 11 (Porras y Gamboa) (printed p. 126), teaches: The 1949 constitution's article 98 gives citizens the right to group in parties and in the same breath bans parties of communist tendency; the book weighs the two motives (1948 resentments, cold war anticommunism), calls the restriction very dangerous because any group could be labelled "slightly communist" with no appeal, prints manuel mora's speech after ten years of forced silence, and closes with the 1975 repeal.

Costa Rica's own way: the commercial workbook twin of the CONED 11 anthology teaches the same civics as a fixed cycle (my previous ideas, basic concepts, widening my knowledge, I apply my knowledge) — and it is blunt where the anthology is neutral: the banned Communist Party is judged "a very dangerous restriction", abstention is tabled year by year from the TSE's own figures, and the answer offered is civic education itself — student elections run with the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones.
