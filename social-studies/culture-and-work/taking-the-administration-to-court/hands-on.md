# Hands-on — Taking the Administration to Court

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Topic 3.4 "Resolving disputes between administration and citizens by review"
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: How a citizen fights the state lawfully — opened by the Bayangol case (a governor's 2017 order gave citizen G land that overlapped the plot under citizen B's fence); an administrative act is the administration's everyday instrument and therefore the thing to be controlled; the two roads, in order: first the pre-trial procedure (урьдчилан шийдвэрлэх ажиллагаа) — the complaint goes to the directly superior body or official (defined, with the capital governor over the district governor as the example), who may annul the act, annul it in part, or order a new act, within thirty days of receiving the complaint — the citizen's own window is thirty days from learning of the act, restorable for three months for good reason — and only a complaint decided (or undecidable) there may go on; then the Administrative Court — the claim in writing with its six required parts, the third parties whose interests the act touches, the evidence (what counts: explanations, witness statements, documents, expert conclusions, recordings, site inspections) and who must gather it (the court itself, demanding it from bodies and officials), the judge's order within seven days, the respondent's answer in fourteen, the appeal in fourteen days, and the Supreme Court's word in thirty — final. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
