# Hands-on — The Government of Papua New Guinea

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The Government of Papua New Guinea  and
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: How Papua New Guinea is governed — its government and how it is organised. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## History of democracy in Papua New Guinea
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Democratic concepts in traditional PNG society (the Melanesian Way — communal land, fair shares and the Melanesian Equilibrium, leaders accountable to the people); how colonial rule (British Papua, German New Guinea, Australian administration) shaped democracy; and independent PNG's road to the Westminster system — a constitutional parliamentary democracy with the Governor-General representing the monarch. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Leaders of democratic countries
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: How the leaders of democratic countries differ — a Prime Minister, a Governor-General and a President, and what each role does. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Levels of governments in democratic nations
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The levels of government in Papua New Guinea — national, provincial and Local Level Government — how ward councillors, LLG presidents, provincial members and national leaders exemplify democratic ideals, and decentralisation of power. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The form and origin of contemporary traditional and constitutional government in Papua New Guinea
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The form and origin of contemporary traditional and constitutional government in Papua New Guinea — Local Level Governments (Councillors), Provincial government, the National Parliament (Members of Parliament, Cabinet / National Executive Council, Prime Minister), government and opposition parties; the responsibilities of the different levels of government; the history of constitutional government in PNG; Independence Day and self-government; and citizens' constitutional rights and responsibilities in elections, including how to enrol to vote. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
