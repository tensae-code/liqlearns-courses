# The Government of Papua New Guinea — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's part teaches: how Papua New Guinea is governed — its government and how it is organised.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's part teaches: democratic concepts in traditional PNG society (the Melanesian Way — communal land, fair shares and the Melanesian Equilibrium, leaders accountable to the people); how colonial rule (British Papua, German New Guinea, Australian administration) shaped democracy; and independent PNG's road to the Westminster system — a constitutional parliamentary democracy with the Governor-General representing the monarch.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's part teaches: how the leaders of democratic countries differ — a Prime Minister, a Governor-General and a President, and what each role does.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's part teaches: the levels of government in Papua New Guinea — national, provincial and Local Level Government — how ward councillors, LLG presidents, provincial members and national leaders exemplify democratic ideals, and decentralisation of power.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson's part teaches: the form and origin of contemporary traditional and constitutional government in Papua New Guinea — Local Level Governments (Councillors), Provincial government, the National Parliament (Members of Parliament, Cabinet / National Executive Council, Prime Minister), government and opposition parties; the responsibilities of the different levels of government; the history of constitutional government in PNG; Independence Day and self-government; and citizens' constitutional rights and responsibilities in elections, including how to enrol to vote.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: False — it stands on Papua New Guinea's teacher guides only, named in lesson/sources.md, as teacher-guide evidence.**
