Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 7): Grade 7 civics is the nation itself — how Papua New Guinea is governed, its elections and government services, and how its courts and problem-solvers keep the peace — beside a culture strand that climbs from lifestyle and identity to national events.

Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 9): Grade 9 Political Science is a full civics course built on PNG itself — democracy's principles, then democracy's PNG history from the Melanesian Way and communal land through colonial rule to the Westminster constitutional monarchy of 1975, PNG's symbols and pledge, its bilateral and multilateral relations, and citizenship that ends with the PNG Constitution as the basis of day-to-day civic life.

Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 10): Grade 10 deepens Grade 9's civics into the machinery of the state — the Constitution as the legal basis of government (Section 99's three arms), separation and balance of powers, what parliamentarians actually do, PNG's three levels of government and decentralisation — and ends with a full human-rights unit: PNG's rights laws, the Constitution's rights, enforcing the rights of women, people with disability and marginalised groups, and children's rights under the Lukautim Pikinini Act.

Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science Upper Primary TG, Grades 6–8): the 2003 guide teaches the same climb as the Standards Based guides but through outcomes — local groups in Grade 6, PNG's constitutional government and elections in Grade 7, world governments and regional organisations in Grade 8 — with the wantok system, traditional law beside constitutional law, and class elections run by secret ballot with first-past-the-post and preferential counting.
