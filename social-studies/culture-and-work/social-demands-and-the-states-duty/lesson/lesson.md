# Social Demands and the States Duty

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: hondurans demand of the State health, work, education, housing, security, care for children and families, roads and ports; everyone has the right to ask, request or claim (demandar) that needs be met, and the State is obliged to listen and satisfy them through its secretariats, institutions and laws; citizens also cooperate — through water boards and the patronato, demands reach the Alcaldía — as Honduras's books teach it. ## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 37 Necesidades de la población centroamericana

Honduras Grade 4 G4 Student CCSS, printed p. 201, teaches: Central America's population needs — poverty and malnutrition rising (FAO and the regional food-security programme), corruption denying citizens opportunities and deepening inequality, youth exposed to transnational gangs; the answers taught are society organising itself, opening study and work opportunities, and access to school and university for all, with indigenous peoples' demands on the State included. ## Sub lesson 3 — Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way

Honduras's own way: Grade 3 is where the state appears in full — nationality by birth and registration, the Constitution as supreme law, the three powers with their real Honduran numbers (128 deputies, 15 magistrates, elections every four years), and citizens' social demands as claims the State is obliged to hear. Grade 4 widens civics to Central America and to rights as law — human rights with the Constitution's own Article 60 and the habeas corpus and amparo remedies, sex versus gender taught for equality, and Honduras's real civic organisations (CODEH, COFADEH, the unions) named as civil society. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
