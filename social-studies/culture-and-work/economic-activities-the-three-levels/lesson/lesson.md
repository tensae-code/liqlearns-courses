# Economic activities — the three levels

## Do it first

Ask at home: what work do the adults in your family do? Sort each job into primary, secondary or tertiary.

## Sub lesson 1 — Producing, distributing, consuming

Start with your parents' work. **Economic activity is the whole movement by which useful goods and services are produced, distributed and consumed to meet human wants.**

It comes in **three levels — primary, secondary and tertiary** — taken one by one through the lessons that follow this one.

[Picture 11506 — a three-step staircase: from nature, to making, to service]

## Sub lesson 2 — What a country's mix says about its development (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide reads a country's development off its workforce (teacher guide — evidence): **developed countries carry a large share of manufacturing, with many people in secondary activities; developing economies are basically agrarian**.

In agriculture the contrast is sharp (teacher guide — evidence): in developing countries **many people work the land but production is little**, while developed countries, practising modern methods, **produce a surplus with very few people engaged**. And tertiary activities are support services — an aid to the production process rather than producers of goods.
