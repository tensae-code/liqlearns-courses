**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Guatemala, Ukraine, Ecuador, Bolivia, Palestine, Bangladesh, Turkmenistan, Dominican Republic).**

Ukraine's own way: A "Geography: Regions and Countries" course — every region is done in the same order (position, nature/resources, population, primary/secondary/tertiary sectors) then typological country studies with rubrics (country visiting-card, capital, pride of the country, unique experience), ending with Ukraine's own place in the world; research tasks (Дослідження) embedded in chapters. That way is stocked here as the way Ukraine teaches this thing.

Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs, its activity numbers key to its own companion textbook, and whole student-book topics have no guide counterpart. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence, never as a page-key to the student book. Pupil content in this lesson comes from the Amhara student book.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 3 book): the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 8 book): at Superior level the book turns thematic — history of work and social transformation, then Latin America and Ecuador today; Geography enters as a full cartography tema (map studies explicitly tied to Earth's formation, continents, oceans, climates, natural risks and contingency plans) and as the economic-sectors tema on Ecuador. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Honesty note: the ministry's 2024 transición-curricular PDFs, as held, reuse one body per sublevel — the Grade 5 body also carries the Grade 6 and Grade 7 covers (Subnivel Media), and the Grade 8 body also carries the Grade 9 and Grade 10 covers (Subnivel Superior). That content is credited once, under its true Grade 5 / Grade 8 identity, exactly as the placements state; the genuine Ecuador Grade 6, 7, 9 and 10 Estudios Sociales books are NOT held (library debt).

Bolivia's own way (Grade 10, Secundaria 4): Secundaria 4 is the nineteenth-century history year, and its geography is the geography of what Bolivia lost and what it lives from: the Atacama Litoral and the sea it no longer has, Potosí's silver and the tin north, the Amazon rubber boom, and a closing resources unit that runs from natural-resource classification to Vivir Bien and sustainable development.

Palestine's own way (Grade 7, PS G7P1): the world is taught continent by continent through one fixed lens — astronomical location (latitude and longitude) and geographical location first, then surface features and general characteristics — with the spread of the Islamic state across each continent as the historical thread.

Palestine's own way (Grade 8, PS G8P1): the Arab homeland is toured as one region in a single unit — location, relief, climate, population, economic activities, and water security — before the book turns to Ottoman history and citizenship.

Palestine's own way (Grade 9, PS G9P2): the course closes at world scale — the diversity of natural environments, peoples and economies, then the age's challenges (population explosion, famines, poverty) framed as matters for international cooperation.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 12): The final secondary year turns from national history to the country's present-day human geography, studied region by region: 150 years of population growth, accelerated urbanisation, the economy's branches and foreign trade, and the spatial distribution of the population across Cibao, Este and Suroeste, closing on migration and Santo Domingo as a fragmented metropolis.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 5): Three geography units climb from the planet to the country: general geography and the American continent's location, then how climate varies across the continents — with climographs the children draw and read themselves — then the Dominican Republic's own climate, activities and population, ending with a “discovering the geography of my country” field activity.
