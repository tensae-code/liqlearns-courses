The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Pakistan's Grade 10 PS10 (printed p. 116); Grade 9 PS9 (printed pp. 6–7); Grade 12 PS12-2014 (printed pp. 103–104); Grade 10 PS10-KPK (printed p. 101 (chapter outcomes; chapter pp. 101–128)), teaches: Ch. 6 Salient Features: Protecting the Rights of Minorities, Ch. 1 Islamic State and the Rights of Minorities, Ch. 8 Islamic Democratic State and National Integration: Religious Freedom; Right of Non-Muslims, Ch. 4 The Role of Minorities in Pakistan — teaches: minorities hold full citizenship — their worship, property and lives are protected by law and by the society's own norms, a feature the book counts among Pakistan's strengths; Pakistan was declared an Islamic welfare state, not a theocracy — non-Muslims hold equal status and equal rights, with Jinnah's 11 August 1947 Constituent Assembly address quoted as the charter; the Islamic state gives religious minorities freedom of faith and worship, counts non-Muslim citizens (Zimmis) as rights-holders whose lives and properties the state must secure, and builds integration on equity, tolerance and equal rights for all; the minorities' role traced with specific reference to Quaid-i-Azam's speech of 11 August 1947, which the chapter sets as the definition of their status.

Pakistan's own way: at Class 10 the state tells its story whole — ideology first, then the society it made: thirteen named features of Pakistani culture, minorities' rights written into the culture chapter, and a full closing chapter on women's rights, women nation-builders and the laws that protect them. the English-medium Class 9 book states the ideology in plain definitions, quotes Jinnah's promise that minorities "are free to go to your places of worship", and closes with the women's chapter in its sharpest form — myth-versus-fact boxes that refuse to blame victims. the older book teaches the same state from the other end — culture defined before it is described, languages given a whole chapter with Urdu named the language of national integration, and national integration itself defined, factored and argued like a civics concept, not a slogan. KPK's Class 10 book ends where the PCTB book's culture chapter begins — society first (its features and its named evils: feudalism, the curse of dowry, child labour), then culture defined as a people's inherited way of life, province by province, with the minorities' role and Jinnah's 11 August 1947 speech as the closing civic ground.
