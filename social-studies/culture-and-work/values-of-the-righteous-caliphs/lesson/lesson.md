# Values of the Righteous Caliphs

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the values in the caliphs' lives — giving (Abu Bakr giving all his wealth, Uthman equipping the Army of Hardship with three hundred camels and a thousand dinars), justice and good dealing (Uthman returning the land price to the seller who felt cheated, and the Prophet's saying that God admits to Paradise one who is easy as buyer, seller, judge and claimant), and Umar's personal care for a blind old woman of Madinah — as Saudi Arabia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Values of the Righteous Caliphs

Saudi Arabia Grade 7 Social Studies (printed pp. 178–180), teaches: The values in the caliphs' lives — giving (Abu Bakr giving all his wealth, Uthman equipping the Army of Hardship with three hundred camels and a thousand dinars), justice and good dealing (Uthman returning the land price to the seller who felt cheated, and the Prophet's saying that God admits to Paradise one who is easy as buyer, seller, judge and claimant), and Umar's personal care for a blind old woman of Madinah. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saudi Arabia's way — the book's own teaching way

Saudi Arabia's own way: the Grade 7 book moves from planning and organising time, through shura and the values of the Righteous Caliphs, personal identity, self-development and social interaction, to national security in its concept, pillars, fields and institutions and the citizen's part in it, and closes with dialogue — its concept, pillars and manners, and national dialogue. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
