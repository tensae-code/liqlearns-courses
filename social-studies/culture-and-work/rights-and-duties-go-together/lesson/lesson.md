# Rights and Duties Go Together

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: every right carries its duty, and the duties come in two kinds — the values a citizen keeps (care of parents, the society's customs and traditions, teachers' counsel) and the legal duties the state lays down (loyalty to the state, obeying the laws, helping to uphold the law, paying taxes) — as Sri Lanka's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 4 Rights and duties

Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000, printed p. 105, teaches: Every right carries its duty, and the duties come in two kinds — the values a citizen keeps (care of parents, the society's customs and traditions, teachers' counsel) and the legal duties the state lays down (loyalty to the state, obeying the laws, helping to uphold the law, paying taxes). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way

Sri Lanka's own way: the combined book's second half is a full civics course in disguise — democracy first as an idea with its thinkers and its six conditions, then Sri Lanka's own 1978 constitution opened article by article: the rights chapter with its Ombudsman and Human Rights Commission, the vote's road from McCallum to Donoughmore, the preferential presidential election, Parliament's 225 seats, the cabinet that falls with its bill, provinces and pradeshiya sabhas, and a court ladder that ends in village mediation boards. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
