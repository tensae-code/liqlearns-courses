**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Mexico, Turkey).**

Turkey's own way (Sosyal Bilgiler 6. Sınıf, 2. Kitap): the Grade 6 democracy area asks who actually moves a government — the people's needs, parties, civil society, media and the kamuoyu (public opinion) they form together — and then follows citizenship itself online, where rights are now exercised through a screen and the active citizen is the one who still writes the petition.


Mexico's own way (Grade 8 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades): the second year turns from living together to judging and acting — pupils weigh ethical judgements on real injustice, learn how laws are made and changed in Mexico's own Congress, practise mediation, and end as watchdogs: citizen participation is taught as the duty to see public money administered with honesty, transparency and legality.

**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Mexico, Turkey, Palestine, Pakistan)..**

Pakistan's own way (Book 1): citizenship is taught before everything else — a child first learns what a citizen, a global citizen and a digital citizen is, then Pakistan's own 1973 Constitution, its federal and provincial organs with real seat counts, and only then history, geography and economics.

Pakistan's own way (Book 2): Class 5 deepens Class 4 — rights now change with time, the UN and the 1948 Declaration enter, freedom of speech gets its own lesson with John Stuart Mill quoted, and government is compared across federal, provincial AND local levels with a table of who does what.

Pakistan's own way (Book 11): the same Single National Curriculum in the KPK board's English — citizenship taught identically in Peshawar and Lahore, with the global-citizen lesson anchored by Dr Ruth Pfau, the German doctor who gave 57 years to Pakistan's leprosy patients, and Malala Yousafzai, KPK's own daughter, as the worked examples in the pupil's copy.

Pakistan's own way (Book 12): the KPK Class 5 book gives the citizen's charter in two plain lists — seven rights (ending in the right to vote) against nine responsibilities (ending in honest dealing) — and makes Abdul Sattar Edhi, who "served the suffering humanity", its model of what a responsible citizen is.
