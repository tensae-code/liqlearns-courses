**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Syria). Oman is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 3, Term 2) (teacher guide — evidence): at Grade 3 the subject turns from feelings to law-shaped habits — rights first, then duties, then the homeland held as a trust (أمانة); and the same rights-and-duties pair is then taught again at world scale, where equality, safety and being heard are the child's global rights.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 3, Term 1) (teacher guide — evidence): rights and duties are taught at family scale before homeland scale — and the family unit ends with the children drafting a ميثاق, a charter, for their own family and pledging to keep it: constitution-making at the kitchen table.

Country honesty note: Oman's student books were not held — its placements are based on teacher guides, and the G7 Term-2 content is a named GAP (the Grade 7 framework assigns the Basic Statute of the State and government institutions to Grade 7, no Term 1 unit teaches them, and the Term 2 book was not reached, as filed in the oman-placements.md record-only section); all Oman placements in this lesson are evidence only, outside acceptance counts.

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 4): Grade 4 drops the Me-and-You ring structure for a straight civics unit that defines citizenship in one sentence — love of the land, defending it, enjoying the rights, committing to the duties, respecting the state's systems and laws — then cashes each word out: rights are what the state hands the citizen free (school, clinic, clean streets), duties are what the citizen hands back, down to crossing at the pedestrian crossing and not throwing litter; the unit closes by honouring the martyrs.
