# The Property Right And How It Arises — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: the property right as the root right — Rothbard's claim that every human right is at bottom a property right, beginning with the property in one's own person (which is why no one may "own" another's free expression); what ownership is — the right to possess, use and dispose of a thing (Constitution Article 16.3; the Civil Code of 2002), including intellectual wealth — a work, a patent, a name and reputation — guarded by the Copyright and Patent laws; how ownership arises, in the Code's own diagram: by contract (sale, gift, exchange, loan, inheritance-by-will and the rest) and by law (finding lost property, discovering treasure, gathering strayed livestock, joining or dividing things, processing, acquiring by court decision or seizure) — with the traps marked: property taken without the legal ground is no ownership at all, and immovable property is owned only from its registration; how the right is exercised and defended — by agreement or contract between the parties (the grazing-land agreement under Civil Code Article 151), and, where it is violated without crime, by a claim (нэхэмжлэл) to the civil court, whose required contents the lesson lists and then drafts in full for the case of the boots from Narantuul market that tore in three days; and the crimes against property in the 2015 Criminal Code's Chapter 17 — theft, robbery, fraud, embezzlement and eleven more, pictured one by one.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: True**
