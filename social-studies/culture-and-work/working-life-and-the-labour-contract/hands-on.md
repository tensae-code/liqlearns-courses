# Hands-on — Working Life And the Labour Contract

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Section 3.4 "Labour legal relations"
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Is work a right or a duty? — the Constitution answers both ways (Article 16: the right to choose a profession, to fair conditions, pay and rest, and no forced labour; Article 17: the duty to work, raise one's children, and protect nature); the labour relation joins employee and employer through the labour contract, whose essential terms the Labour Law fixes (the post's name, its duties, the wage, the working conditions — Article 21.1) and which must be in writing; who may work — from sixteen, at fifteen with parents' consent, at fourteen only in approved light work; the working day of eight hours and forty a week, overtime paid at 1.5 times; how a contract ends — the employer's grounds in Article 40 (the unit closed or staff cut, the worker unfit, sixty reached and pension due, repeated discipline breaches, betrayed trust over money, election to another post) and the disciplinary ladder (warning; wage cut up to 20% for up to three months; dismissal) — tested on the case of the fast-food worker made to work twelve-hour days without the overtime pay the law promises. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
