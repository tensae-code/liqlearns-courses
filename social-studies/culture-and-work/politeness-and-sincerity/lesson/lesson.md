# Politeness and Sincerity

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: greetings join hearts — the greetings of every day, practised with a "greeting tree" the class grows — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — (1) 気もちのよいふるまいを (Pleasant behaviour)

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 54), teaches: Greetings join hearts — the greetings of every day, practised with a "greeting tree" the class grows. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 読みもの「あいさつじま」("Greeting Island")

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 60), teaches: The monkeys of Greeting Island who found greetings a bother and left the island; Keita, who felt the same, tries greeting and is answered (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — (1) だれに対しても真心をもって (With a true heart toward everyone)

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 56), teaches: Sincerity and manners toward every person, and the true heart inside Japanese greeting culture. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — (1) 礼儀正しく真心をもって (Politely and with a true heart)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 56), teaches: Greetings and manners as the joining of hearts. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 5 — 江戸しぐさに学ぼう (Learning from Edo manners)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 58), teaches: The considerate manners of the Edo period as a living model. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 6 — Japan's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 7 — Japan's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 8 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
