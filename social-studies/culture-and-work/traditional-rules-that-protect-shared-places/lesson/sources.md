**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Nauru, Niue).**

Nauru's own way: Nauru has no book of its own in this set — the remaining strands arrive as shared Pacific teaching: the sea is held in trust from the ancestors for future generations, fishing is governed by named values and by community rules (taboo, closure, ban), and the catch is shared with the whole village.

Niue's own way: Niue's own pages carry its community order in miniature: each district owns its stretch of coast, everyone in the district has equal access, the whole community co-operates when the migrating fish run — and the fono ban closes the reef while they do.
