**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Nauru, Niue, Tuvalu). Tuvalu's Teacher Guide part is teacher-guide evidence only — Tuvalu is counted here on its student reader (Book 3).**

Nauru's own way: Nauru has no book of its own in this set — the remaining strands arrive as shared Pacific teaching: the sea is held in trust from the ancestors for future generations, fishing is governed by named values and by community rules (taboo, closure, ban), and the catch is shared with the whole village.

Niue's own way: Niue's own pages carry its community order in miniature: each district owns its stretch of coast, everyone in the district has equal access, the whole community co-operates when the migrating fish run — and the fono ban closes the reef while they do.

Tuvalu's own way: Tuvalu's pages in the shared set are its own community life: women and children gathering on the reef, men diving for pearl shell, pulou bird-netting — work shared by role and by family, under values and rules the whole community keeps.

Tuvalu's own way (teacher guide — evidence): The guide's Tuvalu notes state the country's own teaching plainly: the ancestors' conservation closures — land and lagoon set aside by the community until healthy again — are Tuvalu's own rule for shared places. That evidence is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
