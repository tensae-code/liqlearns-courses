**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Yemen, Libya). Bolivia is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Bolivia's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the teachers' compendium carries two civics units no held student book carries — the Year 3 pair of women's-protection laws, taught from real cases (a councilwoman driven from office; the murdered concejala Juana Quispe), and the Year 5 citizenship unit, which builds Bolivian citizenship straight from CPE Articles 141–144 and ends with the stateless person who can neither study, work nor marry.

Yemen's own way (G6 National Education): Grade 6 National Education builds citizenship in widening circles — family, then tribe, then constitution — and teaches the tribe honestly as a real social component with its own customs and a role in keeping social peace, before the constitution unit names the citizen's rights, duties and the qualities of the good citizen (love of homeland shown in deeds, guarding public property, constructive criticism). That evidence is stocked here as the way Yemen teaches this thing; it is counted in the acceptance count above (student book).

Libya's own way (National Education G6): Grade 6 defines the citizen by a chain of relations — rights paired with duties, freedom paired with responsibility, then one lesson per circle the child belongs to (family, school, neighbourhood) — and grounds the rights lesson in named world instruments: "human rights" as the UN defines them, the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child, and UNICEF.
