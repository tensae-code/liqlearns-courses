# Hands-on — Building Good Relationships with Others

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Building Good Relationships with Others
Work through this part with your hands: good relationships are built by desiring and deciding to live harmoniously — eating, reading and playing together peacefully and helping each other in difficult and good times — and the techniques for maintaining them with fellow pupils. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Good Relationships with Friends
Work through this part with your hands: building and maintaining good relationships with friends at home and at school, starting from identifying the people you live with. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Relationship in the Society
Work through this part with your hands: a relationship is how two or more people, groups, societies or nations associate and link together; good relationships begin in the family and extend to school and community, are built and maintained, and require treating people equally without discriminating by gender, ethnic group, religion, race or social position. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Caring for Oneself and for Others
Work through this part with your hands: avoid behaviour that disturbs peace in the family and at school, denounce and report socially unacceptable behaviour and cruelty (to the school counsellor), take part in family activities, and build good relationships with neighbours by visiting, joining clubs and associations — a freedom the constitution allows — and helping others solve their problems, as Moses helped the orphan Juma with study materials, hospital care and friendship. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Tanzania's way — the books' own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: say how Tanzania's books teach this thing, in your own words, from the lesson. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
