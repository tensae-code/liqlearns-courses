Morocco's own way (Book 1, Dalil Moufid Ijtiamiat 5e): Moroccan primary Social Studies is one integrated subject (History + Geography + Civic Education) built around "my homeland"; its civics third is a full civic-education programme in its own right — civic behaviour defined as the citizen's relation to society and to the state through its institutions, civic values (freedom, dignity, solidarity, equality, tolerance, rejecting discrimination) learned by practice not memorisation, and the classroom itself run as a small democracy (a negotiated class charter and an elected class cooperative with ballot box and vote count).

Morocco's own way (Book 3, Fadaa Ijtiamiat 1AC): collège Social Studies carries a full Citizenship Education component built on ten value lessons in two axes — five personal-social values (dignity, freedom, equality, justice, solidarity), then tolerance, democracy, peace, the role of law, and a field visit to a human-rights institution — each lesson built from constitutional and international-charter texts.

Pakistan's own way (Book 1): citizenship is taught before everything else — a child first learns what a citizen, a global citizen and a digital citizen is, then Pakistan's own 1973 Constitution, its federal and provincial organs with real seat counts, and only then history, geography and economics.

Pakistan's own way (Book 9): the brand-new PECTAA book opens the final school year with the constitution as a living document — its amendments listed one by one to 2017 — and sets Pakistan's own rights charter side by side with the UN's, asking students to compare them.

Pakistan's own way (Book 11): the same Single National Curriculum in the KPK board's English — citizenship taught identically in Peshawar and Lahore, with the global-citizen lesson anchored by Dr Ruth Pfau, the German doctor who gave 57 years to Pakistan's leprosy patients, and Malala Yousafzai, KPK's own daughter, as the worked examples in the pupil's copy.
