# Video script — Education as a Right and the Four Pillars

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Honduras Grade 9 G9 Student CCSS, printed p. 32, teaches: Education is the best road to success and has carried social prestige since Egyptians, Greeks, Romans, Maya and Aztecs; the Delors report "Learning: the treasure within" (UN) sets the four pillars of modern education — learning to know, to be, to do and to live together, including the intelligent, peaceful solution of conflicts; Honduras's exclusion is quantified (school coverage 25% for adolescents in its figures; illiteracy 12.8%, some 900,000 people), making defence of the right to education a duty.

Honduras Grade 8 G8 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 128, teaches: The right to education is a fundamental, universal human right: it lets every person acquire knowledge and reach a full social life, and is vital for the economic, social and cultural development of all societies; it means free compulsory primary education for all, an obligation to develop secondary education accessible to every child, equitable access to higher education, and basic education for those who never completed primary school; through education, common principles and a society's values pass to new generations.

Honduras Grade 9 G9 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 24, teaches: The global scenario needs citizens who exercise democracy critically and build knowledge, so education must evolve from the school that served industrial society to one that prepares for the knowledge society; literacy is not mere decoding but a form of representing the world and participating in it, and the basic skill-set now runs to information handling, communication, problem-solving, critical thought, creativity, innovation, autonomy, collaboration and teamwork (21st Century Skills); UNESCO (2013) is cited for the State's commitment to guarantee the right to education so it is a reality for every human being, not only an idea.

Honduras Grade 9 G9 Telebásica Student CCSS, printed p. 39, teaches: Each person is unique and unrepeatable, and education — at home and at school — forms the personality inseparably from it; study is an activity of the intelligence for understanding, and it silently trains labour, order, strength, justice and responsibility, with techniques of its own (underline the essential, outline, prefer books over superficial web pages); to study well and reach school success three things are needed — poder (having the faculties), querer (the motivation, whose lack — not lack of intelligence — is why many fail) and saber estudiar.

Honduras's own way (Book 9): the cycle closes with vocation and rights — three full lessons on the generations of human rights (with Honduras's own record: the most-violated civil right is physical integrity; Garífuna and Miskito peoples organising for third-generation rights), then the UN from Honduras's seat and the country's foreign relations told through its border awards.

Honduras's own way (Book 18): the final guide deepens the pupil book's rights cycle into doctrine and machinery — the UN treaty bodies and the three pillars, the ancient sources of rights from Hammurabi to the Iroquois constitution, the 1966 Covenants — and widens Bloque 4 from the pupil book's UN seat to Honduras's own regional house: the OAS, ODECA and SICA, and a foreign-relations history that ends at the 1992 Hague judgment and the 2014 Isla Conejo dispute.

Honduras's own way (Book 19): the Telebásica twin of Grade 9 teaches the same cycle at a distance learner's pace, each sequence paired with its television programme — and it is the more Honduras-specific of the two books on rights: CONADEH's creation decrees dated one by one, women's suffrage won by Decree 29 of 1955 with the literacy limit named, and the protective laws listed with their La Gaceta dates.
