# Education as a Right and the Four Pillars

## Sub lesson 5 — Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way

Honduras's own way: the cycle closes with vocation and rights — three full lessons on the generations of human rights (with Honduras's own record: the most-violated civil right is physical integrity; Garífuna and Miskito peoples organising for third-generation rights), then the UN from Honduras's seat and the country's foreign relations told through its border awards. the final guide deepens the pupil book's rights cycle into doctrine and machinery — the UN treaty bodies and the three pillars, the ancient sources of rights from Hammurabi to the Iroquois constitution, the 1966 Covenants — and widens Bloque 4 from the pupil book's UN seat to Honduras's own regional house: the OAS, ODECA and SICA, and a foreign-relations history that ends at the 1992 Hague judgment and the 2014 Isla Conejo dispute. the Telebásica twin of Grade 9 teaches the same cycle at a distance learner's pace, each sequence paired with its television programme — and it is the more Honduras-specific of the two books on rights: CONADEH's creation decrees dated one by one, women's suffrage won by Decree 29 of 1955 with the literacy limit named, and the protective laws listed with their La Gaceta dates.
