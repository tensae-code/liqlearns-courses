**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Algeria). Vanuatu's and Sierra Leone's parts are teacher-guide evidence only, named here, counted nowhere.**

Vanuatu's own way (teacher guide — evidence): social citizenship is taught as roles before rules — the Year 1 child maps who they are in family and village (chief, pastor, nurse, police, women's and youth group leaders), learns that roles change and are not fixed by gender, and only in Years 5–6 reaches rights, and how a community's rules are made and by whom.

Algeria's own way: civic education is one named lesson inside every unit of the single unified Grade 1 book, always under the running header "I love my homeland" (أحب وطني) for the symbols lessons — greeting, school card, respecting elders and kindness to the young first, then flag, currency, identity documents and the anthem "Qassaman" by Moufdi Zakaria.

Sierra Leone's own way (teacher guide — evidence): primary civics is taught as Sierra Leone's own citizenship — the flag's green-white-blue meanings, the coat of arms, anthem and pledge, and the country's own provinces, districts and 190 chiefdoms.
