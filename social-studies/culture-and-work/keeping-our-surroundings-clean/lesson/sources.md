**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Algeria, Libya, Serbia, Iraq). Vanuatu's and Sierra Leone's parts are teacher-guide evidence only, named here, counted nowhere.**

Vanuatu's own way (teacher guide — evidence): social citizenship is taught as roles before rules — the Year 1 child maps who they are in family and village (chief, pastor, nurse, police, women's and youth group leaders), learns that roles change and are not fixed by gender, and only in Years 5–6 reaches rights, and how a community's rules are made and by whom.

Algeria's own way: civic education is one named lesson inside every unit of the single unified Grade 1 book, always under the running header "I love my homeland" (أحب وطني) for the symbols lessons — greeting, school card, respecting elders and kindness to the young first, then flag, currency, identity documents and the anthem "Qassaman" by Moufdi Zakaria.


Serbia's own way (Grade 1 Свет око нас): the integrated subject opens with the child herself (me, my feelings, my family) and widens circle by circle — family, home, neighbours, school, street — with rights taught from the first year as inseparable from duties ("every right seeks a duty"), and Serbia's own school slava, Saint Sava's Day on 27 January, taught as every school's feast.

Serbia's own way (Grade 2 Свет око нас): the second year widens the circles again — family rules are now made by all members together, the settlement's people become "fellow citizens" with shared things (street, park, river, school, bus are all "ours"), and the state enters through its symbols: flag, coat of arms and the anthem "Боже правде", with the behaviour owed while it plays.

Serbia's own way (Grade 3 Природа и друштво): the third-grade book widens "my region" into the whole republic — its many peoples named one by one as equal citizens, work divided into making and serving, and the environment taught as a duty: soil, water and parks protected by rules every child can keep.

Sierra Leone's own way (teacher guide — evidence): primary civics is taught as Sierra Leone's own citizenship — the flag's green-white-blue meanings, the coat of arms, anthem and pledge, and the country's own provinces, districts and 190 chiefdoms.

Libya's own way (National Education G4): the whole book is written in the first person — every lesson is "I and …" (I and my family, I and my school, I and the neighbourhood) — citizenship starts as the child's own relationships, and even the environment unit is framed as the child's own behaviour: what I throw away, what I plant, which beach I clean.

Libya's own way (National Education G6): Grade 6 defines the citizen by a chain of relations — rights paired with duties, freedom paired with responsibility, then one lesson per circle the child belongs to (family, school, neighbourhood) — and grounds the rights lesson in named world instruments: "human rights" as the UN defines them, the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child, and UNICEF.
