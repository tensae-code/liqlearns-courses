# Types of Human Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: rights divide into civil and political rights (freedom — of expression and opinion, of knowledge and thought — the right to life, equality before the law, dignity such as a good reputation, respect for privacy, and the right to stand and to vote in elections); economic rights (the right to work and to obtain a suitable standard of living); social rights (education, health care, suitable food and drink, security and safety); and environmental rights (living in a clean environment free of pollution, and a share in protecting it and in the work of development) — as Palestine's book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Types of Human Rights

Palestine Grade 6 Social Studies Part 1 (printed p. 85), teaches: rights divide into civil and political rights (freedom — of expression and opinion, of knowledge and thought — the right to life, equality before the law, dignity such as a good reputation, respect for privacy, and the right to stand and to vote in elections); economic rights (the right to work and to obtain a suitable standard of living); social rights (education, health care, suitable food and drink, security and safety); and environmental rights (living in a clean environment free of pollution, and a share in protecting it and in the work of development). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 6 Social Studies Part 1): the constitution is taught from Palestine's own draft Basic Law — pupils extract legal rules from its actual articles (the people as source of the authorities, a democratic parliamentary system, equality before the law) before they are given the definition, and the three authorities are each named by the Palestinian institution that holds them. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
