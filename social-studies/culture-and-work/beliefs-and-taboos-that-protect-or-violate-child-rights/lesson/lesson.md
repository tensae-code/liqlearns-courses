# Beliefs and Taboos That Protect or Violate Child Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: beliefs are ideas people hold to be true and taboos are actions a cultural group forbids; beliefs and taboos that uphold child rights include respect for elders, the taboo on harming children, valuing education and the community's responsibility to protect children — while female genital mutilation, child marriage and corporal punishment violate children's rights — as Zimbabwe's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 31: Indigenous cultural beliefs and taboos

Zimbabwe, *PlusOne Social Science Grade 4 Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 98), teaches: Beliefs are ideas people hold to be true and taboos are actions a cultural group forbids; beliefs and taboos that uphold child rights include respect for elders, the taboo on harming children, valuing education and the community's responsibility to protect children — while female genital mutilation, child marriage and corporal punishment violate children's rights. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 6) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 6) (teacher guide — evidence): child rights are sourced, not just listed — the Constitution of Zimbabwe Section 81, the African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child and the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child; cultural beliefs are tested against those rights (FGM, child marriage and corporal punishment named as violations), and local welfare includes the traditional Zunde Ramambo / Isiphala Senkosi (the chief's granary) alongside councils, BEAM and church feeding schemes. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
