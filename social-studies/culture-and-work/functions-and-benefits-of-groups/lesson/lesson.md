# Functions and Benefits of Groups

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: groups have common goals, clearly defined status, shared values and common symbols such as uniforms; their functions are interdependence for survival, security and belonging, shared experience, and social control — the individual conforms to the group's rules, bringing order in society; joining groups lets members practise justice, loyalty and cooperation and develop acceptable attitudes and values — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Characteristics, functions and benefits of groups

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 87–88), teaches: Groups have common goals, clearly defined status, shared values and common symbols such as uniforms; their functions are interdependence for survival, security and belonging, shared experience, and social control — the individual conforms to the group's rules, bringing order in society; joining groups lets members practise justice, loyalty and cooperation and develop acceptable attitudes and values. ## Sub lesson 2 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: the secondary book teaches the same citizenship at national scale — CARICOM as the region's own institution, the family as society's oldest institution, women's equality written into the 1980 Constitution and the Acts of the 1990s, and the state answering pollution through the Environmental Protection Agency.
