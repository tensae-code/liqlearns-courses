**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Rwanda, Ecuador, Dominican Republic, Dominica, Fiji, Nepal, Bulgaria, Iran). Zambia is teacher-module evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 Social Studies & Creative Arts G4): the family budget is taught by contrasting two households' real monthly income-and-expenditure tables (Rs. 10,000 against Rs. 15,000), and a family's income is drawn as a pie chart of its sources — milk, chickens, vegetables.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 3 Social Studies Year 3): Economics enters at Year 3 through the child's own pocket money and two working families' budgets in Fiji dollars — a fisherman paid monthly and a carpenter paid weekly.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 5 Year 5 Social Studies): A full primary money curriculum in one unit — work and wages first, then the Fiji dollar against other currencies, then a family budget, a personal saving plan and what banks do.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 6 Year 6 Social Science): The Year 5 money unit is deepened with employer-employee relations, a worked fortnightly family budget, and income tax and FNPF deductions explained through what the government pays for.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 2 Ciencias Sociales 2° Primaria): Economics enters through the family and the community — goods are introduced from what a child receives and consumes at home, money from family purchases, and budgeting from a story about neighbours who spend on parties and brand clothes and then cannot buy food.

Zambia's own way: Economics at Grade 4 is money and enterprise in kwacha — market sellers, maize farmers and shopkeepers as the entrepreneurs, a K10/K20 in play money for the needs-vs-wants lessons, and a classroom market day. The budget is a shopping list in kwacha that puts needs before wants. This Zambia material stands on the teacher module as evidence only.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): from P5 the medium is English and the organising frame is the child's own PROVINCE — its location, services, environment, climate, population and infrastructure — before the frame widens to the whole country at P6. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Rwanda is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): P6 widens the frame from the province to the country, then to East Africa and Africa — Rwanda is located by latitude and longitude (1°S–3°S, 28°E–31°E), its landlocked position is taught as the reason for regional co-operation, and every unit is built around a problem statement with the pupil's book activities answered in the guide. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Rwanda is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies SB): the pupil's book teaches the province through named Rwandan things — the five provinces with Kigali City as the case study, Rwanda's four seasons by their Kinyarwanda names (Umuhindo, Urugaryi, Itumba, Icyi), and the country's two international airports (Kigali and Kamembe) in the transport-places lesson.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies SB): the P6 pupil's book is the fullest Geography book of the set — a whole unit on direction and location works Rwanda onto the East African and African maps by latitude and longitude and time zones, then physical features and climate, and a climate-change sequence (types, change, effects, overcoming) sits inside it; regional co-operation is taught bloc by bloc (COMESA, CEPGL, ECCAS, AU, NEPAD).

Ecuador's own way (Ecuador Grade 13 Emprendimiento y Gestión): Economics enters the entrepreneurship course through the family and the small venture — costs are worked through a school-desk factory costed month by month in dollars, and every unit closes with a financial-education section that takes the same tools home to the family budget and the family's debts. The family budget itself is drawn up monthly, income (salaries, overtime, microenterprise income) against spending sorted as essential, of medium importance, or eventual/emergency, closing with the month's surplus or deficit.


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 2 Technology and Entrepreneurship): the Grade 1 concepts return one level up — professions are sorted into makers of goods and performers of services who are paid for their labour, and the family budget is a folder the child makes and fills with the family's own income-and-expense plan.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 5 Technology and Entrepreneurship): from Grade 5 the subject becomes a real textbook and Economics gets its own section — scarcity is named as the science's central problem, and price is taught through two market diagrams: big demand lifts prices, big supply pushes them down until some firms go bankrupt.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 6 Technology and Entrepreneurship): Grade 6 prices a product the Bulgarian way — cost price (себестойност) built from direct and indirect costs, a calculation (калкулация) that adds the planned profit, and an "alternative price" for the road not taken.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 4 Social Studies): the family-budget table (income / expense / saving) is introduced at Grade 4 through a boy saving for a football, with his 50,000-toman apple-picking wage as the income example.
