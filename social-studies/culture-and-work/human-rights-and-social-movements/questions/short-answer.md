# Human Rights and Social Movements — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Social Movements and Human Rights in Mexico (Grade 7) — human rights are universal and inseparable from the person, and were won through struggles — the 1789 Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, and Olympe de Gouges' 1791 answer demanding the same rights for women (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: An Ethical Stance and Today's Movements (Grade 9) — today's social and political movements — gender equality, climate justice, migrants' rights — and the ethical stance from which adolescents can promote and defend human rights (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Mexico's way — the book's own teaching way — Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. Mexico's own way (Grade 9): the third year turns the course outward to action — participation spaces for children and adolescents, community projects for democratic culture, and democracy's own challenges (corruption, violence, disenchantment) named as things the young citizen is expected to work on. (any close wording).**
