## Sub lesson 1 — Social Movements and Human Rights in Mexico (Grade 7)

Mexico Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 1 (secundaria) (printed pp. 323–325), teaches: Human rights are universal and inseparable from the person, and were won through struggles — the 1789 Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, and Olympe de Gouges' 1791 answer demanding the same rights for women. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — An Ethical Stance and Today's Movements (Grade 9)

Mexico Grade 9 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 3 (secundaria) (printed pp. 239–247), teaches: Today's social and political movements — gender equality, climate justice, migrants' rights — and the ethical stance from which adolescents can promote and defend human rights. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mexico's way — the book's own teaching way

Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. Mexico's own way (Grade 9): the third year turns the course outward to action — participation spaces for children and adolescents, community projects for democratic culture, and democracy's own challenges (corruption, violence, disenchantment) named as things the young citizen is expected to work on. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
