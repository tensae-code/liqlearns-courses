# How the President is Elected

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Iran is governed by three powers under the Leadership, and "the government" means the executive; the President is elected by the people's direct vote for four years (one consecutive re-election allowed), from candidates whose qualification the Guardian Council has confirmed, after election campaigning; the Leader then endorses (تنفیذ) the result, the President swears his oath in the Majlis, and forms a cabinet whose ministers need the Majlis's vote of confidence — as Iran's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — How the President is Elected

Iran Social Studies Grade 8 (student book, Persian), printed p. 14, teaches: Iran is governed by three powers under the Leadership, and "the government" means the executive; the President is elected by the people's direct vote for four years (one consecutive re-election allowed), from candidates whose qualification the Guardian Council has confirmed, after election campaigning; the Leader then endorses (تنفیذ) the result, the President swears his oath in the Majlis, and forms a cabinet whose ministers need the Majlis's vote of confidence. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iran's way — the book's own teaching way

Iran's own way: the civics year is built as an institutional tour taken in the order a citizen meets the state — cooperation in home and neighbourhood, then the executive (this book's "government"), then the judiciary — and every institution is introduced through newspaper headlines and the pupil's own town. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
