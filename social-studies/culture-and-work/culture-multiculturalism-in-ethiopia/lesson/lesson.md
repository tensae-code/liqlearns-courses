# Culture — multiculturalism in Ethiopia

## Do it first

With partners, dramatise respectful, tolerant living together: one short scene in a market or school where different cultures meet well.

## Sub lesson 1 — Many cultures, one place

**Multiculturalism is different kinds of culture present in one area** — differing in **language, religion, ethnicity, customary law, custom and values**.

**Ethiopia is the book's prime example**, with **more than 80 nations, nationalities and peoples**, each with its own culture. A country holding many cultures holds a **beauty**.

[Picture 11505 — a town square where many dress, language and faith groups meet]

## Sub lesson 2 — Respect and tolerance — and diversity widened (teacher guide — evidence)

The great value inside multiculturalism is **respect and tolerance**.

The teacher guide widens diversity itself (teacher guide — evidence): difference is expressed through **nationality, language, religion, race, and also health difference, sexual orientation, gender, age, disability and ethnicity**. The guide also names **human race** as a cultural element (teacher guide — evidence): a category of people that is **socially constructed**, sharing distinctive physical characteristics such as skin colour, facial features and stature — in the key's words, physical differences that groups and cultures consider socially significant.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Say what multiculturalism is and name four ways cultures in one place can differ. Give Ethiopia's number: more than 80 nations, nationalities and peoples. Name multiculturalism's great value, and say how the guide widens diversity (teacher guide — evidence).
