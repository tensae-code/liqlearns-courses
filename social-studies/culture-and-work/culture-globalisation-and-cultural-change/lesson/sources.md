**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Brazil, Costa Rica, Mongolia, Ecuador, Samoa, Tanzania). Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere. South Sudan is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Tanzania's own way (Standard Six, Civic and Moral Education Standard Six Pupil's Book): TIE teaches civics as a good-governance wheel (the cover itself: transparency, accountability, participation, human rights, integrity, efficiency, reconciliation, democracy, equality, rule of law around “GOOD GOVERNANCE”) and builds the state from the child's own levels — street, village, ward, district, region, nation — with the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar as the founding fact and Kiswahili, unity and humanism (utu) as named national treasures.

Tanzania's own way (Standard Five, Social Studies Standard Five Pupil's Book): Tanzanian culture is defined across 120+ ethnic groups with Kiswahili as the connecting national language; traditions and customs are taught as the community's ethics system — they promote hard work, cooperation, mutual help, harmonious living and conflict resolution, discipline, and self-reliance.

Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 8): the Grade 8 year is the LOCAL year — every chapter is re-anchored to the child's own орон нутаг (local area/homeland): its environment and problems, its heritage, its economy and market, its governance. The book's Law/Civics parts (Chapters IV–V, printed pp. 63–94) are named in the placements, not placed. Mongolia's own way (Grade 11): the final year is the state-and-world year — cultural globalization and freedom, monetary policy, foreign trade and investment, and Mongolia's signature “third neighbour” and soft-power policies taught as named topics. The book's Sociology part (Chapter I Topic 1.2, printed p. 29) and Law/Civics parts (Chapter III, printed pp. 71–103; Chapter V, printed pp. 139–165; Chapter VI except the placed topics, printed pp. 167–176) are named in the placements, not placed. That way is stocked here as the way Mongolia teaches this thing.

Honesty: pupil content comes from the Amhara student book. The Addis Ababa Grade 8 teacher guide is NOT edition-matched to this student book and is truncated (only printed pp. 1–47, Units 1–3, in hand); no guide coverage is claimed for this lesson beyond what its sources rows name.

Costa Rica's own way: the CONED 11 anthology opens with a Geography unit on globalisation read "from the geographic viewpoint" (spatial flows of production and consumption, global cities, space-time compression), then turns to Costa Rica's own 20th-century history; its commercial workbook twin, Porras y Gamboa's *Estudios Sociales 11*, teaches the same ministry programme as a fixed cycle (problem question, basic concepts, my previous ideas, widening my knowledge, I apply my knowledge, problem-based learning, exam preparation) with heavy map-work and Costa Rica-specific data: ICE internet-coverage map, services-export league table, Costa Rica's geo-strategic bridge position between North/South America and the Caribbean. That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 8 book): at Superior level the book turns thematic — history of work and social transformation, then Latin America and Ecuador today; Geography enters as a full cartography tema (map studies explicitly tied to Earth's formation, continents, oceans, climates, natural risks and contingency plans) and as the economic-sectors tema on Ecuador. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Honesty note: the ministry's 2024 transición-curricular PDFs, as held, reuse one body per sublevel — the Grade 5 body also carries the Grade 6 and Grade 7 covers (Subnivel Media), and the Grade 8 body also carries the Grade 9 and Grade 10 covers (Subnivel Superior). That content is credited once, under its true Grade 5 / Grade 8 identity, exactly as the placements state; the genuine Ecuador Grade 6, 7, 9 and 10 Estudios Sociales books are NOT held (library debt).

Samoa's own way (Year 9, Book 3): Culture and development are taught comparatively — Samoan cultural treasures and symbols first, then development measured and lived through India and Samoa side by side, with map-profile work on both countries.

Samoa's own way (Year 10, Book 2): Identity and cultural interaction are taught from the Samoan self outward, and place change is taught through one big Pacific-region case — deforestation in Indonesia, read off South-West Pacific maps.

Samoa's own way (Year 11, Book 2): A single Pacific commodity carries the resources teaching — the coconut, from production factors to industry administration and national decision-making in Samoa — set against OPEC's oil, Pacific co-operation and Samoa's own development planning.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 12 S4 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Mirrors the student's book's 9 units and adds South Sudan case studies the student book only hints at — Mining in South Sudan, corruption and development in South Sudan, and the importance of international trade to South Sudan in particular. That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
