# Lesson plan — The Media the Fourth Power

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** The Media the Fourth Power
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Mongolia, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Media the Fourth Power — What mass media are — the press, radio, television, news agencies, documentary film and electronic press that deliver true information quickly and regularly; why the media are called the fourth power — because they secure the citizen's freedom to express views, an inseparable part of democratic values; their work in a democracy, in three universal functions — to inform, to help citizens form their own views and take part, and to oversee and criticise (the watchdog that exposes government error and rouses the public) — with entertainment and education beside them; the principles media must keep in return — truth for the whole public over one person's interest, equal reach regardless of age, sex, faith or education, being the people's voice, and protecting journalists' rights; Mongolia's evidence — the 2015 Democratic Governance study finding media judged dependent on politics and money (42.6% not agreeing they are independent), and the 2016 trust scores, national television at 90 and national radio at 80 while the yellow press is trusted least; and the new arrival — social media, set beside mass media in one picture — as Mongolia's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
