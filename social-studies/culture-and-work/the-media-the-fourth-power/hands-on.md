# Hands-on — The Media the Fourth Power

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Topic 5.3 "The media's influence in modern society and political life"
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: What mass media are — the press, radio, television, news agencies, documentary film and electronic press that deliver true information quickly and regularly; why the media are called the fourth power — because they secure the citizen's freedom to express views, an inseparable part of democratic values; their work in a democracy, in three universal functions — to inform, to help citizens form their own views and take part, and to oversee and criticise (the watchdog that exposes government error and rouses the public) — with entertainment and education beside them; the principles media must keep in return — truth for the whole public over one person's interest, equal reach regardless of age, sex, faith or education, being the people's voice, and protecting journalists' rights; Mongolia's evidence — the 2015 Democratic Governance study finding media judged dependent on politics and money (42.6% not agreeing they are independent), and the 2016 trust scores, national television at 90 and national radio at 80 while the yellow press is trusted least; and the new arrival — social media, set beside mass media in one picture. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
