# Video script — The Media the Fourth Power

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Grade 11 Social Studies, printed p. 150, teaches: What mass media are — the press, radio, television, news agencies, documentary film and electronic press that deliver true information quickly and regularly; why the media are called the fourth power — because they secure the citizen's freedom to express views, an inseparable part of democratic values; their work in a democracy, in three universal functions — to inform, to help citizens form their own views and take part, and to oversee and criticise (the watchdog that exposes government error and rouses the public) — with entertainment and education beside them; the principles media must keep in return — truth for the whole public over one person's interest, equal reach regardless of age, sex, faith or education, being the people's voice, and protecting journalists' rights; Mongolia's evidence — the 2015 Democratic Governance study finding media judged dependent on politics and money (42.6% not agreeing they are independent), and the 2016 trust scores, national television at 90 and national radio at 80 while the yellow press is trusted least; and the new arrival — social media, set beside mass media in one picture.

Mongolia's own way (Book 9): the final year goes deep on law as the citizen's toolkit — property, harm, crime, the complaint against the state itself, and the national and UN machinery that guards rights — with sociology closing on how societies change and what moves them.
