Costa Rica Grade 11 Estudios Sociales 11 (Porras y Gamboa) (printed p. 99), teaches: Costa Rica's own constitution taught as its own way: a Constituent Assembly after the civil war writes a modern constitution on the 1871 base — autonomous institutions created, the Legislative Power strengthened over the other powers, discrimination by sex, ethnicity or religion prohibited, women given the vote, the Central Bank and the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones created, the Social Guarantees kept, the army abolished so its money goes to education and health and a civilian culture is forged, banks nationalised, and a neutral Civil Service to end political clientelism.

Costa Rica's own way: the commercial workbook twin of the CONED 11 anthology teaches the same civics as a fixed cycle (my previous ideas, basic concepts, widening my knowledge, I apply my knowledge) — and it is blunt where the anthology is neutral: the banned Communist Party is judged "a very dangerous restriction", abstention is tabled year by year from the TSE's own figures, and the answer offered is civic education itself — student elections run with the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones.
