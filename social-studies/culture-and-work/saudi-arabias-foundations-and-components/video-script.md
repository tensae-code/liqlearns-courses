Saudi Arabia Grade 10 Social Studies (Secondary Tracks, First Common Year) (printed pp. 14–22), teaches: The Kingdom stands on the Basic Law's identity — an Arab Islamic state of full sovereignty, Islam its religion, the Quran and the Prophet's Sunnah its constitution, Arabic its language, Riyadh its capital, the qibla of the Muslims and land of the Two Holy Mosques, linking the world's continents between the Arabian Gulf and the Red Sea; Vision 2030 was built on these components for a vibrant society, a thriving economy and an ambitious nation; the eight components — religious (the beating heart of the Islamic world, receiving millions of pilgrims yearly), national (King Abdulaziz's recovery of Riyadh in 1319H/1902 and a national unity the Peninsula had not known since the first Islamic era), political (the monarchy in the founder's descendants, the most fit pledged allegiance — the bay'ah — on God's Book and His Prophet's Sunnah), economic (among the world's largest oil producers and exporters with huge reserves, a leading force in OPEC, great gas and mineral wealth), touristic (al-Ula, Qiddiya, the Red Sea islands, Amaala, NEOM), civilisational (a heritage millions of years deep, al-Hijr and the Rajajil columns), social (the family as the Saudi society's nucleus, raised on the Islamic creed, loyalty and obedience, respect of the system, and a society holding fast together, the state barring all that leads to division and strife), and geographic.

Saudi Arabia's own way: the Grade 10 Tracks book starts from the components that make a state strong and influential and Saudi Arabia's own foundations and components, then follows the Kingdom's work for Arab, Islamic and international causes, and surveys the organisations it belongs to — the European Union as a model of integration, the League of Arab States, the Muslim World League, the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, OPEC, the United Nations and the G20.
