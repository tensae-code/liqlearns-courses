# Gender Perspective and Equality

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the gender perspective — seeing how socially built prejudices make men and women unequal in exercising their rights, and using that lens in language, ideas and daily dealings to build a just, equal society free of violence — as Mexico's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Gender Perspective (Grade 7)

Mexico Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 1 (secundaria) (printed pp. 315–316), teaches: The gender perspective — seeing how socially built prejudices make men and women unequal in exercising their rights, and using that lens in language, ideas and daily dealings to build a just, equal society free of violence. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethical Judgements on Injustice and Discrimination (Grade 8)

Mexico Grade 8 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 2 (secundaria) (printed pp. 293–300), teaches: Ethical judgement practised on everyday injustice — inclusion, interculturality and the gender perspective as tools, and substantive equality (same treatment and opportunities for all, in Mexico's General Law for Equality between Women and Men) against subordination, exclusion and power relations. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mexico's way — the book's own teaching way

Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. Mexico's own way (Grade 8): the second year turns from living together to judging and acting — pupils weigh ethical judgements on real injustice, learn how laws are made and changed in Mexico's own Congress, practise mediation, and end as watchdogs: citizen participation is taught as the duty to see public money administered with honesty, transparency and legality. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
