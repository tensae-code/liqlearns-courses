# The Civilised Society

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: civilisation as the alternative to barbarism — the periods of savagery (appropriating economy), barbarism (herding and farming) and civilisation (towns, writing, crafts, industry, science, art), and inside civilisation the agrarian-craft, industrial and post-industrial information-computer types (Toffler); civilisations as cultural-historical types with their own cultures (Danilevsky, Toynbee); and the contradictions of modern civilisation — everyday comfort bought by destroying nature, standardisation against national-cultural identity, Belarus standing at the crossroads of the cultural influences of West and East, North and South — so progress must be judged by humanistic criteria: the harmonious relation of man, society and nature — as Belarus' book teaches it. ## Sub lesson 1 — Civilised society (Цывілізаванае грамадства) — Grade 9

Belarus Grade 9 Person. Society. State, Book 2 (printed p. 121), teaches: Civilisation as the alternative to barbarism — the periods of savagery (appropriating economy), barbarism (herding and farming) and civilisation (towns, writing, crafts, industry, science, art), and inside civilisation the agrarian-craft, industrial and post-industrial information-computer types (Toffler); civilisations as cultural-historical types with their own cultures (Danilevsky, Toynbee); and the contradictions of modern civilisation — everyday comfort bought by destroying nature, standardisation against national-cultural identity, Belarus standing at the crossroads of the cultural influences of West and East, North and South — so progress must be judged by humanistic criteria: the harmonious relation of man, society and nature. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way

Belarus' own way: the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
