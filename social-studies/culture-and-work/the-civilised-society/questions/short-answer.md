# The Civilised Society — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Civilised society (Цывілізаванае грамадства) — Grade 9 — civilisation as the alternative to barbarism — the periods of savagery (appropriating economy), barbarism (herding and farming) and civilisation (towns, writing, crafts, industry, science, art), and inside civilisation the agrarian-craft, industrial and post-industrial information-computer types (Toffler); civilisations as cultural-historical types with their own cultures (Danilevsky, Toynbee); and the contradictions of modern civilisation — everyday comfort bought by destroying nature, standardisation against national-cultural identity, Belarus standing at the crossroads of the cultural influences of West and East, North and South — so progress must be judged by humanistic criteria: the harmonious relation of man, society and nature (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way — the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state. (any close wording).**
