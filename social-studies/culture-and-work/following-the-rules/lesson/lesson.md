## Sub lesson 1 — Following the Rules

Afghanistan Grade 6 Social Studies (Dari), printed p. 45, teaches: law is learned from school rules first (arrive on time, attend to the teacher, leave class only with permission); a society that does not apply and respect its law falls into disorder, insecurity and crime, so state and citizens alike are bound to uphold it. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Afghanistan's way — the book's own teaching way

Afghanistan's own way: the Afghan state is taught as the child's own house — society needs a state as a family needs order; the 2004 constitution is the "mother law"; the two houses are named in Pashto (Wolesi Jirga, Meshrano Jirga) and every right (children's, women's, human dignity) is grounded first in Islam and then in the constitution's own articles. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
