## Sub lesson 5 — Papua New Guinea's way — the books' own teaching way

Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 3): the youngest primary guide starts civics at home scale — what a community is and how it changes, how people relate in families, school and church, and rules in different places including safety rules for travel by vehicle and canoe — beside a culture strand taught by doing: singing and dancing, fishing and hunting, ceremonies for birth and death, food taboos and sacred places. Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 4): the community strand climbs a ladder of responsibility — the work people do, then how to behave toward others, then the roles and responsibilities of everyone from parents and friends to the PNG Defence Force, the police and government departments. Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 6): in senior primary the community strand becomes civics proper — the groups and organisations of PNG, how they relate and speak for people, and how development changes the community — while the culture strand runs from rituals to taking part in cultural events. Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science Upper Primary TG, Grades 6–8): the 2003 guide teaches the same climb as the Standards Based guides but through outcomes — local groups in Grade 6, PNG's constitutional government and elections in Grade 7, world governments and regional organisations in Grade 8 — with the wantok system, traditional law beside constitutional law, and class elections run by secret ballot with first-past-the-post and preferential counting. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
