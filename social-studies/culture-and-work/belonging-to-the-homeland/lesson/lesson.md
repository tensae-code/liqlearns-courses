# Belonging to the Homeland

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: belonging is the state in which a person feels his joining of a particular group, bound to it by a relation reflected positively in his behaviour; belonging to the homeland strengthens the relation of the individuals, the society and the homeland with one another, a relation that makes the enemy fear approaching or touching this homeland's security; and among the most important means that embody belonging to the homeland: complying with the public rulings and rules, the rules of work and the rules of behaviour, and preserving public property and the cleanliness of the public facilities, places and streets on the homeland's land, through participating in the voluntary and charitable works that aim at serving society; adopting the method of conscious dialogue when disagreements and disputes occur, between individuals or different groups, and keeping away from the negative behaviours — using violence as a means of solving problems and disputes, cooperating with the enemies to harm the homeland's and society's interest, not committing to the laws, stealing public property and funds and assaulting their sanctity; and belonging increases the strength of the bond between persons through their love of their homeland, returning to the homeland success, renaissance and progress — as Iraq's books teach it. ## Sub lesson 1 — Belonging to the Homeland

Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies (printed pp. 138–139), teaches: Belonging is the state in which a person feels his joining of a particular group, bound to it by a relation reflected positively in his behaviour; belonging to the homeland strengthens the relation of the individuals, the society and the homeland with one another, a relation that makes the enemy fear approaching or touching this homeland's security; and among the most important means that embody belonging to the homeland: complying with the public rulings and rules, the rules of work and the rules of behaviour, and preserving public property and the cleanliness of the public facilities, places and streets on the homeland's land, through participating in the voluntary and charitable works that aim at serving society; adopting the method of conscious dialogue when disagreements and disputes occur, between individuals or different groups, and keeping away from the negative behaviours — using violence as a means of solving problems and disputes, cooperating with the enemies to harm the homeland's and society's interest, not committing to the laws, stealing public property and funds and assaulting their sanctity; and belonging increases the strength of the bond between persons through their love of their homeland, returning to the homeland success, renaissance and progress. ## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: values are defined first as a system — standards that steer behaviour and spread peace "regardless of sex, religion, nationality, language or race" — and citizenship is cashed out in named rights (education, work, candidacy, the vote, freedom of opinion and belief), while the internet chapter warns in plain terms that the open network "is governed by no restrictions or conditions".
