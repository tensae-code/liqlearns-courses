The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Kiribati Year 8 Social Science TG Term 1 (printed p. 71), teaches: Unit 3 Governance and Division of Power, §4.5.3.2 — teaches: Kiribati’s division of power — executive (Beretitenti and Cabinet), legislative (Speaker and the elected Maneaba ni Maungatabu) and judiciary (Chief Justice, High Court, magistrates) checking and balancing each other to protect civil rights, the roles of each office, and how a bill passes and receives assent.

Kiribati's own way: Governance is taught as a citizen’s audit — pupils learn to tell good leadership from poor (with Fiji’s coups, Stalin and Saddam as the warning examples), then open up their own state: three branches, an elected Maneaba ni Maungatabu, a Beretitenti who must assent to bills yet can be overridden, and parliamentary broadcasts on Kiribati radio as everyday transparency.
