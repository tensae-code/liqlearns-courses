**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Micronesia, Philippines). Kiribati is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Kiribati's own way: Governance is taught as a citizen’s audit — pupils learn to tell good leadership from poor (with Fiji’s coups, Stalin and Saddam as the warning examples), then open up their own state: three branches, an elected Maneaba ni Maungatabu, a Beretitenti who must assent to bills yet can be overridden, and parliamentary broadcasts on Kiribati radio as everyday transparency.

Micronesia's own way: present-day government is taught as “Micronesia's Two Governments” — the elected FSM state/national system and the traditional leadership system running beside it — and every structure (federal levels, checks and balances, the rotating presidency) is explained through the compromises of the first constitutional convention and through village-level stories (a Yap bus bill stopped by chiefs, a fishing-fee fight between governors and Palikir, the Amnesty Bill stopped by the people).

Philippines's own way: the whole third unit of Grade 4 is one civics arc — the national government and its three branches first, then levels down to the local government, then what government does for citizens (health, education, peace, infrastructure, rights), closing on local government working with community servants; the Bill of Rights is taught straight from Article III of the Philippine Constitution.
