# Video script — Customs and Traditions of My Municipality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Nicaragua Grade 3 Estudios Sociales G3 (printed p. 75), teaches: Customs (repeated acts) vs traditions handed down orally across generations — patronal festivals, La Purísima, Palo de Mayo, El Güegüense — as the municipality's own identity.

Nicaragua Grade 2 Convivencia y Civismo G2 (printed p. 73), teaches: The community's most representative customs and traditions as shared identity.

Nicaragua Grade 5 Convivencia y Civismo G5 (printed p. 28), teaches: The traditions of one's own people.

Nicaragua Grade 6 Convivencia y Civismo G6 (printed p. 26), teaches: Why patronal and traditional festivals must be conserved.

Nicaragua Grade 6 Convivencia y Civismo G6 (printed p. 31), teaches: Nicaragua's traditional festivals and celebrations as unique heritage.

Nicaragua Grade 4 Convivencia y Civismo G4 (printed p. 39), teaches: The Pacific region's own traditions.

Nicaragua Grade 4 Convivencia y Civismo G4 (printed p. 41), teaches: The Caribbean Coast's peoples and traditions.

Nicaragua Grade 4 Convivencia y Civismo G4 (printed p. 42), teaches: The north and centre regions' traditions and customs.

Nicaragua's own way: community is taught through Rama Cay — an ancestral Caribbean island community governed by a council of elders — and citizenship through the current government's named municipal social programmes and the Caribbean Coast autonomy system. civics starts at age 7 with living-together values, then the child's own Constitution, Children's Code and national symbols, and closes on organising for the common good — the state and its duties are present from the first civics book. the upper-primary civics book teaches the state itself — Constitution, Executive and Judicial powers, the DGI tax office — and sets Nicaragua's symbols beside all six Central American neighbours'. the final primary civics book widens from Nicaragua to Central America (symbols and heroes compared across the isthmus) and from values to the machinery of rights — the Children's Convention, the CNA code, consumer rights and tax education. Grade 4 teaches cultural identity region by region (Pacific, Caribbean Coast, north and centre each with their own traditions) before the Constitution's principles and the Powers of the State.
