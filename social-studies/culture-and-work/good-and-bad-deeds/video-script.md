# Video script — Good And Bad Deeds

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Grade 1 Civic Education, printed p. 18, teaches: Deeds are good or bad in three ways — in thought (kind vs envious), in speech (truthful and gentle vs lying and gossiping) and in body (saving a life, giving, walking rightly vs killing, stealing, walking wrongly) — and how a bad deed is put right.

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 2, printed p. 15, teaches: A good deed comes from a beautiful heart — it is done sincerely, with compassion for people, animals and plants, helping without expecting anything back; good and bad deeds of thought, word and body are set side by side (kind thoughts against envy and greed, truthful speech against lies and insults, saving life against taking what was not given).

Mongolia's own way (Book 1): civic education for six-year-olds is pure Mongolian custom — greeting elders the Mongolian way, the ger and the five snouts, colour symbolism, the biyelgee dance and the Tsagaan Sar greeting embrace — with traditional script verses running down every page beside the Cyrillic.

Mongolia's own way (Book 2): Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact.
