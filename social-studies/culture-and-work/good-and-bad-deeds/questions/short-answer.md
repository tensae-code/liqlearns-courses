# Good And Bad Deeds — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Topic 7 Сайн уу, муу юу? (Good or bad?) — Deeds are good or bad in three ways — in thought (kind vs envious), in speech (truthful and gentle vs lying and gossiping) and in body (saving a life, giving, walking rightly vs killing, stealing, walking wrongly) — and how a bad deed is put right (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Topic 4 "Good and bad deeds" (Сайн ба муу үйл) — A good deed comes from a beautiful heart — it is done sincerely, with compassion for people, animals and plants, helping without expecting anything back; good and bad deeds of thought, word and body are set side by side (kind thoughts against envy and greed, truthful speech against lies and insults, saving life against taking what was not given) (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way — civic education for six-year-olds is pure Mongolian custom — greeting elders the Mongolian way, the ger and the five snouts, colour symbolism, the biyelgee dance and the Tsagaan Sar greeting embrace — with traditional script verses running down every page beside the Cyrillic. Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact (any close wording).**
