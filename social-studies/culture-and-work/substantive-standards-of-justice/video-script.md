South Korea's Grade 10 MiraeN Integrated Society, printed p. 166; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society, printed p. 175, teaches: VI.주제2 정의의 실질적 기준, VI.1-02 정의의 실질적 기준 — teaches: the substantive standards of justice — distribution by ability, need and contribution; the substantive standards of justice for fair distribution.

South Korea's own way: the high-school common subject 통합사회 (Integrated Society) is built around nine key questions asked from temporal, spatial, social and ethical perspectives at once; its civics core is Unit IV (human rights guaranteed by the constitution, law-abiding spirit and citizen participation) and Unit VI (justice and inequality), and its peace unit teaches the peaceful resolution of Korea's own division and of East Asia's history conflicts as the closing civics content. Jihaksa splits every section into two numbered lessons, so its civics teaching is the most granular of the three publishers — human rights alone gets five lessons (meaning/change, modern society, the constitution's role, law-abiding participation, problems and solutions), and its justice unit ends on the institutions and practical plans for a just society.
