# How We Become Members of Society — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: how a human being becomes a member of society — opened by two cases: Daniel, the Florida girl found in 2005 at seven unable to speak or feed herself after total neglect, and Spitz's 1945 orphanage study, where children raised without a caregiver's love fell behind in body and mind; socialization (нийгэмшилт) is the lifelong process of taking in society's experience, norms and roles — it runs in stages (the child, the adult taking new statuses, the elder, and re-socialization when life overturns one's values) and through agents: the family first (its love and its own standing shape the child's place), then the school with its formal duties, the peer group whose approval steers behaviour, the media (children aged 2–11 average 27.6 hours of television a week), and the workplace — and its product is identity (адилсал), the person's settled sense of who they are.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: True**
