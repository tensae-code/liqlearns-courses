# Herding the Large Livestock

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the large livestock (бод мал — cattle, horses, camels) are pastured by season and by kind: cattle graze out early and come home at dusk, horses are driven to distant pasture and watched by turns, camels seek the cool places; the yak (сарлаг) of the high mountains and the Mongol cow of the steppe are fed and grazed differently, and each kind's fattening depends on fitting pasture and water to its nature — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 11 "The special way of herding large livestock" (Бод мал маллах онцлог)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 34, teaches: The large livestock (бод мал — cattle, horses, camels) are pastured by season and by kind: cattle graze out early and come home at dusk, horses are driven to distant pasture and watched by turns, camels seek the cool places; the yak (сарлаг) of the high mountains and the Mongol cow of the steppe are fed and grazed differently, and each kind's fattening depends on fitting pasture and water to its nature. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
