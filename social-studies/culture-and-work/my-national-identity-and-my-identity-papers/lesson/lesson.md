# My National Identity and My Identity Papers

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: what the Emirati national identity is, made of the customs, traditions and folk heritage that form it — honouring the guest (إكرام الضيف) and the folk games (المريحانة، الغميضة، خوصة بوصة، الحبل، الصقلة), whose rules children must respect, keeping the heritage being a national duty; words of the home dialect; and the child's own identity papers in the story of Saif — the birth certificate, the family book (خلاصة القيد), the identity card and the passport, "my document for travel" — as United Arab Emirates's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — My national identity

United Arab Emirates Social Studies & National Education, G2 teacher guide, volume 2, printed p. 59, teaches: what the Emirati national identity is, made of the customs, traditions and folk heritage that form it — honouring the guest (إكرام الضيف) and the folk games (المريحانة، الغميضة، خوصة بوصة، الحبل، الصقلة), whose rules children must respect, keeping the heritage being a national duty; words of the home dialect; and the child's own identity papers in the story of Saif — the birth certificate, the family book (خلاصة القيد), the identity card and the passport, "my document for travel" This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — United Arab Emirates' way — the book's own teaching way

United Arab Emirates' own way: for seven-year-olds the state is a person first — the whole identity unit hangs on the founding builder: who Zayed was and what he built, then what "my identity" is made of (down to the child's own identity papers), then the child's share: my homeland is my responsibility. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
