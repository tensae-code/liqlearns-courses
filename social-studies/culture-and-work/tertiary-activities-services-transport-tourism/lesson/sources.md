**Acceptance count: taught in 15 countries (Ethiopia, Ukraine, Turkmenistan, Dominican Republic, Bahamas, Venezuela, Mauritania, Kuwait, Iran, Moldova, Nepal, Jordan, Nicaragua, Bhutan, Armenia). Sri Lanka is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Ukraine's own way: A "Geography: Regions and Countries" course — every region is done in the same order (position, nature/resources, population, primary/secondary/tertiary sectors) then typological country studies with rubrics (country visiting-card, capital, pride of the country, unique experience), ending with Ukraine's own place in the world; research tasks (Дослідження) embedded in chapters. That way is stocked here as the way Ukraine teaches this thing.

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 5): Three geography units climb from the planet to the country: general geography and the American continent's location, then how climate varies across the continents — with climographs the children draw and read themselves — then the Dominican Republic's own climate, activities and population, ending with a “discovering the geography of my country” field activity.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 11 G11 CS Geografía de Venezuela): a final-year economic geography of Venezuela alone — population past/present/future, human development in numbers, then resource by resource (minerals, land, fisheries, forests, oil, water/hydroelectricity) each read through sovereignty, collective participation and environmental conscience, closing on Latin American union.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 9 G9 TG English 2018) (teacher guide — evidence): competency-based guide — every unit pairs a syllabus table (competency level, content, learning outcomes, periods) with lesson-planning notes and a specimen group activity; geography is taught region-first (Asia, then Sri Lanka itself in depth), closing with hands-on 1:50,000 topographic-sheet work (Matara sheet No. 91). That evidence is stocked here as the way Sri Lanka teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 9 geo-3as-m): A home-and-region year in three units: the Mauritanian geographic space (the country set astronomically between 15°–27° N and 5°–17° W, area about 1,030,700 km²), Mauritania's economic activities, then the two spaces Mauritania belongs to — the Maghreb and West Africa — ending on sub-regional economic integration.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 10 geo-4as-m): This book re-teaches the Grade 9 programme lesson-for-lesson (its contents list is identical to Book 8's) at greater depth and with fuller map work — its Mauritania unit works through three location maps (in the Arab world, geographic and astronomical location) where the Grade 9 book works from text. Filed as its own book because it is a distinct, longer edition (116 pp vs 104 pp), not a duplicate file.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T2): Term 2 turns from the Arab homeland's nature (Term 1) to its people, its wealth, its problems and its futures, each unit fronted by a guide character — the Architect/Engineer leads Unit 2, the Crisis Observer Unit 3, the Future Pioneer Unit 4. Unit 1 (Geography) covers population in Kuwait and the Arab world; Unit 2 (Geography) resources and wealth; Unit 3 contemporary challenges — natural, political, social and economic problems, and Kuwait's own; Unit 4 visions and ambitions — national visions, Kuwait Vision 2035, future projects, the digital future and Arab integration; the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/Civics — Unit 3 Lesson 2's treatment of the Palestine question (from 1948) and of nuclear risks (weapons, radiation leaks, nuclear waste), printed pp. 54–56 — the geographic parts (Ceuta and Melilla's strategic position; the Sudan split) are placed below. Health/values — Unit 3 Lesson 3, social problems: addiction (drugs, smoking, digital) and unaware digital interaction, rumours and blind imitation, pp. 57–59. Values — Unit 3 Lesson 5's bullying section, p. 62. Civics/planning — Unit 4 Lesson 1, the concept of a national vision and the traits of a successful one, pp. 72–73; Lesson 2, Kuwait Vision 2035 (turning Kuwait into a financial and trade hub; its seven pillars) and its digital projects, pp. 74–76 — the development projects' Geography is placed below. ICT — Unit 4 Lesson 4, the digital future: digital transformation, smart government services and artificial intelligence in the Arab world (Saudi SDAIA, Jordan's Smart Desert), pp. 79–80. Civics/institutions — Unit 4 Lesson 5, Arab integration: its definition, importance and fields, and its organisations — the Arab League (Cairo), the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development (Kuwait), ALECSO (Tunis), the Arab Organization for Agricultural Development (Khartoum) — and the joint Kuwait–Oman Duqm refinery, pp. 81–82. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": a meeting of old friends in Souq Al-Mubarakiya, pp. 88–89; Nabati poetry as Kuwaiti cultural heritage (the Diwaniya of Nabati Poets), p. 90; the Kuwaiti grandmother, the beating heart of the family, pp. 92–93; the neighbour's duty — a story set in the pearl-diving season, pp. 94–96 (the heritage part is placed below).

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 11 G11 Geography): a human geography of the world year — political map and state typology, natural resources by sphere, population and settlements, then the world economy branch by branch (agriculture, each industry, services, transport, tourism), closing with economic-geographic characterisations of 14 states on every continent (Egypt among them) written to one template, plus case studies including labour migration at local/regional/global scale and using Google Maps/Google Earth as geo-information portals.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 9 Geography): the human-geography year of one's own country, written as a citizen's handbook — it opens with the Constitution's own words on territory, weighs the advantages and disadvantages of the country's economic-geographic position (including EU candidate status, June 2022), and treats the wine industry as national patrimony with roots in the Cucuteni-Tripolie civilisation; every theme carries "current problems", case studies and debates about the pupils' own locality, and the two practical works study the home locality itself (its population; its tourist sights).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 6 np-g6): Grade 6 has a full "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth) unit — Earth's introduction, latitude/longitude, outer-layer structure, then Nepal itself: geographical description, climate, rivers/glaciers/lakes, natural vegetation, natural resources and map study, closing with disaster management; a population unit follows (demography, the census). Economic activities are organised by region and resource: agriculture and industry, services and trade, tourism, and economic resources (land and water; forest, minerals and people).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G5 SS T2): Term 2 teaches the economy by sectors, then location skills (relative/absolute location, GPS) and Jordan's Arab world region by region — Nile Valley and Horn of Africa, then the Maghreb.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 Geography T2): the final Geography term is climate (atmosphere, elements, classifications, change), environment (components, problems, protection, Jordanian ecotourism), and economic geography (production, agriculture, industry, services, development).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 5 Social Studies): Grade 5 spirals from the Earth and lat/long straight into Bhutan itself — its location, three physical zones, neighbours, major rivers and protected areas — so world geography is anchored in the home country's map first.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 Geography student): opens school geography with the geographic map itself (plan, scale, azimuth, projections) before any Earth science, and runs every theme to a summary lesson plus practical work; world economy is taught branch by branch with Armenia (ՀՀ) used as the worked example in the closing questions.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Geography student): the senior Geography course is two big themes only — the world economy in depth (structure, division of labour, every branch, transport systems, trade) and then the environment (ecological balance, pollution of air and water, land degradation, biodiversity, ecosystem services), i.e. economy first, its ecological bill second.
