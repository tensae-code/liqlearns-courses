## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 10 "The matchless wonder — the morin khuur" (Хосгүй гайхамшиг — Морин хуур)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 2, printed p. 33, teaches: The horse-head fiddle was born from the people's love of the horse — its legend tells of a man who carved his dead horse's head and made its hair sing; its melodies picture the horse's gaits and the life of the homeland, it has its own respectful way of being held (татлага), the master player G. Jamyan began its teaching, and since 1992 the Morin Khuur Ensemble has played it to the world. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
