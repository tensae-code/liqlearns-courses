# Law and Morality

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the comparison of legal rules (written, set by the state, governing outward behaviour, for order and stability) with moral rules (unwritten values that refine conduct), and law's character — general and equal: the speed-limit law applies to every driver and every violator is held to account without exception, with speed cameras ensuring the law is carried out justly — as Kuwait's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Legal rules and moral rules

Kuwait Grade 8 Term 1 (printed pp. 91–96), teaches: The comparison of legal rules (written, set by the state, governing outward behaviour, for order and stability) with moral rules (unwritten values that refine conduct), and law's character — general and equal: the speed-limit law applies to every driver and every violator is held to account without exception, with speed cameras ensuring the law is carried out justly. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kuwait's way — the book's own teaching way

Kuwait's own way: amid a book about the Islamic world, Kuwait teaches law abstractly and concretely at once — what law is, why a society without it falls into disorder, how legal rules differ from moral rules, and the speed camera on a Kuwaiti road as the picture of a law that applies to everyone without exception. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
