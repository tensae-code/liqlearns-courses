# Lesson plan — Democracys Values And Mongolias Constitution

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Democracys Values And Mongolias Constitution
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Mongolia, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Democracys Values And Mongolias Constitution — Democracy from its first Mongolian morning — the Democratic Union's first rally on Human Rights Day, 10 December 1989, and the petition it carried to the party's Central Committee and the People's Great Khural (end one-party direction of the state; citizens' inviolable rights placed above the party; a permanent parliament; national questions settled by referendum); what democracy is (demos + kratos — the people's power), in its direct and representative forms; its five principles — openness, justice, the rule of law, respect for the minority (the minority is not a negligible thing: its rights are protected by law), and consensus; its values drawn as one diagram — human rights, citizen participation, equal rights, accountability and transparency, the multi-party system, oversight of power, the rule of law, education, and regular, free, fair elections; and the two constitutions side by side — 1960's articles against 1992's Article 16, so pupils can see exactly what changed — as Mongolia's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
