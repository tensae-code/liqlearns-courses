Mongolia Civic Ethics Grade 6, printed p. 43, teaches: Criticism (шүүмжлэл) is a teaching word with a purpose — "do not praise, do not flatter" runs the old saying, for flattery hides faults while criticism, heard with patience, reveals them; the six ways of listening (to the end, without looking away, without doing something else, calmly, respectfully, attending to the meaning); the story of Galdan, whose father answered the boy's every complaint until the boy understood the teaching inside the harshness; and answering a friend's mocking, teasing words (шоглож хэлсэн үг) without anger — hearing the affection inside them and never letting them wound the friendship.

Mongolia's own way (Link book L2): from Grade 6 the civics subject changes its name and its method — no longer "Civic Education" but "Civic Ethics" (Иргэний ёс зүйн боловсрол), a self-observation workbook in the Buddhist-inflected Mongolian moral tradition: the pupil keeps a diary of their own acts of body, speech and mind, and ethics is practised as a daily discipline before it is ever a rule about others.
