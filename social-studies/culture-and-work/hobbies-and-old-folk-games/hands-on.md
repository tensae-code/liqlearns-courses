# Hands-on — Hobbies and Old Folk Games

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: a hobby as an activity or interest a person practises to meet his own needs and develop his skills and abilities; the controls of practising it (as Ahmed swims at the club under a coach, twice at the weekend for an hour) and its benefits (discharging energy usefully, building an independent character, raising school achievement); the fields of hobbies — innovation (inventing devices), creativity (drawing, writing plays), competition (football), learning (reading) and collecting (old artefacts, pictures and stamps) — with the Ministry of Education's Neighbourhood Clubs initiative inside public schools; and the old folk games that strengthened social relations and spread affection among friends and neighbours (Umm Tisa', a thinking game of planning; al-Khatta, a movement game of jumping on squares). Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

Say how Saudi Arabia's book teaches this thing, in your own words, from the lesson.
