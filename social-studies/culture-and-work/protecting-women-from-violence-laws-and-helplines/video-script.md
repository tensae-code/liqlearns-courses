# Video script — Protecting Women from Violence Laws and Helplines

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Pakistan's Grade 10 PS10 (printed pp. 164–166); Grade 9 PS9 (printed pp. 102–105), teaches: Ch. 8 Violence Against Women: Definition, Constitutional Provisions and Government Efforts, Ch. 4 Violence Against Women: Myths and Facts, Laws and Government Efforts — teaches: what counts as violence, what the constitution promises, and the machinery built to protect — the Punjab Marriage Restraint Act 2015, the Punjab Protection of Women Against Violence Act 2016, district protection officers and committees, and the 1043 helpline; CEDAW is named; the myths are answered one by one (violence is only the offender's fault; it happens inside homes too), then the machinery — Punjab Marriage Restraint Act 2015, Protection of Women Against Violence Act 2016, district Violence Against Women Centres, DWPO rescue teams, helpline 1043 and SMS 8787 — and a "Do you know?" box on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and CEDAW.

Pakistan's own way: at Class 10 the state tells its story whole — ideology first, then the society it made: thirteen named features of Pakistani culture, minorities' rights written into the culture chapter, and a full closing chapter on women's rights, women nation-builders and the laws that protect them. the English-medium Class 9 book states the ideology in plain definitions, quotes Jinnah's promise that minorities "are free to go to your places of worship", and closes with the women's chapter in its sharpest form — myth-versus-fact boxes that refuse to blame victims.
