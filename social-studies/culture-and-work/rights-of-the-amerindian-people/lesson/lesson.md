# Rights of the Amerindian People

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the Amerindians were the first people of Guyana and the land belonged to them; today their land rights are disregarded by some miners and loggers, the government has made laws to protect their land and way of life, and Amerindian communities have the right to decide how the resources of their land are used — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rights of the Amerindians

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 185–186), teaches: The Amerindians were the first people of Guyana and the land belonged to them; today their land rights are disregarded by some miners and loggers, the government has made laws to protect their land and way of life, and Amerindian communities have the right to decide how the resources of their land are used. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
