**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Cambodia).**

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 7 Social Studies G7): lower-secondary civics opens with gender and human rights before friendship and family, and its community chapter is a full election course in miniature — voting, class representatives, candidacy, taking part, and the qualities of the elected. That way is stocked here as the way Cambodia teaches this thing.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 8 Social Studies G8): Grade 8 civics is organised as widening circles of relationship — others, family, community — and its community chapter teaches Cambodia's real elections: commune–sangkat councils first, then the National Assembly. That way is stocked here as the way Cambodia teaches this thing.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 10 Social Studies G10): the civics course ends at the state itself — rule of law, the Kingdom's own Constitution (taught through its 1947, 1972, 1975 and 1993 texts), its top institutions, and the citizen's rights, duties, voice and self-protection; its culture-of-peace chapter carries two distinctively Khmer lessons, ancient temple architecture as national heritage and the matriarchal root of Khmer society. That way is stocked here as the way Cambodia teaches this thing.
