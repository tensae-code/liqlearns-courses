Cambodia's own way: lower-secondary civics opens with gender and human rights before friendship and family, and its community chapter is a full election course in miniature — voting, class representatives, candidacy, taking part, and the qualities of the elected. Grade 8 civics is organised as widening circles of relationship — others, family, community — and its community chapter teaches Cambodia's real elections: commune–sangkat councils first, then the National Assembly. the civics course ends at the state itself — rule of law, the Kingdom's own Constitution (taught through its 1947, 1972, 1975 and 1993 texts), its top institutions, and the citizen's rights, duties, voice and self-protection; its culture-of-peace chapter carries two distinctively Khmer lessons, ancient temple architecture as national heritage and the matriarchal root of Khmer society.
