## Sub lesson 1 — Democracy

Tanzania Standard Six tz-civic6-en (printed p. 30), teaches: How democracy is practised in Tanzania — freedom of expression, free and fair elections managed under law (electing the President, MPs and local leaders), multiparty politics since 1992, freedom of assembly and peaceful demonstration, participation, transparency and accountability — plus the qualities of a national leader and the principles and importance of good governance. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Democracy — Government from the People

Tanzania Standard Five tz-civic5-en (printed p. 139), teaches: Democracy is government that draws legitimacy from the people and works for the people, deciding by the majority (voters are citizens of 18 years and above); it guarantees human rights, truth and transparency, freedoms of expression, worship, association and movement, equality before the law, responsible leaders, and power divided among judiciary, legislature and executive; political parties educate citizens, criticise the government, contest elections and must maintain peace, and political tolerance — respecting others' opinions and accepting election results — brings peace, harmony and stability; the 1977 constitution's first chapter sets out basic rights and duties. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tanzania's way — the books' own teaching way

Tanzania's own way (Standard Six, Civic and Moral Education Standard Six Pupil's Book): TIE teaches civics as a good-governance wheel (the cover itself: transparency, accountability, participation, human rights, integrity, efficiency, reconciliation, democracy, equality, rule of law around “GOOD GOVERNANCE”) and builds the state from the child's own levels — street, village, ward, district, region, nation — with the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar as the founding fact and Kiswahili, unity and humanism (utu) as named national treasures. Tanzania's own way (Standard Five, Civic and Moral Education Standard Five Pupil's Book): Standard Five civics teaches the state from the ward up — Ward Development Committee, district council and its heads of departments, regional commissioner — and the nation through its symbols in detail (the emblem's thirteen signs, the shilling, the Uhuru torch first lit 9 December 1961, national festivals from Zanzibar Revolution Day to Farmers' Day); democracy is taught with political parties' duties and political tolerance, and peace as peaceful coexistence that condemns segregation on any ground. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
