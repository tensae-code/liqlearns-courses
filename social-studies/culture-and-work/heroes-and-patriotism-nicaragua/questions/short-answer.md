**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Los héroes y las heroínas nos dieron grandes ejemplos de patriotismo — Nicaragua's heroes and heroines as examples of patriotism (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Recordando a nuestros héroes y heroínas — Remembering Nicaragua's heroes and heroines (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Héroes y Próceres Dignos de Imitar — Heroes and founding figures worthy of imitation (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Próceres y forjadores de mi patria — The founding figures and makers of the homeland (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Nicaragua's way — the books' own teaching way — civics starts at age 7 with living-together values, then the child's own Constitution, Children's Code and national symbols, and closes on organising for the common good — the state and its duties are present from the first civics book. Grade 3 civics adds Nicaragua-specific law to the values base — women's rights under Ley 779 and a Mother Earth declaration — and respect for public and private institutions as a civic duty. the upper-primary civics book teaches the state itself — Constitution, Executive and Judicial powers, the DGI tax office — and sets Nicaragua's symbols beside all six Central American neighbours'. Grade 4 teaches cultural identity region by region (Pacific, Caribbean Coast, north and centre each with their own traditions) before the Constitution's principles and the Powers of the State. (any close wording).**
