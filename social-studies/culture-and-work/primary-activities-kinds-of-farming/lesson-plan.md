# Lesson plan — Primary activities — kinds of farming

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Primary activities — kinds of farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 9 — Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md ladder: culture, economic activities, trade, and tax).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the three sortings of farming — by product, by purpose, and by input — and how to place any farm inside them
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title, at Grade 8 depth in the course's level ladder (levels, not grades).
A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **By product, and by purpose** — By product: crop farming, livestock farming and mixed farming.…
2. **By input — extensive and intensive** — By capital, labour and land used: extensive farming uses much land and little input; intensive farming uses little land and much money and labour. The same farm…

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Primary activities — agriculture (in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort farming by product, by purpose and by input.
2. Tell extensive from intensive farming.
3. Place a real local farm in all three sortings.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
