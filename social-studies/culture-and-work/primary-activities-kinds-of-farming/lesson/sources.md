Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — and Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P2): the settlement story is told as a ladder — farming, animal domestication, the agricultural village, the Palestinian village of today, then the city located on the map — and the year closes with the road itself: what a road is, its parts, and how to cross it safely.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Geography): A full senior Geography course built on the Pacific itself — high and low island formation, island climates and ecosystems, then population of New Zealand, Asia and Samoa, and Samoan farming (bananas) beside Australian uranium mining.

Eritrea's own way (ER G8 Social Studies): Grade 8 is the Eritrea-physical-geography year — relief, hydrology and water shortage, people-environment adaptation and Eritrea's strategic Red Sea location are taught as four full Geography units before the History units begin.

India's own way (Grade 8 Resources and Development): Grade 8 is a resources year, world-first but India-anchored throughout: resources and their conservation, then land/soil/water/vegetation, minerals and power, agriculture, industries and human resources — every world pattern is paired with its Indian distribution map and an Indian case study (Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad textiles, Bangalore IT, a farm in India set against a farm in the USA).

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 7 Social Studies): from Grade 7 the combined Social Studies book splits into four named subject sections; its Geography section teaches people-in-environments — hot zones, deserts and oases, polar peoples, climate zones — through world case studies (Kenya farmers, Amazon, Inuit, Sahara oil) before turning to world population.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 10 Social Studies): at upper-secondary the Geography section becomes thematic world geography — industrial location, world agriculture, the Asia-Pacific newly industrialised countries, population growth and urbanisation — each chapter a single long study with maps and photographs.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Years 11–12 Geography): Senior geography splits into Human and Physical volumes; the human volume is organised as four issue-chapters (population/settlement, tourism, agriculture, marine resources), each run the same way — nature, problems, ways of minimising, contemporary issues, future — with Pacific/Fiji theme-country cases.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 8 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the local-to-global ladder completes — after province (G5), nation (G7), Grade 8's Environment and Resources strand is global: global climate, global settlement and population, global farming, urbanization and logging impacts, global hazards and disaster resilience. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 10 UG S3 Geography Home-Study): COVID-era self-study booklet: first half is East Africa's human geography (towns/ports, transport, trade) plus heavy practical map-work and photograph interpretation on Ugandan survey-map extracts (Tororo, Kakuto, Samia); second half is a "studies in development" comparison — the Rhinelands and landlocked Switzerland held up as models against East Africa.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 9 SD Geography 9): A single-country book: the whole of Sudan taught in three units — its physical geography, its human geography, then its environmental problems — with map-work activities on Sudan's own maps (states, roads, the Nile) and an infographic "resources of Sudan" spread; the preface warns its statistics change and are only indicators.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 6 SD Geography 6): A whole-Sudan book in three units — its natural environments (desert to rich savanna, plus the Red Sea coast), its productive resources taught environment by environment (farming, herding with the transhumance year of the Kabbabish and Kawahla, industry, mining), then its human geography: population, public services and its important cities one by one.
