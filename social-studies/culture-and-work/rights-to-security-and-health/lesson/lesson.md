# Rights to Security and Health

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: security as one of the most important human rights, stated in the Convention on the Rights of the Child and widened by the 1994 Human Development Report (food, health, environmental, economic and personal security); personal security is every human's right and attacking it is a crime the law punishes — as Mauritania's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Right to Security

Mauritania Grade 4 Civic Education SB (printed p. 27), teaches: Security as one of the most important human rights, stated in the Convention on the Rights of the Child and widened by the 1994 Human Development Report (food, health, environmental, economic and personal security); personal security is every human's right and attacking it is a crime the law punishes. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Right to Health

Mauritania Grade 4 Civic Education SB (printed p. 29), teaches: Health among the rights in the child-rights convention and the world's constitutions; the right means prevention first, then treatment in good time, with health-insurance and solidarity funds carrying the costs. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mauritania's way — the book's own teaching way

Mauritania's own way: Year 4 is the commune — the whole first half walks the municipality's own law (Law 87/289), its mayor, councillors and council, and what it owes the school, the playground and the health centre; the second half turns to identity, belonging, two rights, and the behaviour values This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
