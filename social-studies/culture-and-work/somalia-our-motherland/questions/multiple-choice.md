# Somalia Our Motherland — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The part of this lesson called "Soomaaliya Dalkayaga Hooyo (Somalia, Our Mother Country)" is mainly about —
a) loving the motherland as a duty learned young — the country's rivers, seas, valleys, plains, mountains, farms, great cities, tall buildings, historic places, roads, gardens and wild animals are named so the children grow to love, unite around and work for their country and its flag  b) ancient kings only  c) the Moon's phases
**Answer: a) loving the motherland as a duty learned young — the country's rivers, seas, valleys, plains, mountains, farms, great cities, tall buildings, historic places, roads, gardens and wild animals are named so the children grow to love, unite around and work for their country and its flag**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** The part of this lesson called "Dalkeennu waa Soomaaliya (Our Country Is Somalia)" is mainly about —
a) the homeland lesson retold for second-graders — Somalia as the one country all its children share  b) ancient kings only  c) the Moon's phases
**Answer: a) the homeland lesson retold for second-graders — Somalia as the one country all its children share**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** The part of this lesson called "Somalia's way — the books' own teaching way" is mainly about —
a) this subject was created by the 1969 Revolution to form the new Somali person — personality (qofnimo) is built, not born: family, food, dress, schooling and society shape it, and the school's job is to graft onto the child Somali-ness, straight morals, a living conscience, patriotism, responsibility and tolerance, in that order, from the flag outward to the society the pupil lives in.  b) ancient kings only  c) the Moon's phases
**Answer: a) this subject was created by the 1969 Revolution to form the new Somali person — personality (qofnimo) is built, not born: family, food, dress, schooling and society shape it, and the school's job is to graft onto the child Somali-ness, straight morals, a living conscience, patriotism, responsibility and tolerance, in that order, from the flag outward to the society the pupil lives in.**
