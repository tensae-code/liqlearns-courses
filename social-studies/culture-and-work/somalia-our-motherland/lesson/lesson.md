# Somalia Our Motherland

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: loving the motherland as a duty learned young — the country's rivers, seas, valleys, plains, mountains, farms, great cities, tall buildings, historic places, roads, gardens and wild animals are named so the children grow to love, unite around and work for their country and its flag — as Somalia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Soomaaliya Dalkayaga Hooyo (Somalia, Our Mother Country)

Somalia Grade 1 Barbaarinta iyo Cilmiga Beesha (Teacher Guide) (printed p. 14), teaches: loving the motherland as a duty learned young — the country's rivers, seas, valleys, plains, mountains, farms, great cities, tall buildings, historic places, roads, gardens and wild animals are named so the children grow to love, unite around and work for their country and its flag. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dalkeennu waa Soomaaliya (Our Country Is Somalia)

Somalia Grade 2 Barbaarinta iyo Cilmiga Beesha (printed p. 1), teaches: the homeland lesson retold for second-graders — Somalia as the one country all its children share. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Somalia's way — the books' own teaching way

Somalia's own way (Grade 1): this subject was created by the 1969 Revolution to form the new Somali person — personality (qofnimo) is built, not born: family, food, dress, schooling and society shape it, and the school's job is to graft onto the child Somali-ness, straight morals, a living conscience, patriotism, responsibility and tolerance, in that order, from the flag outward to the society the pupil lives in. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Somalia's own way (Grade 2): Grade 2 teaches the same civic world through people, not topics — a mother, a father, a fisherman-hero — and through the child's own uniformed corps, the October Revolution Flowers, whose members are known by cleanliness, honesty, bravery and love of the flag. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
