# Society Structure Classes Groups and Mobility — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: The social portrait of Belarus (Социальный портрет Беларуси) — Grade 11 — sociology as the science that "paints" a society's portrait; the social composition of a country — groups by place of residence (about 74% urban), ethnic groups (130 nationalities: Belarusians about 81%, Russians 11%, Poles 4%, Ukrainians 2%), 25 registered confessions, and groups by property, profession, income and education, with the structure shifting as in other developed industrial states (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: The social structure of society (Социальная структура общества) — Grade 10 — society as self-organised groups with common interests and values; the horizontal structure (demographic, ethnic, confessional, professional and kin groups — one person pictured as woman, Belarusian, agro-town resident at once) and the vertical structure, where groups differ by property and income, power, education and prestige — classes as the large groups of the class approach (Marx), with stratification by income, power, education and prestige (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way — (Grade 11) the whole Grade 11 course is the state describing itself — the 1994 Constitution and its 1996/2004 referendums, the President as guarantor of the Constitution, a two-chamber National Assembly and elected local Soviets of deputies under appointed executive committees, and two state languages — so its civics is placed as Belarus's own constitutional order, not as generic civics. (Grade 10) the Grade 10 course teaches society and politics in general concepts, but anchors them at home — the Statute-era separation of powers on Belarusian lands, Article 36's freedom of association and Article 49's right to education quoted from its own Constitution, and Belarusian parties and public associations as the living examples of the political system. (any close wording).**
