# Community Service Organisations

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: community service organisations — water boards, communal patronatos, cooperatives — and national ones — the Fire Corps, the Red Cross, health centres and hospitals, schools — each with its own functions (social, rescue, health, education) that together improve the community's social, economic, political and cultural situation and strengthen democracy and participation — as Honduras's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 30 Honduras y sus formas de organización

Honduras Grade 3 G3 Student CCSS, printed p. 138, teaches: Community service organisations — water boards, communal patronatos, cooperatives — and national ones — the Fire Corps, the Red Cross, health centres and hospitals, schools — each with its own functions (social, rescue, health, education) that together improve the community's social, economic, political and cultural situation and strengthen democracy and participation. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 31 Organizaciones sociales de Honduras

Honduras Grade 4 G4 Student CCSS, printed p. 171, teaches: The family is the first organisation we belong to — by the Universal Declaration the natural and fundamental unit of society, protected by society and the State; Honduras's social organisations defend human rights (COFADEH, CODEH, CIPRODEH), health (children's-cancer and hospital foundations), labour rights (unions such as STIBYS and STENEE) and peasant causes, doing voluntary work that links civil society with the State in solving the country's social problems. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way

Honduras's own way: Grade 3 is where the state appears in full — nationality by birth and registration, the Constitution as supreme law, the three powers with their real Honduran numbers (128 deputies, 15 magistrates, elections every four years), and citizens' social demands as claims the State is obliged to hear. Grade 4 widens civics to Central America and to rights as law — human rights with the Constitution's own Article 60 and the habeas corpus and amparo remedies, sex versus gender taught for equality, and Honduras's real civic organisations (CODEH, COFADEH, the unions) named as civil society. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
