# I Am a Person My Name and My Qualities

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: who I am — my name, my family, my birth certificate, my talents and qualities, and how each person is distinct — as Libya's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — I Am a Human Being

Libya Grade 4 National Education G4 (printed p. 8), teaches: who I am — my name, my family, my birth certificate, my talents and qualities; people share some traits and differ in others, and each person is distinct. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Libya's way — the book's own teaching way

Libya's own way: the whole book is written in the first person — every lesson is "I and …" (I and my family, I and my school, I and the neighbourhood) — citizenship starts as the child's own relationships, and even the environment unit is framed as the child's own behaviour: what I throw away, what I plant, which beach I clean. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
