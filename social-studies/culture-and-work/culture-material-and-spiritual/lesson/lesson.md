# Culture — material and spiritual culture

## Do it first

List your community's material culture: tools, building goods, foods, clothing. Aim for ten items.

## Sub lesson 1 — Material culture — seen and touched

Culture divides in two. **Material culture: all the tangible things people have made and use through history** — farm tools, house-building goods and methods, food goods, clothing kinds — **seen by the eye and touched by the hand**.

[Picture 11503 — two boxes: tools and dress in one, music and mourning in the other]

## Sub lesson 2 — Spiritual culture — held in the mind

**Spiritual (non-material) culture: untouchable wealth that shows through people's acts and expressions** — **music, mourning, religion** — **held in the mind, not the hand**. A grief or a joy is spiritual culture, not material — you cannot pick it up, but a whole community can share it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Sort any cultural thing into material or spiritual, and say why. Name the book's material examples and its spiritual examples.

## Do It Yourself

1. List your community's material culture: tools, building goods, foods, clothing. Aim for ten items.
2. With partners, stage one local ceremony as a short drama — a wedding, mourning, reconciliation or festival dress — and name its spiritual parts aloud.
