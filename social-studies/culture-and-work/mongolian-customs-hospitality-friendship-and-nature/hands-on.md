# Hands-on — Mongolian Customs Hospitality Friendship And Nature

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Topic 3 "The custom of friendship" (Нөхөрлөх ёс)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: True friendship through Temüjin and Bo'orchu, who shared danger and refused reward, and through the tale of the dog, cat and mouse, whose friendship envy and stealing destroyed; my own friend, how we help each other, and how saying sorry keeps a friendship. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Topic 10 "From the custom of protecting nature" (Байгалиа хамгаалах ёсноос)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The world is two worlds — the "container world" (сав ертөнц: earth, water, air, space) and the "nutrient world" (шим ертөнц: people, animals, plants) held inside it, so harming one harms the other; the state itself worships great mountains (төрийн тахилга) and at the ovoo ceremony asks the mountain and water spirits for rain and mercy; the hunter's ten taboos (never take a herd whole, never more than need, never a mother with young, never an animal newly come to the land); and the sacred удган мод tree that may never be cut. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Topic 1 "The custom of visiting" (Зочлох ёс)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Every country has its own custom of visiting; the guest is honoured from the first greeting at the ger door through being seated, served and seen off; the taboos for a guest's arrival — do not peer through the door felt, do not enter in wet or open clothing, do not take a knife or ladle in hand — and children do not join the grown-ups' talk. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Topic 2 "The custom of hosting" (Зочлуулах ёс)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Whoever comes, the custom of receiving is kept strictly — the visitor calls "Hold the dog!" (Нохой хорио) and waits to be invited in from the horse; the host seats the guest in the place of honour, hears the purpose of the visit, and answers the household's work with the matching blessing — at milking, at tanning, at churning airag; the guest's own taboos: never ride up noisily to the door, never step over tether, whip or lasso, never draw your knife from its sash as you enter. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Topic 6 "The Mongol custom of moving" (Монгол нүүдэллэх ёс)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The nomads' moving (нүүдэл) is a whole custom of care: the household head first goes to "see the land" (нутаг үзэх) and consults its people; the old camp place is cleaned and its peg holes filled before leaving, and the first of the milk is offered to the nature of the place that fed them; the ger comes down in order, the crown wheel loaded last, with the taboos of the road — "don't let the load tilt" (ачаа хазайна), "the camel strains" (тэмээ туйлna); on the road the horses are driven first, elders and children follow the loads, and the host rides last; passing travellers are greeted and exchange news; at the new place the three hearth stones are honoured and the ger's door set to the south-east. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Topic 11 "Our locality's customs" (Манай нутгийн ёс)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Every locality (нутаг) has its own treasured customs, kept in its language, dress, songs, games and festivals and handed down by its elders; the peoples of Mongolia are mapped in their aimags — Khalkh, Zakhchin, Torguud, Kazakh, Ööld, Uriankhai, Myangad, Khoton, Dörvöd, Bayad, Tsaatan, Darkhad, Buriad, Barga, Üzemchin, Dariganga — each with its own dress; and the locality's monuments are gathered into a little book — the Khoit Tsenkher cave paintings, the Chinggis statue at Dadal, Erdene Zuu, the Tsogt Tsagaan Baishin ruins, Khamar monastery — with a visit to the local museum. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
