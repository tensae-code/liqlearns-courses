## Sub lesson 2 — Topic 10 "From the custom of protecting nature" (Байгалиа хамгаалах ёсноос)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 35, teaches: The world is two worlds — the "container world" (сав ертөнц: earth, water, air, space) and the "nutrient world" (шим ертөнц: people, animals, plants) held inside it, so harming one harms the other; the state itself worships great mountains (төрийн тахилга) and at the ovoo ceremony asks the mountain and water spirits for rain and mercy; the hunter's ten taboos (never take a herd whole, never more than need, never a mother with young, never an animal newly come to the land); and the sacred удган мод tree that may never be cut. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 1 "The custom of visiting" (Зочлох ёс)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 5, teaches: Every country has its own custom of visiting; the guest is honoured from the first greeting at the ger door through being seated, served and seen off; the taboos for a guest's arrival — do not peer through the door felt, do not enter in wet or open clothing, do not take a knife or ladle in hand — and children do not join the grown-ups' talk. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Topic 2 "The custom of hosting" (Зочлуулах ёс)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 7, teaches: Whoever comes, the custom of receiving is kept strictly — the visitor calls "Hold the dog!" (Нохой хорио) and waits to be invited in from the horse; the host seats the guest in the place of honour, hears the purpose of the visit, and answers the household's work with the matching blessing — at milking, at tanning, at churning airag; the guest's own taboos: never ride up noisily to the door, never step over tether, whip or lasso, never draw your knife from its sash as you enter. ## Sub lesson 5 — Topic 6 "The Mongol custom of moving" (Монгол нүүдэллэх ёс)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 23, teaches: The nomads' moving (нүүдэл) is a whole custom of care: the household head first goes to "see the land" (нутаг үзэх) and consults its people; the old camp place is cleaned and its peg holes filled before leaving, and the first of the milk is offered to the nature of the place that fed them; the ger comes down in order, the crown wheel loaded last, with the taboos of the road — "don't let the load tilt" (ачаа хазайна), "the camel strains" (тэмээ туйлna); on the road the horses are driven first, elders and children follow the loads, and the host rides last; passing travellers are greeted and exchange news; at the new place the three hearth stones are honoured and the ger's door set to the south-east. ## Sub lesson 6 — Topic 11 "Our locality's customs" (Манай нутгийн ёс)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 41, teaches: Every locality (нутаг) has its own treasured customs, kept in its language, dress, songs, games and festivals and handed down by its elders; the peoples of Mongolia are mapped in their aimags — Khalkh, Zakhchin, Torguud, Kazakh, Ööld, Uriankhai, Myangad, Khoton, Dörvöd, Bayad, Tsaatan, Darkhad, Buriad, Barga, Üzemchin, Dariganga — each with its own dress; and the locality's monuments are gathered into a little book — the Khoit Tsenkher cave paintings, the Chinggis statue at Dadal, Erdene Zuu, the Tsogt Tsagaan Baishin ruins, Khamar monastery — with a visit to the local museum. ## Sub lesson 7 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact. Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other. Grade 3 turns from the family to the whole moral order — deeds and their fruits, the moving camp, the spirits of land and water, the state's banner and emblem — and ends with the child's first political ideas: deciding together, equal rights, and a government the citizens elect and pay for. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
