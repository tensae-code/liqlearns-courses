Iran's book, the *Iran Grade 11 Geography (2)* (printed p. 137), teaches: Lesson 10: The capital (پایتخت) — usually becoming the country's biggest and most important city or metropolis; the factors in choosing a capital site; capitals moved from one city to another (Rio de Janeiro to Brasília in Brazil, Karachi to Islamabad in Pakistan); and the patterns of political management of space — in the federal pattern the centre coordinates the states, which follow it in defence, foreign policy and the national currency.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 11 Geography (2)): the whole book is built on one organising idea — the region (ناحیه). Four chapters divide the world into regions four ways: what a region is, natural regions (climate, landforms, living, coasts, mountains — with Nepal as the highland case study), human regions (cultural, economic), and political regions (the state, borders, geopolitics — with the Persian Gulf and the European Union as case studies).
