**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Indonesia).** Indonesia's teacher-guide parts in this lesson are teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere — Indonesia counts through its student-book parts.

Indonesia's own way (Book 1): IPAS Grade 4 teaches community through kearifan lokal (local wisdom) — pupils interview classmates and teachers about habits handed down in their families (jamu drinking, speaking Batak at home) before any national culture is named.

Indonesia's own way (Book 2): the guide's stated aim for Bab IV is unity — pupils dig out the noble values (nilai luhur) inside their own locality's traditions and then apply local-wisdom values to keep persatuan dan kesatuan (unity and oneness) amid diversity.

Indonesia's own way (Book 3): the guide's Bab VI essential material states roles, rules and responsibilities as one civic package across three environments — home first, then school, then masyarakat (society).

Indonesia's own way (Book 4): the primary IPAS course ends its social strand on the state motto itself — Bhinneka Tunggal Ika (unity in diversity) — taught through two discussion cases: a migrant child mocked for her accent, and a grandmother's maghrib prohibition weighed against a lit modern field.
