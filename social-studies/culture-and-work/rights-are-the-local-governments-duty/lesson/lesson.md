# Rights Are the Local Governments Duty

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: citizen and state are the two ends of one relation — the person is the rights-holder (эрх эдлэгч) and the state the duty-bearer (үүрэг хүлээгч), because rights exist from birth while the state alone can be made to answer for them; the national rights system has three parts (state bodies, the national human-rights institution, civil society), and the state's four duties toward every right are to respect it, to provide/fulfil it (schools and hospitals), to protect it from others' violation, and to promote progress in it; at the local level the administration delivers those duties as services — and answers for failures, as in the case of the 14-year-old whose asthma worsened in Ulaanbaatar's smoke while the city's air stayed far above the permitted pollution level — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Section 4.5 "Providing human rights is the local administration's duty"

Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies, printed p. 76, teaches: Citizen and state are the two ends of one relation — the person is the rights-holder (эрх эдлэгч) and the state the duty-bearer (үүрэг хүлээгч), because rights exist from birth while the state alone can be made to answer for them; the national rights system has three parts (state bodies, the national human-rights institution, civil society), and the state's four duties toward every right are to respect it, to provide/fulfil it (schools and hospitals), to protect it from others' violation, and to promote progress in it; at the local level the administration delivers those duties as services — and answers for failures, as in the case of the 14-year-old whose asthma worsened in Ulaanbaatar's smoke while the city's air stayed far above the permitted pollution level. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
