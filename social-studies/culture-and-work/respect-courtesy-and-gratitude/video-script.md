# Video script — Respect Courtesy and Gratitude

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Student Workbook 2017, printed pp. 1–9, teaches: courtesy learned as a poem and a checklist — "Good Manners" (we say thank you, we say please, we don't interrupt or tease, we listen when people talk to us) and ten courteous behaviours the child ticks as their own, sealed by an "I am Sorry!" card drawn for someone they were rude to; and respect itself — treating parents, teachers, friends, grandparents and guests the way you like to be treated; and Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Teacher's Handbook 2017, printed pp. 1–10, teaches: TEACHER: respect as polite speech and behaviour that treats self and others with humanity and dignity regardless of gender or standing — practised as courtesy ("polite words, kind ways": thank you, please, sorry, excuse me, listening without interrupting) and as valuing people for what each contributes — parents who care, teachers who teach, the cleaner who keeps the school clean; learn respect to earn respect.

Bhutan's own way: the workbook turns each value into a week of doing — a sorry-card actually drawn and given, a day planned to the minute, kind acts collected as leaves on a classroom Kindness Tree until they earn a fruit, and obedience sealed with written pledges to teacher and parents; the values are Bhutan's own (Tha-Dam-Tse Ley-Judy, Tsawa Sum) carried in stories the whole world tells (the Giving Tree, Little Red Riding Hood, the Itsy Bitsy Spider).
