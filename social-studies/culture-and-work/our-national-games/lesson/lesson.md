# Our National Games

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: games are the people's wisdom at play, fitted around work and never played idly for long ("a game is good up to the top of the head" is the warning); stone games, in which children build a whole model camp — ger walls, door, herd — from chosen stones; the 80-plus shagai (anklebone) games, the four sides of the bone named horse, camel, sheep and goat; and the word games, ending in дайралцаан, the sung verbal duel accompanied by the morin khuur — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 12 (games) "From the customs of Mongolian national games" (Монгол үндэсний тоглоом наадмын ёсноос)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 44, teaches: Games are the people's wisdom at play, fitted around work and never played idly for long ("a game is good up to the top of the head" is the warning); stone games, in which children build a whole model camp — ger walls, door, herd — from chosen stones; the 80-plus shagai (anklebone) games, the four sides of the bone named horse, camel, sheep and goat; and the word games, ending in дайралцаан, the sung verbal duel accompanied by the morin khuur. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
