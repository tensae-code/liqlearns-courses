## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 9: Roles of village heads, headmen, chiefs and councillors

Zimbabwe, *PlusOne Social Science Grade 5 Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 29), teaches: Zimbabwe's local leadership ladder — village head solves minor disputes, ensures welfare and supervises rituals; headman assists and reports to the chief; chief oversees the whole community, conducts rain-asking ceremonies, keeps law and order and upholds moral standards; councillor represents the community in local government and promotes development — learned by interviewing real local leaders. ## Sub lesson 3 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 7) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 7) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 5 civics runs on two ladders at once — the traditional ladder (village head → headman → chief, with the councillor bridging into the state) and the citizen ladder (rights matched one-for-one with responsibilities: "every right has a matching responsibility"), with Zimbabwe-specific protectors named (Police Victim-Friendly Unit, village health workers, social workers). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 11) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 11) (teacher guide — evidence): the parallel Grade 7 book adds what the PlusOne guide does not teach — elections themselves (forms of elections, the electoral process, role-played class elections for class monitor) — and teaches international organisations as a table of UN agencies with their functions and years of formation.
