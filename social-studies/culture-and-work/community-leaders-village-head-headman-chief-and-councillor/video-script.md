# Video script — Community Leaders Village Head Headman Chief and Councillor

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe Grade 5 zw-g5 PlusOne Social Science Grade 5 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 29), teaches: Zimbabwe's local leadership ladder — village head solves minor disputes, ensures welfare and supervises rituals; headman assists and reports to the chief; chief oversees the whole community, conducts rain-asking ceremonies, keeps law and order and upholds moral standards; councillor represents the community in local government and promotes development — learned by interviewing real local leaders.
Zimbabwe Grade 7 zw-ppp-g7 Best Approach to Heritage-Social Studies Teacher's Resource Book Grade 7 (printed pp. 1–6), teaches: The hierarchy of indigenous leadership in Zimbabwe, the role of indigenous leaders, and how chiefs are installed and buried.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 7) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 5 civics runs on two ladders at once — the traditional ladder (village head → headman → chief, with the councillor bridging into the state) and the citizen ladder (rights matched one-for-one with responsibilities: "every right has a matching responsibility"), with Zimbabwe-specific protectors named (Police Victim-Friendly Unit, village health workers, social workers).

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 11) (teacher guide — evidence): the parallel Grade 7 book adds what the PlusOne guide does not teach — elections themselves (forms of elections, the electoral process, role-played class elections for class monitor) — and teaches international organisations as a table of UN agencies with their functions and years of formation.
