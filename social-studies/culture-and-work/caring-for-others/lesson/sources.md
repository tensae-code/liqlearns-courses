Tanzania's own way (Standard Six, Civic and Moral Education Standard Six Pupil's Book): TIE teaches civics as a good-governance wheel (the cover itself: transparency, accountability, participation, human rights, integrity, efficiency, reconciliation, democracy, equality, rule of law around “GOOD GOVERNANCE”) and builds the state from the child's own levels — street, village, ward, district, region, nation — with the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar as the founding fact and Kiswahili, unity and humanism (utu) as named national treasures.

Registry note: the original coined string `culture-and-work/caring-for-others-and-treating-people-fairly` is recorded in the provenance rows above, exactly as filed; it resolves through the slug registry §3 into this canonical lesson, `culture-and-work/caring-for-others`.

Mauritius’s own way (teacher guide — evidence): Values taught through the child’s widening circles — self, people around me, then "I love my country" — with Mauritius’s own flag colours, anthem words, and its three islands (Mauritius, Rodrigues, Agalega) as the identity content, and unity taught through the country’s many festivals.
