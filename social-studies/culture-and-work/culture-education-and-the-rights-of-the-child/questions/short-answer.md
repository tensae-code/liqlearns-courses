# Culture Education and the Rights of the Child — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Culture and human rights (Культура і правы чалавека) — Grade 9 — to be a cultured person in the information-computer civilisation — a person's level of culture and education now directly decides his place in production and society, so education, "the basis of a civilised society", stands at the centre of the social rights: the right to education (Article 26 of the Universal Declaration; Article 49 of the Constitution, on accessible and free general secondary and vocational education); and the rights of the child — the legislative defence of children's rights drawn as a scheme of three documents: the Declaration of the Rights of the Child (1959), the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989) and the Law of the Republic of Belarus "On the Rights of the Child" (1993), childhood lasting to 18, with the child's own duties to society, the state and his family set beside his rights (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way — the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state. (any close wording).**
