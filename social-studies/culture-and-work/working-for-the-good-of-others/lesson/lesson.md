# Working for the Good of Others

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: work whose fruit is other people's good — cleaning the road, helping the injured, mending the bridge — done while working for one's own living — as Myanmar's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Contribution for bringing advantages for other people

Myanmar Morality and Civics Grade 3 (printed p. 30), teaches: Work whose fruit is other people's good — cleaning the road, helping the injured, mending the bridge — done while working for one's own living. ## Sub lesson 2 — Work together for everyone's good

Myanmar Morality and Civics, Grade 4 (printed p. 14), teaches: Joining hands in work whose benefit is everyone's. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Myanmar's way — the book's own teaching way

Myanmar's own way: the Grade 3 units keep the story-and-poem shape but argue finer points: fairness is NOT equal shares (the hungry son gets bread, the full daughter a candy; the girl with glasses sits in front), trust is keeping a friend's weakness secret, and the clean-environment unit teaches Reduce–Reuse–Recycle by name. Grade 4 names children's RIGHTS as a picture inventory (food, water, a house, clothes, care, medicine, play, learning, family) and turns environmental care into two full units — preserving the earth and the natural surroundings, with drought, flood, felled forests and factory smoke as the evidence. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
