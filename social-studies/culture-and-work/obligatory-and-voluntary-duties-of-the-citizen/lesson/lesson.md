# Obligatory and Voluntary Duties of the Citizen

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the citizen's duties in two kinds — obligatory duties the law accounts for (guarding the state's secrets, respecting the constitution and laws, keeping the homeland's security and defending it, protecting public facilities, performing national service) and voluntary duties done willingly without pay (taking part in society and protecting the environment, contributing to community projects, serving the community's members) — as United Arab Emirates's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The duties diagram

United Arab Emirates Social Studies & National Education, G6 Term 1, printed p. 63, teaches: the citizen's duties in two kinds — obligatory duties the law accounts for (guarding the state's secrets, respecting the constitution and laws, keeping the homeland's security and defending it, protecting public facilities, performing national service) and voluntary duties done willingly without pay (taking part in society and protecting the environment, contributing to community projects, serving the community's members) This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — United Arab Emirates' way — the book's own teaching way

United Arab Emirates' own way: citizenship at Grade 6 is a whole unit of its own between History and Geography — its duties are split into what the law compels and what the citizen volunteers, its model citizens are the mothers of martyrs, and its proof of goodness is measured in tonnes of aid flown to a hundred countries. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
