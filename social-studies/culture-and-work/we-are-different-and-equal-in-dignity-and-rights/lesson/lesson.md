# We Are Different And Equal In Dignity And Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: each person is unique and different, yet all are equal in dignity and in rights, as Angola's Constitution art. 23 guarantees — no one may be harmed or privileged for ancestry, sex, race, language, religion or condition — as Angola's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — As nossas diferenças e semelhanças

Angola Educação Moral e Cívica G5 (printed p. 9), teaches: Each person is unique and different, yet all are equal in dignity and in rights, as Angola's Constitution art. 23 guarantees — no one may be harmed or privileged for ancestry, sex, race, language, religion or condition. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Angola's way — the book's own teaching way

Angola's own way: a moral-and-civic book built on the child's own reflection — every unit opens with the pupil's own life (my habits, my family, my school) and only then names the rule; equality is taught straight from Angola's own Constitution (art. 23: all are equal before the Constitution and the law), and the book ends not on the state but on national reconciliation — building a better Angola, then a better world. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
