Uganda's own way (Uganda Excel P6 SST): P6 looks outward and inward at once — the East African Community's own organs and symbols beside the member states' flags, then the citizen: what citizenship is, the child's rights, what violating rights looks like (mob justice named plainly), and how elections actually run.

Vanuatu's own way (teacher guide — evidence): social citizenship is taught as roles before rules — the Year 1 child maps who they are in family and village (chief, pastor, nurse, police, women's and youth group leaders), learns that roles change and are not fixed by gender, and only in Years 5–6 reaches rights, and how a community's rules are made and by whom.

**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Uganda, Rwanda). Vanuatu is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Rwanda's own way (Primary Three — Rwanda P3 SB Kiny): P3 widens duty from the household to the state: an extended-family member's responsibilities are listed as supporting government programmes (health insurance, cooperatives, sending children to school), and local democracy is taught as a procedure — who may stand, how campaigns run, how votes are cast, counted and announced, and the pledge to vote at 18

Rwanda's own way (Primary Four — Rwanda P4 SRS SB): P4 states rights before duties of the state: rights belong to everybody whatever their place, age, sex or wealth; equality is then split from equity (same treatment vs a fair share of what each needs), and district government is taught as a council that collects local taxes and elects its own Mayor

Rwanda's own way (Primary Five — Rwanda Grade 5 P5 SB): the province is the child's civic world — complementarity (everyone's work fitting together) is the organising value, and national symbols, the 2003/2015 constitution and province leadership are taught as Rwanda's own frame inside it
