Pakistan's Grade 4 SS4 (printed p. 16); Grade 5 SS5 (printed pp. 12–13); Grade 10 PS10 (printed p. 115); Grade 12 PS12-2014 (printed p. 87); Grade 4 SS4-KPK (printed p. 15); Grade 5 SS5-KPK (printed p. 26), teaches: Ch. 2 Dress, Ch. 2 Dresses of Pakistan, Ch. 6 Salient Features: Dress and Food (dress), Ch. 6 Salient Features: Dress, Ch. 2 Dress, Ch. 2 (chapter exercise) — teaches: shalwar kameez is worn all over Pakistan, shaped by each region's weather and custom — turbans and caps, Sindhi topi and ajrak, Balochi dress, northern woollens; the national shalwar kameez takes a regional face in each province — Sindhi topi and ajrak, Punjabi dress with phulkari, Balochi embroidered dress, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa's waistcoat and cap, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir dress — each pictured child by child; shalwar kameez as the national dress with regional variations in cloth, cut and ornament from province to province; dress follows province, climate and tradition — dhoti, kurta and turban giving way to shalwar; women's shalwar, kurta and dupatta; sherwani, waistcoat, coat and pant in the cities; shalwar qamees the national dress in its regional styles — dhoti kurta, lungi and turban in the villages, waist coat and sherwani, desert ladies' gharara and bangles, the black cloak and caps of Chitral valley and Hunza; Ajrak confirmed as a special part of Sindhi dress, and glass, wool and silk thread in Balochi embroidery — dress as provincial identity, tested as fact.

Pakistan's own way: citizenship is taught before everything else — a child first learns what a citizen, a global citizen and a digital citizen is, then Pakistan's own 1973 Constitution, its federal and provincial organs with real seat counts, and only then history, geography and economics. Class 5 deepens Class 4 — rights now change with time, the UN and the 1948 Declaration enter, freedom of speech gets its own lesson with John Stuart Mill quoted, and government is compared across federal, provincial AND local levels with a table of who does what. at Class 10 the state tells its story whole — ideology first, then the society it made: thirteen named features of Pakistani culture, minorities' rights written into the culture chapter, and a full closing chapter on women's rights, women nation-builders and the laws that protect them. the older book teaches the same state from the other end — culture defined before it is described, languages given a whole chapter with Urdu named the language of national integration, and national integration itself defined, factored and argued like a civics concept, not a slogan. the same Single National Curriculum in the KPK board's English — citizenship taught identically in Peshawar and Lahore, with the global-citizen lesson anchored by Dr Ruth Pfau, the German doctor who gave 57 years to Pakistan's leprosy patients, and Malala Yousafzai, KPK's own daughter, as the worked examples in the pupil's copy. the KPK Class 5 book gives the citizen's charter in two plain lists — seven rights (ending in the right to vote) against nine responsibilities (ending in honest dealing) — and makes Abdul Sattar Edhi, who "served the suffering humanity", its model of what a responsible citizen is.
