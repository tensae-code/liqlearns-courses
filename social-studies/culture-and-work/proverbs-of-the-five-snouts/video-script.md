The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 31, teaches: The people's wisdom about life is carried in proverbs drawn from the herding life — "a person is known by their tongue, an animal by its legs", "do not sit idle in summer, do not panic in a zud"; parents teach their children through proverbs, matching the saying to the moment, and the child learns to hear the deep meaning inside the comparison.

Mongolia's own way (Book 4): the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other.
