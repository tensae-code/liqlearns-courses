# Proverbs of the Five Snouts

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the people's wisdom about life is carried in proverbs drawn from the herding life — "a person is known by their tongue, an animal by its legs", "do not sit idle in summer, do not panic in a zud"; parents teach their children through proverbs, matching the saying to the moment, and the child learns to hear the deep meaning inside the comparison — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 10 "Sayings and proverbs connected with the five snouts" (Таван хошуу малын холбогдолтой хэлц, зүйр цэцэн үг)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 31, teaches: The people's wisdom about life is carried in proverbs drawn from the herding life — "a person is known by their tongue, an animal by its legs", "do not sit idle in summer, do not panic in a zud"; parents teach their children through proverbs, matching the saying to the moment, and the child learns to hear the deep meaning inside the comparison. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
