# Video script — Kindness from the Other Persons Place

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 66), teaches: Thinking of the people around you and what you can do for them — for old people, for younger children — because on this earth everyone lives by helping each other.
Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 70), teaches: A wolf and a bear meet on a bridge only one can cross; the bear lifts the wolf over instead of quarrelling (reading).
Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 62), teaches: Kindness that starts from imagining the other person's feelings.
Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 66), teaches: Reaching out to an old woman with kindness until hearts meet (reading).
Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 60), teaches: Compassion — standing where the other person stands.
Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 66), teaches: Kindness and sacrifice for another (reading story).

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood.

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out.

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination.
