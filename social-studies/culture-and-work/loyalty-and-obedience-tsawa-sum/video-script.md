# Video script — Loyalty and Obedience Tsawa Sum

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Student Workbook 2017, printed pp. 53–58, teaches: obedience learned through Little Red Riding Hood, printed in the workbook as a play to perform — what her mother told her, what befell her when she stopped for flowers and talked to the wolf — and sealed with three written obedience pledges each to the child's teacher and parents; and Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Teacher's Handbook 2017, printed pp. 55–61, teaches: TEACHER: loyalty as the strong support that protects the bonds between people — Tha Damtshig, mutual trust, and affection for Tsawa Sum, the King, country and people at the root of Bhutanese identity — and its first lesson, obedience: listening to parents, teachers and elders as respect for people, rules and laws, yet never blind obedience, for a child must be able to say no to a wrong order (Little Red Riding Hood's disobedience is the cautionary tale).

Bhutan's own way: the workbook turns each value into a week of doing — a sorry-card actually drawn and given, a day planned to the minute, kind acts collected as leaves on a classroom Kindness Tree until they earn a fruit, and obedience sealed with written pledges to teacher and parents; the values are Bhutan's own (Tha-Dam-Tse Ley-Judy, Tsawa Sum) carried in stories the whole world tells (the Giving Tree, Little Red Riding Hood, the Itsy Bitsy Spider).
