## Sub lesson 1 — Loyalty and Obedience Tsawa Sum

Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Student Workbook 2017, printed pp. 53–58, teaches: obedience learned through Little Red Riding Hood, printed in the workbook as a play to perform — what her mother told her, what befell her when she stopped for flowers and talked to the wolf — and sealed with three written obedience pledges each to the child's teacher and parents; and Bhutan Class III Values and Citizenship Education Teacher's Handbook 2017, printed pp. 55–61, teaches: TEACHER: loyalty as the strong support that protects the bonds between people — Tha Damtshig, mutual trust, and affection for Tsawa Sum, the King, country and people at the root of Bhutanese identity — and its first lesson, obedience: listening to parents, teachers and elders as respect for people, rules and laws, yet never blind obedience, for a child must be able to say no to a wrong order (Little Red Riding Hood's disobedience is the cautionary tale). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bhutan's way — the book's own teaching way

Bhutan's own way: the workbook turns each value into a week of doing — a sorry-card actually drawn and given, a day planned to the minute, kind acts collected as leaves on a classroom Kindness Tree until they earn a fruit, and obedience sealed with written pledges to teacher and parents; the values are Bhutan's own (Tha-Dam-Tse Ley-Judy, Tsawa Sum) carried in stories the whole world tells (the Giving Tree, Little Red Riding Hood, the Itsy Bitsy Spider). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
