# Hands-on — Law as the Measure of Freedom and Responsibility

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Law (права) as the fundamental basis of human civilisation in its two senses — objective law, the system of generally binding norms of behaviour established by the state in the form of laws and other acts, and subjective law, a person's own rights and freedoms, which are the measure of his freedom; law asserts the universal equality of people before the law, and a person's rights are his from birth (the 1948 Universal Declaration, taken into the preamble of Belarus's Constitution); the theory of natural law — rights belong to the person by nature, not by the state's gift (Aristotle and Cicero to Locke, Rousseau, Diderot and Radishchev); human rights in the past, where rights were estate-and-class privileges — Roman law, Russkaya Pravda, Magna Carta 1215, Magdeburg law, the Statutes of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, the English Bill of Rights 1689, the US Declaration of Independence 1776 and the French Declaration of 1789, in the book's development-of-the-idea table; and the dignity of the human person (Kant) as the ground of all rights. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
