# Video script — Types of Groups Primary and Secondary

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 83–84), teaches: A group is two or more persons linked by a common purpose or interest, and groups give us a sense of direction, reveal our attitudes, organise us for positive development and prepare us for the world; primary groups are small and face-to-face with intimacy and permanence — the family and the class with its teacher — while secondary groups are larger and lack that intimacy, like the whole school at General Assembly.

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 84–86 (NEW slug first proposed in the line above)), teaches: The peer group of the same age and status is very influential and can help members develop loyalty, make and abide by rules and gain belonging; formal groups have rules, membership requirements and an organised structure (the school, Girl Guides, Boy Scouts, Lions Clubs); informal groups form spontaneously for the needs of the moment; interest groups gather around a shared interest (Bible Club, Debating Society, Drama Group); negative groups display attitudes society does not expect — taking drugs, breaking laws and vandalism.

Guyana's own way: the secondary book teaches the same citizenship at national scale — CARICOM as the region's own institution, the family as society's oldest institution, women's equality written into the 1980 Constitution and the Acts of the 1990s, and the state answering pollution through the Environmental Protection Agency.
