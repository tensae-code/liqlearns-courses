# Hands-on — Types of Groups Primary and Secondary

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: A group is two or more persons linked by a common purpose or interest, and groups give us a sense of direction, reveal our attitudes, organise us for positive development and prepare us for the world; primary groups are small and face-to-face with intimacy and permanence — the family and the class with its teacher — while secondary groups are larger and lack that intimacy, like the whole school at General Assembly. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The peer group of the same age and status is very influential and can help members develop loyalty, make and abide by rules and gain belonging; formal groups have rules, membership requirements and an organised structure (the school, Girl Guides, Boy Scouts, Lions Clubs); informal groups form spontaneously for the needs of the moment; interest groups gather around a shared interest (Bible Club, Debating Society, Drama Group); negative groups display attitudes society does not expect — taking drugs, breaking laws and vandalism. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
