# Types of Groups Primary and Secondary

## Sub lesson 1 — What is a group? Primary and secondary groups

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 83–84), teaches: A group is two or more persons linked by a common purpose or interest, and groups give us a sense of direction, reveal our attitudes, organise us for positive development and prepare us for the world; primary groups are small and face-to-face with intimacy and permanence — the family and the class with its teacher — while secondary groups are larger and lack that intimacy, like the whole school at General Assembly. ## Sub lesson 2 — Peer, formal, informal, interest and negative groups

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 84–86 (NEW slug first proposed in the line above)), teaches: The peer group of the same age and status is very influential and can help members develop loyalty, make and abide by rules and gain belonging; formal groups have rules, membership requirements and an organised structure (the school, Girl Guides, Boy Scouts, Lions Clubs); informal groups form spontaneously for the needs of the moment; interest groups gather around a shared interest (Bible Club, Debating Society, Drama Group); negative groups display attitudes society does not expect — taking drugs, breaking laws and vandalism.
