Mongolia Civic Ethics Grade 6, printed p. 33, teaches: The teaching word (сургаал үг) is the distilled experience of the ancestors, forgotten by the young at their cost — Chinggis's Bilig teachings and Ravjaa's verse agree: a fault-correcting word, however harsh, taken humbly is medicine, and taken proudly is poison; how to receive correction — first ask what the person means by it and why they say it to me, listen to the end without interrupting, do not take offence, receive it gently, and then correct the fault; Togtokhtör Wang's household teaching and the paper-bird poem close the unit: there is no person who does not need teaching.

Mongolia's own way (Link book L2): from Grade 6 the civics subject changes its name and its method — no longer "Civic Education" but "Civic Ethics" (Иргэний ёс зүйн боловсрол), a self-observation workbook in the Buddhist-inflected Mongolian moral tradition: the pupil keeps a diary of their own acts of body, speech and mind, and ethics is practised as a daily discipline before it is ever a rule about others.
