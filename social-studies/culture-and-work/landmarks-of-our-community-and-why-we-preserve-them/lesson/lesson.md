# Landmarks of Our Community and Why We Preserve Them

## Sub lesson 1 — Landmarks

Guyana Grade 7 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 1 (printed p. 71), teaches: A landmark is an important place, monument, building or structure that enables one to locate an area; landmarks may have historical significance (forts, monuments, trees, tombs) or religious significance (churches, mosques, temples); landmarks and boundaries help us to find places in our environment; some landmarks are under the care of the National Trust of Guyana — and we must preserve our landmarks. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: Book 1 opens secondary Social Studies where the primary books end — the child as citizen of nested communities (family, school, village or town, country), national symbols learned part by part with their meanings, and local government taught through the rates-and-taxes bargain: services exist because citizens pay for them and care for what they buy. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
