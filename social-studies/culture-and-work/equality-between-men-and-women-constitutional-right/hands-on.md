# Hands-on — Equality Between Men and Women Constitutional Right

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Equality between men and women is a constitutional right
Work through this part with your hands: Morocco Grade 6 Al-Massar Ijtiamiat, printed p. 146, teaches: gender-based discrimination defined from stereotyped pictures (the woman cleaning, the man cooking); Article 19 of the Moroccan constitution quoted; the numbers of inequality (illiteracy, activity, parliament, ministers); a job-interview story in which a qualified woman is refused; CEDAW (1979, in force 1981) as the international charter against discrimination against women; Moroccan women who broke the stereotypes (a scholar, a writer, the first woman pilot); and role-play of a school awareness skit against stereotyped images. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Moroccan women between yesterday and today
Work through this part with your hands: Morocco Grade 6 Al-Massar Ijtiamiat, printed p. 152, teaches: a class project researching how Moroccan women's lives and rights have changed — the 2009 municipal elections poster, the 2011 constitution, the family code (مدونة الأسرة), women's participation in public and political life, and organising education for girls in difficult situations. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Morocco's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: Morocco's own way: the Grade 6 civics course is a working apprenticeship in democracy — it opens with law, freedom and responsibility (Rousseau: no freedom without responsibility), runs the class as a republic (charter, elected cooperative), then walks out of the school gate: local democracy is learned by studying the elected communal council from the 2011 Constitution (Articles 135 and 139), visiting the commune headquarters, simulating a full council meeting with agenda, president, budget rapporteur and association representatives, and finally pleading a real solidarity proposal before that council. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
