# Equality Between Men and Women Constitutional Right — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This part teaches: Morocco Grade 6 Al-Massar Ijtiamiat, printed p. 146, teaches: gender-based discrimination defined from stereotyped pictures (the woman cleaning, the man cooking); Article 19 of the Moroccan constitution quoted; the numbers of inequality (illiteracy, activity, parliament, ministers); a job-interview story in which a qualified woman is refused; CEDAW (1979, in force 1981) as the international charter against discrimination against women; Moroccan women who broke the stereotypes (a scholar, a writer, the first woman pilot); and role-play of a school awareness skit against stereotyped images.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This part teaches: Morocco Grade 6 Al-Massar Ijtiamiat, printed p. 152, teaches: a class project researching how Moroccan women's lives and rights have changed — the 2009 municipal elections poster, the 2011 constitution, the family code (مدونة الأسرة), women's participation in public and political life, and organising education for girls in difficult situations.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This part teaches: Morocco's own way: the Grade 6 civics course is a working apprenticeship in democracy — it opens with law, freedom and responsibility (Rousseau: no freedom without responsibility), runs the class as a republic (charter, elected cooperative), then walks out of the school gate: local democracy is learned by studying the elected communal council from the 2011 Constitution (Articles 135 and 139), visiting the commune headquarters, simulating a full council meeting with agenda, president, budget rapporteur and association representatives, and finally pleading a real solidarity proposal before that council.
**Answer: True**
