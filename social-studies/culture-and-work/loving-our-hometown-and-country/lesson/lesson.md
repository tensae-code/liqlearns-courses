# Loving Our Hometown and Country

## Sub lesson 1 — (5) ふるさとに親しみをもって (With love for our hometown)

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 150), teaches: The town you grow up in — school, park, library, hospital, shopping street, friends — introduced in a town newspaper the pupils make, with its festivals and favourite places. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 読みもの「ぎおんまつり」("The Gion Festival")

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 154), teaches: Riding a float in Kyoto's Gion Festival, and cousin Kazu from Hakata whose own town keeps the Dontaku festival (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — (5) きょう土を愛する心をもって (With a heart that loves our hometown)

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 158), teaches: The hometown that raised your heart — its people, life, nature and festivals — introduced to others, with the poet Ishikawa Takuboku who loved his hometown. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — (7) 郷土や国を愛する心を (A heart that loves hometown and country)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 164), teaches: The traditions and culture handed down in your hometown, and carrying the country forward. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 5 — 国家・社会の一員として ―読みもの「米百俵」("As a Member of State and Society — 'A Hundred Bales of Rice'")

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 174), teaches: Nagaoka's choice to spend relief rice on a school — education before immediate relief (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 6 — Japan's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 7 — Japan's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 8 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
