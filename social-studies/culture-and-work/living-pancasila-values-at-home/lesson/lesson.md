## Sub lesson 2 — Indonesia's way — the books' own teaching way

Indonesia's own way: Grade 2 turns Pancasila from a symbol to copy into behaviour at home — each principle (sila) is matched to a picture-symbol on the Garuda shield and then to a concrete act a 7-year-old can do in the family. Grade 3 is the first book to name the state the child lives in — your own street is taught as part of the NKRI (Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia), and rights are introduced paired, always, with duties. Grade 4 introduces the child's first government — the perangkat desa/kelurahan, the village and urban-ward officials — inside a book about the child's own street, before any national institution is named. Grade 5 names the machinery of Indonesian civic life — norma (religious, decency, social and legal norms as one tree) and musyawarah, deciding by deliberation until consensus (mufakat), practised first in the family. the primary Pancasila course closes by widening every earlier theme to the nation — norms become norms of berbangsa dan bernegara (national and state life), and gotong royong is finally named as bela negara, defending the country by working together. the guide frames the whole subject through the Profil Pelajar Pancasila — six learner dimensions (faith and noble character, global diversity, gotong royong, independence, critical reasoning, creativity) that every chapter must build. the final primary guide states the subject's position (kedudukan) explicitly — Pendidikan Pancasila is the vehicle of the state ideology in school — and sequences outcomes to the NKRI chapters. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
