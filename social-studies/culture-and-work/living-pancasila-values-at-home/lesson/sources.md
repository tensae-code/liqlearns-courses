**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Indonesia).** Indonesia's teacher-guide parts in this lesson are teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere — Indonesia counts through its student-book parts.

Indonesia's own way (Book 1): Grade 2 turns Pancasila from a symbol to copy into behaviour at home — each principle (sila) is matched to a picture-symbol on the Garuda shield and then to a concrete act a 7-year-old can do in the family.

Indonesia's own way (Book 2): Grade 3 is the first book to name the state the child lives in — your own street is taught as part of the NKRI (Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia), and rights are introduced paired, always, with duties.

Indonesia's own way (Book 3): Grade 4 introduces the child's first government — the perangkat desa/kelurahan, the village and urban-ward officials — inside a book about the child's own street, before any national institution is named.

Indonesia's own way (Book 4): Grade 5 names the machinery of Indonesian civic life — norma (religious, decency, social and legal norms as one tree) and musyawarah, deciding by deliberation until consensus (mufakat), practised first in the family.

Indonesia's own way (Book 5): the primary Pancasila course closes by widening every earlier theme to the nation — norms become norms of berbangsa dan bernegara (national and state life), and gotong royong is finally named as bela negara, defending the country by working together.

Indonesia's own way (Book 6): the guide frames the whole subject through the Profil Pelajar Pancasila — six learner dimensions (faith and noble character, global diversity, gotong royong, independence, critical reasoning, creativity) that every chapter must build.

Indonesia's own way (Book 7): the final primary guide states the subject's position (kedudukan) explicitly — Pendidikan Pancasila is the vehicle of the state ideology in school — and sequences outcomes to the NKRI chapters.
