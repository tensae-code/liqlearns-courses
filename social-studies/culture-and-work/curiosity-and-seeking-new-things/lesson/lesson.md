# Curiosity and Seeking New Things

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: curiosity as the starting point of discovery — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — (5) 進んで新しいものを求めて (Willingly seeking new things)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 42), teaches: Curiosity as the starting point of discovery. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 読みもの「天からの手紙」("A Letter from Heaven")

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 46), teaches: A scientist's lifelong curiosity through Nakaya Ukichiro's snow research (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
