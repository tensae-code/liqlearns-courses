# Personal Freedom Its Limits and Legal Protection

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: personal freedom as the basis of the legal status of the personality — a person's dignity is fixed in the whole integral system of his rights, political, economic and cultural, and freedom is his ability to choose and act on inner reasoning, not external compulsion (the book distinguishes political, personal, professional, moral, family and even intimate freedoms, and defines freedom as the right to do everything that does not harm others); freedom is not arbitrariness — the Constitution allows limits only on the basis of law and only for national security, public order, morality, public health and the rights of others (Article 23); legal protection — the presumption of innocence, the right to legal aid and defence (Articles 59–62), freedom of movement and choice of residence (Article 30), freedom of thought, conscience and the press (Articles 31, 33, 34), all guarded by the constitutional defence of the person and by the prokuratura's supervision — as Belarus' book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The right to personal freedom (Права на асабістую свабоду) — Grade 9

Belarus Grade 9 Person. Society. State, Book 2 (printed p. 177), teaches: Personal freedom as the basis of the legal status of the personality — a person's dignity is fixed in the whole integral system of his rights, political, economic and cultural, and freedom is his ability to choose and act on inner reasoning, not external compulsion (the book distinguishes political, personal, professional, moral, family and even intimate freedoms, and defines freedom as the right to do everything that does not harm others); freedom is not arbitrariness — the Constitution allows limits only on the basis of law and only for national security, public order, morality, public health and the rights of others (Article 23); legal protection — the presumption of innocence, the right to legal aid and defence (Articles 59–62), freedom of movement and choice of residence (Article 30), freedom of thought, conscience and the press (Articles 31, 33, 34), all guarded by the constitutional defence of the person and by the prokuratura's supervision. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way

Belarus' own way: the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
