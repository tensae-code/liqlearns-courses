# The Homeland and What It is Made of

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the homeland is a defined area of land on which a group of people live, bound by shared ties, among them citizenship, language, history and the shared interests; and it is the right of every human being to belong to a homeland he belongs to not by residence only, but by true mutual belonging; the homeland includes the land — an essential foundation for the homeland's existence, for no state is established without land, and our homeland Iraq's land is distinguished by its beautiful, varied natural features, of mountains, hills, plateaus, plains and valleys rich in wealth and natural resources; the population — the group of individuals living on Iraq's land with their different nationalities and religions, such as the Arabs, Kurds, Turkmen, Christians, Sabian Mandaeans, Shabak and Yazidis and others, who love it and defend it; the waters — the rivers and lakes that exist within the homeland's borders, and in our homeland Iraq are the two greatest rivers, the Tigris and the Euphrates, from which Iraq took its name, the Land of the Two Rivers; and the airspace — the homeland is not limited to the land and the waters, but includes also the sky above that land and those waters; and the pupil loves his homeland because it is the land he lives on with his people's sons and his fathers and grandfathers lived on, his civilisation and roots were built on it, and it supplies him with abundant goods — as Iraq's books teach it. ## Sub lesson 1 — The Homeland and What It is Made of

Iraq Grade 4 Social Studies (printed pp. 55–58, 60), teaches: The homeland is a defined area of land on which a group of people live, bound by shared ties, among them citizenship, language, history and the shared interests; and it is the right of every human being to belong to a homeland he belongs to not by residence only, but by true mutual belonging; the homeland includes the land — an essential foundation for the homeland's existence, for no state is established without land, and our homeland Iraq's land is distinguished by its beautiful, varied natural features, of mountains, hills, plateaus, plains and valleys rich in wealth and natural resources; the population — the group of individuals living on Iraq's land with their different nationalities and religions, such as the Arabs, Kurds, Turkmen, Christians, Sabian Mandaeans, Shabak and Yazidis and others, who love it and defend it; the waters — the rivers and lakes that exist within the homeland's borders, and in our homeland Iraq are the two greatest rivers, the Tigris and the Euphrates, from which Iraq took its name, the Land of the Two Rivers; and the airspace — the homeland is not limited to the land and the waters, but includes also the sky above that land and those waters; and the pupil loves his homeland because it is the land he lives on with his people's sons and his fathers and grandfathers lived on, his civilisation and roots were built on it, and it supplies him with abundant goods. ## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: civics starts at the school desk — the first community the Grade 4 child is given duties toward is the school family — and the homeland is then built piece by piece (land, people, waters, sky), with every one of Iraq's communities named in the population list, before rights are matched one-for-one with duties. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
