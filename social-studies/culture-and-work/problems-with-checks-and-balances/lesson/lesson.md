## Sub lesson 1 — Problems with Checks and Balances

Micronesia Grade 11 Micronesian Government (printed p. 269), teaches: because the president must be a congressman chosen by Congress, some Micronesians argue Congress holds too much power over the president — a live argument about the system itself. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Micronesia's way — the book's own teaching way

Micronesia's own way: present-day government is taught as “Micronesia's Two Governments” — the elected FSM state/national system and the traditional leadership system running beside it — and every structure (federal levels, checks and balances, the rotating presidency) is explained through the compromises of the first constitutional convention and through village-level stories (a Yap bus bill stopped by chiefs, a fishing-fee fight between governors and Palikir, the Amnesty Bill stopped by the people). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
