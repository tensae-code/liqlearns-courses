# The Four Spheres of Social Life

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the life of society as four interconnected spheres, drawn in the book's own scheme — the productive/economic sphere (production of material goods; productive forces and production relations; property relations), the social sphere (groups by sex and age, ethnic groups — clan, tribe, nationality, nation — with national self-consciousness, and classes picked out by property, profession, place of residence and income), the political sphere (the struggle for state power and its use — the state, political parties, public organisations) and the spiritual sphere (science, art, religion, philosophy, morality); and, framing them, the idea of the socio-economic formation (Spencer's organism comparison, Marx's formation theory) — as Belarus' book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way

Belarus' own way: the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
