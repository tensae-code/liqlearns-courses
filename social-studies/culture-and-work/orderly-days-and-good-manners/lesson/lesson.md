# Orderly Days and Good Manners

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: keeping rules and order so every day feels good — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — きそく正しく、気もちのよい毎日を (An orderly, pleasant every day)

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 10), teaches: Keeping rules and order so every day feels good. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 生活をふりかえってみよう (Let's look back at our daily life)

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 20), teaches: Reviewing how you spend your days and whether you live them energetically and well. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Japan's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
