# Secondary activities — modern light and heavy industry

## Do it first

Sort ten factory-made things in your home into light-industry goods and heavy-industry goods. Write the sorting.

## Sub lesson 1 — Modern manufacturing

**Modern manufacturing industries produce in factories with machines driven by electric or other power.** Their marks: **very large output, much capital, powerful machines, a high division of labour** and **much fully or partly trained manpower**.

[Picture 11513 — two factory panels: a textile mill and a shipyard]

## Sub lesson 2 — Light and heavy

**Light industries** use **few raw materials** and make **consumer goods** — **textiles, food, house and office goods**.

**Heavy industries** use **bulky inputs in bulk** and make **heavy goods** — **metal engineering, shipbuilding, railway engineering, aircraft and vehicles** — and demand the **most capital and raw material**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Name the five marks of modern manufacturing. Tell light from heavy industry, with the book's examples of each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort ten factory-made things in your home into light-industry goods and heavy-industry goods. Write the sorting.
2. Compare a cottage weaver and a textile mill: labour, power, output. Write three differences using the lesson's marks.
