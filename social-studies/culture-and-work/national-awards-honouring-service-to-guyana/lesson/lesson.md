# National Awards Honouring Service to Guyana

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: National Awards are special gifts from a National Award Committee, presented by the Executive President, to citizens (and others who helped Guyana) for work done for the country — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our National Awards

Guyana Grade 4 Social Studies For Our Children Book 4 (printed p. 46), teaches: National Awards are special gifts from a National Award Committee, presented by the Executive President, to citizens (and others who helped Guyana) for work done for the country. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Types of National Awards

Guyana Grade 4 Social Studies For Our Children Book 4 (printed p. 47 (NEW slug first proposed in the line above)), teaches: The award ladder — Order of Excellence (highest), Order of Roraima, Cacique Crown of Honour, Golden Arrow of Achievement, Medal of Service — and who receives each. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — National Awards

Guyana Grade 5 Social Studies For Our Children Book 5 (printed p. 77 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 4 above)), teaches: The award ladder in full — the Order of Excellence (highest, for citizens of high esteem), the Order of Roraima, the Cacique Crown of Honour, the Golden Arrow of Achievement, the Medal of Service, and the Military Service Star for GDF officers — and who receives each. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: Book 4 turns from "my community" to "our homeland" — Guyana is taught as six peoples who each brought a way of life, and citizenship is taught through honour (National Awards) and shared observance (Road Safety, Fire Prevention and Youth Weeks) before it is taught through rules. Book 5 widens the child from Guyana to the Caribbean Community — the peoples are taught by what each group still does today (foods, villages, festivals), neighbours are taught as people who help each other in disasters, and leadership is taught whole: Mayor, Touchau, Regional Chairman and trade-union leader side by side, all elected for a fixed time and removable. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
