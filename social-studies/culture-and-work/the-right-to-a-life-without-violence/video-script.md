# Video script — The Right to a Life Without Violence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mexico Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 1 (secundaria) (printed pp. 311–314), teaches: The school must be a violence-free space; school violence named in its kinds — bullying in verbal, social and physical forms, always an abuse of power — alongside gender, emotional, physical, psychological, sexual and economic violence.

---

Mexico Grade 8 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 2 (secundaria) (printed pp. 301–308), teaches: School violence violates pupils' rights and dignity (UNESCO 2020: over 30% of the world's pupils have suffered bullying); the protection measures, reporting mechanisms and the institutions that work for a life free of violence, extended to gender violence and human trafficking.

---

Mexico Grade 9 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 3 (secundaria) (printed pp. 299–312), teaches: Naming gender and sexual violence and human trafficking so they are not normalised — how they appear, and the protection and reporting mechanisms for a life free of violence, in person and online (first placed from Grade 7 in this wave).

---

Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. Mexico's own way (Grade 8): the second year turns from living together to judging and acting — pupils weigh ethical judgements on real injustice, learn how laws are made and changed in Mexico's own Congress, practise mediation, and end as watchdogs: citizen participation is taught as the duty to see public money administered with honesty, transparency and legality. Mexico's own way (Grade 9): the third year turns the course outward to action — participation spaces for children and adolescents, community projects for democratic culture, and democracy's own challenges (corruption, violence, disenchantment) named as things the young citizen is expected to work on.
