# The Right to a Life Without Violence

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the school must be a violence-free space; school violence named in its kinds — bullying in verbal, social and physical forms, always an abuse of power — alongside gender, emotional, physical, psychological, sexual and economic violence — as Mexico's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A School Free of Violence (Grade 7)

Mexico Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 1 (secundaria) (printed pp. 311–314), teaches: The school must be a violence-free space; school violence named in its kinds — bullying in verbal, social and physical forms, always an abuse of power — alongside gender, emotional, physical, psychological, sexual and economic violence. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Preventing School and Gender Violence and Trafficking (Grade 8)

Mexico Grade 8 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 2 (secundaria) (printed pp. 301–308), teaches: School violence violates pupils' rights and dignity (UNESCO 2020: over 30% of the world's pupils have suffered bullying); the protection measures, reporting mechanisms and the institutions that work for a life free of violence, extended to gender violence and human trafficking. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Protection and Reporting against Gender Violence and Trafficking (Grade 9)

Mexico Grade 9 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 3 (secundaria) (printed pp. 299–312), teaches: Naming gender and sexual violence and human trafficking so they are not normalised — how they appear, and the protection and reporting mechanisms for a life free of violence, in person and online (first placed from Grade 7 in this wave). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Mexico's way — the book's own teaching way

Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. Mexico's own way (Grade 8): the second year turns from living together to judging and acting — pupils weigh ethical judgements on real injustice, learn how laws are made and changed in Mexico's own Congress, practise mediation, and end as watchdogs: citizen participation is taught as the duty to see public money administered with honesty, transparency and legality. Mexico's own way (Grade 9): the third year turns the course outward to action — participation spaces for children and adolescents, community projects for democratic culture, and democracy's own challenges (corruption, violence, disenchantment) named as things the young citizen is expected to work on. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
