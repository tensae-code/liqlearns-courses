## Sub lesson 1 — Section 4.2 "Do we know the principles of human rights?"

Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies, printed p. 66, teaches: After the Second World War the world's peoples built the UN (1945) and adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) — Eleanor Roosevelt holding the poster; the two 1966 Covenants carry it into law, and Mongolia is party to some forty rights treaties; the eight principles — respect for human dignity; non-discrimination; inalienability (rights cannot be taken away); universality; indivisibility; interdependence; equality; and accountability — each with its emblem, and the Declaration's articles set side by side with Chapter Two of Mongolia's own Constitution. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the local year turns rights and government into neighbourhood questions — the human-rights chapter ends with the soum administration's duties and Ulaanbaatar's air, and the governance chapter is one long worked example: a playground, a Khural decision, and the children who answer it.
