# Rights and Duties in Balance and Legal Culture

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: rights and duties as two sides of one status — everyone who holds natural rights must also know and fulfil his duties before society (the Constitution of the Republic of Belarus obliges everyone to observe the Constitution and laws, respect others' dignity, rights and lawful interests, protect nature, pay taxes, care for parents, and defend the Motherland); against this stand legal nihilism and moral cynicism, which destroy law-abidingness; and legal culture — laid down in childhood as legal consciousness, knowledge of the law and lawful behaviour, on whose level the lawfulness and legal order of the whole society depend (the UN declared 1996–2005 the decade of human-rights education) — as Belarus' book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way

Belarus' own way: the civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
