## Sub lesson 2 — South Korea's way — the books' own teaching way

South Korea's own way: the high-school common subject 통합사회 (Integrated Society) is built around nine key questions asked from temporal, spatial, social and ethical perspectives at once; its civics core is Unit IV (human rights guaranteed by the constitution, law-abiding spirit and citizen participation) and Unit VI (justice and inequality), and its peace unit teaches the peaceful resolution of Korea's own division and of East Asia's history conflicts as the closing civics content. Jihaksa splits every section into two numbered lessons, so its civics teaching is the most granular of the three publishers — human rights alone gets five lessons (meaning/change, modern society, the constitution's role, law-abiding participation, problems and solutions), and its justice unit ends on the institutions and practical plans for a just society. the 2022 curriculum splits Integrated Society into two volumes; volume 2 is the civics-heavy one — human rights and the constitution, justice and inequality, then globalisation and peace — and it closes by naming the pupil's own role outright: "life as a global citizen in the future society" (세계시민으로서의 삶). Its enrichment boxes (사회 고수 되기) add the proportionality principle in limiting basic rights, redistributive justice for the socially weak, and the death penalty debated as a human-rights question.
