## Sub lesson 2 — Pakistan's way — the book's own teaching way

Pakistan's own way: citizenship is taught before everything else — a child first learns what a citizen, a global citizen and a digital citizen is, then Pakistan's own 1973 Constitution, its federal and provincial organs with real seat counts, and only then history, geography and economics. Class 5 deepens Class 4 — rights now change with time, the UN and the 1948 Declaration enter, freedom of speech gets its own lesson with John Stuart Mill quoted, and government is compared across federal, provincial AND local levels with a table of who does what. at Class 10 the state tells its story whole — ideology first, then the society it made: thirteen named features of Pakistani culture, minorities' rights written into the culture chapter, and a full closing chapter on women's rights, women nation-builders and the laws that protect them. the older book teaches the same state from the other end — culture defined before it is described, languages given a whole chapter with Urdu named the language of national integration, and national integration itself defined, factored and argued like a civics concept, not a slogan. the KPK Class 5 book gives the citizen's charter in two plain lists — seven rights (ending in the right to vote) against nine responsibilities (ending in honest dealing) — and makes Abdul Sattar Edhi, who "served the suffering humanity", its model of what a responsible citizen is. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
