# Decision Making and Autonomy — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: we can think and therefore decide what we want for our lives; decisions bring results we must assume, and even from mistakes we learn; autonomy is acting by our own thoughts, telling good from bad, just from unjust, right from wrong (Esther, 9, who decides to found a computer company).
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: the person is a being with reason, self-awareness and their own identity, forming character through life; freedom lets us decide, and we are responsible for the consequences, positive or negative; responsibility is the value in our conscience that weighs the consequences of our acts; a life project sets short-, medium- and long-term goals.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: personal decisions are determinations to solve situations or start projects, taken in family, school, friend-group and church contexts; Honduras's good future depends on each citizen deciding responsibly; competencies (knowledge, skills, dexterity) are acquired with effort and persistence, and decisions should come with autonomy and personal integrity — honesty in every act.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: a decision is a resolution taken about something, and decision-making is choosing between alternatives; society answers our behaviour with stimuli (public recognition for valued actions) or social sanctions (for actions valued negatively), which steer what we keep practising.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson's fifth part teaches: each person lives by their values — what they hold desirable or good; deliberation seeks not formal compliance but the wisest, most reasoned, most prudent decision, the one that most realises our values; tolerance inside a rule of law means respecting different ways of learning, capacities and cultural diversity, and prejudice is fed by stereotypes from media and family education.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This lesson's sixth part teaches: think, decide, act — decision-making is an essential life skill; a problem is recognised, reflected on, and alternatives weighed, guided by the values instilled in us; decisions are sporadic, individual or group; our values show what really interests us and help us choose along the way (the opening case: a girl who keeps the change while her little sister watches).
**Answer: True**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** This lesson's seventh part teaches: across growth we win abilities that make us independent and productive, and the professional's mental skills must be trained constantly — flexibility and adaptation, accepting constructive criticism, the resolutive and propositive capacity that finds opportunities in unfavourable situations; confidence and personal motivation are themselves cultivable skills, with teamwork, working under pressure and planning goals, and every skill must be framed in the ethical and moral values of social life; valuing our own abilities and others' guides the choice of profession.
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: False — it also stands on Honduras's teacher guide(s), named in lesson/sources.md, as teacher-guide evidence.**
