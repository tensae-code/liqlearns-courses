# Decision Making and Autonomy — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 2 Mi poder de decisión — We can think and therefore decide what we want for our lives; decisions bring results we must assume, and even from mistakes we learn; autonomy is acting by our own thoughts, telling good from bad, just from unjust, right from wrong (Esther, 9, who decides to found a computer company) (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 1 Mi proyecto de vida — The person is a being with reason, self-awareness and their own identity, forming character through life; freedom lets us decide, and we are responsible for the consequences, positive or negative; responsibility is the value in our conscience that weighs the consequences of our acts; a life project sets short-, medium- and long-term goals (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 2 Decidiendo mi futuro — Personal decisions are determinations to solve situations or start projects, taken in family, school, friend-group and church contexts; Honduras's good future depends on each citizen deciding responsibly; competencies (knowledge, skills, dexterity) are acquired with effort and persistence, and decisions should come with autonomy and personal integrity — honesty in every act (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 2 Nuestras decisiones — A decision is a resolution taken about something, and decision-making is choosing between alternatives; society answers our behaviour with stimuli (public recognition for valued actions) or social sanctions (for actions valued negatively), which steer what we keep practising (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does the fifth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 3 Actuar con valores — Each person lives by their values — what they hold desirable or good; deliberation seeks not formal compliance but the wisest, most reasoned, most prudent decision, the one that most realises our values; tolerance inside a rule of law means respecting different ways of learning, capacities and cultural diversity, and prejudice is fed by stereotypes from media and family education (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** What does the sixth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 4 La importancia de saber decidir — Think, decide, act — decision-making is an essential life skill; a problem is recognised, reflected on, and alternatives weighed, guided by the values instilled in us; decisions are sporadic, individual or group; our values show what really interests us and help us choose along the way (the opening case: a girl who keeps the change while her little sister watches) (any close wording).**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** What does the seventh part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 2, teoría adicional — Across growth we win abilities that make us independent and productive, and the professional's mental skills must be trained constantly — flexibility and adaptation, accepting constructive criticism, the resolutive and propositive capacity that finds opportunities in unfavourable situations; confidence and personal motivation are themselves cultivable skills, with teamwork, working under pressure and planning goals, and every skill must be framed in the ethical and moral values of social life; valuing our own abilities and others' guides the choice of profession (any close wording).**

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** What does the eighth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way — Grade 3 is where the state appears in full — nationality by birth and registration, the Constitution as supreme law, the three powers with their real Honduran numbers (128 deputies, 15 magistrates, elections every four years), and citizens' social demands as claims the State is obliged to hear. Grade 5 teaches democracy as a continental story — Latin America's dictatorships and transitions, integral citizenship (political, civil, social), social movements from the 1954 banana strike onward — while the pupil's own rehearsal is the student government with its annual operating plan and suggestion box. Grade 6 completes primary civics with the world system — international organisations defined as voluntary associations of sovereign states, the UN and its specialised agencies with their real founding dates, and Honduras's own memberships and poverty figures (52.6% in poverty, World Bank 2018) as the home case. Grade 8 is the moral-and-legal year — moral conscience and dilemmas, the Anti-Bullying Law read article by article, the rule of law traced to 1789, and human rights taught through the Americas' own instruments (the American Declaration, the OAS, the anti-torture and forced-disappearance conventions). the final guide deepens the pupil book's rights cycle into doctrine and machinery — the UN treaty bodies and the three pillars, the ancient sources of rights from Hammurabi to the Iroquois constitution, the 1966 Covenants — and widens Bloque 4 from the pupil book's UN seat to Honduras's own regional house: the OAS, ODECA and SICA, and a foreign-relations history that ends at the 1992 Hague judgment and the 2014 Isla Conejo dispute. (any close wording).**
