# Video script — Government Social Programmes in the Municipality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Nicaragua Grade 3 Estudios Sociales G3 (printed p. 77), teaches: The national government's municipal welfare programmes (Amor para los más chiquitos, Usura Cero, Hambre Cero, Casas/Calles para el Pueblo, Plan Techo, school nutrition and school packs) as rights-restoring public action.

Nicaragua Grade 10 CCSS Geografía Módulo G10 (printed p. 111), teaches: The Government's emblematic social programmes (Hambre Cero, Usura Cero, Viviendas Dignas) and free universal health and education as restored rights under the Person, Family and Community model.

Nicaragua Grade 10 CCSS Geografía Módulo G10 (printed p. 115), teaches: The Hambre Cero productive food bond (pregnant cattle and pigs, poultry, seeds) given to rural families with the woman as partner and owner, for food security and sovereignty.

Nicaragua Grade 10 CCSS Geografía Módulo G10 (printed p. 116), teaches: Usura Cero — ten years of micro-credit for women opening small businesses, with repeat credits for successful partners.

Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year (printed p. 114), teaches: The Hambre Cero productive bond (pregnant cow and sow, poultry, seeds, building materials) delivered to poor families with the woman as partner-owner — over 50,000 cows and 600,000 birds distributed, women organised in 1,411 nuclei and 225 cooperatives — as the government's road out of poverty.

Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year (printed p. 115), teaches: The Usura Cero micro-credit programme for women, presented beside Hambre Cero as state social policy.

Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year (printed p. 151), teaches: The State's alternatives against national vulnerability — the National Human Development Plan to reduce poverty, support to the disaster-prevention system (SINAPRED), social programmes to eradicate poverty, and Civil Defence organisation.

Nicaragua's own way: community is taught through Rama Cay — an ancestral Caribbean island community governed by a council of elders — and citizenship through the current government's named municipal social programmes and the Caribbean Coast autonomy system. even the pure-Geography distance module stops to teach the State's hand in family welfare — free health and education as restored rights, and the named productive and credit programmes, each with the woman as the programme's named partner. the final secondary book presents anti-poverty policy as the State's first answer to national vulnerability — the National Human Development Plan and the named programmes, with women's cooperatives as their delivery form.
