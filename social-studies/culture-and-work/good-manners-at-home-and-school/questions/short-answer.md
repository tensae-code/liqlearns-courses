# Good Manners at Home and School — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Living politely — Good manners at the table, at home and with guests, and not disturbing others, as picture pairs of polite and rude behaviour (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Good manners at home — Politeness at home and in communication, down to saying thank you, taught with a poster the class makes (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Myanmar's way — the book's own teaching way — every value is taught as a named story or picture-sequence about one child (or one lion cub) plus a question set — never as a rule list; the patriotism chapter teaches love of country as the eight ethnic peoples in their own dress holding hands around the Independence Monument. Grade 6 is where the values course becomes a civics course — rights and responsibilities are named as such, the school has a council, rules are explained up to the rules of a country, and the last unit is peace: what conflict is, the bundle-of-firewood lesson, peace symbols, and rowing in unison. (any close wording).**
