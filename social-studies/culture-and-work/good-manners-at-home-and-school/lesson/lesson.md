# Good Manners at Home and School

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: good manners at the table, at home and with guests, and not disturbing others, as picture pairs of polite and rude behaviour — as Myanmar's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Living politely

Myanmar Morality and Civics, Grade 1 (printed p. 9), teaches: Good manners at the table, at home and with guests, and not disturbing others, as picture pairs of polite and rude behaviour. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Good manners at home

Myanmar Teacher Guide — Morality and Civic — Grade 6 (printed p. 14), teaches: Politeness at home and in communication, down to saying thank you, taught with a poster the class makes. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Myanmar's way — the book's own teaching way

Myanmar's own way: every value is taught as a named story or picture-sequence about one child (or one lion cub) plus a question set — never as a rule list; the patriotism chapter teaches love of country as the eight ethnic peoples in their own dress holding hands around the Independence Monument. Grade 6 is where the values course becomes a civics course — rights and responsibilities are named as such, the school has a council, rules are explained up to the rules of a country, and the last unit is peace: what conflict is, the bundle-of-firewood lesson, peace symbols, and rowing in unison. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
