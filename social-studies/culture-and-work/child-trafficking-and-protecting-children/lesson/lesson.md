## Sub lesson 1 — Child Trafficking and Protecting Children

Bhutan Class VI Social Studies VI, printed p. 35, teaches: child trafficking — moving children for forced labour or sexual use — its causes (poverty, oppression, missing rights and opportunities, war), its consequences (health harm, lost schooling, disease, depression), and prevention by community groups, rules and education, with the RBP, RENEW and NCWC named as the agencies fighting it. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bhutan's way — the book's own teaching way

Bhutan's own way: the primary course closes by putting the whole state in front of the child — what a government is, Bhutan's own change from monarchy to Democratic Constitutional Monarchy in 2008, its three branches told through the courts' own ladder and the kabney/patang ranks the King bestows, the Article 7 fundamental rights quoted from Bhutan's Constitution, and a social-problems chapter that names Bhutan's own helping agencies (Royal Bhutan Police, RENEW, NCWC) for every problem.
