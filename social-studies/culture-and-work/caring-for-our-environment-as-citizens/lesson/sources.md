**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Tanzania).**

Tanzania's own way (Standard Six, Civic and Moral Education Standard Six Pupil's Book): TIE teaches civics as a good-governance wheel (the cover itself: transparency, accountability, participation, human rights, integrity, efficiency, reconciliation, democracy, equality, rule of law around “GOOD GOVERNANCE”) and builds the state from the child's own levels — street, village, ward, district, region, nation — with the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar as the founding fact and Kiswahili, unity and humanism (utu) as named national treasures.

Tanzania's own way (Standard Five, Civic and Moral Education Standard Five Pupil's Book): Standard Five civics teaches the state from the ward up — Ward Development Committee, district council and its heads of departments, regional commissioner — and the nation through its symbols in detail (the emblem's thirteen signs, the shilling, the Uhuru torch first lit 9 December 1961, national festivals from Zanzibar Revolution Day to Farmers' Day); democracy is taught with political parties' duties and political tolerance, and peace as peaceful coexistence that condemns segregation on any ground.

**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Tanzania, Dominican Republic).**

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 3): at Grade 5 the projects turn constitutional — pupils explore the Dominican Constitution itself, meet the Legislative Power and their elected representatives, learn the child-protection Ley 136-03 as the law that protects them personally, and meet taxes as the citizen's contribution to collective wellbeing.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 9): the combined Grade 5 volume teaches the same constitutional content as the Moral Cívica guide but as social-science study — the Senate's exclusive attributions cited by article (Art. 80), the ayuntamiento's functions listed, and taxes argued in a group activity: "pagamos impuestos para que nuestro país se desarrolle".

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 17): the final volume teaches democracy by close-reading the country's own canon — Article 7 and Article 2 of the 2015 Constitution, a classic text on love of country, and Hostos's call for a rational government — and names the system's diseases honestly: party clientelism and support bought with favours "neither legal nor ethical".
