# State Institutions Public and Private

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the state stands on institutions that share its tasks to serve citizens and residents, in two kinds — public institutions, the state institutions (ministries such as Interior, Education, Health, Defence, Justice; authorities; government hospitals), and private, commercial institutions (companies, banks, factories, markets); state institutions are the government bodies charged with serving the homeland and the citizen — as Saudi Arabia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — State Institutions Public and Private

Saudi Arabia Grade 5 Social Studies (printed pp. 106–107), teaches: The state stands on institutions that share its tasks to serve citizens and residents, in two kinds — public institutions, the state institutions (ministries such as Interior, Education, Health, Defence, Justice; authorities; government hospitals), and private, commercial institutions (companies, banks, factories, markets); state institutions are the government bodies charged with serving the homeland and the citizen. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saudi Arabia's way — the book's own teaching way

Saudi Arabia's own way: the Grade 5 book teaches security and state institutions as service — security is defined first, the citizen is named the first line of security, the security agencies are listed one by one with their tasks, state institutions are split into public and private, and each region's Emir receives citizens and hears their needs.
