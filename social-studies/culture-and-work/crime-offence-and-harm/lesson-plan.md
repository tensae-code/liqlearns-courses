# Lesson plan — Crime Offence And Harm

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Crime Offence And Harm
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Mongolia, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Crime Offence And Harm — Kant's line as the motto — fair criticism is the best prevention of crime; the burglary case that opens it (B climbed through Zh's window twice, and the second time beat him when challenged — whose rights were violated, and what should each man have done?); the master distinction, drawn as a diagram: a legal violation (эрх зүйн зөрчил) splits into crime (гэмт хэрэг) — only what the Criminal Code names, answered by criminal liability — and the non-crime offences (administrative, property and disciplinary), with theft written out in both codes side by side (the Criminal Code of 2015 against the Offences Law of 2017, where the same taking without criminal marks is an administrative offence answered by compulsory training or arrest of seven to thirty days); the offence tables for copyright and for children's rights, with their fines in tögrög; harm itself analysed — гэм хор as the genus, damage (хохирол, the direct result) against loss (хор уршиг, what flows from it) — and the Civil Code's remedies by the kind of harm: to property (restore the thing, replace it alike, repair it, or pay), to honour (compensation assessed in money, an apology demanded, lost income restored), to health (lost wages and the costs of care), to life (funeral costs and support for those the dead person maintained); the first move when a crime is known — inform the police at once, for failing to report is itself an offence; and the lawyer's place — legal aid as a constitutional right, the Bar's register (2,738 licensed advocates in 2018), and the free state advocate for those who cannot pay (the 2013 law; 58 branches) — as Mongolia's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
