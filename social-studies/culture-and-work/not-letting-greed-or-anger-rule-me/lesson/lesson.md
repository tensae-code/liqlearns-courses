# Not Letting Greed Or Anger Rule Me

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: greed (шунал) always wants more and is never filled, like salt water drunk against thirst; anger and pride (уур омог) are a coal in the mind that burns its holder first — the angry person suffers more than the one they rage at; and the counter-practice: each pupil writes their own "rule" (өөрийн дүрэм) — a personal law in proverbs of their own making ("right thought, right word, right deed") — and follows it from today — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Section 1.1, greed and anger units

Mongolia Civic Ethics Grade 6, printed p. 17, teaches: Greed (шунал) always wants more and is never filled, like salt water drunk against thirst; anger and pride (уур омог) are a coal in the mind that burns its holder first — the angry person suffers more than the one they rage at; and the counter-practice: each pupil writes their own "rule" (өөрийн дүрэм) — a personal law in proverbs of their own making ("right thought, right word, right deed") — and follows it from today. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: from Grade 6 the civics subject changes its name and its method — no longer "Civic Education" but "Civic Ethics" (Иргэний ёс зүйн боловсрол), a self-observation workbook in the Buddhist-inflected Mongolian moral tradition: the pupil keeps a diary of their own acts of body, speech and mind, and ethics is practised as a daily discipline before it is ever a rule about others. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
