# Fairness and Treating Everyone Equally

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: never treating anyone differently or leaving them out — bullying and exclusion examined from both sides — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — 分けへだてをしない (Do not shut anyone out)

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 178), teaches: Never treating anyone differently or leaving them out — bullying and exclusion examined from both sides. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — (2) 公正、公平な態度で (With a fair and just attitude)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 132), teaches: Men and women, children and the old — every person equally an irreplaceable human; how biased views arise. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — 読みもの「愛の日記」("Ai's Diary")

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 136), teaches: Discrimination through the diary of a girl whose Vietnamese classmate is lonely and a man abused for "not being Japanese" (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Japan's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 5 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
