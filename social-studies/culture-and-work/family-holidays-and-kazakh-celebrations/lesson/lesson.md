# Family Holidays and Kazakh Celebrations

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: family holidays as special days a family prepares and celebrates together — Kazakh hospitality round the dastarkhan, the Tusau keser first-steps rite, Family Day in September, and the Nauryz festival with its national games and seven-ingredient dish — as Kazakhstan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Family Holidays and Kazakh Celebrations

Kazakhstan Grade 1 Poznanie mira (919/929) (printed p. 33), teaches: a holiday as a special day a family prepares and celebrates together; Kazakh hospitality round the dastarkhan, the Tusau keser rite that cuts the bonds when a baby takes its first steps, and Kazakhstan's Family Day in September. Kazakhstan Grade 2 Poznanie mira NIS (1031/1019) (printed p. 66), teaches: Nauryz ("new day") on 22 March, the spring equinox, honoured as the Great Day of the People — a state holiday and holiday of unity, a symbol of spring renewal, love, fertility and friendship, with national games (baiga, asyki, kazaksha kures) and the main dish nauryzkozhe cooked from seven ingredients. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kazakhstan's way — the books' own teaching way

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 1): the primary course opens inside the child's own circles — class, family, school rules — and only then widens to the state: independence is taught as a checklist of a real state (territory, people, president and government, constitution, symbols, capital, tenge), and Kazakh family culture (the Tusau keser first-steps rite, the dastarkhan, hospitality) carries the family theme. Kazakhstan's own way (Book 3): the NIS telling names things the Atamura book leaves general — citizenship by birth with rights and duties quoted from the Constitution itself (including Article 34's duty to respect the state symbols), the Kazakh custom of asar (the whole village doing unpaid work too heavy for one person) set beside modern volunteering, and the school collective drawn as a full staff chart from director to janitor. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
