# Similar and Different But Equal

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: children differ by sex, looks, age, abilities and interests — a classmate in a wheelchair plays and learns alongside — yet all children are future people with the same rights, and difference is a wealth to respect, not a reason for anger — as Serbia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Слични и различити (Similar and different)

Serbia Grade 1 Свет око нас 1 (printed pp. 10–11), teaches: children differ by sex, looks, age, abilities and interests — a classmate in a wheelchair plays and learns alongside — yet all children are future people with the same rights, and difference is a wealth to respect, not a reason for anger. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Serbia's way — the books' own teaching way

Serbia's own way: the integrated subject opens with the child herself (me, my feelings, my family) and widens circle by circle — family, home, neighbours, school, street — with rights taught from the first year as inseparable from duties ("every right seeks a duty"), and Serbia's own school slava, Saint Sava's Day on 27 January, taught as every school's feast. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
