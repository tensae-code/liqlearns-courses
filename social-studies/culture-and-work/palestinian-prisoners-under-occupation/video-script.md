The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2, printed p. 59, teaches: from the British Mandate to today, hundreds of thousands of Palestinians have been imprisoned, under the harshest oppression, torture and humiliation; Britain built prisons such as Sarafand and Acre, and after the occupations of 1948 and 1967 the occupation built prisons from north to south — Ashkelon, Ramla, Ofer, the Negev desert prison and others — and practised physical and psychological torture on Palestinian prisoners: beating, the shabeh stress position, electric shocks, sleep deprivation, solitary confinement, banning visits, and medical neglect that killed dozens of prisoners inside the prisons, besides fines, administrative detention, and arresting women and children; in 1974 the PLO made 17 April of every year the Palestinian Prisoner's Day, a national day of loyalty to the prisoners and their sacrifices and of support for their right to freedom.

Palestine's own way: human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy.
