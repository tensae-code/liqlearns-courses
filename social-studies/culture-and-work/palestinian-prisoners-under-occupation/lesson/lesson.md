# Palestinian Prisoners Under Occupation

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: from the British Mandate to today, hundreds of thousands of Palestinians have been imprisoned, under the harshest oppression, torture and humiliation; Britain built prisons such as Sarafand and Acre, and after the occupations of 1948 and 1967 the occupation built prisons from north to south — Ashkelon, Ramla, Ofer, the Negev desert prison and others — and practised physical and psychological torture on Palestinian prisoners: beating, the shabeh stress position, electric shocks, sleep deprivation, solitary confinement, banning visits, and medical neglect that killed dozens of prisoners inside the prisons, besides fines, administrative detention, and arresting women and children; in 1974 the PLO made 17 April of every year the Palestinian Prisoner's Day, a national day of loyalty to the prisoners and their sacrifices and of support for their right to freedom — as Palestine's book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Palestinian Prisoners Under Occupation

Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2 (printed p. 59), teaches: from the British Mandate to today, hundreds of thousands of Palestinians have been imprisoned, under the harshest oppression, torture and humiliation; Britain built prisons such as Sarafand and Acre, and after the occupations of 1948 and 1967 the occupation built prisons from north to south — Ashkelon, Ramla, Ofer, the Negev desert prison and others — and practised physical and psychological torture on Palestinian prisoners: beating, the shabeh stress position, electric shocks, sleep deprivation, solitary confinement, banning visits, and medical neglect that killed dozens of prisoners inside the prisons, besides fines, administrative detention, and arresting women and children; in 1974 the PLO made 17 April of every year the Palestinian Prisoner's Day, a national day of loyalty to the prisoners and their sacrifices and of support for their right to freedom. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2): human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
