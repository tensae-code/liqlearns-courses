# Justice and Fairness

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: justice — giving everyone their due, in the family, the courts, trade and the school — as Libya's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Justice

Libya Grade 5 National Education G5 (printed p. 33), teaches: justice is a supreme value: giving every right-holder their due without regard to colour, sex or nature; justice in the family, the courts, trade and the school — including the extra exam time a pupil with Down syndrome needs, which is justice, not favour (Convention on the Rights of the Child art. 2). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Libya's way — the book's own teaching way

Libya's own way: Grade 5 is a pure values book — eight lessons, each a single civic virtue (equality, justice, solidarity, tolerance, accepting the other, dialogue), each anchored in a real instrument a child can hold: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the Convention on the Rights of the Child, UNESCO's 1995 Declaration on Tolerance, and the Libyan Scouts' "A World of Peace for Children" charter, which the pupil signs. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
