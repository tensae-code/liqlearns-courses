# Video script — Human Rights and Peaceful Living

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

South Sudan Primary 2 P2 Social Studies SB, printed p. 76, teaches: good citizens support human rights; deciding well promotes unity, promotes peace and supports human rights — pictured as a police officer stopping a fight and children holding a "promote peace to protect human rights" banner.

South Sudan Primary 2 P2 Social Studies TG, printed p. 51, teaches: the guide's own examples — police show support for human rights by arresting a criminal instead of leaving him to mob justice, and people support human rights by giving relief food to those hit by drought and floods; our decisions determine our relationship with other people (the human-rights lesson is filed NEW from Book 3 in this wave).

South Sudan Primary 5 P5 Social Studies SB, printed p. 57, teaches: human rights are the rights everyone is entitled to; respecting them and gender equality promotes security, justice and peace and unity, protects the rights of people with special needs, and ensures equal distribution of resources — pictured by a suspect being rescued from mob justice (the human-rights lesson is filed NEW from Book 3 in this wave).

South Sudan Primary 6 P6 Social Studies SB, printed p. 54, teaches: human rights are basic rules for fair treatment of all human beings, and every human being has a duty to respect them — the book lists the right to equality, a fair trial and life, and the freedoms of association, assembly, speech and religion, and sets a recited pledge to respect and stand up for others' rights (the human-rights lesson is filed NEW from Book 3 in this wave).

South Sudan Primary 8 P8 Social Studies SB, printed p. 69, teaches: equity is the equal and just distribution of resources, opportunities and power; human rights are categorised as social (housing, freedom from hunger, security, education), civil (fair trial, vote, expression, information, association, movement), economic (property, trade, employment) and environmental rights; justice is fair treatment without discrimination by gender, race or religion, enforced through courts (the human-rights lesson is filed NEW from Book 3 in this wave).

South Sudan's own way: A community is defined by common interests across different traditions and languages, with peace promoted inside the definition itself; civic life appears at age 7 as a ballot box and a president's swearing-in photograph, and decision-making is taught as a citizen's skill that ends in human rights and peaceful living.
