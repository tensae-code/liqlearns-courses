# The Rule of Law — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: The long history of Chinese law from the first written codes through the Chinese legal tradition, and the new-era road — the Common Programme, the 1954 Constitution, the Chinese socialist legal system with the Constitution at its core, and the turn from 法制 (legal system) to 法治 (rule of law).
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: Building a rule-of-law country — a complete legal system under the Constitution, administration strictly according to law, impartial justice, and power supervised, so that state power runs within the law and citizens' rights are protected.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: What a rule-of-law government is — functions scientific, powers and duties fixed by law, enforcement strict, open and fair, clean and honest, trusted by the people — and how it is built through lawful decision-making, open government affairs and accountable administration.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: A rule-of-law society — law recognised and generally obeyed, public life orderly, rights relieved through law, disputes settled through mediation, arbitration and the courts, with public legal services and legal aid reaching everyone.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on China's student book, named in lesson/sources.md.**
