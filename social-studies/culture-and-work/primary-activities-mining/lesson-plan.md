# Lesson plan — Primary activities — mining

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Primary activities — mining
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 9 — Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md ladder: culture, economic activities, trade, and tax).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** mining — what minerals are, extraction as taking them out, and the factors that decide whether a deposit is dug at all
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title, at Grade 8 depth in the course's level ladder (levels, not grades).
A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What minerals are** — Minerals are naturally occurring chemical substances or compounds in or on the crust — solid, liquid or gas: non-metallic and metallic ores, petroleum, natural …
2. **What decides whether they are dug** — A mineral in the ground is not yet a mine. The factors that decide extraction: the deposit's size, its depth, its grade/quality, price, the level of technology,…

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a mineral is, with the book's named examples.
2. Say what extraction is.
3. Name the factors that decide whether a deposit is dug.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
