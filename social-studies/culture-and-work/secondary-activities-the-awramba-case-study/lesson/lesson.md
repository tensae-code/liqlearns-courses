# Secondary activities — the Awramba case study

## Do it first

Write the Awramba rule — everyone works to their ability and shares equally — and test it against one group you belong to: what would change if your group lived by it?

## Sub lesson 1 — A community that shares the work

This is a limited study, told as a summary. The **Awramba community** lies in **Fogera woreda, South Gondar — 8 km past Woreta on the Debre Tabor road**. It holds about **104 households and 388 family members**, and was founded in **1972 E.C. by Ato Zumra Nuru**.

Awramba practises **gender equality in fact**, holds **work as its creed**, and runs itself by its own rules through its **farmers', craft and multi-purpose cooperative**: **everyone works to their ability and shares equally**, the able supporting the weak, so that economic change includes all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Weaving taught, position secured

**Zumra taught his weaving skill to his followers.** Training followed, and the community's **weaving industry grew** until its members' **economic position was secured**. A cottage craft, taught person to person and organised in a cooperative, carried a whole community.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Place Awramba and give its size and founder. Say its three marks: gender equality in fact, work as its creed, equal sharing through its cooperative. Tell how weaving secured the community's position.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the Awramba rule — everyone works to their ability and shares equally — and test it against one group you belong to: what would change if your group lived by it?
2. Retell the Awramba story to a younger learner in five sentences, keeping place, founder and weaving.
