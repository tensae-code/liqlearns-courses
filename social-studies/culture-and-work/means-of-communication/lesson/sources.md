**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Angola).**

Angola's own way: Grade 2 widens each Grade 1 topic by one step — the family gains kinship names and needs, the school gains its parts and the care of school materials, and the book closes with two short themes that are pure community life: work, and transport and communications. Grade 3 is where Estudo do Meio turns outward — the child's own locality is inventoried place by place (school, hospital, market, bank, water fountain), and Angola's customs are taught region by region: dress, mother tongue, food, and festivals such as efico and circumcision celebrations. Grade 4 names the idea itself — instituições públicas: buildings that belong to no private person but serve everyone — and defines quality of life as a checklist a community can judge itself by (education, health, housing, food, hygiene, culture, leisure), ending on the shared duties of sanitation and water.
