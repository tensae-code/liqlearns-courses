India's own way (India Grade 6 Social and Political Life-I): Economics enters through lived livelihood stories — named workers (a landless labourer, a small farmer in debt to a trader, a rickshaw puller, a casual garment worker, a salaried manager) whose earnings, loans and job security are compared in tables.

India's own way (India Grade 7 Social and Political Life-II): Markets are taught as a chain you can walk — from a cotton farmer tied to a trader's loan, through ginning, spinning, putting-out weavers and an export factory, to a $26 shirt in a US store, with the money split counted at every step.

India's own way (India Grade 9 Economics): The whole first Economics course is built on Indian cases — a fictional UP village for production, two boys (Sakal who studies, Vilas who cannot) for human capital, Tendulkar poverty-line numbers, and the FCI/PDS machinery for food security.

India's own way (India Grade 10 Understanding Economic Development): Development economics taught entirely on Indian data and institutions — per-capita and HDI comparisons with Sri Lanka and Kerala against Haryana, MGNREGA, Sonpur village credit, SEZs, and COPRA consumer courts.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 1): A full direct-approach course built on the circular flow, with Fiji examples throughout — ginger supply curves, Fiji import tables, quarry and land-rent income, the Attu family budget, Fiji credit facilities, sugar-industry production stages and Reserve Bank of Fiji policy tools.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Social Science student): the senior Social Science course turns practical — after ideologies it teaches the market economy and then personal finance (choosing financial services, savings, credit, consumer rights, fraud) as life skills.

Ecuador's own way (Ecuador Grade 13 Emprendimiento y Gestión): Economics enters the entrepreneurship course through the family and the small venture — costs are worked through a school-desk factory costed month by month in dollars, and every unit closes with a financial-education section that takes the same tools home to the family budget and the family's debts. Family debts are sorted by their use — good debts that "pay themselves" (land, a house, business premises, inventory, the children's education) against bad debts taken on for pleasure or unnecessary consumption (a new phone, unneeded clothing, a party).

Laos' own way (Laos Grade 11 Business Combined student): costs and capital are taught with full kip arithmetic — a machine bought for 120,000,000 kip is depreciated over its 5-year life at 24,000,000 kip a year, inside a printed table of asset lives (buildings 20 years at 5%, machinery 8-10 years, vehicles 5 years).

Laos' own way (Laos Grade 11 Business student): the market is defined three ways at once — everyday (a place to buy and sell), economic (the group of buyers and sellers who exchange), and marketing (people with a need and the money to pay) — and costs are drilled with the SMOC sorting exercise (Salary, Material, Other, Capital).


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 7 Technology and Entrepreneurship): Grade 7 registers a firm — the class founds a student company, chooses between sole trader, partnership and limited company by who answers with their own property, writes its founding budget, and scores its market chance before it sells anything.
