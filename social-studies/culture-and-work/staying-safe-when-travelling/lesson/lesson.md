# Staying Safe When Travelling

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the road-crossing drill (look right, look left, look right again; cross quickly when clear) and walking on the right-hand side of the road — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kerb Drill

Guyana Grade 1 Social Studies For Our Children Book 1 (printed p. 30), teaches: The road-crossing drill (look right, look left, look right again; cross quickly when clear) and walking on the right-hand side of the road. Guyana Grade 2 Social Studies For Our Children Book 2 (printed p. 26), teaches: River-travel safety — keeping away from sandbanks and rocks and reading the warnings buoys give. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Nationally Celebrated Weeks: Road Safety Week

Guyana Grade 4 Social Studies For Our Children Book 4 (printed p. 52 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 1 above)), teaches: Every October the Police Traffic Department reminds drivers, cyclists and pedestrians to use the road with care; Schools' Safety Patrols, talks, rallies and leaflets. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 5 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: a story-led first book built around two children, Ron and Sue — family, school, rules and nation are all met through their home, street and school day, with a draw/name/complete task on almost every page. Book 2 widens Book 1's world — the same Ron and Sue now meet family types, class rules, neighbourhood shops and, for the first time, the country's leaders (President, Prime Minister, Ministers) and its national days (Republic Day, May Day, Enmore Martyrs' Day, Freedom Day). Book 4 turns from "my community" to "our homeland" — Guyana is taught as six peoples who each brought a way of life, and citizenship is taught through honour (National Awards) and shared observance (Road Safety, Fire Prevention and Youth Weeks) before it is taught through rules. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
