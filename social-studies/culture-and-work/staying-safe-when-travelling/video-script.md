# Video script — Staying Safe When Travelling

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Guyana Grade 1 Social Studies For Our Children Book 1 (printed p. 30), teaches: The road-crossing drill (look right, look left, look right again; cross quickly when clear) and walking on the right-hand side of the road.

Guyana Grade 2 Social Studies For Our Children Book 2 (printed p. 25 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 1 above)), teaches: Obeying safety rules when travelling — bicycles with bells, riding on the left-hand side of the road.

Guyana Grade 2 Social Studies For Our Children Book 2 (printed p. 26), teaches: River-travel safety — keeping away from sandbanks and rocks and reading the warnings buoys give.

Guyana Grade 4 Social Studies For Our Children Book 4 (printed p. 52 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 1 above)), teaches: Every October the Police Traffic Department reminds drivers, cyclists and pedestrians to use the road with care; Schools' Safety Patrols, talks, rallies and leaflets.

Guyana's own way: a story-led first book built around two children, Ron and Sue — family, school, rules and nation are all met through their home, street and school day, with a draw/name/complete task on almost every page. Book 2 widens Book 1's world — the same Ron and Sue now meet family types, class rules, neighbourhood shops and, for the first time, the country's leaders (President, Prime Minister, Ministers) and its national days (Republic Day, May Day, Enmore Martyrs' Day, Freedom Day). Book 4 turns from "my community" to "our homeland" — Guyana is taught as six peoples who each brought a way of life, and citizenship is taught through honour (National Awards) and shared observance (Road Safety, Fire Prevention and Youth Weeks) before it is taught through rules.
