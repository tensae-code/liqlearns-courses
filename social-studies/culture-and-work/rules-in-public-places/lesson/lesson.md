# Rules in Public Places

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: relations of respect and courtesy with classmates, teachers, family and neighbours; obeying the school regulations; and courtesy in transport — giving your seat to the elderly, to pregnant women and to mothers with newborns, not speaking loudly, not writing on or scratching the walls — as Cuba's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rules in Public Places

Cuba Grade 3 El mundo en que vivimos (printed pp. 236–237), teaches: Relations of respect and courtesy with classmates, teachers, family and neighbours; obeying the school regulations; and courtesy in transport — giving your seat to the elderly, to pregnant women and to mothers with newborns, not speaking loudly, not writing on or scratching the walls. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cuba's way — the book's own teaching way

Cuba's own way: third-graders join Nature Protection Brigades — organised pioneer groups that clean and care for the classroom, school and community — and the book's closing civics chapter binds courtesy, the patriotic symbols and the traffic law into one code of “comportamiento social”. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
