## Sub lesson 6 — United Arab Emirates' way — the books' own teaching way

United Arab Emirates' own way: at Grade 11 the moral strand is taught as one man's method — the whole reader is built on Sheikh Zayed's "ethical approach" (النهج الأخلاقي): values are never listed abstractly for long, they are proven from stands he took — liberating Kuwait, refusing the Iraq embargo's harm, and reconciling Egypt and Libya in a single sitting in 1974. at six years old the course already runs on two rails — the working country (how we travel, the jobs our grandfathers did, the job I will do) and the inherited country (Zayed the father, the days we love, our heritage) — and every lesson plan is required to name the citizenship concepts it builds: belonging, citizenship, social responsibility, cooperation, national identity. the volume closes the year with identity — a whole unit on Zayed as "an identity and witnessed stances", in which the Emirati woman's changing place in society and the citizen's social responsibility are taught as parts of that identity. for seven-year-olds the state is a person first — the whole identity unit hangs on the founding builder: who Zayed was and what he built, then what "my identity" is made of (down to the child's own identity papers), then the child's share: my homeland is my responsibility. the term pairs the world outside (Asia's great states) with the heritage inside — the founder's civilising work, the country's material and moral inheritance, and the Emirati school itself studied as one of the nation's own institutions. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
