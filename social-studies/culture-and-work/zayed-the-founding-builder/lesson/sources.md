**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (United Arab Emirates).** The teacher-guide parts named above are teacher-guide evidence only; United Arab Emirates is counted here on its student book part.

United Arab Emirates' own way (Zayed's Ethical Approach, G11 Term 1 reader): at Grade 11 the moral strand is taught as one man's method — the whole reader is built on Sheikh Zayed's "ethical approach" (النهج الأخلاقي): values are never listed abstractly for long, they are proven from stands he took — liberating Kuwait, refusing the Iraq embargo's harm, and reconciling Egypt and Libya in a single sitting in 1974. That way is stocked here as the way United Arab Emirates teaches this thing.

United Arab Emirates' own way (Social Studies & National Education, G1 teacher guide, volume 2) (teacher guide — evidence): at six years old the course already runs on two rails — the working country (how we travel, the jobs our grandfathers did, the job I will do) and the inherited country (Zayed the father, the days we love, our heritage) — and every lesson plan is required to name the citizenship concepts it builds: belonging, citizenship, social responsibility, cooperation, national identity. That evidence is stocked here as the way United Arab Emirates teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

United Arab Emirates' own way (Social Studies & National Education, G9 teacher guide, volume 2) (teacher guide — evidence): the volume closes the year with identity — a whole unit on Zayed as "an identity and witnessed stances", in which the Emirati woman's changing place in society and the citizen's social responsibility are taught as parts of that identity. That evidence is stocked here as the way United Arab Emirates teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

United Arab Emirates' own way (Social Studies & National Education, G2 teacher guide, volume 2) (teacher guide — evidence): for seven-year-olds the state is a person first — the whole identity unit hangs on the founding builder: who Zayed was and what he built, then what "my identity" is made of (down to the child's own identity papers), then the child's share: my homeland is my responsibility. That evidence is stocked here as the way United Arab Emirates teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

United Arab Emirates' own way (Social Studies & National Education, G6 teacher guide, volume 2) (teacher guide — evidence): the term pairs the world outside (Asia's great states) with the heritage inside — the founder's civilising work, the country's material and moral inheritance, and the Emirati school itself studied as one of the nation's own institutions.
