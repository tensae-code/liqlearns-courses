# Zayed the Founding Builder — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of the lesson teaches: the values the chapter itself names — equality, cohesion and solidarity, joint Arab action, sacrifice, justice — shown in action: standing with Kuwait until its liberation and return to the GCC's march, the October 1973 war stand, and refusing to let the embargo on Iraq starve its people while rejecting its occupation of Kuwait
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of the lesson teaches: Sheikh Zayed as the father of the nation — the founder whose portrait opens the school year, presented to first-graders as the country's father and the source of its values of giving and building
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of the lesson teaches: how the environment shaped the Emirati woman's character in the old days; the roles women played before the Union and valuing them; the main factors that changed women's social place after the Union; and the roles Emirati women play today — a society's change read through its women
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of the lesson teaches: Sheikh Zayed as the ruler of Al Ain — his efforts in Al Ain and Abu Dhabi before the Union and after it; his roles and responsibilities as President of the state; the Union proclaimed in 1971 of seven emirates; the state's emblem, the falcon (الصقر); and what he built for the people — schools, hospitals and housing — the founder learned as the homeland's builder
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This part of the lesson teaches: the founding builder's life in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi and his role in building the Union; his role in developing the state and guaranteeing the people's welfare (رفاهية الشعب); good citizenship (المواطنة الصالحة) explained as holding fast to virtuous values and principles; and the world appreciation (التقدير العالمي) the founder won in the field of the environment
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This part of the lesson teaches: at Grade 11 the moral strand is taught as one man's method — the whole reader is built on Sheikh Zayed's "ethical approach" (النهج الأخلاقي): values are never listed abstractly for long, they are proven from stands he took — liberating Kuwait, refusing the Iraq embargo's harm, and reconciling Egypt and Libya in a single sitting in 1974. at six years old the course already runs on two rails — the working country (how we travel, the jobs our grandfathers did, the job I will do) and the inherited country (Zayed the father, the days we love, our heritage) — and every lesson plan is required to name the citizenship concepts it builds: belonging, citizenship, social responsibility, cooperation, national identity. the volume closes the year with identity — a whole unit on Zayed as "an identity and witnessed stances", in which the Emirati woman's changing place in society and the citizen's social responsibility are taught as parts of that identity. for seven-year-olds the state is a person first — the whole identity unit hangs on the founding builder: who Zayed was and what he built, then what "my identity" is made of (down to the child's own identity papers), then the child's share: my homeland is my responsibility. the term pairs the world outside (Asia's great states) with the heritage inside — the founder's civilising work, the country's material and moral inheritance, and the Emirati school itself studied as one of the nation's own institutions.
**Answer: True**
