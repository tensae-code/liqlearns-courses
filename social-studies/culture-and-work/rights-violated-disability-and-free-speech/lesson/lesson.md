# Rights Violated Disability And Free Speech

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: how rights fail in real life — from Mongolia's 2010 report to the UN: people with disabilities are shut out of information, culture and sport (television news without sign-language translation), out of theatres and cinemas whose buildings have no access, out of public transport with no provision — so their rights to education, work and a full social life are violated together, and many leave their home districts for the capital; and how rights hang together — freedom of expression (speech, writing and publishing, art, peaceful assembly) is bound to the rights to education, health and work, which is why regimes burn books: the Qin emperor's burning of Confucius's books in 213 BC and the Nazi book-burning in Berlin in 1933, answered today by Germany's Day of the Book; the media's four duties — informing, forming citizens' views, overseeing and criticising, and educating — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 9 is where the spiral turns civic — communication first (how people actually reach each other), then rights as a working system of state duties and citizen duties, then the state itself: how a law is made, how a cabinet decides, how a court sits, and the seven doors through which a citizen walks into all three.
