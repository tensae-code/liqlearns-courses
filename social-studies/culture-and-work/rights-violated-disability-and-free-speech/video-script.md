# Video script — Rights Violated Disability And Free Speech

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Grade 9 Social Studies, printed p. 72, teaches: How rights fail in real life — from Mongolia's 2010 report to the UN: people with disabilities are shut out of information, culture and sport (television news without sign-language translation), out of theatres and cinemas whose buildings have no access, out of public transport with no provision — so their rights to education, work and a full social life are violated together, and many leave their home districts for the capital; and how rights hang together — freedom of expression (speech, writing and publishing, art, peaceful assembly) is bound to the rights to education, health and work, which is why regimes burn books: the Qin emperor's burning of Confucius's books in 213 BC and the Nazi book-burning in Berlin in 1933, answered today by Germany's Day of the Book; the media's four duties — informing, forming citizens' views, overseeing and criticising, and educating.

Mongolia's own way (Book 7): Grade 9 is where the spiral turns civic — communication first (how people actually reach each other), then rights as a working system of state duties and citizen duties, then the state itself: how a law is made, how a cabinet decides, how a court sits, and the seven doors through which a citizen walks into all three.
