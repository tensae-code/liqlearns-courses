# What Society is and the Golden Rule of Politeness

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: society as all the people around you, joined by common rules, customs and goals; etiquette and politeness as society's rules, crowned by the golden rule — treat others as you wish to be treated — as Kazakhstan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What Society is and the Golden Rule of Politeness

Kazakhstan Grade 2 Poznanie mira (970/980) (printed p. 12), teaches: society as all the people around you, joined by common rules of behaviour, customs, work, study and rest; society's rules are called etiquette (giving up a seat to elders, helping an elderly person cross, standing when the teacher enters), crowned by the golden rule of politeness — treat others as you wish to be treated — and care for public property as a child's own contribution. Kazakhstan Grade 2 Poznanie mira NIS (1031/1019) (printed p. 10), teaches: politeness as behaving so that others find it pleasant to be with you — respecting elders, not interrupting, always greeting; a father's example (never sitting at table before grandfather, seating everyone in the bus before himself, greeting the neighbour grandfather first) and the honoured place at the family table. Kazakhstan Grade 3 Poznanie mira (992/999) (printed p. 82), teaches: society as a union of people with common interests and goals; from birth a person lives in society — family, school class, college or university group, work colleagues, friends, neighbours — and everywhere among people the question is how to behave: the norms of behaviour accepted in public places, whose basis is the eternal spiritual values, with scenes to judge (in a shop, theatre, bus, library). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kazakhstan's way — the books' own teaching way

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 2): Grade 2 turns the Grade 1 circles outward — the child meets "society" itself, etiquette as society's rules and the golden rule of politeness, then rights and duties read straight out of the Constitution, and service to society taught through volunteers and four named Kazakh figures (Abai, Akhmet Baitursynov, Kanysh Satpaev, Bauyrzhan Momyshuly). Kazakhstan's own way (Book 3): the NIS telling names things the Atamura book leaves general — citizenship by birth with rights and duties quoted from the Constitution itself (including Article 34's duty to respect the state symbols), the Kazakh custom of asar (the whole village doing unpaid work too heavy for one person) set beside modern volunteering, and the school collective drawn as a full staff chart from director to janitor. Kazakhstan's own way (Book 4): Grade 3 gives the child the Constitution itself — its preamble read aloud ("We, the people of Kazakhstan…"), the citizen's rights drawn as a diagram and Constitution Day named — and roots the family in the Kazakh shezhire, the genealogy every Kazakh was expected to know to the seventh generation (Zheti ata). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
