# Culture — what culture is

## Do it first

List five things from one ordinary day at home — a greeting, a food, a tool, a song, a rule — and sort each under the guide's parts list: symbol, language, religion, belief, norm, value or artefact.

## Sub lesson 1 — A whole way of life

**Culture is the way of life of a society or a people.** Nothing human stands outside it. It is a wide idea: it holds beauty, growth, experience, invention, language, art and traditions.

Culture is **not found in nature**. People make it, learn it and accumulate it through history: ways of working, living and speaking, the material things people make, and the spiritual values they hold. And culture **grows and widens** — it does not sit still.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its parts (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide names culture's parts in a list (teacher guide — evidence): **symbols, language, religion, beliefs, norms, values and artefacts**. Hold the list against your own community: each part is something you can point to in a real day.

[Picture 11527 — three arrows into one child: teaching, imitation and a group passing a custom on]

## Sub lesson 3 — How culture passes on (teacher guide — evidence)

Culture is not born in a person — it is **passed on socially** (teacher guide — evidence). It develops from changes in socially transmitted beliefs, knowledge, customs, skills, attitudes and languages, and it shapes people's behaviour as they acquire it from other members of society through **teaching, imitation and other forms of social transmission**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: Say what culture is in one sentence, in your own words. Name culture's parts from the guide's list (teacher guide — evidence). Explain how a child acquires culture — teaching, imitation and social transmission (teacher guide — evidence).

## Do It Yourself

1. List five things from one ordinary day at home — a greeting, a food, a tool, a song, a rule — and sort each under the guide's parts list: symbol, language, religion, belief, norm, value or artefact.
2. Watch a younger child learn one custom — a greeting, a coffee step, a game rule. Write whether they learned it by being taught, by imitating, or by the group passing it on.
3. Ask an elder: what is one thing people do today that their grandparents did not? That is culture growing and widening.
