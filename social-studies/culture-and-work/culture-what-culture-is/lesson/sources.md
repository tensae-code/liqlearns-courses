They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs, its activity numbers key to its own companion textbook, and whole student-book topics have no guide counterpart. Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): The Grade 6 book is civics-led (government, elections, courts, constitution, finance ministry) but opens the primary course's widest geography: the world's continents and oceans, political and physical maps, global warming and natural disasters, human resources and the job market, worldwide transport and communication through Nassau's ports of entry, and a closing cultural comparison of The Bahamas with Africa, Australia, Brazil and Japan.

Zimbabwe's own way (ECD A) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum organised by cross-cutting topics repeated at every level (Identity, National history, Heritage, Work and leisure, Transport and communication, Shelter, Global issues…); teaching is play-, song- and role-play-based with workbook colouring/cutting tasks; local examples throughout (totems, pada hopscotch, veld fires, Zimbabwe outline map). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (ECD B) (teacher guide — evidence): Same HBC topic spiral as ECD A, one step deeper — transport starts from traditional/indigenous means (walking, animal-drawn) before modern vehicles; heritage sorted hands-on into animals / plants (baobab, maize) / artefacts (ngoma, hosho, mbira). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 1) (teacher guide — evidence): From Grade 1 the guide is unit-numbered (41 units across the same 14 HBC topics) with suggested answers to the Learner's Book; geography grows outward from home/school — my country and its ethnic groups and neighbours (South Africa, Zambia, Botswana), then transport, shelter, and Zimbabwe-specific hazards (veldfires, water disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 2) (teacher guide — evidence): The spiral widens from family to community — ethnic groups and languages of Zimbabwe (Kalanga, Sotho and others), production in the community, shelter types in the neighbourhood (granary, silos, community hall, clinic), and global issues introduced as floods and deforestation. Colombia's own way (CS_Grado3_02): Grade 3's second cartilla is the culture-and-coexistence cartilla (the grade's space/territory content sits in Cartilla 1); culture is taught as expressions, myths, customs and traditions of the child's own community.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T1): Grade 8 widens the region a second time — from the Arab homeland (Grade 7) to the whole Islamic world — and reverses the balance: two History-led units on the Islamic state's rise and civilization come first, then two full Geography units (the natural environment; its effect on economy and society), a Civics unit on law in Kuwait, and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait". Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/religion — Unit 1, a historical glimpse of the Islamic state and its civilization: the heavenly religions and the Arab world's old civilizations, printed pp. 14–15; the rise of the Islamic state and the conquests movement, pp. 16–18; the concept and foundations of Islamic civilization, pp. 19–22 (the culture definition is placed below); its characteristics — faith and work, universality, tolerance, moderation — pp. 23–24. Civics/History — Unit 2 Lesson 1, the political and administrative manifestations: the caliphate, the ministry, the governorship, the diwans and the police, pp. 30–32. History of science — Unit 2 Lesson 4, the social, cultural and scientific manifestations: society's classes by profession, the status of women, the people of the covenant in farming, industry and trade, the scientific centres (the two Holy Mosques, Al-Azhar, Baghdad's House of Wisdom) and the translation of Muslim scholars' works in Europe, pp. 40–43 (the al-Idrisi map part is placed below). Civics — Unit 5, law as the basis of stability and building society in Kuwait: the definition, rules and aims of law; law and morality; the characteristics of the legal rule, pp. 85–96. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": the founding of Al-Mubarakiya school in 1911, Kuwait's first regular school, pp. 100–101; the etiquette of replies, pp. 102–103; the fragrance of neighbourliness, pp. 104–105; "What do you say, folks?" — consultation (shura) under Sheikh Salem Al-Mubarak, pp. 106–107 (the customs part is placed below).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G5 SS T2): Term 2 teaches the economy by sectors, then location skills (relative/absolute location, GPS) and Jordan's Arab world region by region — Nile Valley and Horn of Africa, then the Maghreb.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 6 Social Studies): Grade 6 turns to Bhutan's own climate zones, population and signature environment topics — hydropower, urbanisation and glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF) — a hazard set specific to a Himalayan kingdom.
