# Hands-on — Using the Internet Safely

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## I understand the benefits and dangers of the internet
Work through this part with your hands: Morocco Grade 5 Dalil Moufid Ijtiamiat (Teacher Guide), printed p. 156, teaches: what the internet is and how children use it; its benefits and its dangers; safe use by a vulnerable age group — recognising unsafe situations, protecting oneself online, and proposing a dialogue between two children about protection from internet dangers. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## I help protect my private life
Work through this part with your hands: Morocco Grade 6 Al-Massar Ijtiamiat, printed p. 128, teaches: what in internet space can threaten a child's private life (oversharing, strangers, published photos and data); the rights and duties that go with it — my right that adults (parents and teachers) not expose my data, my duty not to publish pictures or personal information without permission, to think before clicking, to protect my accounts and passwords, to respect others and report what troubles me; the story of a pupil whose Facebook account was kept by her mother after a quarrel spread it through the school; and Morocco's own data-protection commission (CNDP) and its children's project. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Morocco's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: Morocco's own way: Moroccan primary Social Studies is one integrated subject (History + Geography + Civic Education) built around "my homeland"; its civics third is a full civic-education programme in its own right — civic behaviour defined as the citizen's relation to society and to the state through its institutions, civic values (freedom, dignity, solidarity, equality, tolerance, rejecting discrimination) learned by practice not memorisation, and the classroom itself run as a small democracy (a negotiated class charter and an elected class cooperative with ballot box and vote count). Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Morocco's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: Morocco's own way: the Grade 6 civics course is a working apprenticeship in democracy — it opens with law, freedom and responsibility (Rousseau: no freedom without responsibility), runs the class as a republic (charter, elected cooperative), then walks out of the school gate: local democracy is learned by studying the elected communal council from the 2011 Constitution (Articles 135 and 139), visiting the commune headquarters, simulating a full council meeting with agenda, president, budget rapporteur and association representatives, and finally pleading a real solidarity proposal before that council. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
