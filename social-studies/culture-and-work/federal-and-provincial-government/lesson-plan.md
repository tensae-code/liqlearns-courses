# Lesson plan — Federal and Provincial Government

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Culture and Work · **Lesson:** Federal and Provincial Government
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Work (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Social Studies remaining-strands expansion (Pakistan, 2026-11-17).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ch. 3 Federal and Provincial Government, Ch. 3 Need of Federal Government; Comparison of the Formation of Government at Federal, Provincial and Local Level, Ch. 3 Federal Structure Under the 1973 Constitution: President and Prime Minister, Ch. 5 The Federal Executive and Important Officials, Ch. 3 Need of Federal Government; Formation of Government at Federal, Provincial and Local Level — teaches: where a country is large and its people speak many languages, government is shared — one central (federal) government for the whole country and provincial governments for the parts; Pakistan's 1973 Constitution divides powers between the two; Pakistan's four provinces, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir cannot be run from one desk — the federal government is formed by the National Assembly and Senate under the President and Prime Minister, the provincial by its assembly under the Chief Minister; the President as elected head of state acting on the Prime Minister's advice, and the Prime Minister as head of the federal government, chosen by and accountable to the National Assembly, holding the executive powers of legislation, budget and defence; how the federation actually runs — President, Prime Minister and Cabinet, and the secretariat machine beneath them (ministries, divisions, attached departments, the Secretary as administrative head) — with provincial and district governments allotted the local matters in the chapter's own introduction; Pakistan is a federation of four provinces with AJK, Gilgit Baltistan and the capital territory; the federal government runs economy, defence and foreign affairs, and its structure is drawn whole — Legislature (Parliament: President, Senate, National Assembly), Executive (Prime Minister, Cabinet, administrative units), Judiciary (Supreme Court, Islamabad High Court) — as Pakistan's books teach it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
