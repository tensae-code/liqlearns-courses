# The October Revolution and the New State

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the 21 October 1969 Revolution as a question-and-answer catechism — there was no Supreme Revolutionary Council or new government in 1965; the Revolution was born on 21 October 1969; Jaalle Siyaad is president now; since he came, work, hospitals and schools have grown, hospitals became free, and the written Somali script spread — the child learns the new state as the bringer of change and progress — as Somalia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kacaanka (The Revolution)

Somalia Grade 1 Barbaarinta iyo Cilmiga Beesha (Teacher Guide) (printed p. 22), teaches: the 21 October 1969 Revolution as a question-and-answer catechism — there was no Supreme Revolutionary Council or new government in 1965; the Revolution was born on 21 October 1969; Jaalle Siyaad is president now; since he came, work, hospitals and schools have grown, hospitals became free, and the written Somali script spread — the child learns the new state as the bringer of change and progress. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dhalashadii Kacaanka Oktoobar (The Birth of the October Revolution)

Somalia Grade 3 Barbaarinta iyo Cilmiga Beesha (printed p. 5), teaches: how the Revolution was born — the corruption and division before it and the night of 21 October 1969. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Muxuu Qabtay Kacaanku? (What Did the Revolution Do?)

Somalia Grade 3 Barbaarinta iyo Cilmiga Beesha (printed p. 8), teaches: the Revolution's works as the proof of the new state — what it built and changed for the people. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Somalia's way — the books' own teaching way

Somalia's own way (Grade 1): this subject was created by the 1969 Revolution to form the new Somali person — personality (qofnimo) is built, not born: family, food, dress, schooling and society shape it, and the school's job is to graft onto the child Somali-ness, straight morals, a living conscience, patriotism, responsibility and tolerance, in that order, from the flag outward to the society the pupil lives in. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Somalia's own way (Grade 3): Grade 3 turns from people to the working nation — its lessons are built on daily experience and field visits (to farms, cooperatives and self-help workplaces), because a pupil of this age learns society by visiting it; politics is taught as one of the three growth directions of the pupil, alongside the social and the economic. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
