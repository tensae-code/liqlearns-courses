**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Costa Rica, Mongolia, Palestine, Indonesia, Dominican Republic, Eritrea, India, Uzbekistan).**

Indonesia's own way (Grade 12): Grade 12 is applied human geography built entirely on Indonesian cases — regional development and spatial planning (tata ruang) measured against a Happiness Index, Industry 4.0 / Society 5.0, and Indonesia's own geopolitics and ASEAN-era cooperation as pillars of national resilience. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 11): the final year is the state-and-world year — cultural globalization and freedom, monetary policy, foreign trade and investment, and Mongolia's signature “third neighbour” and soft-power policies taught as named topics. The book's Sociology part (Chapter I Topic 1.2, printed p. 29) and Law/Civics parts (Chapter III, printed pp. 71–103; Chapter V, printed pp. 139–165; Chapter VI except the placed topics, printed pp. 167–176) are named in the placements, not placed. That way is stocked here as the way Mongolia teaches this thing.

Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs, its activity numbers key to its own companion textbook, and whole student-book topics have no guide counterpart. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence, never as a page-key to the student book. Pupil content in this lesson comes from the Amhara student book.

Costa Rica's own way: the CONED 11 anthology opens with a Geography unit on globalisation read "from the geographic viewpoint" (spatial flows of production and consumption, global cities, space-time compression), then turns to Costa Rica's own 20th-century history; its commercial workbook twin, Porras y Gamboa's *Estudios Sociales 11*, teaches the same ministry programme as a fixed cycle (problem question, basic concepts, my previous ideas, widening my knowledge, I apply my knowledge, problem-based learning, exam preparation) with heavy map-work and Costa Rica-specific data: ICE internet-coverage map, services-export league table, Costa Rica's geo-strategic bridge position between North/South America and the Caribbean. That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Palestine's own way (Grade 6, PS G6P2): a mostly historical semester; its Geography survives in the trade geography of pre-Islamic Arabia (activities, markets mapped, trade with neighbouring regions) and in a closing traffic-safety lesson.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 12): The final secondary year turns from national history to the country's present-day human geography, studied region by region: 150 years of population growth, accelerated urbanisation, the economy's branches and foreign trade, and the spatial distribution of the population across Cibao, Este and Suroeste, closing on migration and Santo Domingo as a fragmented metropolis.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

India's own way (Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): Contemporary India-II is the human/economic book, matching Part I's physical book: India's resources, forests, water, agriculture, minerals and energy, manufacturing and the "lifelines" (transport, communication, trade) — taught throughout as development-versus-environment argument, with movements and case studies (Chipko, Narmada Bachao, rooftop rainwater harvesting in the Thar) carrying the criticism.

Uzbekistan's own way (Uzbekistan Grade 9 Economic and Social Geography of the World): Grade 9 turns from physical to economic and social geography of the whole world: Part I works through the political map, world natural resources, world population, the scientific-technical revolution and the branches of the world economy; Part II then reviews every region in a fixed country-profile pattern (territory and position, natural resources, population, economy, transport, foreign ties), with Central Asia and Uzbekistan's neighbours given the fullest treatment, and closes with the global problems of humanity.
