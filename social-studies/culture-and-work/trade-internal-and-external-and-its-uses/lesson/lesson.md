# Trade — internal and external trade, and its uses

## Sub lesson 1 — Internal and external

A country's trade is **internal** — buying, selling and exchanging **inside the country**; its size depends on the country's **area** and on **transport and communication development** — or **external/international**, between countries. External trade has two faces in the student book: **imports and exports**.

## Sub lesson 2 — What trade does — and barter, entrepot (teacher guide — evidence)

Trade's uses: **getting what people need through the market; carrying goods from surplus to scarcity for profit; creating jobs and division of labour; pushing countries to produce for the world market; pushing quality and competitiveness; letting countries substitute their own products and cut foreign pressure; and moving knowledge, skill and technology between countries**. Its preconditions: **different products in different places, over-production here and under-production there, demand for raw materials, and adequate communication services**.

The teacher guide formalises trade's history and faces (teacher guide — evidence): the **earliest form of trade was bartering** — exchange of one article for another of equal value — **before money became the medium of exchange**; and external trade has **three faces, not two: import, export and entrepot trade**. The guide also keys **Ethiopia's export trade as dominated by agricultural products such as coffee** (teacher guide — evidence).
