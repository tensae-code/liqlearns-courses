# The Right To A Name Nationality And Citizenship

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: every child has the right to a name, a nationality and citizenship — as Angola's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — O registo, elemento de identidade: direito ao nome, à nacionalidade e à cidadania

Angola Educação Moral e Cívica G5 (printed p. 98), teaches: Every child has the right to a name, a nationality and citizenship. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — O registo e a sua importância

Angola Educação Moral e Cívica G5 (printed p. 100), teaches: Civil registration — why registering a birth matters: it proves who you are and opens your rights. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Angola's way — the book's own teaching way

Angola's own way: a moral-and-civic book built on the child's own reflection — every unit opens with the pupil's own life (my habits, my family, my school) and only then names the rule; equality is taught straight from Angola's own Constitution (art. 23: all are equal before the Constitution and the law), and the book ends not on the state but on national reconciliation — building a better Angola, then a better world. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
