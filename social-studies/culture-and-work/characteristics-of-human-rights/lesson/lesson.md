# Characteristics of Human Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: human rights are universal — they apply to all people without discrimination by religion, colour, sex or standing; interconnected and interlocked — all of a person's rights must be respected without exception, for it is not acceptable to enjoy the right to education while being denied security, or to respect one right and violate another; and inalienable — they exist in every person and no individual or group may dispose of them except as the law provides in specific cases — as Palestine's book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Characteristics of Human Rights

Palestine Grade 6 Social Studies Part 1 (printed p. 84), teaches: human rights are universal — they apply to all people without discrimination by religion, colour, sex or standing; interconnected and interlocked — all of a person's rights must be respected without exception, for it is not acceptable to enjoy the right to education while being denied security, or to respect one right and violate another; and inalienable — they exist in every person and no individual or group may dispose of them except as the law provides in specific cases. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 6 Social Studies Part 1): the constitution is taught from Palestine's own draft Basic Law — pupils extract legal rules from its actual articles (the people as source of the authorities, a democratic parliamentary system, equality before the law) before they are given the definition, and the three authorities are each named by the Palestinian institution that holds them. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
