# Protecting Monuments of History and Culture

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: why monuments of history and culture must be protected — they preserve the memory of great events and people — and how museums keep a people's cultural heritage — as Kazakhstan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Protecting Monuments of History and Culture

Kazakhstan Grade 3 Poznanie mira (992/999) (printed p. 54), teaches: whole cultures once vanished without trace because nobody kept their memory; monuments preserve the memory of great events and people and educate respect for the country's history; the kinds of historical and cultural monuments (Turkestan, Almaty, Uralsk, Semey pictured), respecting the past as respect for the people who lived before you and loved their home and land, and the child's own memo of what they can do so monuments survive. Kazakhstan Grade 4 Poznanie mira (110/88) (printed p. 49), teaches: the folk saying that a person who does not know their history is not worthy of the future; museums are created to preserve the cultural heritage of the past and come in kinds — historical, art, literary, local-history (краеведческие), technical; every city and district centre has its local-history museum, whose displays run from the region's nature and ethnography to its famous countrymen; each exhibit "tells" of the past in its own way, a museum can begin from a small collection at home, in school or in the village — and visitors compose the rules of behaviour in a museum. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kazakhstan's way — the books' own teaching way

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 4): Grade 3 gives the child the Constitution itself — its preamble read aloud ("We, the people of Kazakhstan…"), the citizen's rights drawn as a diagram and Constitution Day named — and roots the family in the Kazakh shezhire, the genealogy every Kazakh was expected to know to the seventh generation (Zheti ata). Kazakhstan's own way (Book 5): Grade 4 is where the primary course turns civic in earnest — the republic's own map of 14 oblasts and three cities of republican significance, an elected school council drawn as a working model of the state, the Constitution's rights quoted article by article, and the country's face to the world built from the UN seat, the closed Semipalatinsk test site and EXPO-2017. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
