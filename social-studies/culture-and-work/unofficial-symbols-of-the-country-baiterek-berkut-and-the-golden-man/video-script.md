Kazakhstan Grade 3 Poznanie mira (992/999) (printed p. 50, printed p. 132), teaches: a country's unofficial symbols beside its state symbols — for Kazakhstan the Baiterek, the Mangilik El triumphal arch, the Golden Man on the winged bars, the soaring berkut and steppe wormwood, and the folk name Uly Dala eli, the Land of the Great Steppe.

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 4): Grade 3 gives the child the Constitution itself — its preamble read aloud ("We, the people of Kazakhstan…"), the citizen's rights drawn as a diagram and Constitution Day named — and roots the family in the Kazakh shezhire, the genealogy every Kazakh was expected to know to the seventh generation (Zheti ata). Kazakhstan's own way (Book 5): Grade 4 is where the primary course turns civic in earnest — the republic's own map of 14 oblasts and three cities of republican significance, an elected school council drawn as a working model of the state, the Constitution's rights quoted article by article, and the country's face to the world built from the UN seat, the closed Semipalatinsk test site and EXPO-2017.
