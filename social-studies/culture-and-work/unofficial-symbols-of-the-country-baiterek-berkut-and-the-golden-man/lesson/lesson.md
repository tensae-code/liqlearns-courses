## Sub lesson 1 — Unofficial Symbols of the Country Baiterek Berkut and the Golden Man

Kazakhstan Grade 3 Poznanie mira (992/999) (printed p. 50), teaches: beside the state symbols every country has unofficial national symbols tied to its history, culture and nature — for Kazakhstan the Baiterek (the Tree of Life with the Samruk bird's golden egg), the Mangilik El triumphal arch (symbol of modern Kazakhstan and the triumph of its people), the Golden Man on the winged bars atop the Independence Monument, the soaring berkut (golden eagle) as a symbol of freedom — the bird on the State Flag itself — and steppe wormwood, a pinch of which travellers carried to remember home. Kazakhstan Grade 4 Poznanie mira (110/88) (printed p. 132), teaches: Kazakhstan has a folk name — "Uly Dala eli", the Land of the Great Steppe, proposed at the 550th anniversary of the Kazakh Khanate as a name reflecting the country's present, past and future, the cradle and home of the whole multinational Motherland; the country is recognised in the world by other images too — coat of arms, flag, Baiterek, the Yasawi mausoleum — and the pupils compose a national brand of Kazakhstan (and a brand of their own small homeland) from its symbols, sights and products. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kazakhstan's way — the books' own teaching way

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 4): Grade 3 gives the child the Constitution itself — its preamble read aloud ("We, the people of Kazakhstan…"), the citizen's rights drawn as a diagram and Constitution Day named — and roots the family in the Kazakh shezhire, the genealogy every Kazakh was expected to know to the seventh generation (Zheti ata). Kazakhstan's own way (Book 5): Grade 4 is where the primary course turns civic in earnest — the republic's own map of 14 oblasts and three cities of republican significance, an elected school council drawn as a working model of the state, the Constitution's rights quoted article by article, and the country's face to the world built from the UN seat, the closed Semipalatinsk test site and EXPO-2017. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
