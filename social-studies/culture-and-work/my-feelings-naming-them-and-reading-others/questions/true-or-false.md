# My Feelings Naming Them and Reading Others — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's part teaches: Naming joy, sadness, anger and fear, reading feelings from other children's faces and gestures at a birthday scene, and saying what you feel and why.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's part teaches: Reading feelings and their causes in four scenes, choosing the fitting way to express your own (express the feeling; it is no shame if you cried), and helping a friend who fears loud sounds without mocking her feelings (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1).
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's part teaches: The feelings game — draw a feeling card, mime it, let the class guess — then telling of a time you felt joy, sadness, anger or fear, and reading the feeling in Nour's cup, Taym's sickbed, Samar's fright and Jaber's anger (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1).
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's part teaches: The whole primary series opens with widening rings — Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland — taught almost entirely through picture scenes with a bear mascot, one "I can…" outcome per spread; rights arrive at age six as lived scenes (the child shut out of play has lost a right; the state gives every child the vaccine free)..
**Answer: True**
