# Hands-on — Teaching Social Studies to Pupils with Intellectual Disability

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: why Social Studies is a core subject for pupils with intellectual disability — their chief limitation is weak adaptive behaviour, with behavioural and emotional difficulties beside it, and the curriculum's end is independent living: taking one's own life into one's own hands and controlling it (Goodwin, 1991); the topic areas run in a scope-and-sequence across Grades 3–6 — self-knowledge and self-direction (rights and responsibilities) among them — with skills (reading, writing, speaking, number and art skills carried inside the subject's activities) and values and attitudes named as strands of their own; the teacher's greatest care must go to what is actually learned and assessed, and in every exercise the point is generalisation — carrying the learning into the real environment of life and making it usable; the educational package (the software of strategies, models, tasks, frameworks, guidance, assignments and tests, with its hardware) completes the book. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
