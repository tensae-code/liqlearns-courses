# Tsagaan Sar the White Month

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Tsagaan Sar, the lunar new year — the zolgokh greeting embrace performed with elders, the festival greeting words ("Амархан сайн байна уу?"), and the shagai ankle-bone games played at the festival — as Mongolia's books teach it. ## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 13 Сайхан шинэлээрэй (Happy New Year)

Mongolia Grade 1 Civic Education, printed p. 30, teaches: Tsagaan Sar, the lunar new year — the zolgokh greeting embrace performed with elders, the festival greeting words ("Амархан сайн байна уу?"), and the shagai ankle-bone games played at the festival. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Topic 14 "From the customs of Tsagaan Sar food and offering" (Цагаан сарын идээ, шуус тавих ёсноос)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 52, teaches: The White Month, the nation's greatest festival, kept since the ancestors: the whole-sheep offering (их бүхэл) is laid out in its fixed order — head, ууц, хаа, гуя, neck and сээр — and never in an ordinary household; on the eve (битүүн) the family "closes" the old year with fullness games and full dishes; and on the first morning comes шинэлэх ёс — the золгох greeting in strict order of age, the younger supporting the elder's elbows, the хадаг offered on upturned palms, snuff exchanged, and the blessing answers wished back. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 19 "Tsagaan Sar symbolism and the taboo custom" (Цагаан сарын бэлгэдэл ба цээрийн ёсноос)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 53, teaches: White is the Mongols' most honoured colour — a white heart, a white road, white food; the festival's honoured dish is white food, and a guest first tastes the white food and tastes it again on leaving, so that no work is hindered and the white blessing travels with them; and the Tsagaan Sar taboos guard the whole festival — no lending out in debt at the greeting, no bad words on the eve, no quarrelling or fighting, nothing taken out of the ger in the first six days, no staying overnight at another's home on the first night. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: civic education for six-year-olds is pure Mongolian custom — greeting elders the Mongolian way, the ger and the five snouts, colour symbolism, the biyelgee dance and the Tsagaan Sar greeting embrace — with traditional script verses running down every page beside the Cyrillic. Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
