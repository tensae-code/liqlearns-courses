# Video script — Not Taking What is Not Given

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Vietnam Grade 1 Đạo đức 1, printed pp. 50–51, teaches: Ask and borrow, never just take — a friend's toys, a library book, a drink in a shop.

Vietnam Grade 4 Đạo đức 4, printed pp. 25–30, teaches: Ask before you touch, borrow gently and give back (the little mouse who could not rest until he returned the borrowed axe), mend what you tear before returning it, hand back the hat a friend forgot, and never take home the ball you found or pick the neighbour's fruit (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 1 of this file; counted here as an existing target).

Vietnam's own way: Đạo đức (Ethics) is Vietnam's standalone primary moral-education subject; every lesson runs the same four moves — a song to open, picture stories to explore, practice pictures to judge, a role-play to apply — and closes with a two-line rhyme that states the duty; the family is taught three-generational (ông bà – cha mẹ – anh chị em) and school rules are taught as the child's first shared rules. Grade 4 turns outward to society's work and property: gratitude is owed to every worker including the night street-sweeper of Tố Hữu's poem; "public property" (của công) is named as a legal-moral category of its own — what belongs to everyone is guarded by everyone — and the book closes with the child's place in law itself: the basic rights and duties of children, including a children's forum where pupils speak.
