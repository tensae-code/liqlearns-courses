# Not Taking What is Not Given — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Never take or use others' things without asking — ask and borrow, never just take — a friend's toys, a library book, a drink in a shop (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Respect other people's property — ask before you touch, borrow gently and give back (the little mouse who could not rest until he returned the borrowed axe), mend what you tear before returning it, hand back the hat a friend forgot, and never take home the ball you found or pick the neighbour's fruit (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 1 of this file; counted here as an existing target) (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Vietnam's way — the book's own teaching way — Đạo đức (Ethics) is Vietnam's standalone primary moral-education subject; every lesson runs the same four moves — a song to open, picture stories to explore, practice pictures to judge, a role-play to apply — and closes with a two-line rhyme that states the duty; the family is taught three-generational (ông bà – cha mẹ – anh chị em) and school rules are taught as the child's first shared rules. Grade 4 turns outward to society's work and property: gratitude is owed to every worker including the night street-sweeper of Tố Hữu's poem; "public property" (của công) is named as a legal-moral category of its own — what belongs to everyone is guarded by everyone — and the book closes with the child's place in law itself: the basic rights and duties of children, including a children's forum where pupils speak. (any close wording).**
