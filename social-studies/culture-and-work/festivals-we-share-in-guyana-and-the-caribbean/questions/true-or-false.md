# Festivals We Share in Guyana and the Caribbean — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: Guyanese and Caribbean people visit each other's festivals (Mashramani in Guyana, Carnival in Trinidad, Diwali in both) and exchange festival foods with friends — mithai, parsad, gulgula and other sweets and savoury dishes.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: Amerindian Heritage Month (begun 1995, honouring Stephen Campbell, the first Amerindian Member of Parliament), Emancipation Day with its vigils and libation ceremonies, Arrival Day, and Mashramani, the republic celebration "after hard work".
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: Phagwah, the festival of colours; Diwali, the festival of lights, with diyas and rangoli; Eid-ul-Adha, the feast of sacrifice, whose food is shared with the poor; and Christmas, celebrated by almost everyone in Guyana.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: Celebrations bring communities together, help people understand one another, teach gratitude, make people proud of who they are and keep their culture alive for their children.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson's fifth part teaches: Family celebrations — Mother's Day, Diwali, Eid-ul-Adha and Christmas — and that families celebrate the same day differently: Sheldon's family marks birthdays with a party, Jaya's with prayers and a priest's blessing.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This lesson's sixth part teaches: School traditions — Sports Day and Culture Day, when learners display their culture in clothes, food, music and dance, and school Mashramani — celebrations are important because they bring togetherness and unity.
**Answer: True**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on Guyana's student book, named in lesson/sources.md.**
