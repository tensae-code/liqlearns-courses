# Video script — Festivals We Share in Guyana and the Caribbean

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Guyana Grade 6 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 56–62), teaches: Guyanese and Caribbean people visit each other's festivals (Mashramani in Guyana, Carnival in Trinidad, Diwali in both) and exchange festival foods with friends — mithai, parsad, gulgula and other sweets and savoury dishes.

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 39–42 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 8 above)), teaches: Amerindian Heritage Month (begun 1995, honouring Stephen Campbell, the first Amerindian Member of Parliament), Emancipation Day with its vigils and libation ceremonies, Arrival Day, and Mashramani, the republic celebration "after hard work".

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 43–46), teaches: Phagwah, the festival of colours; Diwali, the festival of lights, with diyas and rangoli; Eid-ul-Adha, the feast of sacrifice, whose food is shared with the poor; and Christmas, celebrated by almost everyone in Guyana.

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed p. 47), teaches: Celebrations bring communities together, help people understand one another, teach gratitude, make people proud of who they are and keep their culture alive for their children.

Guyana Grade 1 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 37–38 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 8 above)), teaches: Family celebrations — Mother's Day, Diwali, Eid-ul-Adha and Christmas — and that families celebrate the same day differently: Sheldon's family marks birthdays with a party, Jaya's with prayers and a priest's blessing.

Guyana Grade 1 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 40–41), teaches: School traditions — Sports Day and Culture Day, when learners display their culture in clothes, food, music and dance, and school Mashramani — celebrations are important because they bring togetherness and unity.

Guyana's own way: the newest ministry series teaches citizenship from the child's own roles outward — family duties, community roles, youth responsibilities at home and across the Caribbean, then power, privilege and human rights in plain language, with Guyanese and Caribbean case studies (Annette Arjoon-Martins, Mia Mottley) and a value component woven into every unit. the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught. the series opens at Grade 1 inside the home — the family that cooks and gardens together, the chores shared out fairly, the groups a small child already belongs to, and rules taught as the way a home and a classroom stay in harmony.
