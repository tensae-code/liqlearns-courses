## Sub lesson 1 — Festivals we share

Guyana Grade 6 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 56–62), teaches: Guyanese and Caribbean people visit each other's festivals (Mashramani in Guyana, Carnival in Trinidad, Diwali in both) and exchange festival foods with friends — mithai, parsad, gulgula and other sweets and savoury dishes. Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 39–42 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 8 above)), teaches: Amerindian Heritage Month (begun 1995, honouring Stephen Campbell, the first Amerindian Member of Parliament), Emancipation Day with its vigils and libation ceremonies, Arrival Day, and Mashramani, the republic celebration "after hard work". ## Sub lesson 3 — Festivals of the faiths

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 43–46), teaches: Phagwah, the festival of colours; Diwali, the festival of lights, with diyas and rangoli; Eid-ul-Adha, the feast of sacrifice, whose food is shared with the poor; and Christmas, celebrated by almost everyone in Guyana. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Why celebrations are important

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed p. 47), teaches: Celebrations bring communities together, help people understand one another, teach gratitude, make people proud of who they are and keep their culture alive for their children. ## Sub lesson 5 — Celebrations at home

Guyana Grade 1 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 37–38 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 8 above)), teaches: Family celebrations — Mother's Day, Diwali, Eid-ul-Adha and Christmas — and that families celebrate the same day differently: Sheldon's family marks birthdays with a party, Jaya's with prayers and a priest's blessing. ## Sub lesson 6 — Celebrations at school

Guyana Grade 1 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 40–41), teaches: School traditions — Sports Day and Culture Day, when learners display their culture in clothes, food, music and dance, and school Mashramani — celebrations are important because they bring togetherness and unity.
