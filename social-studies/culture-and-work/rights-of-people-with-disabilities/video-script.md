The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2, printed p. 63, teaches: national legislation and international conventions affirm the rights of persons with disabilities — respect for their dignity, independence and freedom to choose their decisions, full and effective participation in society, and equality in rights and duties with other, non-disabled persons — and providing the material environment that enables their participation, in buildings, roads, housing, means of transport and joining education; among these conventions is the International Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities of 2006, which the State of Palestine joined in April 2014, and Palestine has its own disability law, issued in 2009 as the book prints it.

Palestine's own way: human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy.
