Indonesia's Grade 3 PP-G3 student (printed p. 64); Grade 3 PP-G3 student (printed p. 71); Grade 4 PP-G4 student (printed p. 52); Grade 6 PP-G6 student (printed p. 62); Grade 3 PP-G3 teacher (printed p. 77), teaches: Bab 2 C. Hak dan Kewajibanku di Rumah, Bab 2 D. Hak dan Kewajibanku di Sekolah, Bab 2 C. Mendapatkan Hak dan Melakukan Kewajiban, Bab 3 B. Mengenal Kewajiban Anak, Panduan Khusus Bab 2: Aku Patuh Aturan — teaches: my rights and my duties at home, taught as a pair; my rights and my duties at school, taught as a pair; receiving rights and doing duties — the pair stated as one lesson; a child's obligations — listing your duties, home tidiness rules, and the duty to be brave and honest; guidance for school rules and agreements and rights/duties at home and school.

Indonesia's own way: Grade 3 is the first book to name the state the child lives in — your own street is taught as part of the NKRI (Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia), and rights are introduced paired, always, with duties. Grade 4 introduces the child's first government — the perangkat desa/kelurahan, the village and urban-ward officials — inside a book about the child's own street, before any national institution is named. the primary Pancasila course closes by widening every earlier theme to the nation — norms become norms of berbangsa dan bernegara (national and state life), and gotong royong is finally named as bela negara, defending the country by working together. the guide gives teachers their own reading (Bahan Bacaan Guru) per chapter, so the civics content is taught to the teacher first, then to the class.
