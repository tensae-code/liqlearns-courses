# Fix Your Shortcomings Grow Your Strengths

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: everyone has many good points — kind to friends, reads many books, runs fast — found and told to each other with medal cards, and your own good points too — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — よいところを見つけたよ (I found the good points)

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 158), teaches: Everyone has many good points — kind to friends, reads many books, runs fast — found and told to each other with medal cards, and your own good points too. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — (5) 自分の良い所をのばして (Grow your good points)

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 46), teaches: Knowing your strengths and weaknesses and growing the good points, with the lives of scientist Marie Curie and manga artist Tezuka Osamu. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — 読みもの「うれしく思えた日から」("From the Day I Felt Glad")

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 50), teaches: A child who keeps practising what they are bad at, cheered on by everyone, until the day it works (reading). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — (6) 短所を改め、長所をのばして (Fix your shortcomings, grow your strengths)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 50), teaches: Knowing your own good points and the self you want to change. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 5 — Japan's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 6 — Japan's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 7 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
