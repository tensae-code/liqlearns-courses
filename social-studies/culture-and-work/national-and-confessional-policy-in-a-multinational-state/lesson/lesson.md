## Sub lesson 1 — National and confessional policy (Национальная и конфессиональная политика) — Grade 11

Belarus Grade 11 Obshchestvovedenie (printed p. 149), teaches: Belarus as a land of interethnic and interconfessional accord — about half the adults believers, Orthodoxy and Catholicism the largest confessions beside some 1,000 Protestant communities and Jewish and Muslim associations, with religious tolerance and no conflicts on national or religious grounds; the state's national policy — the 1990 Law on Languages, the 1995 referendum giving Russian equal status with Belarusian, ethnic tolerance (about two-thirds of Belarusians have relatives of other nationalities) — as a condition of the country's stable development. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belarus's way — the book's own teaching way

Belarus' own way: the whole Grade 11 course is the state describing itself — the 1994 Constitution and its 1996/2004 referendums, the President as guarantor of the Constitution, a two-chamber National Assembly and elected local Soviets of deputies under appointed executive committees, and two state languages — so its civics is placed as Belarus's own constitutional order, not as generic civics.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
