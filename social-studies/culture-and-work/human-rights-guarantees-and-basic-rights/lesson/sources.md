**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (South Korea).**

South Korea's own way (Book 1): the middle-school Social Studies ② volume opens with civics, not geography — human rights and the Constitution first, then the three state institutions one by one, and the full text of the Constitution of the Republic of Korea printed as an appendix for pupils to read; it closes with global-citizenship units (global problems, reducing inter-regional inequality).

South Korea's own way (Book 10): this Cheonjae edition reorganises the same national frame into twelve units of its own — human rights get a whole unit that ends in remedies (국가인권위원회, 헌법 소원) and labour rights, and the book closes with 더불어 사는 세계 ("the world we live in together"): hunger, biodiversity, appropriate technology and fair trade as the pupil's own global agenda.

South Korea's own way (Book 11): a second Cheonjae author team's telling of the same frame — its Unit I wrap-up drills the exact constitutional mechanics of rights (restriction only by law, for national security, order or public welfare, never touching the essential content), and its labour pages speak directly to teenagers: young workers hold the same legal protections, and wages, hours, rest and leave are guaranteed by statute.

South Korea's own way (Book 12): the 2022 middle-school course opens its society half with the pupil's own formation — socialisation and self-identity, status and roles, and the conflict and discrimination inside our society — then builds the full civics ladder in one volume: democracy's ideals and history, elections and political actors, local self-government, law in daily life and how trials work, and finally human rights with the limits and remedies for basic rights and the relief for violated labour rights.
