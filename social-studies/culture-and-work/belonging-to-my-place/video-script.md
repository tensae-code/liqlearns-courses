# Video script — Belonging to My Place

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Oman Grade 1 Identity & Citizenship TG T1, printed pp. 60–61, teaches: The unit is passed not on facts but on a feeling — the child ends it belonging to the place she lives in, the second component of citizenship practised at street level.

Oman Grade 1 Identity & Citizenship TG T2, printed pp. 14–15, 27, teaches: Belonging, practised at the scale of the whole country — the second component of citizenship, aimed this term at the homeland and its symbols.

Oman Grade 5 Social Studies TG T1, printed pp. 87–94, teaches: The two concepts defined and argued — belonging grows from loving the homeland and prizing its history and civilisation, drives work for its sake, binds its sons together and guards its interests; the family's and the school's roles in growing it are discussed outright.

Oman Grade 5 Social Studies TG T1, printed pp. 95–103, teaches: What belonging looks like in practice — diligence in study and honesty in work as the road to the homeland's progress, respecting national occasions and taking part in them, keeping Omani dress, language, customs and traditions, and respecting the regulations and laws.

Oman Grade 2 Identity & Citizenship TG T2, printed pp. 14–15, 25, teaches: Belonging at homeland scale for a seven-year-old — describing the homeland's features, showing its heritage, and taking pride in the Omani personalities who influenced its history.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 1, Term 1): citizenship is its own school subject from age six, built as widening circles — the child first names herself, her feelings and her family, and only then her neighbourhood; the state prints its map and anthem at the front of the teacher's guide, and the fourth unit-component is always participation, not obedience.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 1, Term 2): term 2 completes the widening circles — after self, family and neighbourhood come the homeland (its flag raised every morning, its Sultan named with a prayer, its Renaissance told as one man's building) and then the whole world, where the Omani child is taught that difference itself is what makes a good citizen.

Oman's own way (Social Studies Grade 5, Term 1): even inside the combined Social Studies book, the identity strand keeps its own unit with its own name — belonging and loyalty are taught as concepts with definitions, facts and values (among them: respecting the regulations and laws, preserving Omani culture and civilisation, volunteering), not left as decoration on the history lessons.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 2, Term 2): the world unit is taught as three verbs of the heart — communicate, interact, empathise — while the homeland unit grounds belonging in heritage, folk tales and the Omani personalities who shaped history.
