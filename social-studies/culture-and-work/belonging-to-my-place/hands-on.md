# Hands-on — Belonging to My Place

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Belonging to My Place 1
Work through this part with your hands: The unit is passed not on facts but on a feeling — the child ends it belonging to the place she lives in, the second component of citizenship practised at street level. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Belonging to My Place 2
Work through this part with your hands: Belonging, practised at the scale of the whole country — the second component of citizenship, aimed this term at the homeland and its symbols. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Belonging to My Place 3
Work through this part with your hands: The two concepts defined and argued — belonging grows from loving the homeland and prizing its history and civilisation, drives work for its sake, binds its sons together and guards its interests; the family's and the school's roles in growing it are discussed outright. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Belonging to My Place 4
Work through this part with your hands: What belonging looks like in practice — diligence in study and honesty in work as the road to the homeland's progress, respecting national occasions and taking part in them, keeping Omani dress, language, customs and traditions, and respecting the regulations and laws. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Belonging to My Place 5
Work through this part with your hands: Belonging at homeland scale for a seven-year-old — describing the homeland's features, showing its heritage, and taking pride in the Omani personalities who influenced its history. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Oman's way
Say how Oman's books teach this thing, in your own words, from the lesson.
