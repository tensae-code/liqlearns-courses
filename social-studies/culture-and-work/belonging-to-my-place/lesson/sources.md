Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 1, Term 1) (teacher guide — evidence): citizenship is its own school subject from age six, built as widening circles — the child first names herself, her feelings and her family, and only then her neighbourhood; the state prints its map and anthem at the front of the teacher's guide, and the fourth unit-component is always participation, not obedience.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 1, Term 2) (teacher guide — evidence): term 2 completes the widening circles — after self, family and neighbourhood come the homeland (its flag raised every morning, its Sultan named with a prayer, its Renaissance told as one man's building) and then the whole world, where the Omani child is taught that difference itself is what makes a good citizen.

Oman's own way (Social Studies Grade 5, Term 1) (teacher guide — evidence): even inside the combined Social Studies book, the identity strand keeps its own unit with its own name — belonging and loyalty are taught as concepts with definitions, facts and values (among them: respecting the regulations and laws, preserving Omani culture and civilisation, volunteering), not left as decoration on the history lessons.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 2, Term 2) (teacher guide — evidence): the world unit is taught as three verbs of the heart — communicate, interact, empathise — while the homeland unit grounds belonging in heritage, folk tales and the Omani personalities who shaped history.

Country honesty note: Oman's student books were not held — its placements are based on teacher guides, and the G7 Term-2 content is a named GAP (the Grade 7 framework assigns the Basic Statute of the State and government institutions to Grade 7, no Term 1 unit teaches them, and the Term 2 book was not reached, as filed in the oman-placements.md record-only section); all Oman placements in this lesson are evidence only, outside acceptance counts.
