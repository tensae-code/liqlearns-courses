The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Guyana Grade 7 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 1 (printed pp. 17–18), teaches: The attitudes we are expected to adopt — honesty (not only returning things to the rightful owner, but being honest with ourselves, showing sympathy to friends in times of need, displaying trust and showing loyalty); punctuality ("the early bird catches the worm"); respect — show respect for elders and those in authority, obey teachers and prefects, and always be courteous, with thank you, pardon me, good day, please, excuse me and may I help as part of our everyday vocabulary, our etiquette, our life; and obeying rules — keeping classrooms and surroundings tidy, caring for public and private property, obeying the laws of our country and avoiding delinquent groups.

Guyana's own way: Book 1 opens secondary Social Studies where the primary books end — the child as citizen of nested communities (family, school, village or town, country), national symbols learned part by part with their meanings, and local government taught through the rates-and-taxes bargain: services exist because citizens pay for them and care for what they buy.
