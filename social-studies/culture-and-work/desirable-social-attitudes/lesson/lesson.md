# Desirable Social Attitudes

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the attitudes we are expected to adopt — honesty (not only returning things to the rightful owner, but being honest with ourselves, showing sympathy to friends in times of need, displaying trust and showing loyalty); punctuality ("the early bird catches the worm"); respect — show respect for elders and those in authority, obey teachers and prefects, and always be courteous, with thank you, pardon me, good day, please, excuse me and may I help as part of our everyday vocabulary, our etiquette, our life; and obeying rules — keeping classrooms and surroundings tidy, caring for public and private property, obeying the laws of our country and avoiding delinquent groups — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Desirable social attitudes

Guyana Grade 7 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 1 (printed pp. 17–18), teaches: The attitudes we are expected to adopt — honesty (not only returning things to the rightful owner, but being honest with ourselves, showing sympathy to friends in times of need, displaying trust and showing loyalty); punctuality ("the early bird catches the worm"); respect — show respect for elders and those in authority, obey teachers and prefects, and always be courteous, with thank you, pardon me, good day, please, excuse me and may I help as part of our everyday vocabulary, our etiquette, our life; and obeying rules — keeping classrooms and surroundings tidy, caring for public and private property, obeying the laws of our country and avoiding delinquent groups. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: Book 1 opens secondary Social Studies where the primary books end — the child as citizen of nested communities (family, school, village or town, country), national symbols learned part by part with their meanings, and local government taught through the rates-and-taxes bargain: services exist because citizens pay for them and care for what they buy. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
