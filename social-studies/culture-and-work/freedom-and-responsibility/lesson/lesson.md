# Freedom and Responsibility

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: freedom and responsibility — my freedom is responsible, bounded by law and by others' rights — as Libya's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — My Freedom Is Responsible

Libya Grade 6 National Education G6 (printed p. 18), teaches: freedom is each person's right to take the decisions that suit them by their own will, without compulsion — so long as it agrees with the law, harms neither values nor others; freedom is a responsible act, and absolute freedom without responsibility is chaos. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Libya's way — the book's own teaching way

Libya's own way: Grade 6 defines the citizen by a chain of relations — rights paired with duties, freedom paired with responsibility, then one lesson per circle the child belongs to (family, school, neighbourhood) — and grounds the rights lesson in named world instruments: "human rights" as the UN defines them, the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child, and UNICEF. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
