**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Cambodia, South Sudan).**

South Sudan's own way (Book 3): A community is defined by common interests across different traditions and languages, with peace promoted inside the definition itself; civic life appears at age 7 as a ballot box and a president's swearing-in photograph, and decision-making is taught as a citizen's skill that ends in human rights and peaceful living.

South Sudan's own way (Book 7): Law enters as the rules each community governs itself by, with arrest and the court as its pictures; the moral half of the conflict unit is a right/wrong table judged by community eyes — gossiping and not greeting elders are wrongs alongside stealing — and the unit closes on resisting negative peer pressure, with drug abuse named as the Health-course part (named here, not placed).

South Sudan's own way (Book 9): Citizenship is taught as a sung list of duties — loyalty, voting, peace, defending the country, paying taxes, obeying laws, helping in disasters — and leadership is measured against citizenship itself: to be a good leader a person must first be a good citizen, and the President is named as the example of both.
