## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: in the old Arabia of warring tribes, thieves and plague, a shepherd, a farmer and a merchant each despair of his trade; the children plead with them and the merchant prays for a man to protect the land — Imam Muhammad bin Saud founds the state, security spreads until the Hajj roads are safe, trade flourishes, and the people gain one identity and one ruler — as Saudi Arabia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Security and Unity Under One State

Saudi Arabia Grade 4 Social Studies (printed pp. 29–33), teaches: In the old Arabia of warring tribes, thieves and plague, a shepherd, a farmer and a merchant each despair of his trade; the children plead with them and the merchant prays for a man to protect the land — Imam Muhammad bin Saud founds the state, security spreads until the Hajj roads are safe, trade flourishes, and the people gain one identity and one ruler. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saudi Arabia's way — the book's own teaching way

Saudi Arabia's own way: the Grade 4 book opens with citizenship — national identity, the system of government, national symbols and the founding story of security and unity under one state — and returns in Unit 6 to responsible citizenship through family and society, rights and duties, the good citizen, teamwork, and heritage stories that teach patience and hard work. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
