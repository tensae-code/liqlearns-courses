# Cleaning the coast — effects and fixes

## Do it first

A polluted bay: the fish thin out, the beach smells, the boats come back emptier. Who is harmed — only the fish? List everyone and everything that loses. Then list what could be done. Read on with both lists open.

## Sub lesson 1 — Effects — on marine life and coastal communities

Pollution's effects fall first on **marine life**: the fish and sea creatures whose water is fouled — and through them on the **coastal communities** who fish, trade and eat from that water. The guide's answer key (teacher guide — evidence) states why ocean pollution matters at the largest scale: **oceans are the source of the earth's surface moisture and of fish and other marine life** — dirty the ocean and you touch the planet's water and food together.

The section's **case study** grounds the account in one real coast — the method matters as much as the place: name the sources, trace their effects on life and livelihoods, and only then judge the fixes. That order — sources, effects, fixes — is the discipline of the whole topic.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fixes — stop the sources, protect the filters

The fixes answer the sources one by one, which is how the section sets its closing work — pupils **suggesting solutions** to the pollution they have traced. Against sewage and industrial discharge: **treatment and satisfactory disposal facilities** before water reaches the coast — the guide names unsatisfactory disposal as the heart of the most frequent cause, so fixing disposal fixes the biggest source first. Against the rest: control dumping and ships' discharges, guard against oil accidents, and **protect and restore the wetlands and mangroves** whose filtering the coast has been losing.

Hold the order as a rule for any environmental problem: biggest source first, natural defences protected, and every fix matched to a named cause. A fix that names no cause is a wish, not a solution.

## Say it to your companion

Take your companion through one coast's full story: sources, effects, fixes — in that order. Your companion interrupts with a 'fix' that names no cause; you send it back and repair it together.
