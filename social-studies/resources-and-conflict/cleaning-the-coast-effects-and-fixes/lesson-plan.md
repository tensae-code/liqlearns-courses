# Lesson plan — Cleaning the coast — effects and fixes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Cleaning the coast — effects and fixes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Geographic Issues and Public Concerns in Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Coastal pollution harms marine life and the coastal communities who live from the sea — and because oceans give the Earth its surface moisture and its fish and marine life, cleaning the coast means stopping the sources, protecting the mangrove filters, and treating wastes before they reach the water.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands directly on What pollutes Africa's coasts — this lesson is its second half: effects, the case study's lesson, and solutions.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Effects — on marine life and coastal communities** — What the pollution does when it arrives.
2. **Fixes — stop the sources, protect the filters** — Solutions in the order of the problem.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What pollutes Africa's coasts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the effects on marine life and coastal communities.
2. State why ocean pollution matters planet-wide (moisture; fish and marine life).
3. Use the case-study order: sources, effects, fixes.
4. Match fixes to causes — treatment/disposal, discharge control, oil guarding, mangrove protection.
