**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Laws That Limit War — the idea of putting a limit to the suffering wars cause arose with Henry Dunant after the Battle of Solferino in 1859, to preserve the rights of prisoners, the wounded and civilians during wars and to let neutral humanitarian organisations work to relieve their suffering; with the emergence of international humanitarian law the international family held meetings and concluded agreements to protect human rights from the violations belligerents might commit, covering prisoners, the wounded, civilians and the neutral international institutions — among the landmarks: the first meeting of the International Committee for Relief to Wounded Soldiers in 1863, known from 1876 as the International Committee of the Red Cross; the four Geneva Conventions of 1949, which provided for protecting wounded and sick soldiers on land and at sea, the medical crews, prisoners of war and civilians, won the world's consensus, and had the two Protocols attached in 1977; and the Ottawa Treaty of 1997 (the Canadian capital), which bans the use of anti-personnel landmines, their transfer and stockpiling, and obliges every state to remove and destroy them after armed conflicts end, within no more than ten years of the agreement's start, each state striving to identify all the suspected mined areas (any close wording).**
