**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does part 1 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Peer mediators 1 — the mediator's toolkit — settlement (تسوية), a way of ending a dispute both sides accept though each gets less than he wanted; concession (تنازل), giving something up to end a dispute; and active listening (الإصغاء النشط), concentrating fully on what the other says — and the five styles of facing disputes, from forcing a win to working together for the fair outcome (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does part 2 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Peer mediators 2 — mediation practised — the story of two classmates whose quarrel is ended by talking and listening to each other, then role-play scenes in which pupils mediate a dispute themselves, using settlements and concessions, and judge whether the characters reached one (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does part 3 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: United Arab Emirates' way — the book's own teaching way — in the Moral Education course, nine-year-olds are trained as peer mediators with a named toolkit — settlement, concession, active listening — and the five ways of facing a dispute are taught through animals (the shark forces, the turtle withdraws, the bear yields, the fox compromises, the owl solves); responsibility is taught by a lost wallet with 800 dirhams in it. (any close wording).**
