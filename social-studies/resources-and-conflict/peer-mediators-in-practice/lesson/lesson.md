# Peer Mediators in Practice

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the mediator's toolkit — settlement (تسوية), a way of ending a dispute both sides accept though each gets less than he wanted; concession (تنازل), giving something up to end a dispute; and active listening (الإصغاء النشط), concentrating fully on what the other says — and the five styles of facing disputes, from forcing a win to working together for the fair outcome — as United Arab Emirates's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Peer mediators 1

United Arab Emirates Moral Education, G4 Term 2 teacher guide, printed p. 41, teaches: the mediator's toolkit — settlement (تسوية), a way of ending a dispute both sides accept though each gets less than he wanted; concession (تنازل), giving something up to end a dispute; and active listening (الإصغاء النشط), concentrating fully on what the other says — and the five styles of facing disputes, from forcing a win to working together for the fair outcome This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Peer mediators 2

United Arab Emirates Moral Education, G4 Term 2 teacher guide, printed p. 47, teaches: mediation practised — the story of two classmates whose quarrel is ended by talking and listening to each other, then role-play scenes in which pupils mediate a dispute themselves, using settlements and concessions, and judge whether the characters reached one This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — United Arab Emirates' way — the book's own teaching way

United Arab Emirates' own way: in the Moral Education course, nine-year-olds are trained as peer mediators with a named toolkit — settlement, concession, active listening — and the five ways of facing a dispute are taught through animals (the shark forces, the turtle withdraws, the bear yields, the fox compromises, the owl solves); responsibility is taught by a lost wallet with 800 dirhams in it. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
