# Sustainable development — meeting needs without stealing the future

## Sub lesson 1 — The idea and its definition

The concept grew through world conferences: Stockholm in 1972, where the environment first took the world stage and UNEP was founded; Rio in 1992, whose Earth Summit produced Agenda 21 with its Principle 1 putting human beings at the centre; and Johannesburg in 2002, emphasising the social and economic sides.

The definition that governs it all comes from the Brundtland Commission's 1987 report 'Our Common Future': development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three dimensions, nested

Sustainable development stands on three dimensions. Economic: continuing production, manageable debt, and no sectoral imbalance. Environmental: a stable resource base, no overexploitation of renewables, and biodiversity and ecosystem services maintained. Social: fair distribution and opportunity, health, education and gender equity.

The book draws them as nested rings: economy sits inside society, and society inside environment. The order matters — there is no economy without a society, and no society without the environment that holds it.
