Haiti is named here as teacher-sequence evidence only, and Niue as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere. Guatemala is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 12 Social Studies & Life Skills): Grade 12 economics is the development unit with real tables — sector shares of GDP, the trade growth and direction tables, Nepal's growth rates through the COVID year, the HDI bands, and Nurkse's vicious circle drawn as two loops, supply side and demand side.

Vietnam was already counted in this lesson from an earlier wave — the Vietnam Economics placements above are stocked as depth, with the acceptance count unchanged.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 12 Economic & Legal Education): the macro course is built on Vietnam's national accounts — the expenditure formula GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), GDP/GNI tables against China and Singapore, the 8.12% growth of 2022, sector-share charts 2018 vs 2022, and the 50/30/20-style family budget rule (50% essentials / 20% non-essentials / 30% saving, investment and reserve).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 9 Term 2): the middle-stage course closes by going global — economic relations between states, OPEC as the oil-producers' organisation Kuwait belongs to, and world challenges (poverty, the food crisis) framed as production-versus-consumption problems.

Niue's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide localises the shared Pacific set country by country — its "Links to formal learning" section carries a dedicated Niue chapter (what sustainability means for Niueans; Niue curriculum links to SDG 4.7), the model other Pacific countries' sets follow. Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): The environment is studied from the schoolyard outward — a field diagnosis of the local environment (bare soils, dried springs, waste, noise), scaled up to a map and graph of the whole territory's problems (deforestation, erosion, water), then turned into a pupil-designed sustainable land-use project for a greener Haiti. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

China's own way: senior-high human geography built entirely around China's own questions — population opens the book (world distribution, push–pull migration, carrying capacity and the Hu Huanyong line as a self-study window), then villages and towns, then where each industry sits and how that changes, then transport and regional development in both directions, closing with the environment and China's own national strategies (main functional zones, the Yangtze Economic Belt, blue-economy space); every chapter ends with a problem inquiry drawn from Chinese everyday life (migrant workers, choosing where to live, physical shops, city traffic, low-carbon food), and two sections carry geographic-information-technology applications (GIS in city management inside Chapter 2 §2; GIS in delivery services inside Chapter 3 §3). That way is stocked here as the way China teaches this thing.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 6 Term 2): Economics arrives as one late unit built entirely on Jordanian ground — Jordan's solar and wind projects, water scarcity and water harvesting, and a closing project on Jordan's water problem; production is taught through a biscuit's journey from wheat field to home.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 8 Term 1): Development is taught head-on — growth against development, Malthus against the food supply, and the labour force counted the Jordanian way (everyone aged 15–65 able to work), before the sustainable-development goals close the unit.
