**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, China, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Libya, Armenia, South Sudan). Haiti is named here as teacher-sequence evidence only, and Niue as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Niue's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide localises the shared Pacific set country by country — its "Links to formal learning" section carries a dedicated Niue chapter (what sustainability means for Niueans; Niue curriculum links to SDG 4.7), the model other Pacific countries' sets follow. That evidence is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): The environment is studied from the schoolyard outward — a field diagnosis of the local environment (bare soils, dried springs, waste, noise), scaled up to a map and graph of the whole territory's problems (deforestation, erosion, water), then turned into a pupil-designed sustainable land-use project for a greener Haiti. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Book error taught corrected, exactly as the placements flag it: Unit 3's history mislabels its conferences — it calls the 1972 Stockholm conference '(known as the Brundtland Report)' and places a 'second UNCED' in Nairobi in 1987 — while the same spread later correctly attributes the Brundtland Commission's 1987 report 'Our Common Future'. This lesson teaches the corrected sequence (Stockholm 1972 and UNEP's founding; Brundtland 1987; Rio 1992; Johannesburg 2002) and names the error here only.

China's own way: senior-high human geography built entirely around China's own questions — population opens the book (world distribution, push–pull migration, carrying capacity and the Hu Huanyong line as a self-study window), then villages and towns, then where each industry sits and how that changes, then transport and regional development in both directions, closing with the environment and China's own national strategies (main functional zones, the Yangtze Economic Belt, blue-economy space); every chapter ends with a problem inquiry drawn from Chinese everyday life (migrant workers, choosing where to live, physical shops, city traffic, low-carbon food), and two sections carry geographic-information-technology applications (GIS in city management inside Chapter 2 §2; GIS in delivery services inside Chapter 3 §3). That way is stocked here as the way China teaches this thing.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 8): the year's Geography is one long chapter, "Protect the Bond between Nature and People": pupils model the world's biomes, compare world forest cover in 2000 and 2020, map Bangladesh's own forests and disaster-prone areas, and read a long inquiry-reader on biomes, sea resources and the Sundarbans mangrove.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Địa lí 10): a single Grade 10 course that runs the whole discipline — map craft, the Earth's spheres, then economic geography sector by sector — with Vietnam itself as the standing worked example (its power-plant and rice maps, the Red River's regime at Hanoi, Hanoi's climate chart, Vietnam's GDP structure, its internet and tourism figures) inside world-scale treatment.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 12 LY G12 Environmental Geography): The geography course closes with a full environmental-geography book: the environment as a system of living and non-living parts, the world's great environments (water, then land biome by biome), the social environment and its balance with nature, the problems of settlements, the five pollutions (air, water, soil, food, noise) — and protection ending in sustainable development.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Geography student): the senior Geography course is two big themes only — the world economy in depth (structure, division of labour, every branch, transport systems, trade) and then the environment (ecological balance, pollution of air and water, land degradation, biodiversity, ecosystem services), i.e. economy first, its ecological bill second.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 11 S3 Geography SB): A world-geography book (trade routes, megacities, Southeast Asia) anchored back to South Sudan throughout — humanitarian airlift airfields, Thuraya satellite phones, a tourism plan for South Sudan, Juba as the urbanisation example.
