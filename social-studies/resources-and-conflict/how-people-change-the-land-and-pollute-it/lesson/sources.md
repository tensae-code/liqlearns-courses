**Acceptance count: taught in 17 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Nauru, Tuvalu, Niue, Angola, Colombia, Malaysia, Eritrea, Cambodia, Rwanda, Uganda, Moldova, Mozambique, Jordan, Nicaragua, Bhutan). Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere. Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — land Geography is taught through Pacific farming itself: taro, pulaka and the coconut "tree of life", with Niue's own crops, exports and name-story ("behold the coconut") as anchor examples, and shifting cultivation/fallowing taught as sound science. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — and Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Angola's own way (Grade 5): Geography opens as "geographic space" — the natural space and the human-built space (Luanda port, Benguela railway, Cambambe dam, Huambo housing) — before any physical topic; Angola's own position, borders, population figures (INE) and farm/fishery tables carry the world topics. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado7 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria Grade 7 guide teaches the medieval/colonial world through territory — how societies transformed their natural environment, represented their territory, and used and exploited its natural resources.

Malaysia's own way (MY T5 Geografi Tingkatan 5): the final form is the world's systems book — advanced graphs and photo interpretation, then weather and climate at world scale, vegetation and wildlife by climate zone, energy, and the environmental bill for economic activity.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): Grade 7 turns fully to skills and Eritrea's land — map scale, area and population arithmetic first, then Eritrea's natural heritage (Red Sea coast, wildlife) and Eritrea's own disaster set (drought, locusts, earthquakes, volcanism) as the environmental core.

Eritrea's own way (ER G4 Social Studies): the Tigrinya-medium primary book teaches Geography through the child's own surroundings first — our environment, protecting our soil, water, plants and animals, then maps and globes, community life, and the shapes of the land.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): from P5 the medium is English and the organising frame is the child's own PROVINCE — its location, services, environment, climate, population and infrastructure — before the frame widens to the whole country at P6. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Rwanda is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies SB): the pupil's book teaches the province through named Rwandan things — the five provinces with Kigali City as the case study, Rwanda's four seasons by their Kinyarwanda names (Umuhindo, Urugaryi, Itumba, Icyi), and the country's two international airports (Kigali and Kamembe) in the transport-places lesson.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 6 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): in Senior Primary the strands are re-ordered and Environment and Resources comes first and largest — a full local-environment course (physical/human environment, environmental change, human impacts, sustainability, natural hazards) taught through sample guided lessons with benchmarks, before Civics, Culture, History and Trade. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 5 G5 Geography): first Geography year built as a single Earth-systems arc — planet, envelopes (lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere/biosphere), human society, then a closing "Protecting the Earth" chapter; every paragraph opens with "you will be able to" outcomes and chapters close with evaluation tests, plus two practical applications (map work; a week-long weather calendar).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 3): a systematic geomorphology course — rocks and Earth's interior first, then isostasy, continental drift and plate tectonics, then internal agents (folds, faults, earthquakes, volcanoes in four lessons), then external agents and the modelled landscapes of hot, temperate, cold and coastal environments, closing with the geological cycle.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G7 SS T1): Grade 7 Social Studies weaves civics (citizenship, democracy, rights) around one big geography unit — environment, resources and population — plus Levantine ancient history and Jordanian heritage.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G4 SS T1): Grade 4 Term 1 grounds the child in Jordanian society and dress, then an environment-resources-population unit: agriculture, industry, energy (renewable and not, in Jordan), human impact and Jordan's wildlife.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G10 Geography T2): Grade 10 Term 2 turns to the planet's problems and futures — climate change, impact assessment, sustainable development, energy — then tours the New World continent by continent.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 4 Social Studies): Social Studies IV–VI integrates Geography with Civics, Culture, Economics, History and Religion (Foreword); Geography is taught from the child's home, village and district outward, with Bhutanese materials (stone, mud, wood, straw houses) and local forest and hazard examples throughout.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 5 Social Studies): Grade 5 spirals from the Earth and lat/long straight into Bhutan itself — its location, three physical zones, neighbours, major rivers and protected areas — so world geography is anchored in the home country's map first.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 7 Geography): From Class VII Geography becomes a standalone subject organised in three strands (Time and Space; Physical Environment; People and Environment), every world topic closed with its Bhutan case — climate of Bhutan, vegetation types of Bhutan, minerals of Bhutan, and Bhutan's Himalayan disaster set (earthquake, flood, windstorm, fire, GLOF).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.
