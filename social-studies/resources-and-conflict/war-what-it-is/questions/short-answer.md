# War What It is — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: War What It is — war is an ancient, worldwide phenomenon that nations, peoples and states have practised through their history; it is the opposite of peace — a conflict between two parties in which each party uses its material and non-material weapons to achieve its goals and win victory over the other; its causes across history are summed up as economic — the oldest, the struggle over economic resources to control world markets and obtain raw materials, like the First and Second World Wars; religious — religious extremism and distance from tolerance and its high values igniting destructive wars, like the Frankish wars Europe launched on the Islamic East in the Middle Ages; national — the newest, beginning in Europe with the Industrial Revolution, which entrenched the nation-state and grew the conflicts between them, like the dispute of Bulgaria, Yugoslavia and Greece over Macedonia and the Balkan national conflicts that broke Yugoslavia into Serbia, Croatia, Bosnia and others; and political — arising from clashes in international relations, like the Arab–Zionist wars of 1948 and 1967 (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Types of War — wars are divided by their parties and geographic extent into civil wars — internal wars between two or more local parties in the state or society, whatever their political, economic or social motives and the foreign interventions in them, like the Lebanese, Russian and Chinese civil wars, and the Arab homeland's present civil wars in Sudan, Iraq, Syria, Libya, Yemen and Somalia; regional wars — between two or more parties in a defined geographic region, like the Iraq–Iran war of 1980 and the Arab wars with the Zionist occupation, 1948 and the 1982 aggression on Lebanon; and world wars — the largest kind, with many participants and wide geographic scope, of which humanity has known only the First (1914–1918) and the Second (1939–1945); and the world knew another kind, the Cold War — the rivalry, conflict and tension between the Soviet Union and the United States after the Second World War in many fields: leaders' statements, exchanging accusations in the media, the arms race, the space race, weapons tests, manoeuvres, military exhibitions and recruiting spies and agents, lasting until the Soviet Union's collapse in 1991 (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: What War Leaves Behind — wars leave economic results — they strike all the economic, agricultural, industrial and commercial sectors, temporarily or permanently, through the material and human costs they demand and the losses to the means of production and their infrastructure, such as ports, airports, banks, roads, bridges and water and electricity networks; social results — the numbers of killed, wounded, prisoners and missing, the distortion of the population's age and sex structure, violations of human rights and international norms and laws, falling standards of living and of health and education services, and the spread of poverty, famines, epidemics and child labour; and political results — the collapse of empires and states, political regimes falling and others rising, revolutions and military coups breaking out, and, from the wars national liberation movements fought against colonialism, winning the right of self-determination and the independent state, like the Algerian liberation movement against the French occupation, while the Palestinian revolution still struggles against the occupation for complete independence (any close wording).**
