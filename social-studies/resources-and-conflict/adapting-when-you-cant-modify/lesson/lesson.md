# Adapting when you can't modify

## Do it first

Before you read, look at your own clothing and your own house: both are answers to your climate. List three more answers your community gives to its environment every day. The lesson sorts them into two kinds.

## Sub lesson 1 — Autonomous and planned

Adaptation sorts into two kinds. Autonomous — or spontaneous — adaptation is what people do on their own, from experience, without any plan handed down: the farmer shifting a sowing date, the family raising a store floor. Planned adaptation is deliberate and organised: a community or government decides, designs and builds the adjustment in advance.

## Sub lesson 2 — The worked adaptations

The book works its examples plainly: using land slope for irrigation — taking the environment's own shape as the tool; building on high ground against floods; thin clothes for heat; warm clothes for cold. Each is the same move in different dress: where the environment cannot be modified, modify the human side of the meeting.

## Sub lesson 3 — Adjustment as a way of life

Adaptation is not surrender: it is the second half of the human–environment relationship. People modify what they can — and adapt to what they cannot, and a wise community knows which of its problems belongs to which half.
