# Natural Resources — Using Resources Well

## Do it first

Think of a resource near you that is NOT being used, though people need it — a spring nobody protects, a wood nobody plants, a mineral nobody digs.

What is missing? Money? Skill? A road? Permission? Hold your answer. The book lists six conditions, and yours is probably one of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — What makes use possible

A resource in the ground is not yet a resource in use. The book names the **conditions that help a country use its resources**:

1. **Capital capacity** — money to do the work.
2. **Where the resource is** — its location.
3. **Trained manpower** — people with the skill.
4. **The state of technology**.
5. **Government policy**.
6. **The resource's natural condition**.

Missing even one can hold a resource unused: a rich deposit with no road, no money and no trained hands gives the country nothing.

## Sub lesson 2 — The methods of proper use and care

The book's **methods of proper use and care**: **controlling population growth; using alternative energy sources (wind, geothermal, solar); increasing and protecting plant cover; covering exposed lands; and modern land use — soil and water conservation, more plant cover, and fencing off protected places.**

[Picture 11553 — a cared-for slope: terraces, trees, a fenced spring]

The teacher guide's practical measures list runs alongside (teacher guide — evidence): **use alternative energy such as solar and wind, plant more trees, treat and recycle industrial sewage and waste, reduce emissions, and practise crop rotation.** The two lists agree in spirit — take pressure off, put cover back, waste less — and each adds items the other lacks.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unwise use, counted as one bill (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide counts the cost of unwise use as **one bill** (teacher guide — evidence): **water shortages, loss of forest cover, depletion of minerals and extinction of species** — extended to **pollution, degradation of ecosystems, desertification and global warming**, and to **pollution of water and air, drought, ozone depletion and climate change**.

[Picture 11568 — one landscape, cared-for and stripped]

Read the bill as one sum, not eight separate accidents: unwise use in one place sends its cost into water, forest, soil and air together.

## Say it to your companion

Name the six conditions to your companion. Then take one local resource and say: which condition is weakest for it, and which care method would help it first?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one local resource. Write the six conditions beside it and mark each: present, weak, or missing.
2. Write a three-step proper-use plan for that resource, using at least two methods from the lesson.
3. Copy the guide's bill for unwise use and underline the two items your place already shows, if any.
