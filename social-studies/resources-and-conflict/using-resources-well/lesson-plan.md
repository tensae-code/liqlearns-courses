# Lesson plan — Natural Resources — Using Resources Well

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Natural Resources — Using Resources Well
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** proper use rests on six conditions — capital, location, trained manpower, technology, policy and the resource's own condition — and on concrete methods of care; the teacher guide adds its own measures list and the single bill for unwise use.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the two resource sorts. It climbs from People and Resources' care lessons by naming the enabling conditions and by counting unwise use as one bill (teacher evidence).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What makes use possible** — capital, location, trained manpower, technology, policy, natural condition
2. **The methods of proper use and care** — population control, alternative energy, plant cover, fencing, modern land use — plus the guide's measures
3. **Unwise use, counted as one bill (teacher guide — evidence)** — water shortages, lost forest, depleted minerals, extinct species, pollution, desertification, warming

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six conditions that help a country use its resources.
2. Name the book's methods of proper use and care, and the guide's practical measures list.
3. Describe a cared-for slope: terraces, trees, a fenced spring.
4. Recite the guide's bill for unwise use: water shortages, lost forest, depleted minerals, extinct species — and more.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes conditions with methods → conditions make use *possible* (money, skill, policy); methods are how use is done *properly* (cover, fencing, alternative energy).
- Cannot start the plan → begin from the Do-it-first resource: the missing thing they named is the weakest condition; the plan answers it.
- Reads the bill as separate accidents → the guide counts it as one bill from one cause: unwise use.
