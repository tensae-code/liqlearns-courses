Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2, printed p. 55, teaches: international humanitarian law (the law of war, or of armed conflicts) is the set of basic principles and legal rules aiming to limit the effects armed conflicts cause to people who do not take part in the fighting (civilians — men, women, the elderly, children) or who can no longer take part (the wounded and prisoners); the protection extends to private property such as homes and cultural places and to public property that is not a military target — places of worship such as mosques and churches, museums and schools — and to the medical teams that give humanitarian aid; its aim is to guarantee respect for human rights and to lessen the woes, destruction and suffering of war.

Palestine's own way: human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy.
