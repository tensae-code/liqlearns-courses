# International Humanitarian Law

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: international humanitarian law (the law of war, or of armed conflicts) is the set of basic principles and legal rules aiming to limit the effects armed conflicts cause to people who do not take part in the fighting (civilians — men, women, the elderly, children) or who can no longer take part (the wounded and prisoners); the protection extends to private property such as homes and cultural places and to public property that is not a military target — places of worship such as mosques and churches, museums and schools — and to the medical teams that give humanitarian aid; its aim is to guarantee respect for human rights and to lessen the woes, destruction and suffering of war — as Palestine's book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2): human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy.
