# Lesson plan — Conflict over Resources — What Conflict Is

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conflict over Resources — What Conflict Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** resources are not reachable everywhere — where distribution is high for one place and low or none for another, groups striving each to benefit more than the other are pushed into conflict; conflict itself means quarrel, struggle, disagreement, enmity, fighting and misunderstanding.
**This lesson's own level:** it opens the conflict half of the title. It stands on the resource lessons: scarcity and uneven spread are resource facts before they are conflict facts.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural Resources — What They Are — uneven resources are this lesson's ground. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain why resources breed conflict: uneven distribution plus each side striving to benefit more.
2. Give the two dictionary definitions of conflict as printed.
3. Name conflict's faces from the definitions: quarrel/strife; struggle, difference, disagreement, enmity, fighting, misunderstanding.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks conflict needs weapons → Leslau's faces begin at difference and misunderstanding; fighting is only the last face.
- Misses an ingredient → both are needed: a limited/uneven resource AND striving to benefit more than the other.
- Mixes the dictionaries → the Institute gives ጥል/ጠብ (quarrel/strife); Leslau (1973) gives the six English faces.
