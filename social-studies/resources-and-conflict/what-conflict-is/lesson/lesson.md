# Conflict over Resources — What Conflict Is

## Do it first

Imagine one small spring between two villages in a dry season. Both villages' cattle are thirsty. There is not enough water for both at once.

What happens next, step by step? Write the steps before you read — the book begins exactly here.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why resources breed conflict

**Natural resources are not reachable everywhere: one area's distribution is high, another's low or none.**

[Picture 11561 — two villages, one small spring]

When groups or individuals meet that limit, **each striving to benefit more than the other pushes them into conflict**. Note the two ingredients, both needed: the resource is **limited or uneven**, and each side **strives for more than the other**. A plentiful resource evenly shared breeds no quarrel; a scarce one, striven over, breeds it at once.

## Sub lesson 2 — What conflict is — the dictionaries' words

The book then defines conflict itself through two dictionaries.

The **Ethiopian Languages Institute's Amharic dictionary** gives **"ጥል/ጠብ" — quarrel/strife**.

The **Leslau (1973) Amharic–English dictionary** gives: **struggle, difference, disagreement, enmity, fighting, misunderstanding**.

Hold the two together: conflict is a quarrel that can wear many faces — from a silent difference and a misunderstanding, through disagreement and enmity, to open fighting. The rest of this title's conflict lessons sort those faces: by their roots, by their kinds, and by how they are settled.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the spring story using the book's two ingredients — uneven distribution, and striving to benefit more. Then give one dictionary definition of conflict from memory.
