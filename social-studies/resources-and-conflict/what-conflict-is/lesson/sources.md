Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): Grade 7 turns fully to skills and Eritrea's land — map scale, area and population arithmetic first, then Eritrea's natural heritage (Red Sea coast, wildlife) and Eritrea's own disaster set (drought, locusts, earthquakes, volcanism) as the environmental core.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T2): Term 2 turns from the Arab homeland's nature (Term 1) to its people, its wealth, its problems and its futures, each unit fronted by a guide character — the Architect/Engineer leads Unit 2, the Crisis Observer Unit 3, the Future Pioneer Unit 4. Unit 1 (Geography) covers population in Kuwait and the Arab world; Unit 2 (Geography) resources and wealth; Unit 3 contemporary challenges — natural, political, social and economic problems, and Kuwait's own; Unit 4 visions and ambitions — national visions, Kuwait Vision 2035, future projects, the digital future and Arab integration; the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Health/values — Unit 3 Lesson 3, social problems: addiction (drugs, smoking, digital) and unaware digital interaction, rumours and blind imitation, pp. 57–59. Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T2): Term 2 is the challenges book for the Islamic world. Unit 1 (Geography) is a full environmental unit — pollution in its four kinds, global warming, waste and recycling, and the answer through sustainable development. Unit 2 mixes Geography and Civics — wars, political borders, Muslim minorities, the population explosion, poverty and illiteracy. Units 3–4 are Civics: the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, its organs and summits, its subsidiary bodies, and Kuwait's own humanitarian role. The values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Note: this book counts the OIC at 57 member states (Unit 3), while Book 5 counted 58 Islamic states — organisation membership against a geographic count. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics/human rights — Unit 2 Lesson 3's detail on the challenges facing Muslim minorities: Arakan (Rakhine) in Myanmar, whose Muslims are at least 15% of the country's people and face killing, displacement and the erasing of their identity, printed pp. 45–48; and Xinjiang in China, home of some 11.5 million Uyghur Muslims (2020 census), p. 47 (the migration cause of minority formation is placed below). Education — Unit 2 Lesson 5, the illiteracy problem: adult literacy in OIC states about 73%, Qatar highest at 97%, Kazakhstan's literacy-eradication rate 99.8% against Sierra Leone's 6% (2022 table), pp. 53–55. Civics/institutions — Unit 3, Kuwait and the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation: founded at Rabat in 1969 after the burning of Al-Aqsa Mosque, 57 member states, headquarters Jeddah, renamed in 2011; its principles and political, economic and cultural-social goals; its organs (Islamic Summit, Council of Foreign Ministers, General Secretariat, International Islamic Court of Justice); and its summits — the first (Rabat 1969), fifth (Kuwait 1987), thirteenth (Istanbul 2016) and fourteenth (Makkah 2019), pp. 60–73. Civics/humanitarian — Unit 4, the role of the OIC's organisations and Kuwait's aid: the Union of OIC News Agencies (1972, Jeddah), the Islamic Development Bank (1975, Jeddah — its project support led by transport at 40.1%), ICESCO (1982, Rabat; 40% of the world's 757 million illiterate people live in Islamic states), the Kuwait Red Crescent Society (1966) and the International Islamic Charitable Organization (1986, Kuwait; 32,000 small development projects in 27 Islamic states), pp. 78–92. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": national unity (mixed crews on the diving ships; standing together in epidemics and in the 1990 resistance), pp. 98–99; a ray of light from selflessness — the Al-Hailaq famine of 1868, which ran from the middle Euphrates to Al-Ahsa and the Gulf's eastern coast, and the two Yusufs who fed Kuwait's hungry, pp. 100–101; the home clinic — Kuwaiti women as the family's folk doctors, pp. 104–105 (the Al-Haddama flood part is placed below).
