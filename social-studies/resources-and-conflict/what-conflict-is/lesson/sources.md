Fiji's own way (Grade 9): Society is analysed through Fiji's own structures — the iTaukei hierarchy of Turaga, Sau Turaga, Matanivanua, Bete, Bati, Mataisau, Gonedau and Tauvanua with its ascribed chiefly status — and conflict is settled the iTaukei way, by Bulubulu.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): Grade 7 turns fully to skills and Eritrea's land — map scale, area and population arithmetic first, then Eritrea's natural heritage (Red Sea coast, wildlife) and Eritrea's own disaster set (drought, locusts, earthquakes, volcanism) as the environmental core.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T2): Term 2 turns from the Arab homeland's nature (Term 1) to its people, its wealth, its problems and its futures, each unit fronted by a guide character — the Architect/Engineer leads Unit 2, the Crisis Observer Unit 3, the Future Pioneer Unit 4. Unit 1 (Geography) covers population in Kuwait and the Arab world; Unit 2 (Geography) resources and wealth; Unit 3 contemporary challenges — natural, political, social and economic problems, and Kuwait's own; Unit 4 visions and ambitions — national visions, Kuwait Vision 2035, future projects, the digital future and Arab integration; the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T2): Term 2 is the challenges book for the Islamic world. Unit 1 (Geography) is a full environmental unit — pollution in its four kinds, global warming, waste and recycling, and the answer through sustainable development. Unit 2 mixes Geography and Civics — wars, political borders, Muslim minorities, the population explosion, poverty and illiteracy. Units 3–4 are Civics: the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, its organs and summits, its subsidiary bodies, and Kuwait's own humanitarian role. The values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Note: this book counts the OIC at 57 member states (Unit 3), while Book 5 counted 58 Islamic states — organisation membership against a geographic count. Belarus's own way (Grade 9): The civics track is the pupil's own state and law read from inside — "our state" is taught straight from the 1994 Constitution of the Republic of Belarus (unitary, democratic, social, law-governed; the people as the only source of power; the 1996 referendum; the 1999 Union treaty with Russia), rights are taught article by article from that Constitution (Arts. 1–3, 21–25, 30, 41–49, 53, 59–62) beside the Universal Declaration, and the course closes not with a test but with a discussion, "I — a citizen of the Republic of Belarus", on the citizen's own responsibility before the state.
