# Careless Use — Soil Erosion, Normal and Accelerated

## Do it first

Find two slopes in your mind: a grassy slope that has looked the same for years, and a bare, gullied slope that worsens every rainy season.

Soil is moving on both. Why is one movement harmless — even needed — and the other a disaster? That difference is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Normal erosion — the necessary kind

Soil's main problem is **erosion: soil being moved from place to place by moving agents**.

But not all erosion is a problem. **Some erosion is natural and necessary — old soil must lift off the rock for new soil to form, keeping formation and accumulation in balance.**

**Normal (geological) erosion keeps pace with, or stays below, soil formation.** The slope loses a skin of soil about as fast as new soil is made beneath. That slope can look the same for a lifetime — because, at that pace, it nearly is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Accelerated erosion — the human kind

**Accelerated erosion strips made soil faster than it forms.**

Its causes: **climate change hitting plant cover**, and people — **felling forests, repeatedly overgrazing grass, and backward ploughing**. In practice, **all accelerated erosion is human-made**.

[GIF 11571 — storm water stripping a bare slope]

Its cost is counted in the field: **it takes the soil's fertility, starves plants of nutrients and cuts farm output.** The gullied slope is not only uglier — it is poorer, and every rainy season poorer still.

Compare the book's two damaged-land photographs as it asks: in each, ask which erosion is at work, and what human act fed it.
