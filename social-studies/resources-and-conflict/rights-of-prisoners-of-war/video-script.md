The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2, printed p. 58, teaches: fixed rules protect prisoners of war: they may not be pursued for taking part in the fighting, and their detention is not a punishment but only to stop their further part in the war, so they must be released and returned to their homelands as soon as the war ends; they may be tried only if they commit grave crimes; they must be treated humanely in all circumstances, protected from killing, torture and every act that degrades human dignity; they must be given a suitable place, food, clothing, cleanliness and medical care; civilian detainees must be protected and not held under house arrest or detained for security reasons, and released when the reason ends; and prisoners must be allowed to communicate with their families until their release.

Palestine's own way: human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy.
