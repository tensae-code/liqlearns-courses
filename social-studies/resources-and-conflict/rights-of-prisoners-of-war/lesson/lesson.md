# Rights of Prisoners of War

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: fixed rules protect prisoners of war: they may not be pursued for taking part in the fighting, and their detention is not a punishment but only to stop their further part in the war, so they must be released and returned to their homelands as soon as the war ends; they may be tried only if they commit grave crimes; they must be treated humanely in all circumstances, protected from killing, torture and every act that degrades human dignity; they must be given a suitable place, food, clothing, cleanliness and medical care; civilian detainees must be protected and not held under house arrest or detained for security reasons, and released when the reason ends; and prisoners must be allowed to communicate with their families until their release — as Palestine's book teaches it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rights of Prisoners of War

Palestine Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2 (printed p. 58), teaches: fixed rules protect prisoners of war: they may not be pursued for taking part in the fighting, and their detention is not a punishment but only to stop their further part in the war, so they must be released and returned to their homelands as soon as the war ends; they may be tried only if they commit grave crimes; they must be treated humanely in all circumstances, protected from killing, torture and every act that degrades human dignity; they must be given a suitable place, food, clothing, cleanliness and medical care; civilian detainees must be protected and not held under house arrest or detained for security reasons, and released when the reason ends; and prisoners must be allowed to communicate with their families until their release. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Palestine's way — the book's own teaching way

Palestine's own way (Grade 7 Social Studies Part 2): human rights are taught where Palestine lives them — the Geneva Conventions are read against the occupation's prisons, with the prisoners' own day (17 April) as a national day, and disability is taught with the occupation named among its causes in Palestine, down to the 1987 bone-breaking policy. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
