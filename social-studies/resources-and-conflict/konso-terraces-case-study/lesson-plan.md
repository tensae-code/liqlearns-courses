# Lesson plan — Conservation — the Konso Terraces Case Study

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conservation — the Konso Terraces Case Study
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Konso people farm stony, dry, rugged land behind traditional stone terraces that keep the soil from washing away — work UNESCO recognised in 2011 as the Konso Cultural Landscape.
**This lesson's own level:** it is a story lesson (case study, summary only). It stands on Soil Erosion and What Conservation Means — the terraces are those lessons answered in stone.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Careless Use — Soil Erosion, Normal and Accelerated — the terraces answer accelerated erosion. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Conservation — What Conservation Means — terracing is one of its seven soil techniques. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Place Konso: 595 km south-west of Addis Ababa; nine peoples, most speaking Afaan Konso.
2. Explain how stone terraces on stony, dry, rugged land keep soil from washing away.
3. State the recognition: Konso Cultural Landscape registered by UNESCO in 2011.
4. Name Konso's social shapes in the study: three mora grounds, waka stones, olaahita generation poles.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks terraces are decoration → each stone step slows rain and keeps soil from washing away; that is their farming work.
- Places Konso as a city near Addis → 595 km south-west of Addis Ababa, nine peoples, most speaking Afaan Konso.
- Mixes waka and olaahita → waka are stones for great deeds; olaahita are poles planted as a generation hands power to the next.
