# Natural Resources — Renewable Resources

## Do it first

Think of the sun, the wind and the rain. Can people use them up — finish them — the way a pile of firewood finishes?

Now think of a forest, a herd, a field's soil. Can those finish?

Both kinds are called renewable. This lesson shows why they are not the same kind of renewable.

## Sub lesson 1 — Renewables that cannot run out — but can be spoiled

**Renewable resources replace and renew themselves through natural processes**, and can give without interruption if people follow careful, care-based use.

Some simply **cannot run out on a human timescale**. The book's table runs: **solar energy** (driving the water cycle and warming the Earth for life), **wind energy, geothermal energy, the water cycle, the atmosphere and precipitation** — and the wider stock includes **the Earth's water, air, natural beauty, tides and waves, atomic and geothermal energy**.

The book pictures two Ethiopian examples: a **geothermal plant**, taking heat from inside the Earth, and the **Adama wind farm**, taking the wind's push.

Now the warning for this kind: **use cannot change these stocks' quantity, but it can damage their quality.** You cannot use up the air — but you can dirty it. You cannot finish the water cycle — but you can pollute the water it brings.

## Sub lesson 2 — Renewables that can disappear

Other renewables live closer to the edge: **plants, animals and soil**.

These three **support each other**. Plants hold the soil; soil feeds the plants; animals live on both. Harm one beyond its strength and the others weaken too.

Used with care, they renew themselves year after year. But **if used beyond their capacity they can be diminished, damaged or even disappear**. A forest cut faster than it regrows, a herd grazed beyond the grass, a soil cropped until it is tired — each is renewable, and each can still be lost.

That is the whole lesson in one line: renewable means *it can come back* — never *it cannot be lost*.

## Say it to your companion

Name one renewable that cannot run out and one that can disappear. For the first, say how its quality can still be damaged. For the second, say what 'beyond its capacity' means.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw two columns: 'cannot run out' and 'can disappear'. Write at least four renewables in each, from the lesson.
2. Under the first column, write one way people damage a quality without changing a quantity.
3. Under the second, write one local plant, animal or soil resource and the use that would push it beyond its capacity.
