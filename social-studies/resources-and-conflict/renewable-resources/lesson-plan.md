# Lesson plan — Natural Resources — Renewable Resources

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Natural Resources — Renewable Resources
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** renewable resources replace and renew themselves through natural processes — some can never run out in quantity, though their quality can still be damaged; others, like plants, animals and soil, disappear if used beyond their capacity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Natural Resources — What They Are, and climbs from Kinds of Natural Resources (People and Resources), where renewable meant simply "renews itself". Here the book splits renewables in two and adds the quality warning.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the renewables that cannot run out in quantity: solar, wind, geothermal, the water cycle, the atmosphere, precipitation — and tides, waves and the Earth's water and air.
2. State the quality warning: use cannot change those stocks' quantity, but it can damage their quality.
3. Name the renewables that can disappear — plants, animals, soil — and say when: used beyond their capacity.
4. Recognise the Adama wind farm and a geothermal plant as the book's pictured examples.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts soil with the never-ending stock → soil is in the second kind: it supports plants and animals, and beyond capacity it can be diminished or disappear.
- Thinks 'cannot run out' means 'cannot be harmed' → the quality warning: quantity stays, quality can be damaged — dirty air, polluted water.
- Cannot name a pictured example → Adama wind farm (wind) and the geothermal plant (Earth's heat).
