# Settling resource conflicts — six ways

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: a quarrel over water can end six ways — which one fits? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — How conflicts show themselves

A conflict shows itself from hidden grievance and distrust, through rule-breaking, to sabotage and violence. Its actors run from households to multinationals and NGOs — the same quarrel can hold all of them at once.

## Sub lesson 2 — The six ways to settle

Avoidance steps away; coercion forces an end; negotiation talks it out between the parties; mediation brings a third party who helps but cannot decide; arbitration brings a third party who decides; adjudication puts the decision to a judge.

## Sub lesson 3 — Fieldwork — your own area

The unit closes with a field project: study how your own area manages its land, water and forest resources, and how it resolves its disputes. (Teacher guide — evidence) Every fieldwork group follows the guide's safety code: first-aid kit, compass, whistle, torch and extra food in the group rucksack, no lone working in water or boats, and care on rough and steep ground.
