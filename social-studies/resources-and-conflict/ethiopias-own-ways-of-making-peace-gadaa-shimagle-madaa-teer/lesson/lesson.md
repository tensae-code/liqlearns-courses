# Ethiopia's own ways of making peace — Gadaa, Shimagle, Mada'a, T'eer

## Do it first

Who settles disagreements in your family or neighbourhood? Write who it is and why people accept their word. That acceptance — legitimacy — is what this lesson is about.

## Sub lesson 1 — Four systems of Ethiopia

Ethiopia's peoples run their own long-tested systems for settling resource and inter-group disputes, built on elders and customary law. Among the Amhara, the Shimagle — elder mediators — hear and settle. Among the Oromo, the Gadaa system works under the Abbaa Gadaa.

Among the Afar, the Mada'a is administered through figures such as the Kedo Abba and has managed conflicts between Afar clans and with neighbours. The Somali system is the T'eer. Four peoples, four systems — one shared pattern: the community's own elders, applying the community's own law.

## Sub lesson 2 — What they carry — and their limits

The unit presents these systems as carrying unique values: local legitimacy — decisions are accepted because they come from inside; restoration over punishment — the aim is to repair relations, not only to penalise; and knowledge transmitted across generations in farming, fishing, forestry and water management.

They also have their limitations, which the book names alongside the values. The lesson to keep: conflict management in Ethiopia did not arrive from outside; it was built here, and it still works here.
