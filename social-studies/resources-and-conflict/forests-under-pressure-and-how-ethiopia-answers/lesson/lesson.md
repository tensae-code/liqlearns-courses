# Forests under pressure — and how Ethiopia answers

## Do it first

A map of Ethiopia’s forests in 1950, then 1980: the green is draining away. Read the pressure — then the answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — The collapse

Rapid population growth drives over-exploitation of resources, and the worked Ethiopian case is the forest: forest cover fell from about 16% of the country in the early 1950s to about 3.6% in the early 1980s. More people needed more fuelwood, more farm land and more timber — and the forest paid.

[Picture 21052 — a forest-cover shrinking chart, 1950s to 1980s to now]

## Sub lesson 2 — The answers

Ethiopia answers on three fronts, each taught in this title: community forest protection with strict legal measures; the soil and forest conservation toolkit; and mass planting — the Green Legacy. Pressure is the problem statement; the rest of this title is the reply.

## Sub lesson 3 — Forests under pressure — the world picture (Grade 11 depth)

The Grade 11 book widens the same pressure to the whole world. First, what a forest *does* — its **services**: it supports **livelihoods**, **stabilises the climate**, gives **flood control**, **stores carbon**, and provides **habitat** for living things. Against those services stands the loss: about **13 million hectares of forest a year** are converted to other uses, driven by plantation agriculture, ranching, biofuels, mining, urbanisation and trade.

The remedies mirror Ethiopia's own practice: afforestation and reforestation like the Green Legacy, integration with soil-and-water conservation, alternative energy, bylaws, participatory forest management and watershed protection. A closing note adds that the non-renewables — fossil fuels, gold and diamonds — are under the fiercest pressure of all.
