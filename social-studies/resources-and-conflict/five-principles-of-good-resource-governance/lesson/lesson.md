# Five principles of good resource governance

## Do it first

Write the worst-run shared thing you know — a water point, a market, a field. What exactly is badly run about it? Keep your list; the four failures will probably name it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four failures

Four governance failures root most resource conflicts. First: unclear, overlapping or poorly enforced rights and laws — nobody knows whose the resource is. Second: discriminatory policies that marginalise specific groups.

Third: unequal distribution of development's benefits and burdens — some gain, others pay. Fourth: lack of public participation and transparency — decisions made behind doors. Name the failure, and the remedy follows.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five principles

The principles answer in order. One: clarify rights, responsibilities, laws and mandates in law, recognising the poor's and marginalised people's rights. Two: make equitable access a national policy outcome tied to livelihoods and poverty reduction. Three: require environmental and social impact assessments for all major projects, done transparently and published.

Four: expand public participation and collaborative, community-based management. Five: keep monitoring for unexpected impacts even after rights and assessments are in place — good governance does not end at the signing.
