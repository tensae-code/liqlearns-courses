# Ethiopia among its neighbours

## Do it first

Draw Ethiopia and leave the edges empty. Now fill in the ring of neighbours, one by one, and the sea it almost touches. Read the position.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ring of neighbours

Ethiopia is bordered by six countries: Eritrea to the north, Djibouti to the north-east, Somalia to the east and south-east, Kenya to the south, South Sudan to the west and Sudan to the north-west. It is landlocked — no coast of its own — yet lies near the Red Sea and the Middle East.

[Picture 21059 — a Horn of Africa map with the six neighbours and the Red Sea labelled]

## Sub lesson 2 — What the position does

The position has consequences: trade must cross neighbours to reach ports, so relations with the ring are economic lifelines; and standing at the Horn, near the Red Sea routes, gives Ethiopia strategic weight beyond its size. A landlocked country’s neighbours are its coastline by agreement.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the six neighbours in order around the ring, and what the position costs and gives.
