# Lesson plan — Natural Resources — What They Are

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Natural Resources — What They Are
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** natural resources are the material and non-material things found in nature that people, using their knowledge, can turn to use — and whether a country uses them properly or not is what decides development and prosperity.
**This lesson's own level:** it is the Grade 8 climb on People and Resources: the learner has already met resource kinds, depletion and care in Kinds of Natural Resources and Resource Depletion in Ethiopia. This lesson goes deeper — what a resource is, and why use, not possession, decides prosperity. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What a natural resource is** — the book's definition — material and non-material things in nature that knowledge turns to use
2. **Use decides prosperity** — every country lives from its resources; proper use or poor use is the difference
3. **What resources do for a country (teacher guide — evidence)** — teacher evidence: revenue, jobs, and less poverty

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Kinds of Natural Resources (People and Resources) — it sorts the resources this lesson defines. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: The Two-Way Relationship (People and Resources) — it names your own locality's resources. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a natural resource in the book's words.
2. Give material and non-material examples from your own place.
3. State why proper use, not mere possession, decides a country's prosperity.
4. Name, from teacher evidence, three things resources do for a country: revenue, jobs and less poverty.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Names only things people made → a chair is made; the wood it came from is the resource. Ask what nature gave before the making.
- Cannot find a non-material resource → sunlight, wind and natural beauty are the book's own kind: real, used, and impossible to hold.
- Thinks a rich country is rich because it has more → return to the book's sentence: the difference is proper use or not.
