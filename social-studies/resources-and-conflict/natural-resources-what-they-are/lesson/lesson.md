# Natural Resources — What They Are

## Do it first

Look around where you are sitting. Name everything that came from nature before people worked on it — the wood, the water, the soil under the building, the sunlight, the air. Then name one thing nature gives that you cannot touch at all.

Hold both lists. The lesson gives the book's definition, and it covers both lists.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a natural resource is

**Natural resources are the material and non-material things found in nature that people, using their knowledge, can turn to use.**

Material things you can touch: soil, water, forest trees, minerals. Non-material things you cannot hold: sunlight, wind, the air's work, natural beauty. Neither kind is a resource until knowledge turns it to use — a mineral underground that nobody knows how to use gives nothing yet.

In pairs, the book sets a table to complete: the soils — **clay soil, loam and sandy soil** — and the plant kinds, with the uses of each. Different soils grow different plants, and each plant kind has its own uses. Fill the table from your own place first, then check it against what grows near you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Use decides prosperity

Every country meets its people's needs from its natural resources. No country lives without them.

So why are some countries developed and prosperous and others not? The book's answer is not *how much* a country has. **The difference between development and prosperity comes from whether a country uses its resources properly or not.** A land rich in resources used carelessly stays poor; a land with less, used properly, can prosper.

Report, as the book asks, on how people where you live meet four things: **land, water, air and the living world**. In each meeting, ask: is this proper use, or not?

## Sub lesson 3 — What resources do for a country (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide adds the country's side of the same point, in money words (teacher guide — evidence): **natural resources contribute towards fiscal revenue and income, provide job opportunities and reduce poverty.**

Revenue is the money a government collects. Jobs are people's work. When resources are used properly, all three move together: the resource brings income, the work on it employs people, and employed people with income are lifted out of poverty. When resources are wasted, all three are lost together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition in your own words. Then name one material and one non-material resource from your place, and finish with the prosperity sentence: what decides whether a country prospers?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the definition from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. List six resources of your place — three material, three non-material — and write the use knowledge turns each one to.
3. Write two sentences: one way your community uses a resource properly, and one way it does not.
