Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zambia's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Competence-Based Curriculum teaching modules written to bridge a textbook gap: each topic is taught by doing — observation walks, drawing a community map of local resources, role-play and a school tree-planting and clean-up campaign, with Zambian examples (Zambezi and Kafue rivers, copper, maize soil, charcoal burning). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zambia teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

The teacher guide used for the parts labelled (teacher guide — evidence) is NOT edition-matched to the student book: the guide is an Addis Ababa bureau book (2022, in English) and the student book is an Amhara bureau book (© 2015 E.C., in Amharic); they follow the same national syllabus family but are not companions, and the guide's activity numbers key to its own companion textbook. The guide in hand is also truncated — only printed pp. 1–47 (Units 1–3) are present; Units 4–7 (printed pp. 48–109) are missing. Guide material is therefore used only as labelled teacher evidence folded into this pupil lesson; it never counts as pupil-taught on its own.
