# Forests and the poor — a two-way street

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: does cutting a forest make a poor family richer, or poorer? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Causation both ways

Poverty causes deforestation — and so does wealth. More income alone may not stop the poor clearing: the book refuses the simple story in both directions.

[Picture 27071 — a paired image — intact forest providing services, deforested slope putting lives at risk]

## Sub lesson 2 — Gains and losses in one act

Deforestation can create assets for some poor people, a field where none was. The same clearing destroys the forest goods and services poor households depend on — fuel, fodder, food, medicine, water regulation.

## Sub lesson 3 — The three-part answer

The forest strategy that balances both goals has three parts: strengthen poor and marginalised people's forest rights and participation (community forestry, agroforestry); integrate forests into development through governance reform and investment; and protect local and global values through protected areas and markets for biodiversity and carbon.
