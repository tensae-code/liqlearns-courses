# Governing natural resources fairly

## Do it first

A company wants the forest your community uses. Write three things your community should be guaranteed before any decision. Compare them with the rights in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What governance is — and who pays

Natural resource governance is the norms, institutions and processes deciding how power and responsibility over resources are exercised, how decisions are taken, and how citizens — women, men, indigenous peoples, local communities — participate in and benefit from management. Good governance can serve both people and biodiversity.

Indigenous communities are hit hardest by exploitation — monoculture farming, industrial fishing, logging, mining and dams — so governance centres their self-determination and tenure rights.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights and the three pillars

The protections have names. UNDRIP Article 10: no forced removal — relocation only with free, prior, informed consent and fair compensation. The Convention on Biological Diversity recognises traditional knowledge. And the Aarhus Convention sets three public-participation pillars for everyone.

The pillars are: access to information; participation in decision-making; and access to justice. Your environment, your say — in that order: first know, then take part, then be able to challenge.
