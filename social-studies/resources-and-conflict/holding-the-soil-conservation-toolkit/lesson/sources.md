Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Eritrea's own way (ER G4 Social Studies): the Tigrinya-medium primary book teaches Geography through the child's own surroundings first — our environment, protecting our soil, water, plants and animals, then maps and globes, community life, and the shapes of the land.

India's own way (Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): Contemporary India-II is the human/economic book, matching Part I's physical book: India's resources, forests, water, agriculture, minerals and energy, manufacturing and the "lifelines" (transport, communication, trade) — taught throughout as development-versus-environment argument, with movements and case studies (Chipko, Narmada Bachao, rooftop rainwater harvesting in the Thar) carrying the criticism.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 English): geography starts from the child's own school and home — location in the Grama Niladhari → Divisional Secretariat → District → Province hierarchy, a plan of the school garden, home-made weather instruments, and care of the home surroundings — before widening to Sri Lanka's location in the world in the final chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 Tamil 2007): dialogue-driven lessons (teacher–pupil conversations) that open with Sri Lanka's location and a comparison with the world's other islands, then work through the home and its surroundings, caring for that environment, and finish with map-making and scale.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 4): two half-courses bound together — pedogeography (soil composition, profile, properties, pedogenesis, Dokuchaev classification, soil conservation with terrace/sucalco practice) then hydrogeography (basins and river regimes, groundwater, water balance, lakes, oceans, tides and currents).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 9 Geography): The Intermediate level (IX–X) treats each world topic at full textbook depth and keeps the Bhutan case inside it — Soils of Bhutan, agro-ecological zones and farming practices of Bhutan, and common disasters in Bhutan (including GLOF) alongside the world disaster survey.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 12 Geography): Class XII closes the Advanced course with specialist topics (surveying, geospatial technologies, glaciers and the "Third Pole", Köppen in full, fluvial processes, Davis's cycle of erosion) and Bhutan data chapters — fertility and mortality trends in Bhutan, migration trends, hydropower in Bhutan, and creative industries including Bhutanese film.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Geography student): the senior Geography course is two big themes only — the world economy in depth (structure, division of labour, every branch, transport systems, trade) and then the environment (ecological balance, pollution of air and water, land degradation, biodiversity, ecosystem services), i.e. economy first, its ecological bill second.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 9 Geografía de Cuba G9 (2012)): The 2012 edition follows the G9 programme chapter for chapter (archipiélago → naturaleza → panorama económico → desarrollo social → situación medioambiental → regiones) and is explicit about its own dating: data before 31 Dec 2010 use the 1975 provincial division, while Chapter 6 teaches the new 16-province organisation of 1 Jan 2011. NOTE — this is NOT the same book as the Biblioteca copy filed next (Book 16): that copy is the wholly rewritten 2025 Tercer Perfeccionamiento edition, so the two are placed separately.
