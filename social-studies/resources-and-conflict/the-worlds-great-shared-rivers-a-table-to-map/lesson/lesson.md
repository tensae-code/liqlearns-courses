# The world's great shared rivers — a table to map

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: can you turn a table of fifteen rivers into a world map? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading the table

A data table of 15 major basins gives each river's source, basin size, flow volume, destination, number of riparian countries and length. A table like this is a map waiting to be drawn.

[Picture 27032 — the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, with the Blue Nile below it]

## Sub lesson 2 — The named basins

Among the fifteen: the Nile, Congo, Zambezi and Okavango in Africa; the Indus and Ganges in Asia; the Mekong in south-east Asia; and La Plata in South America.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rivers at work

Photographs show the economic life of African rivers: the Kariba and GERD dams, Victoria Falls, Inga, and Niger fishing. Your task: plot all fifteen basins on a sketch world map, from the table alone.
