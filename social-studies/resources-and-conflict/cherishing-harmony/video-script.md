Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 8, teaches: Harmony (эв эе) is the Mongols' oldest treasure — "united, we stand; divided, we fall"; Alan Gua taught her five sons with the arrows: one arrow each broke easily, the bound five none of them could break; and when friends quarrel — as in the dialogue over a birthday gift, where a careless word about a classmate's family wounds — the quarrel is settled by listening, forgiving and making peace (the topic's opening page is one of the two pages missing on the ministry server; placed from its surviving pages).

Mongolia's own way (Link book L1): Grade 3 turns from the family to the whole moral order — deeds and their fruits, the moving camp, the spirits of land and water, the state's banner and emblem — and ends with the child's first political ideas: deciding together, equal rights, and a government the citizens elect and pay for.
