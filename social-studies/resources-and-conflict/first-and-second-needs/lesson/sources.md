Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 2 Amasomo Mbonezamubano TG) (teacher guide — evidence): lower-primary Social Studies is taught in Kinyarwanda through the child's widening home ground — family, then school, village (Umudugudu) and cell (Akagari), Rwanda's own administrative ladder — with the environment, transport and communication treated as things the child can see and care for locally. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Rwanda is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 2 Amasomo Mbonezamubano SB): the pupil's book works picture-first — every lesson opens with photographs to discuss (“Mwitegereze aya mashusho muyaganireho”) — and Geography is done outdoors: pupils walk around the school describing where things are, and sort the transport of their own cell into land, water and air.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 np-g4b): The newer (2078 BS) trial book reorganises the same ground by units ending in "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth): Earth as our common home, our needs from the Earth, our local level, and drawing a map / making a map of our village; economic life is taught through production, income-generating work and the haat bazaar (periodic market).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 Estudios Sociales G7 full-year): A full national Geography in five units — territorial patrimony and cartography, physical geography (relief, climate, hydrography, resources), population ("¿Cuántas mujeres, cuántos hombres?"), production, and a whole unit on natural-risk prevention — followed by six units of national History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I state institutions (printed pp. 14–16, Civics); Unit VI origin societies (printed pp. 128–143, History, except the geographical-setting topic placed below); Unit VII European expansion and the Colony (printed pp. 148–165, History); Unit VIII Independence (printed pp. 170–184, History); Unit IX economic/political evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 190–205, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty and the Somoza period (printed pp. 210–233, History); Unit XI Nicaragua 1979 to the present (printed pp. 244–264, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 Estudios Sociales G8 full-year): Grade 8 widens the lens to the whole American continent: five Geography units on America's territorial patrimony, physical landscape, ethnic-cultural diversity, space-and-economy and environmental protection (closing with Nicaragua's own SINAPRED disaster system), then five History units. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI American origins (printed pp. 125–138, History); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans and colonial domination (printed pp. 145–165, History); Unit VIII emancipation processes (printed pp. 167–184, History); Unit IX changes in Latin American society (printed pp. 185–194, History); Unit X America in the 20th century (printed pp. 197–222, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 CCSS Módulo Autoformativo G8): The distance-learning parallel of the Grade 8 full-year book: the same five Geography-of-America units rewritten for self-study, repeatedly narrowing from the continent to Centroamérica, El Caribe and Nicaragua itself (e.g. climate zones and water protection are treated region by region down to Nicaragua). Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI pre-Columbian origins (printed pp. 166–177, History); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans (printed pp. 182–201, History); Unit VIII emancipation of the indigenous peoples (printed pp. 206–220, History); Unit IX transformations after independence (printed pp. 224–246, History); Unit X America and the great changes of the 20th century (printed pp. 252–286, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 Estudios Sociales G9 full-year): The classroom parallel of the Grade 9 Geography module (Book 12): five world-Geography units working continent by continent (Asia, Europe, Africa and Oceania in turn for position, relief, climate, hydrography, population and economies), closing on risk prevention with disaster impact in Nicaragua and the world; the second half is world History from human evolution to the modern age. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI evolution of human beings (printed pp. 137–144, History); Unit VII first agricultural civilisations (printed pp. 147–164, History, except the geographical-setting topics placed below); Unit VIII classical civilisations (printed pp. 165–189, History, except the topic placed below); Unit IX the medieval world (printed pp. 191–205, History); Unit X age of great changes (printed pp. 209–223, History, except the geographical discoveries placed below).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 CCSS Geografía Módulo G9): A pure-Geography self-study module (no History/Civics in this book): the Grade 9 world-Geography course taught continent by continent — position and political organisation, physical landscape, human diversity, production and consumption, and a closing planet-care unit on prevention and mitigation — with Nicaragua used as the home reference point throughout.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.
