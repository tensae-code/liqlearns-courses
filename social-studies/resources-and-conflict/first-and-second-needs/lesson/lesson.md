# Natural Resources — First and Second Needs

## Do it first

Write everything you needed before you left home today: food, water, air, clothes, the roof, the cooking fire.

Now write what made those possible at the right amount and quality — the tools, the knowledge, the energy, the work behind them.

Two lists. The book calls them first-order and second-order needs.

## Sub lesson 1 — First-order needs — what keeps life going

Resources underwrite **life's necessities** — and also a society's **security capacity**: self-defence and sovereignty, law enforcement, knowledge and invention.

**First-order needs** are the direct ones:

- **Foods** that give energy, build the body and fight disease
- **Clean water** with the minerals the body needs
- **Unpolluted air** with enough oxygen
- **A settled daily climate**
- **Materials for clothes and shelter**
- **Energy for cooking and heating**

Take any one away and life itself is threatened the same day.

## Sub lesson 2 — Second-order needs — what secures the first

**Second-order needs** are the needs **that secure the first at the wanted amount and quality and organise safety and capacity reliably**:

- **Minerals and energy sources**
- **Science-and-technology experience**
- **The means of producing, changing and consuming goods**

Clean water (first order) stands because pipes, pumps and treatment knowledge (second order) secure it at the wanted quality. Food stands because tools, fertiliser knowledge and transport secure it at the wanted amount. The second order is not luxury — it is the first order's guarantee.

## Say it to your companion

Take your companion's morning list. Sort every item into first or second order, and for two first-order items, name the second-order need that secures each one.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the two shelves and fill each with at least five items from your home's real life.
2. Pick one first-order need and trace it back: what second-order needs secure it at the wanted amount and quality? Write the chain.
3. Write one sentence on what happens to first-order needs in a place where second-order needs fail.
