# Lesson plan — Natural Resources — First and Second Needs

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Natural Resources — First and Second Needs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** human needs from resources split in two: first-order needs that keep life going directly, and second-order needs that secure the first at the wanted amount and quality.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Using Resources Well. It needs nothing beyond the resource lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **First-order needs — what keeps life going** — energy, building and disease-fighting foods; clean water; unpolluted air; settled climate; clothes, shelter, cooking energy
2. **Second-order needs — what secures the first** — minerals and energy, science and technology, the means of producing, changing and consuming

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural Resources — Using Resources Well — the conditions and methods this lesson's needs stand behind. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the first-order needs: foods, clean water, unpolluted air, a settled climate, clothing and shelter materials, cooking and heating energy.
2. List the second-order needs: minerals and energy sources, science-and-technology experience, the means of producing, changing and consuming goods.
3. Explain the relation: second-order needs secure the first at the wanted amount and quality.
4. Name what resources underwrite beyond life: a society's security capacity.

Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls technology a luxury → second-order needs secure the first at the wanted amount and quality; without them the first order fails in amount or quality.
- Puts minerals only in second order and forgets fuels → energy for cooking and heating is first order; minerals and energy *sources* as a secured supply are second order — the book lists both, at different levels.
- Cannot see security capacity → resources also underwrite self-defence and sovereignty, law enforcement, knowledge and invention, as the book states.
