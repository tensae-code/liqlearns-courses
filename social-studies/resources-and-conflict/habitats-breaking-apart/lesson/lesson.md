# Habitats breaking apart

## Sub lesson 1 — What habitat is

A habitat is the physical conditions that let a species survive and reproduce. Human landscape change is the chief modern destroyer of habitats; natural events — floods, volcanoes, earthquakes — destroy them too.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five ways of breaking

Landscape change works five ways: perforation (holes punched in), dissection (cuts through), fragmentation (breaking into pieces), shrinkage (pieces getting smaller) and attrition (pieces vanishing). Learn the five as a sequence — a forest usually suffers them in that order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Extinction debt

Species can linger in ruined habitat without breeding, so the losses arrive late — an extinction debt owed to the future. The loss of large cavity trees is the book's illustration: the birds that nest in them vanish years after the last old tree falls. Paired world maps set mammal richness against the degree of fragmentation.
