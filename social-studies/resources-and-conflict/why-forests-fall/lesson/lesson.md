# Why forests fall

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: a policy can fell a forest without touching an axe — how? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Underlying causes

The driver analysis mirrors Unit Three's method. Underlying causes: climate change, population pressure, culture, and flawed policies that create tenure insecurity and weak enforcement. Policy can fell a forest without touching an axe.

[Picture 27083 — paired photographs — fuelwood collection, and forest burning for shifting cultivation]

## Sub lesson 2 — Proximate acts

The underlying causes work through proximate acts: clearing and burning for farms, fuelwood and timber taking, settlement and infrastructure, mining, commercial logging and overgrazing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Consequences and controls

Consequences in full: carbon loss, biodiversity and habitat loss, broken water cycles, drought and famine, erosion and flooding, nutrient depletion, worsened warming. The controls repeat the course's refrain: afforestation, reforestation, area closure, controlled burning and logging, alternative energy.
