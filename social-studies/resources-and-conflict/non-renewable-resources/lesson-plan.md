# Lesson plan — Natural Resources — Non-Renewable Resources

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Natural Resources — Non-Renewable Resources
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** non-renewable resources exist in limited amount and cannot replace themselves once used — wholly consumed kinds like petroleum, and metallic minerals that can serve again when melted down and remade.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Renewable Resources and climbs from Kinds of Natural Resources (People and Resources), where exhaustible was one line. Here the book adds the minerals table, the recyclable split, and the hundreds-of-millions-of-years reason.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a non-renewable resource and give the book's minerals table: diamond, copper, petroleum and natural gas, uranium, tin.
2. Split non-renewables in two: wholly consumed, and metallic minerals that can be melted and used again.
3. Explain why they cannot renew: geological processes over hundreds of millions of years.
4. Place copper and petroleum correctly on opposite sides of the recyclable split.

Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls recycled copper 'renewable' → recycling stretches a limited amount; it does not replace it where it was dug.
- Puts petroleum in the melt-again box → burned fuel is wholly consumed; only metallic minerals serve again when melted.
- Thinks 'limited' means 'rare this year' → the limit is geological: formation takes hundreds of millions of years.
