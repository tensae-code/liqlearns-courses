Jamaica's own way (Grade 5 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Grade 5 Social Studies pairs a heritage year (Europeans and Africans, national heroes, Caribbean culture) with a physical-environment year — erosion, locating Jamaica and the Caribbean by compass and latitude/longitude, types of rainfall, the Solar System, plants and water pollution. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P1): Social Studies opens with a full Earth-science unit (shape, movements, surface, life components, disasters) taught through Palestinian examples — seasons read off Palestinian crops, surface forms read off Palestine's own map, which pupils draw with its borders.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 5): Three geography units climb from the planet to the country: general geography and the American continent's location, then how climate varies across the continents — with climographs the children draw and read themselves — then the Dominican Republic's own climate, activities and population, ending with a “discovering the geography of my country” field activity.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 5) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 5 transport is taught as a landlocked country's system — goods by rail/road/air/sea, animal transport (riding, leading, herding), and the roadports, seaports and airports Zimbabwe depends on; shelter links heritage sites (Matobo Hills) and map-drawing of Zimbabwe to building safely in disaster-prone areas. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 7 Social Science): Year 7 widens the lens to the whole South Pacific — island types, "islandness" and vulnerability, Pacific hazards, and resources owned by tribes and clans; Fiji is taught as the hub of the South West Pacific.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 is the foundation for senior Geography and History — Fiji's own physical geography (tectonics, relief, rivers, climate, soils) taught in full, then its industries, population and urbanisation, all with Fiji data.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 8 Geografija 8): Grade 8 is pure world regional geography, continent by continent (Africa, Australia/Oceania, the Americas and polar regions), each region worked through the same grid — position/relief/waters, climate/vegetation, population, economy — and the book closes with two world-problems units (rainforest loss, desert spread, water conflicts, the Aral Sea).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 8 Geography G8): Grade 8 leaves the Arab world and tours the "new" continents in a fixed six-lesson pattern (location and surface, natural environments, settlement/migration, population and cities, resources and economy, one big country study — USA, Brazil, Australia), gives Antarctica a whole unit as a land of ice and science, and closes with a unit of contemporary global challenges (water, food, pollution, warming, population, desertification).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T2): Term 2 turns from the Arab homeland's nature (Term 1) to its people, its wealth, its problems and its futures, each unit fronted by a guide character — the Architect/Engineer leads Unit 2, the Crisis Observer Unit 3, the Future Pioneer Unit 4. Unit 1 (Geography) covers population in Kuwait and the Arab world; Unit 2 (Geography) resources and wealth; Unit 3 contemporary challenges — natural, political, social and economic problems, and Kuwait's own; Unit 4 visions and ambitions — national visions, Kuwait Vision 2035, future projects, the digital future and Arab integration; the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/Civics — Unit 3 Lesson 2's treatment of the Palestine question (from 1948) and of nuclear risks (weapons, radiation leaks, nuclear waste), printed pp. 54–56 — the geographic parts (Ceuta and Melilla's strategic position; the Sudan split) are placed below. Health/values — Unit 3 Lesson 3, social problems: addiction (drugs, smoking, digital) and unaware digital interaction, rumours and blind imitation, pp. 57–59. Values — Unit 3 Lesson 5's bullying section, p. 62. Civics/planning — Unit 4 Lesson 1, the concept of a national vision and the traits of a successful one, pp. 72–73; Lesson 2, Kuwait Vision 2035 (turning Kuwait into a financial and trade hub; its seven pillars) and its digital projects, pp. 74–76 — the development projects' Geography is placed below. ICT — Unit 4 Lesson 4, the digital future: digital transformation, smart government services and artificial intelligence in the Arab world (Saudi SDAIA, Jordan's Smart Desert), pp. 79–80. Civics/institutions — Unit 4 Lesson 5, Arab integration: its definition, importance and fields, and its organisations — the Arab League (Cairo), the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development (Kuwait), ALECSO (Tunis), the Arab Organization for Agricultural Development (Khartoum) — and the joint Kuwait–Oman Duqm refinery, pp. 81–82. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": a meeting of old friends in Souq Al-Mubarakiya, pp. 88–89; Nabati poetry as Kuwaiti cultural heritage (the Diwaniya of Nabati Poets), p. 90; the Kuwaiti grandmother, the beating heart of the family, pp. 92–93; the neighbour's duty — a story set in the pearl-diving season, pp. 94–96 (the heritage part is placed below).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T2): Term 2 is the challenges book for the Islamic world. Unit 1 (Geography) is a full environmental unit — pollution in its four kinds, global warming, waste and recycling, and the answer through sustainable development. Unit 2 mixes Geography and Civics — wars, political borders, Muslim minorities, the population explosion, poverty and illiteracy. Units 3–4 are Civics: the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, its organs and summits, its subsidiary bodies, and Kuwait's own humanitarian role. The values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Note: this book counts the OIC at 57 member states (Unit 3), while Book 5 counted 58 Islamic states — organisation membership against a geographic count. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics/human rights — Unit 2 Lesson 3's detail on the challenges facing Muslim minorities: Arakan (Rakhine) in Myanmar, whose Muslims are at least 15% of the country's people and face killing, displacement and the erasing of their identity, printed pp. 45–48; and Xinjiang in China, home of some 11.5 million Uyghur Muslims (2020 census), p. 47 (the migration cause of minority formation is placed below). Education — Unit 2 Lesson 5, the illiteracy problem: adult literacy in OIC states about 73%, Qatar highest at 97%, Kazakhstan's literacy-eradication rate 99.8% against Sierra Leone's 6% (2022 table), pp. 53–55. Civics/institutions — Unit 3, Kuwait and the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation: founded at Rabat in 1969 after the burning of Al-Aqsa Mosque, 57 member states, headquarters Jeddah, renamed in 2011; its principles and political, economic and cultural-social goals; its organs (Islamic Summit, Council of Foreign Ministers, General Secretariat, International Islamic Court of Justice); and its summits — the first (Rabat 1969), fifth (Kuwait 1987), thirteenth (Istanbul 2016) and fourteenth (Makkah 2019), pp. 60–73. Civics/humanitarian — Unit 4, the role of the OIC's organisations and Kuwait's aid: the Union of OIC News Agencies (1972, Jeddah), the Islamic Development Bank (1975, Jeddah — its project support led by transport at 40.1%), ICESCO (1982, Rabat; 40% of the world's 757 million illiterate people live in Islamic states), the Kuwait Red Crescent Society (1966) and the International Islamic Charitable Organization (1986, Kuwait; 32,000 small development projects in 27 Islamic states), pp. 78–92. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": national unity (mixed crews on the diving ships; standing together in epidemics and in the 1990 resistance), pp. 98–99; a ray of light from selflessness — the Al-Hailaq famine of 1868, which ran from the middle Euphrates to Al-Ahsa and the Gulf's eastern coast, and the two Yusufs who fed Kuwait's hungry, pp. 100–101; the home clinic — Kuwaiti women as the family's folk doctors, pp. 104–105 (the Al-Haddama flood part is placed below).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 9 Social Studies G9 T1): Grade 9 turns outward to the whole world through a single theme — the environment. Four full Geography units work it from both directions: what the environment is and how it is divided; the environment's effect on humans (where people live, what they do, state power, disasters); the human effect on the environment (ecological balance, pollution of air, water, soil and by noise); and the other environmental problems (radioactive pollution, global warming, desertification, water scarcity) with Kuwait's and the world's efforts against them. A Civics unit on law and crime follows, and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics/law — Unit 5, law protects society from crime: the concept of crime and its two elements — a legal rule forbidding the act, and an act causing harm — against wrong behaviour and school infractions, printed pp. 90–92; the three pillars of crime — the legal pillar ("no crime and no punishment except by text"), the material pillar (the act actually done; the law does not punish intentions) and the moral pillar (intent against error), pp. 93–95; classifying crimes by seriousness, the worse the harm the graver the crime — drug crimes, electronic blackmail and violent assault as the gravest examples, pp. 96–98; drug crimes — use and possession defined, their effects on the individual (addiction, poisoning, death, falling school and work performance) and on society (more theft and violence, burdens on health and law services), and Kuwait's penalties under Decree-Law 159 of 2025, pp. 99–102; unit assessment, pp. 103–105. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": welcoming the guest — honouring the guest as an old Kuwaiti duty owed no return, the Amir joining the ardha folk dance at state receptions, pp. 108–109; trust and dhimma in old Kuwait — people dealing on word and honour, with few bonds and little work for lawyers, pp. 112–113; the replies in condolence — "Azzam Allah ajrakum" answered by "Ajrana wa ajrakum", mourners received for three days after the burial, pp. 114–115 (the diwaniya station is placed below).
