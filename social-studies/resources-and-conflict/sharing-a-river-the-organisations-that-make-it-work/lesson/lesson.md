# Sharing a river — the organisations that make it work

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: how do countries share a river without fighting over it? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why institutions

One basin cannot be managed country by country: upstream acts land downstream. So the countries that share a river — its riparians — build joint institutions to manage it together.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Nile and its neighbours

The Nile Basin Initiative grew from TECCONILE in 1992 to the NBI of 1999, with Ethiopia a full member, aiming at equitable use and a shared legal framework. Around Africa stand the Lesotho Highlands scheme, the Zambezi River Authority, the Kagera Basin Organization, the Southern African Power Pool and SARCCUS.

## Sub lesson 3 — The model commission

The US–Canada International Joint Commission of 1909 is held up as the model: it has resolved every case referred to it. Good institutions turn a shared river from a quarrel into a system.
