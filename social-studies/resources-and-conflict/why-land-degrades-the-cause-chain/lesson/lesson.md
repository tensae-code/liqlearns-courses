# Why land degrades — the cause chain

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: is a ruined field the farmer's fault — or is the chain longer? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — What degradation is

Degradation is the overall loss of quality and quantity of land resources. It is taught as a two-storey table, because the acts you can see stand on drivers you often cannot.

## Sub lesson 2 — Underlying and proximate causes

Underlying drivers: rapid population growth, climate change, rough topography, improper land use, poor policy and tenure insecurity. They work through proximate acts: intensive tillage, deforestation, overgrazing, ploughing up-and-down slopes, and no fallowing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where the chain ends

The consequences run to lost biodiversity, desertification, low yields, hunger, poverty, food insecurity and migration. Every consequence in the list is a human cost, not only a land cost.

## The MLC extension — density and degradation (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 minimum competencies expect learners to **explain how population density affects degradation levels of vegetation, water and soils** (teacher guide — evidence). The cause chain in this lesson is where that explanation belongs: the denser the population on a piece of land, the harder its vegetation, water and soils are pressed.
