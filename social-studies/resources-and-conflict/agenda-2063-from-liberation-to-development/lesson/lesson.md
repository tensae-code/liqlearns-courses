# Agenda 2063 — from liberation to development

## Do it first

Your grandfather's generation fought for flags and freedom. Your generation's fight is named in this lesson. Guess the four priorities before you read them — what would YOU put first for the continent's next fifty years?

## Sub lesson 1 — The old agenda — liberation (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide (teacher guide — evidence) frames Agenda 2063 by what came before: the **OAU-era agenda** was the struggle **against apartheid** and **for political independence** — freeing the continent's countries from colonial rule and racial government. That agenda largely **won**: the flags were raised; the states became formally free.

A won struggle leaves a question behind it: freedom for what? The guide's framing answers with a **refocus** — the same continental energy, turned to new work. This part, and the next, rest on the guide's fuller statement of the plan's purpose (printed p. 102).

## Sub lesson 2 — The four new priorities — the refocus (teacher guide — evidence)

The refocused agenda has **four priorities**, as the guide states them: **inclusive social and economic development**; **continental and regional integration**; **democratic governance**; and **peace and security**. Read them as a set, not a menu: development needs integration's markets, governance's trust and peace's quiet — each priority holds the others up.

And the frame in time: a **50-year vision running 2013–2063** — fifty years counted from the continental organisation's fiftieth anniversary year, giving the plan its name. The student book's framework and the guide's refocus are one plan read at two depths: the frame, and the turn it makes from liberation to development.
