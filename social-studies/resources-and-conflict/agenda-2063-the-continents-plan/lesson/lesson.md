# Agenda 2063 — the continent's plan

## Do it first

It is 2063 — fifty years after 2013. Picture the Africa you would want a plan to have built by then: connected, prosperous, at peace, self-directed. Now read the plan the continent actually adopted, and compare it with your picture.

## Sub lesson 1 — What Agenda 2063 is

**Agenda 2063** is the **African Union's master plan** — the continent's **strategic framework** for **inclusive, sustainable transformation**. Its horizon is in its name: adopted at the fifty-year mark of the continental organisation, it aims at the next fifty years, to 2063.

Its stated destination: **unity, self-determination, freedom, progress and collective prosperity** — pursued **under Pan-Africanism and the African Renaissance**, the ideas that Africa's peoples share one future and that the continent is rising again by its own hand. A framework is not a wish list: it is the frame into which national plans and the Union's work are meant to fit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lineage and delivery — OAU to AU, flagship programmes

The plan's lineage runs **OAU → AU**: the Organisation of African Unity, founded for liberation and unity, became the African Union, and Agenda 2063 is the Union's forward framework — the same continental house, turned from winning independence to building development.

Delivery runs through **flagship programmes** — named, continent-scale projects that carry the framework's goals into railways, energy, trade, peace and the rest of the plan's fields. When you meet a great continental project presented as part of the plan, you are meeting the framework at work: the vision in the frame, the flagships on the ground.
