# Lesson plan — What pollutes Africa's coasts

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** What pollutes Africa's coasts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Geographic Issues and Public Concerns in Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's coasts are polluted above all by discharge of municipal sewage and industrial waste into coastal waters, with dumping at sea, ships' sewage and rubbish, cargo handling, seabed exploration and exploitation, accidental oil spills and land-borne pollutants carried by air completing the guide's seven sources.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the human–environment lessons — this is the coastal case, from the student book's §7.3 with the guide's fuller source list.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The student book's named polluters** — Industrial waste, sewage, oil spills.
2. **The guide's seven sources — the fuller list (teacher guide — evidence)** — Sewage first — and the mangrove warning.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Push and pull — why Africans move (the same unit). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the student book's polluters: industrial waste, municipal sewage, oil spills and discharges.
2. List the guide's seven sources of coastal pollution.
3. Name the most frequent cause.
4. Explain mangroves and wetlands as natural filters whose loss speeds nutrient build-up.

## If the learner is stuck
Count the sources on seven fingers from the guide's list; if one escapes you, remember the pattern — land throws away (sewage, industry, dumping, air-borne), sea traffic adds (ships, cargo, oil), and the seabed itself is worked (exploration). The most frequent cause is the first finger: sewage and industrial discharge.
