# What pollutes Africa's coasts

## Do it first

Stand at a shore in your mind: a town behind you, ships ahead, a river arriving from inland. List everything that could dirty that water. Then read the seven sources — how many of yours match?

## Sub lesson 1 — The student book's named polluters

The student book names the coast's polluters plainly: **industrial waste discharge** — factories releasing their wastes to the sea; **municipal sewage** — towns' wastewater reaching coastal waters; and **oil spills and other discharges** into the marine environment. Three sources, all one pattern: what land throws away, the coast receives.

Note the geography of it: a coast is where every inland flow ends. Rivers, drains and outfalls all deliver to the same narrow strip where people fish, swim, trade and live — which is why coastal pollution concentrates its harm exactly where coastal life concentrates.

## Sub lesson 2 — The guide's seven sources — the fuller list (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide's stabilisation points (teacher guide — evidence) enumerate the sources more fully: discharge of **sewage and industrial waste from coastal channels**; **dumping of wastes at sea**; discharge of **sewage and rubbish from ships**; **cargo handling**; **exploration and exploitation of the seabed and ocean floor**; **accidental oil pollution**; and pollutants arriving **from land by air and other routes**. The **most frequent cause** is named: discharge of municipal sewage and industrial wastes into coastal waters and estuaries through unsatisfactory disposal facilities.

The same passage warns what is being lost while this happens: **wetlands and mangroves** — the coast's natural filters for sediment, excess nitrogen and wastes — are being destroyed, so nutrient build-up in coastal waters accelerates; habitat destruction, over-fishing and pollution together are degrading the coastal marine environment. (This fuller list sits in the guide inside its migration lesson — it is re-homed here to the student book's §7.3, where it belongs.)
