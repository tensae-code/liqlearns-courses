# Video script — Culture of Peace and Democratic Solutions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Dominican Republic — Moral Cívica 5° Primaria student guide (printed p. 68), teaches: Culture of peace and peaceful solution of conflicts, democratic solutions, and recognising social, family and youth violence.

Dominican Republic — Moral Cívica 6° Primaria student guide (printed p. 73), teaches: Culture of peace and conflict situations — harmonious relations in the world and the pupil as an ambassador of peace who resolves problems without violence.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 5° Primaria (printed p. 158), teaches: The culture of peace — an education oriented to peace that teaches people to value coexistence, with effective communication.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 6° Primaria (printed p. 168), teaches: Culture of peace and conflictive situations — peace as a relation of harmony.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 2° Secundaria (printed p. 180), teaches: The golden rule of peace as a universal principle for fostering peace.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 3° Secundaria (printed p. 178), teaches: Conflict solution and culture of peace — respect and empathy as fundamental to solving conflicts, recognising and valuing others' opinions and perspectives.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 4° Secundaria (printed p. 197), teaches: Building and sustaining a culture of peace is an educational challenge — a new way of seeing, understanding and living in society — closed by a group activity on human rights in relationships and a signed agreement of intentions.

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 6° Secundaria (printed p. 220), teaches: A pupil panel "Together for a culture of peace", with questions directed to the panellists.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 3): at Grade 5 the projects turn constitutional — pupils explore the Dominican Constitution itself, meet the Legislative Power and their elected representatives, learn the child-protection Ley 136-03 as the law that protects them personally, and meet taxes as the citizen's contribution to collective wellbeing.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 5): the primary cycle closes by widening the circle — from how my community is organised and who organises it (juntas de vecinos, authorities, institutions), through tax duties written into the Constitution and the Judicial Power, to the pupil as an "ambassador of peace" in home, school and community.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 9): the combined Grade 5 volume teaches the same constitutional content as the Moral Cívica guide but as social-science study — the Senate's exclusive attributions cited by article (Art. 80), the ayuntamiento's functions listed, and taxes argued in a group activity: "pagamos impuestos para que nuestro país se desarrolle".

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 10): the last primary volume grounds the State in its thinkers and its text — Montesquieu's separation of powers, the Constitution as the backbone (columna vertebral) of the State, the Escudo defined by Article 32, and the municipality split into its two organs, the regidores who norm and oversee and the Alcaldía that executes.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 12): Grade 8 reads the Constitution's own Preamble as the primary text ("Nosotros, representantes del pueblo dominicano, libre y democráticamente elegidos…"), then turns outward — the OAS, a Latin American peace treaty, and EDUCA, the Dominican private-sector education body, as a home example of participative solutions.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 13): Grade 9 civics pairs the abstract frame (human rights and governance, the rule of law) with the country's named social ill — a full unit on narcotráfico, its violence and the building of a drug-free society through education.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 14): the Grade 10 citizen-education unit is built as close reading — pupils paraphrase the texts aloud in their own words: the Constitution as Carta Magna / Ley de Leyes, sovereignty residing exclusively in the people, Locke's division of powers, and democratic coexistence resting on education and the culture of peace.

Dominican Republic's own way (Book 17): the final volume teaches democracy by close-reading the country's own canon — Article 7 and Article 2 of the 2015 Constitution, a classic text on love of country, and Hostos's call for a rational government — and names the system's diseases honestly: party clientelism and support bought with favours "neither legal nor ethical".
