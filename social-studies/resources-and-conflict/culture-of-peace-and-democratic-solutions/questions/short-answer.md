**9. (Sub lesson 9)** What does the ninth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Dominican Republic's way — the book's own teaching way — at Grade 5 the projects turn constitutional — pupils explore the Dominican Constitution itself, meet the Legislative Power and their elected representatives, learn the child-protection Ley 136-03 as the law that protects them personally, and meet taxes as the citizen's contribution to collective wellbeing. the primary cycle closes by widening the circle — from how my community is organised and who organises it (juntas de vecinos, authorities, institutions), through tax duties written into the Constitution and the Judicial Power, to the pupil as an "ambassador of peace" in home, school and community. the combined Grade 5 volume teaches the same constitutional content as the Moral Cívica guide but as social-science study — the Senate's exclusive attributions cited by article (Art. 80), the ayuntamiento's functions listed, and taxes argued in a group activity: "pagamos impuestos para que nuestro país se desarrolle". the last primary volume grounds the State in its thinkers and its text — Montesquieu's separation of powers, the Constitution as the backbone (columna vertebral) of the State, the Escudo defined by Article 32, and the municipality split into its two organs, the regidores who norm and oversee and the Alcaldía that executes. Grade 8 reads the Constitution's own Preamble as the primary text ("Nosotros, representantes del pueblo dominicano, libre y democráticamente elegidos…"), then turns outward — the OAS, a Latin American peace treaty, and EDUCA, the Dominican private-sector education body, as a home example of participative solutions. Grade 9 civics pairs the abstract frame (human rights and governance, the rule of law) with the country's named social ill — a full unit on narcotráfico, its violence and the building of a drug-free society through education. the Grade 10 citizen-education unit is built as close reading — pupils paraphrase the texts aloud in their own words: the Constitution as Carta Magna / Ley de Leyes, sovereignty residing exclusively in the people, Locke's division of powers, and democratic coexistence resting on education and the culture of peace. the final volume teaches democracy by close-reading the country's own canon — Article 7 and Article 2 of the 2015 Constitution, a classic text on love of country, and Hostos's call for a rational government — and names the system's diseases honestly: party clientelism and support bought with favours "neither legal nor ethical". (any close wording).**
