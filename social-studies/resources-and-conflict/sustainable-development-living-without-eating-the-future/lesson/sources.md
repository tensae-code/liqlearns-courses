Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — land Geography is taught through Pacific farming itself: taro, pulaka and the coconut "tree of life", with Niue's own crops, exports and name-story ("behold the coconut") as anchor examples, and shifting cultivation/fallowing taught as sound science. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. The student book's body numbering for Unit Two drifts from its own contents (regions printed as 2.4, factors as 2.5, local classification as 2.6, where the contents and the teacher guide's contents agree on 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5); this lesson follows the contents numbering and teaches no misprint.

Guatemala's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Social Studies is taught through three long “learning experiences” rooted in the country's own peoples (Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino); Geography enters as lived territory — the Maya unity of peoples and territory, care for Mother Earth, and a linguistic map of Guatemala played as a lotería game. That evidence is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Costa Rica's own way: a distance-education anthology organised around "the human footprint on geographic space" — every unit opens with a generating question and objectives, and Costa Rica is used as the home case inside world topics (tropical forest conservation, food production problems). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 10, Secundaria 4): Secundaria 4 is the nineteenth-century history year, and its geography is the geography of what Bolivia lost and what it lives from: the Atacama Litoral and the sea it no longer has, Potosí's silver and the tin north, the Amazon rubber boom, and a closing resources unit that runs from natural-resource classification to Vivir Bien and sustainable development.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.

India's own way (Grade 8 Resources and Development): Grade 8 is a resources year, world-first but India-anchored throughout: resources and their conservation, then land/soil/water/vegetation, minerals and power, agriculture, industries and human resources — every world pattern is paired with its Indian distribution map and an Indian case study (Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad textiles, Bangalore IT, a farm in India set against a farm in the USA).

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies SB): the P6 pupil's book is the fullest Geography book of the set — a whole unit on direction and location works Rwanda onto the East African and African maps by latitude and longitude and time zones, then physical features and climate, and a climate-change sequence (types, change, effects, overcoming) sits inside it; regional co-operation is taught bloc by bloc (COMESA, CEPGL, ECCAS, AU, NEPAD).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 6 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): in Senior Primary the strands are re-ordered and Environment and Resources comes first and largest — a full local-environment course (physical/human environment, environmental change, human impacts, sustainability, natural hazards) taught through sample guided lessons with benchmarks, before Civics, Culture, History and Trade. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.
