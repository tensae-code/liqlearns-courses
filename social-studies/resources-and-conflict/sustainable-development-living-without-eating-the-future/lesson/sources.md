**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Niue, Costa Rica, Bolivia, Bangladesh). Guatemala is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere. Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — land Geography is taught through Pacific farming itself: taro, pulaka and the coconut "tree of life", with Niue's own crops, exports and name-story ("behold the coconut") as anchor examples, and shifting cultivation/fallowing taught as sound science. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. The student book's body numbering for Unit Two drifts from its own contents (regions printed as 2.4, factors as 2.5, local classification as 2.6, where the contents and the teacher guide's contents agree on 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5); this lesson follows the contents numbering and teaches no misprint.

Guatemala's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Social Studies is taught through three long “learning experiences” rooted in the country's own peoples (Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino); Geography enters as lived territory — the Maya unity of peoples and territory, care for Mother Earth, and a linguistic map of Guatemala played as a lotería game. That evidence is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Costa Rica's own way: a distance-education anthology organised around "the human footprint on geographic space" — every unit opens with a generating question and objectives, and Costa Rica is used as the home case inside world topics (tropical forest conservation, food production problems). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 10, Secundaria 4): Secundaria 4 is the nineteenth-century history year, and its geography is the geography of what Bolivia lost and what it lives from: the Atacama Litoral and the sea it no longer has, Potosí's silver and the tin north, the Amazon rubber boom, and a closing resources unit that runs from natural-resource classification to Vivir Bien and sustainable development.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.
