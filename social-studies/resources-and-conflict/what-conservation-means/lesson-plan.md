# Lesson plan — Conservation — What Conservation Means

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conservation — What Conservation Means
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** conservation is protecting natural resources from danger, spoilage, misuse and waste, and using them properly, in the wanted amount, for a long time — with a technique for each resource: soil, water, forest and wildlife.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Using Resources Well. The guide's four-resource technique catalogue folds in as a labelled new reference sub lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The book's definitions** — protecting from danger; defending from spoilage and waste; keeping for future use; proper use in the wanted amount for a long time; a good relation between people and resources
2. **Keeping each resource (teacher guide — evidence)** — soil's seven techniques; water, forest and wildlife conservation defined; sustainable use by rate

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural Resources — Using Resources Well — the methods this lesson defines and catalogues. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the book's working definitions of conservation.
2. Explain 'use that does not exceed the resource's capacity for replacement' (guide).
3. Name the seven soil techniques: terracing, afforestation, crop rotation, reforestation, shelter belt, check dams, strip cropping.
4. Say what forest and wildlife conservation protect, and what harms wildlife.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Merges conservation with 'not using at all' → definition 4: using properly, in the wanted amount, for a long time. Conservation is wise use, not no use.
- Cannot recall the seven techniques → picture the hillside: terraces, planted forest (afforestation/reforestation), rotating crops, shelter belt, check dam, strip cropping.
- Thinks wildlife harm is only hunting → the guide keys six harms, from deforestation to climate change.
