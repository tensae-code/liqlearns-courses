Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Zambia's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 4's Competence-Based Curriculum module teaches managing resources by doing — planting trees, grass and flowers, sorting and disposing of waste, controlling pollution and running a clean-up campaign, inside a topic taught through observation walks, a community map and role-play, with Zambian examples (Zambezi and Kafue rivers, copper, maize soil, charcoal burning); Grade 8's Geography module, built around Zambia itself, follows degradation with a sub-topic on environmental protection. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zambia teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

The teacher guide used for the parts labelled (teacher guide — evidence) is NOT edition-matched to the student book: the guide is an Addis Ababa bureau book (2022, in English) and the student book is an Amhara bureau book (© 2015 E.C., in Amharic); they follow the same national syllabus family but are not companions, and the guide's activity numbers key to its own companion textbook. The guide in hand is also truncated — only printed pp. 1–47 (Units 1–3) are present; Units 4–7 (printed pp. 48–109) are missing. Guide material is therefore used only as labelled teacher evidence folded into this pupil lesson; it never counts as pupil-taught on its own. The guide's Unit 3 numbering disagreement (see Using Resources Well) also touches this lesson's §3.2 citation; the build cites the guide's body headings and printed pages. No student-book error flagged by the placements touches this lesson.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 10, Secundaria 4): Secundaria 4 is the nineteenth-century history year, and its geography is the geography of what Bolivia lost and what it lives from: the Atacama Litoral and the sea it no longer has, Potosí's silver and the tin north, the Amazon rubber boom, and a closing resources unit that runs from natural-resource classification to Vivir Bien and sustainable development.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 4) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 4 heritage turns practical — Indigenous Knowledge Systems (aloe, mubvamaropa, moringa) and conservation of natural resources (gully filling, afforestation); transport names Zimbabwe's major roads and railways and their economic importance; shelter compares indigenous types (tsapi granary) with shelters outside Zimbabwe (igloo, pyramid) and the materials and climate factors behind them. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Malaysia's own way (MY T5 Geografi Tingkatan 5): the final form is the world's systems book — advanced graphs and photo interpretation, then weather and climate at world scale, vegetation and wildlife by climate zone, energy, and the environmental bill for economic activity.

Eritrea's own way (ER G6 Social Studies): one integrated Social Studies book per grade weaving History, Geography, Civics and Culture strands; Geography is taught through Eritrea itself first (Eritrea's location and size, Eritrean settlement, migration and disasters) with very practical, activity-led lessons.

Eritrea's own way (ER G8 Social Studies): Grade 8 is the Eritrea-physical-geography year — relief, hydrology and water shortage, people-environment adaptation and Eritrea's strategic Red Sea location are taught as four full Geography units before the History units begin.

Eritrea's own way (ER G4 Social Studies): the Tigrinya-medium primary book teaches Geography through the child's own surroundings first — our environment, protecting our soil, water, plants and animals, then maps and globes, community life, and the shapes of the land.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies SB): the P6 pupil's book is the fullest Geography book of the set — a whole unit on direction and location works Rwanda onto the East African and African maps by latitude and longitude and time zones, then physical features and climate, and a climate-change sequence (types, change, effects, overcoming) sits inside it; regional co-operation is taught bloc by bloc (COMESA, CEPGL, ECCAS, AU, NEPAD).

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 4 Social Studies): Same four-strand frame as Year 3, now centred on the village/community — place and resources are taught through the child's own village, river, beachfront and village green ('rara').

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 5 Social Studies): Geography is Fiji-first — the child locates Fiji among Pacific neighbours, works on the map of Fiji's islands and divisions, and learns land care through cane farming, river-farm flooding and village land use.
