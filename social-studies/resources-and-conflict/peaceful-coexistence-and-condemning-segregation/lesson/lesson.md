## Sub lesson 1 — Peaceful Coexistence and Condemning Segregation

Tanzania Standard Five tz-civic5-en (printed p. 154), teaches: Peaceful coexistence is living together peacefully regardless of differences in religion, political ideology, sex or socio-economic status; Tanzania's peace comes from solidarity, mutual respect and brotherly love; segregation by race, religion, sex or tribe is immoral and must be strongly condemned — human beings are equal by God's design — and respecting cultural differences, right to life and human dignity keeps lasting peace. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tanzania's way — the books' own teaching way

Tanzania's own way (Standard Five, Civic and Moral Education Standard Five Pupil's Book): Standard Five civics teaches the state from the ward up — Ward Development Committee, district council and its heads of departments, regional commissioner — and the nation through its symbols in detail (the emblem's thirteen signs, the shilling, the Uhuru torch first lit 9 December 1961, national festivals from Zanzibar Revolution Day to Farmers' Day); democracy is taught with political parties' duties and political tolerance, and peace as peaceful coexistence that condemns segregation on any ground. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
