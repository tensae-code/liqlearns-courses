# Careless Use — Water Pollution and Shortage

## Do it first

Cup your hands under the cleanest water you know. It has no smell, no taste, no colour.

Now picture the dirtiest stream you know. What is in it that should not be there? Who put it there?

Pollution and shortage are the two water troubles of this lesson. Name one example of each from your own place before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pollution — purity spoiled

**Pollution is an unwanted change in the physical, chemical or biological character of water, air, soil or food that harms the health, life or activity of people and other living things.**

Water by nature is **without smell, taste or colour — pure**. **Water pollution is unwanted things present in it that spoil that purity.**

[Picture 11557 — a fouled stream below a settlement]

And the end of it, if nothing is done: **without pollution-control methods, such water becomes unusable and can kill.** A stream can be full and still give a village nothing to drink.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shortage — too little, used badly

Shortage is the other water problem — and the book ties it to use, not only to rain: **using the little water there is improperly, without saving and with waste**.

Its end is visible: **animals and plants die or dry up, and people and animals migrate in search of water.** A place does not only run dry from the sky; it runs dry from the hand.

The chapter points forward from here to its later study of shrinking water — this lesson's two troubles are the seed of that bigger one.

## Say it to your companion

Define pollution for your companion without looking. Then tell the story of shortage in three steps: little water, improper use without saving, migration.
