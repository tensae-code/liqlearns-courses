# Stop them early — the four phases of invasion

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: why is the cheapest day to stop an invader the day before it arrives? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four phases

Introduction: arrival and first establishment. Naturalisation: self-sustaining populations spread and integrate. Colonisation: rapid competitive multiplication. Dispersal: establishment in new places.

[Picture 27068 — a spread-versus-cost curve, with the four phases marked along it]

## Sub lesson 2 — The cost curve

The book's curve sets spread against management cost. Prevention, while the species is absent, is cheapest of all. Eradication is possible only while populations are small and localised.

## Sub lesson 3 — Late is expensive

Once an invader is widespread, containment is the best available, and the end of the road is suppression at great long-term cost. Every phase you wait multiplies the bill — the whole lesson in one curve.
