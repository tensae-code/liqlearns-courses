# Reconciliation Restoring a Broken Relationship — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: Reconciliation as re-establishing a broken relationship, on the four principles of mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing and mutual responsibility.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: The signs that reconciliation is being achieved — regained self-respect for Indigenous people, relations of mutual respect, and a changed relationship with governments.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: What reconciliation is, why it matters to each person, and taking action — researching what others have done (Project of Heart, Orange Shirt Day) and designing an action project to teach others the truth.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: Reconciliation as the re-establishment of a broken relationship — restoring friendship and harmony on the Royal Commission's four principles (mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing, mutual responsibility).
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson's fifth part teaches: The Truth and Reconciliation Commission, set up so all Canadians gain a fuller understanding of the schools and their legacy, and the Government of Canada's 2008 apology.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This lesson's sixth part teaches: Individuals and groups across Canada taking action for reconciliation (Project of Heart, Reconciliation Canada), and the class planning its own activity to pass on what it has learned.
**Answer: True**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: False — it stands on Canada's teacher guide only, named in lesson/sources.md, as teacher-guide evidence.**
