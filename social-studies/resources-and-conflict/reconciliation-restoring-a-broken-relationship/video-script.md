# Video script — Reconciliation Restoring a Broken Relationship

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Canada Social Studies 10 IRSR Guide (printed pp. 3–4), teaches: Reconciliation as re-establishing a broken relationship, on the four principles of mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing and mutual responsibility.

Canada Social Studies 10 IRSR Guide (printed p. 53), teaches: The signs that reconciliation is being achieved — regained self-respect for Indigenous people, relations of mutual respect, and a changed relationship with governments.

Canada Social Studies 10 IRSR Guide (printed pp. 64–67), teaches: What reconciliation is, why it matters to each person, and taking action — researching what others have done (Project of Heart, Orange Shirt Day) and designing an action project to teach others the truth.

Canada Social Studies 5 IRSR Guide (printed pp. 3–4), teaches: Reconciliation as the re-establishment of a broken relationship — restoring friendship and harmony on the Royal Commission's four principles (mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing, mutual responsibility).

Canada Social Studies 5 IRSR Guide (printed p. 67), teaches: The Truth and Reconciliation Commission, set up so all Canadians gain a fuller understanding of the schools and their legacy, and the Government of Canada's 2008 apology.

Canada Social Studies 5 IRSR Guide (printed p. 69), teaches: Individuals and groups across Canada taking action for reconciliation (Project of Heart, Reconciliation Canada), and the class planning its own activity to pass on what it has learned.

Canada's own way: Reconciliation is taught as a relationship to be rebuilt — on four principles the Royal Commission named: mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing and mutual responsibility.

Canada's own way: The Grade 5 guide opens with the land and the family as the first teachers, before the school system is named — reconciliation at this level begins from respect for the land and learning handed down the generations.
