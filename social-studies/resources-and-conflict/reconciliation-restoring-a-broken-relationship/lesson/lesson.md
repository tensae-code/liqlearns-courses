# Reconciliation Restoring a Broken Relationship

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: reconciliation as re-establishing a broken relationship, on the four principles of mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing and mutual responsibility — as Canada's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A Renewed Relationship (Social Studies 10)

Canada Social Studies 10 IRSR Guide (printed pp. 3–4), teaches: Reconciliation as re-establishing a broken relationship, on the four principles of mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing and mutual responsibility. Canada Social Studies 5 IRSR Guide (printed pp. 3–4), teaches: Reconciliation as the re-establishment of a broken relationship — restoring friendship and harmony on the Royal Commission's four principles (mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing, mutual responsibility). Canada Social Studies 5 IRSR Guide (printed p. 67), teaches: The Truth and Reconciliation Commission, set up so all Canadians gain a fuller understanding of the schools and their legacy, and the Government of Canada's 2008 apology. ## Sub lesson 6 — Taking Action

Canada Social Studies 5 IRSR Guide (printed p. 69), teaches: Individuals and groups across Canada taking action for reconciliation (Project of Heart, Reconciliation Canada), and the class planning its own activity to pass on what it has learned. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 7 — Canada's way — the book's own teaching way

Canada's own way: Reconciliation is taught as a relationship to be rebuilt — on four principles the Royal Commission named: mutual recognition, mutual respect, sharing and mutual responsibility. The Grade 5 guide opens with the land and the family as the first teachers, before the school system is named — reconciliation at this level begins from respect for the land and learning handed down the generations. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
