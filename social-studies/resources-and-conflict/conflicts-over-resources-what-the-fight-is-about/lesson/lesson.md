# Conflicts over resources — what the fight is about

## Do it first

Recall a disagreement over something shared — water, land, grazing. Write what the fight was really about: whose it was, who could use it, who decided, or who gained. Compare with the four contests.

## Sub lesson 1 — Categories — what is at stake

Resource conflicts are categorised by the resource at stake. Extractive resources — hydrocarbons, minerals, gemstones, timber — are taken out and sold. Land and water are the other two great stakes: lived on, farmed, drunk and fought over where they run short.

A conflict's dynamics are shaped by the resource's own nature: a diamond field, a pasture and a river do not produce the same kind of fight.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four contests — and when conflict turns bad

Underneath the categories, four contests drive resource conflicts, and they often overlap: over ownership — whose resource is it; over access — who may use it; over decision-making in management — who decides; and over distribution of revenues, benefits and burdens — who gains, who pays.

The actors range from local users to nation states. And conflict itself is not necessarily negative: disagreement can be argued out. It becomes destructive when the mechanisms for managing it break down.
