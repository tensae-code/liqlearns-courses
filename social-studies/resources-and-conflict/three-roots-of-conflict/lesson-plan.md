# Lesson plan — Conflict — Its Three Roots

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conflict — Its Three Roots
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** following Kart as printed, conflict grows from three roots: economics (resource limits and rivalry), values (clashing ways of life and world-views), and power (one side straining to dominate, the other striving to win its balance back).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What Conflict Is. The scholar's name is taught as printed — Kart, as the placements record it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The economic root and the values root** — limits breed rivalry; clashing outlooks and forced ways of life breed values conflict
2. **The power root** — domination strained for; balance striven back; ends in accepted defeat and supremacy

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Conflict over Resources — What Conflict Is — the quarrel this lesson roots. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Kart's three roots: economic, values, power.
2. Explain each root in the book's terms, with its mechanism.
3. Sort a given quarrel to its root, and say why.
4. State how a power conflict ends as printed: when one side accepts defeat and the other's supremacy.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner sorts three known quarrels by guessed root before Kart's three are named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Kart's three roots one by one — economic rivalry from limits, values clashing, power and the struggle for balance (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 11562] at the three roots: a picture of three panels — two traders over one plot, two customs colliding, one lord over a village — the student sorts a new quarrel to its panel.
   - ADD AUDIO in the power part: a voice reading the power-conflict ending aloud — the student repeats it aloud.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name the three roots and sort two fresh quarrels to theirs (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/ — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the root-sorting of three real disagreements, with reasons (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts every quarrel as economic → ask what is scarce: if nothing material is scarce but outlooks clash, it is values; if rank itself is the prize, it is power.
- Cannot state the power ending → as printed: one side accepts defeat and the other's supremacy.
- Drops the scholar → the typology is Kart's as printed; keep the name with the three roots.
