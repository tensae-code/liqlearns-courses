# Three ways land breaks down

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: land can break physically, chemically or biologically — can you tell them apart? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Physical breakdown

Physical degradation compacts, crusts, seals and thins the soil — even its colour changes. The book photographs it: pale degraded soil in Tigray, and land on the Soddo–Shashemene road.

[Picture 27079 — Ethiopian photographs — pale degraded soil in Tigray, severely degraded land near Alaba Kulito]

## Sub lesson 2 — Chemical breakdown

Chemical degradation drains nutrients, and tips water and soil chemistry out of balance: eutrophication, acidification and alkalinisation. The land may look whole while its chemistry fails.

## Sub lesson 3 — Biological breakdown, causes and impacts

Biological degradation is loss of biological mass, through deforestation, overgrazing and harmful spraying. Causes split into natural events and human drivers from population pressure to weak institutions; impacts run through ecosystems to livelihoods, food insecurity, and forced conflict and migration, as in Afar.
