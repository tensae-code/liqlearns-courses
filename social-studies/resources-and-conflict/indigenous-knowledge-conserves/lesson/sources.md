**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Nauru, Tuvalu, Niue, Colombia, Fiji, Venezuela). Guatemala is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere. Zimbabwe is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — land Geography is taught through Pacific farming itself: taro, pulaka and the coconut "tree of life", with Niue's own crops, exports and name-story ("behold the coconut") as anchor examples, and shifting cultivation/fallowing taught as sound science. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — and Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. The teacher guide was first published in 2022 against the student book's 2023; the student book is the learner text of record, and the guide is cited as evidence only.

Guatemala's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Social Studies is taught through three long “learning experiences” rooted in the country's own peoples (Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino); Geography enters as lived territory — the Maya unity of peoples and territory, care for Mother Earth, and a linguistic map of Guatemala played as a lotería game. That evidence is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 4) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 4 heritage turns practical — Indigenous Knowledge Systems (aloe, mubvamaropa, moringa) and conservation of natural resources (gully filling, afforestation); transport names Zimbabwe's major roads and railways and their economic importance; shelter compares indigenous types (tsapi granary) with shelters outside Zimbabwe (igloo, pyramid) and the materials and climate factors behind them. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado9 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria Grade 9 guide is organised as questions about campesino life — globalisation, why a resource-rich country has poverty, how Afro-descendant, indigenous and campesino communities face environmental change, and how rural communities administer and defend their territory.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 6 Social Science): Year 6 turns outward to the whole Fiji map — map conventions, island groups, towns, rivers and roads — and to Fiji's climate and sea resources, with conservation taught through traditional taboos and harvest seasons.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 4 G4 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): geography is the first block ("Conóceme, soy Venezuela") and is built as widening circles — the child's house drawn to a stated scale (1 cm on the plan = 1 m), then Tomás's town, then Venezuela's territory and borders, then Latin America and the unequal world.
