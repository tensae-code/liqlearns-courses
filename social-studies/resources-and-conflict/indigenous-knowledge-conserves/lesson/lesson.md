# Indigenous knowledge conserves

## Do it first

Before you read, ask: what does the oldest person you know know about local plants that no book in your school teaches? Hold one example in mind. This lesson argues that knowledge is a conservation system.

## Sub lesson 1 — Knowledge accumulated over generations

Indigenous knowledge is the deep, accumulated knowledge African communities hold of biodiversity and its uses — which plants heal, which feed, which poison; which grounds rest and which bear. It was gathered by observation over generations and is carried in practice, not only in words.

## Sub lesson 2 — The lead case — plant medicine

The lead case is plant medicine. Local plant remedies are locally available, cheap, and accepted by the people who use them — and they are vital where clinics and health professionals are few. A medicine garden known and used is a forest with defenders: knowledge gives the resource a value that protects it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Practices that conserve

The argument completes itself: indigenous practices themselves conserve natural resources. Communities that know what their biodiversity does for them guard it — so conservation that ignores indigenous knowledge throws away both the knowledge and its built-in protection.
