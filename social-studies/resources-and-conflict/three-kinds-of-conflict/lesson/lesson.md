# Conflict — Interpersonal, Intergroup, International

## Do it first

Picture three arguments: two friends over a borrowed tool; two villages over grazing; two countries over a river.

Same word — conflict — for all three. What changes as the circle widens? Fisher, as the book prints him, sorts them by who is inside the quarrel. Guess the three sorts before you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Between people, and between groups

Following **Fisher** as printed:

**Interpersonal conflict** is **between two people whose wants, aims, approach or communication fail to fit**. It **eases if they narrow their difference and reconcile their aims** — the smallest circle has the most direct cure.

**Intergroup conflict** is **between groups of like outlook, culture and descent**. It **flares when one group seeks dominance, exclusion and contempt**, and it **cools without a lasting solution if the weakened group accepts its low standing out of limited capacity or differing outlook** — cooled, note, not solved.

## Sub lesson 2 — Between countries

**International conflict** is **between countries**.

Its chief faces are named plainly: **war opened by one country on another**, and **economic sanctions** — a country punishing another through trade and money rather than arms.

The Abay case study that closes this title is an international conflict in Fisher's sense: countries, a shared river, and the threat of the first chief face behind the argument. When you reach it, sort it here first, then by Kart's roots, before judging it.

## Say it to your companion

Recite Fisher's three kinds in widening order. For each, give the mechanism in one sentence, and for the first two, give how each eases or cools.
