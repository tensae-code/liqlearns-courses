# Lesson plan — Conflict — Interpersonal, Intergroup, International

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conflict — Interpersonal, Intergroup, International
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** following Fisher as printed, conflict comes in three widening kinds: between two people, between groups, and between countries — whose chief faces are war and economic sanctions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Three Roots: roots say why conflict grows; Fisher's kinds say between whom.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Between people, and between groups** — interpersonal: unfitting wants, aims, approach, communication; intergroup: like groups, dominance, exclusion, contempt
2. **Between countries** — international conflict; its chief faces: war opened by one country on another, and economic sanctions

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Conflict — Its Three Roots (Kart) — why quarrels grow, before this lesson's between-whom. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Fisher's three kinds in widening order.
2. Explain each kind's mechanism: unfitting wants/aims/communication; dominance, exclusion and contempt between groups; war and sanctions between countries.
3. State how an interpersonal conflict eases, and how an intergroup conflict cools without a lasting solution.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls any two-group quarrel international → international is between countries; groups inside one country are intergroup.
- Thinks cooling equals solving → intergroup conflict can cool *without a lasting solution* when the weakened group accepts low standing; the book marks the difference.
- Forgets sanctions → war and economic sanctions are international conflict's two chief faces.
