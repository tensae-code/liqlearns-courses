# The vicious cycle — poverty and a damaged environment

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: which comes first — poverty that damages land, or damaged land that deepens poverty? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two definitions, locked together

Poverty is lack of the whole range of capital assets for a livelihood. Environment is the renewable and non-renewable resource base. The nexus — the joining — of the two is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — How the cycle turns

Poverty drives people to overexploit the resources they live on day to day — the only capital they can reach. Degradation then deepens poverty, and the spiral runs downward, dynamic and different in each place.

## Sub lesson 3 — The policy consequence

Because the two are locked, poverty policy must be 'mainstreamed' with environmental care: a programme that fights poverty while ignoring the resource base feeds the cycle it means to break.
