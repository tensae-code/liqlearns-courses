# Invaders — species out of place

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: how can a plant be a weed in one country and a treasure in another? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — What makes an invader

Invasive alien species are plants, animals and microbes spreading beyond their historic range. They succeed as outstanding competitors in their new home — the place whose own species never evolved against them.

[Picture 27066 — a mayapple beside a stand of purple loosestrife]

## Sub lesson 2 — The open door

Climate change and disturbance open the door: flood, fire and ploughing clear ground and stress natives. A flood can also be used against invaders — the same force, turned around.

## Sub lesson 3 — Plasticity — the invader's tool

The terrestrial treatment rests on phenotypic plasticity: one genotype reshaping itself to local conditions. The mayapple decides two years ahead whether a shoot will be vegetative or sexual; purple loosestrife reshapes its leaves to local light.
