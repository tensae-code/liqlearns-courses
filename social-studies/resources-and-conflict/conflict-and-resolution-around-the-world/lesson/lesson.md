## Sub lesson 4 — Papua New Guinea's way — the books' own teaching way

Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 9): Grade 9 Political Science is a full civics course built on PNG itself — democracy's principles, then democracy's PNG history from the Melanesian Way and communal land through colonial rule to the Westminster constitutional monarchy of 1975, PNG's symbols and pledge, its bilateral and multilateral relations, and citizenship that ends with the PNG Constitution as the basis of day-to-day civic life. Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science TG Grade 10): Grade 10 deepens Grade 9's civics into the machinery of the state — the Constitution as the legal basis of government (Section 99's three arms), separation and balance of powers, what parliamentarians actually do, PNG's three levels of government and decentralisation — and ends with a full human-rights unit: PNG's rights laws, the Constitution's rights, enforcing the rights of women, people with disability and marginalised groups, and children's rights under the Lukautim Pikinini Act. Papua New Guinea's own way (teacher guide — evidence) (Social Science Lower Secondary TG, Grades 9–10): the old lower-secondary course makes citizens by doing — a mock campaign and election counted by limited preferential voting (LPV), a mock parliament debate, and a hard look at corruption and nepotism — then turns outward to PNG's place in the global community and how conflicts, including Bougainville, are resolved. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
