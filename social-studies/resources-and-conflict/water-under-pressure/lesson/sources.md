Palestine's own way (Grade 8, PS G8P1): the Arab homeland is toured as one region in a single unit — location, relief, climate, population, economic activities, and water security — before the book turns to Ottoman history and citizenship.

Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.

Egypt's own way (Grade 11): a whole secondary book on development geography alone, built as concept → resources → economy → people: the idea of development traced as it widened (economic, then human, then sustainable development, with its principles of comprehensiveness, integration and sustainability), resources always paired with how to manage them, and the human-development unit closing with full worked country models (Malaysia profiled in depth — position, poverty fall, education, health, electronics and car industries — and Brazil) before the future of development in the Arab homeland.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Geography): The final-year course is hazard- and water-centred for an island nation — volcanic eruptions and tropical cyclones (including Cyclone Heta on Niue), the hydrological cycle and water management, then atmosphere and climate change with Pacific consequences.

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

India's own way (Grade 8 Resources and Development): Grade 8 is a resources year, world-first but India-anchored throughout: resources and their conservation, then land/soil/water/vegetation, minerals and power, agriculture, industries and human resources — every world pattern is paired with its Indian distribution map and an Indian case study (Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad textiles, Bangalore IT, a farm in India set against a farm in the USA).

India's own way (Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): Contemporary India-II is the human/economic book, matching Part I's physical book: India's resources, forests, water, agriculture, minerals and energy, manufacturing and the "lifelines" (transport, communication, trade) — taught throughout as development-versus-environment argument, with movements and case studies (Chipko, Narmada Bachao, rooftop rainwater harvesting in the Thar) carrying the criticism.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 9 Geography G9): every unit is taught twice at two scales — the Arab homeland first, then Syria — with parallel lesson pairs (relief, climate, water, soil, agriculture, industry, transport, tourism); Unit 3's framing is pointed: "huge human resources… and weak investment", and the subsoil lessons are titled "hidden treasures".
