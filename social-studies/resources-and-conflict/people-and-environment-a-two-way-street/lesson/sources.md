Jamaica's own way (Grade 4 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Social Studies is one of four subjects fused into a single Exploratory Core guide, organised by four attainment themes; Geography enters through Jamaica itself — Taino settlement maps, cardinal points, Jamaica's physical features, parishes, weather and the Earth in the Solar System — always with hands-on map and model work. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): The environment is studied from the schoolyard outward — a field diagnosis of the local environment (bare soils, dried springs, waste, noise), scaled up to a map and graph of the whole territory's problems (deforestation, erosion, water), then turned into a pupil-designed sustainable land-use project for a greener Haiti. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

The Grade 10 book's related treatment also stands in the lesson "The two-way relationship" in People and Resources; the parts are cross-named here so neither hides the other.

Guatemala's own way (teacher guide — evidence): Social Studies is taught through three long “learning experiences” rooted in the country's own peoples (Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino); Geography enters as lived territory — the Maya unity of peoples and territory, care for Mother Earth, and a linguistic map of Guatemala played as a lotería game. That evidence is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Guatemala's own way in the básico cycle (teacher guide — evidence): the básico cycle opens Social Studies as peace-building (“Juntos construimos la paz”) — the whole first-year guide is organised around kinds of violence, with cultural values, including each culture's conception of the human–nature relationship, as the counterweight. That evidence is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Angola's own way (Grade 5): Geography opens as "geographic space" — the natural space and the human-built space (Luanda port, Benguela railway, Cambambe dam, Huambo housing) — before any physical topic; Angola's own position, borders, population figures (INE) and farm/fishery tables carry the world topics. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 3): For third graders geography is the home island first: the planet and its components, then the Antilles and the relief of their own country, then countryside versus city, and finally where their own families came from — migration and a family census the children run themselves.

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Geography): A full senior Geography course built on the Pacific itself — high and low island formation, island climates and ecosystems, then population of New Zealand, Asia and Samoa, and Samoan farming (bananas) beside Australian uranium mining.

Colombia's own way (CS_Fichas G1): a Grade 1 "fichas" (worksheet) book built around the characters Alejo and Mariana; geography enters through the child's own region — its landscape, nature's gifts, typical foods, festivals and dress — before any map work.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado2_01): Escuela Nueva self-paced guides (parts A–D: explore, deepen, practise, apply) set in rural Colombia — veredas, resguardos, coffee regions; geography starts from the walk home-to-school, the classroom plan and the home municipality (Aracataca/Ciénaga worked example).

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado8 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria guide teaches Grade 8 through the student's own environment and land — ecosystems where you live, how you relate to your environment economically and culturally, and migration/displacement from "our land" — inside a mostly historical course.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado9 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria Grade 9 guide is organised as questions about campesino life — globalisation, why a resource-rich country has poverty, how Afro-descendant, indigenous and campesino communities face environmental change, and how rural communities administer and defend their territory.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): Grade 7 turns fully to skills and Eritrea's land — map scale, area and population arithmetic first, then Eritrea's natural heritage (Red Sea coast, wildlife) and Eritrea's own disaster set (drought, locusts, earthquakes, volcanism) as the environmental core.

Eritrea's own way (ER G8 Social Studies): Grade 8 is the Eritrea-physical-geography year — relief, hydrology and water shortage, people-environment adaptation and Eritrea's strategic Red Sea location are taught as four full Geography units before the History units begin.

Eritrea's own way (ER G4 Social Studies): the Tigrinya-medium primary book teaches Geography through the child's own surroundings first — our environment, protecting our soil, water, plants and animals, then maps and globes, community life, and the shapes of the land.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 9 Social Science): Secondary geography opens with a full map-skills chapter — grid references, contours and cross-sections practised on Fiji maps — before environment use and conservation, including Pacific methods (shifting cultivation, rotational grazing, appropriate fishing methods).

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG P6 SST Home-Study): East Africa is the unit of study throughout: population, resources, crops, mining, lakes and transport are all taught at East African Community scale with sketch maps learners draw themselves. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: The Cradle of the Human Race in East Africa and sources of history (History, printed pp. 1–6); ethnic groups and migrations — Bantu, Bachwezi (History, pp. 7–16); early kingdoms, chiefdoms and clan systems (History, pp. 11–23); culture and religions in East Africa (pp. 24–28); The Road to Independence and explorers/missionaries/colonialists (History, pp. 48–52); electoral process and democracy (Civics, pp. 53–54); modern communication gadgets (pp. 44–46, named here).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).
