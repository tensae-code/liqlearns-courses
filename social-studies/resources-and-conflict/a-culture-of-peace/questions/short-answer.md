# A Culture of Peace — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: A Culture of Peace in Family, School and Community (Grade 7) — a culture of peace — communication, empathy, tolerance, respect and peaceful conflict-solving practised in the family, at school (including facing bullying) and in the community — as the way discrimination and racism are prevented (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Deciding in a Culture of Peace (Grade 8) — deciding against competition and hyper-individualism — the principles and values of the culture of peace, and decision-making through critical thinking and ethical judgement that weighs alternatives and their implications for the whole community (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Conflict and the Culture of Peace (Grade 9) — peace as a value and a capacity — interpersonal, social and political conflicts distinguished, and participation strategies for environments built on a culture of peace (first placed from Grade 7 in this wave) (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Mexico's way — the book's own teaching way — Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. Mexico's own way (Grade 8): the second year turns from living together to judging and acting — pupils weigh ethical judgements on real injustice, learn how laws are made and changed in Mexico's own Congress, practise mediation, and end as watchdogs: citizen participation is taught as the duty to see public money administered with honesty, transparency and legality. Mexico's own way (Grade 9): the third year turns the course outward to action — participation spaces for children and adolescents, community projects for democratic culture, and democracy's own challenges (corruption, violence, disenchantment) named as things the young citizen is expected to work on. (any close wording).**
