# A Culture of Peace

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a culture of peace — communication, empathy, tolerance, respect and peaceful conflict-solving practised in the family, at school (including facing bullying) and in the community — as the way discrimination and racism are prevented — as Mexico's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A Culture of Peace in Family, School and Community (Grade 7)

Mexico Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 1 (secundaria) (printed pp. 305–307), teaches: A culture of peace — communication, empathy, tolerance, respect and peaceful conflict-solving practised in the family, at school (including facing bullying) and in the community — as the way discrimination and racism are prevented. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Deciding in a Culture of Peace (Grade 8)

Mexico Grade 8 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 2 (secundaria) (printed pp. 265–272), teaches: Deciding against competition and hyper-individualism — the principles and values of the culture of peace, and decision-making through critical thinking and ethical judgement that weighs alternatives and their implications for the whole community. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Conflict and the Culture of Peace (Grade 9)

Mexico Grade 9 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades 3 (secundaria) (printed pp. 264–270), teaches: Peace as a value and a capacity — interpersonal, social and political conflicts distinguished, and participation strategies for environments built on a culture of peace (first placed from Grade 7 in this wave). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Mexico's way — the book's own teaching way

Mexico's own way (Grade 7): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution. Mexico's own way (Grade 8): the second year turns from living together to judging and acting — pupils weigh ethical judgements on real injustice, learn how laws are made and changed in Mexico's own Congress, practise mediation, and end as watchdogs: citizen participation is taught as the duty to see public money administered with honesty, transparency and legality. Mexico's own way (Grade 9): the third year turns the course outward to action — participation spaces for children and adolescents, community projects for democratic culture, and democracy's own challenges (corruption, violence, disenchantment) named as things the young citizen is expected to work on. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
