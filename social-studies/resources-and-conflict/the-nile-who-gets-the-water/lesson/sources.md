Book discrepancy carried exactly as the placements flag it: the student book's Unit Three summary gives Egypt's 1959 Nile share as 58.5 BCM, where its own treaty table in section 3.6 gives 55.5 BCM. This lesson teaches the table's figure, 55.5 BCM, and names the summary's 58.5 BCM as the book's internal inconsistency; the teacher guide does not resolve it.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 8 Geografija 8): Grade 8 is pure world regional geography, continent by continent (Africa, Australia/Oceania, the Americas and polar regions), each region worked through the same grid — position/relief/waters, climate/vegetation, population, economy — and the book closes with two world-problems units (rainforest loss, desert spread, water conflicts, the Aral Sea).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 9 SD Geography 9): A single-country book: the whole of Sudan taught in three units — its physical geography, its human geography, then its environmental problems — with map-work activities on Sudan's own maps (states, roads, the Nile) and an infographic "resources of Sudan" spread; the preface warns its statistics change and are only indicators.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 9 S1 Geography SB): Geography is built around South Sudan itself — its own physical features, resources, floods/drought experience and the Nile — with paper-folding and paper-tearing practical experiments for folding, faulting and sea-floor spreading, and group discussion of local examples in nearly every topic.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 9 S1 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide mirrors the student's book unit by unit (its printed contents list the same 8 units) and adds content maps, key inquiry questions, expected answers and practical activities — including the sea-floor-spreading paper practical — for every topic. That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — South Sudan is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).
