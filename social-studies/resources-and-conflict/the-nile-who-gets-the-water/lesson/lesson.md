# The Nile — who gets the water?

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: over 85 percent of the Nile flows from Ethiopia — so who was given the water in 1959? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Potential and use

Per-capita water use stands at about 1,666 cubic metres in Ethiopia, 1,981 in Sudan and 830 in Egypt — below the 1,700 benchmark. Over 85 percent of the Nile's water flows from Ethiopia, yet use was historically dominated downstream.

## Sub lesson 2 — The treaties that excluded Ethiopia

The 1929 exchange of notes recognised Egypt's 'historic rights'; proposals for Owen Falls and Aswan followed. Then the 1959 Egypt–Sudan treaty divided all the water between the two of them — 55.5 billion cubic metres to Egypt, 18.5 to Sudan — with Ethiopia, the source of most of the water, absent.

## Sub lesson 3 — Toward a fair framework

The story runs on to the Cooperative Framework Agreement, creating a permanent Nile Basin Commission — the attempt to replace division-by-exclusion with equitable sharing.
