# Conflict — Negotiation, Mediation, Arbitration

## Do it first

Two neighbours quarrel over a boundary. Picture three endings: they talk it out alone; an elder both trust sits between them; a court official hears them and orders the boundary, fining the one who moved the stones.

Three different endings — who decides in each? That question sorts this whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Negotiation and mediation

Following **Imboighe** as printed, the first two methods leave the decision with the parties themselves.

**Negotiation** — the parties themselves must **both want to come together and settle their difference by discussion**, often prepared by a third party, and **resting on willingness**. Its limit is the same as its strength: **it breaks off if one side refuses**.

**Mediation (ሽምግልና)** — a **third party accepted by both sides** works to **reconcile them to the very end and bring them back to their former state**. But hear who decides: **the final reconciliation decision is the two parties' own joint agreement**. Mediation **works only while both sides honour the mediator and are ready to keep their word**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Arbitration — the method that decides for them

**Arbitration (ዳኝነት)** differs from both.

The third party here **holds the legal power to decide what will settle the dispute**. It **hears both sides together, identifies the wrongdoer, restores the peaceful state and can pass punishment**.

And its sharpest difference: **it does not need the parties' consent to take up the case, following the law**. Where negotiation dies the moment one side refuses, arbitration begins exactly there.

Now do the book's own closing task: **map which methods your own community actually uses, and how** — who negotiates, who mediates, what goes to arbitration, and what each is trusted with.

## Say it to your companion

Define all three methods for your companion using one question only — who decides? — then add each method's condition or power in one more sentence each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three methods in a table: method, who decides, what it rests on, what stops it (or what starts it).
2. Map your community: one real case for each method your community uses, with names of the roles (not the quarrellers).
3. Write which method you would choose for the spring quarrel from What Conflict Is, and defend the choice by root and kind.
