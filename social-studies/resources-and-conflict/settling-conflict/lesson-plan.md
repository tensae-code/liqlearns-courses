# Lesson plan — Conflict — Negotiation, Mediation, Arbitration

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conflict — Negotiation, Mediation, Arbitration
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** following Imboighe as printed, conflict is settled three ways: negotiation — the parties themselves, by discussion and willingness; mediation (ሽምግልና) — an accepted third party reconciles, but the decision stays the parties' own; arbitration (ዳኝነት) — a third party with legal power decides, hears, identifies the wrongdoer and can punish, without needing consent to take the case.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Roots and the Kinds: a settlement method fits only when the quarrel's root and circle are known.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define negotiation and its condition: both parties willing; it breaks off if one side refuses.
2. Define mediation (ሽምግልና): who decides, and its two conditions — honouring the mediator, keeping one's word.
3. Define arbitration (ዳኝነት): legal power to decide, hearing, identifying the wrongdoer, punishment, no consent needed to take the case.
4. Map, as the book asks, which methods your own community actually uses.

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives mediation the decision → in mediation the final decision is the parties' own joint agreement; the mediator reconciles, never decides.
- Thinks arbitration needs both sides' agreement → arbitration does not need consent to take up the case; it follows the law.
- Merges negotiation's helper with mediation → a third party may *prepare* a negotiation, but negotiation itself is the parties settling by discussion, resting on willingness.
