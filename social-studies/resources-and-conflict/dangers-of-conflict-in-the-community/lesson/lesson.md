# Dangers of Conflict in the Community

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: conflicts are dangerous — they bring death, poverty and arrest, cause disunity and separate family members — as South Sudan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Activity 5 Dangers of conflict in the community

South Sudan Primary 3 P3 Social Studies SB, printed p. 47, teaches: conflicts are dangerous — they bring death, poverty and arrest, cause disunity and separate family members. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 5: effects of conflict (poem)

South Sudan Primary 4 P4 Social Studies SB, printed p. 70, teaches: conflict may lead to a fight, to hatred, to things destroyed and to people dying — we should always live in peace and love each other (the dangers-of-conflict lesson is filed NEW from Book 5 in this wave). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Activity 10 Effects of conflicts in South Sudan

South Sudan Primary 7 P7 Social Studies SB, printed p. 113, teaches: war's effects beyond deaths — refugees camping in foreign countries, poverty as houses, trading centres, schools and churches are destroyed, deserted homes, and poor infrastructure; conflicts are major causes of poverty, underdevelopment and ill health (the dangers lesson is filed NEW from Book 5 in this wave). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — South Sudan's way — the book's own teaching way

South Sudan's own way: Conflict is taught from the child's own ground — its named causes are theft, refusing other people's religions and corruption; its solvers are the payam chief and the village elder; and the flag itself carries the country's peace story, its two white meanings tied to the 1972 Addis Ababa and 2005 Naivasha agreements. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
