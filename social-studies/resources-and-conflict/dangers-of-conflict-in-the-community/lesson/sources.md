**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (South Sudan).**

South Sudan's own way (Book 5): Conflict is taught from the child's own ground — its named causes are theft, refusing other people's religions and corruption; its solvers are the payam chief and the village elder; and the flag itself carries the country's peace story, its two white meanings tied to the 1972 Addis Ababa and 2005 Naivasha agreements.

South Sudan's own way (Book 7): Law enters as the rules each community governs itself by, with arrest and the court as its pictures; the moral half of the conflict unit is a right/wrong table judged by community eyes — gossiping and not greeting elders are wrongs alongside stealing — and the unit closes on resisting negative peer pressure, with drug abuse named as the Health-course part (named here, not placed).

South Sudan's own way (Book 13): Gender equality is argued from South Sudanese lives — an elder who educates his daughters against tradition, and four named women leaders — and peace itself is put in women's hands: the book prints the view that gender equality is a tool that can end South Sudan's civil war.
