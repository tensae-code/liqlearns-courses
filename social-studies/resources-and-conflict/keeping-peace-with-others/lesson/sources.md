**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Mexico, South Sudan).** South Sudan's teacher-guide parts are evidence only, counted nowhere in addition — South Sudan counts through its student-book parts.

South Sudan's own way (Book 1): Community is learned through Okot's own family and compound first — children's duties include looking after cattle and younger siblings, safety means fencing the home and reporting thieves to the police, and peace is taught as class-made rules against fighting plus a peace song in the mother tongue.

South Sudan's own way (Book 2) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide frames Unit 4 as active citizenship — appreciating every community group's contribution (neighbours, church members, families, workers) and learning teamwork through a real community project such as maintaining roads, water and sanitation.

South Sudan's own way (Book 5): Conflict is taught from the child's own ground — its named causes are theft, refusing other people's religions and corruption; its solvers are the payam chief and the village elder; and the flag itself carries the country's peace story, its two white meanings tied to the 1972 Addis Ababa and 2005 Naivasha agreements.
