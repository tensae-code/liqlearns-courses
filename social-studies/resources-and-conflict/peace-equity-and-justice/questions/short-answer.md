# Peace Equity and Justice — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 8 La paz, un valor en permanente construcción — Peace is a value under permanent construction; the UN Charter's Article 1 sets the purposes — maintain international peace and security, suppress aggression, settle by peaceful means; peace also means tranquillity, and it implies equity and justice — a people suffering inequality and injustice has its peace at risk; Central America's internal wars cost thousands of lives, and the region holds two Nobel Peace laureates (Óscar Arias 1987, Rigoberta Menchú); Gandhi: there is no way to peace, peace is the way (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Anexo: Decálogo para crecer en paz — A ten-point decalogue for growing in peace, to be photocopied and posted in the classroom — love yourself, value your friends, find what unites you with others above what separates you, respect others' opinions to feed dialogue, learn to listen, finish your tasks well, fulfil your responsibilities because others need it, work in teams ("nobody knows more than everyone together"), share your things, and strive for peace inside yourself so peace exists around you (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Lección 8, teoría adicional — Peace is a value born inside us, fed daily by family and church, and is the fruit of understanding and listening to others' needs before our own; a world of peace requires equal opportunities and respect for everyone's fundamental rights; at home there is peace when each lives in peace with the others — no shouting, complaints, criticism or lies — building family relations on sincerity, disinterested love, calm, affection and generosity (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way — secondary civics starts from the family as a legal institution — the Family Code (Decree 76-84) enters in Lesson 1 — and builds to the State itself: its elements (population, territory, power), Montesquieu's separation, and the full legislative process ending in publication in La Gaceta. (any close wording).**
