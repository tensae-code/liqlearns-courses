# Lesson plan — Natural Resources — How Use Changes Over Time

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Natural Resources — How Use Changes Over Time
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** demand on resources keeps growing, widening and changing as population, civilisation, technology and cities grow — present use is hugely wasteful, and the guide names the five pressure factors and what rising consumption does.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First and Second Needs. The guide's pressure factors and consumption consequences fold in as labelled evidence.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Contrast ancient basic needs with today's multiplying non-basic wants.
2. Name the four growers: population growth, deepening civilisation, technology, city growth.
3. State the book's verdict on present use: hugely wasteful, giant pressure, giving could be interrupted.
4. Name the guide's five pressure factors and five consequences of rising consumption.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks basic needs have changed → basics are similar; it is non-basic wants that multiply, driven by the four growers.
- Counts technology only as a help → the guide lists industrial and technological development among the pressure factors too; both faces are in the books.
- Forgets uninhabited places → air, oceans, deserts and polar regions are named precious resources owed to the future generation.
