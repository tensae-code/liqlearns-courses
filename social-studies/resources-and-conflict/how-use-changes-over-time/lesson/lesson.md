# Natural Resources — How Use Changes Over Time

## Do it first

Ask a grandparent, or imagine one: what did a family need, fifty years ago, that nature had to give? Food, water, wood, grazing, a roof.

Now list what your family 'needs' this week. How much longer is the second list?

That lengthening list is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Wants grow

The ancients' basic needs were met by what nature gave. Today's basic needs are similar — food, water, shelter have not changed.

What has changed is everything around them. **Population growth, the deepening of civilisation, technology and city growth keep multiplying non-basic wants**, so **human demand on resources keeps growing, widening and changing**.

[Picture 11555 — one valley, three eras of wants]

Each new want reaches back to a resource: a phone to minerals, a city house to stone, sand and timber, a motorbike to fuel. The list lengthens; the land does not.

## Sub lesson 2 — Waste and the duty to the future

The book's verdict on the present is plain: **present use is described as hugely wasteful, putting giant pressure on the world's resources until their giving could be interrupted.**

And the duty reaches even where nobody lives: **uninhabited places — air, oceans, deserts, polar regions — are still precious resources to be handed carefully to the future generation.**

Care in use has therefore become decisive — not a decoration on use, but the thing that decides whether resources keep giving at all.

## Sub lesson 3 — What drives misuse (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide names the pressures behind misuse (teacher guide — evidence): **overpopulation, poor farming practices, overconsumption, pollution, and industrial and technological development**.

[Picture 11567 — five pressures on one landscape]

And its keyed answers name what high and changing consumption does: **soil degradation, water shortages, biodiversity loss, damage to ecosystem functions and global warming** — with **food insecurity** as the sustainability worry when changing consumption meets population increase.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four growers of demand. Then name the guide's five pressure factors, and match one pressure near your home to one consequence from the guide's list.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw your valley in three eras: a grandparent's time, now, and your grandchildren's time if present use continues. Mark the forest, water and soil in each.
2. List your family's five newest wants and the resource each one reaches back to.
3. Write the book's waste verdict from memory, then check it word by word.
