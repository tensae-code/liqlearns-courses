# Social Cohesion and Living Together

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: peaceful talk (listening to others, not shouting, not joining conversations that are not ours) and peaceful school life (working together, helping, working with love, not discouraging others) — as Rwanda's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Promoting the Culture of Peace

Rwanda P2 SB Kiny, printed p. 52, teaches: peaceful talk (listening to others, not shouting, not joining conversations that are not ours) and peaceful school life (working together, helping, working with love, not discouraging others). ## Sub lesson 5 — Rwanda's way — the books' own teaching way

Rwanda's own way (Primary Two — Rwanda P2 SB Kiny): P2 civics starts with who you belong to (small family, wide family, the exact Kinyarwanda name for every relation), then the two smallest rungs of the state — the village and the cell, their elected committees and what umuganda owes them — and the behaviour of peace: playing without discrimination, listening without shouting, and the rights of children and of people with disabilities

Rwanda's own way (Primary Five — Rwanda Grade 5 P5 SB): the province is the child's civic world — complementarity (everyone's work fitting together) is the organising value, and national symbols, the 2003/2015 constitution and province leadership are taught as Rwanda's own frame inside it

Rwanda's own way (Primary Six — Rwanda P6 SB): P6 civics is nationhood — unity through 'Ndi Umunyarwanda', co-operation through umuganda, the world's organisations and Rwanda's own (REB, NURC), Rwandan values and Itorero, and the three organs with the citizen's duties beside the government's This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
