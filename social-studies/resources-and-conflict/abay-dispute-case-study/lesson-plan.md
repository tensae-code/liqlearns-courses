# Lesson plan — Conflict — the Abay Dispute Case Study

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Resources and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conflict — the Abay Dispute Case Study
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Resources and Conflict (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md: natural resources, their care, and conflict over resources).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Abay (Nile), the world's longest transboundary river, crosses 11 basin countries; Ethiopia's Abay and Tekeze carry about 85% of the flow, the Grand Renaissance Dam is built mainly for electricity — and the guide names the politics of such shared water hydro-politics.
**This lesson's own level:** it is a story lesson (case study, summary only) and the title's summit: roots, kinds and settlement all meet in one real river. The guide's hydro-politics frame folds in as labelled evidence.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The river and its shares** — 11 basin countries; White Nile 15%, Abay 70%, Tekeze/Atbara 15%; Abay and Tekeze about 85%
2. **What the basin states seek — and the block** — irrigation, power, supply, fisheries, tourism, food security; the dam for electricity; Egypt's obstruction and war talk
3. **Hydro-politics — the guide's frame (teacher guide — evidence)** — water conflict defined; hydro-politics as the study of inter-state politics over shared water; towards equitable utilisation

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Conflict — Negotiation, Mediation, Arbitration, and the Roots and Kinds lessons — classify this case with them. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the basin's 11 countries by upper, middle and lower course as the study lists them.
2. Give the flow shares: White Nile about 15%, Abay about 70%, Tekeze/Atbara about 15% — Abay and Tekeze about 85% together.
3. State what basin states now seek from the river, what the Renaissance Dam is mainly for, and the study's account of Egypt's obstruction and war talk.
4. Define hydro-politics and water conflict (guide), and name the move towards equitable utilisation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the shares → chant them in order down the river of the story: White Nile 15, Abay 70, Tekeze 15 — and 70 + 15 = 85 from Ethiopian ground.
- Puts Egypt among the contributors → the study's point: Egypt contributes no water, yet obstructs the others' use.
- Defines hydro-politics as river engineering → it is the study of inter-state *politics* over the management of shared water resources.
