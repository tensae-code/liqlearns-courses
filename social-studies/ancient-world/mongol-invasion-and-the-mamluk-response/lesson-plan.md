# Lesson plan — The Mongol invasion — and the Mamluk response

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Mongol invasion — and the Mamluk response
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Libya, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Mongol invasion and the Islamic response** — Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow
2. **Mongol rule in Iraq** — after Baghdad's fall, Mongol Ilkhans rule Iraq while the rest of the Arab world lives under other masters until the Ottomans come
3. **The Mongol raids** — Genghis Khan unites the steppe; Hulagu burns Baghdad's libraries and kills the caliph in 1258; Sultan Qutuz executes the Mongol envoys, and at Ain Jalut in 1260 Baybars breaks the myth of Mongol invincibility
4. **The Mamluks halt the Mongols** — the Mongols sack Baghdad in 1258 and march on the Levant and Egypt; the Mamluks destroy their army at Ain Jalut in 1260 and clear them from the Levant

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
