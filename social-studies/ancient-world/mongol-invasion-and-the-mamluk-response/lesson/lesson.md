# The Mongol invasion — and the Mamluk response

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Mongol invasion and the Islamic response

Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongol rule in Iraq

After Baghdad's fall, Mongol Ilkhans rule Iraq while the rest of the Arab world lives under other masters until the Ottomans come

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Mongol raids

Genghis Khan unites the steppe; Hulagu burns Baghdad's libraries and kills the caliph in 1258; Sultan Qutuz executes the Mongol envoys, and at Ain Jalut in 1260 Baybars breaks the myth of Mongol invincibility

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Mamluks halt the Mongols

The Mongols sack Baghdad in 1258 and march on the Levant and Egypt; the Mamluks destroy their army at Ain Jalut in 1260 and clear them from the Levant

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: The Mongol invasion and the Islamic response — Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow; Mongol rule in Iraq — after Baghdad's fall, Mongol Ilkhans rule Iraq while the rest of the Arab world lives under other masters until the Ottomans come; The Mongol raids — Genghis Khan unites the steppe; Hulagu burns Baghdad's libraries and kills the caliph in 1258; Sultan Qutuz executes the Mongol envoys, and at Ain Jalut in 1260 Baybars breaks the myth of Mongol invincibility; The Mamluks halt the Mongols — the Mongols sack Baghdad in 1258 and march on the Levant and Egypt; the Mamluks destroy their army at Ain Jalut in 1260 and clear them from the Levant. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
