# The first Turks of the steppe

## Do it first

Imagine a land with no fences and no end: grass to every horizon, winters that kill, and wealth that walks on four legs. A people shaped by that land will not live like farmers. Before you read on, guess their house, their food and their fastest treasure. Hold your guesses; the steppe has already answered all three.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first Turkish traces, and a way of life shaped by geography

Turkey's Grade 9 book gives the Turks a whole unit of their own, and it starts with the land. The first Turkish traces in Eurasia — the Huns among them — belong to the steppe: the endless grassland belt across the continent's heart.

Then the book reads the life out of the land. The horse gives speed and milk and war-power; the felt tent gives a house that packs up and moves with the herds; the herds themselves are wealth that walks. Horse, felt tent and herds are not customs chosen at random — they are answers to the grassland. The book's beat: the geography of the steppe writes the Turks' whole way of life.

[Picture slot — a picture of a steppe camp — felt tents in a ring, herds of horses and sheep grazing to the horizon, a rider watching over them — the student points to the tent and the herds and says how each one answers the grassland.]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 1 beat: The first Turkish traces, and a way of life shaped by geography. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory: name the steppe as the Turks' first homeland in the book, and the Huns among their first traces in Eurasia.
2. Write, from memory: name the three answers to the grassland: horse, felt tent and herds.
3. Write, from memory: say why a felt tent suits the steppe: it packs up and moves with the herds.
