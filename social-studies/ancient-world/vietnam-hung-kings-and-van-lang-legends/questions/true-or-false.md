# Vietnam Hung Kings And Van Lang Legends — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** dragon lord Lạc Long Quân and fairy Âu Cơ have 100 children; the eldest becomes Hùng Vương and founds Văn Lang at Phong Châu; later prince Lang Liêu wins the throne with humble rice cakes, bánh chưng and bánh giầy.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Bài 7, legends of the Hùng Kings' era

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Lịch sử và Địa lí 4 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Bài 7, legends of the Hùng Kings' era

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** the 18th Hùng King sets Mountain God and Water God a bride-price race for his daughter Mỵ Nương; the loser raises the rivers every year in revenge, explaining Vietnam's annual floods.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Bài 5, the legend of Sơn Tinh and Thuỷ Tinh

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Lịch sử và Địa lí 5 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Bài 5, the legend of Sơn Tinh and Thuỷ Tinh

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** one altar logic runs from a peasant family's shelf to the national festival at Đền Hùng.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Chuyên đề 1 Ancestor worship scaled to a nation: the family cult of the dead widens to clan, village and country until the Hùng Kings stand as the whole people's common ancestors — a state rite already under Lê Thánh Tông, fixed to the 10th day of the third lunar month by Khải Định in 1917, given a officials' holiday by Hồ Chí Minh's decree of February 1946, and a paid public holiday from 2007; Phạm Văn Đồng in 1969 calls it the nation's unique folk belief
