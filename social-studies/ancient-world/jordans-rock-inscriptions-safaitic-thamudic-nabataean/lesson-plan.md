# Lesson plan — Jordan's rock inscriptions — Safaitic, Thamudic and Nabataean

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Jordan's rock inscriptions — Safaitic, Thamudic and Nabataean
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the badia from north to south is an open museum of scratched stones, a register of every people and caravan that passed; the Safaitic herders of the north-eastern desert leave their names, tribes, sheep, camels and horses; the Thamudic tribes known to Assyrian records draw people and animals across Wadi Rum; the Nabataeans write an Aramaic script that grows, in time, into the Arabic script itself — the Turkmaniyya tomb text at Petra is its longest inscription — and the Bedouin wasm brand-marks on camels still echo the ancient letters
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
