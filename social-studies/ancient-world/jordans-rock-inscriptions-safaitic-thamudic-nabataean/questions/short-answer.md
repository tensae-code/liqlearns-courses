# Jordan's rock inscriptions — Safaitic, Thamudic and Nabataean — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Lesson 4: the ancient rock inscriptions of Jordan.
**Answer: the badia from north to south is an open museum of scratched stones, a register of every people and caravan that passed; the Safaitic herders of the north-eastern desert leave their names, tribes, sheep, camels and horses; the Thamudic tribes known to Assyrian records draw people and animals across Wadi Rum; the Nabataeans write an Aramaic script that grows, in time, into the Arabic script itself — the Turkmaniyya tomb text at Petra is its longest inscription — and the Bedouin wasm brand-marks on camels still echo the ancient letters (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
