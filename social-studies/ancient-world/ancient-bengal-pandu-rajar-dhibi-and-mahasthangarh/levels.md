# Ancient Bengal — Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh — villages grow into a city
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Say what Pandu Rajar Dhibi is: a copper-and-stone-age mound settlement. Name Bengal's first walled city: Mahasthangarh (Pundranagar), on the Karatoya. Say what Bengal's oldest Brahmi stone inscription is and its date: c. 300 BCE, at Mahasthangarh.

## Sub lesson 2 — The agricultural economy — rice, inscriptions and copper-plate grants
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Name the three kinds of evidence for ancient Bengal's farming: granary rice, the inscription's crop lists, copper-plate land grants. Name crops and produce the grants name: mango, betel, coconut and cotton (and rice). Say how old the granary rice near Pandu Rajar Dhibi is: about 3,500 years.
