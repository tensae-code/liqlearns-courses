# Video script — Ancient Bengal — Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bangladesh book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Two mounds hold Bengal's beginning. The Grade 6 book takes us first to Pandu Rajar Dhibi, a settlement mound from the copper-and-stone age.

Then to Mahasthangarh on the Karatoya — the walled city of Pundranagar, with Bengal's oldest Brahmi stone inscription, from about 300 BCE.

Village to city: that is the growth the two sites prove.

The Grade 7 book asks what ancient Bengal lived on — and the ground answers: rice.

A granary near Pandu Rajar Dhibi held rice 3,500 years old. The Mahasthan inscription lists crops, and copper-plate land grants name mango, betel, coconut and cotton.

Three kinds of evidence, one verdict: a rich farming Bengal.
