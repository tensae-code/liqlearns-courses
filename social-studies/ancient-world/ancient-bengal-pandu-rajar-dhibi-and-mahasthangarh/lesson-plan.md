# Lesson plan — Ancient Bengal — Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient Bengal — Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Bangladesh, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two mounds tell Bengal's beginning: Pandu Rajar Dhibi, a copper-and-stone-age settlement, and Mahasthangarh (Pundranagar) on the Karatoya, Bengal's first walled city, holding its oldest Brahmi stone inscription (c. 300 BCE).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh — villages grow into a city** — a copper-and-stone-age mound at Pandu Rajar Dhibi and the walled city of Mahasthangarh (Pundranagar) on the Karatoya, with Bengal's oldest Brahmi stone inscription (c. 300 BCE), show villages growing into Bengal's first city
2. **The agricultural economy — rice, inscriptions and copper-plate grants** — 3,500-year-old rice from a granary near Pandu Rajar Dhibi, the Mahasthan Brahmi inscription's crop lists, and copper-plate land grants naming mango, betel, coconut and cotton prove a rich farming Bengal

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what Pandu Rajar Dhibi is: a copper-and-stone-age mound settlement.
2. Name Bengal's first walled city: Mahasthangarh (Pundranagar), on the Karatoya.
3. Say what Bengal's oldest Brahmi stone inscription is and its date: c. 300 BCE, at Mahasthangarh.
4. Name the three kinds of evidence for ancient Bengal's farming: granary rice, the inscription's crop lists, copper-plate land grants.
5. Name crops and produce the grants name: mango, betel, coconut and cotton (and rice).
6. Say how old the granary rice near Pandu Rajar Dhibi is: about 3,500 years.
