# Lesson plan — Greek Myth Heracles And His Labours

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Greek Myth Heracles And His Labours
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Greece, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** baby Heracles, son of Alcmene, strangles the two snakes Hera sends to his cradle, and the frightened household sees the hero he will become
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2, ch. 1 The birth of Heracles** — baby Heracles, son of Alcmene, strangles the two snakes Hera sends to his cradle, and the frightened household sees the hero he will become
2. **Unit 2, ch. 2 The Nemean lion** — sent to serve Eurystheus, Heracles wrestles the unpierceable lion bare-handed and comes back wearing its skin, terrifying the king into a jar
3. **Unit 2, ch. 3 The Lernaean Hydra and the Erymanthian boar** — every Hydra head cut grows back two until Iolaus burns the necks, and Heracles then traps the giant boar in deep snow and carries it alive
4. **Unit 2, ch. 4 The golden-horned deer, the Stymphalian birds, the Augean stables** — a year chasing Artemis's deer, bronze-beaked birds scattered by noise, and a whole filthy stable cleaned in one day by diverting two rivers
5. **Unit 2, ch. 5 More labours** — the fire-breathing mares of Diomedes, Hippolyta's belt among the Amazons, and the cattle of three-bodied Geryon at the edge of the world
6. **Unit 2, ch. 6 Cerberus of Hades and the golden apples of the Hesperides** — Heracles wrestles the three-headed dog of the dead bare-handed and tricks Atlas into fetching the golden apples, then tricks him back under the sky
7. **Unit 2, ch. 7 The end of Heracles** — the poisoned shirt of Nessus burns Heracles, he dies on a pyre on Oeta, and Zeus raises him to Olympus as a god
8. **Unit 2, ch. 1 Birth of Heracles exercises** — pupils read the feelings of the children in the snake-cradle picture — image evidence for the hero's first feat
9. **Unit 2, ch. 2 Nemean lion exercises** — picture clues show how pupils can tell the lion fight could only be won bare-handed
10. **Unit 2, ch. 3 Hydra and boar exercises** — pupils notice Heracles spares some beasts and captures them alive — cleverness, not only strength
11. **Unit 2, ch. 4 Deer, birds and stables exercises** — exercises follow the year-long deer chase and the one-day river-cleaning of Augeas's stables
12. **Unit 2, ch. 5 More labours exercises** — map and picture work follows Heracles to Thrace, the Amazons and the far west
13. **Unit 2, ch. 6 Cerberus and the golden apples exercises** — pupils retell how Heracles outwitted Atlas and descended living into Hades
14. **Unit 2, ch. 7 End of Heracles exercises** — pupils say what they would tell Heracles at his fiery end — empathy work on the hero's death and deification
15. **Revision lesson for Units 2, 3, 4** — pupils answer why Heracles was the Greeks' most beloved hero and what good he did people — heroes compared across the three units

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
