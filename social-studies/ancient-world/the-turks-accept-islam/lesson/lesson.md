# The Turks accept Islam

## Do it first

A battle beside a Central Asian river in 751 is remembered for paper — but Turkey's book remembers it as a door. Through it, over generations, a whole people walks toward a new faith, and the faith changes them — and is changed by them. Before you read on, guess what a steppe people would keep, even after everything changes. Hold your guess; the book answers honestly: much.

## Sub lesson 1 — From Talas to the Karakhanids — the first Turkish Muslim state

Turkey's Grade 9 book opens its last unit with the meeting its whole course has been walking toward. The encounter at Talas in 751 brings Turks and the Islamic world into lasting contact; from that meeting, over generations, the Turks accept Islam — not in a day, but people by people, until the Karakhanids stand as the first Turkish Muslim state.

The book's beat gives the meeting its scale: a battle beside a river in 751 starts a thousand-year meeting of steppe and Islam.

In your mind's eye: the meeting of steppe and Islam — a Turkish khagan's camp beside a growing Muslim city, tents and domes in one view, the Talas river beyond. The student points to the tents and the domes and says what each side brings.

## Sub lesson 2 — Islam's effect on Turkish state and society

Then the book asks what the faith changed. The khagan's titles change; the law takes a new source beside custom; the city — with its mosque, market and school — grows beside the camp as a new centre of life.

But the book is honest about the other direction too: the steppe keeps its customs as well. The Turks who accept Islam remain the steppe's children — its horsemanship, its assemblies, its ways of honour — inside a new faith. Change and continuity, weighed together: that is how the book teaches a people's conversion.
