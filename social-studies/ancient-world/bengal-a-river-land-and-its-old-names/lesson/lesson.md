# Bengal — a river land and its old names

## Do it first

Before there were maps of Bangladesh, there was a land of rivers. The same land carried many names — Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata, Harikela. Before you read on, guess: why would one land need so many names? Hold your guess; the rivers are coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bengal as a region — a river land with old names

Bangladesh's Grade 6 book begins Bengal's story with the land itself. Bengal is a region ringed by the Himalayas, by hills, and by the Bay of Bengal — a river land whose natural shape stayed the same even while the states drawn on it kept changing. Across time the land was known by many names: Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela. The book's point is plain: the shifting borders of states never matched the land's natural borders, so to know Bengal you start with its rivers and its old names, not with any one kingdom.

In your mind's eye: the Bengal delta from above — the Ganges, Padma, Brahmaputra and Meghna rivers braiding through green flood-fed fields to the Bay of Bengal, with the Himalayas on the horizon. Name the four great rivers and the sea.

## Sub lesson 2 — Landforms shape settlement — living with the rivers

The Grade 6 book then shows the land shaping the people. Settlers cleared forest and built their villages beside the great rivers — the Ganges, the Padma, the Brahmaputra and the Meghna — because the flood-fed soil beside them grew rich crops. But the rivers' gifts had a price: flood, storm and tiger were part of life in Bengal from the beginning. Settlement in Bengal is a bargain with water: the same floods that feed the fields can also take them.

## Sub lesson 3 — The peoples who made Bengal's culture

Bangladesh's Grade 8 book picks up the land's story at the level of its peoples. Bengal's culture was not made by one people: Austric language peoples, then Dravidian language peoples, and then Arya language peoples entered Bengal in turn. Their mixing — of languages, customs and ways of life — laid the diverse foundation of Bengali society, language and custom that everything later in Bengal's history builds on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 3 beats: Bengal as a region — a river land with old names; Landforms shape settlement — living with the rivers; The peoples who made Bengal's culture. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

2. Write, from memory: list Bengal's old names: Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela.
3. Write, from memory: explain why state borders and the land's natural borders did not match.
4. Write, from memory: name the four great rivers people settled beside: Ganges, Padma, Brahmaputra and Meghna.
5. Write, from memory: say why the flood-fed soil drew settlers.
6. Write, from memory: name the price of the rivers' gifts: flood, storm and tiger.
