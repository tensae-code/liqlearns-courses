# Hands-on — Bengal — a river land and its old names

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Name the land

Draw the Bengal region from memory: the Himalayas at the top, hills at the sides, the Bay of Bengal below, and the great rivers running down. Write the six old names — Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata, Harikela — around your drawing.

## Two maps

Draw two outlines of the same river land: one showing the natural region, one showing a kingdom's border cutting across it. Write one sentence on why the two lines do not match.

## The bargain

Make a two-column chart: what the rivers give (water, flood-fed soil, fish, travel) and what they take or threaten (flood, storm, tiger). Fill each side from the lesson.

## Settle here

On your river drawing, mark where you would build a village and write two reasons from the lesson — one gift you want, one danger you must live with.

## Three waves

Draw three arrows entering your Bengal map in order — Austric, Dravidian, Arya — and under each write one thing a mixing of peoples can create: new words, new customs, new foods.

## Mixing today

Write down one word, food or custom from your own home that you think came from a different people long ago, and one sentence on how it might have arrived.
