# Lesson plan — Bengal — a river land and its old names

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Bengal — a river land and its old names
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Bangladesh, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bengal is a land the rivers made: ringed by the Himalayas, hills and the Bay of Bengal, it was known across time as Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela — its shifting state borders never matching its natural ones.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bengal as a region — a river land with old names** — ringed by the Himalayas, hills and the Bay of Bengal, this river land was known across time as Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela, its shifting state borders never matching its natural ones
2. **Landforms shape settlement — living with the rivers** — people clear forest, settle beside the Ganges, Padma, Brahmaputra and Meghna, farm the flood-fed soil and live with flood, storm and tiger as the price of the rivers' gifts
3. **The peoples who made Bengal's culture** — Austric, Dravidian and then Arya language peoples enter Bengal in turn, and their mixing lays the diverse foundation of Bengali society, language and custom

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the natural ring around Bengal: the Himalayas, the hills and the Bay of Bengal.
2. List Bengal's old names: Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela.
3. Explain why state borders and the land's natural borders did not match.
4. Name the four great rivers people settled beside: Ganges, Padma, Brahmaputra and Meghna.
5. Say why the flood-fed soil drew settlers.
6. Name the price of the rivers' gifts: flood, storm and tiger.
7. Name, in order, the language peoples who entered Bengal: Austric, Dravidian, then Arya.
8. Explain how their mixing founded Bengali society, language and custom.
