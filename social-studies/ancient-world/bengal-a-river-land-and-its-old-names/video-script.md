# Video script — Bengal — a river land and its old names

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bangladesh book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

One land, many names. Bangladesh's Grade 6 book opens Bengal's history with the land itself — ringed by the Himalayas, the hills and the Bay of Bengal.

Across the centuries this river land was called Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela. Kingdoms rose and redrew their borders, but the rivers kept their own map.

That is the book's first lesson: to know Bengal, start with the land and its old names.

Why did people settle in Bengal? The Grade 6 book answers with the rivers: the Ganges, the Padma, the Brahmaputra and the Meghna.

Beside them, flood-fed soil grew rich crops, so people cleared the forest and built their villages on the banks.

But the gift had a price — flood, storm and tiger. Bengal's first history is a bargain with water.

Who made Bengal's culture? The Grade 8 book answers: not one people, but three waves mixing.

First came Austric language peoples, then Dravidian language peoples, then Arya language peoples.

Their mixing laid the diverse foundation of Bengali society, language and custom — the ground every later chapter of Bengal's story stands on.
