# Lesson plan — The Arabian Peninsula — cradle of civilisations

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Arabian Peninsula — cradle of civilisations
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Saudi Arabia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Arabian Peninsula as the Arabs' homeland since antiquity and a cradle of civilisations — 'Ad, Thamud, Madyan, al-Maqar, Dadan, the Nabataeans and Kinda — a bridge between Egypt and Iraq, the birthplace of the Arabic language, its ancestors writing their history in rock inscriptions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: long before Islam, kingdoms rise and vanish in the peninsula's deserts, leaving carved rocks as their only books. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Homeland of the Arabs** — The peninsula is the Arabs' homeland since antiquity, a bridge between Egypt and Iraq, and the birthplace of the Arabic language.
2. **Kingdoms of the peninsula** — 'Ad, Thamud, Madyan, al-Maqar, Dadan, the Nabataeans and Kinda — civilisations that rose in the peninsula long before Islam.
3. **History written in rock** — The peninsula's ancestors wrote their history in rock inscriptions — carved rocks are their surviving books.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why the book calls the peninsula a cradle of civilisations — the Arabs' homeland since antiquity.
2. Name at least three early peoples or kingdoms of the peninsula — from 'Ad, Thamud, Madyan, al-Maqar, Dadan, the Nabataeans and Kinda.
3. Say where the peninsula sits — a bridge between Egypt and Iraq — and what language was born there.
4. Explain, in their own words, how carved rocks can be a people's books.
