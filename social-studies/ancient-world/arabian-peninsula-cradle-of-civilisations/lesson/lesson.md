# The Arabian Peninsula — cradle of civilisations

## Do it first

Imagine a land of deserts where kingdoms rose and vanished so long ago that no paper, no library, no palace archive survives from them. How would you ever know they existed? Before you read on, decide: what could a desert people leave behind that would still speak thousands of years later? Hold your answer; the rocks will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Homeland of the Arabs

The Saudi Arabia Grade 4 book opens its Unit 7 history of the peninsula with a claim a Saudi child is meant to carry: the Arabian Peninsula is the Arabs' homeland since antiquity — and a cradle of civilisations.

The book gives the peninsula three honours. It is a bridge between Egypt and Iraq, the two great river civilisations on either side of it. It is the birthplace of the Arabic language. And it is a land where civilisation did not arrive from elsewhere — it grew here, early.

In your mind's eye: a desert valley in the Arabian Peninsula with carved rock inscriptions in the foreground and caravan routes stretching toward Egypt on one side and Iraq on the other. The student points to the carved rock and says what it is and why it matters.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kingdoms of the peninsula

The book names the peninsula's early peoples and kingdoms: 'Ad, Thamud, Madyan, al-Maqar, Dadan, the Nabataeans and Kinda.

Some of these names live in the Qur'an's stories as well as in history; some are known from the stones they left. Together they make the book's point: long before Islam, the peninsula was not empty sand waiting for history — it was full of peoples, kingdoms and cities of its own.

## Sub lesson 3 — History written in rock

How do we know? The book's answer is the one carved in stone: the peninsula's ancestors wrote their history in rock inscriptions.

Where other lands left libraries, the desert peoples left carved rocks — names, journeys, prayers and boasts cut into stone that the dry air kept. Those inscriptions are the peninsula's first books, and they are still being read.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: the peninsula is the Arabs' homeland since antiquity, a bridge between Egypt and Iraq and the birthplace of Arabic; its early kingdoms include 'Ad, Thamud, Madyan, al-Maqar, Dadan, the Nabataeans and Kinda; and their history survives in rock inscriptions — carved rocks as their only books. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory, three of the peninsula's early peoples or kingdoms.
2. Write the peninsula's three honours as the book gives them — homeland, bridge, birthplace of Arabic.
3. Write two sentences explaining how a carved rock can be a book.
