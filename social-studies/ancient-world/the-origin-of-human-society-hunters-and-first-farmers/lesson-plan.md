# Lesson plan — The origin of human society — hunters and first farmers

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The origin of human society — hunters and first farmers
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Myanmar, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ch.4.1–4.2: early hunters develop weapons, tools and fire, then agriculture gradually becomes part of human civilisation in Mesopotamia and Egypt.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Myanmar's Grade 6 national story, as the Myanmar History teacher guide teaches it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Hunters — stone weapons and fire** — Early, middle and late Rock Age hunters develop weapons and tools and master fire: bands of hunters learn to shape stone and tame fire, the first technologies of the human story.
2. **First farmers — agriculture and country life** — Agriculture gradually becomes part of human civilisation, in the Mesopotamia region and Egypt: planting replaces chasing, villages replace camps, and farming remakes how humans live together.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
