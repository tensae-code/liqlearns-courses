# India — janapadas and mahajanapadas

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Rajas of peoples became rajas of lands — janapadas, then mahajanapadas with fortified capitals, salaried armies, and taxes paid as bhaga, a sixth of the crop. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Janapadas and mahajanapadas; Painted Grey Ware

Rajas of peoples become rajas of lands where "the jana set its foot"; excavated settlements at Purana Qila, Hastinapura and Atranjikhera yield huts, cattle and fine Painted Grey Ware for special feasts; the greatest janapadas ring their capitals with massive fortification walls.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Armies, taxes and changes in agriculture

Kings who build forts and keep salaried, year-round armies must tax — bhaga, a sixth of the crop, labour from craftsmen, animals from herders; iron ploughshares bite heavy clay and transplanted paddy multiplies the harvest, on the bent backs of dasas and landless kammakaras.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Janapadas and mahajanapadas; Painted Grey Ware; Armies, taxes and changes in agriculture. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
