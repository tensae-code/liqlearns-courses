# The Maya

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — City-states in the rainforest

In the rainforests of Mesoamerica, the Maya built a civilisation of city-states — no single emperor, but rival cities, each with its king, its temples and its pyramids rising above the trees.

Farming in forest clearings fed them; war and trade bound them together and set them against each other. Their world was brilliant and demanding at once.

## Sub lesson 2 — A calendar and a full writing

The Maya kept one of the ancient world's most exact calendars, tracking sun, moon and Venus in carved stone. And they wrote — a full writing system of glyphs, carved on monuments and painted in books.

Writing matters: it means the Maya speak to us in their own words, not only through their ruins.

## Sub lesson 3 — What the Maya teach the comparison

Stand the Maya beside Egypt and Mesopotamia and the same marks appear: farming surplus, cities, kings, priests, writing, calendars. Civilisation was invented more than once, on more than one continent.

That is the lesson of this stop: the ancient world's map is wider than one river valley.
