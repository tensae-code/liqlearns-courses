# The Taïno of Quisqueya

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: An island people spread across an archipelago — villages, ball courts and sea paths link Quisqueya's communities, ruled by chiefs called caciques, long before any European sail appears. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — A territory and an archipelago society

An island people spread across an archipelago — villages, ball courts and sea paths link quisqueya's communities long before any european sail appears (Séance 2, Un territoire et une société « archipéliques »)

## Sub lesson 2 — The Taïno and the caciquat

Chiefs called caciques rule the villages — but pupils test whether the 'caciquat' they read about is taïno reality or a shape imposed by spanish writers (Séance 3, Les Taïno et le caciquat, des constructions ethno-historiques ?)
