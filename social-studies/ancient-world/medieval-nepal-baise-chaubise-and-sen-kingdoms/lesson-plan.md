# Lesson plan — Medieval Nepal — Baise, Chaubise and Sen kingdoms

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Medieval Nepal — Baise, Chaubise and Sen kingdoms
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Nepal, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** pupil groups research and present: the Baise states west of the Kali Gandaki and the Chaubise in the Gandak region, born as the Sinja and valley kingdoms weakened and local chiefs declared themselves kings (Kaski's Sinja chiefs ruling some 300 years); and the Sen kingdom, founded when the Muslim-invasion refugee Jog Sen's line under Rudra Sen made Palpa a state in 1550 BS, split by Mukunda Sen among his sons and later broken into Makwanpur, Chaudandi and Vijaypur
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
