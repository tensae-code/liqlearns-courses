# Lesson plan — Americas' first peoples — Paleoindian and Archaic

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Americas' first peoples — Paleoindian and Archaic
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Guatemala, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the first peoples of the Americas hunted mammoth across the Bering ice in the Paleoindian period, then settled, planted maize, beans and chile, built villages and traded in the Archaic.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the animals the first hunters chased: mammoth and mastodon.
2. Name the crossing the hunters made: the ice-covered Bering Strait.
3. Name the first crops planted: maize, beans and chile.
