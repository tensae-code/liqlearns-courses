# Lesson plan — Ukraine enters the Middle Ages

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ukraine enters the Middle Ages
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Ukraine, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the course opens by periodising the Middle Ages and looking back at ancient Ukraine — Cimmerians, Scythians and Sarmatians in the steppe, Greek poleis like Olbia and Chersonesus on the coast — before the Slavs, from whose farming communities the story continues.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ancient Ukraine before the Slavs** — Before the Middle Ages begin, the book looks back at ancient Ukraine. Cimmerians, Scythians and Sarmatians ride the steppe, and Greek poleis like Olbia and Chersonesus stand on the Black Sea coast.
2. **The Slavs arrive** — After those ancient peoples come the Slavs. From their farming communities the medieval story of Ukraine continues — the Slavs are the people the rest of the course follows.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- No lesson is required before this one. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Periodise the Middle Ages as the book opens the course, and name the ancient peoples who came before on Ukrainian lands: Cimmerians, Scythians and Sarmatians in the steppe.
2. Name the Greek poleis on the coast — Olbia and Chersonesus — and say that the story continues from the Slavs' farming communities.
