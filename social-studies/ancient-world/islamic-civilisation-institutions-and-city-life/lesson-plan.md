# Lesson plan — Islamic civilisation — its state, markets and cities

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Islamic civilisation — its state, markets and cities
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Kuwait, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Islamic civilisation built a working state — caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police — paid for by markets, crafts, farming and trade, and housed in planned stone cities.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Political and administrative manifestations of Islamic civilisation** — caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police take shape as a working state, with Ali ibn Abi Talib's police chief, the sahib al-shurta, keeping the streets (Unit 2 Lesson 1: Political and administrative manifestations of Islamic civilisation)
2. **Economic manifestations — markets, crafts, farming and trade** — markets, crafts, farming and trade fill the Islamic world's purse, the wealth that pays for its cities and schools (Unit 2 Lesson 2: Economic manifestations)
3. **Economic manifestations continued, and urban manifestations** — the money story runs on into streets, mosques and houses as Islamic cities rise in planned stone (Unit 2 Lesson 3: Economic manifestations continued, and urban manifestations)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
