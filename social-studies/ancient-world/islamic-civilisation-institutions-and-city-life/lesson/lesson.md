# Islamic civilisation — its state, markets and cities

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Islamic civilisation built a working state — caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police — paid for by markets, crafts, farming and trade, and housed in planned stone cities. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Political and administrative manifestations of Islamic civilisation

Caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police take shape as a working state, with ali ibn abi talib's police chief, the sahib al-shurta, keeping the streets.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Economic manifestations — markets, crafts, farming and trade

Markets, crafts, farming and trade fill the islamic world's purse, the wealth that pays for its cities and schools.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Economic manifestations continued, and urban manifestations

The money story runs on into streets, mosques and houses as islamic cities rise in planned stone.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: political and administrative manifestations of islamic civilisation; economic manifestations — markets, crafts, farming and trade; economic manifestations continued, and urban manifestations. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
