# Lesson plan — Early migrations of peoples

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Early migrations of peoples
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 2, Topic 2.4, Man and Migration: why early peoples move — climate, hunger, pressure — and what moves with them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: migration is the engine that carries tools, seeds and languages across the ancient world. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three pushes the book gives for early migration: climate, hunger and pressure.
2. Say what a migrating people carries with it: tools, seeds, animals and language.
3. Explain how migration spreads skills and mixes peoples across the ancient world.
4. Give one example, in their own words, of something that reached a new land by moving with people.
