# Ancient civilisations — China

## Do it first

Four families rule one land, one after another, for nearly two thousand years. Write what a land gains when each new ruling family keeps the same writing, the same rivers, the same trades. China is that land — walk its line of dynasties.

## Dynasties in sequence

China's civilisation grew on the **Yellow River — the Huang He — and the Yangtze**. First the **Shang (1600–1050 BC)**, centred on the Huang He valley: **bronze vessels, horse chariots and writing**. Then the **Zhou (1050–256 BC)**: expansion, **coin money, iron tools, written laws, literature and philosophy**. Then the land broke into the **Warring States period (476–221 BC)**. Then the **Qin (221–206 BC)** **unified China under Shih Huang-ti**. Then the **Han (206 BC–220 AD)**: **arts and science flourished**, and **Confucian philosophy spread**. ## Silk, porcelain and picture-writing

China **traded with Constantinople, India and Aksum** — yes, with Ethiopia's own ancient kingdom — and was **famed for silk and porcelain**. Its writing is **logographic**: **words are built from pictures**, and written **on paper**. A civilisation that writes in pictures, trades across the world, and outlasts every dynasty that rules it — that is ancient China.
