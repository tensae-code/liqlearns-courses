# Cyprus under the Ptolemies

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this question in your mind: under the Ptolemies Cyprus lost its kings and became one province of an Egyptian empire — its copper, timber, wine and harbours feeding Ptolemaic power for nearly three centuries. As you read, test every part of the story against that one thing — who came, what they did, and what changed because of it. Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The end of the Cypriot kingdoms; administration — the koina (325–30 BC)

Alexander's successors abolish Cyprus's kings, and Ptolemaic governors rule the island as one province of an Egyptian empire.

In your mind's eye: the key place or moment of Cyprus under the Ptolemies as the book shows it. Point to it and say what it shows.

## Sub lesson 2 — The importance of Cyprus for the Ptolemaic kingdom — the Kyrenia ship

Cypriot copper, timber, wine and harbours feed Ptolemaic power, and a merchant ship sunk off Kyrenia still tells the trade story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Daily life; art — theatre of the Hellenistic age

Hellenistic Cypriots live in Greek-style cities, watch plays in stone theatres and fill houses with new fashions.

## Sub lesson 4 — The religion of the Cypriots in the Hellenistic age

Old Cypriot cults blend with Greek gods and ruler worship under Ptolemaic rule.
