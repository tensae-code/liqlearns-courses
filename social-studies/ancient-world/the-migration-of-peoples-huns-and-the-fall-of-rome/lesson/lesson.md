# The Migration of Peoples — the Huns and the fall of Rome

## Do it first

Push one people westward on the steppe, and the push travels — people against people, like falling stones in a line — until it reaches the gates of Rome itself. Before you read on, decide: can a migration on one continent close an age on another? Hold your answer; the Kavimler Göçü is the book's proof.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kavimler Göçü — the Huns' push and Rome's end

Turkey's Grade 9 book closes its Turkish unit with a migration that changed two continents. The Huns, a steppe people, push westward out of Asia — and their push does not stop with them. People pressed against people: the Germanic peoples in the Huns' path move in their turn, crossing Rome's frontiers not as visitors but as peoples on the move.

The chain ends at Rome itself. An empire already strained cannot hold borders that whole peoples are crossing, and the western Roman world gives way. The book's beat: a steppe people's migration crosses two continents and closes the ancient world in Europe — the Kavimler Göçü, the Migration of Peoples.

In your mind's eye: the great migration in motion — Hun horsemen driving westward while Germanic peoples with carts and families stream toward Rome's frontiers. The student traces the push with a finger and says where it ends.
