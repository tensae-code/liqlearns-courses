# Lesson plan — The Lucayans — first people of The Bahamas

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Lucayans — first people of The Bahamas
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Bahamas, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Lucayans, a peaceful Arawak-speaking people who sailed from South America, settle the Bahama islands by the sea — fishing, farming cassava, sweet potato and corn, living in branch-and-grass huts and sleeping in hammocks — and leave the hammock as their gift to the world
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bahamian History: the Lucayans, first people of the islands** — a peaceful Arawak-speaking people sail from South America, settle by the sea, fish and farm cassava, sweet potato and corn, live in branch-and-grass huts and sleep in hammocks (Bahamian History: the Lucayans, first people of the islands)
2. **Lucayans and Eleutheran Adventurers: the Lucayans** — the peaceful Lucayans, skilled sailors and fishermen who live in huts, sleep in hammocks and live off the land, leave a legacy the whole world still uses — the hammock (Lucayans and Eleutheran Adventurers: the Lucayans)
3. **Early Settlers: the Lucayans, first people to come to the islands** — the first arrivals are peaceful sailors and fishermen who live in huts, sleep in hammocks and live off the land in harmony with nature (Early Settlers: the Lucayans, first people to come to the islands)
4. **Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 1: the timeline of peoples** — after Columbus, group after group finds refuge on the islands — Eleutheran Adventurers (1684 in this telling), pirates, Loyalists, slaves — each changing the islands for better or worse; with Woodes Rogers an organised society begins that leads, idea by idea, to independence (Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 1: the timeline of peoples)
