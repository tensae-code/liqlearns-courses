# Video script — The Lucayans — first people of The Bahamas

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bahamas books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

a peaceful Arawak-speaking people sail from South America, settle by the sea, fish and farm cassava, sweet potato and corn, live in branch-and-grass huts and sleep in hammocks; the peaceful Lucayans, skilled sailors and fishermen who live in huts, sleep in hammocks and live off the land, leave a legacy the whole world still uses — the hammock; the first arrivals are peaceful sailors and fishermen who live in huts, sleep in hammocks and live off the land in harmony with nature; after Columbus, group after group finds refuge on the islands — Eleutheran Adventurers (1684 in this telling), pirates, Loyalists, slaves — each changing the islands for better or worse; with Woodes Rogers an organised society begins that leads, idea by idea, to independence

Hold that story: The Lucayans, a peaceful Arawak-speaking people who sailed from South America, settle the Bahama islands by the sea — fishing, farming cassava, sweet potato and corn, living in branch-and-grass huts and sleeping in hammocks — and leave the hammock as their gift to the world
