# The Lucayans — first people of The Bahamas — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bahamian History: the Lucayans, first people of the islands
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: a peaceful Arawak-speaking people sail from South America, settle by the sea, fish and farm cassava, sweet potato and corn, live in branch-and-grass huts and sleep in hammocks

## Sub lesson 2 — Lucayans and Eleutheran Adventurers: the Lucayans
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the peaceful Lucayans, skilled sailors and fishermen who live in huts, sleep in hammocks and live off the land, leave a legacy the whole world still uses — the hammock

## Sub lesson 3 — Early Settlers: the Lucayans, first people to come to the islands
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the first arrivals are peaceful sailors and fishermen who live in huts, sleep in hammocks and live off the land in harmony with nature

## Sub lesson 4 — Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 1: the timeline of peoples
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: after Columbus, group after group finds refuge on the islands — Eleutheran Adventurers (1684 in this telling), pirates, Loyalists, slaves — each changing the islands for better or worse; with Woodes Rogers an organised society begins that leads, idea by idea, to independence
