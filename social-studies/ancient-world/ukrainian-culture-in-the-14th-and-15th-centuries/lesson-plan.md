# Lesson plan — Ukrainian culture in the 14th and 15th centuries

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ukrainian culture in the 14th and 15th centuries
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Ukraine, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** no state of their own, yet the culture holds — church and monastery schools, students like Yurii Drohobych reaching Bologna and Kraków (he becomes rector at Bologna), fortress and church building from Lutsk to Kamianets.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Schools and scholars abroad** — No state of their own, yet the culture holds. Church and monastery schools teach at home, and students like Yurii Drohobych reach Bologna and Kraków — Drohobych becomes rector at Bologna.
2. **Building from Lutsk to Kamianets** — Fortress and church building rises from Lutsk to Kamianets. A stateless culture still builds in stone — learning and building carry the people's identity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Ukrainian towns, crafts and Magdeburg law. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how culture held without a state: church and monastery schools at home, and scholars like Yurii Drohobych reaching Bologna and Kraków and becoming rector at Bologna.
2. Name the building that carried the culture: fortresses and churches from Lutsk to Kamianets.
