# Coins and mints of Jordan — from the Decapolis to the Umayyad dinar — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Lesson 7: coinage (al-maskukat) in Jordan.
**Answer: from barter to numismatics — Rome lets the Decapolis cities strike their own copper and bronze, stamped with the city's name and Latin values; the first Muslims keep Byzantium's gold dinar and Persia's silver dirham, then arabise them step by step (Umar adds "alhamdulillah", Uthman "Allahu akbar", Mu'awiya writes his name even in Pahlavi); Abd al-Malik puts his own image where the emperor's stood, then strikes the pure Islamic dinar in 77 AH/696; the little copper fals (from Latin follis, "money-bag") serves daily trade, and Amman and Jerash work as mints (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
