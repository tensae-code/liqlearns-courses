# Jordan in the Crusader, Ayyubid and Mamluk ages — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Europe's kings exploit a divided Islamic world and take Jerusalem in 1099, planting Edessa, Antioch, Tripoli and the Kingdom of Jerusalem; Baldwin raises Montreal castle at Shobak and seizes Ayla (Aqaba) to command the Syria–Egypt road, and the Franks rebuild Karak — until Nur al-Din Zangi reads Jordan's importance and moves, setting the stage for Hattin (1187).
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lesson 4: Jordan under the Frankish invasion of Bilad al-Sham

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *تاريخ الأردن — الصف الحادي عشر، الفرع المهني (History of Jordan, Grade 11, Vocational Track), Term 2 — Unit 2: Jordan in the Islamic Ages; Unit 3: Jordan in the Modern Age* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lesson 4: Jordan under the Frankish invasion of Bilad al-Sham

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Saladin, founder of the Ayyubid state (1173–1229 in the book's dating), treats Jordan as the link between Egypt and Syria, the muster-ground and the Hajj road; having freed Karak he grants it to his brother al-Adil, and al-Mu'azzam Isa rebuilds its walls, imports craftsmen and makes the city feed itself; al-Nasir Dawud follows; Ayyubid mosques rise at Rimoun, Irbid, Tabaqat Fahl, Harthas and Bayt Ras, and Jordanian scholars — from Ayla, Shobak, Husban, Ajloun, Ba'un and Karak — teach as far as the Ba'uniyya dome in Damascus, before the Mongols enter Damascus in 1260.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lesson 5: Jordan in the Ayyubid age
