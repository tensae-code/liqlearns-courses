**11. (Sub lesson 6)** the Phù Nam kingdom's main territory is today's Nam Bộ, in the lower Mekong, where every year silt is deposited into fertile plains favourable to agriculture and a dense canal network serves boat traffic; its social basis is the long-standing culture of Nam Bộ — the pre-Óc Eo culture — for by the late 1st millennium BCE agriculture, crafts and goods exchange are developing and the farming–fishing–trading village structure prepares early towns in parts of Nam Bộ, the premise for great social change and the birth of the state; the land is a meeting place of peoples, and the quoted archaeology (Đặng Văn Thắng) identifies the Phù Nam residents mainly as tribes of the Malayo-Polynesian group of the Austronesian language family, a people who in ancient times lived on the coasts and islands of Southeast Asia, familiar with water life and skilled at seafaring, living long alongside the indigenous residents and together building toward the kingdom; Indian civilisation influences Phù Nam strongly, spread mainly through sea trade, visible in the kingdom's founding legend itself and in its politics, thought, architecture and religion; the kingdom is founded around the beginning of the Common Era by gathering many small states, its king holding the highest power and ruling by royal and divine authority together, helped by officials in a multi-tier government — simple for over two centuries after the founding, then increasingly complete from the 3rd to the 5th century as Phù Nam rises to a powerful kingdom of wide influence, the quoted source (Lương Ninh) recording that the ancient states Đốn Tôn, Xích Thổ and Chân Lạp submitted to Phù Nam, paid tribute, and could have no independent diplomacy except through Phù Nam; its economy makes it one of the most important trading centres of the age, merchants from China, India and Persia calling at the Óc Eo port area — the quoted inventory of Óc Eo products runs from raw goods (agarwood, fragrant oils, ivory, peacocks, five-coloured parrots) to processed ones (gold and silver wares, glassware, jade, tortoiseshell, white sandalwood statues, silk and cloth), and the photographs show Óc Eo-culture coins and a Roman gold coin found at the Óc Eo site; crafts (metallurgy, glass-melting, shipbuilding, pottery) and rice-and-livestock agriculture serve both home needs and foreign trade; material life runs on rice with meats and seafood, on simple dress (men in loincloths, bare-chested; women in skirts and tops with bracelets and earrings), on large stilt houses of wood with leaf roofs suited to the watery, hot and humid climate — excavation at the Óc Eo field in An Giang has found the wooden pile traces of stilt houses packed densely over a wide area — and on boats along the canals and rivers; spiritually the people worship many gods, the Sun God typically, keep fertility beliefs, and through trade with India receive Buddhism and Hinduism (statues of the Buddha and of Vishnu from Óc Eo are pictured); and their customs bury the dead many ways — water burial, cremation, earth burial and exposure burial — the mourning family shaving head and beard and wearing white.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Bài 9.3, the Phù Nam civilisation
