# Lesson plan — Medieval world — Byzantium and Justinian

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Medieval world — Byzantium and Justinian
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — The Ancient World (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Byzantium** survived a thousand years on **position, wealth and a strong army** — and under **Justinian (527–565)** it expanded west, gave Europe the **Justinian Code**, built **Hagia Sophia**, and brought **silk-making** home.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met the ancient civilisations and medieval states named so far in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Byzantium endures — and Justinian** — Why the East lasted: position, wealth, strong army. Justinian's reign: western expansion, the Justinian Code, Hagia Sophia, silk smuggled from China; then shrinkage until 1453.
2. **The keeper of Greco-Roman civilisation (teacher guide — evidence)** — The guide's frame: Byzantium as the centre that kept Greco-Roman and Christian civilisation alive in Europe while the West declined, and the Crusades as Western Europe's military response in the Holy Land.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the three reasons the East survived: position, wealth, strong army.
2. Tell Justinian's works: western expansion, the Justinian Code, Hagia Sophia, silk-making brought from China — and the empire's shrinkage to 1453.
3. State the guide's framing of Byzantium as keeper of Greco-Roman and Christian civilisation (teacher guide — evidence).
