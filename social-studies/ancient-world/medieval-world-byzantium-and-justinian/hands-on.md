# Hands-on — Medieval world — Byzantium and Justinian

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Wall and dome
Build Constantinople from blocks or mud: a long wall with towers on one side, a domed building (a bowl upside down on a box) as Hagia Sophia on the other. Label Justinian's four works on cards around it.

## Code in one book
Gather five 'laws' your group lives by, write them in one small booklet and title it 'Our Code' — Justinian's work was gathering scattered laws into one book Europe could use.

## The thousand-year wall (Grade 9)
Draw Constantinople's land walls and sea walls on a sketch. Underneath, list what had to keep working for a city to hold an empire for a thousand years — walls, taxes, faith, law — using this lesson and its earlier parts.

## Grade 11 depth — Two Romes in depth — the fallen West and the thousand-year East

Re-draw this lesson's map or table with the Grade 11 part added in a second colour, then tell the new part's story aloud from the drawing alone. Where the drawing cannot carry the story, the story is not yet learned — re-read that part.
