# The Italian Communes — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Unità 2, Cap. 4 §§1–2 The medieval cities; The development of the Communes.
**Answer: Italian city-dwellers swear collective oaths and govern themselves through consuls and podestà, raising tower-houses and writing their own statutes while bishops and emperors look on (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Italy's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *La storia. Progettare il futuro — Vol. 1: Dall'anno Mille al Seicento (Alessandro Barbero, Chiara Frugoni, Carla Sclarandis)* (Italy), Unità 2, Cap. 4 §§1–2 The medieval cities; The development of the Communes, printed pp. 116, 124.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: L'Europa dei comuni; Lo scontro tra l'imperatore e i comuni italiani; Il giuramento di Pontida.
**Answer: the communes swear their oath at Pontida (1167) and beat Barbarossa's empire at Legnano; canon law grows in the same cities as their other constitution (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Italy's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Lo spazio del tempo 1 — Storia Documenti Storiografia dall'XI al XVII secolo (Andrea Giardina, Giovanni Sabbatucci, Vittorio Vidotto)* (Italy), L'Europa dei comuni; Lo scontro tra l'imperatore e i comuni italiani; Il giuramento di Pontida, printed pp. 27, 30, 32.**
