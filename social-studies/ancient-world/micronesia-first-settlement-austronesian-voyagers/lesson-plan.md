# Lesson plan — Micronesia — first settlement: Austronesian voyagers

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Micronesia — first settlement: Austronesian voyagers
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Micronesia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Micronesia was settled by Austronesian voyagers who left crowded villages in southeast China for Taiwan about 5,000 BC and then travelled island by island along two paths into Micronesia, navigating by sung star paths in outrigger canoes and landing with the tools, animals and seed crops to build a life on each new island.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told in order, part by part, as Micronesia's books tell it. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Centuries of migration and the two paths of settlement** — say that Austronesian families left southeast China for Taiwan about 5,000 BC and reached Micronesia along two paths, while older peoples had crossed the Sunda and Sahul land bridges.
2. **Traveling beyond the horizon: the outrigger, star songs and the tool kit** — say that navigators sang star paths instead of writing them, tacked against the wind in outrigger canoes, and landed with adzes, coir rope, pigs, chickens and seed crops.
3. **The settlement science in the atolls' own languages** — say that the Kapingamarangi, Mwoakilloa and Nukuoro readers retell the same two-wave settlement science — land-bridge peoples first, then Austronesian Lapita voyagers — in each atoll's own language.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
