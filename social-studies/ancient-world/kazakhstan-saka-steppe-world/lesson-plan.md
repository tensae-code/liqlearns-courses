# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan Saka Steppe World

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan Saka Steppe World
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** iron weapons and horse gear hand the steppe to mounted warrior tribes
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§20–21 The Early Iron Age: main character of the era** — iron weapons and horse gear hand the steppe to mounted warrior tribes
2. **§22–23 The Sakas in written sources** — Persian and Greek writers describe the Sakas from outside while their kurgans speak from inside
3. **§24 Steppe tribal unions and their settlement** — Saka tribes unite and divide the steppe into their territories
4. **§25–26 Cattle-breeders of Northern and Eastern Kazakhstan** — northern and eastern herders live by herds, bronze and burial mounds
5. **§27–28 Sakas of Southern and Central Kazakhstan** — southern Sakas raise the great royal kurgans, Issyk among them
6. **§29–30 Sauromatians and Massagetae** — Sauromatian warrior women and the Massagetae who defeated Cyrus people the western steppe
7. **2.1 Mastering iron in Kazakhstan** — iron tools and weapons spread and the early-Iron-Age world opens
8. **3.1 Historical information about the Sakas** — Persian inscriptions and Greek writers introduce the pointed-hat Sakas
9. **3.2 The "Golden Man" archaeological find** — the Issyk kurgan yields a warrior in gold — Kazakhstan's emblem is dug out of the earth
10. **3.3 Shilikty and Besshatyr royal kurgans** — royal burial mounds the size of hills guard Saka kings and their treasure
11. **3.4 The Berel kurgans** — frozen Berel graves keep horses and art intact for two thousand years
12. **3.5 The Tasmola archaeological culture** — Tasmola's moustache-shaped stone ridges mark a distinct Saka world in the centre
13. **3.6 Saka queen Tomiris** — Queen Tomiris defeats Cyrus of Persia and avenges her son on the battlefield
14. **3.7 Shirak's feat** — the shepherd Shirak mutilates himself to lead the Persian army into a waterless trap
15. **§19 Periodising the Iron Age and its features** — iron arrives and the age of mounted tribal states opens
16. **§20 Mastering iron and its place in early nomads' life** — iron tools and weapons arm the first nomads for the open steppe
17. **§27 Sakas on Kazakhstan's territory** — the Saka tribes take the whole steppe from Altai to the Aral
18. **§28 Ancient Greek and Persian information about the Sakas** — Herodotus and Persian inscriptions introduce the Sakas to written history
19. **§29 Archaeological information about the Sakas** — kurgan excavations let the Sakas speak without written words
20. **§30 The Issyk kurgan: the Golden Man** — a young warrior in golden armour rises from the Issyk mound as the nation's symbol
21. **§31 Royal kurgans of the Shilikty valley** — giant royal mounds guard Saka kings in the Shilikty valley
22. **§32 Besshatyr — a sacred, revered place** — five great kurgans ringed with menhirs form a Saka sanctuary
23. **§33 Berel — relics kept in 'eternal ice'** — frozen Berel graves preserve horses, wood and felt for two millennia
24. **§34 Saka applied art: the animal style** — gold beasts twist and fight in the famous Scythian-Saka animal style
25. **§35 Saka religious beliefs and rites** — sun and fire cults and funeral feasts order Saka spiritual life
26. **§36 Tasmola culture: the secret of the 'moustached kurgans'** — stone ridges stream from Tasmola mounds like moustaches across the steppe
27. **§37 The Sakas' communal structure** — clans and tribal unions hold Saka society together
28. **§38 Queen Tomiris (Tumar)** — Tomiris defeats Cyrus and ends the Persian king's life at the river battle
29. **§39 The Sakas' social structure** — kings, warriors, herders and slaves take their places in Saka order
30. **§40 Shirak's feat** — the shepherd Shirak cripples himself to doom the Persian army in the desert
31. **§41 Saka tribes' relations with neighbouring countries** — Sakas trade, raid and treat with Persia, Bactria and China
32. **2.1 Mastering iron in Kazakhstan (lesson plans)** — iron's arrival is taught as the turn into the nomad age
33. **3.1 Historical information about the Sakas (lesson plans)** — Persian and Greek texts are read against the Saka map
34. **3.2 The Golden Man find (lesson plans)** — the Issyk discovery is staged as an archaeology detective lesson
35. **3.3 Shilikty and Besshatyr royal kurgans (lesson plans)** — royal mounds are measured and compared as royal power in earth
36. **3.4 The Berel kurgans (lesson plans)** — the frozen graves lesson shows preservation as a gift of the ice lens
37. **3.5 The Tasmola archaeological culture (lesson plans)** — moustached kurgans teach how archaeologists name a culture
38. **3.6 Saka queen Tomiris (lesson plans)** — Tomiris's victory over Cyrus is read as history and as legend
39. **3.7 Shirak's feat (lesson plans)** — the shepherd's self-sacrifice anchors a lesson on sources and heroism
