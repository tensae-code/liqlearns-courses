# The south Arabian kingdoms — Ma'in, Saba and Himyar

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: In Arabia's irrigated south, Ma'in, Saba of the Marib dam and Himyar grow rich on the incense roads before sea trade and invasion end their age. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Models of the civilisations of ancient Yemen

Ma'in trades from Qarnaw in the fertile Jawf; Saba builds the great dam of Marib and Queen Bilqis visits Solomon, then fades as the dam breaks and trade takes to the sea; Himyar unites Yemen from Zafar until Abyssinia and Persia invade; Yemen writes in the twenty-nine letters of the Musnad, terraces its mountains and grows rich on incense — its myrrh reaches even Egypt's temples and embalmers.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Civilisations of the Arabian Peninsula before Islam: the southern kingdoms

In the irrigated south the kingdoms rise in turn — Ma'in, Saba of the Ma'rib dam, and Himyar — farming states grown rich on the incense roads.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Models of the civilisations of ancient Yemen; Civilisations of the Arabian Peninsula before Islam: the southern kingdoms. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
