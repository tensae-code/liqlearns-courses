# Lesson plan — The south Arabian kingdoms — Ma'in, Saba and Himyar

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The south Arabian kingdoms — Ma'in, Saba and Himyar
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In Arabia's irrigated south, Ma'in, Saba of the Marib dam and Himyar grow rich on the incense roads before sea trade and invasion end their age.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Models of the civilisations of ancient Yemen** — Ma'in trades from Qarnaw in the fertile Jawf; Saba builds the great dam of Marib and Queen Bilqis visits Solomon, then fades as the dam breaks and trade takes to the sea; Himyar unites Yemen from Zafar until Abyssinia and Persia invade; Yemen writes in the twenty-nine letters of the Musnad, terraces its mountains and grows rich on incense — its myrrh reaches even Egypt's temples and embalmers
2. **Civilisations of the Arabian Peninsula before Islam: the southern kingdoms** — In the irrigated south the kingdoms rise in turn — Ma'in, Saba of the Ma'rib dam, and Himyar — farming states grown rich on the incense roads
