# Lesson plan — Political conditions of the Arabs before Islam

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Political conditions of the Arabs before Islam
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Yemen, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Arabia before Islam is a patchwork — Kinda in Najd and the Hijaz, Quraysh in Mecca and the Yemeni tribes Aws and Khazraj in Yathrib, the Lakhmids
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Political conditions of the Arabs before Islam** — arabia before Islam is a patchwork — Kinda in Najd and the Hijaz, Quraysh in Mecca and the Yemeni tribes Aws and Khazraj in Yathrib, the Lakhmids, Nabataeans and Ghassanids in the north — squeezed between two great neighbours, Persia at al-Mada'in and Byzantium at Constantinople
2. **States of the north of the Peninsula between the Birth and the Mission** — while Rome and Persia fight their long war over trade and resources with the Levant and Iraq as the arena, Arab buffer states line the frontier — the Nabataeans at rock-carved Petra, Yemeni migrants who even fight the Jews settled among them; Palmyra, whose Queen Zenobia seizes Rome's weakness, declares independence and overruns the Levant, Egypt and part of Turkey; Yemeni Kinda at al-Faw guarding central Arabia until internal quarrels fell it; the Ghassanids, Rome's Christian Arab tool in the south of the Levant; and the Lakhmids at al-Hira, Persia's Arab tool on the Euphrates
