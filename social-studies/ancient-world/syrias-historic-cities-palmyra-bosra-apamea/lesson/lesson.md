# Syria's historic cities — Palmyra, Bosra and Apamea

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Palmyra, Bosra, Apamea, Damascus and Aleppo — Syria's historic cities carry every age of its story in stone. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Palmyra, Bosra, Apamea and Rusafa

Palmyra in the middle of the Badia with its temples, tombs and colonnaded street ending at the triumphal arch, Bosra's great amphitheatre in Hawran, Apamea above the Ghab plain, and Rusafa, the desert city the Umayyad caliph Hisham took as his retreat.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Damascus and Aleppo — old city and new

Damascus is the oldest inhabited capital in the world, split into an old city inside its wall — the Umayyad Mosque, the citadel and the old souks — and a new city, and Aleppo likewise joins an old part, with its famous citadel, to a new one.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Palmyra, Bosra, Apamea and Rusafa; Damascus and Aleppo — old city and new. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
