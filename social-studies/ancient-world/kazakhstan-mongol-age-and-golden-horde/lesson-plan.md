# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan Mongol Age And Golden Horde

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan Mongol Age And Golden Horde
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Temujin unites the Mongols in 1206 and turns them outward
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§30 Chinggis Khan and the creation of the Mongol state** — Temujin unites the Mongols in 1206 and turns them outward
2. **§31–32 Turko-Mongol campaigns into Central Asia and Kazakhstan; the defence of Otrar** — Otrar's governor resists, the city is destroyed, and the steppe is conquered
3. **§33 Chinggis Khan's empire: the uluses** — the empire is divided among Chinggis's sons into ulus appanages
4. **§34 Creation of the Golden Horde (Ulus of Jochi)** — Batu builds the Jochid state that will rule the western steppe
5. **§35 The Golden Horde in flower** — Sarai's wealth, Islam under Uzbek Khan and steppe-wide trade mark the peak
6. **§36 Crisis and break-up of the Golden Horde** — plague, Timur's blows and rival khans shatter the Horde
7. **§37 Creation of the Ak Orda (White Horde) state** — the eastern Jochid lands form their own White Horde state
8. **§38 Political history of the Ak Orda** — Ak Orda khans fight Timur and the Uzbeks for the Syr Darya cities
9. **§39 The Nogai Horde** — the Nogai mirzas build a nomad power west of the Volga
10. **§40 The Khanate of Abulkhair** — Abulkhair's Uzbek khanate gathers the Desht-i Kipchak — and cracks
11. **§41 Creation of Moghulistan: territory and ethnic composition** — the Chagatai east becomes Moghulistan in Semirechye and Kashgaria
12. **§42 Emir Timur and the struggle against his conquests** — Timur smashes Tokhtamysh and the steppe states resist his empire
13. **§43 Socio-economic relations and state order in 14th–15th c. Kazakhstan** — uluses, taxes and cities organise post-Horde society
14. **§44 Art and literature of the 13th–15th centuries** — epics and chronicles flower in the Horde's successor states
15. **§45 Features of traditional cattle-breeding** — the four-kind herd and its seasonal round are perfected
16. **§46 Nomads' applied art develops, 13th–15th c.** — felt, silver and leather crafts reach new refinement
17. **§30 Chinggis Khan and the Mongol state's creation (lesson plan)** — Temujin's unification is staged as state-building under pressure
18. **§31–32 Turko-Mongol campaigns; the defence of Otrar (lesson plan)** — Otrar's siege is told from defenders' and conquerors' sides
19. **§33 Chinggis Khan's empire: the uluses (lesson plan)** — the ulus division is mapped as an inheritance puzzle
20. **§34 Creation of the Golden Horde, Ulus of Jochi (lesson plan)** — Batu's western state takes shape in the lesson sequence
21. **§35 The Golden Horde in flower (lesson plan)** — Sarai's markets and Uzbek Khan's Islam mark the peak lessons
22. **§36 Crisis and break-up of the Golden Horde (lesson plan)** — plague and Timur's campaigns dismantle the Horde in class analysis
23. **§37 Creation of the Ak Orda state (lesson plan)** — the White Horde emerges as the east's own Jochid state
24. **§38 Political history of the Ak Orda (lesson plan)** — Ak Orda's wars for the Syr Darya cities fill the lessons
25. **§39 The Nogai Horde (lesson plan)** — Nogai mirzas and their herds people the western steppe lessons
26. **§40 The Khanate of Abulkhair (lesson plan)** — Abulkhair's rise and fracture set up the Kazakh founding
27. **§41 Creation of Moghulistan: territory, ethnic composition (lesson plan)** — Moghulistan's peoples and lands are assembled on the map
28. **§42 Emir Timur and the struggle against his conquests (lesson plan)** — Tokhtamysh's resistance to Timur is weighed in debate
29. **§43 Moghulistan in the 15th century (lesson plan)** — Moghulistan's khans and the Kazakh secession close its arc
30. **§44 Socio-economic relations and state order, 14th–15th c. (lesson plan)** — ulus administration and taxes are diagrammed
31. **§45 Art and literature of the 13th–15th centuries (lesson plan)** — Horde-age epics and chronicles are read as literature and source
32. **§46 Features of traditional cattle-breeding (lesson plan)** — the four-kind herd's year is charted in the economy lesson
33. **§47 Nomad applied art achievements, 13th–15th c. (lesson plan)** — felt and silver masterpieces evidence craft at its peak
34. **§26–27 The Mongols conquer Kazakhstan's territory** — resistance city by city ends in the steppe's incorporation into an empire
35. **§28–29 Kazakhstan inside the Mongol Empire** — ulus rule, taxes and the yam post organise conquered Kazakhstan
36. **§30 Eastern Desht-i Kipchak separates politically: the Ak Orda** — the White Horde becomes the east's own state
37. **§31 Moghulistan (mid 14th–early 16th c.)** — the Chagatai east holds Semirechye as Moghulistan
38. **§32 The state of Amir Timur and his descendants** — Timur's empire and his heirs press on the steppe's south
39. **§33 The Abulkhair Khanate (1428–1468)** — Abulkhair's Uzbek state gathers — then loses — the Desht
40. **§34 The Nogai Horde; the Siberian Khanate** — Nogai and Siberian khanates flank the Kazakh lands
41. **§35 Social and political structure of the 13th–first-half-15th c. states** — ulus order and khanly courts are compared across successor states
42. **§36 The states' economy, 13th–first half 15th c.** — herds, cities and caravan taxes feed the post-Horde world
43. **§37 Spiritual culture develops, 13th–first half 15th c.** — epic singers and chroniclers keep the Horde age's memory
44. **3.1 How did the Mongols reach the level of statehood?** — Chinggis's reforms turn tribes into an organised empire
45. **3.2 How much resistance did the local population show the Mongol invasion?** — Otrar's six-month defence becomes the invasion's emblem
46. **3.3 Who changed Central Asia more — the Kipchaks or the Mongols?** — two steppe peoples' legacies are weighed against each other
47. **3.4 What changes came to the political map from the Mongol uluses' formation?** — Jochi's and Chagatai's uluses partition the steppe
48. **3.5 What was the Ak Orda's role in the formation of Kazakh statehood?** — the White Horde's order becomes the khanate's foundation
49. **3.6 What political changes did events in the Abulkhair and Moghulistan states bring?** — rival khanates set the stage for a Kazakh breakaway
50. **3.7 Why did Edige's and Koshim Khan's attempts to strengthen their states fail?** — the Nogai mirza and Siberian khan cannot hold their unions together
