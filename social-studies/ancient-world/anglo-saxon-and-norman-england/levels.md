# Anglo-Saxon and Norman England — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Two million people live under king, earls, thegns, ceorls and slaves, owing service for land; status is unusually fluid — a prospering peasant or travelling merchant can rise to thegn

## Sub lesson 2 — 1.2 The last years of Edward the Confessor and the succession crisis
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — The childless Edward leans on the Godwin family he married into; Harold's embassy to Normandy and the northern rising that exiles Tostig (1065) leave the succession open

## Sub lesson 3 — 1.3 The rival claimants for the throne
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — On Edward's death Harold claims the deathbed promise and the Witan's backing, against William of Normandy and Harald Hardrada, whose invasion Harold destroys at Stamford Bridge

## Sub lesson 4 — 1.4 The Norman invasion
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — William lands at Pevensey; at Hastings a rumour of his death nearly breaks his army until he lifts his helmet, and Harold's shield wall is drawn downhill and cut off

## Sub lesson 5 — 2.1 Establishing control
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — The earls submit at Berkhamstead rather than defend walled London; William rewards his followers with land, builds castles and creates the Marcher earldoms to guard the Welsh border

## Sub lesson 6 — 2.2 The causes and outcomes of Anglo-Saxon resistance, 1068–71
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Edwin and Morcar flee court and raise the North, Edgar the Aethling lends a royal name, and Hereward the Wake holds Ely; each rising is bought off or crushed in turn

## Sub lesson 7 — 2.3 The legacy of resistance to 1087
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — William answers northern rebellion with the Harrying of the North (1069–70), a devastation chroniclers called extraordinary and some modern historians call genocide, then completes the transfer of land to Normans

## Sub lesson 8 — 2.4 Revolt of the Earls, 1075
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Uniquely, Norman earls — Roger de Breteuil and Ralph de Gael — rebel alongside the Anglo-Saxon Waltheof; loyal Normans and English together defeat them, ending armed resistance

## Sub lesson 9 — 3.1 The feudal system and the Church
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — William rebuilds society as a feudal ladder — land for military service, even abbots doing homage and paying relief — and Normanises and reforms the English Church under Lanfranc

## Sub lesson 10 — 3.2 Norman government
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — William keeps much Anglo-Saxon government, strengthens sheriffs, reserves the royal forest, and in 1085–86 orders the Domesday survey to record every landholder's wealth

## Sub lesson 11 — 3.3 The Norman aristocracy
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — The new lords display superiority in stone buildings, dress and shaved hairstyles and French speech; Bishop Odo, the king's half-brother and co-regent, rises on land-grabs and falls by imprisonment

## Sub lesson 12 — 3.4 William I and his sons
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — The stern, avaricious Conqueror quarrels with his eldest son Robert Curthose; on his death (1087) Normandy goes to Robert and England to William Rufus, splitting the inheritance he had united
