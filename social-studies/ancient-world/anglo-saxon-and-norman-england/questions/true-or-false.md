# Anglo-Saxon and Norman England — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Two million people live under king, earls, thegns, ceorls and slaves, owing service for land; status is unusually fluid — a prospering peasant or travelling merchant can rise to thegn.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* is about 1.2 The last years of Edward the Confessor and the succession crisis.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The childless Edward leans on the Godwin family he married into; Harold's embassy to Normandy and the northern rising that exiles Tostig (1065) leave the succession open.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.2 The last years of Edward the Confessor and the succession crisis

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* is about 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.2 The last years of Edward the Confessor and the succession crisis

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** On Edward's death Harold claims the deathbed promise and the Witan's backing, against William of Normandy and Harald Hardrada, whose invasion Harold destroys at Stamford Bridge.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.3 The rival claimants for the throne

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* is about 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.3 The rival claimants for the throne

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** William lands at Pevensey; at Hastings a rumour of his death nearly breaks his army until he lifts his helmet, and Harold's shield wall is drawn downhill and cut off.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.4 The Norman invasion

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* is about 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.4 The Norman invasion

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** The earls submit at Berkhamstead rather than defend walled London; William rewards his followers with land, builds castles and creates the Marcher earldoms to guard the Welsh border.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 2.1 Establishing control

**10. (Sub lesson 5)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* is about 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society.
**Answer: False** — it is about 2.1 Establishing control

**11. (Sub lesson 6)** Edwin and Morcar flee court and raise the North, Edgar the Aethling lends a royal name, and Hereward the Wake holds Ely; each rising is bought off or crushed in turn.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 2.2 The causes and outcomes of Anglo-Saxon resistance, 1068–71

**12. (Sub lesson 6)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* is about 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society.
**Answer: False** — it is about 2.2 The causes and outcomes of Anglo-Saxon resistance, 1068–71

**13. (Sub lesson 7)** William answers northern rebellion with the Harrying of the North (1069–70), a devastation chroniclers called extraordinary and some modern historians call genocide, then completes the transfer of land to Normans.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 2.3 The legacy of resistance to 1087

**14. (Sub lesson 7)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* is about 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society.
**Answer: False** — it is about 2.3 The legacy of resistance to 1087

**15. (Sub lesson 8)** Uniquely, Norman earls — Roger de Breteuil and Ralph de Gael — rebel alongside the Anglo-Saxon Waltheof; loyal Normans and English together defeat them, ending armed resistance.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 2.4 Revolt of the Earls, 1075

**Answer: False** — it is about 2.4 Revolt of the Earls, 1075

**17. (Sub lesson 9)** William rebuilds society as a feudal ladder — land for military service, even abbots doing homage and paying relief — and Normanises and reforms the English Church under Lanfranc.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.1 The feudal system and the Church

**19. (Sub lesson 10)** William keeps much Anglo-Saxon government, strengthens sheriffs, reserves the royal forest, and in 1085–86 orders the Domesday survey to record every landholder's wealth.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.2 Norman government
