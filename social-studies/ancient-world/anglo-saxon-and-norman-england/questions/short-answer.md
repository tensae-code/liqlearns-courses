# Anglo-Saxon and Norman England — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society.
**Answer: Two million people live under king, earls, thegns, ceorls and slaves, owing service for land; status is unusually fluid — a prospering peasant or travelling merchant can rise to thegn. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In the United Kingdom's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England, c1060–1088* (Grade 10), 1.1 Anglo-Saxon society, printed p. 9.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: 1.2 The last years of Edward the Confessor and the succession crisis.
**Answer: The childless Edward leans on the Godwin family he married into; Harold's embassy to Normandy and the northern rising that exiles Tostig (1065) leave the succession open. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: On Edward's death Harold claims the deathbed promise and the Witan's backing, against William of Normandy and Harald Hardrada, whose invasion Harold destroys at Stamford Bridge. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: The earls submit at Berkhamstead rather than defend walled London; William rewards his followers with land, builds castles and creates the Marcher earldoms to guard the Welsh border. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: Edwin and Morcar flee court and raise the North, Edgar the Aethling lends a royal name, and Hereward the Wake holds Ely; each rising is bought off or crushed in turn. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: William answers northern rebellion with the Harrying of the North (1069–70), a devastation chroniclers called extraordinary and some modern historians call genocide, then completes the transfer of land to Normans. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**17. (Sub lesson 9)** Retell this part in your own words: 3.1 The feudal system and the Church.
**Answer: William rebuilds society as a feudal ladder — land for military service, even abbots doing homage and paying relief — and Normanises and reforms the English Church under Lanfranc. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: William keeps much Anglo-Saxon government, strengthens sheriffs, reserves the royal forest, and in 1085–86 orders the Domesday survey to record every landholder's wealth. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: The new lords display superiority in stone buildings, dress and shaved hairstyles and French speech; Bishop Odo, the king's half-brother and co-regent, rises on land-grabs and falls by imprisonment. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**23. (Sub lesson 12)** Retell this part in your own words: 3.4 William I and his sons.
**Answer: The stern, avaricious Conqueror quarrels with his eldest son Robert Curthose; on his death (1087) Normandy goes to Robert and England to William Rufus, splitting the inheritance he had united. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
