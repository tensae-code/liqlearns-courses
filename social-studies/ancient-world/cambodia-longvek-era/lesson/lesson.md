# Cambodia — the Longvek era

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Ang Chan I (1516–1566) founds a new walled capital at Longvek and holds a still-large Cambodia (map, p. 132) between rising Siam and Vietnam As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 2 Lesson 2, The Longvek era (16th century)

Ang Chan I (1516–1566) founds a new walled capital at Longvek and holds a still-large Cambodia (map, p. 132) between rising Siam and Vietnam (Ch. 2 Lesson 2, The Longvek era (16th century))

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 4 Lesson 3, The Longvek era (16th–17th centuries)

Ang Chan walls a new capital at Longvek (its moats and ramparts still traceable, photos p. 110); Siam presses from the west, and after Longvek's fall the court wanders again (Ch. 4 Lesson 3, The Longvek era (16th–17th centuries))

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 3, The Longvek era in the 16th century

Longvek is a fortress-city of moats, five gates and a royal palace with pagodas, open to Portuguese and Japanese traders — built strong after Ang Chan's death (1526) against the Siamese threat (History Ch. 2 Lesson 3, The Longvek era in the 16th century)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.
