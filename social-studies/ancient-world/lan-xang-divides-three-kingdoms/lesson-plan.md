# Lesson plan — Lan Xang Divides Three Kingdoms

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Lan Xang Divides Three Kingdoms
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Laos, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** princes quarrel over the succession, the kingdom splits into rival Lao states, and Siam moves in to rule them
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Part II, Lesson 10: Lan Xang breaks apart and is invaded by Siamese feudalism** — princes quarrel over the succession, the kingdom splits into rival Lao states, and Siam moves in to rule them
2. **Section 2, Part V, Lesson 13: 1695–1713 — Lan Xang divided, 1695–1713** — Sourigna Vongsa dies, the succession is disputed, and Lan Xang splits into Vientiane, Luang Prabang and Champasak
3. **Part II, Lesson 10: Lan Xang divides; Siam invades** — princes quarrel over the throne, the kingdom splits, and Siam moves in
4. **Part V, Lesson 13: 1695–1713 — Lao feudal decline; Lan Xang divided** — the succession is disputed and Lan Xang splits into three kingdoms
