# Video script — Our master Ibrahim

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Yemen's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Ibrahim, born in Iraq in the Babylonian age, smashes his people's idols and faces the tyrant Nimrod, is thrown into the fire and saved, then raises the Kaaba in Mecca with his son Ismail and founds the Hajj rites people still perform.
Ibrahim is born in southern Iraq under the first Babylonian state, where a tyrant authority claims divinity, bans the mention of God on pain of burning and makes idolatry the state religion, and the boy — son of Azar the idol-maker — grows up untouched by it, reasons his way past star, moon and sun that set, and begins his call with his own father before facing Nimrod and the whole society.
arguments exhausted, Ibrahim waits for the festival day when the temple stands empty, smashes every idol but the biggest and hangs the axe on it, answers the public trial by pointing at the chief idol until the crowd's own conscience stirs — then hardens — they build a vast pyre and hurl him in by catapult, the fire turns cool and safe, and in open debate he silences Nimrod: God brings the sun from the east, bring it from the west.
Ibrahim migrates with Sarah and his nephew Lot to the blessed land, settles in Palestine where al-Khalil still bears his name, sends Lot to Sodom, and is given Ismail by Hajar and then Ishaq by Sarah — two lines of prophets, Ismail's running to Muhammad; he carries Hajar and the infant Ismail to Mecca to rebuild the Kaaba on Adam's foundations, sees in a dream that he must sacrifice his son, and at the very moment of obedience God ransoms the boy with a great sacrifice, the root of Eid al-Adha's takbirs.
