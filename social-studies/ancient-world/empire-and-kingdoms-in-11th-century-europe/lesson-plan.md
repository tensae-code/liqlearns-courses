# Lesson plan — Empire And Kingdoms In 11th Century Europe

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Empire And Kingdoms In 11th Century Europe
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Italy, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Ottonian emperors rebuild imperial power in Germany while the weak Capetians hold a small French royal domain and Norman adventurers carve kingdoms from England to southern Italy
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unità 1, Cap. 2 §§1–2 The rebirth of the Empire in Germany; Capetian France and the Norman kingdoms** — the Ottonian emperors rebuild imperial power in Germany while the weak Capetians hold a small French royal domain and Norman adventurers carve kingdoms from England to southern Italy
2. **Il quadro europeo e l'universalismo cattolico; L'Impero; I regni; I normanni in Italia** — Ottonian emperors invest bishop-counts to hold Germany and Italy (the body text follows Otto I to Otto III's Roman dream), weak kings share Europe with new regna, and Normans take the South
