# Christianity — from persecuted sect to state religion

## Do it first

In one century, followers of a crucified teacher hide from Roman police. Three centuries later, their faith IS the Roman state — its churches rising over the forum, its God on the emperor's standard. How did a persecuted sect become an empire's religion? Follow the turn.

## Sub lesson 1 — A teaching in Palestine — and Rome's answer

Christianity began in **1st-century Palestine**, on the teaching of **Jesus**, born at **Bethlehem in Roman Judea**. His followers carried the teaching outward — and Rome first saw Christians as a **threat**. Under **Nero (AD 54–68)** the persecution began: Christians were punished for a faith the empire read as disloyalty, refusing the gods and the emperor-cult of the state.

[Picture 22508 — a map of Christianity's spread from Palestine across the Roman world]

For generations the pattern held: a small, stubborn sect, growing quietly through the empire's cities even as emperors struck at it. Nothing in those years suggested what was coming — that an emperor himself would turn.

## Sub lesson 2 — The turn — Constantine, Milan, and an official religion

The turn came with **Constantine**. His **conversion in 312** changed an empire: in **313** the **Edict of Milan** granted **toleration** — Christianity could be practised openly, its churches and property restored. The persecuted walked out of hiding into the sunlight of imperial favour.

One more step completed the arc: under **Theodosius**, Christianity became the Empire's **official religion**. From state religion, it spread — across the Roman world and, in the centuries after, **across the world**. Ethiopia's own chapter of that spread — Frumentius at the Aksumite court — is told in the Ethiopia title of this course, and it begins only a generation after Milan.

## Say it to your companion
