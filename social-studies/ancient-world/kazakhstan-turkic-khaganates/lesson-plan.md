# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan Turkic Khaganates

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan Turkic Khaganates
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bumin and Istemi found the first great Turkic empire across the steppe
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§2 The Turkic Khaganate (552–603)** — Bumin and Istemi found the first great Turkic empire across the steppe
2. **§3–4 The Western and Eastern Turkic Khaganates** — the khaganate splits into rival West and East, each ruling its half of Eurasia
3. **§5 The Turgesh Khaganate (704–756)** — the Turgesh take the West's throne and hold Semirechye against Arabs and China
4. **§6 The Karluk Khaganate (756–940)** — the Karluks succeed in the south-east and guard the Silk Road towns
5. **§7 Written sources on Turkic rulers' politics** — Chinese annals and runic stones let pupils judge the khagans from evidence
6. **§8 Old Turkic writing** — the Orkhon runes carve Kultegin's and Bilge Khagan's deeds in stone
7. **§9 The Battle of Atlakh (Talas), summer 751** — on the Talas river Turks and Arabs break China's westward march — paper-making changes hands
8. **§10 The Oghuz state (late 9th–early 11th c.)** — the Oghuz yabghu state rises on the Syr Darya and Aral steppe
9. **§11 The Kimak Khaganate (9th–early 11th c.)** — the Kimaks rule the Irtysh steppe with towns, herds and river trade
10. **§12 The military art of the Turks** — mounted archery and feigned retreat make Turkic armies feared on two continents
11. **§13 The traditional economy of the nomads** — seasonal herding is systematised as the steppe's productive order
12. **§14 The Turks' worldview** — Tengri the sky god, Umai and the earth-water spirits order the Turkic cosmos
13. **§15 How Turkic peoples' migration shaped Eurasia's history** — Turkic migrations redraw the peoples' map from Siberia to Anatolia
