## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ch. 3 The poetry and religion of the Greeks** — Homer's epics and the Olympian gods give scattered Greeks one language of story and worship, with hospitality to strangers as a sacred duty
2. **Ch. 4 Art and writing** — Greeks borrow the Phoenician alphabet, add vowels, and paint geometric vases whose shapes serve every use of daily life
3. **Ch. 9 Letters** — archaic Greece fills with poets and wise sayings — literature becomes the Greeks' shared school
4. **Ch. 3 Poetry and religion exercises** — pupils find Olympian gods' names hidden in today's everyday words
5. **Ch. 4 Art and writing exercises** — close observation of a geometric vase turns decoration into evidence
6. **Ch. 9 Letters exercises** — an acrostic spells out the great poet — archaic literature learned by wordplay
7. **Ch. C2 The cultural rebirth** — the alphabet returns, Homer's epics are written down, and the first Olympic Games date the Greek revival
8. **Ch. D9 Letters (Archaic)** — lyric poets, the first philosophers and early historians turn the borrowed alphabet into Greek literature
