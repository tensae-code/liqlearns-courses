## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 15, Hồ Quý Ly's reforms and the state of Đại Ngu (1400–1407)** — as the Trần rot (Chu Văn An's petition to behead seven corrupt officials is refused and he retires to teach), Hồ Quý Ly seizes power, renames the country Đại Ngu, caps land and slave holdings, issues paper money, builds the stone citadel of Tây Đô in three months, casts great cannon, and promotes chữ Nôm and examinations
2. **Bài 15, the fall of the Hồ to the Ming, 1407** — the Ming invade on the pretext of the usurpation; Đa Bang and Đông Đô fall in January 1407 and Tây Đô by June; Hồ Nguyên Trừng's verdict is quoted — "I do not fear fighting; I fear the people's hearts do not follow" — for the Hồ had trusted fortresses and a regular army and shut themselves into passive defence
3. **Bài 9 Hồ Quý Ly's reforms: as the Trần rot (Chu Văn An's rejected petition to behead seven flatterers), the usurper-reformer caps nobles' fields and household slaves, issues paper money on pain of death for hoarding copper, counts every child from age two for the army, builds Tây Đô's stone citadel and divine cannons, forces monks under fifty back to lay life, and raises chữ Nôm — yet the reforms stay half-done and the Ming still come** — the boldest reformer in Vietnam's feudal age rebuilds everything except the people's trust
