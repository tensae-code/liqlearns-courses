# Lesson plan — Crime and punishment in medieval England

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Crime and punishment in medieval England
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (United Kingdom, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In medieval England, local communities, the king and the Church share justice — and conquest rewrites it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England** — Local communities, the king and the Church share justice in Anglo-Saxon England; the turn is 1066, when a foreign conqueror rewrites what counts as crime
2. **1.2 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Norman England** — William, who took the throne by force at Hastings, tightens royal grip on the law with the Forest Laws, the murdrum fine and trial by combat
3. **1.3 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in the later Middle Ages** — Henry II centralises justice with Justices in Eyre, coroners and Justices of the Peace, heresy becomes a crime, yet old manor courts and local punishments carry on below
4. **1.4 Case study: the influence of the Church on crime and punishment** — The Church, owning a fifth of the country's wealth, offers sanctuary and Benefit of Clergy to escape punishment; Henry II tries and fails to cut that power back
