# Crime and punishment in medieval England

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: In medieval England, local communities, the king and the Church share justice — and conquest rewrites it. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England

Local communities, the king and the Church share justice in Anglo-Saxon England; the turn is 1066, when a foreign conqueror rewrites what counts as crime.

William, who took the throne by force at Hastings, tightens royal grip on the law with the Forest Laws, the murdrum fine and trial by combat.

## Sub lesson 3 — 1.3 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in the later Middle Ages

Henry II centralises justice with Justices in Eyre, coroners and Justices of the Peace, heresy becomes a crime, yet old manor courts and local punishments carry on below.
