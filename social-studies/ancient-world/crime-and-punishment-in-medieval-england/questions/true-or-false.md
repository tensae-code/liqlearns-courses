# Crime and punishment in medieval England — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Local communities, the king and the Church share justice in Anglo-Saxon England; the turn is 1066, when a foreign conqueror rewrites what counts as crime.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 1.2 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Norman England.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** William, who took the throne by force at Hastings, tightens royal grip on the law with the Forest Laws, the murdrum fine and trial by combat.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.2 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Norman England

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.2 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Norman England

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Henry II centralises justice with Justices in Eyre, coroners and Justices of the Peace, heresy becomes a crime, yet old manor courts and local punishments carry on below.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.3 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in the later Middle Ages

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.3 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in the later Middle Ages

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** The Church, owning a fifth of the country's wealth, offers sanctuary and Benefit of Clergy to escape punishment; Henry II tries and fails to cut that power back.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 1.4 Case study: the influence of the Church on crime and punishment

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England.
**Answer: False** — it is about 1.4 Case study: the influence of the Church on crime and punishment
