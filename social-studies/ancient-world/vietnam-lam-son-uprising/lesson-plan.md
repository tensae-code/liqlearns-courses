## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 12, the Lam Sơn uprising, 1418–1427** — at the 1416 Lũng Nhai oath Lê Lợi and 18 heroes swear together; in 1418 he raises the banner as Bình Định Vương and, with Nguyễn Trãi's strategy of winning hearts, fights the Ming for ten years to complete victory
2. **Bài 12, story of Lê Lai giving his life for his lord** — besieged on Chí Linh mountain in 1418, Lê Lai puts on Lê Lợi's robe, charges out and is killed in his place; the saying runs "the 21st for Lê Lai, the 22nd for Lê Lợi"
3. **Bài 12, the Chi Lăng victory** — the Ming relief army is drawn into the Chi Lăng pass; cannon fire signals the ambush from both slopes and commander Liễu Thăng falls to a javelin at Mã Yên slope, breaking the relief of Đông Quan
4. **Bài 16, the raising of Lam Sơn and the hard years, 1418–1423** — at the Lũng Nhai oath of 1416 Lê Lợi and eighteen heroes swear to expel the Ming; Nguyễn Trãi joins him; three times the army falls back on the Chí Linh mountains, once to barely a hundred men — Lê Lai disguises himself as Lê Lợi to draw off the pursuit and dies, and in the famine of 1421 Lê Lợi kills his own elephants and horses to feed the troops
5. **Bài 16, the turn: Nghệ An and the march north, 1424–1426** — Nguyễn Chích's plan — "Nghệ An is strategic ground, wide land, many people" — breaks the encirclement; by late 1424 Nghệ An is free and the liberation runs from Thanh Hoá to the Hải Vân pass; in 1426 the army marches north, the people rise everywhere, and the Ming shut themselves in Đông Quan
6. **Bài 16, total victory: Tốt Động – Chúc Động, Chi Lăng – Xương Giang and the Đông Quan oath, 1426–1427** — Vương Thông's reinforcements are ambushed at Tốt Động – Chúc Động (November 1426); in October 1427 Liễu Thăng is killed in the Chi Lăng pass ambush and Lê Lợi sends his seal and the news to the second column, which flees; on 10 December 1427 Lê Lợi and Vương Thông swear the Đông Quan oath on the Hồng river bank, Nguyễn Trãi proclaims the Bình Ngô đại cáo, and in January 1428 the Ming withdraw
7. **Chuyên đề 3.IV Nguyễn Trãi (1380–1442): Lê Lợi's strategist of "tâm công" — attacking the enemy's hearts; his letters, said to be worth 100,000 troops, win cities without slaughter; after victory he drafts the Bình Ngô đại cáo and founds chữ Nôm literature with the Quốc âm thi tập; UNESCO honours him in 1980** — a pen besieges Đông Quan more cheaply than an army, then writes the independence proclamation in verse
8. **Bài 8 The Lam Sơn uprising (1418–1427): after the Hồ fall, the Ming rename the country Giao Chỉ and burn its books; Lê Lợi swears the Lũng Nhai oath (1416), survives three retreats to Chí Linh, turns south on Nguyễn Chích's plan, wins Tốt Động–Chúc Động (1426) and Chi Lăng–Xương Giang (1427), then ends the war with the Đông Quan oath and the Bình Ngô đại cáo** — ten years from hunted band to liberation army, closed not with a massacre but a sworn peace
