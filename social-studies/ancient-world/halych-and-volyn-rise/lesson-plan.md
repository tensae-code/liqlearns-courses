# Lesson plan — Halych and Volyn rise

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Halych and Volyn rise
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Ukraine, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in the west Yaroslav Osmomysl makes Halych rich on salt and the Dnister trade while Volyn grows beside it — and all the while Pechenegs, Torks and Polovtsians ride the steppe and Crimea changes hands beyond the mountains.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Halych rich on salt, Volyn beside it** — In the west, Yaroslav Osmomysl makes Halych rich on salt and the Dnister trade. Volyn grows beside it — the west rises while the old centre weakens.
2. **The steppe peoples and Crimea** — All the while Pechenegs, Torks and Polovtsians ride the steppe. Beyond the mountains, Crimea changes hands — the south is its own world of nomads and ports.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Rus-Ukraine fragmented — Kyiv, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how Halych grew rich under Yaroslav Osmomysl: on salt and the Dnister trade, with Volyn growing beside it.
2. Name the nomadic peoples who rode the steppe in this age: Pechenegs, Torks and Polovtsians — and that Crimea changed hands beyond the mountains.
