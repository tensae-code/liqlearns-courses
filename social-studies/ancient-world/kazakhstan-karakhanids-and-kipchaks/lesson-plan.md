# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan Karakhanids And Kipchaks

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan Karakhanids And Kipchaks
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** old khaganates fall and new powers — Karakhanids, Kipchaks, Khwarezm — divide the steppe
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§16 The political map of the Great Steppe changes, 10th–early 13th c.** — old khaganates fall and new powers — Karakhanids, Kipchaks, Khwarezm — divide the steppe
2. **§17 The Karakhanid state (942–1212)** — the Karakhanids unite the south-east and take Islam as a state faith
3. **§18 Islam established in the Karakhanid state** — mosques, madrasas and Sufi sheikhs root Islam in the steppe cities
4. **§19 The Kipchak Khanate (early 11th c.–1219)** — the Kipchaks hold the whole Desht-i Kipchak from Irtysh to Danube
5. **§20–21 The Naimans, Kereits and Jalairs** — three great tribal states neighbour the Kipchaks on the eve of the Mongols
6. **§16 The Great Steppe's political map changes, 10th–13th c. (lesson plan)** — pupils redraw the steppe map as powers rise and fall
7. **§17 The Karakhanid state, 940–1210 (lesson plan)** — the first Turkic Muslim state is built lesson by lesson
8. **§18 Islam established (lesson plan)** — Islam's rooting in steppe cities is traced through mosques and schools
9. **§19 The Kipchak Khanate, early 11th c.–1219 (lesson plan)** — the Kipchak confederation's vast steppe is mapped and peopled
10. **§20–21 Naimans, Kereits and Jalairs (lesson plan)** — three tribal states are compared on the Mongols' eve
11. **§11 The Karakhanid state (942–1212)** — the Karakhanids unite the south-east under Islam's banner
12. **§12 The Karakitai state (1128–1212)** — the Kara Khitai gurkhan rules Turkic Muslims from Balasagun
13. **§13 Naimans, Kereits, Jalairs (12th–early 13th c.)** — three tribal states stand on the Mongols' threshold
14. **§14 The Kipchak Khanate (11th–early 13th c.)** — the Kipchaks' Desht stretches unbroken to the Danube
15. **1.5 What proves the Kimeks developed an urban culture?** — Kimek towns on the Irtysh challenge the 'pure nomad' picture
16. **2.1 How much did the political map change in the 10th–early 13th centuries?** — Karakhanids, Khorezm and Kipchaks replace the khaganates
17. **2.2 Why did the Karakhanid state declare Islam its state religion?** — Satuk Boghra's conversion turns a steppe state Muslim
18. **2.3 The Naimans, Kereits and Jalayirs on the medieval political stage** — Christian Kereit and Buddhist Naiman courts neighbour the Muslim south
19. **2.4 Why was the Eurasian steppe called Desht-i Kipchak?** — the Kipchak confederation gives the whole steppe its name
