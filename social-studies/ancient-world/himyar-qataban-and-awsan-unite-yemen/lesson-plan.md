# Lesson plan — Himyar, Qataban and Awsan

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Himyar, Qataban and Awsan
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Yemen, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Awsan in Wadi Markha and Qataban in Wadi Bayhan give way to Himyar at Zafar, which unites all Yemen under Shammar Yuhar'ish, king of Saba and Dhu Raydan and Hadramawt and Yamnat
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Himyar, Qataban and Awsan** — awsan in Wadi Markha and Qataban in Wadi Bayhan give way to Himyar at Zafar, which unites all Yemen under Shammar Yuhar'ish, king of Saba and Dhu Raydan and Hadramawt and Yamnat, down to Abu Karib As'ad and the last king, Sayf bin Dhi Yazan
2. **States of the ancient Yemeni civilisations (the Himyarite age)** — in the second Sabaean age, the Himyarite kings of Saba and Dhu Raydan unite Yemen from their capital Zafar — Dhamar Ali Watar annexes Raydan, Shammar Yuhar'ish adds Hadramawt and most of Yemen, Abu Karib As'ad al-Kamil stretches rule the length and breadth of Arabia — until the last king, Yusuf As'ar Yath'ar, called Dhu Nuwas, turns to Judaism, massacres the people of Najran, and brings the Abyssinians down on Yemen with Byzantine ships
