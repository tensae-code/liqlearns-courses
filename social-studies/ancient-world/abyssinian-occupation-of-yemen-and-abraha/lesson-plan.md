A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Abyssinian occupation of Yemen** — after the Himyarite king Dhu Nuwas burns the believers of Najran in the trenches, Abyssinian armies cross in 525 CE and take Zafar, their ruler Abraha builds a great church to turn pilgrims from Mecca and marches with elephants to demolish the Kaaba, and flocks of birds destroy his army in the Year of the Elephant, the year Muhammad is born
2. **Yemen's condition before Islam: the Abyssinian invasion and the Yemeni resistance** — rome and Persia compete for Yemen's trade-road position and play its Christian-Jewish conflict until Dhu Nuwas burns Najran's Christians in the trenches and Byzantium's ally Abyssinia invades in 523 CE under Aryat; Abraha rules after him, tries to demolish the Kaaba and is destroyed, and Abyssinian rule lasts some fifty years — but the Yemenis never submit, and Sayf bin Dhi Yazan wins Persia's help through al-Hira's mediation, lands with Wahriz and some 800 men in 575 CE, and the Yemenis rise with him to kill Masruq and free their country
