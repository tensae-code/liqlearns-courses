# Jordan's Iron Age kingdoms — Ammon, Moab and Edom

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: the kingdoms of Edom, Moab and Ammon rise on Jordan's land; Moab's King Mesha records his victories on the basalt Mesha Stele from his. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 3 Lesson 1: journey in the Capital Governorate

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: the ancient kingdoms in Jordan

Edom rises in the south with its capital at Busayra, Ammon holds the centre from Rabbat Ammon (Amman) between Wadi al-Mujib and the Zarqa, and Moab leaves King Mesha's stele

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age (1200–332 BCE)

Arab tribes out of the peninsula found three kingdoms — Edom in the red-sandstone south, whose people channel rain into dams and wells and guide caravans to the Gulf (the Nabataeans inherit their building craft); Moab, whose King Mesha defeats the Hebrews, paves roads, builds Madaba, and leaves his victories on the black basalt Mesha Stele, oldest of its kind, to his war-god Chemosh; and Ammon, ruling from Rabbath Ammon — today's Amman — under kings like Nahash I, its farmers guarding their vines from towers like Rujm al-Malfouf

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 5 — Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age

the same three kingdoms with the academic details filled in — Moab's capital moves to Dhiban in Madaba, set between Wadi al-Mujib and Wadi al-Wala for its fortress site and farmland; the Tell Siran bottle is found inside the University of Jordan's own campus; and in 582 BCE the Ammonite kingdom becomes an Assyrian dependency

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.
