## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2 Lesson 1: ancient civilisations in Jordan** — the kingdoms of Edom, Moab and Ammon rise on Jordan's land; Moab's King Mesha records his victories on the basalt Mesha Stele from his capital Dhiban
2. **Unit 3 Lesson 1: journey in the Capital Governorate** — Amman, among the oldest inhabited cities in the world, was the Ammonites' capital Rabbat Ammon from the 7th century BCE, and its Citadel still overlooks the city
3. **Unit 4 Lesson 3: the ancient kingdoms in Jordan** — Edom rises in the south with its capital at Busayra, Ammon holds the centre from Rabbat Ammon (Amman) between Wadi al-Mujib and the Zarqa, and Moab leaves King Mesha's stele
4. **Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age (1200–332 BCE)** — Arab tribes out of the peninsula found three kingdoms — Edom in the red-sandstone south, whose people channel rain into dams and wells and guide caravans to the Gulf (the Nabataeans inherit their building craft); Moab, whose King Mesha defeats the Hebrews, paves roads, builds Madaba, and leaves his victories on the black basalt Mesha Stele, oldest of its kind, to his war-god Chemosh; and Ammon, ruling from Rabbath Ammon — today's Amman — under kings like Nahash I, its farmers guarding their vines from towers like Rujm al-Malfouf
5. **Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age** — the same three kingdoms with the academic details filled in — Moab's capital moves to Dhiban in Madaba, set between Wadi al-Mujib and Wadi al-Wala for its fortress site and farmland; the Tell Siran bottle is found inside the University of Jordan's own campus; and in 582 BCE the Ammonite kingdom becomes an Assyrian dependency
