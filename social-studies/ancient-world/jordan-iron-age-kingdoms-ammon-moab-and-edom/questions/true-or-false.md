# Jordan's Iron Age kingdoms — Ammon, Moab and Edom — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** the kingdoms of Edom, Moab and Ammon rise on Jordan's land; Moab's King Mesha records his victories on the basalt Mesha Stele from his capital Dhiban.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 2 Lesson 1: ancient civilisations in Jordan

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *الدراسات الاجتماعية — الصف الرابع، الفصل الدراسي الأول (Social Studies, Grade 4, Term 1)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 2 Lesson 1: ancient civilisations in Jordan

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Amman, among the oldest inhabited cities in the world, was the Ammonites' capital Rabbat Ammon from the 7th century BCE, and its Citadel still overlooks the city.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 3 Lesson 1: journey in the Capital Governorate

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *الدراسات الاجتماعية — الصف الخامس، الفصل الدراسي الأول (Social Studies, Grade 5, Term 1)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 3 Lesson 1: journey in the Capital Governorate

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Edom rises in the south with its capital at Busayra, Ammon holds the centre from Rabbat Ammon (Amman) between Wadi al-Mujib and the Zarqa, and Moab leaves King Mesha's stele.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Unit 4 Lesson 3: the ancient kingdoms in Jordan

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *الدراسات الاجتماعية — الصف السابع، الفصل الدراسي الأول (Social Studies, Grade 7, Term 1)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Unit 4 Lesson 3: the ancient kingdoms in Jordan

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Arab tribes out of the peninsula found three kingdoms — Edom in the red-sandstone south, whose people channel rain into dams and wells and guide caravans to the Gulf (the Nabataeans inherit their building craft); Moab, whose King Mesha defeats the Hebrews, paves roads, builds Madaba, and leaves his victories on the black basalt Mesha Stele, oldest of its kind, to his war-god Chemosh; and Ammon, ruling from Rabbath Ammon — today's Amman — under kings like Nahash I, its farmers guarding their vines from towers like Rujm al-Malfouf.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age (1200–332 BCE)

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *تاريخ الأردن — الصف الحادي عشر، الفرع المهني (History of Jordan, Grade 11, Vocational Track), Term 1 — Unit 1: Jordan in the Ancient Ages* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age (1200–332 BCE)

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** the same three kingdoms with the academic details filled in — Moab's capital moves to Dhiban in Madaba, set between Wadi al-Mujib and Wadi al-Wala for its fortress site and farmland; the Tell Siran bottle is found inside the University of Jordan's own campus; and in 582 BCE the Ammonite kingdom becomes an Assyrian dependency.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age

**10. (Sub lesson 5)** This part of *تاريخ الأردن — الصف الحادي عشر، الفرع الأكاديمي (History of Jordan, Grade 11, Academic Track), Term 1 — Unit 1: Jordan in the Ancient Ages; Unit 2: Jordan in the Islamic Ages; Unit 3: Jordan in the Modern Age (all three units in one term)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lesson 2: Jordan in the Iron Age
