## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The state of Saba** — saba, first at Sirwah then at Marib, unifies Yemen under Karib'il Watar, builds the Marib Dam and trades between north and south Arabia, and its queen Bilqis meets Solomon and, in the book's telling, brings the Yemenis into his faith
2. **States of the ancient Yemeni civilisations (Saba)** — saba, the longest and oldest of Yemen's kingdoms, rules first from Sirwah then Marib, reaches the al-Ghala oasis in the north of the Hijaz, and its most famous hour is Queen Bilqis's dealings with Solomon, which the Qur'an itself records
3. **Queen Bilqis: a civilisation and faith** — bilqis, a king's daughter ruling Saba at its height — great army, rich farms, the incense trade — receives Sulayman's letter, consults her chiefs who boast of strength yet leave the decision to her, tests him with a treasure-gift which he firmly refuses, then travels herself; her throne is brought to Jerusalem faster than an eye blinks and slightly altered to test her, the glass floor of the palace she mistakes for water, and she declares her Islam with Sulayman and goes home to rule Yemen by it
