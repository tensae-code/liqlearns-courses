# Lesson plan — Islam comes to Afghanistan

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Islam comes to Afghanistan
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Afghanistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a land with no strong central state, its provinces divided, proves ready for the new faith, and the book asks why its people accepted Islam
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 1 (Afghanistan and Islam), Lesson 1: The entry of Islam into Afghanistan** — a land with no strong central state, its provinces divided, proves ready for the new faith, and the book asks why its people accepted Islam
2. **Lesson 20: The state of Afghanistan at the advent of Islam** — in the late 6th and early 7th centuries the land is run by local amirs and Kushan and Hephthalite remnants; Balkh's Nine-Dome mosque is counted its first
3. **Lesson 1: The general state of Afghanistan at the advent of Islam** — on Islam's eve the country lacks political, economic and social stability, ruled in pieces since the later 6th century, and the lesson asks what inclined its people toward Islam
4. **Lesson 2: The entry and spread of Islam in Afghanistan** — the new faith enters and spreads through the land province by province

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
