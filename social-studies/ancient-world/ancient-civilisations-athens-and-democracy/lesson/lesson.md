# Ancient civilisations — Athens and democracy

## Do it first

In your school, who makes the rules — one person, a few, or everyone together? Now imagine a whole city where every citizen walks to one hill and votes on the laws himself. That city existed. Its name is Athens.

## A city of crafts and the sea

**Athens** stands in **Attica, in central-east Greece**. After **750 BC** its people mostly **crafted, fished and traded** — workshop, boat and market people, not a farming aristocracy. A city of makers and traders was about to remake government itself.

## Democracy — the citizens rule themselves

Athens' order was **democracy — history's first direct democracy in practice**: the **citizens themselves made law and policy**, voting in person, as the picture shows. **Cleisthenes (570–508 BC)** legislated **free speech and equality before the law**, and set up a **council of 500, chosen yearly by lot** — by chance, so no family could own a seat. Under **Pericles (461–429 BC)** participation widened further, and **Athenian democracy reached its peak**.

## Grade 9 depth — The Grade 9 book's Greece — Hellenes, poleis and a classical legacy

The Grade 9 book calls Greece — emerging about **2,500 years ago** — the **birthplace of Western civilisation**. Its people, the **Hellenes**, were **culturally united** — common language, religion and culture — yet **never united politically**: they lived in **poleis**, city-states of a town plus its villages and farmland, **Athens and Sparta** the best known.

[Picture 22523 — the Parthenon above Athens and a Greek theatre]

The classical and Hellenistic achievements are listed like a treasury: **architecture (the Parthenon)**, the **invention of drama**, **marble and bronze sculpture**, **history-writing** — Herodotus on the Persian Wars, Thucydides' unbiased account of the Peloponnesian War — and **medicine**. Many small cities, one shared culture, and a legacy the world still studies: that is the Grade 9 Greece.


## Grade 11 depth — Greece in depth — Athens, Sparta, philosophy and Alexander

The Grade 11 book sets the two Greek templates against each other once more, at full length: Athens, inventing democracy in its assembly; Sparta, organising a whole society as an army — and then Greece's mind outrunning both, in philosophy that asked what justice and truth themselves are. Alexander carries the Greek world east in a generation, and the Hellenistic age spreads its cities, libraries and mixed cultures from Egypt to India.
