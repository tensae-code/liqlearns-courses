## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The early Malay kingdoms** — from the first centuries, kingdoms rise across the Malay world — Funan the first, Champa, Kedah Tua in the Bujang Valley, Chih-Tu, Gangga Nagara, Beruas, Srivijaya (7th century), Mataram (9th), Santubong and Majapahit (13th) — leaving Hindu-Buddhist monuments (Borobudur, Prambanan, Muara Jambi), trading spices, tin and gold, and exchanging envoys with China and India (Unit 7 Kerajaan Melayu Awal: the early Malay kingdoms)
2. **The famous kingdoms of Alam Melayu** — Funan is founded by the Brahmin Kaundinya at Vyadhapura; Srivijaya is founded by Dapunta Hyang Sri Jayanasa at Palembang in 683, takes Bangka and Taruma, and under Sangramadhananjaya in the 8th century conquers Sumatra, southern Myanmar, the Malay Peninsula and the Kra Isthmus; Angkor builds its capital at Angkor Thom (Bab 1: the famous kingdoms of Alam Melayu)
3. **Majapahit, Kedah Tua and Gangga Nagara** — Majapahit is founded in 1294 by Raden Vijaya with Mongol help, rules from Trowulan, and is steadied by Patih Gajah Mada under Queen Tribhuwana Tunggadewi; in the peninsula, Kedah Tua's candi dot the Bujang Valley and Gangga Nagara works as a port from the 6th century — all of them contemporaries of Rome and the other outside powers, trading and exchanging envoys (Bab 1: Majapahit, Kedah Tua and Gangga Nagara)
4. **How the Alam Melayu kingdoms lived** — one civilisation in many kingdoms — Malay language and writing, a literature of hikayat and syair, candi architecture, a ranked social structure, tin-gold-spice economies run from river-mouth ports, and a religious journey from Hindu-Buddhist faiths to Islam that leaves a unique shared heritage (Bab 2–4: how the Alam Melayu kingdoms lived)
