# The early Malay kingdoms — Funan, Srivijaya and Majapahit — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** From the first centuries, kingdoms rise across the Malay world — Funan the first, Champa, Kedah Tua in the Bujang Valley, Chih-Tu, Gangga Nagara, Beruas, Srivijaya (7th century), Mataram (9th), Santubong and Majapahit (13th) — leaving Hindu-Buddhist monuments (Borobudur, Prambanan, Muara Jambi), trading spices, tin and gold, and exchanging envoys with China and India
**Answer: True** — as the book teaches it (Unit 7 Kerajaan Melayu Awal: the early Malay kingdoms).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Funan is founded by the Brahmin Kaundinya at Vyadhapura; Srivijaya is founded by Dapunta Hyang Sri Jayanasa at Palembang in 683, takes Bangka and Taruma, and under Sangramadhananjaya in the 8th century conquers Sumatra, southern Myanmar, the Malay Peninsula and the Kra Isthmus; Angkor builds its capital at Angkor Thom
**Answer: True** — as the book teaches it (Bab 1: the famous kingdoms of Alam Melayu).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Majapahit is founded in 1294 by Raden Vijaya with Mongol help, rules from Trowulan, and is steadied by Patih Gajah Mada under Queen Tribhuwana Tunggadewi; in the peninsula, Kedah Tua's candi dot the Bujang Valley and Gangga Nagara works as a port from the 6th century — all of them contemporaries of Rome and the other outside powers, trading and exchanging envoys
**Answer: True** — as the book teaches it (Bab 1: Majapahit, Kedah Tua and Gangga Nagara).

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** One civilisation in many kingdoms — Malay language and writing, a literature of hikayat and syair, candi architecture, a ranked social structure, tin-gold-spice economies run from river-mouth ports, and a religious journey from Hindu-Buddhist faiths to Islam that leaves a unique shared heritage
**Answer: True** — as the book teaches it (Bab 2–4: how the Alam Melayu kingdoms lived).
