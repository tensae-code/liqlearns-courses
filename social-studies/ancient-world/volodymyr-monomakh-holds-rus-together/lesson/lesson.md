# Volodymyr Monomakh holds Rus together

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: the Liubech congress of princes (1097) fails to stop the feuds, but Monomakh — campaigner against the Polovtsians, author of the famous Instruction to his children — reunites most of Rus under Kyiv once more, and his son Mstyslav holds it together until 1132. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Liubech fails, Monomakh rises

The Liubech congress of princes in 1097 fails to stop the feuds. But Volodymyr Monomakh — campaigner against the Polovtsians — reunites most of Rus under Kyiv once more.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Instruction and Mstyslav

Monomakh is also the author of the famous Instruction to his children. His son Mstyslav the Great holds Rus together until 1132 — the last holding of the old unity.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of Volodymyr Monomakh holds Rus together in your own words, part by part, using the sub lesson headings as your beats. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Say what the Liubech congress of 1097 failed to do, and what Monomakh did instead: reunited most of Rus under Kyiv.
2. Say what Monomakh wrote and how long the unity lasted: the Instruction to his children; his son Mstyslav held Rus together until 1132.
