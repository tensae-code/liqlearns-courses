# Medieval world — the Ottomans

## Do it first

A small Anatolian chiefdom becomes the empire that will one day take Constantinople. Its founder gives it his own name. What does a small state need to outgrow all its neighbours? The Ottomans' answer begins with an army that never disbands.

## Osman founds, Orhan builds

The Ottoman Empire was **founded at the start of the 14th century by Osman (1300–1326)**, the Anatolian Turkish leader **whose name became 'Ottoman'**. The Turks had **accepted Islam in the 10th century** and **excelled in military organisation and weapons**. **Orhan (1326–1359)** formed the standing **Janissary army** — soldiers who serve permanently, not seasonally — and **first took European land, at Gallipoli**. Follow the growth in the picture: Anatolia first, then the toe-hold in Europe.

## Murad I enters the Balkans

**Murad I (1359–1389)** carried the Ottomans **into the Balkans** — from Gallipoli's toe-hold, the empire now spread through south-eastern Europe. Three reigns, three steps: Osman founds, Orhan organises and crosses, Murad expands. The state Byzantium's lesson watched shrinking now faces the power that will end it in 1453.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three reigns in order — Osman, Orhan, Murad I — with what each one did, and where Gallipoli and the Balkans sit in the story.
