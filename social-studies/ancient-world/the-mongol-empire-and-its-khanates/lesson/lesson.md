## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Ch. 4.2.1 Ögödei Khan consolidates the Great Mongol Empire — Ögödei builds Karakorum's walls, extends the yam and the law, and sends the armies that take Rus' and Hungary; Ch. 4.2.2 The empire under Güyük and Möngke Khans — regencies and succession struggles end with Möngke, whose census, religious tolerance and world-embracing court make the empire a single system; Ch. 4.2.3 The Great Yuan state — Khubilai moves the capital to Khanbaliq (Beijing), takes the Chinese dynastic name Yuan in 1271 and completes the conquest of the Song; Ch. 4.2.4 The Mongols' Golden Horde state — Batu's ulus on the Volga rules the Russian principalities for two centuries — the "Mongol yoke" of Russian history; Ch. 4.2.5 The Mongols' Chagatai state — Chinggis's second son's ulus holds Central Asia's oases and steppe, astride the Silk Road; Ch. 4.2.6 The Mongols' Ilkhanate state — Hülegü takes Baghdad in 1258 and founds the Ilkhanate; his heirs adopt Islam and Persian culture; Ch. 3.4 The Mongol Empire after Chinggis Khan — Ögödei, Güyük, Möngke and Khubilai extend and administer the largest land empire ever — and its succession quarrels begin; Ch. 3.5 The empire's breakup: Yuan, Golden Horde, Chagatai and Ilkhanate — one empire becomes four khanates, each going its own way — Yuan in China, the Horde on the Volga, Chagatai in Central Asia, the Ilkhans in Persia.
