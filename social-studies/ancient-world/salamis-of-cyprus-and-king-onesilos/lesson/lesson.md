# Salamis of Cyprus and King Onesilos

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this question in your mind: salamis was Cyprus's richest city-kingdom — it struck its own coins, buried its kings with horses and chariots, and under Onesilos led the island's revolt against Persia. As you read, test every part of the story against that one thing — who came, what they did, and what changed because of it. Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Salamis, an important city — how coins were minted; burial customs

Salamis grows rich and powerful, strikes its own coins and buries its kings with horses and chariots in royal tombs.

In your mind's eye: the key place or moment of Salamis of Cyprus and King Onesilos as the book shows it. Point to it and say what it shows.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Cypriot revolt against the Persians; the battle and death of Onesilos

Onesilos of Salamis leads Cyprus into the Ionian revolt, dies in battle against the Persians, and the island falls back under Persian rule, his tomb later honoured as a hero's.

## Sub lesson 3 — A panhellenic attempt to liberate Cyprus

Greek fleets and generals campaign to free Cyprus from Persia, winning battles but never holding the island.
