# Lesson plan — Salamis of Cyprus and King Onesilos

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Salamis of Cyprus and King Onesilos
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Cyprus, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Salamis was Cyprus's richest city-kingdom — it struck its own coins, buried its kings with horses and chariots, and under Onesilos led the island's revolt against Persia.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Salamis, an important city — how coins were minted; burial customs** — Salamis grows rich and powerful, strikes its own coins and buries its kings with horses and chariots in royal tombs (Salamis, an important city — how coins were minted; burial customs)
2. **The Cypriot revolt against the Persians; the battle and death of Onesilos** — Onesilos of Salamis leads Cyprus into the Ionian revolt, dies in battle against the Persians, and the island falls back under Persian rule, his tomb later honoured as a hero's (The Cypriot revolt against the Persians; the battle and death of Onesilos)
3. **A panhellenic attempt to liberate Cyprus** — Greek fleets and generals campaign to free Cyprus from Persia, winning battles but never holding the island (A panhellenic attempt to liberate Cyprus)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Answer, in their own words: what happened to Onesilos and to Cyprus after the revolt?
2. Answer, in their own words: did the Greek attempts to free Cyprus succeed?
