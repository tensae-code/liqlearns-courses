# Lesson plan — Ancient civilisations — Mesopotamia

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient civilisations — Mesopotamia
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — The Ancient World (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Mesopotamia** — Greek for the land between the rivers — is where civilisation began, about **3500 BC**, and it was never one single state.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met the ancient civilisations and medieval states named so far in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What makes a civilisation (teacher guide — evidence)** — The river-valley recipe: farming and domestication → permanent settlement → towns and writing; the common elements — organised state, religion, towns, government, writing — and the working glossary.
2. **Between two rivers** — Mesopotamia between the Euphrates and Tigris: Sumerians, Akkadians, Babylonians and Assyrians in turn; royal, religious, absolute government; a society of farmers, priests, soldiers, craftsmen, merchants, employees and slaves.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the river-valley recipe and the common elements of the first civilisations (teacher guide — evidence).
2. Say what Mesopotamia means, name its two rivers, and date its beginning to about 3500 BC.
3. Name the four peoples in turn — Sumerian, Akkadian, Babylonian, Assyrian — and place Sumer, Akkad and Assyria on the map.
4. Describe Mesopotamia's government and society.
