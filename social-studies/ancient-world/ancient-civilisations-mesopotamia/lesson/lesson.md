# Ancient civilisations — Mesopotamia

## Do it first

Name a great river you know. Now ask: what would happen to the people living on its banks if the river gave them water every year, rich soil, and a road for boats — all in one? Keep your answer. This lesson begins where that question was first answered.

## Sub lesson 1 — What makes a civilisation (teacher guide — evidence)

Civilisation began with a recipe, and the recipe starts on a river bank. When people learned **crop cultivation** and **animal domestication**, they no longer had to wander for food: farming produced **permanent settlement**. Settlements grew into **urban centres — towns** — and towns needed records, so **writing** was born. The first civilisations formed on **river banks** because rivers gave three gifts at once: **steady water, fertile land, and transport and communication**. And almost all of them rested on the same **common elements**: a **well-organised state, religious belief, towns, government and a writing system**. Learn the working words too: a **lugal** was the absolute ruler of a Sumerian city-state; a **republic** is ruled by elected representatives; an **empire** is a large land ruled by one power; **democracy** is rule by the citizens; **oligarchy** is rule by a few; a **polis** is a Greek city-state; Rome had **consuls** as its chief officials, **patricians** as its noble class and **plebeians** as its common people. You will meet every one of these words again in this title.

## Sub lesson 2 — Between two rivers

**Mesopotamia** is a Greek word: it means **the land between the rivers** — the **Euphrates** and the **Tigris**. Here, in the **fertile crescent**, civilisation began about **3500 BC**. But hear this clearly: Mesopotamia was **never one single state**. Civilisations rose there **in turn**: the **Sumerians** in the south-east, the **Akkadians** in the centre and north-west — their land was later called **Babylonia** — and the **Assyrians** in the north; so we speak of Sumerian, Akkadian, Babylonian and Assyrian civilisations on the same ground. Government was **royal, religious and absolute** — the king's word was law, and the gods stood behind it. The land lived by **farming and trade**, and its society held **farmers, priests, soldiers, craftsmen, merchants, employees and slaves**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grade 11 depth: Mesopotamia in depth — cities, cuneiform and the law code

Between Tigris and Euphrates the Grade 11 book slows down: Sumerian cities inventing the urban template; cuneiform turning accounts into literature and law; Hammurabi's code publishing justice on stone for all to read; and then the empires in succession — Babylonian and Assyrian — inheriting the valley's cities, scripts and ambitions. First cities and first written laws: the valley's double gift.
