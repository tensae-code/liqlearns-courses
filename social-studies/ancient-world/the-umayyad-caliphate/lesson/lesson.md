# The Umayyad Caliphate

## Do it first

The first caliphs were chosen by the community's leading men. Then the caliphate passes to a family, and the capital moves to Damascus — and an office becomes an inheritance. Before you read on, decide what changes when a chosen ruler is replaced by a born one. Hold your answer; the Umayyads are the book's case.

## Sub lesson 1 — The caliphate becomes a dynasty at Damascus

Turkey's Grade 9 book follows the rise of Islam with the state it became. Under the Umayyads, the caliphate changes its nature: the ruler is no longer raised by choice among the community's leaders, but succeeds by birth within one family — a dynasty, with its capital at Damascus.

The empire the dynasty rules is vast, and Damascus governs it as a hereditary monarchy in all but name. The book's beat is the change itself: rule by election gives way to rule by inheritance — and every later Islamic state inherits the question the Umayyads first posed: is the caliph chosen, or born?
