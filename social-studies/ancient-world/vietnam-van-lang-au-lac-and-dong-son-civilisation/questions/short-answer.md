**15. (Sub lesson 8)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 9.1, the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc civilisation.
**Answer: more than 2,000 years ago three civilisations tied to three ancient states gradually form on Vietnam's territory — the lesson's opening artefacts are the Ngọc Lũ bronze drum (Ninh Bình), the Trà Kiều altar (Đà Nẵng) and an Óc Eo-culture kendi vessel; the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc civilisation (also called the Red River civilisation, the ancient Viet civilisation) is indigenous, its owners the ancient Viet communities, formed from the early 1st millennium BCE to the early Common Era in today's Bắc Bộ and Bắc Trung Bộ; its natural basis is the basins of the Red, Thái Bình, Mã and Cả rivers — fertile land, a dense river system and a tropical monsoon climate favourable to plants and animals and above all to wet-rice farming, with copper, iron, tin and lead deposits feeding metallurgy — though the same monsoon brings drought and flood, and the legend of Sơn Tinh and Thủy Tinh vividly reflects the constant flood threat and the wish to conquer the floods of the Red River delta; its social basis is the Phùng Nguyên culture of about 4,000 years ago, flourishing in the Đông Sơn culture, as over two millennia the development of tools and production dissolves primitive society, differentiates it and brings the state into being — archaeologists keep discovering Đông Sơn bronze traces across Vietnam, refuting the view some scholars held that the culture was imported; the Văn Lang state arises about 2,700 years ago and lasts until 208 BCE with its capital at Phong Châu (Phú Thọ), organised from the Hùng King at the centre (helped by the Lạc hầu) through the Bộ under a Lạc tướng to the Chiềng/chạ under a Bồ chính, and is succeeded by the Âu Lạc state (208–179 BCE), capital Cổ Loa (Hanoi), under King Thục Phán – An Dương Vương; a quoted source (Phan Huy Lê ed., History of Vietnam, vol. I) divides Hùng Vương-era society into three strata — the aristocracy of the ruling apparatus, the slaves at the bottom (commune members fallen poor or into debt, or captured outsiders sold into slavery to serve aristocratic families), and the free people, the rural commune members, the most numerous and the main production force — the villages linking together for irrigation and reclamation and for common defence, acclaiming a common chief, which is a basis for the first state; the economy clears land on hills, foothills and high riverbanks, works swiddens and irrigated rice fields, and in the Đông Sơn age the crafts surge — bronze casting above all, its finely made tools, weapons, drums and jars still partly mysterious in technique, with casting moulds, melting pots and bronze spills found at Làng Cả (Phú Thọ), Làng Vạc and Đông Mơm (Nghệ An) and drum-mould fragments at Luy Lâu (Bắc Ninh), and nearly 300 Đông Sơn bronze drums discovered in Vietnam (drums are also found across Central Vietnam and Southeast Asia — Vietnam is an early and important centre of their emergence and spread); material life runs on rice, vegetables and fish, on skirts and yếm tops for women and loincloths for men, on ornaments of horn, ivory, stone and metal, on stilt houses of wood, bamboo and leaves clustered into hamlets, and on boats and rafts along the waterways; spiritual life worships ancestors and community benefactors, the nature gods and fertility, and its art reaches a high aesthetic level — the drums themselves are musical instruments of festivals and great occasions, decorated with racks of 2–4 drums and 6–8 gongs and scenes of men and women in fine dress and feather headdresses dancing, singing and playing khèn, sênh, bells and clappers; Phan Huy Lê's quoted judgement closes it: the ancient Viet civilisation with the Đông Sơn bronze drum as its symbol is truly a wet-rice agricultural civilisation on the foundation of the village community and an embryonic state structure, reaching a fairly high level of development and laying a firm foundation for the whole later existence and development of the Vietnamese state and nation (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
