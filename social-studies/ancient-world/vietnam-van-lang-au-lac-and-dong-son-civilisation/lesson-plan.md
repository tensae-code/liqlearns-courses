## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 11, achievements of the Red River civilisation** — about 2,700 years ago the first Vietnamese civilisation raises the states of Văn Lang and Âu Lạc, casts the Đông Sơn bronze drums and rings Cổ Loa citadel with three walls under An Dương Vương
2. **Bài 11, the life of the ancient Việt read from the Ngọc Lũ drum** — pictures cast on a bronze drum show stilt houses, boats, rice-pounding and feathered dancers, so children reconstruct a whole way of life from one object
3. **Bài 5, the founding of Văn Lang and Âu Lạc** — around the 7th century BC Văn Lang arises in the northern river plains, known through the Con Rồng cháu Tiên legend and Đông Sơn finds; in 208 BC Thục Phán, having beaten back the Qin, becomes An Dương Vương of Âu Lạc with his capital at Cổ Loa
4. **Bài 5, the economic life of Văn Lang and Âu Lạc** — wet-rice farmers who also cast bronze tools, weapons and jewellery, make pottery, weave and build boats — a farming civilisation read from its ploughshares, axes and drums
5. **Bài 14, the organisation of Văn Lang** — about the 7th century BC, bronze and iron, irrigation and defence call the first Vietnamese state into being: 15 bộ under the Hùng Vương, Lạc hầu and Lạc tướng, villages under Bồ chính — 18 generations, still without writing or written law
6. **Bài 14, Âu Lạc and the Cổ Loa citadel** — Lạc Việt and Âu Việt unite against the Qin and choose Thục Phán, who in about 208 BC rules Âu Lạc from Cổ Loa, a spiral citadel of three rings and 16,000 m of wall, its crossbow triggers still dug up today
7. **Bài 14, the material and spiritual life of Văn Lang – Âu Lạc** — plough farming ("agriculture with the plough"), Ngọc Lũ drums and Đào Thịnh bronze jars, stilt houses; ancestor and nature-god worship, tattoos, blackened teeth, betel, and bánh chưng and bánh giầy at festival time
8. **Bài 9.1, the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc civilisation** — more than 2,000 years ago three civilisations tied to three ancient states gradually form on Vietnam's territory — the lesson's opening artefacts are the Ngọc Lũ bronze drum (Ninh Bình), the Trà Kiều altar (Đà Nẵng) and an Óc Eo-culture kendi vessel; the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc civilisation (also called the Red River civilisation, the ancient Viet civilisation) is indigenous, its owners the ancient Viet communities, formed from the early 1st millennium BCE to the early Common Era in today's Bắc Bộ and Bắc Trung Bộ; its natural basis is the basins of the Red, Thái Bình, Mã and Cả rivers — fertile land, a dense river system and a tropical monsoon climate favourable to plants and animals and above all to wet-rice farming, with copper, iron, tin and lead deposits feeding metallurgy — though the same monsoon brings drought and flood, and the legend of Sơn Tinh and Thủy Tinh vividly reflects the constant flood threat and the wish to conquer the floods of the Red River delta; its social basis is the Phùng Nguyên culture of about 4,000 years ago, flourishing in the Đông Sơn culture, as over two millennia the development of tools and production dissolves primitive society, differentiates it and brings the state into being — archaeologists keep discovering Đông Sơn bronze traces across Vietnam, refuting the view some scholars held that the culture was imported; the Văn Lang state arises about 2,700 years ago and lasts until 208 BCE with its capital at Phong Châu (Phú Thọ), organised from the Hùng King at the centre (helped by the Lạc hầu) through the Bộ under a Lạc tướng to the Chiềng/chạ under a Bồ chính, and is succeeded by the Âu Lạc state (208–179 BCE), capital Cổ Loa (Hanoi), under King Thục Phán – An Dương Vương; a quoted source (Phan Huy Lê ed., History of Vietnam, vol. I) divides Hùng Vương-era society into three strata — the aristocracy of the ruling apparatus, the slaves at the bottom (commune members fallen poor or into debt, or captured outsiders sold into slavery to serve aristocratic families), and the free people, the rural commune members, the most numerous and the main production force — the villages linking together for irrigation and reclamation and for common defence, acclaiming a common chief, which is a basis for the first state; the economy clears land on hills, foothills and high riverbanks, works swiddens and irrigated rice fields, and in the Đông Sơn age the crafts surge — bronze casting above all, its finely made tools, weapons, drums and jars still partly mysterious in technique, with casting moulds, melting pots and bronze spills found at Làng Cả (Phú Thọ), Làng Vạc and Đông Mơm (Nghệ An) and drum-mould fragments at Luy Lâu (Bắc Ninh), and nearly 300 Đông Sơn bronze drums discovered in Vietnam (drums are also found across Central Vietnam and Southeast Asia — Vietnam is an early and important centre of their emergence and spread); material life runs on rice, vegetables and fish, on skirts and yếm tops for women and loincloths for men, on ornaments of horn, ivory, stone and metal, on stilt houses of wood, bamboo and leaves clustered into hamlets, and on boats and rafts along the waterways; spiritual life worships ancestors and community benefactors, the nature gods and fertility, and its art reaches a high aesthetic level — the drums themselves are musical instruments of festivals and great occasions, decorated with racks of 2–4 drums and 6–8 gongs and scenes of men and women in fine dress and feather headdresses dancing, singing and playing khèn, sênh, bells and clappers; Phan Huy Lê's quoted judgement closes it: the ancient Viet civilisation with the Đông Sơn bronze drum as its symbol is truly a wet-rice agricultural civilisation on the foundation of the village community and an embryonic state structure, reaching a fairly high level of development and laying a firm foundation for the whole later existence and development of the Vietnamese state and nation
9. **Bài 3 Vietnam's pre- and proto-historic art: applied art born of making utensils, the horned face carved in Đồng Nội cave (Hòa Bình culture), the Đồng Đậu clay cow of 3,500 years ago, and the Đông Sơn bronze drum — among the most important archaeological artefacts found in Southeast Asia** — before Vietnam had writing it had bronze: the drum is the archive
