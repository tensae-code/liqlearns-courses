# Lesson plan — Pacific voyaging and island exchange

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Pacific voyaging and island exchange
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Nauru, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue, Rotuma, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Futuna and Uvea were once part of one large exchange community — island peoples regularly voyaged between islands circulating wealth, skills and goods, so one sea joined many homes. The Nauru Years 5–6 reader calls this "The Ocean in our blood".
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single picture a child can hold: islands that look separate on a map were joined by voyaging, and what travelled in the boats — wealth, skills and goods — made them one community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **One large exchange community** — Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue, Rotuma, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Futuna and Uvea were once part of one large exchange community.
2. **Voyaging between islands** — island peoples regularly voyaged between islands; the sea joined homes instead of dividing them.
3. **What travelled** — wealth, skills and goods circulated between the islands, so each island shared in what the others had and knew.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Trade routes that linked the old world. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the islands the reader names as once part of one large exchange community.
2. Say how the islands stayed joined — peoples regularly voyaged between them.
3. Say what circulated on those voyages — wealth, skills and goods.
4. Explain, in their own words, why the reader calls this "The Ocean in our blood" — one sea joining many homes.
