# Trade in the medieval world

## Do it first

Medieval kings make war in the chronicles, but between the wars something quieter is stitching the world together: a farmer's surplus, a town market, a guild's workshop, a caravan on a road older than any kingdom. Before you read on, guess which did more to join the medieval world — its wars or its trade. Hold your guess; the caravans will argue their case.

## Sub lesson 1 — Economy from agriculture to trade

Turkey's Grade 9 book builds the medieval economy from the ground up. It starts with agriculture — the farming that feeds everyone — then watches surplus gather into towns, where markets form and crafts organise themselves into guilds that guard each trade's skills and standards.

Then the book lifts its eyes to the great trade routes: caravans crossing the steppe and desert, ships linking the ports, carrying goods between worlds that are otherwise enemies. Its beat reverses the chronicles: caravans and ships stitch the medieval world together richer than its wars divide it.

In your mind's eye: a medieval caravan reaching a walled market town, bales unloaded while guild craftsmen work at open workshops nearby. The student points to the goods and says where they might have come from and who made the town's own goods.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 1 beat: Economy from agriculture to trade. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory: tell the economy's chain in order: farming, surplus, towns and markets, guilds, long trade routes.
2. Write, from memory: say what a guild is: craftsmen of one trade organised to guard its skills and standards.
3. Write, from memory: name what travels the great routes: caravans by land and ships by sea, between worlds otherwise divided.
