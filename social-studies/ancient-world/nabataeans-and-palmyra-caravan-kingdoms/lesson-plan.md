**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two Arab caravan kingdoms grow rich as middlemen of the desert — the Nabataeans carve Petra out of living rock, run their monarchy with the people's shura
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Nabataean and Palmyrene civilisations** — two Arab caravan kingdoms grow rich as middlemen of the desert — the Nabataeans carve Petra out of living rock, run their monarchy with the people's shura, mint coins and work copper and iron until Rome diverts the trade to the Red Sea and seizes them in 106 CE; then Palmyra, neutral between Persia and Rome, takes Petra's place, its merchants speaking Aramaic and Greek and trading to Gaul and the China Sea, until Queen Zenobia's ambition to build an Arab empire brings Aurelian down on the city — she is captured and Palmyra falls in 274 CE
