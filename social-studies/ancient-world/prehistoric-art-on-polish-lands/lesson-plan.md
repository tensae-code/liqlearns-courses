# Lesson plan — Prehistoric art on Polish lands

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Prehistoric art on Polish lands
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Poland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The world story of prehistoric art comes home — the caves, amber-working and early settlements of the Polish lands show the same Palaeolithic and Neolithic turns happening in the learners' own ground.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The story comes home** — After the world's caves and first farms, the book turns to the Polish lands themselves.
2. **Caves and amber** — The caves, amber-working and early settlements of the Polish lands carry the early story.
3. **The same turns, in our own ground** — The Polish lands show the same Palaeolithic and Neolithic turns — hunting art first, then settled farming life — happening in the learners' own ground.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
