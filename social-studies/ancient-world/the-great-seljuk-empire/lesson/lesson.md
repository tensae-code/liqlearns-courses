# The Great Seljuk Empire

## Do it first

A clan of Oghuz Turks rides out of the steppe, defeats an empire at Dandanakan, and within a generation holds the caliph's Baghdad under its protection — then builds the schools and the government machine to keep it. Before you read on, guess which matters more to holding an empire: winning it, or organising it. Hold your guess; Nizam al-Mulk has an answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — From Dandanakan to an empire, 1040–1157

Turkey's Grade 9 book closes with the first great Turkish-Islamic empire. The Seljuks, a clan of Oghuz Turks, defeat the Ghaznavids at Dandanakan in 1040 — and the victory opens an empire: within a generation their sultans rule from Central Asia to the Mediterranean, and Baghdad's caliph stands under Seljuk protection.

The book's beat keeps the scale honest: a clan of Oghuz Turks defeats the Ghaznavids and inherits the caliphate's protection — the steppe's grandchildren now hold Islam's centre.

## Sub lesson 2 — Government and society — sultan, vizier, iqta and the Nizamiyya madrasas

Winning is only half the book's story; the other half is the machine. At its head the sultan; beside him the vizier — and the book names the greatest of them, Nizam al-Mulk, who organises the state. Land is granted as iqta to support soldiers and officials, and across the empire rise the Nizamiyya madrasas, the schools that train its servants and spread its learning.

The beat: Nizam al-Mulk builds the machine — and the schools — of a Turkish-Islamic empire. Organisation, the book teaches, is what a victory needs to become an age.
