# Lesson plan — Medieval world — states and empires

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Medieval world — states and empires
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one chapter holds a dozen kinds of rule, from emperor to feudal lord — and the period's politics are read through its soldiers.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: one chapter holds a dozen kinds of rule, from emperor to feudal lord — and the period's politics are read through its soldiers. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the medieval powers the book surveys: Byzantium, the Sassanids, feudal Europe and the steppe powers.
2. Say what feudal rule means: a weak centre, lords ruling their own lands and men.
3. Say how the emperor, the feudal lord and the steppe khan each raise an army.
4. Explain, in their own words, why the book reads the period's politics through its soldiers.
