# Ancient civilisations — India

## Do it first

Two ruined cities are dug out of the ground: brick houses in straight streets, clay vessels, household things. No king's name anywhere. What can ruins alone tell you about the people who lived there? Hold that question — India answers it.

## Cities on two rivers

India's civilisation began about **2500 BC** in the valleys of the **Indus** and the **Ganges**, founded by the **Dravidian peoples**. Its proof is in the ground: the ruins of **Harappa** and **Mohenjo-daro** — **brick houses, clay vessels and household goods** — show a real, organised, city-building civilisation of great age. Look at the picture: two rivers, two ruined cities on the Indus side, and a whole world told by bricks and pots.

## Invasion, and the first dynasty

About **1500 BC** the civilisation ended, as **climate disasters** struck and **Aryan tribes invaded the north**, driving the Dravidians south and founding small states. Much later, in the **4th century BC**, **Alexander** took India's north-west. Then **Chandragupta Maurya (324–297 BC)** organised the liberation, defeated the Greeks, and in **321 BC** founded **India's first dynasty** — it lasted to **185 AD** — **unifying the north** under one rule.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion India's story in order: the Dravidian beginning on two rivers, the ruined cities that prove it, the ending about 1500 BC, and Chandragupta's dynasty unifying the north.
