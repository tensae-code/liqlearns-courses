**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** India Gupta Delhi Mughal

**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 5, India under the Gupta: a golden age** — Chandragupta I unites the Ganges plain in the early 4th century; iron tools, irrigation and trade enrich the realm, and the Chinese monk Faxian's record praises its lenient laws, charity houses and hospitals — its iron pillar still does not rust
2. **Bài 5, the Delhi Sultanate and Akbar's Mughal empire** — Turkic Muslims found the Delhi Sultanate in 1206 and drive south; two centuries on, the Mughal Akbar (1556–1605) rebuilds India with 15 provinces, surveyed land, fair taxes, new crops and a policy of harmony among peoples and faiths
3. **Bài 5, the culture of feudal India** — Brahmanism ripens into Hinduism, Buddhism divides and travels abroad, Islam arrives with Delhi; Sanskrit is perfected, Kalidasa writes Shakuntala (praised by Goethe), and three faiths raise Khajuraho's temples and the 31 rock caves of Ajanta
