# Lesson plan — The Crusades and the Muslim response

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Crusades and the Muslim response
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Saladin restores Jerusalem at Hattin, and when the Crusaders turn on Egypt herself, al-Kamil floods the Delta and Shajar al-Durr holds the state together to capture Louis IX at al-Mansura.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Egypt in the Ayyubid age: Saladin and the Crusades** — Hattin restores Jerusalem and lifts Muslim hearts; Richard's crusade ends in the peace of 1192 and Saladin dies the next year; his brother al-Adil sees that the Crusaders now aim at Egypt herself — the Fifth Crusade takes Damietta, but al-Kamil floods the Delta plain and the invaders, cut off from their base, evacuate Egypt in 1221 for nothing; a generation later Louis IX lands for the Seventh Crusade, al-Salih Ayyub dies mid-crisis, and his widow Shajar al-Durr hides the death, holds the state together and sees Louis captured at al-Mansura
