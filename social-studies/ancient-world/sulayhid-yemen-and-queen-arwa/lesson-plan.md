## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Sulayhid state (439-532 AH)** — ali bin Muhammad al-Sulayhi, seasoned guarding the Hajj road and preaching the Fatimid call with Cairo's leave, declares himself from Haraz, defeats the Zaydis of Saada, Al Hatim of Sanaa, Al Zari' of Aden and Bani Najah of Zabid and unites Yemen to Mecca's doorstep; his killer Sa'id al-Ahwal captures his widow Asma, his son al-Mukarram storms Sanaa and frees her, moves the capital to Jibla on his wife Sayyida Arwa's advice, and when illness takes him Arwa rules Yemen herself — the first woman to rule Yemen after Islam — until her death ends the state
2. **The development of women's roles: the ruling queen (Unit 3 Lesson 3, fifth part)** — among the models of women's rising roles — Aisha the scholar and teacher, women soldiers in the armies, political guides stronger in counsel than the caliph himself — the book names Yemen's own Sayyida bint Ahmad al-Sulayhi, Queen Arwa, who ruled the Sulayhid state and stands among the most famous kings of Yemen in Islamic history, beside Egypt's Shajar al-Durr
3. **Yemeni states that contributed: the Sulayhid state (Unit 4 Lesson 2)** — the Sulayhids unite Yemen under one leadership for nearly a century, and Queen Arwa rules almost fifty years with justice, building the Jibla mosque and some twenty-two mosques, endowing scholars' and students' salaries, while Sulayhid roads are cut through mountains with pillars, arches and bridges that still stand
