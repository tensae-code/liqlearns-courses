# The Viking Age and the Norse world

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Between 800 and 1050 Norse sailors build ever better ships and open the world — raiding, trading and settling from Russia to America — while at home in Iceland free farmers govern themselves at the Althing at Þingvellir. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Origins and the ships

a seafaring people builds ever better ships, and the ships open the world — the Norse people's origins and the development of their ships (Uppruni, student book pp. 8–13)

## Sub lesson 2 — What a Viking was, 800–1050

not everyone in the age was a Viking — "to go a-viking" was a job, not a people (Víkingaöld, student book pp. 14–15)

## Sub lesson 3 — Raids, voyages and steering without maps

Norse sailors raid, trade and settle from Russia to America, steering by sun, birds and memory (Víkingar herjuðu víðs vegar, student book pp. 16–27)

## Sub lesson 4 — Erik the Red and the seeress

outlawed Erik colonises Greenland, and a seeress is feasted and questioned about the future (Eiríks saga rauða, extracts, ch. 3–4)

## Sub lesson 5 — Free farmers and the Althing

free farmers govern themselves at the Althing, where the allsherjargoði opens the assembly — Norse society, government and religion, and the Althing at Þingvellir (Norræn samfélög, student book pp. 28–37)

## Sub lesson 6 — Inside the turf hall

inside the turf hall, women run the household, weave and keep the keys while men farm and sail — homes and daily life (Heimkynni og daglegt líf, student book pp. 38–46)
