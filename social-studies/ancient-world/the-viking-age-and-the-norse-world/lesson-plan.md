# Lesson plan — The Viking Age and the Norse world

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Viking Age and the Norse world
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Iceland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Between 800 and 1050 Norse sailors build ever better ships and open the world — raiding, trading and settling from Russia to America — while at home in Iceland free farmers govern themselves at the Althing at Þingvellir.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Origins and the ships** — a seafaring people builds ever better ships, and the ships open the world — the Norse people's origins and the development of their ships (Uppruni, student book pp. 8–13)
2. **What a Viking was, 800–1050** — not everyone in the age was a Viking — "to go a-viking" was a job, not a people (Víkingaöld, student book pp. 14–15)
3. **Raids, voyages and steering without maps** — Norse sailors raid, trade and settle from Russia to America, steering by sun, birds and memory (Víkingar herjuðu víðs vegar, student book pp. 16–27)
4. **Erik the Red and the seeress** — outlawed Erik colonises Greenland, and a seeress is feasted and questioned about the future (Eiríks saga rauða, extracts, ch. 3–4)
5. **Free farmers and the Althing** — free farmers govern themselves at the Althing, where the allsherjargoði opens the assembly — Norse society, government and religion, and the Althing at Þingvellir (Norræn samfélög, student book pp. 28–37)
6. **Inside the turf hall** — inside the turf hall, women run the household, weave and keep the keys while men farm and sail — homes and daily life (Heimkynni og daglegt líf, student book pp. 38–46)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
