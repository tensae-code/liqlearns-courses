# Lesson plan — Egypt — Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Egypt — Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Egypt in the Ptolemaic and Roman eras** — Alexander, Aristotle's pupil, is welcomed in 332 BC as a liberator from Persia, kneels to Amun at Siwa and plans Alexandria (331 BC); his general Ptolemy founds a Greek dynasty that lasts three centuries — the saqia in the fields, the Pharos lighthouse, the great Library — until Cleopatra VII falls at Actium in 31 BC; Rome then milks Egypt for wheat and taxes and persecutes its Christians, so Egyptians welcome Amr ibn al-As in 641 as deliverance
2. **Egypt under the rule of the Ptolemies** — Alexander enters in 332 BC, honours Amun, treats Egyptians well and keeps their administration, and founds Alexandria on the village of Raqoda; dying at Babylon in 323 BC he leaves Egypt to Ptolemy son of Lagos, king from 305 BC, whose dynasty fuses Egyptian and Greek into the Hellenistic civilisation — a monopoly economy that repairs canals, spreads the saqia, divides the land into five classes, guards prices, mints coins and makes Alexandria the greatest port of the ancient world under its lighthouse
3. **Egypt under Roman rule, 30 BC – 641 AD** — Rome wants Egypt for her wheat; Hadrian marvels at Alexandria where not even the blind are idle — glass, papyrus and weaving in every hand — and the Romans dig canals and fill the ships; but they also invent taxes the ancient world never heard of, more than two hundred kinds, counted by a census every fourteen years, with unpaid forced labour beside them — until one historian sums Roman Egypt up as a sad story of exploitation ending in collapse
