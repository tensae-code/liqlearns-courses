# Egypt — Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Egypt in the Ptolemaic and Roman eras.
**Answer: Alexander, Aristotle's pupil, is welcomed in 332 BC as a liberator from Persia, kneels to Amun at Siwa and plans Alexandria (331 BC); his general Ptolemy founds a Greek dynasty that lasts three centuries — the saqia in the fields, the Pharos lighthouse, the great Library — until Cleopatra VII falls at Actium in 31 BC; Rome then milks Egypt for wheat and taxes and persecutes its Christians, so Egyptians welcome Amr ibn al-As in 641 as deliverance. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Egypt's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Prim5 T1 "Egypt — the Story of a Homeland and a Civilisation"* (Grade 5 (Fifth Primary), Term 1), Egypt in the Ptolemaic and Roman eras, printed p. 58.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Egypt under the rule of the Ptolemies.
**Answer: Alexander enters in 332 BC, honours Amun, treats Egyptians well and keeps their administration, and founds Alexandria on the village of Raqoda; dying at Babylon in 323 BC he leaves Egypt to Ptolemy son of Lagos, king from 305 BC, whose dynasty fuses Egyptian and Greek into the Hellenistic civilisation — a monopoly economy that repairs canals, spreads the saqia, divides the land into five classes, guards prices, mints coins and makes Alexandria the greatest port of the ancient world under its lighthouse. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Egypt's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Sec10 "Egypt the Civilisation — the Civilisation of Egypt and the Ancient World"* (Grade 10 (First Secondary), full year), Egypt under the rule of the Ptolemies, printed p. 95.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: Egypt under Roman rule, 30 BC – 641 AD.
**Answer: Rome wants Egypt for her wheat; Hadrian marvels at Alexandria where not even the blind are idle — glass, papyrus and weaving in every hand — and the Romans dig canals and fill the ships; but they also invent taxes the ancient world never heard of, more than two hundred kinds, counted by a census every fourteen years, with unpaid forced labour beside them — until one historian sums Roman Egypt up as a sad story of exploitation ending in collapse. (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** In Egypt's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Sec10 "Egypt the Civilisation — the Civilisation of Egypt and the Ancient World"* (Grade 10 (First Secondary), full year), Egypt under Roman rule, 30 BC – 641 AD, printed p. 114.**
