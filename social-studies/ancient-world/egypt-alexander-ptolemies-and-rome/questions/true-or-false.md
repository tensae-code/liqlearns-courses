# Egypt — Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Alexander, Aristotle's pupil, is welcomed in 332 BC as a liberator from Persia, kneels to Amun at Siwa and plans Alexandria (331 BC); his general Ptolemy founds a Greek dynasty that lasts three centuries — the saqia in the fields, the Pharos lighthouse, the great Library — until Cleopatra VII falls at Actium in 31 BC; Rome then milks Egypt for wheat and taxes and persecutes its Christians, so Egyptians welcome Amr ibn al-As in 641 as deliverance.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Egypt in the Ptolemaic and Roman eras

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Prim5 T1 "Egypt — the Story of a Homeland and a Civilisation"* is about Egypt under the rule of the Ptolemies.
**Answer: False** — it is about Egypt in the Ptolemaic and Roman eras

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Alexander enters in 332 BC, honours Amun, treats Egyptians well and keeps their administration, and founds Alexandria on the village of Raqoda; dying at Babylon in 323 BC he leaves Egypt to Ptolemy son of Lagos, king from 305 BC, whose dynasty fuses Egyptian and Greek into the Hellenistic civilisation — a monopoly economy that repairs canals, spreads the saqia, divides the land into five classes, guards prices, mints coins and makes Alexandria the greatest port of the ancient world under its lighthouse.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Egypt under the rule of the Ptolemies

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Sec10 "Egypt the Civilisation — the Civilisation of Egypt and the Ancient World"* is about Egypt under Roman rule, 30 BC – 641 AD.
**Answer: False** — it is about Egypt under the rule of the Ptolemies

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Rome wants Egypt for her wheat; Hadrian marvels at Alexandria where not even the blind are idle — glass, papyrus and weaving in every hand — and the Romans dig canals and fill the ships; but they also invent taxes the ancient world never heard of, more than two hundred kinds, counted by a census every fourteen years, with unpaid forced labour beside them — until one historian sums Roman Egypt up as a sad story of exploitation ending in collapse.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Egypt under Roman rule, 30 BC – 641 AD

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Sec10 "Egypt the Civilisation — the Civilisation of Egypt and the Ancient World"* is about Egypt in the Ptolemaic and Roman eras.
**Answer: False** — it is about Egypt under Roman rule, 30 BC – 641 AD
