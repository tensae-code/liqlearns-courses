# Lesson plan — Ancient civilisations — Rome divides

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient civilisations — Rome divides
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — The Ancient World (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Rome's empire **split to defend itself** — **West (Latin, Rome)** fell in **476 AD**, while the **East (Greek, Constantinople)** endured **another 1,000 years, to 1453**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met the ancient civilisations and medieval states named so far in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain the split: West and East, their languages and capitals, and why the empire divided.
2. Give the two end dates — West 476 AD, East 1453 AD — and who ended each.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
