# Ancient civilisations — Rome divides

## Do it first

One empire, too wide to defend, cuts itself in two. One half falls quickly; the other lives a thousand years more. Before reading: what could make the eastern half so much harder to kill? Keep your guesses — Byzantium's lesson will test them.

## One empire becomes two

The Roman empire **split to defend itself** in two: the **West — Latin-speaking, capital Rome** — and the **East, also called Byzantine — Greek-speaking, capital Constantinople**, the city **founded by Constantine in 330 AD**. In the picture, watch one colour become two: same empire, two fates.

[Picture 11360 — one empire splitting into two halves]

## Two falls, a thousand years apart

In **476 AD, Germanic peoples — Goths and Visigoths — destroyed the Roman state in the West**. But the **East lasted another 1,000 years**, until the **Ottoman Turks took Constantinople in 1453**. So 'the fall of Rome' is two falls: 476 in the West, 1453 in the East — and the second fall closes the medieval world this title is walking into.
