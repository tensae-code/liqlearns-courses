# Ancient civilisations — Sparta, and Athens against Sparta

## Do it first

Two cities, one people, opposite answers to life. One says: every citizen votes. The other says: every boy, from seven years old, belongs to the army. You are seven once — which city would have owned your childhood? Meet Sparta.

## Sparta — the soldier city

**Sparta**, in the **southern Peloponnese**, was **farming-based**, in **three classes: Spartan landowners, free perioikoi, and helot farm slaves**. Its state was an **oligarchy — a landowners' state with no place for democracy** — where **every boy from age seven owed military training**, and the people were **famed as warriors**. Hold it against Athens in the picture: assembly against training ground, vote against spear. Judge it yourself, as the book asks: set the age-seven duty beside your own age.

## Greece's gifts, and Greece's wars

Greece's contributions, as the book's diagram lists them: **sport — the Olympics and the marathon — Hippocratic medicine, building, theatre, literature, mathematics and philosophy**. Then the wars: **Athens beat Persia at Marathon in 490 BC**, yet was ruined; **Sparta won the Athens–Sparta civil war (431–404 BC)**; and in **338 BC Philip of Macedon conquered Greece**, and its freedom ended.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion Sparta's order — classes, oligarchy, the age-seven duty — then Greece's gifts, and the three war dates in order.
