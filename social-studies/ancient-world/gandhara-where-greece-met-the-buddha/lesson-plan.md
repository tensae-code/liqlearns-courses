# Lesson plan — Gandhara — where Greece met the Buddha

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Gandhara — where Greece met the Buddha
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Pakistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In Pakistan's own north-west, Gandhara stands where empires collide — Persians, Alexander, Ashoka and Kanishka pass through, Greek sculptors carve the Buddha's face, and the White Huns burn it down.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Gandhara — where Greece met the Buddha** — In Pakistan's own north-west, around Peshawar, Swat and Taxila, a kingdom stands where empires collide — Persian satraps, Alexander's march of 327–326 BCE, Ashoka turning it Buddhist and raising stupas, Kanishka spreading the faith, and Greek sculptors carving the Buddha's face — until the White Huns burn it down around 500 CE

2. **The Kushans carry Gandhara's Buddhism east** — The Kushans, Central Asian nomads turned kings — Kadphises I, Vima Kadphises with his gold coins, and Kanishka (127–151 CE), whose empire stitches the Silk Road from China to Rome; under them Gandhara's Buddhism travels to China and Sanskrit learning flourishes
