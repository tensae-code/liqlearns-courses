# Lesson plan — The Arabian Peninsula before Islam

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Arabian Peninsula before Islam
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Saudi Arabia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a tribal land with no single state — the lost peoples Tasm and Jadis, wars between tribes and towns, values of courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour, and a rich caravan economy centred on Makkah — the land as the Grade 4 book paints it on the eve of Islam.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: a land of rival tribes and merchant cities grows rich on caravans while waiting for a unity it does not yet have. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A tribal land, no single state** — The lost peoples Tasm and Jadis; a land of tribes and towns with no single state, and wars between tribes and towns.
2. **The values of the tribes** — Courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour — and Khadija bint Khuwaylid directing her caravans from Makkah to Syria and Yemen.
3. **Caravan wealth** — Makkah as the meeting point of caravan routes, the winter and summer journeys, Silk Road goods from China passing to Europe, farming in Yathrib, Taif and Yamama, and herding.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how the land was ruled before Islam — tribes and towns, no single state — and name Tasm and Jadis.
2. Name the social values the book praises — courage, generosity, protecting the neighbour.
3. Say what Khadija bint Khuwaylid did — directed her caravans from Makkah to Syria and Yemen.
4. Explain, in their own words, how Makkah grew rich as the meeting point of caravan routes.
