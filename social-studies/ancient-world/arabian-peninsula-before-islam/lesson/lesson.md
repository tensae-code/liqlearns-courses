# The Arabian Peninsula before Islam

## Do it first

Picture a land with no king, no capital, no single law — only tribes and towns, often at war. Now picture that same land growing rich, its merchants sending caravans to Syria and Yemen and handling goods from as far as China. Can a land be divided and prosperous at once? Before you read on, decide how. Hold your answer; the caravans will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A tribal land, no single state

The Saudi Arabia Grade 4 book's second peninsula lesson (Unit 7, Lesson 26) paints the land's people and their condition before Islam.

Older peoples had already vanished: the book names the lost peoples Tasm and Jadis. The land the lesson describes is a tribal land with no single state — tribes and towns, each its own power, with wars between tribes and towns. There is no king of Arabia, no one law over it.

[Picture slot — a picture of a camel caravan arriving at Makkah at sunset, merchants unloading goods while tents and low houses spread around the town — the student points to the caravan and says where it has come from and where its goods travel next.]

## Sub lesson 2 — The values of the tribes

A divided land still held shared values, and the book names them with pride: courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour.

Its example is a merchant of Makkah: Khadija bint Khuwaylid, directing her caravans from Makkah to Syria and Yemen. In the book's telling she stands for the land's best side — wealth handled with standing and trust, in a land held together not by a state but by honour.

## Sub lesson 3 — Caravan wealth

The land's economic life ran on movement. Makkah was the meeting point of caravan routes — the winter and summer journeys the book names, carrying goods out and bringing goods in. Along the great routes, Silk Road goods from China passed through toward Europe.

And where water allowed, the land fed itself: farming in Yathrib, Taif and Yamama, and herding across the dry country. A land of rival tribes and merchant cities had grown rich on caravans — while waiting for a unity it did not yet have.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: the land was tribal, with no single state — Tasm and Jadis lost, tribes and towns at war; its honoured values were courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour, with Khadija directing her caravans from Makkah; and Makkah grew rich as the meeting point of caravan routes, on the winter and summer journeys. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory, how the land was ruled before Islam, and the names Tasm and Jadis.
2. Write the three values the book praises, and what Khadija directed.
3. Write two sentences explaining how a land with no single state could still grow rich.
