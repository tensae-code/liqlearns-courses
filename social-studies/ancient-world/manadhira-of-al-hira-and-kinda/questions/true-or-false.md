# The Manadhira and Kinda civilisations — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** at al-Hira on the Euphrates, 'Amr ibn Adi founds the Lakhmid emirate that serves Persia yet becomes a court of Arab poetry — al-Nabigha and the Mu'allaqat poets recite there — and a bridge carrying Persian culture to the Arabs, its Syriac-based script a root of Arabic writing; Persia's king, fearing al-Nu'man III's drive for Arab unity, lures and kills him, and the Arabs answer united at Dhu Qar in 610 CE; inland, Kinda — its capital Qaryat al-Faw a market city with its own coinage, writing in Yemen's Musnad script — tries under Hujr and al-Harith to unite the whole Peninsula and perishes in the attempt, leaving the poet Imru' al-Qays to avenge his father.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The Manadhira and Kinda civilisations
