# Cambodia — the rise of Angkor — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Jayavarman II (802–850), back from captivity in Java, is proclaimed universal monarch on Phnom Kulen in 802 and unites the two Chenlas; Yasovarman I founds the city of Angkor in the 9th century, and temple-building kings raise Angkor Wat over a land watered by great reservoirs.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Ch. 4 Lesson 5, The Angkor era

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *សិក្សាសង្គម ថ្នាក់ទី៥ (Social Studies, Grade 5)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Ch. 4 Lesson 5, The Angkor era

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** the empire runs 802–1432 through six centuries of temple kings: Jayavarman II (802–850), Indravarman I (877–889; Bakong and a 3,000 × 8,000 m reservoir), Yasovarman I (889–900; Bakheng and the great eastern baray).
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in History Ch. 2 Lesson 1, The Angkor Empire

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *សិក្សាសង្គម ថ្នាក់ទី៨ (Social Studies, Grade 8)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about History Ch. 2 Lesson 1, The Angkor Empire

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** the reign-by-reign account runs to Indravarman II (944–968), Jayavarman V (968–1001), then Suryavarman I (1010–1050) and Udayadityavarman II (1050–1066), builder of the Baphuon and the 8 × 2.2 km western reservoir.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in History Ch. 2 Lesson 4, The kings of Angkor

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *សិក្សាសង្គម ថ្នាក់ទី១០ (Social Studies, Grade 10)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about History Ch. 2 Lesson 4, The kings of Angkor
