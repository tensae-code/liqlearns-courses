# Lesson plan — Cambodia — the rise of Angkor

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Cambodia — the rise of Angkor
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Cambodia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Jayavarman II (802–850), back from captivity in Java, is proclaimed universal monarch on Phnom Kulen in 802 and unites the two Chenlas; Yasovarman I founds the city of Angkor in the 9th century, and temple-building kings raise Angkor Wat over a land watered by great reservoirs
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ch. 4 Lesson 5, The Angkor era** — Jayavarman II (802–850), back from captivity in Java, is proclaimed universal monarch on Phnom Kulen in 802 and unites the two Chenlas; Yasovarman I founds the city of Angkor in the 9th century, and temple-building kings raise Angkor Wat over a land watered by great reservoirs.
2. **History Ch. 2 Lesson 1, The Angkor Empire** — the empire runs 802–1432 through six centuries of temple kings: Jayavarman II (802–850), Indravarman I (877–889; Bakong and a 3,000 × 8,000 m reservoir), Yasovarman I (889–900; Bakheng and the great eastern baray).
3. **History Ch. 2 Lesson 4, The kings of Angkor** — the reign-by-reign account runs to Indravarman II (944–968), Jayavarman V (968–1001), then Suryavarman I (1010–1050) and Udayadityavarman II (1050–1066), builder of the Baphuon and the 8 × 2.2 km western reservoir.
