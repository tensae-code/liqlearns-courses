# Lesson plan — The rise and expansion of Islam

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The rise and expansion of Islam
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — the medieval world thread; the Grade 11 book's Islam lesson, with the Grade 9 guide's four channels stocked as labelled evidence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Islam was born in Arabia under the Prophet Muhammad, united Arabia under the caliphates, and expanded with extraordinary speed across the Middle East, North Africa and Spain — spreading, as the Grade 9 guide names it, through four channels: conquest, trade, pilgrimage and missionaries — and building a civilisation of scholarship and trade.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the medieval-world lessons — Byzantium and the fall of the West are the world Islam expanded into. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Birth in Arabia; the caliphates; the great expansion** — Islam under Muhammad in Arabia; the caliphates' leadership; expansion across the Middle East, North Africa and Spain, with Islamic civilisation's scholarship and trade.
2. **How Islam spread — four channels (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 9 guide's activity answer: Islam spread through military conquest, trade, pilgrimage and missionaries.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the medieval-world lessons in this title (Byzantium; feudal Europe). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell Islam's birth: Arabia, Muhammad, the caliphates.
2. Map the expansion to c.750: Middle East, North Africa, Spain.
3. Recite the guide's four channels of spread.
4. Name two gifts of Islamic civilisation the book teaches: scholarship and trade.
