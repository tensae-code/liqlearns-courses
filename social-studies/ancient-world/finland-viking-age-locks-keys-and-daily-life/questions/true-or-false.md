# Finland — Viking Age locks, keys and daily life — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** For the first time valuables are kept behind locks, and an iron key is needed to open them.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Esine 4: Rauta-avain (iron key, Viking Age c. 800–1050)

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Esihistorian mysteeriesineet (student, FI/SV)* is about Sääty-yhteiskunta 1800-luvun Suomessa (the estate society: into what groups were people divided, and how did each group live?).
**Answer: False** — it is about Esine 4: Rauta-avain (iron key, Viking Age c. 800–1050)

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Padlocks with loops for chains guard Viking Age stores, security becoming a thing you carry.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Esine 6: Lukkoja ja avaimia (Iron Age / Viking Age locks and a padlock from Hämeenlinna, c. 800–1050)

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Esihistorian mysteeriesineet (student, FI/SV)* is about Etu- ja sukunimet 1800-luvun Suomessa (first names and surnames; did everyone even have a surname?).
**Answer: False** — it is about Esine 6: Lukkoja ja avaimia (Iron Age / Viking Age locks and a padlock from Hämeenlinna, c. 800–1050)

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** A 59 mm, 7-gram spoon for cleaning ears shows Iron Age daily hygiene, its wax later prized in folk medicine.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Esine 5: Korvalusikka (ear spoon, Viking Age c. 800–1050)

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Esihistorian mysteeriesineet (student, FI/SV)* is about Ryhmäjako (the seven groups: aateliset nobles, papit clergy, porvarit burghers, talonpojat peasants, torpparit crofters, piika ja renki maid and farmhand, loiset the landless lodgers).
**Answer: False** — it is about Esine 5: Korvalusikka (ear spoon, Viking Age c. 800–1050)
