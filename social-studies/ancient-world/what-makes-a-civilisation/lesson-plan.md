# Lesson plan — What Makes A Civilisation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** What Makes A Civilisation
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** civilisation is the material and spiritual progress of human society — the high state of development of a culture, when human society has passed beyond the age of savagery; its basic marks are a state, cities, writing, and advances in social organisation, ethics and the techniques that improve human life, while culture — per the UNESCO declaration adopted at Mexico in August 1982, quoted here — is the whole of the spiritual and material, intellectual and emotional features of a society or group, and culture is as old as humankind while civilisation is created only at society's high stage (Engels' three ages — savagery, barbarism, civilisation — are boxed); the first civilisations form at the end of the 4th millennium BCE in Northeast Africa and West Asia, and the book's chronology table sets the four great Eastern centres side by side: ancient Egypt about 3200–30 BCE, ancient Mesopotamia from the late 4th millennium to the mid-1st millennium BCE, ancient-to-medieval India from the mid-3rd millennium BCE to 1857, and China from about the 21st century BCE to 1911
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
