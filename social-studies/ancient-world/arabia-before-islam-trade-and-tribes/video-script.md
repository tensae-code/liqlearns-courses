# Video script — Arabia before Islam — trade and tribes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Palestine's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

In the Jahiliyya Arabs worship stones, trees, sun and moon beside the Kaaba's idols; the family and then the tribe rule life, women range from Queen Zenobia to daughters buried alive (wa'd), and Bedouin tent-dwellers live hard against settled townsfolk of Yemen, al-Sham, Iraq and the Hijaz.

Hereditary kingdoms (Ma'in, Saba and Himyar in Yemen, the Munadhira in Iraq, Nabataeans, Ghassanids and Palmyra in al-Sham) stand beside tribal sheikhdoms, while Persia and Rome occupy most Arab lands and the Hijaz cities — Mecca under Qusay's Quraysh, feuding Yathrib, Taif — keep their independence.

Abraha's attempt on Mecca in the Year of the Elephant fails, and around 610 CE the Arab tribes unite and beat Persia at Dhi Qar after the Persians kill al-Nu'man, king of al-Hira — the first Arab victory over an empire.

Seasonal fairs like Ukaz east of Taif mix trade with poetry contests and tribal gathering before the Hajj, so the market is Arabia's parliament and newspaper in one.
