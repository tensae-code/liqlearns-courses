# Medieval world — the Dark Age and feudalism

## Do it first

Your town's protectors vanish in a year. Gangs roam. A strong neighbour with armed men offers: give me your farm's deed, work it as my tenant, pay me tribute — and my spears guard your family. Do you sign? Europe signed, everywhere. That signature is feudalism.

## The Dark Age — fall, with bright spots

The medieval first age: **Greek and Roman civilisation fallen, cities decayed, population shrunk** — from **internal division and misuse of power** within, and **external invasion (476)** without. Yet it was not all dark: its **bright spots** were **popes and monks copying ancient writings** for later generations, **churches and palaces still built**, and **knights keeping law and order**. The copied books matter most: without the monks' pens, Greece and Rome would be silence.

## Feudalism — land for protection

**Germanic rulers made laws, taxed, and normalised buying and selling land** — so **land gathered into few hands**. Then the bargain of the age: **people who could not defend themselves gave their land to lords and paid tribute for protection**. That is **feudalism**: **great estates, few landowners, and landless tenants** working land that was once theirs. See it in the picture — the deed passing from tenant to lord before the castle.
