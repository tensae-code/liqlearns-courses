# Lesson plan — Alexander Meets The Scythians

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Alexander Meets The Scythians
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Alexander chases steppe horsemen who refuse pitched battle and slip away unconquered
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§24 Why Alexander could not conquer the Scythians** — Alexander chases steppe horsemen who refuse pitched battle and slip away unconquered
2. **3.8 The Sakas' struggle against Alexander the Great's army** — Saka horsemen harry Alexander on the Jaxartes and he cannot hold the steppe
3. **§42 Alexander the Great's campaign against the Sakas** — Alexander wins a river battle yet cannot conquer the free steppe tribes
4. **3.8 The Sakas' struggle against Alexander's army (lesson plans)** — the Jaxartes fighting is taught from both conqueror's and defenders' sides
5. **§24 Why Alexander could not conquer the Scythians (lesson plan)** — the steppe campaign is taught as the limit of Alexander's power

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
