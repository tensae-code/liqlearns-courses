# Lesson plan — Greek Myth The Trojan War

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Greek Myth The Trojan War
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Greece, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Eris throws the apple "for the fairest", Paris judges for Aphrodite, and his prize — Helen — takes him to Sparta and Menelaus's palace
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 5, ch. 1 The apple of Discord** — Eris throws the apple "for the fairest", Paris judges for Aphrodite, and his prize — Helen — takes him to Sparta and Menelaus's palace
2. **Unit 5, ch. 2 The sacrifice of Iphigenia** — the Achaean fleet is wind-bound at Aulis, the seer Calchas demands Agamemnon's daughter Iphigenia, and the fleet finally sails for Troy
3. **Unit 5, ch. 3 The Achaeans reach Troy** — the kings and heroes of all Greece land before Priam's walled city and settle into a siege that will last ten years
4. **Unit 5, ch. 4 The wrath of Achilles** — dishonoured by Agamemnon, Achilles withdraws from battle with his Myrmidons, and the Achaeans begin to lose
5. **Unit 5, ch. 5 The death of Patroclus** — Patroclus borrows Achilles's armour to save the ships, chases the Trojans too far, and is killed by Hector at the walls
6. **Unit 5, ch. 6 Achilles kills Hector** — grief turns Achilles's rage on Hector; he kills him, drags the body round Patroclus's tomb, and old Priam comes by night to ransom his son
7. **Unit 5, ch. 7 The end of Achilles** — Achilles falls to Paris's arrow in his one vulnerable heel — the turn that leaves the Achaeans despairing of taking Troy by force
8. **Unit 5, ch. 8 The Wooden Horse and the destruction of Troy** — Odysseus's wooden horse smuggles warriors inside the celebrating city, Laocoon's warning is silenced by serpents, and Troy burns in one night
9. **Unit 5, ch. 1 Apple of Discord exercises** — matching work rebuilds the chain from Eris's apple to Paris's judgement
10. **Unit 5, ch. 2 Sacrifice of Iphigenia exercises** — pupils table the Achaean kings and their cities gathering at Aulis
11. **Unit 5, ch. 3 Achaeans reach Troy exercises** — map completion traces each contingent's route from home port to Troy
12. **Unit 5, ch. 4 Wrath of Achilles exercises** — exercises probe why Achilles's anger, not Trojan strength, nearly loses the war
13. **Unit 5, ch. 5 Death of Patroclus exercises** — pupils trace who begged Achilles to return and how Patroclus went out in his armour instead
14. **Unit 5, ch. 6 Achilles kills Hector exercises** — reading work on the duel and Priam's night visit to ransom Hector
15. **Unit 5, ch. 7 End of Achilles exercises** — pupils unpack the living phrase "Achilles heel" from the hero's death — myth surviving in today's language
16. **Unit 5, ch. 8 Wooden Horse exercises** — exercises retell the horse trick and the one night that ended ten years of siege
17. **Revision lesson for Units 5, 6: From myth to reality** — gap-fill on Priam's Troy and its high walls turns the war story back toward the real city behind it
