# Lesson plan — The Islamic Golden Age — a civilisation of science

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Islamic Golden Age — a civilisation of science
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 5, Topic 5.5, The Civilisation of Science (Bilim Medeniyeti): the scientific flowering of the Islamic world, 8th–12th centuries.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: translation, algebra, medicine and observatories make Baghdad and Cordoba the world's classrooms. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the age's span: the 8th to the 12th centuries.
2. Name the two cities the book calls the world's classrooms: Baghdad and Cordoba.
3. Name the four works: translation, algebra, medicine and observatories.
4. Explain, in their own words, why the book calls it a civilisation of science (Bilim Medeniyeti).
