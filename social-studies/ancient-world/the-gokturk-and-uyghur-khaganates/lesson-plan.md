# Lesson plan — The Göktürk and Uyghur khaganates

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Göktürk and Uyghur khaganates
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 4, Topic 4.3, From Clans to State: the Göktürk and Uyghur khaganates; the ruler's kut — power granted by Gök Tanrı (Heaven) — as Mete Han's letter to the Chinese emperor and Bilge Kağan's Orhun words declare.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: the steppe clans become an empire that writes its own story in stone, in its own alphabet. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the two khaganates in order: the Göktürks, then the Uyghurs.
2. Say what kut is: the ruler's power granted by Gök Tanrı (Heaven).
3. Name the book's two proofs: Mete Han's letter to the Chinese emperor, and Bilge Kağan's Orhun inscriptions.
4. Explain, in their own words, why carving the story in stone in their own alphabet matters.
