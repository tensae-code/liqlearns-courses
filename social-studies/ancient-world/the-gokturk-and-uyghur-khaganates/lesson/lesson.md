# The Göktürk and Uyghur khaganates

## Do it first

A steppe clan becomes an empire — and does something no steppe people had done: it writes its own story, in its own alphabet, on standing stones meant to outlast empires. Before you read on, guess what a ruler would carve where everyone must pass. Hold your guess; Bilge Kağan's stones are still speaking.

## Sub lesson 1 — From clans to state — kut, Mete Han's letter and the Orhun stones

Turkey's Grade 9 book follows the steppe clans as they become states: the Göktürk khaganate, and after it the Uyghur khaganate. At the centre of their rule stands one idea — kut, the ruler's sacred fortune: power granted by Gök Tanrı, Heaven, and lost when Heaven withdraws it.

The book proves the idea from the Turks' own voices. Mete Han's letter to the Chinese emperor speaks as a ruler Heaven has raised, not a chief begging leave. And Bilge Kağan's words, carved on the Orhun stones in the Turks' own alphabet, warn his people from the stone itself. The beat: the steppe clans become an empire that writes its own story in stone, in its own alphabet.

In your mind's eye: the Orhun standing stones on the open steppe, carved rows of the old Turkish runic alphabet, a khagan's camp in the distance. The student points to the carved rows and says whose words they carry.
