# Lesson plan — Ibn Battuta — traveller of the Islamic world

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ibn Battuta — traveller of the Islamic world
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (United Arab Emirates, 2026-11-15). This is the History telling of Ibn Battuta; a short First Steps telling (first-steps/ibn-battuta-traveller-and-explorer, from the Geography expansion) already introduces him to beginning readers by tracing his journeys on a map.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ibn Battuta — born in Morocco, his real name Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Muhammad al-Tanji — set out for the Hajj and did not stop travelling for twenty-seven years, crossing more than forty countries on camels and horses and in sailing ships, passing through Algeria, Egypt and Bahrain to India, writing down the customs and traditions of the peoples he saw; his book of travels earned him the title Prince of the Muslim Travellers, and his old description of China as one of the safest countries to travel in is still remembered — as is his name, carried today by Dubai's great Ibn Battuta shopping centre.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single journey a child can follow: a young man leaves home for the pilgrimage and does not stop for twenty-seven years, and his book becomes the map of a whole medieval world. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A young man from Morocco** — Ibn Battuta introduces himself: born in Morocco, his real name Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Muhammad al-Tanji, and his journeys began by setting out for the Hajj.
2. **Twenty-seven years on the road** — he crossed the lands on camels and horses and in sailing ships, visited more than forty countries, passed through Algeria, Egypt and Bahrain to India, and wrote down the customs and traditions of the peoples he saw.
3. **The book and the title** — his achievements close the story: twenty-seven years of travel, the book of his travels, the title Prince of the Muslim Travellers, his old description of China as one of the safest countries to travel in, and Dubai's great Ibn Battuta shopping centre named after him.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The rise and expansion of Islam. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say who Ibn Battuta was — where he was born, and his real name as the book gives it.
2. Say how his journeys began — by setting out for the Hajj — and how long he travelled: twenty-seven years.
3. Say how he travelled and how far: on camels and horses and in sailing ships, to more than forty countries, through Algeria, Egypt and Bahrain to India.
4. Say what he wrote down — the customs and traditions of the peoples he saw — and what his book of travels is.
5. Say what title he earned — Prince of the Muslim Travellers — what he said of China, and what place in Dubai carries his name today.
