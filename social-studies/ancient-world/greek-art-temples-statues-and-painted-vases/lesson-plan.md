# Lesson plan — Greek art — temples, statues and painted vases

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Greek art — temples, statues and painted vases
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Poland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Greeks hunt for perfect proportion — temples tuned like music, statues that step out of stiffness into living balance, and black-and-red vases that carry the myths and the games into every Greek home.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The hunt for perfect proportion** — Greek aesthetics is a hunt for perfect proportion in everything made.
2. **Temples and statues come alive** — Temples are tuned like music, and statues step out of stiffness into living balance.
3. **Vases carry the story home** — Black-and-red vases carry the myths and the games into every Greek home.
