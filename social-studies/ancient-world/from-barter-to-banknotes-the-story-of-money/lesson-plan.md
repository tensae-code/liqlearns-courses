# Lesson plan — From barter to banknotes — the story of money

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** From barter to banknotes — the story of money
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Pakistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Money has a biography — barter, metal coins, paper notes and plastic cards — and on home ground Sher Shah Suri's silver rupiya still gives the rupee its name.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The evolution of money** — Money has a biography — first barter, swapping goods for goods; then metal coins, struck in China around 1000 BCE and in Lydia around 500 BCE, with leather notes in China by 118 BCE; then paper money in China around 806 CE, spreading until notes, and finally plastic cards, carry the world's trade

2. **Money in the Subcontinent** — The same story on home ground — punch-marked coins in the ancient kingdoms, new coinages after the Arab arrival of 712, Sher Shah Suri's silver rupiya that still gives the rupee its name, British coins stamped with the monarch's face after 1857, and in 1948 a new state's own State Bank
