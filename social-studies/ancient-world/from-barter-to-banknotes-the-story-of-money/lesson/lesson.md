# From barter to banknotes — the story of money

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Money has a biography — barter, metal coins, paper notes and plastic cards — and on home ground Sher Shah Suri's silver rupiya still gives the rupee its name. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The evolution of money

Money has a biography — first barter, swapping goods for goods; then metal coins, struck in China around 1000 BCE and in Lydia around 500 BCE, with leather notes in China by 118 BCE; then paper money in China around 806 CE, spreading until notes, and finally plastic cards, carry the world's trade.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Money in the Subcontinent

The same story on home ground — punch-marked coins in the ancient kingdoms, new coinages after the Arab arrival of 712, Sher Shah Suri's silver rupiya that still gives the rupee its name, British coins stamped with the monarch's face after 1857, and in 1948 a new state's own State Bank.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion
