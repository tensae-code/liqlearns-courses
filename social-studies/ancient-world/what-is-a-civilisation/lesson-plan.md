# Lesson plan — What is a civilisation?

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** What is a civilisation?
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A civilisation rises where rivers force cooperation, roads meet, minerals wait in the hills and people struggle with their environment — and Egypt is the oldest model of them all.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Factors for the rise of civilisations** — Rivers that force cooperation, meeting-places of continents, minerals in the hills, safety from invasion, and man's struggle with his environment — with Egypt, owner of the oldest of the ancient world's civilisations, taken as the model, and Jamal Hamdan's "genius of place" as its motto
