# Cyprus Bronze Age — copper and the first cities

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this question in your mind: copper made Cyprus rich and wanted: the island's metal drew ships from Egypt and the Levant, and farmers' villages grew into the first Cypriot cities. As you read, test every part of the story against that one thing — who came, what they did, and what changed because of it. Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — A new material changes people's lives

Copper and bronze reach Cyprus, and the island's copper becomes the treasure outsiders sail for.

In your mind's eye: the key place or moment of Cyprus Bronze Age — copper and the first cities as the book shows it: point to it and say what it shows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Daily life, food and dress in the Bronze Age

Richer houses, new foods and finer clothes mark a world of farmers, smiths and traders.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why and how Bronze Age people moved, and where they built cities

Ships and trade routes tie Cyprus to Egypt and the Levant, and coastal cities rise where the boats land.

## Sub lesson 4 — What Bronze Age people thought and how they expressed it

Gods, sanctuaries, figurines and early signs show belief and communication growing more elaborate.

## Sub lesson 5 — How Bronze Age people organised themselves; relations between groups

Cities need rulers and laws, and Cyprus's kingdoms deal with great powers abroad as partners and prizes.
