# Lesson plan — Afghanistan between empires — Achaemenids, Alexander and Mauryas

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Afghanistan between empires — Achaemenids, Alexander and Mauryas
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Afghanistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** with no central state after the 6th century BCE, the land is dealt among Darius and Xerxes' Persia, Alexander of Macedon and Chandragupta's Mauryas
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lesson 10: Ancient Afghanistan and its relations with foreigners (Achaemenids, Greeks and Mauryans)** — with no central state after the 6th century BCE, the land is dealt among Darius and Xerxes' Persia, Alexander of Macedon and Chandragupta's Mauryas
2. **Lesson 7: Ancient Afghanistan and the Achaemenids** — the land enters the Persian empire of Darius and Xerxes as its eastern satrapies
3. **Lesson 8: Ancient Afghanistan and the Greeks** — Alexander's conquest and the Greek presence remake the land's cities and coinage
4. **Lesson 9: Ancient Afghanistan and the Mauryans** — Chandragupta's Mauryan empire takes the eastern provinces, the third great foreign power of the age
