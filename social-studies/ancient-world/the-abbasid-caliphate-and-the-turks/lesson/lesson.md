# The Abbasid Caliphate and the Turks

## Do it first

Baghdad's caliphs build the richest court of their age — and fill their guard and their armies with Turkish soldiers from the steppe. Before you read on, guess what happens to hired swords inside an empire over two hundred years. Hold your guess; the book's answer shapes the next thousand years.

## Sub lesson 1 — Baghdad's caliphate leans on Turkish soldiers

Turkey's Grade 9 book turns from the Umayyads to their successors, the Abbasids, whose capital Baghdad becomes the centre of a great Islamic civilisation. And inside that state, the book watches one group rise: the Turks.

The caliphs draw Turkish soldiers from the steppe into their service — guards and troops valued for the very skills the grassland taught them. Soldier by soldier, commander by commander, Turks rise inside the Abbasid state. The book's beat is pointed: the empire that will convert the Turks first hires them — the meeting of steppe and Islam begins not with a battle, but with a payroll.

In your mind's eye: baghdad in its glory — the round city's walls and markets, Turkish guards in the caliph's service at the palace gate. The student points to the guards and says where they came from and what they are doing in Baghdad.
