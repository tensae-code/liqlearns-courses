# Video script — Bengal's textiles, boats and ports

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bangladesh book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bengal had two great industries, says the Grade 7 book. The first was cloth — fine textiles praised by the Arab merchant Sulaiman in the 9th century, and coveted by Europeans into the 1600s.

The second was boats, an industry forged by Bengal's rivers.

The looms made what the boats carried: together they clothe and carry a trading people.

How did Bengal trade? The Grade 7 book starts with money: exchange grew from barter to coins — punch-marked coins, and Gupta, Gauda and Harikela coins.

Then the doors to the sea: Tamralipti, Chandraketugarh and Samandar.

Through them, 2,000 years ago, Bengal traded with the Mediterranean, Arabia, Africa and Southeast Asia.
