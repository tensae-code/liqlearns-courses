# Bengal's textiles, boats and ports

## Do it first

Two thousand years ago, ships loaded cloth in Bengal's ports that buyers across the sea fought to own. Before you read on, guess what Bengal made better than anywhere else. Hold your guess; the looms and the boats are coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — Industry — fine textiles and river-forged boats

Bangladesh's Grade 7 book gives ancient and medieval Bengal two great industries. The first is cloth: Bengal's fine textiles were famed far beyond its rivers — praised by the 9th-century Arab merchant Sulaiman, and still coveted by Europeans into the 1600s. The second is boats: a river land needs vessels, and Bengal's boatbuilding industry, forged by its rivers, built them. Together the two industries clothe and carry a trading people: the looms made what the boats carried.

In your mind's eye: a Bengal river port 2,000 years ago — weavers' fine cloth being loaded onto wooden boats at a ghat, sacks and coin pouches changing hands. The student points to the cloth and the boats and says what Bengal sold and what carried it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Trade, commerce and money — coins and ports

Trade needs money and doors to the sea, and the Grade 7 book gives Bengal both. Exchange grew from barter to coins — punch-marked coins, and the coins of Gupta, Gauda and Harikela times. And through the ports of Tamralipti, Chandraketugarh and Samandar, Bengal traded 2,000 years ago with the Mediterranean, Arabia, Africa and Southeast Asia. Coins in the hand and ports on the coast: that is how a river land became a trading land known across half the world.
