# Lesson plan — China's dynastic cycle

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** China's dynastic cycle
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Unit 4 — The Middle Ages and Early Modern World, c. 500 to 1750s (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Imperial China was an **autocracy** — one emperor ruling — but it was administered day to day by **highly educated Confucian scholar-officials**: men who had mastered the classics and governed by their teaching of ranked duty and order.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner having met ancient China — Shang, Zhou and Confucius — in this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Mandate is a physical object → it is heaven's approval — an idea, read from events like floods and rebellions.
- Confuses cycle with the earlier China lesson's dynasties → Shang and Zhou are single turns of this wheel; the cycle is the pattern that contains them all.
