# China's dynastic cycle

## Do it first

A dynasty rises, strong and bright. Generations pass. It ages, weakens, falls — and a new dynasty takes its place, certain its turn will be different. Chinese historians watched this wheel turn for two thousand years and gave it a name, and a judge above it: the Mandate of Heaven.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rule by scholar-officials, judged from heaven

Imperial China was an **autocracy** — one emperor ruling — but it was administered day to day by **highly educated Confucian scholar-officials**: men who had mastered the classics and governed by their teaching of ranked duty and order.

[Picture 22507 — a wheel diagram of a dynasty rising, ageing and falling]

Above the emperor stood an idea: the **Mandate of Heaven**. Rule lasts **while heaven's mandate is kept** — while the dynasty governs well enough to deserve it. Floods, rebellions and decay were read as signs the mandate was slipping away. No emperor ruled by his own say-so alone; heaven was watching, and the people knew it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The wheel turns — rise, decay, replacement

The second idea is the **dynastic cycle**: dynasties **rise, decay and are replaced**. A founder wins power and restores order; his house flourishes; over generations it weakens — corruption, tax burdens, disasters — until it falls, and the founder of the next dynasty takes the Mandate and the wheel begins again. The teacher's guide for this course sets the sharp question pupils must judge: did the dynastic cycle and the Mandate of Heaven **encourage rebellion** in China? Think it through — if a failing dynasty has visibly lost heaven's mandate, what has a rebel actually done wrong? The idea that explained order could also license its overthrow.

## Say it to your companion

Tell the whole story of China's dynastic cycle back to your companion, part by part, in order — names, dates and places included. Your companion listens with the lesson closed and stops you at any name or date they cannot place; open the lesson together and repair that part.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the dynastic wheel with four stations — rise, good rule, ageing, fall — and write one sign of each station.
2. Write a short 'heaven's report' on an imaginary dynasty in its third generation: two signs the Mandate is kept, two that it is slipping.
3. Answer the guide's question in five sentences: did the cycle and the Mandate encourage rebellion in China? Use the Mandate's own logic.
