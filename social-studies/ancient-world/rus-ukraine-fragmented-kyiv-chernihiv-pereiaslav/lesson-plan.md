# Lesson plan — Rus-Ukraine fragmented — Kyiv, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Rus-Ukraine fragmented — Kyiv, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav
**Place in the curriculum:** Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Ukraine, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** after Mstyslav the unity is gone for good — Kyiv is fought over and even sacked by a Rus prince (Andrei Bogoliubsky, 1169), while Chernihiv and Pereiaslav go their own ways on the steppe frontier.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kyiv fought over** — After Mstyslav the unity is gone for good. Kyiv is fought over — and in 1169 even sacked by a Rus prince, Andrei Bogoliubsky: Rus princes now make war on Rus's own capital.
2. **Chernihiv and Pereiaslav go their own ways** — Chernihiv and Pereiaslav go their own ways on the steppe frontier. The one state is now separate principalities, each facing the steppe alone.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Volodymyr Monomakh holds Rus together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what happened to Kyiv in this age: fought over, and sacked in 1169 by the Rus prince Andrei Bogoliubsky.
2. Name the three principalities this lesson follows — Kyiv, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav — and say they went their own ways on the steppe frontier.
