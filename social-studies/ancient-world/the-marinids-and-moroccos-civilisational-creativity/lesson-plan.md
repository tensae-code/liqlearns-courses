# Lesson plan — The Marinids and Morocco's civilisational creativity

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Marinids and Morocco's civilisational creativity
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Morocco, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History Lesson 10: the Marinid state — civilisational creativity — Zenata horsemen become sultans who fill Fez with colleges so beautiful the dynasty is remembered in tile and cedar.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Morocco's Grade 5 national story, as the Al-Moufid teacher guide teaches it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Founding and stages of the Marinid state** — The guide teaches the Marinid state's founding and stages: Zenata horsemen become sultans of Morocco.
2. **Madrasas and the Mawlid — creativity still alive** — Marinid creativity is studied through madrasa architecture — colleges in Fez so beautiful the dynasty is remembered in tile and cedar — and through a Marinid tradition still alive: the celebration of the Prophet's Birthday (Mawlid).
