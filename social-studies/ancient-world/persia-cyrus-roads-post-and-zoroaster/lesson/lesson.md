# Persia — Cyrus, roads, post and Zoroaster

## Sub lesson 1 — Cyrus founds the largest empire of the ancient world

Begin on a high plateau, in the land we now call Iran and Afghanistan. Its people spoke an **'Aryan' language** — and from them rose a king who changed the scale of the ancient world: **Cyrus the Great**.

   About **550 BC** Cyrus founded the **Persian Empire**. It grew until it was **the largest empire of the ancient world** — stretching **from the Balkans to the Indus Valley, and into North Africa**. Egypt, Mesopotamia, the Greek cities of the coast: peoples who had each thought themselves the world's centre now paid tribute to one king of kings.

   An empire that size is a problem before it is a glory. How do you rule lands a messenger needs months to cross? The Persian answer — roads, posts and law — is the next part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Roads and the first postal service — ruling by connection

The Persians built **many new roads** across their empire — royal roads, kept up by the state, with way-stations where travellers and officials could rest and change horses.

   On those roads ran something the world had never had: **the world's first postal service**. A royal courier could carry the king's orders out to a far province and bring its reports back at a speed no earlier empire could match. News, taxes and armies all moved faster because the roads existed.

   Add **law**: the empire governed its many peoples under a shared royal order rather than ruling each province by whim. Government, law — the book names these as Persia's contributions, and the roads and the post are how they reached the ground.

## Sub lesson 3 — Zoroaster and his faith

Persia's third gift was a religion. **Zoroastrianism** was founded by the prophet **Zoroaster**, and it taught the Persians to see the world as a great contest between good and evil, light and darkness — with every person called to choose the good side.

   Hold the three gifts together, as the Grade 9 book places them: an empire of roads, a post that outran every rumour, and a faith of light against darkness. Persia is the ancient world's lesson in scale — how to hold more land, more peoples and more gods under one rule than anyone before had managed.
