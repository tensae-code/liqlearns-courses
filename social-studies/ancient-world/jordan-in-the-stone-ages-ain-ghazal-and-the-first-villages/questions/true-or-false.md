# Jordan in the Stone Ages — Ain Ghazal and the first villages — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** on a timeline that runs from the first tools to the invention of writing (3500 BCE) and Alexander (332 BCE), Jordan's hunters leave their tools at Dawqara in Zarqa; at Eyon al-Hammam in Wadi Zaqallab (Irbid) lies what the book calls the oldest organised cemetery in the world, some 16,000 years old; Neolithic villagers at Ain Ghazal in Amman model the world's oldest plaster statues; and at Wadi Faynan by Wadi Araba, Chalcolithic smiths smelt copper and a class of craftsmen is born.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lesson 1: Jordan in the Stone Ages

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *تاريخ الأردن — الصف الحادي عشر، الفرع المهني (History of Jordan, Grade 11, Vocational Track), Term 1 — Unit 1: Jordan in the Ancient Ages* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lesson 1: Jordan in the Stone Ages

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Dawqara in the Zarqa valley is dated by this book to 2.5 million years, among the oldest sites of the age in Asia, with al-Lajjun in Tafila and Karak; the same villages as the vocational telling (Eyon al-Hammam's cemetery, Ain Ghazal's plaster statues, Faynan's copper) — and a fifth age the vocational book folds away: the Bronze Age of walled, gated, templed towns like Bab al-Dhra in the southern Ghawr of Karak.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lesson 1: Jordan in the Stone Ages

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *تاريخ الأردن — الصف الحادي عشر، الفرع الأكاديمي (History of Jordan, Grade 11, Academic Track), Term 1 — Unit 1: Jordan in the Ancient Ages; Unit 2: Jordan in the Islamic Ages; Unit 3: Jordan in the Modern Age (all three units in one term)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lesson 1: Jordan in the Stone Ages
