# Lesson plan — The Lapita people and Pacific settlement

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Lapita people and Pacific settlement
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Fiji, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** thousands of years ago Lapita voyagers from the Bismarck Archipelago carried a named pottery, farming and seafaring culture to New Caledonia, Fiji, Tonga and Samoa (sherds from about 1500 BC), ancestors of today's Melanesian, Micronesian and Polynesian peoples
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1.1 Lapita Pottery and the three Pacific peoples** — thousands of years ago Lapita voyagers from the Bismarck Archipelago carried a named pottery, farming and seafaring culture to New Caledonia, Fiji, Tonga and Samoa (sherds from about 1500 BC), ancestors of today's Melanesian, Micronesian and Polynesian peoples
2. **Chapter 2 Unit 1 History of Fiji: Pre-Contact Period / What was early Fiji like?** — Lapita pottery found at Lakeba, Beqa, Natunuku and Naigani shows the first Fijians shared a culture with the western Pacific; chiefs rule villages and islands, and daily life mixes farming, fishing, ceremony and the constant threat of war
