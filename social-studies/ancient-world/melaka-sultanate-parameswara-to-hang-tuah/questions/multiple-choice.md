# The Melaka Sultanate — Parameswara to Hang Tuah — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) Society and great figures of the Melaka Sultanate  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) Society and great figures of the Melaka Sultanate** — Melaka's society stacks into rulers (sultan, then pembesar) and ruled (rakyat, then hamba); the sultan reigns and leads the army as head of Islam, the bendahara advises and administers, the laksamana commands the sea — and the age's honoured names are Parameswara, Sultans Muzaffar Shah, Mansur Shah and Alauddin Riayat Shah, Bendahara Tun Perak and Laksamana Hang Tuah

**Answer: a) Unit 8: society and great figures of the Melaka Sultanate** — the book's own heading for this part.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) Parameswara founds Melaka  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) Parameswara founds Melaka** — a Palembang prince flees Majapahit's attack, kills Temagi at Temasik, retreats before Siam through Muar and Sening Ujong, and at the Bertam river watches a white mousedeer kick his hunting dog into the water — a small creature beating a bigger one marks the lucky spot; he founds Melaka, named for the tree he leaned on (Sulalatus Salatin), "Malakat — gathering of trade" to the Arabs, Amalaka to India, on a monsoon-sheltered strait between the eastern and western sea roads

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) Tun Perak, the great bendahara  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) Tun Perak, the great bendahara** — bendahara to four sultans, Tun Perak first wins fame as Penghulu Klang, marching Klang's people to Melaka against Siam; he is chief of the pembesar, the sultan's adviser, chief diplomat (receiving envoys from China and Pasai, sending friendship missions to Siam) and war commander who takes Pahang from Siam and aids Pasai; his torches (jamung) lit along the mangrove shore bluff Siam into cancelling its attack, and when the prince Raja Muhammad kills his son Tun Besar, Tun Perak forbids revenge and commands loyalty to the sultan

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) Hang Tuah, the great laksamana  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) Hang Tuah, the great laksamana** — a Sungai Duyung boy trained in silat rises under Sultan Mansur Shah; he outwits Taming Sari and wins his keris, leads the embassy to China and glimpses the emperor's face by a kangkung riddle, persuades Tun Teja to marry the sultan, defeats Majapahit's hired killers sent by Gajah Mada and saves Tun Perak from a man running amok — then, slandered and sentenced to death, is hidden away by the bendahara; as laksamana he guards Melaka's waters, keeps traders safe, hunts pirates and carries the sultan's proposal to the Puteri of Gunung Ledang

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) The glory of the Melaka Sultanate  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) The glory of the Melaka Sultanate** — government rests on the Pembesar Berempat — Bendahara, Penghulu Bendahari, Temenggung and Laksamana; Melaka becomes an empire, the region's centre for spreading Islam (madrasah and mosques under Sultan Muzaffar Shah, ulama gathering) and the strait's great entrepot, its trade managed by syahbandar with low taxes and Orang Laut sea-people; abroad it exchanges envoys with China (Yin Ching 1403, Zheng He's visit 1405) and Sultan Mansur Shah corresponds with Ryukyu

**11. (Sub lesson 6)** This part of the lesson teaches —

**Answer: a) Melaka as the first nation-state** — the Melaka Sultanate is presented as the inherited model of a negara bangsa — an ordered administration, excellent law, and defined roles for ruler and people — the template Malaysian nationhood claims descent from
