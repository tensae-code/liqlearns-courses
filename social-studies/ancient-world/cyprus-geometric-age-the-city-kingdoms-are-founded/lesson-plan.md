# Lesson plan — Cyprus Geometric Age — the city-kingdoms are founded

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Cyprus Geometric Age — the city-kingdoms are founded
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Cyprus, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** After collapse and migration, settlers founded Cyprus's city-kingdoms between 1050 and 750 BC — each kingdom with its own founding story where myth and history meet.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The founding of new kingdoms in Cyprus (1050–750 BC)** — after collapse and migration, settlers found Cyprus's city-kingdoms, and myth and history together explain where each kingdom came from (The founding of new kingdoms in Cyprus (1050–750 BC))
2. **The life of the people** — farmers, herders, smiths and potters fill the new kingdoms, iron arrives, and geometric-patterned pots mark the age (The life of the people)
3. **Relations with other peoples** — a sea people dominating Mediterranean trade draws little Cyprus into a wide web of exchange (Relations with other peoples)
4. **Movement cards — why people move, how they move, where they build** — colonists, traders and conquerors keep arriving in Cyprus across four ages, each wave building its homes in new places (Movement cards — why people move, how they move, where they build)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
