# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan Silk Road Cities And Culture

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan Silk Road Cities And Culture
**Place in the curriculum:** The Ancient World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Otrar, Taraz and Syganak flower as trade makes urban culture
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§23 The Silk Road's influence on medieval Kazakh cities' culture** — Otrar, Taraz and Syganak flower as trade makes urban culture
2. **§24 Stone-working, fine, pottery and applied art of the ancient Turks** — Turkic craftsmen carve, paint and fire the goods the caravans carry
3. **§25 Medieval urban architecture** — citadels, shakhristans and rabads give the steppe city its shape
4. **§26 Medieval architectural monuments** — mausoleums and mosques that still stand are read as documents
5. **§27 Al-Farabi, great thinker of the world** — the Otrar-born 'Second Teacher' links Greek philosophy to the Islamic world
6. **§28 Medieval art develops on Kazakh land** — painting, music and ornament mature in the Karakhanid cities
7. **§29 Oghuz-Kipchak written monuments** — Korkyt's legends and the Codex Cumanicus preserve Kipchak speech
8. **§23 The Silk Road and medieval Kazakh cities' culture (lesson plan)** — Otrar and Taraz show trade turning into urban culture
9. **§24 Stone-working, fine and pottery art of the ancient Turks (lesson plan)** — craft objects are read as sources in the art lessons
10. **§25 Medieval urban architecture (lesson plan)** — citadel, shakhristan and rabad are built up as a city model
11. **§26 Decorative applied art and medieval architectural monuments (lesson plan)** — surviving mausoleums anchor the monuments lesson
12. **§27 Al-Farabi, great thinker of the world (lesson plan)** — al-Farabi's road from Otrar to Baghdad frames the philosophy lesson
13. **§28 Medieval art develops on Kazakh land (lesson plan)** — music, ornament and painting of the Karakhanid age are surveyed
14. **§29 Oghuz-Kipchak written monuments (lesson plan)** — Korkyt and the Codex Cumanicus preserve the Kipchak word
15. **§16 The Silk Road's Turkic section** — Turkic khaganates tax, guard and profit from the road's middle
16. **§17 The Silk Road's historical significance** — silk, paper, faiths and technologies travel the road both ways
17. **§18–19 Cities develop; architecture (10th–early 13th c.)** — Karakhanid cities raise minarets, mausoleums and caravanserais
18. **§20–21 Trade and agriculture; material culture** — markets, irrigation and crafts fill the material-culture chapters
19. **§22 Spiritual culture; Islam spreads** — Islam roots itself in steppe life through cities and Sufi teachers
20. **§23–24 Science and literature, 9th–early 13th c.** — al-Farabi, Balasaguni and Yassawi make the age a golden one for learning
21. **§25 Cultural relations develop** — embassies and translations link the steppe to China, Iran and Rus'
22. **2.5 Did the Great Silk Road affect the development of international relations?** — embassies and caravans make the road a diplomatic system too
23. **2.6 How did the Great Silk Road affect medieval Kazakhstan's economic and cultural development?** — Otrar and Taraz grow rich on transit trade and its crafts
24. **2.7 What contribution did Turkic culture make to world civilisation?** — Balasaguni, Kashgari and Yasawi carry Turkic learning worldwide
25. **3.8 How do 13th–15th-century art and literature reflect medieval society?** — mausoleums and epics mirror a society between steppe and city
26. **3.9 How did medieval travellers describe Kazakhstan?** — Plano Carpini and Rubruck report the steppe to Europe
