# Africa's wildlife and its parks

## Do it first

Name every wild animal you can that lives only in Africa, or is famous from Africa. Which of them have you seen — in life, in a park, or only in pictures? And who guards the places they live? This lesson answers all three.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mammals, endemic species and marine life

Africa's wildlife cast is led by its **mammals** — the great savanna herds and their hunters, the animals the whole world pictures when it pictures wild Africa. Among them are **endemic species**: kinds found **naturally nowhere else on Earth**, whose whole world population lives inside this continent — lose them here and they are lost everywhere.

The cast does not stop at the shore: Africa's **marine life** — the animals of its coasts and seas — belongs to the same account, governed by the waters around the continent as the land animals are governed by its belts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Parks and sanctuaries — the protection system

Wildlife survives where it is given guarded ground: Africa's system of **parks and sanctuaries** — protected areas where animals and their habitats are defended against hunting and clearing. A park is not a fence around animals; it is the protection of the **whole habitat** — grass, water, cover — that the animals' lives run on.

And the lesson's closing link: wildlife distribution is **governed by factors** of land and climate — the same controls that drew the vegetation map. The herds stand on the savanna because the savanna's grass feeds them; the forest species stay in the forest. Read the vegetation, and you can predict the wildlife — protect the vegetation, and you protect the wildlife.

## Say it to your companion

Introduce Africa's wildlife to your companion: the cast, what 'endemic' means, and what a park really protects. Your companion asks: why do the great herds stand on the savanna and not in the desert?
