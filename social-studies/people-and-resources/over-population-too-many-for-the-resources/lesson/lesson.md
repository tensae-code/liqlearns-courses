# Over-population — too many for the resources

## Do it first

The balance tips: too many people, too little to go round. The symptoms arrive in a grim order. Read the over-population side of the scale.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beyond the optimum

Over-population is population beyond the optimum: more people than the resources and technology can support at good living standards. Its measure is simple — resources per person fall below what a decent life needs.

[Picture 21107 — a balance scale tipping to the people side]

## Sub lesson 2 — The symptoms

The symptoms the book lists: famine and hunger; prolonged unemployment; landlessness; and begging — the book instances old European countries’ history for the last of these. Symptoms are how over-population is recognised on the ground, in lives rather than ratios.

## The optimum itself — the best-fit size

Both sides of this lesson are measured against the **optimum population**: the best-fit size, where a population matches its resources and technology. The book gives the optimum's **indications** — the signs a population is at its best fit: **full employment**, **high average living standards**, and **balanced development**. Hold those three signs in mind: a population beyond the optimum falls away from them on one side, and a population below it falls away on the other.
