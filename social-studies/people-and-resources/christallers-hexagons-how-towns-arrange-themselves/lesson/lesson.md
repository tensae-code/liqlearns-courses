## Sub lesson 1 — Central places and hexagons

Settlement location completes the trio of theories (1933, southern Germany). On a uniform plain, service centres arrange themselves in a hierarchy — a few large centres, more small ones — each with a market area. Circles would leave gaps or overlap; hexagons tile the plain exactly.

## Sub lesson 2 — K = 3 and K = 4

K = 3 is the marketing principle: each higher centre serves itself plus a third of each of its six neighbours. K = 4 is the transport principle: lower centres sit at road midpoints between higher ones, minimising travel.

## Sub lesson 3 — K = 7 — administration

K = 7 is the administrative principle: lower centres lie wholly inside one higher centre's area, so control is clean and no town answers to two masters. Three principles, three different maps from the same plain.
