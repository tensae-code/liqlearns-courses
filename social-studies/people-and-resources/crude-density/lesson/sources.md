**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Eritrea, Slovenia, Uganda, Sudan, Kuwait, Pakistan, Libya, Mozambique, Bhutan). Morocco is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Morocco's own way (teacher guide — evidence): in Morocco's Year 5 guide, population density is met inside "Population in my homeland — distribution and structure", where the meaning of population density belongs to reading the homeland's own population — its distribution read from Morocco's density map is stocked as its own lesson (Morocco's Population Distribution). That evidence is stocked here as the way Morocco teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

The worked answer (10,000,000 ÷ 2,000 = 5,000 people per km², band: high) is the arithmetic of the book's own printed problem, carried out — the placement gives the problem's numbers; the division and the band reading follow from the lesson's own definitions on the same page. No teacher-guide material is folded into this lesson (the guide's density frame for this title sits in its Unit 2/3 population sections and adds nothing to the student book's crude-density page; the formal population-measurement framework is carried, as teacher evidence, by the sister course's Population lessons). No book error flagged by the placements touches this lesson.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): Grade 7 turns fully to skills and Eritrea's land — map scale, area and population arithmetic first, then Eritrea's natural heritage (Red Sea coast, wildlife) and Eritrea's own disaster set (drought, locusts, earthquakes, volcanism) as the environmental core.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 11 SD Geography 11): The human-geography year: population first (races, religions, census, density, dynamics, structure), then a full practical unit on distribution maps and graphs, then regional geography — the world studied continent by continent as regions — and a closing full unit on Sudan itself, natural basis to environmental problems.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Social Studies G6 T2): Term 2 turns from the region's nature (Term 1) to its people and its wealth. Unit 1 is History-led — colonialism to independence — with a single Geography lesson on the economic importance of the Gulf's location; Unit 2 (Geography/Civics) covers population growth, the population-structure imbalance and cultural similarity; Unit 3 (Geography) covers the economic resources — energy, agriculture, animal and fish wealth, industry and trade — and the challenges of development; Unit 4 (Civics) covers GCC cooperation. The same architecture as Term 1: a standards page per unit, lessons, a unit project and a full assessment, a values unit ("Stations with Good Kuwait") and a maps appendix. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 1 Lesson 2, European colonial rivalry over the Gulf (Portuguese, Dutch, British), printed pp. 21–23; Lesson 3, the 1899 protection agreement between Kuwait and Britain, pp. 24–25; Lesson 4, the independence of Kuwait (19 June 1961) and the Gulf States, pp. 26–28. Civics — Unit 1 Lesson 5, the development of political life in Kuwait: the 1962 constitution, sovereignty, democracy, human rights, women's political rights, pp. 29–31. ICT/digital skills (outside this wave's scope) — Unit 2 Lesson 4, the digital culture of the Gulf person: artificial intelligence and its uses, cybersecurity and Kuwait's national cybersecurity centre, pp. 49–51. Civics/media — Unit 2 Lesson 5, cultural awareness: cultural invasion, social media's benefits and harms, the cybercrime law, pp. 52–53. Civics — Unit 4 Lesson 1, the Gulf Cooperation Council: six member states, founded 25 May 1981 in Abu Dhabi, headquarters Riyadh, pp. 82–83; Lesson 2, joint security and defence cooperation (Peninsula Shield), pp. 84–85; Lesson 4, joint cultural and educational cooperation (Arab Bureau of Education, Riyadh; Joint Programme Production Institution, Kuwait), pp. 89–91; Lesson 5, future aspirations of the GCC states and Kuwait Vision 2035, pp. 92–94. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": Bu Tbeila, the Ramadan drummer of old Kuwait, pp. 100–101; etiquette of sitting in the diwaniya, pp. 102–103; a helping hand and a reassured heart, pp. 104–105; a kind word and a gentle reply, pp. 106–107. The maps appendix (p. 108) is present only as instructions for fitting a transparent sleeve over the appendix maps.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 SS5): the Class 5 Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 51–85) deepens the Class 4 spiral — same BOLTS, landforms, weather and population topics, but now with scale types, latitude/longitude and time-zone arithmetic, worked population-density calculations, and graph-drawing exercises from real Pakistani city data.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 8 LY G8 Arab World): The scale widens from Libya (Grade 7) to the whole Arab homeland: one regional geography — location and strategic importance, relief, climate, vegetation, population, economic resources and transport — closed by a full unit on the Arab food problem and food security.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 2): human/economic geography organised sector by sector — population first, then each economic activity taught world-then-Mozambique (agriculture, livestock, fishing, forestry, industry, commerce, tourism, transport), with Mozambique's own distribution tables, ports and exports as the local evidence.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 9 Geography): The Intermediate level (IX–X) treats each world topic at full textbook depth and keeps the Bhutan case inside it — Soils of Bhutan, agro-ecological zones and farming practices of Bhutan, and common disasters in Bhutan (including GLOF) alongside the world disaster survey.
