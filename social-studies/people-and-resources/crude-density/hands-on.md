# Hands-on — Crude Density

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Dots on a square
Draw a square on paper and call it one square kilometre. Put dots for people: first 4 dots (uninhabited), then 15 (low), then 60 (medium), then 150 (high). Feel how the same square changes. This is the four-band picture, made by your own hand.

## Count your classroom
Measure your classroom's length and width in metres and work out its area. Count the people in it during a lesson. If the classroom were a square kilometre of land, what density would your count give — and which band? (Convert carefully: first find people per square metre, then multiply by 1,000,000 — or state your density per 100 m² and compare the crowding by eye.)

## The misleading map
Find or draw a simple map of your kebele or woreda. Shade the land nobody can farm — rock, steep slopes, water, desert. Now answer: if you counted only the unshaded land, would the density rise or fall? By roughly how much? This is the crude measure's weakness, seen on your own ground.
