# Lesson plan — Crude Density

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Crude Density
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the fifth lesson of the title, after Why People Settle Where They Do (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** population density — how many people stand on each square kilometre — its four bands, and the crude density measure (total population ÷ total area), with the warning that crude density counts unfarmable land too, so it can mislead about food supply.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Why People Settle Where They Do — the learner can explain why people are unevenly spread; now they measure how crowded a place is. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what density means: people per square kilometre.
2. Name the four bands: over 100 high; 21–100 medium; 5–20 low; under 5 counted as uninhabited.
3. Work a crude density: total population ÷ total area.
4. State the warning: crude density counts unfarmable land too, so it misleads about food supply.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Divides the wrong way (area ÷ people) → the measure answers "how many people on EACH square kilometre?" — so people go on top, area underneath, and the answer is always "people per km²".
- Memorizes bands but cannot place a number → 5,000 per km² is far over 100: high. Drill the four edges: 100, 20, 5.
- Trusts the crude number fully → ask what the woreda's 2,000 km² is made of: if half of it is rock and desert, the people are really standing on half the land. That is the next lesson's opening.
