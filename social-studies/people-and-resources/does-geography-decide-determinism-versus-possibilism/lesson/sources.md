**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Colombia, Venezuela, Mozambique, Nicaragua).**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. The student book's body numbering for Unit Two drifts from its own contents (regions printed as 2.4, factors as 2.5, local classification as 2.6, where the contents and the teacher guide's contents agree on 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5); this lesson follows the contents numbering and teaches no misprint.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado06 Secundaria Activa): Grade 6 Secundaria Activa starts from the universe and the Earth as a "living machine" (rocks and wrinkles, water, air), then follows humans evolving, migrating from Africa to America and adapting to each continent's environment — geography as the stage for all the history that follows.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 1): the second cycle opens with the discipline itself — geographic thought, its history from Greek antiquity to the determinist/possibilist schools, then cosmography (celestial sphere, solar system, Earth's shape and revolution) before any regional content.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 3 Estudios Sociales G3): The first formal Geography course in the set: Units I–II teach what Geography is, map types, scale, orientation and the Earth, then every later unit works at three nested scales — comunidad, municipio, departamento — ending with Nicaragua's three regions, its volcanic chain and a Köppen climate map of the country. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI municipality origins, customs and citizen participation (printed pp. 72–80, History/Civics, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII first settlers' history and culture (printed pp. 82–93, History); Unit VIII historical celebrations and heroes (printed pp. 94–109, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 4 Estudios Sociales G4): Units I–V are a full national Geography of Nicaragua — coordinates, time zones and seasons first, then the country's position, borders, political division and hydrography, and a closing landscape unit on climate, natural resources, relief by region, earthquakes and hurricanes; the second half of the book is national History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI first settlers' history and culture (printed pp. 120–137, History, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII European presence and colonisation (printed pp. 140–164, History); Unit VIII Independence and the National War (printed pp. 168–183, History); Unit IX Nicaragua 1857–1909 (printed pp. 186–197, History); Unit X Nicaragua in the 20th century (printed pp. 200–221, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 5 Estudios Sociales G5): The scale widens from Nicaragua to the whole Central American isthmus: Units I–IV give the planet and map skills, then Central America's position, relief, climate, hydrography, resources, population and economies, with Nicaragua's place in the isthmus as an explicit topic; the second half is Central American History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit V first settlers, Maya and Aztec history (printed pp. 70–90, History, except the two topics placed below); Unit VI European presence (printed pp. 92–111, History); Unit VII independence process (printed pp. 114–140, History); Unit VIII Central America in the 20th century (printed pp. 142–154, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 Estudios Sociales G7 full-year): A full national Geography in five units — territorial patrimony and cartography, physical geography (relief, climate, hydrography, resources), population ("¿Cuántas mujeres, cuántos hombres?"), production, and a whole unit on natural-risk prevention — followed by six units of national History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I state institutions (printed pp. 14–16, Civics); Unit VI origin societies (printed pp. 128–143, History, except the geographical-setting topic placed below); Unit VII European expansion and the Colony (printed pp. 148–165, History); Unit VIII Independence (printed pp. 170–184, History); Unit IX economic/political evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 190–205, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty and the Somoza period (printed pp. 210–233, History); Unit XI Nicaragua 1979 to the present (printed pp. 244–264, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 CCSS Módulo Autoformativo G7): The distance-learning parallel of the Grade 7 full-year book (Book 7): the same five Geography units rewritten for rural self-study, so every topic is anchored in the learner's own comunidad — its soils, its production lands, its road links and its risk zones. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I government institutions (printed p. 23, Civics); Unit VI first settlers (printed pp. 113–128, History, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans (printed pp. 131–141, History); Unit VIII independence process (printed pp. 145–154, History); Unit IX evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 157–168, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty (printed pp. 173–202, History); Unit XI triumph of the Revolution to the present (printed pp. 205–231, History).
