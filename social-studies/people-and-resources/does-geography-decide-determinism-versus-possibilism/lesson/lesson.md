# Does geography decide? Determinism versus possibilism

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: does your birthplace decide your future? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Determinism — geography as script

Determinism holds that development is dictated by location, climate and landforms. Its line runs from Aristotle through Ritter, Semple and Huntington: temperate lands rich, tropical lands poor, because place decides.

[Picture 27050 — two debaters' desks — one holding a map like a script, the other holding a map like a menu]

## Sub lesson 2 — Possibilism — geography as menu

Possibilism answers that the environment offers possibilities, not necessities; human creativity and technology decide which are taken. Febvre and Vidal de la Blache carry the argument: 'nature is never more than an adviser'.

## Sub lesson 3 — The modern balance

The balance the book states: location still shapes population distribution, transport costs and market access — but it does not decide alone. Place loads the dice; people still throw them.

## Sub lesson 4 — Determinism or possibilism? — the Grade 10 pair

The Grade 10 book teaches the same pair in its interaction unit: determinism — the environment shapes human life — against possibilism — humans choose among the possibilities an environment offers, aided by technology and modernisation. Read with the Grade 11 debate above, the pair is the course's standing question: every later lesson, from adaptation to GIS, quietly answers it again.
