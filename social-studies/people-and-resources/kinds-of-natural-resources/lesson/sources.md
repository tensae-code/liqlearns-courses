**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Nauru, Tuvalu, Niue, Palestine, Angola, Bangladesh, Turkmenistan). Sweden is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — land Geography is taught through Pacific farming itself: taro, pulaka and the coconut "tree of life", with Niue's own crops, exports and name-story ("behold the coconut") as anchor examples, and shifting cultivation/fallowing taught as sound science. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — and Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

No Addis Ababa teacher-guide material is folded into this lesson: the Addis Ababa guide's Unit 3 carries no counterpart to this classification beyond the student book's own teaching, so nothing here rests on teacher evidence. No book error flagged by the placements touches this lesson.

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P1): Social Studies opens with a full Earth-science unit (shape, movements, surface, life components, disasters) taught through Palestinian examples — seasons read off Palestinian crops, surface forms read off Palestine's own map, which pupils draw with its borders.

Angola's own way (Grade 5): Geography opens as "geographic space" — the natural space and the human-built space (Luanda port, Benguela railway, Cambambe dam, Huambo housing) — before any physical topic; Angola's own position, borders, population figures (INE) and farm/fishery tables carry the world topics. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.
