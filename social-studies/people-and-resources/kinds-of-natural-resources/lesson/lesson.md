# Kinds of Natural Resources

## Do it first

Write down the natural resources of your own place — everything the land and water give you: the river, the soil, the trees, the animals, the stones, the sun, the air, and more.

Now try to sort your list into groups. Which ones are alive? Which ones could finish one day? Which ones always come back?

Hold your sorting up against the lesson. There are two sorts to learn — and every resource sits in both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Living and non-living

The first sort asks one question: **is it living?**

- **Biotic resources** — the living ones: **plants and animals**. The forest, the grass, the cattle, the fish.
- **Abiotic resources** — the non-living ones: **minerals, water and soil**. The stone in the hill, the river's water, the field's earth.

Biotic means *of life*. Abiotic means *not of life*. Water is in the abiotic box even though nothing lives without it — the sort asks whether the resource itself is alive, not whether it matters.

## Sub lesson 2 — Can be used up, or renews itself

The second sort asks a different question: **does it come back?**

- **Exhaustible resources** — they can be used up, and they do not renew themselves: **minerals, oil, coal and natural gas**. Once the coal is dug and burned, there is no more of it in that place.
- **Renewable resources** — they renew themselves, inexhaustible when cared for: **air, water, plants and sunlight**. The rain refills the river; the tree drops seeds; the sun rises again.

[Picture 11152 — sorting natural resources: four boxes]

Now put the two sorts together. A **tree** is biotic (living) and renewable. **Coal** is abiotic (non-living) and exhaustible. **Water** is abiotic and renewable. Every resource takes one placing in each sort — that gives the four boxes in the picture.

And here is the warning, the most important line in this lesson:

**Even a renewable resource can be depleted — used up — if people use it faster than it replaces itself.**

A forest is renewable. But cut ten trees for every one that grows, and in time there is no forest. River water renews itself — but a town that takes and dirties more than the rain replaces will still go thirsty. "Renewable" is a promise the resource makes only when people take at the speed it can give.

## Say it to your companion

Name three resources from your own place. For each one, your companion asks the two questions — "Is it alive?" and "Does it come back?" — and you answer both, then finish with the warning sentence in your own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the four boxes: living / non-living down one side, can-run-out / renews-itself down the other — or draw the four boxes side by side as in the picture.
2. Write at least eight resources from your own locality into the boxes, each one placed by both sorts.
3. Circle one renewable resource near you that people are using too fast. Write under it how you can tell.
