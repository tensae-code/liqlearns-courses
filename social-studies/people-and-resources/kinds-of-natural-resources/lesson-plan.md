# Lesson plan — Kinds of Natural Resources

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Kinds of Natural Resources
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the second lesson of the title, after The Two-Way Relationship (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** natural resources sort two ways — living (biotic) against non-living (abiotic), and exhaustible (can be used up) against renewable (renews itself) — and even a renewable resource can be depleted if it is used faster than it replaces itself.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Two-Way Relationship — the learner has already named their own locality's resources. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Living and non-living** — the first sort: biotic resources (plants, animals) against abiotic resources (minerals, water, soil).
2. **Can be used up, or renews itself** — the second sort: exhaustible/non-renewable resources (minerals, oil, coal, natural gas) against inexhaustible/renewable ones (air, water, plants, sunlight) — and the warning that even renewable resources can be depleted when used faster than they replace themselves.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Two-Way Relationship — it names the resources this lesson sorts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort any local resource as biotic or abiotic.
2. Sort any local resource as exhaustible or renewable.
3. Place a resource in both sorts at once (a tree: biotic and renewable; coal: abiotic and exhaustible).
4. State the warning: a renewable resource used faster than it replaces itself can still be depleted.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts water in only one sort → water is the lesson's own double example: abiotic (non-living) and renewable — a resource gets one placing in EACH sort, not one placing overall.
- Thinks renewable means "can never run out" → go back to the warning: a forest cut faster than it regrows is renewable — and gone.
- Confuses biotic with renewable → they are different questions: "Is it alive?" (sort one) against "Does it come back?" (sort two).
