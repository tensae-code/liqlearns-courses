# Case study: living with the Baro River floods

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: what do you do when the flood comes almost every year? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Itang on the Baro

Agropastoralist households on the Baro River in Itang, Gambella, faced a major flood almost every year from 2006 to 2012. Flooding there is not an accident of one bad year — it is the rhythm of life.

[Picture 27056 — a raised-floor house with a ditch ring, beside the flooded Baro plain]

## Sub lesson 2 — The 2007 flood counted

In the 2007 flood, 94 percent of surveyed households lost crops severely and 51 percent lost livestock, with dairy output halved. Numbers like these are what 'impact' means at household level.

## Sub lesson 3 — Defences — and their limit

Short-term defences: moving property and animals to relatives, early harvest, selling stock. Long-term: ditches, raised floors, boundary walls. They work for minor floods but fail as floods worsen — and coping through relatives' help erodes as everyone's social capital is spent.
