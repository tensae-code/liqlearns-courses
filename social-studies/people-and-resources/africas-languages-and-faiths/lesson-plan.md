# Lesson plan — Africa's languages and faiths

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Africa's languages and faiths
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Major Economic and Cultural Activities of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa is a continent of many languages and many faiths — including its folk religions — and the cultural landscapes they form together are part of the continent's human resources: who people are shapes how they live, work and learn.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands in Unit 5 beside the economic activities — culture is taught there as part of how Africa's peoples live and work.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **A continent of many languages** — Linguistic diversity as a fact of African life.
2. **Faiths of Africa — including folk religions** — Religious composition and cultural landscapes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Africa's minerals in the world league — the same unit family's people-and-resources view. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe Africa's linguistic diversity and its place in daily working life.
2. Describe Africa's religious composition, including folk religions.
3. Define cultural landscape and read one from its visible signs.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
Two diversities (languages, faiths), one joint product (cultural landscapes), one geographic claim (culture has a distribution and can be mapped, like climate). Keep folk religions in the faiths list — the book insists on their place.
