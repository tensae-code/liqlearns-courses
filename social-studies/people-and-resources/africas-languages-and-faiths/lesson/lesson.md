# Africa's languages and faiths

## Do it first

Count the languages spoken within one day of your own life — at home, at school, in the market, at worship. Now multiply that by a continent. How many languages and faiths can one continent hold? Read on for the shape of the answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — A continent of many languages

Africa's **linguistic diversity** is among the greatest on Earth: the continent holds **many languages**, spoken in families and communities across every region, so that multilingual daily life — home language, market language, school language — is normal for millions of Africans.

The book places this diversity inside the continent's cultural account deliberately: language is not decoration on economic life, it is one of its working parts — trade, teaching, worship and government all happen **through** the languages people actually speak.

## Sub lesson 2 — Faiths of Africa — including folk religions

Africa's **religious composition** is likewise many-stranded: great world faiths with deep African histories stand alongside **folk religions** — the indigenous belief traditions of African peoples, rooted in their own lands and ancestors — and the book names them as a real part of the continent's religious map, not a footnote to it.

Together, languages and faiths form **cultural landscapes**: visible patterns on the land itself — places of worship, festivals, dress, markets and schools — by which a traveller can read who lives in a place. Culture, in this unit, is a geographic fact: it has a distribution, like climate or population, and it can be mapped.

## Say it to your companion

Describe Africa's diversity to your companion: its languages, its faiths — folk religions included — and what a cultural landscape is. Your companion describes a place they know; you read its cultural landscape from the signs they give.
