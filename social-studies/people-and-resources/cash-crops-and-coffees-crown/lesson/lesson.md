# Cash crops and coffee’s crown

## Do it first

One crop pays more of Ethiopia’s import bill than any other. Its name is famous on every continent. Read coffee’s crown — and the court beneath it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Coffee first

Coffee leads Ethiopia’s export earnings. A table tracks its share of exports: still first, despite a decline since 2018/19. Coffee is both a cash crop for export and a drink at the centre of Ethiopian daily life — the crown is earned abroad and worn at home.

## Sub lesson 2 — The court of cash crops

Behind coffee stand the other export crops: oilseeds, pulses, khat and flowers among them. Together the cash crops are the farm goods that earn the foreign currency the trade lesson spends on fuel, machinery and manufactures.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why coffee wears the crown, and name the court of crops behind it.

## Going further — Older evidence — cash crops in numbers (2011 guide)

An older guide gives the cash crops their smallholder numbers: cereals dominate food crops by area and output, and of the cash crops coffee leads by export value. In 2001/02 coffee, chat and sugarcane were grown by about 1.5 million, 0.5 million and 2.1 million farmers respectively, on average holdings of 0.12, 0.062 and 0.021 hectares — cash cropping is smallholder work. Fruits, vegetables and especially cut flowers for European markets were the fast-growing newcomers. Livestock contributed 23.4% of agricultural exports in 1987/88, about 96% of it hides and skins, then declined as cash crops rose; the key blames unscientific livestock management, poor veterinary services, inadequate infrastructure and contraband border trade for low meat and milk output.
