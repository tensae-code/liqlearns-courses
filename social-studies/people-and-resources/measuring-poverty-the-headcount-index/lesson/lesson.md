# Measuring poverty — the headcount index

## Do it first

A town of 1,000 people, 240 of them below the poverty line. One number summarises the town's poverty. What is it — and what does it hide?

## Sub lesson 1 — Why measure at all

Poverty is measured to keep the poor on the policy agenda, to identify and target them, to monitor interventions, and to judge the institutions meant to help. An unmeasured problem is an ignorable one.

[Picture 25220 — a poverty line drawn across a crowd, heads below it counted]

## Sub lesson 2 — The three steps

The method has three steps: choose a welfare indicator (usually consumption); set the poverty line that separates poor from non-poor; and summarise with a statistic. Change any step and the count changes — which is why honest reports name all three.

## Sub lesson 3 — The headcount index — H/N

The simplest statistic is the headcount index: the share of the population below the line, H/N — the poor counted (H) over everyone (N). For our town: 240/1,000 = 0.24, or 24%. It says how many, and nothing about how poor.
