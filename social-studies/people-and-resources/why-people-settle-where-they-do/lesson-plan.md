# Lesson plan — Why People Settle Where They Do

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Why People Settle Where They Do
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the fourth lesson of the title, after Water, Air, Forest, Wildlife, Soil (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** settlement follows resources — population is unevenly spread because where people settle is tied directly to water, soil, vegetation and climate.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Two-Way Relationship and Kinds of Natural Resources — the learner already knows the resources; now they explain the map of people. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Water and soil draw settlement** — the ancient civilisations settled on the Nile, Euphrates, Tigris and Indus; water serves power, farming and drinking; fertile soil makes a place chosen.
2. **Vegetation and climate** — vegetation cover attracts settlement (with the equator puzzle: dense forest, thin settlement — to be explained); harsh climates thin settlement out, kind climates hold it medium to dense.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Population — distribution (Population, Transport and Maps) — it shows the uneven world this lesson explains. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four controllers of settlement: water, soil, vegetation and climate.
2. Explain the four ancient river civilisations as settlement following water.
3. Say what water serves: power, farming and drinking.
4. Explain the equator puzzle in their own words, and judge a place's settlement from its climate.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists controllers but cannot use them → give a bare place: "a dry hill, no river, poor soil, fierce heat" — would a village grow there? Make them argue from the four.
- Stuck on the equator puzzle → dense forest is not the same as easy farming: the puzzle's point is that vegetation alone does not settle a place — soil, clearings, disease and difficulty of moving through thick forest all press the other way; accept any reasoned explanation that uses the controllers together.
- Confuses settlement with density → this lesson explains WHY people are where they are; the next lessons count HOW MANY stand on each square kilometre.
