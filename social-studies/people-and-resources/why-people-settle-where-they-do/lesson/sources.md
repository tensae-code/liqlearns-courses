Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Bangladesh's own way (Grades 9-10): the shared SSC book's big chapter "Living Together Safely" is a genuine geography course inside the combined book — land-use change read off 1988-vs-2017 Khulna maps, world population and density, remote sensing and GIS with its five components, settlement and urbanisation, desertification, and Bangladesh's own squeeze of limited land, dense population and climate risk.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 7) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 7 turns analytical — factors behind transport-mode choice, up-to-date maps/charts (routes, contours, scale-and-distance work) for domestic, regional and international travel, transport hazards from floods/fog/earthquakes, shelter shortages and siting factors, and desertification with land rehabilitation (afforestation, gully filling). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 6) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 6 shelter becomes settlement geography — development of settlements in Zimbabwe, siting of shelter in rural and urban areas, and disaster and risk management; transport adds road signs/signals/markings and Zimbabwe's transport and communication links with other countries, plus ICT development. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.
