Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Bangladesh's own way (Grades 9-10): the shared SSC book's big chapter "Living Together Safely" is a genuine geography course inside the combined book — land-use change read off 1988-vs-2017 Khulna maps, world population and density, remote sensing and GIS with its five components, settlement and urbanisation, desertification, and Bangladesh's own squeeze of limited land, dense population and climate risk.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 7) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 7 turns analytical — factors behind transport-mode choice, up-to-date maps/charts (routes, contours, scale-and-distance work) for domestic, regional and international travel, transport hazards from floods/fog/earthquakes, shelter shortages and siting factors, and desertification with land rehabilitation (afforestation, gully filling). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 6) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 6 shelter becomes settlement geography — development of settlements in Zimbabwe, siting of shelter in rural and urban areas, and disaster and risk management; transport adds road signs/signals/markings and Zimbabwe's transport and communication links with other countries, plus ICT development. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

Eritrea's own way (ER G6 Social Studies): one integrated Social Studies book per grade weaving History, Geography, Civics and Culture strands; Geography is taught through Eritrea itself first (Eritrea's location and size, Eritrean settlement, migration and disasters) with very practical, activity-led lessons.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 9 Geografija 9): Grade 9 turns home — the whole book is Slovenia itself: first the country as a system (position and borders, relief and rocks, climate/vegetation/waters, settlement, population, settlements, economy, transport, environment), then its five natural regions one by one (Alpine, Pre-Alpine with the Ljubljana basin, Dinaric-Karst, Mediterranean, Pannonian). Named, not placed: the EU unit's history of the European Union and Slovenia-in-EU pros/cons (printed p. 7, Civics/History) and the state symbols inside the opening unit (p. 1, Civics).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 4 UG P4 SST Home-Study): Social Studies organised entirely around "our district": the child's own district's physical features, vegetation, settlement and work are the geography content. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Term Two social activities and ceremonies in the district (printed pp. 17–19); Term Three — Our Leaders in the District, Children's Rights, Responsibilities of People in Our District, Social Services in Our District and caring for social service centres (Civics, printed pp. 20–35).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 9 Geography G9): every unit is taught twice at two scales — the Arab homeland first, then Syria — with parallel lesson pairs (relief, climate, water, soil, agriculture, industry, transport, tourism); Unit 3's framing is pointed: "huge human resources… and weak investment", and the subsoil lessons are titled "hidden treasures".

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 SS5): the Class 5 Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 51–85) deepens the Class 4 spiral — same BOLTS, landforms, weather and population topics, but now with scale types, latitude/longitude and time-zone arithmetic, worked population-density calculations, and graph-drawing exercises from real Pakistani city data.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 2): human/economic geography organised sector by sector — population first, then each economic activity taught world-then-Mozambique (agriculture, livestock, fishing, forestry, industry, commerce, tourism, transport), with Mozambique's own distribution tables, ports and exports as the local evidence.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 5): population geography taught as a developed-world/developing-world contrast throughout — distribution factors, growth, Malthus and the transition, then the two evolutions (developed countries; the "Terceiro Mundo" set) with their opposite population policies, closing on structures, demographic problems (incl. HIV cutting life expectancy below 50) and population–economy/environment relations.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 7 Social Studies): at intermediate level Social Studies becomes the history of ancient civilisations, and every civilisation is opened through its land — relief, climate, soil and rivers are taught as the stage on which each civilisation arose, starting with Iraq's own four relief regions.
