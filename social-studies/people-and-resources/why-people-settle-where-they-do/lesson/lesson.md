## Sub lesson 1 — Water and soil draw settlement

Look at the world map of people and one fact jumps out: **population distribution is uneven**. Crowds here, emptiness there. Why?

Because **settlement is tied directly to resources**. The controllers are four: **water, soil, vegetation and climate**.

**Water first.** The oldest civilisations on Earth settled on rivers: the **Nile**, the **Euphrates**, the **Tigris** and the **Indus**. That was no accident. Water serves a settlement three ways: it gives **power**, it waters **farming**, and it is what people **drink**. A place with sure water can feed a town; a place without it cannot.

**Soil next.** Where the soil is **fertile** — rich and ready to grow food — people choose the place and stay. Where the soil is thin and poor, fields fail and settlements stay small or never start.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vegetation and climate

**Vegetation.** Thick plant cover attracts settlement — wood for building and fuel, grazing for animals, shade and food. But here is the puzzle to solve: at the **equator**, the forest is the densest on Earth — yet settlement there is **thin**. Why would the greenest land hold so few people? Hold that question; you will answer it for your companion below. (Think: is dense forest the same as easy farming? What else presses on a settler there?)

**Climate.** The last controller judges all the others. A **harsh climate** — fierce heat with no water, or bitter cold — **thins** settlement out. A **kind climate** — warmth with rain, no killing extremes — holds settlement **medium to dense**.

So the four controllers work together like four hands on one rope. Water, soil, vegetation, climate: where they pull together, towns rise. Where they fail, the land stays empty — however big it looks on the map.

## Say it to your companion

Name the four controllers from memory. Then explain the equator puzzle to your companion: the densest forest on Earth, thinly settled — why? Your companion listens for an answer that uses the controllers together, not vegetation alone.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take three places you know: your own village or town, a crowded place, and an empty place.
2. For each place, score the four controllers: water — good or weak? soil — fertile or poor? vegetation — thick or thin? climate — kind or harsh?
3. Write one sentence for each place: "People settle here / do not settle here because …", using at least two controllers in every sentence.
