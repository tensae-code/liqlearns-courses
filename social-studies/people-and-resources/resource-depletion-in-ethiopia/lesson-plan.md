# Lesson plan — Resource Depletion in Ethiopia

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Resource Depletion in Ethiopia
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the seventh lesson of the title, after Physiological and Agricultural Density (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** depletion — a resource used faster than it can replace itself — as Ethiopia's own story: the named troubles, the paradox of a resource-rich land suffering famine, what depletion breaks, and the four pressures and three remedies.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Kinds of Natural Resources (the depletion warning) and Water, Air, Forest, Wildlife, Soil (the five troubles) — this lesson gathers them into one national picture. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What depletion means — and Ethiopia's named troubles** — depletion defined; farmland cropped without rest, grazing land stripped, forests felled, lake fish falling, soil eroding, water shrinking.
2. **The paradox** — desert-climate Egypt and Israel and snowbound Norway and Finland feed themselves and even export, while resource-rich Ethiopia suffers repeated famine: is drought alone the cause?
3. **What depletion breaks** — the consequence chains: no oil, nothing moves or is made; scarce things cost more; the phosphorus chain from soil to life itself. (Teacher guide — evidence.)
4. **Four pressures and the remedies** — population pressure, agricultural expansion, rapid urbanisation, resettlement; the damage list; the remedies: agroforestry, social forestry, cities growing upward. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Kinds of Natural Resources and Water, Air, Forest, Wildlife, Soil — this lesson is their gathering. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define depletion and name Ethiopia's six named troubles.
2. State the paradox and argue it: drought alone, or land care too?
3. Trace a consequence chain (phosphorus; oil) from a depleted resource to daily life.
4. Name the four pressures on Ethiopia's resources, the damage they do, and the three keyed remedies.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks depletion means "finished everywhere forever" → it is a race, not a state: used FASTER than it replaces itself. Slow the taking, and the same resource recovers.
- Answers the paradox with "drought" and stops → push with the book's own comparisons: Egypt and Israel are drier; Norway and Finland are snowbound. If climate alone decided, they would starve first. What else differs? Land care — rest for fields, trees kept, soil held.
- Mixes the four pressures up → anchor each to its picture panel: people crowding (population), fields spreading (farming), cities swelling (urbanisation), people moved (resettlement).
