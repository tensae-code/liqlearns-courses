# Resource Depletion in Ethiopia — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Depletion means —
a) a resource that nobody uses  b) a resource used faster than it can replace itself  c) a resource found in only one country
**Answer: b) a resource used faster than it can replace itself.**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Which list names Ethiopia's troubles as the book gives them?
a) Unrested farmland, stripped grazing land, felled forests, falling lake fish, eroding soil, shrinking water  b) Earthquakes, volcanoes, floods, locusts, frost, hail  c) War, taxes, trade, cities, roads, schools
**Answer: a).**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The paradox at the centre of the lesson is —
a) rich lands always starve and poor lands always eat  b) desert Egypt and Israel and snowbound Norway and Finland feed themselves and export, while resource-rich Ethiopia suffers repeated famine  c) famine only happens in hot countries
**Answer: b).**

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** If phosphorus were depleted from the soil, the chain runs —
a) plants grow taller → more oxygen → more life  b) plants cannot grow → no oxygen production → human and other life ceases  c) nothing changes for plants
**Answer: b).**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** When a resource grows scarce, what happens to prices?
a) They fall  b) Food, fuel and energy prices rise  c) They stay fixed by law everywhere
**Answer: b)** — scarcity raises prices, and renewables shipped to depleted areas cost more too.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** Which is NOT one of the four pressures on Ethiopia's resources?
a) Population pressure  b) Agricultural expansion  c) Moonlight
**Answer: c)** — the four are population pressure, agricultural expansion, rapid urbanisation and resettlement.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** The three keyed remedies are —
a) agroforestry, social forestry, and growing urban centres upward  b) more quarrying, more grazing, more cutting  c) importing everything
**Answer: a).**
