# Resource Depletion in Ethiopia — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons"). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number, as
before. The questions live in the type files; this file is the
by-sub-lesson view. The student book's depletion teaching comes
first (definition, troubles, paradox); the teacher guide's two
extensions follow, labelled as evidence in sources.md.

## Sub lesson 1 — What depletion means, and Ethiopia's named troubles
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: define depletion (used faster than it replaces itself) and name Ethiopia's six troubles — unrested farmland, stripped grazing land, felled forests, falling lake fish, eroding soil, shrinking water.

## Sub lesson 2 — The paradox
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: state the paradox (desert and snowbound lands feeding themselves and exporting, resource-rich Ethiopia in repeated famine) and argue it — drought alone is not the whole cause; forest-felling, erosion and land care decide food security.

## Sub lesson 3 — What depletion breaks (teacher guide — evidence)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: trace the oil consequence (nothing moved, made, built, planted, mined or harvested), the price consequence, the metals consequence, and the phosphorus chain link by link to life itself.

## Sub lesson 4 — Four pressures and the remedies (teacher guide — evidence)
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: name the four pressures (population pressure, agricultural expansion, rapid urbanisation, resettlement — in its internal sense), the damage list, and the three remedies (agroforestry, social forestry, cities growing upward).
