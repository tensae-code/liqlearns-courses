# What forests do for the economy

## Do it first

A forest is a warehouse, a water tower and a soil guard, all standing in one place. Read its economic services one by one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Products

Forests supply fuelwood — the cooking fuel of most households; construction timber and poles; and other products from honey to medicines. Forestry is a primary activity: it takes these goods directly from the standing forest.

[Picture 21098 — a forest with fuel, poles, bees and a protected spring marked]

## Sub lesson 2 — Protection, and the small exchange role

Standing forests also protect: they hold watersheds — keeping streams flowing and clean — and guard soils against erosion. Yet for all this, forestry’s direct foreign-exchange role is small: forests earn little export currency beside coffee and the other cash crops.
