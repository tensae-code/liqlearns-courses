# Case study: a herd, three droughts, and the trap

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: one herder, 267 animals, three droughts — what is left? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The herd that was a pension

A veteran herder in Burkina Faso held his retirement in animals: 135 cattle, 87 sheep and 45 goats. A herd like that is savings, insurance and status in one.

[Picture 27057 — a world poverty-risk map, with the 11 most at-risk countries marked]

## Sub lesson 2 — Three droughts

The 1973–74 drought cut the herd down. He partly rebuilt through irrigated gardening — then drought hit again in 2004 and again in 2011, until he was selling his handcart to buy food. Each recovery started from less than the one before: the poverty trap in one life.

## Sub lesson 3 — The geography of risk

The section closes with the map of risk: extreme poverty concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and South and Southeast Asia, the climate 'hot-spots', and the 11 countries most at risk of disaster-induced poverty — Ethiopia listed among them.
