# Weber's triangle — where should the factory go?

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: a factory can stand anywhere — where does it cost least? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The least-cost idea

Industrial location gets its model in 1909: firms minimise total cost, transport first, then adjust for labour costs and agglomeration — the savings of clustering with other firms. The picture is a triangle: the market at one corner, two input sources at the others, the factory pulled among them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The material index

The deciding tool is the material index: the weight of inputs divided by the weight of outputs. Above 1, the process loses weight — locate at the raw material, like ore refining and sugar milling.

## Sub lesson 3 — Below one — to the market

Below 1, the process gains weight — locate at the market, like bottling and brewing. Users of ubiquitous inputs, found everywhere like water, are freed from the sources and likewise go to the market.
