# Hands-on — Too Many, Too Few, Just Right

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Build the balance
Make a simple balance: a stick across a stone, or a beam on a stand, with two pans (lids, cloths or small baskets). Put stones for "people" in one pan and stones for "resources" in the other. Show the three states with your hands — people-side down, resources-side down, level — and say each state's name and one of its signs as you set it.

## Signs hunt
For your own neighbourhood, hunt the signs for one week: Are jobs easy or hard to find? Are new houses going up for arrivals, or are families leaving? Is food shared thin or comfortably? Keep a two-column notebook — signs of pressure, signs of room — and judge the balance at week's end.

## The council debate
In a group: one side argues your locality needs an anti-natalist answer, the other that it needs a pro-natalist one, a third judges, using only signs they can observe. The judge must name the winning side's two strongest observed signs.
