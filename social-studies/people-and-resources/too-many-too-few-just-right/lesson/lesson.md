# Too Many, Too Few, Just Right

## Do it first

Picture a classroom built for thirty learners. Now put ninety learners in it. Chairs run out, air runs out, the teacher's time runs out. **Too many.**

Now picture three learners in that same classroom, with a library, a laboratory and fields outside that nobody has hands enough to work. **Too few.**

Somewhere between the two is the class where everything is used well and nobody is crushed. **Just right.**

A whole country is that classroom, and its resources are the chairs. This lesson names the three states.

## Sub lesson 1 — Too many: over-population

**Over-population** is an **excess of population against available resources** — more people than the land, water, jobs and food can carry well.

You can read it by its signs:

- **Low per-capita income** — the wealth there is, divided among too many, leaves little each.
- **High unemployment and underemployment** — people ready to work, and not enough work.
- **Malnutrition and famine** — food stretched too thin.
- **Low living standards** — crowded, undersupplied lives.
- **Outward migration** — people leaving to find a life elsewhere.

On the balance, the people-side pan crashes down. The resources cannot lift it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Too few: under-population

**Under-population** is the opposite tilt: **too few people for the full use of the resources** — land unworked, resources unlifted, because hands are lacking.

Its signs are the mirror of the first state:

- **High per-capita income** — what there is divides among few.
- **Low unemployment** — workers are wanted, not turned away.
- **Inward migration** — people arrive from elsewhere to take up the room and the work.

On the balance, the resource-side pan sits heavy and the people-side floats. Riches under-used are their own kind of poverty for a country's strength.

## Sub lesson 3 — Just right: optimum population, and the two policy answers

Between the two tilts stands the balance:

**Optimum population is the balanced population–resource relationship** — people enough to use the resources fully, resources enough to carry the people well. Not a number written anywhere: a *relationship*, judged place by place.

And countries act on the balance. Two policy answers:

- **Anti-natalist policy** — adopted to **reduce the birth rate and slow population growth**. A country pressing toward over-population reaches for this one.
- **Pro-natalist policy** — adopted to **increase the birth rate and population growth**. A country sliding toward under-population reaches for that one.

*Natal* means birth: anti-natalist turns births down, pro-natalist turns them up. Governments choose between them by reading the same signs you learned in this lesson — income per person, unemployment, hunger's presence, and which way the people are moving.

## Say it to your companion

Name the three states from memory — too many, too few, just right — with the proper name of each: over-population, under-population, optimum population. Then your companion reads a set of signs aloud ("low income each, no jobs, people leaving…") and you name the state — and the policy that answers it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take one place you know well — a village, a town, a neighbourhood.
2. List the signs you can actually observe: work — easy or hard to find? Food — enough or stretched? People — arriving or leaving?
3. Judge its balance: over, under, or near optimum? Write the judgement and the two strongest signs that support it.
4. Write which policy, if any, fits its condition — and one sentence why.
