# Lesson plan — Too Many, Too Few, Just Right

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Too Many, Too Few, Just Right
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the eighth lesson of the title, after Resource Depletion in Ethiopia (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a population can be too big for its resources (over-population), too small to use them fully (under-population), or balanced with them (optimum population) — and countries answer imbalance with anti-natalist or pro-natalist policies.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Crude Density and Resource Depletion in Ethiopia — the learner can count people on land and knows what happens when the taking outruns the replacing; now they judge the balance itself. It needs nothing else. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see sources.md).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Too many — over-population** — an excess of population against available resources, told by its signs: low income per person, unemployment, malnutrition and famine, low living standards, people migrating out.
2. **Too few — under-population** — too few people for full use of resources, with the opposite signs: high income per person, low unemployment, people migrating in.
3. **Just right — optimum population, and the two policy answers** — the balanced population–resource relationship; anti-natalist policy (reduce the birth rate, slow growth) and pro-natalist policy (increase the birth rate and growth).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Crude Density and Resource Depletion in Ethiopia — the counting and the consequences this lesson's balance stands between. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define over-population, under-population and optimum population.
2. Read a place's condition from its signs (income per person, unemployment, migration direction).
3. Say which policy — anti-natalist or pro-natalist — answers which imbalance, and why.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A balance scale or a beam and two pans, if one is near, helps the hands-on.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks "over" and "under" are about land size → they are about the relationship: people AGAINST resources. A small land with few people and rich soil can be optimum; a huge land can be over-populated if its resources are poor.
- Mixes the migration directions → people LEAVE an over-populated place (outward migration) and ARRIVE at an under-populated one (inward migration) — they move toward the balance.
- Mixes the policies → natal means birth: ANTI-natalist slows births down; PRO-natalist encourages them up. Match each to the imbalance it repairs.
