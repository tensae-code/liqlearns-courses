# Video script — Bahamas Island Natural Resources

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bahamas' Grade 3 student book, the *Social Science, Grade 3* (printed p. 41), teaches: Natural Resources: the resources each Bahamian island is known for — clear waters, coral reefs and pine forests; Abaco for farming; Andros for crabs; Grand Bahama for citrus fruit, onions, corn, sponge, cement and medicine. Bahamas' Grade 4 student book, the *Bahamas Primary Social Studies — Digital Textbook, Grade 4* (PDF p. 8), teaches: Food & Natural Resources: the resources pictured and named for The Bahamas — cascarilla, aragonite and salt. Bahamas' Grade 5 student book, the *Bahamas Primary Social Studies — Digital Textbook, Grade 5* (PDF pp. 23–24), teaches: Marine Resources: The Bahamas is an archipelago whose surrounding water provides marine resources, coral reefs, beaches and food that support its economy; aragonite, a fine calcium carbonate, is mined off the shores of Bimini.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case. Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas. Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism.
