# The Lorenz curve

## Do it first

Plot, from the last lesson's table, the share of income held by the poorest 20%, then 40%, then 60%... What shape grows on the page — and what would perfect equality draw instead?

## Sub lesson 1 — Cumulative shares on a square

The Lorenz curve plots cumulative population share, poorest first, against cumulative income share. From the table: the bottom 20% hold 5%, the bottom 40% hold 14%, and the curve climbs to 100% of income only at 100% of people.

## Sub lesson 2 — The equality diagonal

Perfect equality is the 45-degree diagonal: the bottom 40% receiving 40%, every share matching its people. Every real distribution bows below the diagonal — the more bowed, the more unequal.

## Sub lesson 3 — The picture behind the Gini

Max Lorenz devised the curve in 1905. It is the picture from which the Gini coefficient, the next lesson, is computed: the bow you see here becomes the number there.
