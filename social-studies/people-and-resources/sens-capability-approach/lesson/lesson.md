# Sen's capability approach

## Do it first

Two people receive the same bicycle. One rides it to market daily; the other cannot ride. Did both receive the same thing? Amartya Sen built a theory on your answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Functionings and capabilities

Sen argues income and commodities are only means. What matters is functionings — what a person actually is and does: being nourished, literate, mobile, taking part in community life — and capabilities, the real freedom to achieve them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Conversion factors

Whether a good becomes a functioning depends on conversion factors — personal, social and environmental. His example: a book is worth little to someone who cannot read; it is, at best, cooking fuel. Same book, different capability.

## Sub lesson 3 — Poverty as unfreedom

Poverty is therefore the inability to pursue wellbeing, not merely low income, and headcounts alone cannot measure it. Development widens capabilities; poverty is their absence.
