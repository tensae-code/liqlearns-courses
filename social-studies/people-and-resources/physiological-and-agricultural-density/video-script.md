# Video script — Physiological and Agricultural Density

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The
placements name a worked-problem script for this lesson, so this
script is full: it works all three problems aloud, step by step.

---

Crude density lied to us a little.
It counted ALL the land — even rock, even desert.
Today we count only the land that feeds people.

**First measure: physiological density.**
All the people — divided by the farmland only.

Here is a zone. Twenty million people.
Its land is five thousand square kilometres.
But its farmland is only twenty percent.

Twenty percent of five thousand.
One-fifth of five thousand.
The farmland is **one thousand** square kilometres.

Now divide. Twenty million, divided by one thousand.
Twenty million ÷ one thousand = **twenty thousand**.

Twenty thousand people on every farmland square.
Can that land feed them all? That is the question
this measure asks. And here — the pressure is enormous.

**Second measure: agricultural density.**
Not all the people now — only the **rural** people.
Not all the farmland — only the land **actually cultivated**.

Rural people ÷ cultivated land.
It asks a different question:
how crowded are the farmers on the fields they work?
Many farmers, little cultivated land —
small plots, hard lives.

**Now — all three together.** One territory.
Six hundred thousand people. One hundred thousand square kilometres.
Twenty percent urban. Forty percent crop-covered. Sixty percent farmable.

Crude first: six hundred thousand ÷ one hundred thousand = **six**.
Six per square kilometre. The land looks almost empty.

Physiological: farmable land is sixty percent of one hundred thousand
= sixty thousand. Six hundred thousand ÷ sixty thousand = **ten**.
Ten people must eat from every farmable square.

Agricultural: rural people are eighty percent of six hundred thousand
= four hundred eighty thousand.
Cultivated land is forty percent of one hundred thousand
= forty thousand. Four hundred eighty thousand ÷ forty thousand = **twelve**.
Twelve farming people on every cultivated square.

Six — ten — twelve.
The crude number smiled. The true numbers work harder.
Judge food by the land that feeds people — never by all the land.

Now you. Take the zone problem. Work it aloud, step by step,
the way you just heard it.
