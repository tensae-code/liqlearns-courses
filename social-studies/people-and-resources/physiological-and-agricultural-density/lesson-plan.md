# Lesson plan — Physiological and Agricultural Density

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Physiological and Agricultural Density
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the sixth lesson of the title, after Crude Density (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two sharper densities — physiological density (total population ÷ farmland area) and agricultural density (rural population ÷ cultivated land) — and how to judge a territory's food supply by reading all three densities together.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Crude Density — the learner can work the crude measure and knows why it misleads. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before

1. **Physiological density** — all the people, counted only on the farmland: total population ÷ farmland area, worked on the book's zone problem, then judged for food supply.
2. **Agricultural density** — only the rural people, counted on the land actually cultivated: rural population ÷ cultivated land, worked for a kebele.
3. **Judging food supply — the three densities together** — the book's closing problem: one territory, all three measures, one food-supply judgement.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Crude Density — this lesson exists because the crude measure misleads. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Work a physiological density: find the farmland first, then divide all the people by it.
2. Work an agricultural density: rural people only, cultivated land only.
3. Say what each measure is FOR: physiological judges food pressure on farmland; agricultural judges how crowded the farming people themselves are.
4. Combine all three densities for one territory and give a food-supply judgement.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses total area again → the whole point of these two measures is the smaller denominator: farmland only, or cultivated land only. Ask: "Which land feeds people? Count only that."
- Mixes the two numerators → physiological takes ALL the people (everyone eats); agricultural takes only RURAL people (it measures the farming crowd itself).
- Cannot do the percentage step in the zone problem → do it in words first: 20% of 5,000 km² is one-fifth of 5,000 — one thousand. Then divide.
