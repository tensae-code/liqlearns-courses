# Physiological and Agricultural Density — short answer

Answer in one or two sentences, showing your working where numbers are asked.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A zone's total area is 5,000 km² and its farmland is 20% of it. How many km² of farmland does it have?
**Answer:** 20% of 5,000 = 1,000 km² of farmland.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The zone in question 1 holds 20,000,000 people. Work its physiological density and judge its food supply.
**Answer:** 20,000,000 ÷ 1,000 = 20,000 people per km² of farmland — enormous pressure; the food supply is strained unless food comes from elsewhere or yields are extraordinary.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Why does agricultural density use rural population, not total population?
**Answer:** Because it measures the farming people themselves — how crowded they are on the land they actually cultivate. Town people do not work that land.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Work all three densities for the closing territory: 600,000 people, 100,000 km², 20% urban, 40% crop-covered, 60% farmable.
**Answer:** Crude: 600,000 ÷ 100,000 = 6 per km². Physiological: farmable land 60,000 km²; 600,000 ÷ 60,000 = 10 per km². Agricultural: rural people 480,000; cultivated land 40,000 km²; 480,000 ÷ 40,000 = 12 per km².

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Give the food-supply judgement for the closing territory.
**Answer:** The crude figure (6, low) flatters it. Every farmable km² must feed 10 people and every cultivated km² carries 12 farming people — workable, but far tighter than the crude number promised.
