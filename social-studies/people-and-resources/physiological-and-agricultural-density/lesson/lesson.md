# Physiological and Agricultural Density

## Do it first

Remember the woreda from the last lesson: 10,000,000 people, 2,000 km², crude density 5,000 per km².

Now ask the question the crude measure cannot answer: **how much of those 2,000 km² can actually grow food?**

If the answer is "only a part of it," the people are really standing on that part alone. This lesson builds the two measures that count honestly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Physiological density

**Physiological density = total population ÷ farmland area**

Everyone eats — so the top number is *all* the people. But the bottom number is only the land that can feed them: the **farmland**.

Work the book's zone problem. A zone holds **20,000,000 people**. Its whole area is **5,000 km²**, and its **farmland is 20%** of that.

Step 1 — find the farmland: 20% of 5,000 km² = **1,000 km²**.

Step 2 — divide: 20,000,000 ÷ 1,000 = **20,000 people per km² of farmland**.

Now judge it. The zone's crude density was 20,000,000 ÷ 5,000 = 4,000 per km² — high already. But its physiological density says every square kilometre of farmland must feed **20,000 people**. That is enormous pressure: the food supply is strained to breaking unless food comes in from elsewhere or the farmland is worked far beyond ordinary yields.

That is what physiological density is *for*: **judging the food pressure on the land that feeds people**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Agricultural density

**Agricultural density = rural population ÷ cultivated land**

Now narrow both numbers. The top number is only the **rural population** — the farming people themselves. The bottom number is only the land **actually cultivated** — ploughed and planted now, not merely farmable.

Worked for a kebele the same way: count the kebele's rural people, measure its cultivated land, divide. The answer tells you how crowded the farmers are on their own fields — how thinly the cultivated land is shared among the people who work it. A high agricultural density means many farming people on little cultivated land: small plots, hard lives, and pressure to cultivate more.

Physiological asks: *can the farmland feed everyone?* Agricultural asks: *how crowded are the farming people on the land they actually work?*

## Sub lesson 3 — Judging food supply: the three densities together

Now the closing problem — one territory, all three measures.

A territory holds **600,000 people** on **100,000 km²**. **20% of the people are urban** (so 80% are rural). **40% of the land is crop-covered** (cultivated). **60% of the land is farmable**.

**Crude density:** 600,000 ÷ 100,000 = **6 people per km²** — the low band. The territory looks almost empty.

**Physiological density:** farmable land = 60% of 100,000 = 60,000 km². 600,000 ÷ 60,000 = **10 people per km² of farmable land**.

**Agricultural density:** rural people = 80% of 600,000 = 480,000. Cultivated land = 40% of 100,000 = 40,000 km². 480,000 ÷ 40,000 = **12 rural people per km² of cultivated land**.

Now the judgement. The crude figure (6) flatters the territory — it spreads the people over mountains, water and waste. The truer picture: every farmable square kilometre must feed 10 people, and every cultivated square kilometre carries 12 farming people. The food supply is workable but not roomy — far tighter than "6 per km²" promised. **That is why geographers keep all three measures: each one answers a different question, and only together do they tell the truth.**

## Say it to your companion

Say the two new formulas from memory, and what each is for: physiological — all people on farmland, judging food pressure; agricultural — rural people on cultivated land, judging the farmers' crowding. Then your companion gives you the closing problem's three answers — 6, 10, 12 — and you say which measure each one is.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose a territory you know — your kebele, woreda or zone. Get or estimate its total people, its area, its farmable share and its rural share.
2. Work all three densities for it, showing each step.
3. Write the judgement in two sentences: what does the crude figure claim, and what do the sharper two say about its food supply?
