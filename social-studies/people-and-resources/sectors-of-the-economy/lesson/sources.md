Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 3 Ciencias Sociales 3° Primaria): economic activity is taught through the campo–ciudad contrast — the countryside produces (agriculture, livestock) and the city buys, sells, banks and provides services.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 5 Ciencias Sociales 5° Primaria): the sectors are taught twice — once as a general classification (including a quaternary sector of technology and research) and once through the Dominican economy itself, province by province and industry by industry, with larimar, tobacco and Punta Cana tourism as national examples.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 12 Ciencias Sociales 6° Secundaria): the school-leaving book ends with the national economy measured — GDP, openness, the balance of payments and per-capita income are all taught on Central Bank tables for 2010–2022, and the unit's own conclusion is critical: GDP per capita doubled while the average worker's income stagnated, so other sectors appropriated the increase.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 7 Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 7 teaches the country's own economic story outright — land reform as redistribution for economic empowerment, human capital built through VTCs and colleges, self-reliance through cooperatives and vending, and tourism counted in foreign currency and jobs — and its financial-literacy unit reaches gross and net profit and the profit-and-loss account.
