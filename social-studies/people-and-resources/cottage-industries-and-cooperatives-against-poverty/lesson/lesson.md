# Cottage industries and cooperatives against poverty

## Do it first

A weaver at a home loom employs herself, her brother and a neighbour — no factory, no investor, no vacancy ever advertised. Is this employment? Is it a poverty exit?

## Sub lesson 1 — Cottage industries — production at home

Cottage industries are small, home-based, low-technology production units — in Ethiopia weaving, leather work, pottery, basketry and blacksmithing, plus farm and household equipment. They need little investment, use labour-intensive methods, and employ people — including the self-employed, men and women — whom factories never reach.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cooperatives — the same role at scale

Modern cooperatives such as coffee unions play the same role at larger scale, carrying smallholders into better markets than any one farmer could reach alone. Both routes lift incomes where formal jobs do not exist — production-side exits from poverty.
