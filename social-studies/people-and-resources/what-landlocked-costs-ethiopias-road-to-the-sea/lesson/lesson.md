# What landlocked costs — Ethiopia's road to the sea

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: double a landlocked country's transport costs — what happens to its trade? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The transport penalty

Transport costs bite landlocked trade hard: doubling them cuts trade volume by about 80 percent, and tourism demand up to eightfold. Every kilometre to the sea is taxed by distance itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dependence and charges

A landlocked state depends totally on transit neighbours' infrastructure — Burundi's poor Central Corridor, the Central African Republic's seasonally impassable routes. Transit and customs charges pile up, payable in hard currency, and border delays of 24 to 48 hours are normal; Kampala to Mombasa can take up to 21 days, sometimes 60.

## Sub lesson 3 — Politics, and Ethiopia's case

Weak negotiating power and routes cut by conflict complete the exposure: Bolivia against Chile, Armenia, and Ethiopia's loss of Assab and Massawa after 1997, leaving its trade on a Djibouti route that does not meet its demand. Easing examples exist: SADC and COMESA common warrants, and Bhutan's customs arrangement.
