# Video script — What landlocked costs — Ethiopia's road to the sea

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Transport costs bite landlocked trade hard: doubling them cuts trade volume by about 80 percent, and tourism demand up to eightfold.

A landlocked state depends totally on transit neighbours' infrastructure — Burundi's poor Central Corridor, the Central African Republic's seasonally impassable routes.

Weak negotiating power and routes cut by conflict complete the exposure: Bolivia against Chile, Armenia, and Ethiopia's loss of Assab and Massawa after 1997, leaving its trade on a Djibouti route that does not meet its demand.
