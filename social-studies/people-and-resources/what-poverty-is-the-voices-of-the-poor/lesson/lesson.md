# What poverty is — the voices of the poor

## Do it first

Before any definition: what would poor people themselves say poverty is? Write three sentences in a poor person's voice — then compare with the real voices in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The poor describe it themselves

The unit opens with the poor's own words: a Ugandan woman — no say in public, no food, no progress; a Brazilian group — low salaries, no medicine, food or clothes; a rural Ethiopian group — the young must migrate or join the army to escape hunger. Poverty, in their mouths, is far more than low income.

## Sub lesson 2 — Six dimensions

Six dimensions recur: hunger at the bottom; psychological wounds — powerlessness, shame, voicelessness; missing infrastructure; the ambivalent hope of schooling; dreaded ill-health; and asset management, not income, as the daily focus.

## Sub lesson 3 — The World Bank definition

The World Bank (2000) definition anchors the section: poverty is pronounced deprivation in wellbeing. Short enough to memorise, wide enough to hold all six dimensions — the course's working definition.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson — What poverty is — the voices of the poor — to your companion in your own words: what it is, the one fact you would never want to forget from it, and one example from your own surroundings or from Ethiopia. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
