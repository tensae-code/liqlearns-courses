# Hands-on — Water, Air, Forest, Wildlife, Soil

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The two-tray erosion test
Fill two trays (or shallow boxes) with the same soil, sloped the same way. Cover one tray's soil with grass, leaves or a cloth pierced with holes; leave the other bare. Pour the same cup of water over each, slowly, from the same height. Catch what runs off. Which tray lost more soil? Which runoff water is browner? Say why.

## The five-resources walk
Walk your street or school grounds and find one real sign of each trouble: dirty water, smoky air, a cut stump, a place where wild animals used to be seen, soil washed into a ditch. Note where each sign is — this is your locality's trouble map.

## A remedy you can do
Pick the trouble nearest your home and carry out one small remedy within the week: clear waste from a spring's edge, plant one tree, or spread grass or stones on a washing patch of soil. Write two sentences: what you did, and what it protects.
