# Lesson plan — Water, Air, Forest, Wildlife, Soil

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Water, Air, Forest, Wildlife, Soil
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the third lesson of the title, after Kinds of Natural Resources (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** five great resources — water, air, plants and forests, wildlife, and soil — what each one gives, and the main trouble each one faces: pollution for water and air, depletion for forests, falling numbers for wildlife, erosion for soil.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Kinds of Natural Resources — the learner can already sort resources and state the depletion warning. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture

shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Water and air** — life is impossible without either; the main trouble of both is pollution.
2. **Plants and forests** — food, fuel and building wood; forests are being heavily depleted in Ethiopia and the world.
3. **Wildlife** — scientific, economic and beauty value; numbers are falling, and Ethiopia's own walia ibex and mountain nyala are endangered.
4. **Soil and erosion** — soil is the living/non-living mixture of the crust's top; its main trouble is erosion — rain washing the soil away.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Kinds of Natural Resources — its depletion warning is this lesson's key. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five resources and what each one gives.
2. Name each resource's main trouble: pollution, pollution, depletion, falling numbers, erosion.
3. Say why the walia ibex and the mountain nyala are named with care.
4. Explain erosion: rain washing soil off bare land, and why covered land holds its soil better.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For the hands-on: two trays or boxes, soil, and a cup of water.

1. **Do it first** — the learner names what their household took from water, air, plants, animals and soil today, before any trouble is named (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the troubles up → anchor each to its picture panel: water and air share one trouble (pollution); the forest's trouble is being used up (depletion); wildlife's is falling numbers; soil's is being carried away (erosion).
- Thinks soil is just dead dirt → soil is a mixture, living and non-living together — that is why losing its top layer loses the part plants feed on.
- Cannot say why erosion matters → run the two GIFs again: the soil that washes away in one storm took ages to form.
