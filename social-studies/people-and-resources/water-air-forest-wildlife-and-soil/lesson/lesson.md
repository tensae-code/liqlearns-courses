# Water, Air, Forest, Wildlife, Soil

## Do it first

Think about today — just today. What did your household take from the **water**? From the **air**? From **plants**? From **animals**? From the **soil**?

Write the list. Five resources carry your whole day. Now this lesson asks the hard question for each one: what trouble is it in?

[Picture 11153 — five resources, five troubles]

## Sub lesson 1 — Water and air

**Water.** Life is impossible without it — drinking, washing, watering the fields, the life of rivers and lakes. Its main trouble is **pollution**: waste and dirt poured into the water until the water itself can sicken the people and animals who need it.

**Air.** Life is impossible without it, too — every breath. Its main trouble is also **pollution**: smoke and fumes mixing into the air of towns and factory places.

Two resources, one trouble. When you hear "pollution," think first of water and air.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plants and forests

**Plants and forests** give three great gifts: **food** to eat, **fuel** to cook with, and **wood to build** with.

Their trouble is **depletion** — they are being heavily cut down and used up, in Ethiopia and across the world. A forest is renewable, but remember the warning from the last lesson: taken faster than it grows back, it finishes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Wildlife

**Wildlife** — the wild animals — carries three kinds of value: **scientific** value (they teach us about life), **economic** value (they draw travellers and income), and the value of their **beauty**.

Their trouble: their **numbers are falling**. And Ethiopia holds treasures found almost nowhere else — the **walia ibex** and the **mountain nyala** — and both are **endangered**: if their numbers fall much further, they are gone from the Earth for good.

## Sub lesson 4 — Soil and erosion

**Soil** is the top layer of the Earth's crust — a mixture of **living and non-living** things together: broken rock and sand, mixed with the remains of plants and the small living things inside it. Everything farmed grows out of it.

Its main trouble is **erosion**: water washing the soil away.

[GIF 11161 — rain washing soil off a bare slope]

Watch the rain strike the bare slope. Little channels — rills — open in the surface, deepen, and join into muddy streams. The soil is leaving the hill.

[GIF 11162 — one storm carrying the topsoil away]

One hard storm can do it: the dark, rich **topsoil** — the layer plants feed on — is carried off in the brown water, and a field that fed a family is left thin and poor. Covered land — grass, trees, crops holding the ground — loses far less. Bare land loses most.

For every resource in this lesson, the book's way is the same: look at your own locality, name the **cause** of the trouble near you, and name the **remedy** people there can carry out.

## Say it to your companion

Five resources — name each one's trouble without looking: water, air, plants and forests, wildlife, soil. Then your companion picks any one, and you say what it gives and one remedy for its trouble near your home.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the five panels for your own locality: water, air, plants, wildlife, soil.
2. In each panel write: one thing it gives your household, and the trouble it faces near you.
3. Pick the panel whose trouble is worst near you. Write its cause, and one remedy people there could actually carry out.
