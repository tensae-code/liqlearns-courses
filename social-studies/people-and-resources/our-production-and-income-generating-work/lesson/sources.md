**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Nepal, Uganda, Eritrea, Honduras).**

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 np-g4b): The newer (2078 BS) trial book reorganises the same ground by units ending in "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth): Earth as our common home, our needs from the Earth, our local level, and drawing a map / making a map of our village; economic life is taught through production, income-generating work and the haat bazaar (periodic market).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Primary 4 Home-Study Social Studies): Economics enters through the district — the work people around the child do, how a child adds to family income, and the services the government brings to the district; this placement keeps that way: a child's own work — crops, goats, chickens, selling in shops — counted as a real contribution to family income.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 1 G1 Student CCSS): Economics enters in Grade 1 through family and community work — occupations tied to place and needs, and the family's productive vs reproductive work — long before any money or market content.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 7 G7 Student CCSS): Grade 7 carries the set's first real Economics block — it starts from the community economy and the Banco Central's own activity classification, then builds Honduras's national story: sectors, formal/informal work (70.8% informal per INE), indicators, poverty, globalisation and the foreign lenders behind structural adjustment.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 3 G3 Student CCSS): Grade 3 scales up from family to country — occupations and production for the whole nation, social classes by income, the production-distribution-exchange-consumption chain, and exports/imports through customs with the arancel explained.
