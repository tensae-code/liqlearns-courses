# Lesson plan — Dependency across Africa's regions

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Dependency across Africa's regions
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Population of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Age dependency measures how many young and old dependents each working-age group carries — and the book's regional figures show Sub-Saharan Africa carrying a far heavier load than Northern Africa, because its population is so much younger.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on age structure and median age — dependency is what a young population costs and promises, counted.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Young, working, old — the three groups** — How a population is split for the dependency count.
2. **Regional figures — Sub-Saharan against Northern Africa** — Where the load is heaviest.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Where Africans live — density map. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Split a population into young, working-age and old groups.
2. Define age dependency as the dependents carried per working-age hundred.
3. Compare Sub-Saharan and Northern Africa's loads and give the reason.
4. Connect the load to Africa's median age of 19.7.

## If the learner is stuck
One number explains the map: median age 19.7 — half the continent under twenty. Young populations fill the dependent column; that is why Sub-Saharan Africa's load outweighs the older north's.
