# Dependency across Africa's regions

## Do it first

Count in your own family or compound: how many children and elders are supported by how many working-age adults? That ratio has a geographic name and it differs across a continent. Guess which part of Africa carries the heaviest load — then read the figures.

## Sub lesson 1 — Young, working, old — the three groups

For the dependency count a population is split into three groups: the **young** (children), the **working-age** group, and the **old**. The young and old together are the **dependents** — those the working group supports through its labour, taxes and care. Africa is **young-dominated**: its **median age of 19.7 years** (mid-2020, as printed) against Europe's 42.5 tells the story in one pair of numbers — half of all Africans are under twenty.

**Age dependency** then counts the load: how many dependents stand behind each hundred working-age people. The younger the population, the longer its dependent column — and the heavier each worker's carried share.

## Sub lesson 2 — Regional figures — Sub-Saharan against Northern Africa

The book's **regional dependency figures** compare Africa's parts: **Sub-Saharan Africa** carries the heavy load — its very young age structure fills the dependent column — while **Northern Africa's** figures are markedly lighter, its age structure being older, with a larger working share.

Read the figures as a development fact, not a judgement: a heavy dependency load means each school, clinic and job must be shared from a smaller working base — and it also means a large working generation is coming, if it is educated and employed when it arrives.
