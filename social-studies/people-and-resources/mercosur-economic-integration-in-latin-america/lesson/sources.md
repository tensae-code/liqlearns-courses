**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Nicaragua). Argentina is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 Ciencias Sociales 8° Grado (Módulo Autoformativo, Primera Parte)): Economics arrives as "Space and Economy" for the whole continent — technology, globalisation, integration and free-trade treaties read critically from Central America's position, with the maquila and CAFTA debated from Nicaraguan experience, closing on sustainable development and quality of life.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 Geografía 9° Grado): the distance-education twin of the Grade 9 world-economy unit, organised around technology as the difference between continents — US centre-pivot irrigation against Latin America's rain-fed traditional farming — and closing with the integration stages ladder and the international bodies (down to FAO's Nicaragua country programme) that soften globalisation's impact.

Argentina's own way: a teacher's notebook, not a pupil text: each axis gives the knowledge at stake, then worked teaching proposals built on statistics, graphs, thematic maps and newspaper articles — Geography (Axis 1) is Argentina's population and quality of life today, compared across Latin America, and Mercosur integration studied through maps and a real gas-pipeline case. Economics enters primary Social Studies through Mercosur — integration is taught from children's own border experience, newspaper data and a pupil-built database on the South American gas pipeline case (Eje 3, printed pp. 111–129). This lesson stands on that teacher guide as evidence: it teaches the proposal's thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
