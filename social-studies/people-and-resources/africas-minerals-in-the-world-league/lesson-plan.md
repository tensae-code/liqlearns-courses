# Lesson plan — Africa's minerals in the world league

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Africa's minerals in the world league
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Natural Resource Base of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa holds a commanding place in the world's mineral production — South Africa's chromium, manganese and platinum lead the book's table, with tantalum (including Ethiopia's), bauxite, iron ore and others — a league table every African learner should be able to read.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the natural-resources lessons — renewable and non-renewable — narrowed to the continent's mineral wealth.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Reading the league table — who produces what** — Africa's weight in world minerals.
2. **Tantalum, bauxite, iron ore — and Ethiopia in the table** — The wider mineral cast.
3. **Why mineral wealth doesn't reach people** — The problems of mineral utilisation (Grade 10 student placement 26).
4. **Where the world's great resources lie (teacher guide — evidence)** — The MLC Unit 3 extension.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Africa's six great river basins. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name South Africa's chromium, manganese and platinum as the table's headline.
2. Name tantalum (including Ethiopia), bauxite and iron ore from the table.
3. Explain what a world production league table measures and why weight matters.
