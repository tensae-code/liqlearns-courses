# Africa's minerals in the world league

## Do it first

Name a mineral you have touched today — in a phone, a pan, a roof sheet. Where was it dug? Guess how much of the world's supply of the rarest minerals comes out of African ground. Then read the league table.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading the league table — who produces what

The book's production table ranks **Africa's weight in world minerals**, country by country and mineral by mineral. The headline is **South Africa**: its **chromium, manganese and platinum** place it among the world's leading producers — the table's strongest single-country showing across several minerals at once.

Read a league table the right way: not as trivia, but as weight. A continent that leads the world in a mineral the world cannot do without holds a real card in every negotiation about it — whether it plays that card well is the next lesson's question.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tantalum, bauxite, iron ore — and Ethiopia in the table

Beyond South Africa's three, the table carries **tantalum** — the rare metal of electronics, with **Ethiopia** among its producers — plus **bauxite** (the ore of aluminium) and **iron ore**, and others, each with its African producing countries named.

Keep Ethiopia's line in view: a home-country entry in a world league. The point of the table is not that Africa has minerals — it is **which** minerals, **where**, and **how large a share of the world** each African producer holds.
