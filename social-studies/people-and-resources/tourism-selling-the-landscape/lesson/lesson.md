# Tourism — selling the landscape

## Do it first

Visitors pay to stand where you stand free: before Lalibela, over the Simien edge. What are they buying, and what holds the trade back? Read tourism.

## Sub lesson 1 — The attractions

Ethiopia’s tourism rests on two attraction families: natural — the Simien heights, the Rift lakes, the national parks and their endemics; and cultural — the rock-hewn churches, historic sites and cultural landscapes. Few countries can sell both families at this depth.

## Sub lesson 2 — Contribution and constraints

Tourism contributes earnings, jobs and a reason to protect the attractions themselves. Its growth is constrained — by infrastructure, services and promotion among the brakes the book names in kind: the attractions are world-class, the supporting system is still building.

## Going further — Older evidence — tourism: earnings and obstacles (2011 guide)

An older guide treats tourism — movement away from normal residence for holidays, recreation, business and visits — as a service that earns foreign exchange, employs poor people, expands economic opportunity, pulls in infrastructure that also serves locals (water, transport, electricity), raises living standards and exchanges culture and knowledge. It is measured by tourist flow, foreign-exchange generation and employment creation. Its keyed obstacles: a shortage of tourist facilities, limited promotion, and a lack of professional and skilled personnel — against assets the key names as UNESCO-recognised: Axum's obelisks, Gondar's castles and the walls of Harar.
