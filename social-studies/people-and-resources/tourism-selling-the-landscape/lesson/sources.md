Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Nauru's own way: Nauru uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisors Fay Itaia & Sharon Kam for Nauru) — Geography is taught as ocean stewardship: the Pacific as a connected "sea of islands", its animals, and the traditional fishing knowledge of Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue, ending in a plastic-waste action plan. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: Tuvalu uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisor Valisi Tovia for Tuvalu) — and Tuvalu itself is a case study inside it: Tuvalu's own traditional fishing and bird-catching (printed p. 14) is one of the three country fishing studies. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 3 book): the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Egypt's own way (Grade 6 Term 1): primary school ends by widening from Egypt to the Arab homeland — the 22 Arab states in their Asian (12) and African (10) wings, their shared location, relief, climate and environmental challenges, then the region's economic development (agriculture with its natural and human problems and state solutions such as desalination, land reclamation, dams and modern irrigation; Egypt's New Delta and Toshka projects as worked examples).

Egypt's own way (Grade 7 Term 2): Term 2 turns from Africa's nature to its people and economy — population growth worked from real birth/death-rate charts (1970–2023) with illegal migration treated through Egypt's own 2016 anti-smuggling law, then Africa's economic activities and the practical machinery of integration (trans-African highways, the AfCFTA, chambers of commerce between Egypt and African states).

Egypt's own way (Grade 11): a whole secondary book on development geography alone, built as concept → resources → economy → people: the idea of development traced as it widened (economic, then human, then sustainable development, with its principles of comprehensiveness, integration and sustainability), resources always paired with how to manage them, and the human-development unit closing with full worked country models (Malaysia profiled in depth — position, poverty fall, education, health, electronics and car industries — and Brazil) before the future of development in the Arab homeland.
