**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Nauru, Tuvalu, Bolivia, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Egypt, Malaysia, Sudan, Syria, Kuwait, Afghanistan, Moldova, Jordan). Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — and Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

Pairing: the teacher guide was first published in 2022 and the student textbook in 2023, with the same writers and the same eight units in the same order; the student book is the learner text of record.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 10, Secundaria 4): Secundaria 4 is the nineteenth-century history year, and its geography is the geography of what Bolivia lost and what it lives from: the Atacama Litoral and the sea it no longer has, Potosí's silver and the tin north, the Amazon rubber boom, and a closing resources unit that runs from natural-resource classification to Vivir Bien and sustainable development.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Egypt's own way (Grade 11): a whole secondary book on development geography alone, built as concept → resources → economy → people: the idea of development traced as it widened (economic, then human, then sustainable development, with its principles of comprehensiveness, integration and sustainability), resources always paired with how to manage them, and the human-development unit closing with full worked country models (Malaysia profiled in depth — position, poverty fall, education, health, electronics and car industries — and Brazil) before the future of development in the Arab homeland.

Malaysia's own way (MY T3 Geografi Tingkatan 3): Form 3 turns from skills (tables, graphs, pie charts) to living Malaysia — its natural vegetation and wildlife and the human pressure on both — then outward to the world's biomes, resources and economic cooperation, closing with forest management and recycling.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 4 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the same five-strand structure as Grade 3, widening the child's world from the local area to the district — district features, renewable versus non-renewable resources, and map scale. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 4 Social Studies G4): the whole primary book is one-country geography of Syria — nature, then life (Badia, countryside, city), then work (agriculture, industry, trade, tourism) — with map tools (map symbols, four directions) and a tree-cutting/environment strand built into the opening unit's goals.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 11 Geography (Dari)): an economic-geography year in two halves — first Afghanistan (farming and livestock, energy and mines, industries and trade), then the same themes for the world (water and farming, energy, mines and industries, trade and tourism), each Afghanistan chapter closing with a "review of the situation" lesson on the country's own state.
