# Renewable, non-renewable — and using sustainably

## Do it first

Look around you and name five things you are using that came out of the ground, the sky or a living thing. For each one ask: when it is used up, does more come — or is that the end of it? Then read.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a resource is

A **natural resource** is what people extract and use from an **environment they did not make**. The sunlight, the wind, the soil, the minerals in the ground — people did not make any of them; people take from them and use them. That taking-and-using is what makes something a resource.

## Sub lesson 2 — Renewable and non-renewable

Resources split into two kinds by one test: does the amount renew, or is it fixed? **Renewable** resources replenish as fast as they are used — **sunlight** and **wind** are the book's examples; using them today does not shrink tomorrow's supply. **Non-renewable** resources exist in **fixed amounts** — **petroleum** and **coal** are the book's examples; every barrel and every lump used is gone, and no new supply is being made on any timescale that matters to us.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ecosystem services and sustainable use

The same pages introduce **ecosystem services** — the work living systems do that people depend on — and with them the rule of **sustainable use**: use a resource no faster than it can renew itself. A renewable resource treated carelessly can still be destroyed by over-use; a non-renewable one can only be husbanded. Sustainable use is the habit that keeps both kinds serving the future as well as the present.
