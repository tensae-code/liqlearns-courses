# Von Thünen's rings — farming around a city

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — The isolated state

The first location theory (1826, The Isolated State) imagines a uniform plain around one market city. On that plain, land use falls into rings — not by soil, but by distance.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rings in order

Nearest the city: intensive dairying and vegetables, too perishable to travel. Then firewood and timber forest. Then extensive grain fields. Then ranching, whose animals transport themselves. Last, wilderness. The engine is locational rent: it falls as distance and transport cost rise.

## Sub lesson 3 — Assumptions and the surviving truth

The model assumes an isolated state, a uniform plain and equally rational farmers — none fully true. Its surviving truth stands in every city: land still costs more nearer the centre.
