# Kinds of unemployment

## Do it first

Before you read, think of two people without work: one who quit to start something of their own, and one who searches every day and finds nothing. Are they the same kind of unemployed? Say your answer first — the lesson draws the line exactly there.

## Sub lesson 1 — What employment means

Employment is defined first: willing work at the prevailing wage — being ready and able to work for the wage the market offers. Only against that definition can joblessness be sorted honestly, because not every person without a job stands in the same relation to work.

[Picture 21965 — two jobless figures — one choosing, planning a small business; one searching, asking at a gate]

## Sub lesson 2 — Voluntary unemployment

Voluntary unemployment is joblessness by choice: the book's example is a person quitting a job to start a small business of their own. The person could work but has chosen, for a time, not to take the wage offered — a decision, not a defeat.

## Sub lesson 3 — Involuntary — and who counts

Involuntary unemployment is the other kind: willing to work at the prevailing wage, yet without work. Around the line stand two working words: employability — having the skills and fitness work requires — and active job-seeking, the search that shows willingness is real. Policy treats the kinds differently because they are different.
