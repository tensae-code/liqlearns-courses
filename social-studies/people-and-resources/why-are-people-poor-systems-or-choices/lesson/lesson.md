# Why are people poor — systems or choices?

## Do it first

Pick one poor household you know of. Write one system reason and one choice reason for its poverty. Which reason explains more? Hold your verdict until the end of the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The structural account

The structural account says poverty is produced by systems. Labour-market failure and low wages trap workers; underinvestment in education, health care and social insurance leaves families one shock from disaster.

Deeper structures are named too: patriarchy, capitalism and racial privilege. On this account, the poor are poor because the arrangements around them make and keep them poor — change the systems, and poverty changes.

## Sub lesson 2 — The behavioural account — and the asset lens

The behavioural or cultural account says the opposite: a culture of poverty, in which bad, self-defeating decisions perpetuate deprivation across generations. The book notes this account is favoured by conservative and libertarian writers.

Between the two stands the unit's asset lens: poverty's determinants run through human assets (labour capacity, skills, health), demography, education and policy. Assets are where systems and choices meet — systems decide what assets people can build; choices decide what they do with them.
