# Lesson plan — Africa's economic promise and its hurdles

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Africa's economic promise and its hurdles
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Major Economic and Cultural Activities of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's about 1.3 billion people, large working-age population and rich resources promise inclusive growth — held back by the named hurdles of the slave trade, colonialism, conflict and poor policy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the employment-structure lesson and beside the poverty-trap lesson of the same unit.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The promise — people and resources** — About 1.3 billion people, a large working-age population and rich resources.
2. **The hurdles — the named historical burdens** — Slave trade, colonialism, conflict and poor policy.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Employment structure — reading an economy. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give Africa's population figure as the book states it and name the promise's parts.
2. Name the four hurdles the book's activity names.
3. Explain how the promise and the hurdles can both be true.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else, beyond what hands-on.md names for its activities.
