# Africa's economic promise and its hurdles

## Do it first

Africa is often described in two opposite ways — very rich, and very poor. Before you read, write down what could make both descriptions true at once. Then read the book's own balance.

## Sub lesson 1 — The promise — people and resources

The promise is real, and it is numbered. Africa holds **about 1.3 billion people**, including a **large working-age population**, and **rich human and natural resources**. On those endowments, inclusive growth should be possible across the continent's low- to high-income states: people to work, learn and build, and resources to build with.

## Sub lesson 2 — The hurdles — the named historical burdens

Against the promise stand the hurdles the book's activity names: the **slave trade**, which drained people for centuries; **colonialism**, which organised economies for extraction; **conflict**, which destroys what is built; and **poor policy**, which wastes what remains. These are the named historical burdens that hold Africa's economies back. The lesson of the balance is not that the promise is false — it is that the hurdles are specific, named things, and named things can be faced.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the promise in its three parts and the four hurdles, in the book's order.

3. Say in two sentences why a rich continent can still be poor.
