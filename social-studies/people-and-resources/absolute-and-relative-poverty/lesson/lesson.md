# Absolute and relative poverty

## Do it first

Two neighbours: one has no food or shelter; the other eats and is housed, but stands far below everyone on the street. Are both poor? The answer needs two different words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Absolute poverty — below subsistence

Absolute poverty is the inability to meet subsistence essentials — food, clothing, shelter — counted against lines like $1.25 or $2 a day in PPP terms. It is framed as a failure of basic human dignity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Relative poverty — below your society

Relative poverty is standing far below others in one's society while still meeting basic needs — a failure of distributive justice. It is most visible where absolute poverty is rare: the poverty of comparison, not of survival.

## Sub lesson 3 — Who the poor are

The poor worldwide are disproportionately rural — about 80% in Africa and Asia — agricultural, female, children, and from minority or indigenous groups. Any poverty count that ignores where poverty concentrates will aim its policies wrong.
