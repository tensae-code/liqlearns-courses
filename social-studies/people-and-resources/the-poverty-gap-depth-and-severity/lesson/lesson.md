# The poverty gap — depth and severity

## Do it first

Two towns both count 24% poor. In one the poor sit just below the line; in the other, far below it. The headcount calls them equal. Your task: invent the number that separates them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The total poverty gap

The headcount says nothing about depth. The total poverty gap, TPG, sums how far below the line every poor person falls — read literally, it is the income that would lift all of them exactly to the line, no more.

[Picture 25221 — people in a pit of different depths under a poverty line]

## Sub lesson 2 — The average gap

The average poverty gap divides the total gap by the whole population. It spreads the depth across everyone, poor and non-poor, so towns of different sizes can be compared on depth as the headcount compares them on count.

## Sub lesson 3 — The FGT family — weighting the poorest

The Foster-Greer-Thorbecke family of measures generalises both, and can weight the poorest most heavily — capturing severity and inequality among the poor themselves. One family, three questions: how many, how deep, how unequal among the poor.
