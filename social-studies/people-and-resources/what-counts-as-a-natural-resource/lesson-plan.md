# Lesson plan — What counts as a natural resource

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** What counts as a natural resource
**Place in the curriculum:** The Land and Air climb of the Social Studies course (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** natural resources are the useful materials of the environment, classed as renewable or non-renewable and as biotic or abiotic — and Ethiopia’s heavy pressure on them frames their study.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands at the opening of the natural-resource unit.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Meaning and first classification** — useful, renewable or not
2. **Second classification, and the pressure** — biotic or abiotic

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lessons before this one in the same title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define natural resource.
2. Give both classifications.
3. State Ethiopia’s pressure fact.
