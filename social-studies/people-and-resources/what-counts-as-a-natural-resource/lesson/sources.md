Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 10, Secundaria 4): Secundaria 4 is the nineteenth-century history year, and its geography is the geography of what Bolivia lost and what it lives from: the Atacama Litoral and the sea it no longer has, Potosí's silver and the tin north, the Amazon rubber boom, and a closing resources unit that runs from natural-resource classification to Vivir Bien and sustainable development.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 12, Sexto Año): The final year opens with the census itself — the censista's work as civic-geographic practice — and later teaches geopolitics critically: Haushofer and geopolitical thought, geographic scales, and natural resources as a product of human knowledge (Sunkel), alongside Bolivia's own resource politics: the Gas War, hydrocarbon nationalisation and the urea plant that industrialises gas.

Searchable tags: natural resources, renewable, non-renewable, biotic, abiotic, social studies, Jamaica, curriculum guide evidence, Bolivia, Ciencias Sociales Bolivia

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism.

Samoa's own way (Year 10, Book 3): Access to resources is split into economic and social factors with Pacific examples, and development is taught as change that can bring conflict in places — ending in consensus-building and appropriate technology for small island states.

India's own way (Grade 8 Resources and Development): Grade 8 is a resources year, world-first but India-anchored throughout: resources and their conservation, then land/soil/water/vegetation, minerals and power, agriculture, industries and human resources — every world pattern is paired with its Indian distribution map and an Indian case study (Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad textiles, Bangalore IT, a farm in India set against a farm in the USA).

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 8 Social Science): Year 8 turns fully outward — "Exploring the World": world climate zones read from Fiji's tropical position, then global warming, resource management (including money and time) and globalisation from a Fiji/Pacific viewpoint.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 3 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Social Science is organised in five strands (Community and Organization; Trade; Culture and Society; Environment and Resources; History and Origins) taught through guided lessons that start outdoors in the school grounds — children observe, draw and label the features, resources and maps of their own local community first. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Social Studies G6 T1): Kuwait's intermediate Social Studies is organised as four "learning units" per term, each with its own field label (Geography / History / Civics) printed in the unit's standards page, and every unit closes with a project and a full assessment. Term 1 of Grade 6 takes the whole Arab Gulf as its region — Kuwait first among equals: Unit 1 (Geography) covers the natural features of Kuwait and the Gulf States, Unit 2 (History) the civilizational features, Unit 3 (Geography/History) population origins and migrations, Unit 4 (Geography) the old economic activities; a values unit ("Stations with Good Kuwait") and a maps appendix close the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 2 Lessons 2–5: the historical importance of the Gulf's location and the Dilmun, Tarut, Hili and Magan civilizations, printed pp. 47–50; civilizations in Kuwait (Ubaid at Al-Subiya, Hellenistic Failaka), pp. 50–52; Islamic civilization in the Gulf region, pp. 52–55; Islamic civilization in Kuwait (Kazma, Umm al-Aish, Al-Shuqaya; Dar al-Athar al-Islamiyya), pp. 55–57. History — Unit 3 Lessons 3–5: the Utub migration and the founding of Kuwait, pp. 68–70; the naming of Kuwait and the choice of Al Sabah, pp. 70–73; the founding of the other Gulf States, pp. 74–76. Values/Civics — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": social solidarity (the Failaka story), pp. 101–104; patience, pp. 104–106; visiting the sick, pp. 106–108; the gift of water (Sabil bin Duaij), pp. 108–110.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 2 Conociendo mi Mundo G2): Geography begins hyper-locally — the child's own school, municipality and landscape — and the country is introduced through its two great lakes (Cocibolca and Xolotlán) as the "land of lakes and volcanoes" before any wider world content. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit II family, hygiene and food (printed pp. 9–32, home/health); Unit IV plant/animal/human-body topics (printed pp. 57–93, Natural Science); Unit V national symbols, heroes and traditions (printed pp. 113–143, Civics/History); Unit VI taxes (printed p. 151, Civics); efemérides throughout (History/Civics).
