# The faces of poverty — absolute, relative, persistent

## Do it first

Two households earn the same money in two different countries; one is called poor, the other not. Write how that can be fair. Your answer needs this lesson's second definition.

## Sub lesson 1 — Absolute and relative

Poverty is deficiency in basic needs, and it is measured several ways. Absolute poverty: household income below the level that secures food, drinking water, education and healthcare. That line is set by need, and it does not move when national income grows.

Relative poverty is measured against your own society: households receiving about 50% less than average household income, lacking what their society counts as normal — internet, clean clothes, a safe healthy home. As the economy shifts, the relative line shifts with it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Persistent — and the wider dimensions

Persistent poverty is the relative shortfall lasting: that same gap in two of three years. It carries the deepest long-term damage, because a family cannot plan, borrow or build its way out of a gap that never closes.

Around the three measures sit the wider dimensions the unit maps: health care and status, education, income security, opportunity and empowerment. Poverty is counted in money, but it is lived in all of these at once.
