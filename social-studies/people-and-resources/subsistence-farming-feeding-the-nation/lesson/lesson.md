# Subsistence farming — feeding the nation

## Do it first

Most Ethiopian farms grow first for the family pot, not the market. Small, rain-fed, crops and cattle together. Read subsistence farming closely — it feeds a nation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Its character

Subsistence production is smallholder farming: family plots, worked by the family. It is rain-fed — dependent on the seasons’ rains, with little irrigation. And it is mixed: crops and livestock together on the same holding, each supporting the other. Its character is described region by region across the country.

[Picture 21099 — a mixed smallholder farm — crops, oxen, enset, coffee]

## Sub lesson 2 — Its contribution

This farming feeds the farm households themselves and, through what reaches markets, the country. The backbone of the economy is not estates or schemes; it is millions of subsistence smallholders, whose harvests decide whether Ethiopia eats.
