# Video script — Quintiles, deciles and the Kuznets ratio

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The book's table ranks 20 households from 0.8 to 15.0 income units, total 100, into quintiles — fifths of the population. The bottom 20% receive 5% of income; the second quintile 9%. So the bottom 40% hold 14% while the top 20% hold 51%.

The Kuznets ratio divides the top 20%'s share by the bottom 40%'s: 51/14 ≈ 3.64. Its inverse, the inequality ratio, is 14/51 ≈ 0.27 — the bottom's share as a fraction of the top's. Two readings of one gap: how many times more, or how small a fraction.

Deciles — tenths — sharpen the picture: the bottom 10% receive 1.8% and the top 10% receive 28.5%. And the single richest household, the top 5%, takes 15% — more than the bottom 40% combined (14%). The finer the slice, the sharper the inequality shows.
