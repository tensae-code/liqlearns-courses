**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Kuwait).**

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Social Studies G6 T2): Term 2 turns from the region's nature (Term 1) to its people and its wealth. Unit 1 is History-led — colonialism to independence — with a single Geography lesson on the economic importance of the Gulf's location; Unit 2 (Geography/Civics) covers population growth, the population-structure imbalance and cultural similarity; Unit 3 (Geography) covers the economic resources — energy, agriculture, animal and fish wealth, industry and trade — and the challenges of development; Unit 4 (Civics) covers GCC cooperation. The same architecture as Term 1: a standards page per unit, lessons, a unit project and a full assessment, a values unit ("Stations with Good Kuwait") and a maps appendix. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 1 Lesson 2, European colonial rivalry over the Gulf (Portuguese, Dutch, British), printed pp. 21–23; Lesson 3, the 1899 protection agreement between Kuwait and Britain, pp. 24–25; Lesson 4, the independence of Kuwait (19 June 1961) and the Gulf States, pp. 26–28. Civics — Unit 1 Lesson 5, the development of political life in Kuwait: the 1962 constitution, sovereignty, democracy, human rights, women's political rights, pp. 29–31. ICT/digital skills (outside this wave's scope) — Unit 2 Lesson 4, the digital culture of the Gulf person: artificial intelligence and its uses, cybersecurity and Kuwait's national cybersecurity centre, pp. 49–51. Civics/media — Unit 2 Lesson 5, cultural awareness: cultural invasion, social media's benefits and harms, the cybercrime law, pp. 52–53. Civics — Unit 4 Lesson 1, the Gulf Cooperation Council: six member states, founded 25 May 1981 in Abu Dhabi, headquarters Riyadh, pp. 82–83; Lesson 2, joint security and defence cooperation (Peninsula Shield), pp. 84–85; Lesson 4, joint cultural and educational cooperation (Arab Bureau of Education, Riyadh; Joint Programme Production Institution, Kuwait), pp. 89–91; Lesson 5, future aspirations of the GCC states and Kuwait Vision 2035, pp. 92–94. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": Bu Tbeila, the Ramadan drummer of old Kuwait, pp. 100–101; etiquette of sitting in the diwaniya, pp. 102–103; a helping hand and a reassured heart, pp. 104–105; a kind word and a gentle reply, pp. 106–107. The maps appendix (p. 108) is present only as instructions for fitting a transparent sleeve over the appendix maps.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T2): Term 2 turns from the Arab homeland's nature (Term 1) to its people, its wealth, its problems and its futures, each unit fronted by a guide character — the Architect/Engineer leads Unit 2, the Crisis Observer Unit 3, the Future Pioneer Unit 4. Unit 1 (Geography) covers population in Kuwait and the Arab world; Unit 2 (Geography) resources and wealth; Unit 3 contemporary challenges — natural, political, social and economic problems, and Kuwait's own; Unit 4 visions and ambitions — national visions, Kuwait Vision 2035, future projects, the digital future and Arab integration; the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/Civics — Unit 3 Lesson 2's treatment of the Palestine question (from 1948) and of nuclear risks (weapons, radiation leaks, nuclear waste), printed pp. 54–56 — the geographic parts (Ceuta and Melilla's strategic position; the Sudan split) are placed below. Health/values — Unit 3 Lesson 3, social problems: addiction (drugs, smoking, digital) and unaware digital interaction, rumours and blind imitation, pp. 57–59. Values — Unit 3 Lesson 5's bullying section, p. 62. Civics/planning — Unit 4 Lesson 1, the concept of a national vision and the traits of a successful one, pp. 72–73; Lesson 2, Kuwait Vision 2035 (turning Kuwait into a financial and trade hub; its seven pillars) and its digital projects, pp. 74–76 — the development projects' Geography is placed below. ICT — Unit 4 Lesson 4, the digital future: digital transformation, smart government services and artificial intelligence in the Arab world (Saudi SDAIA, Jordan's Smart Desert), pp. 79–80. Civics/institutions — Unit 4 Lesson 5, Arab integration: its definition, importance and fields, and its organisations — the Arab League (Cairo), the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development (Kuwait), ALECSO (Tunis), the Arab Organization for Agricultural Development (Khartoum) — and the joint Kuwait–Oman Duqm refinery, pp. 81–82. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": a meeting of old friends in Souq Al-Mubarakiya, pp. 88–89; Nabati poetry as Kuwaiti cultural heritage (the Diwaniya of Nabati Poets), p. 90; the Kuwaiti grandmother, the beating heart of the family, pp. 92–93; the neighbour's duty — a story set in the pearl-diving season, pp. 94–96 (the heritage part is placed below).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T1): Grade 8 widens the region a second time — from the Arab homeland (Grade 7) to the whole Islamic world — and reverses the balance: two History-led units on the Islamic state's rise and civilization come first, then two full Geography units (the natural environment; its effect on economy and society), a Civics unit on law in Kuwait, and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait". Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/religion — Unit 1, a historical glimpse of the Islamic state and its civilization: the heavenly religions and the Arab world's old civilizations, printed pp. 14–15; the rise of the Islamic state and the conquests movement, pp. 16–18; the concept and foundations of Islamic civilization, pp. 19–22 (the culture definition is placed below); its characteristics — faith and work, universality, tolerance, moderation — pp. 23–24. Civics/History — Unit 2 Lesson 1, the political and administrative manifestations: the caliphate, the ministry, the governorship, the diwans and the police, pp. 30–32. History of science — Unit 2 Lesson 4, the social, cultural and scientific manifestations: society's classes by profession, the status of women, the people of the covenant in farming, industry and trade, the scientific centres (the two Holy Mosques, Al-Azhar, Baghdad's House of Wisdom) and the translation of Muslim scholars' works in Europe, pp. 40–43 (the al-Idrisi map part is placed below). Civics — Unit 5, law as the basis of stability and building society in Kuwait: the definition, rules and aims of law; law and morality; the characteristics of the legal rule, pp. 85–96. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": the founding of Al-Mubarakiya school in 1911, Kuwait's first regular school, pp. 100–101; the etiquette of replies, pp. 102–103; the fragrance of neighbourliness, pp. 104–105; "What do you say, folks?" — consultation (shura) under Sheikh Salem Al-Mubarak, pp. 106–107 (the customs part is placed below).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 9 Social Studies G9 T2): Term 2 is the citizenship-and-world book, the Civics-heaviest of the nine: global citizenship and community responsibility; the State of Kuwait and international relations; the State of Kuwait and world organisations; and Kuwait and the world facing the future's challenges, closing with the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait". Its Geography runs through culture and identity, trade and oil, food security and sustainable development rather than through landforms. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics — Unit 1 Lessons 1–2: global citizenship defined as a system of values and behaviours expressing a person's belonging to a world community beyond the homeland, its importance and goals; human values (equality, freedom, dignity, justice, cooperation, peace) and digital global citizenship, with Kuwait Vision 2035 for a safe, balanced digital society, printed pp. 14–18. Civics/law — Unit 1 Lesson 4: national belonging defined; Kuwait's COVID-19 response — repatriation flights and full care for citizens abroad, medical and financial aid to stricken states with the World Health Organization; and the National Unity Protection Law, Decree 19 of 2012, pp. 22–24. Civics/History — Unit 2 Lesson 2: political relations and their forms (diplomatic representation, treaties, conferences), with the Kuwait–Britain model from the 1899 protection agreement to independence in 1961, pp. 34–35. Civics — Unit 2 Lesson 5: Kuwait's foreign policy — diplomacy defined, its principles (respecting sovereignty, settling disputes peacefully), the international coalition that liberated Kuwait in 1991, development and humanitarian aid through the Kuwait Fund and the charities (Palestine, Syria, Darfur from 2003, Beirut after the 2020 port explosion), and the United Nations naming Kuwait a centre of humanitarian work in 2014, pp. 42–44. Civics/institutions — Unit 3: world organisations defined and typed (social-humanitarian, economic, cultural-scientific, political, environmental-health), pp. 48–49; the United Nations — founded 1945 after the League of Nations failed, charter written at San Francisco, headquarters New York, Kuwait a member from 1963, its main organs (General Assembly, Security Council of 15 with five permanent members holding the veto, the Economic and Social Council, the International Court of Justice at The Hague, the Secretariat) and its goals, pp. 50–52; the World Health Organization — founded 7 April 1948, headquarters Geneva, Kuwait a member from 1960, its Kuwait office opened 2021 and its 2025 Sheikh Sabah Al-Ahmad award to Kuwait for elderly health care, pp. 53–55; the Food and Agriculture Organization — founded 16 October 1945, headquarters Rome, World Food Day on 16 October, Kuwait a member from 1961 working with it on desert farming, food security and fish farming — and UNESCO (Paris), UNIDO (Vienna) and the ILO (Geneva), pp. 56–58. Civics/framing — Unit 4 Lesson 1: future challenges defined and typed — political, economic, intellectual-cultural and scientific-technical — with awareness, adaptation and innovation as the tools, pp. 68–69. Social — Unit 4 Lesson 4: bullying defined as repeated aggressive behaviour by a stronger party against a weaker, in physical, verbal, social and electronic forms; Kuwait's campaigns (positive-behaviour programmes, "Hand in Hand — No to Bullying") and the UN and UNICEF efforts, with the World Anti-Bullying Day on the first Thursday of November, pp. 77–79. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": Ali Bu Kahil, whose warning saved Kuwait from an approaching enemy fleet, pp. 89–90; and Sheikh Misaed Al-Azmi, who learned smallpox vaccination at Al-Azhar in Cairo and vaccinated Kuwaitis through the 1932 outbreak, making the vaccine himself, pp. 96–97 (the women's-professions and coffee stations are placed below).
