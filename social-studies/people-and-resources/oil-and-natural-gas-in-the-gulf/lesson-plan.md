# Lesson plan — Oil And Natural Gas In The Gulf

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** Oil And Natural Gas In The Gulf
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Geography expansion (Kuwait, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 3 Lesson 1, Energy Resources (Oil and Natural Gas) in the Arab Gulf States: the Gulf States rank first in the world in oil production and reserves and in natural gas reserves; oil production is the crude extracted from the ground, reserves the quantities stored in it; oil moves by pipeline networks to oil ports for export (importers include Japan, the USA and the Netherlands), and part of production is held back as a reserve for future generations; the largest fields — Al-Ghawar in Saudi Arabia, the world's largest oil field; Burgan in Kuwait, the Gulf's second largest and the first field discovered in Kuwait; the North Field in Qatar, the world's largest natural gas field; Unit 2 Lesson 1, Economic Resources in the Arab World: the Arab states produce about 26% of the world's oil and hold about 53% of its reserves, Saudi Arabia the largest exporter; natural gas is presented as clean energy, Qatar a leading producer — this widens Book 2's NEW lesson from the Gulf to the whole Arab world; Unit 2 Lesson 5, Resources and Wealth of Kuwait: oil is Kuwait's first resource, since the Bahra field, produced through the Kuwait Petroleum Corporation; natural gas from land and sea fields, including the new offshore Jazza field discovered in 2025 with low carbon dioxide; Unit 4 Lesson 1, Energy Resources (Oil and Natural Gas): the Islamic world produces 30–35% of the world's crude oil and holds about 58.5% of its reserves (OPEC report, 2024), Saudi Arabia first in production and reserves; it produces about 36% of the world's natural gas — Qatar first among liquefied-gas producers from the North Field, the world's largest gas field, shared with Iran, holder of the second-largest reserves; Kuwait depends mainly on oil, a non-renewable source open to depletion, and must diversify its income through farming, industry and other sectors — this widens Book 2's NEW lesson from the Gulf and Arab world to the Islamic world; Unit 3 Lesson 5, OPEC, a Model of an Organisation Independent of the United Nations: OPEC is the world organisation of oil-exporting states, founded in 1960 at Baghdad by five founders — Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Iran and Venezuela — after the foreign companies cut oil prices without consulting the producers; its headquarters is Vienna; it sets member production quotas to balance the market, balances supply and demand to protect producers and consumers, and channels oil revenues into development; the 2016 OPEC+ agreement brought in non-member exporters (Russia, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Oman); Kuwait is a founder member, oil is about 90% of its exports and the mainstay of its economy, and it exports its oil to China, India, Japan, South Korea, Italy, the Netherlands and the United States — this extends Book 2's NEW oil lesson from the Gulf fields to the exporters' organisation and Kuwait's markets — as Kuwait's book teaches it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
