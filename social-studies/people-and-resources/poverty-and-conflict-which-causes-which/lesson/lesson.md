# Poverty and conflict — which causes which?

## Do it first

Which way does the arrow run — does war make poverty, or poverty make war? Write your first answer. The research answer is more careful, and more interesting.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three hypotheses

Three hypotheses organise the research. First: conflict causes poverty. Destruction, displacement and collapsed states impose long-term costs and intergenerational exclusion — this is the proposition with consensus behind it.

Second: poverty causes conflict. This is the contentious one. Poverty is at most a permissive factor — it interacts with other factors at particular times and places; it is not a deterministic trigger that fires by itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cycle — and the converged response

Third: the two reinforce each other in cycles. Conflict impoverishes; impoverishment, combined with other pressures, makes further conflict more likely; and the wheel turns.

That cycle is why development work and peace work have converged on chronic poverty in conflict-affected states: you cannot treat one side of the wheel and leave the other turning.
