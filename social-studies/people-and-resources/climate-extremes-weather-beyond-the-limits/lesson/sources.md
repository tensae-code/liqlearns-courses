Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Malaysia's own way (MY T5 Geografi Tingkatan 5): the final form is the world's systems book — advanced graphs and photo interpretation, then weather and climate at world scale, vegetation and wildlife by climate zone, energy, and the environmental bill for economic activity.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T1): Grade 7 widens the lens from the Gulf (Grade 6) to the whole Arab homeland, and each unit is fronted by a guide character — the Geographer leads Unit 1, the Narrator of the Eras Unit 2, the Historian of Time Unit 3, the Witness of the Age Unit 4. Unit 1 is the Geography unit: the Arab world treated as one region — its definition and shared traits, its relative and astronomical location, its climate and climate regions, its plant and animal life, its terrain — and then Kuwait placed inside the same natural frame. Units 2–4 are a chronological History sweep (ages and civilizations; Arab-Islamic civilization; colonialism to independence), and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 2, Kuwait and the Arab world through the ages: the rise of the eras (Ice, Stone, Bronze, Iron), printed pp. 40–42; the rise of civilizations and models of the old Arab civilizations (Pharaonic Egypt, Jarma in Libya, Babylon, Saba and its Ma'rib dam), pp. 43–44; the Nabataean civilization and Petra, pp. 45–46; the Kush civilization in Nubia and Meroë, pp. 47–48; civilization stations in Kuwait — Ubaid at Al-Subiya, Dilmun and Hellenistic Ikaros on Failaka Island, pp. 49–50. History — Unit 4, between colonialism and independence: the state of the Arab world in the 18th century and European ambitions, pp. 76–77; the colonial movements and the forms and effects of colonialism, pp. 78–79; Arab resistance movements (Palestine, Libya), pp. 80–81; the independence stage in the Arab world, pp. 82–83; Kuwait from protection (1899) to independence (1961), pp. 84–85.
