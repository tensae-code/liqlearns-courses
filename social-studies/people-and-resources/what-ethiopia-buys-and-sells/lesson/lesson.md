# What Ethiopia buys and sells

## Do it first

Two stacks of cargo at the port: what leaves, what arrives. One stack is worth less than the other, year after year. Read the trade tables.

## Sub lesson 1 — Exports and imports

Ethiopia’s exports are led by farm goods — coffee above all, with oilseeds, pulses, khat and flowers. Its imports are led by fuel, machinery and manufactured goods. The two lists face each other: the country sells what its farms grow and buys what its factories do not yet make.

[Picture 21047 — two cargo stacks — imports taller than exports]

## Sub lesson 2 — The deficit

Read from the figures, the balance is a persistent trade deficit: the value of imports exceeds the value of exports year after year. The deficit is the trade face of the small factory sector — a country that must import manufactures pays for them with farm exports that do not cover the bill.
