# What holds Ethiopian farming back

## Do it first

The backbone has cracks. A tiny split-up plot, thin soil, rain that may not come, a sickle where a machine should be. Read the problems one by one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Land and soil problems

Holdings are small and fragmented — one family’s land split into scattered pieces. Soil erosion and fertility loss wash and wear the plots thinner. A farm can be hard-working and still poor when its land is small, split and eroding.

[Picture 21044 — an eroded, fragmented hillside farm]

## Sub lesson 2 — Rain, tools, pests, roads

Farming depends on rainfall and is struck by drought. Tools are backward — the book instances the sickle harvest where machines would be faster. Pests attack crops, and poor rural infrastructure — roads, storage, services — keeps harvests from markets and help from farms.
