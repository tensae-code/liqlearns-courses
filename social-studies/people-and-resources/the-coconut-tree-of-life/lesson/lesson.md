# The Coconut — Tree of Life

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the coconut — the tree of life: a sea-voyaging seed adapted to the tropics giving food, drink, oil, fibre, timber and thatch, and its life in Niue and Tonga, as Nauru's Grade 5 *People and the Natural Environment in the Pacific* teaches it on printed pp. 11 and 14. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The amazing coconut tree

The reader's second case study is the amazing coconut tree: a sea-voyaging seed, carried by the ocean and adapted to the tropics, that gives a Pacific family food, drink, oil, fibre, timber and thatch from a single tree — which is why it is called the tree of life.

## Sub lesson 2 — Coconuts in Niue and Tonga

The case study follows the coconut island by island. In Niue and Tonga it becomes fans, baskets and canoe lashings, and feeds the coconut crab; Tonga even names the tree's life in fourteen coconut growth stages — a whole culture counted out on one tree.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
