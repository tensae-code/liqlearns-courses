**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (South Sudan, Syria, Eritrea, Bahamas, Kuwait, Palestine, Jordan).** South Sudan was already counted in this lesson from the earlier waves (Primary 8 pupil's book above); the Economics-expansion Primary 4, Primary 5 and Primary 8 rows are stocked as depth for the same country, so the count does not rise.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Term 2): the modern economy is taught as resources-and-development of the Gulf states — oil and gas first, with the danger of depending on oil named from the start, then farming, livestock/fisheries, industry and trade, ending in GCC economic integration.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Term 2): Grade 7 widens from the Gulf to the whole Arab homeland — resources and sectors first, then the Arab world's economic problems (above all economic dependency), then the future: diversification, the New Kuwait vision and Arab integration.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Term 1): Grade 8 teaches economics twice over — as the economic life of the historical Islamic civilisation (irrigation, industries, trade centres, the market inspector), then as the modern Islamic world's resource base under the heading of how the natural environment shapes economy and society.

Bahamas' own way: the economy is taught through the country's real industries — tourism first, then farming and fishing as income and foreign exchange, island by island (Andros, Abaco, Long Island, Exuma, Eleuthera).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 8 P8 Social Studies SB): The climate unit is two-way and practical — how climate shapes people's activities (poor climate ruins roads) and how people adapt: use scarce water efficiently, build flood defences and raise dykes, grow drought-tolerant crops, and make biogas.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 8 P8 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide keeps climate personal and economic — which climate would you choose to live in, and why — and ties the farming system that suits a local area directly to its climatic conditions. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 4 Social Studies): Grade 4 carries a whole unit named Pillars of the Economy in Syria — the population's economic activities (agriculture, industry, trade, and services such as education, health, transport and tourism) — and agriculture stands first among the pillars: the main income of many Syrians, the raw material of its industry, and a surplus for export.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 4 Term 1): The economy is met as the country's working sectors — farming first (with Jordan's own crops and its export surplus), then industry split into extractive and manufacturing, then the energy Jordan must import or harvest from its sun and wind.
