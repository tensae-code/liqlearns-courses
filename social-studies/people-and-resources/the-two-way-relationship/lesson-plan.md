# Lesson plan — The Two-Way Relationship

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** People and Resources · **Lesson:** The Two-Way Relationship
**Place in the curriculum:** People and Resources — the first lesson of the title, on the course path after Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** people and their environment change each other — the relationship runs both ways. People farm, build and cut; the land, water and climate shape how people dress, eat, build and keep their food.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own surroundings — the river, pond, spring, soil, forest and wildlife of their own locality. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **People change the environment** — farming, building houses, roads and factories change the land, the water and the air, for better and for worse.
2. **The environment shapes people** — climate zones change how people dress and eat; the environment is everything around a living thing, in two kinds of factors: living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic). (Teacher guide — evidence.)
3. **Keeping food — how the climate decides** — drying and salting against freezing and canning: hot places spoil food fast, so the climate decides the method. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what "two-way" means here: people change the environment, and the environment changes people's lives back.
2. Name helps and harms in each direction, from their own locality's river, soil, forest and wildlife.
3. Say what the environment is made of: living (biotic) factors and non-living (abiotic) factors.
4. Explain how people keep food in the old way (drying, salting) and the new way (freezing, canning) — and why the climate chooses between them.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A walk around your own home or school grounds helps.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names the natural things of their own place — river, pond, spring, soil, forest, wildlife — and says one way people change each one and one way each one changes people's lives (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the two-way relationship; the environment's two kinds of factors; food preservation as the worked example of climate shaping people's ways (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 11150] at the two-way part: a picture of a village and its river and forest joined by two arrows running opposite ways — the student traces each arrow and says what travels along it: work and building one way, rain, soil and climate the other.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 11151] in the food part: a picture of meat and fish drying and being salted under a hot sun beside a refrigerator and sealed cans — the student says which side is the old way, which is the new way, and why hot places need one of them.
   - ADD AUDIO in the two-way part: a voice reading the helps and harms aloud — "water to drink … wood to build … cutting every tree … dirtying the river" — the student raises a hand on each harm.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the two ways with one example each from your own place (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the two arrows told in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the two-way map of your own locality (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Sees only one direction → ask: "The river gives you water. What do you give the river?" Then turn it round: "The rain decides your planting day. Who decided that — you, or the sky?"
- Mixes up biotic and abiotic → biotic is alive or once alive (other living things); abiotic never lives (temperature, water, sunlight, soil minerals).
- Thinks food drying is "just old" → ask what happens to fresh meat in a hot place with no fridge: the climate forces the method; the method is cleverness, not backwardness.
