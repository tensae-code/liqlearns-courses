## Sub lesson 1 — Coconuts in Tuvalu — toddy and bioethanol

In Tuvalu the coconut gives energy as well as food. Its sap — toddy — serves as a sugar substitute, and coconut bioethanol is a biofuel that could replace about 31% of the nation's petrol: an island's own tree standing in for imported fuel.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nauru's way — energy inside the tree of life

Nauru meets energy resources inside the coconut case study: energy is not a separate chapter of faraway fuels, but one more gift of the tree of life the class has just studied — sap and biofuel counted beside food, drink, oil, fibre, timber and thatch.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
