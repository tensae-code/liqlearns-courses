# The Adal wars — the first phase

## Do it first

Two powers, one trade road, two hundred years of fighting. The first phase of the Adal wars runs reign by reign, like blows traded across generations. Before reading: guess the cause. Is it religion alone — or is something else at stake?

## The causes

The book names two causes, and neither is 'religion' standing alone. First: **control of the Zeila trade route and its wealth** — the same road that built Ifat and Adal. Second: the **highland kingdom's drive to subjugate the sultanates**, set against the sultanates' **drive to recover land and independence**. Trade wealth pulled one way; freedom pulled the other; war followed, from **1314 to 1527**.

## Reign by reign

The fighting runs king by king. **Amda Tsion** opens it. **Seyfe Ar'ed fights Sabr al-Din** of Ifat. **Dawit** wins a string of victories: he **defeats Haqq al-Din II in 1387, Sa'ad al-Din in 1403, and Ahmad in 1430**. **Yishaq** reigns in the struggle. **Zara Yaqob fights Sultan Ahmad and Garad Mahiko of Hadiya**. Then **Ba'eda Maryam, Eskinder and Na'od** carry the wars to the phase's end. Through this whole first phase, the book's verdict is steady: **the highland kingdom was uppermost** — winning more than it lost, holding the sultanates under its hand. But uppermost is not the same as finished. In 1527 the second phase opens, and everything changes.
