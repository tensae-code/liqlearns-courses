# Lesson plan — The Gondar period — city life

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia: Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** The Gondar period — city life
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Ethiopia: Ancient and Medieval (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** At the city's heart stood the **royal compound: palaces, the abun's and the echage's residences** — the chief churchmen housed beside the kings — **and churches**, built in the **Aksum/Zagwe-like building tradition**, stone architecture reaching back a thousand years.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met the periods of Ethiopian history and the states named so far in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the royal compound (palaces, abun's and echage's residences, churches in Aksum/Zagwe-like tradition) and the learning churches (zema, qene).
2. Say Gondar traded on two routes (north Ethiopia; west to Sudan) with a daily market mixing foreign merchants, townspeople and rural folk.
3. Name the Bete Israel's crafts and the peoples Yohannes I (1667–1682) settled in quarters outside the city.
