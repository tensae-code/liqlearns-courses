# Movements of the Somali, Afar and Argoba

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Peoples of the eastern lowlands

East of the highlands, Somali, Afar and Argoba peoples moved and settled across the lowlands and escarpment in this period — herding, trading and farming the corridors between the plateau and the sea.

Their movements redrew the region's human map as surely as any king's campaign.

[Picture 23824 — a movement map of the eastern lowlands]

## Sub lesson 2 — Why peoples move

Movement had drivers: pasture and water for herds, trade openings at the ports, pressure from wars, and the pull of kin already settled. Families and clan sections moved in stages, not single marches.

Read movement as households deciding, season by season — the same settlement logic this course met in Unit 3, now in motion.

## Sub lesson 3 — Filling the corridors

By the period's end, Somali settlement reached deep along the coast and inland valleys, Afar held the salt and caravan corridors, and Argoba communities sat in escarpment towns between the worlds.

The eastern map these movements made is the board on which Harar, the sultanates and later Ethiopia all played.
