# The kawo kingdoms — Wolaita

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kings called kawo

South of the highlands, Wolaita and its neighbours were ruled by kings titled kawo — a kingship with its own court, rituals and dynastic memory, distinct from both the northern negus and the sacral tato of Kafa.

Same land, different crowns: this unit's whole point is that Ethiopia's south invented government in several designs at once.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wars of expansion

Wolaita's kawo kings expanded by war — absorbing neighbours, exacting tribute, and pushing the kingdom's borders outward across the fertile south.

Expansion made Wolaita rich and renowned; it also made it a prize its neighbours, and later the highland empire, watched hungrily.

## Sub lesson 3 — Resistance to highland pressure

As highland power pressed south in later centuries, Wolaita resisted — its kings, terrain and unity making it one of the last southern kingdoms to fall.

Hold the fact for the modern lessons: incorporation, when it came, came to a kingdom with a long memory of independence.
