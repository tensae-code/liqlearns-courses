# How the Christian Highland Kingdom was governed

## Sub lesson 1 — The negus and his court

The restored kingdom was ruled by the negus — the king of kings — whose court was the state's centre: appointing governors, judging great cases, granting land, and leading the army in war.

For centuries the capital moved with the court, camp to camp, consuming each region's surplus in turn — government as a travelling city.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gult and rist — two kinds of land right

Land held the system together. Rist was hereditary family land — the peasant's ancestral claim, passed down and defended fiercely. Gult was a grant from the king of the right to collect tribute from a district — the pay of soldiers, officials and churches.

Confuse them and nothing in this period makes sense. Rist is ownership from below; gult is authority from above — laid over the same fields.

## Sub lesson 3 — Church and state, tribute and tax

 Church and kingdom were partners: the church held great gult grants, kept learning and legitimacy, and crowned and counselled kings; the state protected and endowed the church.

Tribute flowed up from peasant fields through gult-holders to court and camp — the kingdom's fuel, and its friction when holders squeezed too hard.
