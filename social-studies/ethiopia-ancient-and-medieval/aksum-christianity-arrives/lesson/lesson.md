# Aksum — Christianity arrives

## Do it first

A new faith arrives in a kingdom. Does it stay at the palace, or does it reach the farmers? In Aksum it did both — but in two steps, more than a hundred years apart. Watch for the two steps as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ezana and Frumentius — the faith reaches the king

In the reign of **King Ezana (325–360)**, a Syrian named **Frumentius** came to Aksum. He brought the Christian faith, and **about the year 330 Ezana first accepted Christianity himself and then proclaimed it the state religion** of Aksum. Frumentius is remembered by the name **Abune Selama** — 'our father of peace'. Notice the order: the traveller arrives, the king believes, the kingdom's religion changes. But a proclamation at court does not yet change the villages.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Nine Saints — the faith reaches the people

The second step came in the **5th century**, with the **Nine Saints** — nine holy men whose names the tradition keeps in full. They travelled the country, founded monasteries and **carried Christianity to the lower classes**, the farmers and workers the palace never reached. Two great results followed. The **Bible was translated from Greek into Ge'ez**, so the faith could be read and prayed in Ethiopia's own literary language. And Aksum's **ties with the Christian world abroad grew stronger**. A faith that began with one traveller and one king now lived in the people's own language — that is why it endured.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 9 book's long arc — Frumentius to an Ethiopian Patriarch

The Grade 9 book runs Christianity's Ethiopian story its full length. It was introduced in the **early 4th century AD**: **Frumentius (Freminatos)** was consecrated by **Patriarch Athanasius of Alexandria** as **Abuna Salama, first bishop** of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church — and **Alexandria supplied Ethiopia's church head until 1959**, when **Abune Baselios became the first Ethiopian Patriarch**.

Between those poles: Christianity **expanded further under the Zagwe (1150–1270)**, with growth in **art, literature, architecture and music**. Later layers also land: the **Jesuit Catholic attempt of the 16th–17th centuries** — Susenyos's **1622** conversion and its failure — and **Protestantism's arrival mainly in the early 19th century**, spreading widely in the 20th through missions. Sixteen centuries in one sweep: a faith arriving by shipwreck and staying by rooting.
