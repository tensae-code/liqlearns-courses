# Lesson plan — Gadaa — government by age grades and eight-year succession

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** Gadaa — government by age grades and eight-year succession
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Unit 7 — States, Principalities, Population Movements and Interactions in Ethiopia, 13th–mid-16th C. (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Gadaa** is the Oromo indigenous **socio-political system of self-governance**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner having met the Oromo expansion — Gadaa is the system the expanding communities carried with them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Looks for Gadaa's king → the system's point is his absence: rotation by age grade replaces dynasty.
- Thinks eight-year succession is just an election cycle → the grades themselves are life-stages everyone passes through; office follows the grade, not a campaign.
