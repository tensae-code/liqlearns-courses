# Ethiopian history — the periods

## Do it first

Look at a long strip of paper. Mark one end 'very long ago' and the other end 'today'. Now guess: where along the strip would you put the year 1270? And 1855? Keep your guesses — this lesson gives you the two dates that divide Ethiopia's early story, and the names that fill each part.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ancient Ethiopia — to 1270

Ethiopia's history is told in periods, like chapters in one long story. The first great period is **ancient history**, and it runs up to the year **1270**. In this period you will meet the earliest named states. First comes **Punt**, the oldest named land, known from Egyptian records. Then **Da'amat**, centred at Yeha in the north. Then the kingdom of **Aksum**, strong from about the 1st century and greatest from the 4th to the 7th century — a farming and trading kingdom with stone stelae, coins, and the coming of Christianity. Last in the ancient period comes the **Zagwe** dynasty in Lasta, famous for churches cut out of solid rock at Lalibela.

## Sub lesson 2 — Medieval Ethiopia — 1270 to 1855

The second period is **medieval history**, from **1270 to 1855**. It opens in 1270 with the **Solomonic restoration**, when Yekuno Amlak took power and a new line of kings claimed descent from Solomon. The **Christian highland kingdom** — the Christian kingdom centred in the highlands — then grew under kings like Amda Tsion. Alongside it rose the **Muslim sultanates** — Dahlak, Ifat, Hadiya, Bale, Adal and others — built on trade and the Muslim faith. The two sides fought the long **Adal–highland wars**. Then came the **Oromo expansion**, the founding of **Gondar** as capital in 1636, and finally the **Zemene Mesafint**, the era when regional lords, not kings, held real power, until 1855. Learn the two boundary dates — 1270 and 1855 — and you hold the map of the whole story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two boundary dates and what each one opens or closes. Then name, in order, three states or powers from the ancient period and three from the medieval period.
