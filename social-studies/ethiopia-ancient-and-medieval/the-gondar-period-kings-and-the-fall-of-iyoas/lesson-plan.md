# Lesson plan — The Gondar period — kings and the fall of Iyoas

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia: Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** The Gondar period — kings and the fall of Iyoas
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Ethiopia: Ancient and Medieval (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Iyasu I the Great (1682–1706)** filled the golden age's last decades: he **recognised Shewa's rulers and collected yearly tribute** from them; he **revised the Fetha Nagast law book**; sick, he was **treated by the French physician Jacques Poncet (1699–1700)**; he **received three missionaries**; and he **sent seven young Ethiopian Christians as envoys to the Pope in Rome**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met the periods of Ethiopian history and the states named so far in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Iyasu I and Bakaffa** — Law revised, envoys to Rome; a marriage that shapes the next reign.
2. **Mentewab's regency and the fall of Iyoas** — A child king, a powerful grandmother, Oromigna at court — and Ras Mikael Sehul, 1769.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List Iyasu I's (1682–1706) deeds: Shewa recognised and taxed yearly, Fetha Nagast revised, treated by Jacques Poncet, three missionaries received, seven young envoys sent to the Pope.
2. Trace Bakaffa (1721–1730) and Mentewab, Iyasu II (1730–1755) under her regency, Wubit, and Iyoas (1755–1769).
3. Say Mentewab called Ras Mikael Sehul against Begemdir; Mikael killed Iyoas in 1769 and enthroned Tekle Haymanot II.
