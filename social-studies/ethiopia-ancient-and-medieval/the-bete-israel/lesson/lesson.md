# The Bete-Israel

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — A Jewish people of the Ethiopian highlands

The Bete-Israel — the House of Israel — are Ethiopia's Jewish people, living for centuries in the northern highlands. Historians debate their origins: how and when Judaism took root among them is a genuine historical question, argued from chronicles, practice and language.

Debate is not a defect here. It is the lesson: some origins the evidence settles, and some it leaves honestly open.

## Sub lesson 2 — Crafts, land and livelihood

The Bete-Israel were renowned craftspeople — potters, weavers and smiths whose work highland society needed. Their landholding, though, was precarious: craft skill did not always bring secure rights to land.

Hold those two facts together: valued for their hands, vulnerable in their tenure. Much of their later history turns on that tension.

## Sub lesson 3 — Neighbours of the Christian kingdom

To the end of the 13th century, the Bete-Israel lived alongside — and in negotiation with — the Christian kingdom: sometimes integrated, sometimes set apart by faith, craft and land rules.

Their story belongs inside Ethiopian history, not beside it: the kingdom's religious map was never single-coloured.
