# The sultanates — Dahlak

## Do it first

A cluster of islands off the Red Sea coast: too dry to farm richly, yet for centuries everyone wanted them. Why? Because whoever holds the islands holds a door of the sea trade. Meet the first sultanate on our list: Dahlak.

## An island sultanate on trade

The story starts with violence at the port: **Arabs settled the Dahlak islands after taking Adulis in 702**. On the islands a **Muslim sultanate formed in the 10th century**, and it lived by a **trade economy**. In its early centuries Dahlak was the great door: **before Zeila rose, foreign trade ran through Dahlak**. Arab states and Ethiopian states alike **coveted** the islands — a door everyone wants to hold. Yet the Dahlak sultans did something cleverer than fighting for it: they **kept their independence by peaceful means**, balancing the great powers around them for century after century. Only in the **mid-16th century** did the run end, when **Ottoman Turks took** the sultanate. Small islands, long independence: Dahlak's lesson is that trade wealth plus careful peace can hold off empires — for a long time, though not for ever.
