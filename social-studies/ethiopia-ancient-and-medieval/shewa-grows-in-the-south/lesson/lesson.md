# Shewa grows in the south

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — A kingdom apart

While the north fragmented in the Era of the Princes, Shewa — the highland kingdom south of the Abbay bend — grew under its own dynasty, largely spared the warlord wars.

Shewa's kings kept a court, an army and a succession while Gondar's emperors were puppets. The contrast is the lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sahle Selassie expands

Under Sahle Selassie, Shewa expanded and enriched itself: borders pushed outward, trade encouraged, and the kingdom's fields and markets prospered.

He ruled for decades — long reigns build what short ones cannot: roads of custom, full granaries, a name abroad.

## Sub lesson 3 — The kingdom that will reunify Ethiopia

Shewa's line did not stop: from this southern kingdom came Menilek, who would reunify Ethiopia and move its centre south for good.

So this short lesson carries a long fuse — the Era of the Princes ends, in the end, from Shewa.

## Say it to your companion

Tell the whole story back to your companion, part by part, in order — names, dates and places included. Your companion listens with the lesson closed and stops you at any name or date they cannot place; open the lesson together and repair that part.
