# Ethiopian chronicles and hagiographies as sources — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Ethiopia's chronicle tradition is described as —
a) borrowed from Europe in 1900  b) its own indigenous tradition  c) begun in the 1960s
**Answer: b) its own indigenous tradition** — Indigenous, centuries deep.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Ethiopian academic history dates from —
a) the 1960s  b) 1270  c) the early 20th century
**Answer: a) the 1960s** — The tradition changed in the early 20th century; the academic discipline dates from the 1960s.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Chronicles are classed as — sources.
a) secondary  b) primary  c) oral
**Answer: b) primary** — Contemporary with the events, listed with coins and inscriptions.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Before use, a primary source is checked for —
a) authenticity  b) length  c) beauty
**Answer: a) authenticity** — Forgery or real — the first test.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** A hagiography is —
a) a tax register  b) a saint's life, written to glorify  c) a king list
**Answer: b) a saint's life, written to glorify** — Read it gratefully and critically.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Cross-testing a chronicle means checking it against —
a) only later chronicles  b) inscriptions, coins and other sources  c) the writer's family
**Answer: b) inscriptions, coins and other sources** — Independent evidence.
