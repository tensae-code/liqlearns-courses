# Lesson plan — Ethiopian chronicles and hagiographies as sources

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** Ethiopian chronicles and hagiographies as sources
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Units 1 and 3 — the discipline of history, applied to Ethiopia's own texts (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia had its **own indigenous chronicle tradition** — royal histories written at court across the centuries, recording reigns, wars, church affairs and the deeds of kings.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner knowing primary and secondary sources and how to test them — the First Steps title's evidence lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks praise-writing is worthless as evidence → tested, it yields dates, names and sequences; the craft is in the testing, not the discarding.
- Thinks primary means automatically true → primary means contemporary; authenticity is still the first check.
