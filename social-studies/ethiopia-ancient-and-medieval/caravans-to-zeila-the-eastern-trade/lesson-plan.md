# Lesson plan — Caravans to Zeila — the eastern trade

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** Caravans to Zeila — the eastern trade
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Units 3 — Peoples and States in Ethiopia and the Horn to the end of the 13th C. (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Islam reached Ethiopia first as **refuge**: persecuted early Muslims were received by the **Aksumite king** — the Al-Nejashi mosque keeps the memory — and the Prophet is said to have **exempted Ethiopia from jihad** in return.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner having met Islam's arrival in Ethiopia and the pre-Aksumite world — this lesson follows the road that replaced Adulis.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses Adulis and Zeila eras → Adulis is Aksum's northern Red Sea port; Zeila, on the Gulf of Aden, succeeds it for the south-eastern trade.
- Reads the principalities as one empire → a chain of separate sultanates sharing a road, often rivals — beads, not a single crown.
