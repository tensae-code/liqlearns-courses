# Lesson plan — Damot, Kafa and the southern states

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** Damot, Kafa and the southern states
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Unit 7 — States, Principalities, Population Movements and Interactions in Ethiopia, 13th–mid-16th C. (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Damot**, south of the **Blue Nile**, was **probably the earliest** of the southern and central states.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner having met the restored Christian kingdom and the Muslim principalities — the south of this lesson is the third world of the same centuries.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads 'converted' as 'conquered' → conversion and rule are different threads in this lesson; Damot kept its throne while its faith turned.
- Treats the south as empty before the kingdom → the book's whole point is a south of ordered states with their own systems.
