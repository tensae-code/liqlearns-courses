# Damot, Kafa and the southern states

## Do it first

South of the Blue Nile lay another Ethiopia: states of indigenous religion, ruled from forest capitals, trading with both the Christian kingdom and the sultanates — and pressed, century by century, to come under the kingdom's rule. Damot first, probably the earliest of them all.

## Sub lesson 1 — Damot — probably the earliest

**Damot**, south of the **Blue Nile**, was **probably the earliest** of the southern and central states. Its ruler **Motalami** was converted by **Abune Tekle Haymanot in the late 13th century** — a conversion that tied Damot, by faith, toward the Christian kingdom even while it kept its own throne.

[Picture 22513 — a map of the southern and central states with Damot marked earliest]

Damot matters as a door: through it, the highland kingdom's faith, trade and pressure entered the south. The book examines each southern state for its **political and socio-economic order** as the Christian kingdom pressed to bring them under its rule — Damot first in time, and first in that pressure's path.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kafa and its neighbours — a south of many states

Around and beyond Damot stood a whole south: **Kafa, Ennarya, Bizamo, Walaita, Yem and Kambata**, and the **Gurage chiefdoms**, with the **Agaw kingdom of Gojjam** to the north-west. These were states and peoples of **indigenous religion** — their own priests, shrines and orders, the layer this course met in its religion lessons.

Each had its own political and socio-economic order — kings, chiefdoms, markets, tribute — and each faced the same question from the north: trade, convert, submit, resist? Their answers differed, and the differences are the history. When the Oromo expansion of the next lessons redrew the map, it was this crowded, ordered south that it entered — not an empty land.
