# Aksum — the first Hijra

## Do it first

A group of hunted people cross the sea with nothing but their faith, begging shelter from a king of another religion. What does the king do? This true story is still told wherever Islam's first years are told — and its hero is an Ethiopian king.

## The story of the two crossings

In the days when the Prophet Muhammad's first followers were **persecuted** in Arabia, they fled across the Red Sea to Aksum — the journey remembered as the **Hijra** to Ethiopia. There were two crossings. The **first Hijra, in 615**, brought **15–18 people**, named in the account — among them the **Prophet's daughter Ruqayya**. The **second, in 616**, brought **about 119 people under Ja'far ibn Abi Talib**. Altogether **126–129 Muslims** found shelter. The Aksumite king, **al-Ashama — Armah (615–631)** — received them and let them **live in peace, free in their faith**. Most returned to Arabia in **629**; some chose to settle; and the **15–20 who died** in Ethiopia were **buried with honour at Negash**, where their graves are still respected.

## Why the story is kept

This lesson is a story, so hold its meaning the way stories teach. A Christian king sheltered Muslim refugees when no one else would — and refused to hand them back to their persecutors. The book draws the lesson plainly: **Ethiopia as a land of justice, order and welcome**. That reputation, earned in 615, is part of why the Hijra story is told to this day — the first shelter of Islam stood on Ethiopian soil.
