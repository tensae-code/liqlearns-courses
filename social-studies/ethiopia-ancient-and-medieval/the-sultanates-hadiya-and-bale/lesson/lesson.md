# The sultanates — Hadiya and Bale

## Do it first

Not every state's birthday was written down. Two southern sultanates enter the record not at their founding but at the moment a greater king makes them pay tribute. Meet Hadiya and Bale — known by their work, their titles and their neighbours.

## Hadiya — the farming, trading south

**Hadiya's founding date is unrecorded** — history first sees it clearly when it was **made tributary by Amda Tsion in 1316**. Its economy rested on **farming and trade**, and it was **known for supplying slaves to the trade** — a grim line in the record, and part of the honest story of the age's commerce.

## Bale — the garads' land

**Bale's founding date is likewise unrecorded**. It stood **tributary to the highland kingdom from the 14th to the 16th century** — two centuries of paying up to the highland crown. Its rulers carried the title **garad**. And Bale sat in a ring of fellow sultanates: **Dawaro and Sharka to the north, Hadiya to the west, Adal to the east** — a southern world of Muslim states, trading, farming and negotiating with the Christian kingdom above them. When you map Hadiya and Bale, you see the sultanates were not one state but a whole southern system.
