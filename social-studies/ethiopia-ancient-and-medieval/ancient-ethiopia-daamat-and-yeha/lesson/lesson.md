# Ancient Ethiopia — Da'amat and Yeha

## Do it first

In northern Ethiopia, near Aksum, stand the remains of a very old temple at a place called Yeha. Its stones were cut long before Aksum's stelae rose. Who built it? What did their kings call themselves — and why does that title point across the sea?

## A state centred at Yeha

After Punt, the next named state is **Da'amat**, in northern Ethiopia. Its centre was **Yeha**, south of today's Aksum. At Yeha you can still picture the state's greatness: a great temple, and in it a **throne and an idol** were found — signs of kingship and of the gods the people kept.

## The title that points across the sea

Da'amat's kings did not use an Ethiopian title. They used the **South Arabian title mukarrib**, a title with three jobs inside it: **religious guardian, chief judge, and war commander** — priest, judge and general in one word. And the kings **kept their own gods**. Put those two facts together and a careful conclusion follows: there were **close political ties between Ethiopia and South Arabia**, across the Red Sea. Ideas, titles and trade crossed the water — yet Da'amat's kings were no mere copyists; they ruled their own land and worshipped their own gods.
