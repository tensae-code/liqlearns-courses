# The Oromo expansion — causes and course

## Do it first

Into the lands emptied and shaken by the Adal wars, a great movement of people now pours — from the southern highlands outward, in every direction, for over a century. Who were they, why did they move, and what engine powered the movement? That engine has a name: Gada.

## Homeland and causes

The **Oromo** are a **Cushitic-speaking people** whose oral tradition places their homeland in the **Bale highlands — Meda Welabu**. Their expansion ran from the **1550s–1580s** outward across **central, eastern, western, southern and northern Ethiopia, and into Kenya and Somalia**. The book gives three drivers: **population growth** pressing on the homeland's land; the **search for grazing land** for their herds; and a **Gada system that encouraged taking new land** — an age-grade system that organised each generation of men for movement and settlement.

## The course of the movement

The movement's own dates frame it: it **began under the Melbah Gada in 1522**, surged in the decades after mid-century, **slowed after 1604**, and its campaigns ran on **to 1710 for Innarya**. The teacher guide reframes the same movement as one of **population movements producing interaction and integration** among Ethiopia's peoples — and notes that the **Afar and Somali movements began on almost the same impulse, but earlier than the Oromo movement**. Expansion, in that telling, is not only conquest: peoples met, mixed and remade the land together — though the meetings were often war first, as the next lesson's effects will show.
