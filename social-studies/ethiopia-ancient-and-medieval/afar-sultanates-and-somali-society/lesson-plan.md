# Lesson plan — Afar sultanates and Somali society

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** Afar sultanates and Somali society
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Unit 8 — Political, Social and Economic Processes in Ethiopia, mid-16th–mid-19th C. (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The **Afar** inhabit the **'Afar Triangle' of Ethiopia, Eritrea and Djibouti** — land the book presents both as a **cradle of humankind** and as archaeologically rich, with abundant resources and strategic weight (the fossil lessons of this course began in this same Afar earth).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner having met the eastern states — Harar and the Adal world — whose lowland neighbours these peoples are.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Looks for the Afar capital → the form is a confederation; asking for its capital imports another system's shape.
- Thinks clan means disorganised → the Guurti is a structured judiciary at three levels; organisation here wears no crown.
