# Afar sultanates and Somali society

## Do it first

The hottest inhabited land on earth — and a confederation of sultanates governing it without a single capital. Beside them, Somali clans whose council, the Guurti, gave every adult man an equal voice. The east of this course is not empty desert; it is two working systems of rule.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Afar confederation — sultanates of the Triangle

The **Afar** inhabit the **'Afar Triangle' of Ethiopia, Eritrea and Djibouti** — land the book presents both as a **cradle of humankind** and as archaeologically rich, with abundant resources and strategic weight (the fossil lessons of this course began in this same Afar earth).

[Picture 22517 — Afar sultanates in the Triangle, and elders seated in Guurti council under a tree]

Predominantly **Muslim pastoralists**, the Afar organised in a **confederation of sultanates — Aussa, Bidu, Tadjoura and Rahaito** among them — each of several villages with its own culture, and linked in the **14th century**, in Christian sources, to the **Adal Sultanate**. No single throne ruled the Triangle; the confederation was the state-form fitted to a pastoral land of wells and grazing routes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Somali clans and the Guurti

Somali society combined **pastoralists with settled farming and trading communities**, organised in **clans**. Its indigenous governing structure was the **Guurti** — a **council of elders deciding criminal and civil cases**.

The book stresses the Guurti's character: a **highly democratic process in which all adult males had equal access and participation**, working at **sub-clan, clan and inter-clan levels**. Dispute in the morning, council under the tree by afternoon, a decision the clan owns by evening — governance without a palace, scaled from the smallest kin-group to meetings between clans.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grade 11 depth: Afar sultanates and Somali society in depth

The Grade 11 page is short and load-bearing: five Afar sultanates — Aussa, Bidu, Gobaad, Tajura and Rahayito — holding the salt plains and caravan corridors between highland and sea, and beside them Somali clan society, governing itself by kinship and custom along the coast's trade. Whoever held the salt road held a tax on every highland kitchen; the Afar held the road.
