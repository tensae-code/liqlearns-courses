# Aksum — the stelae

## Do it first

Stand next to the tallest building you know and look up. Now imagine a single carved stone, taller than that building, raised by people with ropes, ramps and patience — no cranes. At Aksum, such stones still stand. Measure them with your eyes in this lesson.

## Stones that sign a kingdom

The **stelae** are Aksum's signature architecture — great carved stone pillars raised at Aksum. Three numbers tell their story. The **largest stela was 33 metres** tall; it has **now fallen and lies broken**. The **second, 24 metres**, **still stands at Aksum** today. The **third, 22 metres**, has travelled the world: it was **taken to Italy and returned in 2005**. Hold those numbers against your own body: a person is not even 2 metres tall. The standing stela is more than twelve people high, carved from single stone. A kingdom that could quarry, carve, move and raise such stones commanded strong organisation, skilled craftsmen and great wealth — the stelae are Aksum's power made visible, which is why this course returns to them again and again.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three stelae and their heights, which one still stands, and which one came home in 2005.

## Do It Yourself

1. Measure a wall or tree near you. How many of YOU, head to toe, would make 24 metres?
2. Draw the three stelae as three bars: 33 m, 24 m, 22 m, with a tiny person beside each.
3. Write the traveller stela's story in two sentences: taken to Italy — returned in 2005.
