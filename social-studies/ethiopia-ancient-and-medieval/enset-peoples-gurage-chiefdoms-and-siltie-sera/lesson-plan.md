# Lesson plan — Enset peoples — Gurage chiefdoms and Siltie Sera

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** Enset peoples — Gurage chiefdoms and Siltie Sera
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Unit 8 — Political, Social and Economic Processes in Ethiopia, mid-16th–mid-19th C. (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Southern and south-western Ethiopia are among the country's **most densely populated** areas, and they shared the **'enset-culture complex'**: a way of life built on the enset plant.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner having met the Cushitic states and their Sera systems — this lesson zooms into the enset south that shared them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses Siltie Sera with Sidama Luwa → both are Cushitic self-governance, but Sera here is the Siltie (and Hadiya) system; Luwa is the Sidama eight-year rotation from the survey lesson.
- Reads 'chiefdoms' as 'one kingdom' → the Gurage were a patchwork of chiefdoms; that patchwork is the fact to hold.
