# The Adal wars — Imam Ahmad

## Do it first

In phase one, the highland kingdom was uppermost for two centuries. Then one man united the sultanates and, in sixteen years, nearly overturned the whole order. His name: Imam Ahmad. Follow his rise — and count what his war cost both sides.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Imam's supremacy, 1527–1543

**Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi united the sultanates** and opened the war at **Shimbra Kure in 1527**. Victory followed victory: he held **political supremacy from 1527 to 1543**, and between **1531 and 1535** his armies took **Dawaro, Bale, Wej, Debre Berhan, Tigray and the Red Sea areas**. The highland kingdom, uppermost for two hundred years, was driven to the edge.

## Sub lesson 2 — Foreign soldiers and the end at Weyna Dega

Both sides now reached abroad for soldiers. **Lebna Dengel sought Portuguese help**: the **Bermudez mission came in 1535**, and **400 Portuguese soldiers arrived in 1541**. The Imam, for his part, had **900 Turkish soldiers**. The end came at **Weyna Dega in 1543**, where **the Imam was defeated** — and the supremacy ended with him. The book then counts the **results**, and they are heavy for everyone: **deaths and destruction on both sides; the highland kingdom's best soldiers lost; population displaced**; and yet also seeds of a new age — **lowland Muslims and highland Christians came to live together**, **Portuguese and Ottoman intervention** entered Ethiopian affairs, and the weakened land left **room for the Oromo expansion** that follows in the next lesson.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grade 11 depth: The wars of Imam Ahmad in depth — 1529 to Wayna Daga

The Grade 11 outline is stark: Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi — Gragn — invades from Adal in 1529; campaign by campaign the highland kingdom shatters, its churches burned, its emperor a fugitive; then Portuguese musketeers land, and at Wayna Daga in 1543 the war turns and Gragn falls. Near-collapse and reversal in fourteen years — the closest the medieval kingdom came to ending.
