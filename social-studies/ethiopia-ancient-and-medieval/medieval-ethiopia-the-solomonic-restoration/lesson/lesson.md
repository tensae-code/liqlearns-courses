# Medieval Ethiopia — the Solomonic restoration

## Do it first

In 1270 a new king takes the throne and announces that his line is not new at all — it is the OLD line, restored. Why would a conqueror claim to be a restorer? And what do you do when five kings follow in five years? Read how the medieval kingdom began.

## 1270 — a restoration, by design

In **1270 Yekuno Amlak defeated the last Zagwe king, Yetbarak**, and proclaimed the **Solomonic dynasty restored**. The name was a claim, chosen with care: to call his line 'Solomonic' was to **claim legitimacy** — descent from Solomon — and to **cast the Zagwe as usurpers**, mere holders of a throne that was never theirs. Yekuno Amlak himself **ruled peacefully (1270–1285)** and **handed power to his son Yagbe'a Tsion (1285–1294)** — father to son, exactly as a settled dynasty should.

## Five sons, five years — and Amba Geshen

Then the machine jammed: after Yagbe'a Tsion, **five sons reigned in five years (1294–1299)** — a scramble of short reigns as brothers and claimants struggled for the throne. The remedy was drastic and long-lasting: **all royal descendants except the reigning king were confined on Amba Geshen**, a guarded mountain, so no prince would be free to raise a rival claim. Hold the irony: the dynasty founded to end usurpation kept its own princes prisoner on a mountaintop to survive.

## The moving court — a kingdom without a fixed capital (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 9 teacher guide's Solomonic overview adds the framing this lesson now carries. In this period Christianity expanded further south and the church–state relationship became very strong, almost reaching its peak — "that is why it was called Christian Kingdom". The guide also records the gult right's long life: it lasted in Ethiopian history until the 20th century. And it names the fact the Gondar lesson later turns on: the medieval kings of Ethiopia had no fixed capital city. The court itself moved — a royal camp-city of tents and pavilions, consuming a region's surplus and moving on — government as a travelling city, which is why Fasilides' founding of Gondar in 1636, in the Gondar lessons, is a revolution in itself.

## Grade 11 depth — 1270 in depth — Yekuno Amlak and the Kebra Nagast

The Grade 11 telling slows at the founding moment: in 1270 Yekuno Amlak restores the line that claimed descent from Solomon, and the Kebra Nagast — the Glory of Kings — supplies the claim's charter, binding Ethiopia's dynasty to Solomon and the Queen of Sheba in sacred story. Restoration and legend arrive together, each doing work: one takes the throne, the other makes taking it righteous for six centuries of successors.
