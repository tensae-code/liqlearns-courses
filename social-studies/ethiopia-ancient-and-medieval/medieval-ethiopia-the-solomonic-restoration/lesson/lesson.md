# Medieval Ethiopia — the Solomonic restoration

## Do it first

In 1270 a new king takes the throne and announces that his line is not new at all — it is the OLD line, restored. Why would a conqueror claim to be a restorer? And what do you do when five kings follow in five years? Read how the medieval kingdom began.

## 1270 — a restoration, by design

In **1270 Yekuno Amlak defeated the last Zagwe king, Yetbarak**, and proclaimed the **Solomonic dynasty restored**. The name was a claim, chosen with care: to call his line 'Solomonic' was to **claim legitimacy** — descent from Solomon — and to **cast the Zagwe as usurpers**, mere holders of a throne that was never theirs. Yekuno Amlak himself **ruled peacefully (1270–1285)** and **handed power to his son Yagbe'a Tsion (1285–1294)** — father to son, exactly as a settled dynasty should.

## Five sons, five years — and Amba Geshen

Then the machine jammed: after Yagbe'a Tsion, **five sons reigned in five years (1294–1299)** — a scramble of short reigns as brothers and claimants struggled for the throne. The remedy was drastic and long-lasting: **all royal descendants except the reigning king were confined on Amba Geshen**, a guarded mountain, so no prince would be free to raise a rival claim. Hold the irony: the dynasty founded to end usurpation kept its own princes prisoner on a mountaintop to survive.
