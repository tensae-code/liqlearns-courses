# Egalitarian systems — governing without kings — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Egalitarian systems of governance are orders in which power is —
a) Inherited on a throne  b) Shared, rotated or elected  c) Held by priests only  d) Held by foreign rulers
**Answer: b) Shared, rotated or elected — No king: power circulates.**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The Grade 9 book places these systems as comparisons to —
a) Aksum  b) Gadaa  c) The Funj  d) Mamluk Egypt
**Answer: b) Gadaa — Gadaa is the famous case they are set beside.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Sidama Luwa rotated power every —
a) Year  b) Eight years  c) Generation of forty years  d) Lifetime
**Answer: b) Eight years — The eight-year rotation, under the Mote and the woma-songo council.**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The Gedeo system is called —
a) Sera  b) Ballee  c) Luwa  d) Gult
**Answer: b) Ballee — The Gedeo governed through the ballee system.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Konso's organisation was —
a) A centralised empire  b) Decentralised — walled towns governing themselves  c) A sultanate  d) A priest-state
**Answer: b) Decentralised — walled towns governing themselves — No single ruler over all Konso.**

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Burji's administration was —
a) Hereditary  b) Elective  c) Military  d) Foreign
**Answer: b) Elective — Officials chosen rather than born to office.**
