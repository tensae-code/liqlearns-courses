# Lesson plan — Medieval Ethiopia — trade routes and goods

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Ethiopia: Ancient and Medieval · **Lesson:** Medieval Ethiopia — trade routes and goods
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Ethiopia: Ancient and Medieval (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In the medieval centuries the **trade gateway shifted from Adulis toward Zeila** — though **Mitsiwa kept serving the north-east**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met the periods of Ethiopian history and the states named so far in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The routes and their goods** — The gate shifts from Adulis toward Zeila; two great routes; slaves, ivory, gold and civet; amole salt money.
2. **Two kinds of trade, and the tax on trade (teacher guide — evidence)** — Local weekly markets against long-distance caravans; trade as rulers' taxation and as peaceful mixing of peoples.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the gateway shifted from Adulis toward Zeila, Mitsiwa still serving the north-east.
2. Name the routes Harar–Dawaro–Fatagar (towns Harla, Harar, Chercher, Dakar) and Ifat–Wasel–Awsa, the goods (slaves, ivory, gold, civet) and amole salt-bar money.
3. State the guide's split of local vs long-distance trade, taxation's role, and trade as peaceful interaction (teacher guide — evidence).
