# Medieval Ethiopia — trade routes and goods — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The medieval trade gateway shifted —
a) From Zeila to Adulis  b) From Adulis toward Zeila  c) From Dahlak to Gondar  d) From Berbera to Mitsiwa
**Answer: b) From Adulis toward Zeila** — Shifted from Adulis toward Zeila; Mitsiwa kept serving the north-east.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The east–west route ran —
a) Ifat–Wasel–Awsa  b) Harar–Dawaro–Fatagar  c) Aksum–Yeha–Adafa  d) Dakar–Chercher–Gondar
**Answer: b) Harar–Dawaro–Fatagar** — Harar–Dawaro–Fatagar, with Harla, Harar, Chercher and Dakar rising on it.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Exchange money in the interior was —
a) Gold coins only  b) Amole salt bars  c) Paper notes  d) Cowrie shells only
**Answer: b) Amole salt bars** — Amole salt bars served as exchange money.

**4. (Sub lesson 1)** Which was NOT among the route goods?
a) Slaves  b) Ivory  c) Civet  d) Silk cloth
**Answer: d) Silk cloth** — Goods: slaves, ivory, gold and civet.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Local trade, in the guide, happens —
a) Once a year at Zeila  b) At an open market once a week on a fixed day  c) Only at royal courts  d) Only by sea
**Answer: b) At an open market once a week on a fixed day** — Local trade: weekly open market, subsistence goods (teacher guide — evidence).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Rulers taxed trade in order to —
a) End all markets  b) Strengthen their power, even importing firearms  c) Feed the caravans free  d) Close the routes
**Answer: b) Strengthen their power, even importing firearms** — Trade income strengthened rulers' power (teacher guide — evidence).
