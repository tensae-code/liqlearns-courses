# Gumuz, Shinasha and the gold sheikhdoms of Benishangul — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The Gumuz practised —
a) irrigation empires  b) small-scale shifting (slash-and-burn) cultivation  c) deep-sea fishing
**Answer: b) small-scale shifting (slash-and-burn) cultivation** — Clear, crop, move on.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The Shinasha protected —
a) reserved forests — cutting their trees was forbidden  b) the coast  c) salt mines
**Answer: a) reserved forests — cutting their trees was forbidden** — Forest law fitted to their land.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** The border peoples of this lesson speak languages of the — super-family.
a) Afro-Asiatic  b) Nilo-Saharan  c) Indo-European
**Answer: b) Nilo-Saharan** — Nilo-Saharan-speaking peoples.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The Benishangul sheikhdoms were —
a) Asosa, Benishangul and Khomosha  b) Ifat, Dawaro and Bali  c) Aussa, Bidu and Tadjoura
**Answer: a) Asosa, Benishangul and Khomosha** — Three Muslim sheikhdoms.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** They grew from interaction with —
a) Portuguese sailors  b) Arabic-speaking merchants  c) Roman legions
**Answer: b) Arabic-speaking merchants** — Trade brought the sheikhdoms.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Egypt in the 1820s and the Mahdists in the 1880s targeted the region for its —
a) coffee  b) gold  c) ivory only
**Answer: b) gold** — Gold drew empires; the sheikhdoms defended their independence.
**7. (Sub lesson 3)** What is sheikhdoms?
a) the local rulers of the far-western river peoples
b) a detail belonging to a different lesson in this course
c) the small Muslim-ruled states of Benishangul-Gumuz
**Answer: c) the small Muslim-ruled states of Benishangul-Gumuz**
