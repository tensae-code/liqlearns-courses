# Medieval Ethiopia — Amda Tsion

## Do it first

One king, thirty years, and a kingdom that grows in every direction. Amda Tsion's campaigns redrew the map — and, the book says plainly, filled the kingdom with people and riches. Trace his expansion on the map as you read.

## The campaigns of 1314–1344

**Amda Tsion reigned 1314–1344**, and his reign is a story of expansion. Campaign by campaign, his armies brought new lands under the highland kingdom: **Damot, Gojjam, the Lake Tana area**, and the **eastern and south-eastern Muslim sultanates**, which were made to pay **tribute**. Each name on that list mattered twice over. New lands meant new people — and the book gives the gain in two words: the king gained **manpower and wealth**. Soldiers and workers for the kingdom; tribute and goods for its treasury.

## Why this reign matters

Hold Amda Tsion's map against what came before: the restored dynasty of 1270 was a highland kingdom among neighbours. After Amda Tsion, it was the **great power of the region**, with sultanates at its feet paying tribute. That supremacy set the stage for everything in the next lessons: the sultanates chafing under tribute, Ifat's fall, and the long Adal wars to come. Expansion bought strength — and stored up resistance.
