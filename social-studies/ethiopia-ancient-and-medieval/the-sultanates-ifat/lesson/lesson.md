# The sultanates — Ifat

## Do it first

If a road carries all the gold and ivory of a kingdom to the sea, the state sitting ON that road can tax every load. That state was Ifat — the strongest of the sultanates, until it met Amda Tsion. Read how a road made it great, and unmade it.

## The state on the road

**Ifat** was **founded in the mid-13th century by Umar Walasma**, founder of the Walasma line. Its fortune was geography: **the trade routes linking the Ethiopian interior to Zeila crossed Ifat's territory**, so Ifat **controlled the region's trade** — every caravan between the highlands and the port passed its tolls. That made Ifat **the strongest and foremost of the sultanates**, dominating its neighbours in the **early 14th century**.

## Ifat meets Amda Tsion

Supremacy ended where the trade road met a greater power. **Amda Tsion's campaigns ended Ifat's supremacy**, and **Sultan Sabr al-Din, fighting repeatedly for independence, was defeated and made tributary** — Ifat kept its sultans but now paid tribute to the highland kingdom. The teacher guide pins the geography and the date: Ifat lay on the **north-eastern foothills of the Shewan plateau, extending to the Awash valley**, and was **made tributary by Amda Tsion in 1332**. Ifat's story is the sultanates' story in miniature: trade made it strong; the struggle over trade — who taxes it, who obeys whom — brought it under the highland crown.
