# Susenyos and the Catholic episode

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — An emperor tries to recentralise

Emperor Susenyos came to the throne through civil war and set out to recentralise a kingdom slipping toward its lords. He campaigned, reordered grants, and looked for allies and ideas that could strengthen the crown.

Among the arrivals at his court were Jesuit missionaries from Catholic Europe — and their faith became his fateful instrument.

## Sub lesson 2 — Conversion and imposition

Susenyos converted to Catholicism and, urged by the Jesuits, imposed it on an Orthodox kingdom — new rites, rebaptism, and penalties for refusal.

The kingdom answered with rebellion. Province after province rose, not for a lord's ambition but for the faith of their fathers — and the emperor's armies bled suppressing his own people.

## Sub lesson 3 — Abdication, 1632

In 1632, facing a kingdom in arms against his faith, Susenyos did the rare thing: he abdicated, restoring freedom of worship and handing the throne to his son Fasilides.

The episode closed Ethiopia to Catholic missions for centuries — and taught its rulers the price of imposing belief from the throne.
