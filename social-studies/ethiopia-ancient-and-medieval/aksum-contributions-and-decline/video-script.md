# Video script — Aksum — contributions and decline

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

What did Aksum leave us?
A written language — Ge'ez. Books. Fine crafts.
Coins of gold, silver and bronze.
But the sea was lost. Adulis fell in 702.
At home: revolt, struggles, a weakening land.
The centre moved to Lasta.
About 1150, Aksum ended — its gifts did not.

Debate coda (teacher guide — evidence; Unit 6's set debate, as a filmed two-voice ending): Voice A asks: "Was Aksum's civilisation a copy of others?" Voice B answers from the evidence: "Its language, its coins, its carved stones grew on its own soil — Aksum was built by its own people." The class then debates the question themselves.

[Grade 11 depth scene — The Aksumite Kingdom in depth — trade, coinage and the long decline. The animated map carries the story as the voice speaks.]
The Grade 11 book gives Aksum its whole biography: Red Sea trade through Adulis making it rich; Ezana's conversion making it Christian; coinage carrying its kings' names to foreign markets; Ge'ez writing its records; stelae raising its pride in stone; and Kaleb's expedition across the sea to Himyar at its power's height. The names to hold: Adulis, Kaleb.
