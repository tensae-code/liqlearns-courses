# Medieval Ethiopia — Islam and the sultanates

## Do it first

A faith arrives not by armies but by caravans — carried by traders along the routes they already travel. Where the traders went, mosques rose, and then whole states: the sultanates. Follow the routes first; the states grow along them.

## Trade and faith travel together

Islam entered Ethiopia **peacefully**, through three sea gates: **Dahlak, Zeila and Berbera**. It was **carried by Arab Muslim traders**, and it grew through **peaceful ties with pastoralist communities** along the trade routes. Where did it take root? In the **east and south-east — Harar, Wollo, Bale, Arsi** and their neighbours — lands **where Christianity had not reached**. In the Christian north, by contrast, Islam stayed **limited to the Red Sea shores**, a faith of ports and merchants rather than of the highland interior.

## Sultanates rise along the Zeila route

Where trade and faith settled together, states formed: the **sultanates rose along the Zeila trade route**, the great road from the coast into the interior. That is the pattern to carry into the next lessons — a sultanate is not random: it sits where caravans pass, taxes the trade, and gives the Muslim communities of the east and south-east a government of their own. Dahlak first, then Ifat, Hadiya, Bale, Adal — and, the teacher guide adds, oldest of all, Shewa, which the student book does not carry. The guide also widens the map of gates: Islam entered through **two gateways, Dahlak and Zeila, Zeila being the main gateway to the interior**.

## Grade 11 depth — Ifat, Adal and the eastern sultanates in depth

The eastern sultanates receive their Grade 11 growth story: Ifat, Adal and their sisters rising on the trade routes to Zeila, their wealth in slaves, ivory and gold moving to the sea, their faith and scholarship deepening as their caravans lengthened. Two civilisations — highland Christian and lowland Muslim — grow rich on the same trade from opposite ends of the road.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three entry points, who carried Islam and how, where it spread and where it stayed limited — and why the sultanates rose along the Zeila route.
