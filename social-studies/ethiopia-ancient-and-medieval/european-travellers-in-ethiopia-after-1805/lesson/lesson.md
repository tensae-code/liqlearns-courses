# European travellers in Ethiopia after 1805

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Travellers as sources

From 1805 onward, European travellers — Salt among the first of the century, in the road Bruce had opened — crossed Ethiopia and published what they saw: courts, churches, wars, markets and roads.

Their books are sources like any other: priceless eyewitnesses, and biased ones — read them with the criticism Unit 1 taught.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mixed motives

The travellers came mixed: science and curiosity, trade and diplomacy, and — increasingly — the advance work of empire, mapping a land European powers were learning to covet.

Ethiopian rulers knew it and handled them accordingly: hosting, questioning, and using their visits for their own diplomacy.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading the travellers well

Use the travellers for what only they give — dated, placed, observed detail — and check them against chronicles and oral memory for what they could not see: motives, meanings, the inside of things.

That double reading closes this unit's method where it began: every source tested, none worshipped.
