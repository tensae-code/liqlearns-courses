# Aksum — its economy

## Do it first

A kingdom needs to eat before it can build. Aksum built tall stone stelae and minted coins — so ask first: where did its food come from, and where did its wealth come from? This lesson answers with two words: farming and trade.

## Sub lesson 1 — Farming — the first base

Aksum was **founded about the 1st century** and stood **strongest from the 4th to the 7th century**. Its first base was the land itself. Farmers used **irrigation** — guiding water to the fields — and irrigation **raised farm output**, so more food grew than hands alone could win. The farming people were **peasant rist-holders**: families holding their own rist land, who **paid tribute** — a share of what they grew — to the kingdom. Tribute from many farms fed the court, the army and the builders.

## Sub lesson 2 — Trade — the second base

The second base was **trade, internal and foreign**, running from the **Red Sea port of Adulis** inland to Aksum. Look at the two directions of goods. Aksum **exported** — sent out — **ivory, myrrh, precious stones, incense, spices, gold, rhino horn, hippo hide and tortoise shell**: again, the riches of land and wild animals. It **imported** — brought in — **cloth, household goods, glass, olive oil and wine**: made things its own workshops did not produce. Hold the pattern: Aksum sold what its land gave and bought what other lands made better. Farming fed it; trade enriched it; together they carried a great kingdom for centuries.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion Aksum's two bases. Name three exports and three imports, and say which port the foreign trade ran through.
