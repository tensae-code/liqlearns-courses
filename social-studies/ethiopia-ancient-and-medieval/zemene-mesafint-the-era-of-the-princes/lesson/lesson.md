# Zemene Mesafint — the era of the princes

## Do it first

After 1769, Ethiopia still had emperors — crowned, housed in Gondar, and powerless. The real rulers were the regional lords, the rasoch, who made and broke kings. This is the Zemene Mesafint: the era of the princes, 1769–1855.

## When the lords ruled

The **Zemene Mesafint (1769–1855)** is the medieval period's long dusk. **Central power withered**: Ethiopia **split into many territories**, and the **rasoch — the great lords — became king-makers and king-breakers**, raising emperors up and casting them down. Some lords went further still, **conducting their own foreign relations without Gondar's recognition** — treating with outside powers as if their lordship were a kingdom of its own. Gondar's emperor remained, but as a crown in other men's hands.

## Foreign danger and local defenders

A divided land invites danger from outside. **Muhammad Ali of Egypt, strengthening from 1821, controlled the Red Sea trade route and took Mitsiwa and Harar**. With no central army to answer, **defence fell on local lords** — among them **Dejazmach Wube of the north, Dejazmach Kinfu, and Kassa Hailu of Qwara** — and the book honours the price: they **paid with their lives**. The era that began with a lord killing a king ends with lords dying to defend a land no king could defend. What this age cost the ordinary people is the next and last lesson of the chapter.
