# The Gondar period — founding

## Do it first

For centuries Ethiopia's kings moved — camp to camp, region to region. Then, in 1636, a king built a capital meant to stay: Gondar. Why settle? And why did the king who founded it first expel the foreigners at his court? Read the founding story.

## Two kings: Susenyos, then Fasiledes

The road to Gondar opens with **Susenyos (1607–1632)**, who **ruled from Denbiya** with a **decentralised order**: local rulers kept their own **defence, tax and army duties** — the centre reigned, the regions governed themselves day by day. His successor **Fasiledes (1632–1667)** changed the pattern for good. In **1633 he expelled the foreigners he judged divisive** — clearing the court of influences he saw as splitting the kingdom — and in **1636 he founded Gondar** as the highland kingdom's centre, for **national and religious unity**.

## A capital for 219 years

Gondar's capital role ran **1636–1855** — the whole rest of the medieval period. A fixed capital changed what a kingdom could be: palaces, churches and a court that stayed in one place, gathering the realm around it. Fasiledes kept the outward ties a capital needs too, with **close ties to the rulers of Suakin, Mitsiwa and the Turks** on the coasts. The teacher guide dates Gondar's golden age **1636–1706**, after which royal power declined into the Zemene Mesafint. The next lessons walk inside the city he founded — its compound, markets and peoples — and then through its kings to the fall of Iyoas.
