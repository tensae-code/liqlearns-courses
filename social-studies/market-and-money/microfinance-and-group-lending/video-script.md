# Video script — Microfinance and group lending

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Microfinance's origin story: Muhammad Yunus and the Grameen Bank, born from the failure of state banks to reach the poor. Its institutions provide savings, credit, insurance and transfer services without the collateral conventional banks demand — lending to people whose only wealth is their work and their word.

Group lending is the collateral substitute: the borrowing group itself is the security. Peer pressure keeps members repaying, and a default is covered by the group. Its target clients are named precisely: the economically active poor, who must use funds productively — the poorest and destitute are excluded by design and left to other programmes.

Against informal moneylenders, MFIs charge less and protect more — but moneylenders' procedures are simple, terms clear, service fast and lending character-based, strengths the MFIs deliberately adopted. In Ethiopia, on the book's NBE 2020/21 figures, 41 MFIs held about Birr 47 billion in deposits and about Birr 64.6 billion in loans.
