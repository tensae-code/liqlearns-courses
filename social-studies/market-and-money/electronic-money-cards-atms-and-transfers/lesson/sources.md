**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Dominica, Egypt, Vietnam, Armenia, Bulgaria, Iran). Papua New Guinea is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 3 Social Science TG): Economics enters at Grade 3 as a "Trade" strand built from village life — needs and wants vary by place (a canoe is a need on the coast, not in the highlands), production is taught through making a food garden, and trade starts with traditional exchange and traditional money before modern money and EFTPOS. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 12 Business Studies TG): the Grade 12 Business Studies guide carries a hidden Economics course inside Strand 1 — its Content Standard 1.1 is expressly about "economic principles" and its content background is drawn from Economics textbooks (Bandara's Economics for Grade 12 and Mickleburg's Economics Principles and Policies in the South Pacific) — and it returns to Economics in pricing (elasticity, supply and demand), in consumer protection enforced by the Independent Consumer and Competition Commission (ICCC), and in how goods are priced and paid for on the internet. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Social Science student): Grade 10 is a full Economics course inside Social Science — demand and supply are taught with a school-canteen juice schedule priced in dram, the production-possibilities curve with duduks and shvis (Armenian instruments), and personal finance through Armenian institutions: Central-Bank-licensed lenders, the ACRA credit bureau, the Financial System Mediator and the Rate.am / Appa.am comparison sites, including a fake "Gazprom Armenia" investment-scam case.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Edition divergence carried from the placements: the teacher guide's §6.5 is titled "Money and Electronic Banking" and reduces the section to electronic fund transfers and ATMs only, dropping the student book's advantages/disadvantages of e-money; this lesson follows the student book's fuller §6.5 ("Money and Electronic Money (e-money)"), and the guide's version is labelled (teacher guide — evidence) in the table.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 4 Technology and Entrepreneurship): Grade 4 is where money itself becomes the topic — its history from barter to banknotes, the bank as the go-between of people with surplus money and people who need it, and taxes explained as the price of needs no one can meet alone.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 7 Social Studies): production and consumption are taught with Iranian anchors throughout — the historic bazaar as market and social centre, compulsory third-party car insurance, and the Silk Road crossing Iran as a bridge between East and West.
