# Ethiopia's exchange rate history — the gold peg era

## Do it first

In 1945 one dollar cost 2.48 birr. By 1973 it cost 2.07. The birr got cheaper or dearer against the dollar across those years — which, and who decided?

## Sub lesson 1 — 1945 — the birr is born at 2.48

Ethiopia's legal currency was introduced in 1945 at Birr 2.48 per US dollar, defined as 0.36 grams of fine gold under the 1944 Bretton Woods system. The rate was a peg to gold, held by the state, in a world of fixed rates.

## Sub lesson 2 — Small moves — 1964 and 1971

A slight devaluation took the rate to 2.50 in January 1964. After Bretton Woods collapsed, a revaluation brought it to 2.30 in December 1971 — foreign money now costing fewer birr, the birr itself stronger.

## Sub lesson 3 — 1973 — the 2.07 peg that held

A further revaluation to 2.07 birr per dollar came in February 1973. That peg then held for almost two decades — through revolution and regime change — until the reforms of the 1990s, which the next lesson takes up.
