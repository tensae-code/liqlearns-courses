# Video script — Why markets matter

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A market is not only a place with stalls. It is a framework — the whole arrangement in which buyers and sellers meet, prices form, and goods move. Cambodia's Grade 12 book opens its market chapter by asking why this framework matters, before it studies any single market in detail.

Markets come in kinds: by what is sold — goods, labour, money; by where — local, national, world; and by how sellers stand to each other, from many competing sellers to one. Naming the kind of market tells a buyer or seller what rules of the game they are inside.

No market floats alone. Around it stand forces — buyers' incomes and tastes, the state's laws and taxes, other markets' prices, the wider economy's health. A seller who watches only their own stall misses the forces that will move their prices tomorrow; the framework matters because the forces work through it.
