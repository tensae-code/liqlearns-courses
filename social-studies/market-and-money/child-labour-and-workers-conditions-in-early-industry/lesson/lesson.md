# Child labour and workers' conditions in early industry

## Do it first

Picture a factory town at the end of the 19th century. Who works in the factory — only grown men? Where do the workers live? What do you think each worker is paid, and is it the same for all? Say what you think, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Employment and housing at the end of the 19th century

In Iceland's Grade 8 book, this idea is taught inside 19th-century history, in the part called "Sameinuð stöndum við!" — United we stand! The part begins with **employment** and **housing**: the work people had as industry grew, and the homes workers lived in at the **end of the 19th century**.

Employment and housing belong together in the book's teaching: a worker's condition is not only the job, but also the home the job's pay must hold.

## Sub lesson 2 — Child labour and workers' conditions

Among early industry's workers were **children**. **Child labour** — children working in industry — is one of the workers' conditions the book teaches, alongside the conditions of workers generally: the work they did, the housing they lived in, and the pay they received.

That is what "workers' conditions" means in this lesson: the whole working life of early industry's people — employment, housing, child labour and pay — not the factory machines alone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Source work — comparing how different workers were paid

The book teaches this through **source work**: pupils work with sources **comparing how different workers were paid**. Different workers were not paid the same, and the comparison is the lesson — the learner reads the pay of one worker against the pay of another and sees the difference for themselves.

Do the same comparison yourself. Set two workers side by side: their work, their housing, and their pay. Where the pay differs, ask what differs in the work — that question is the book's source work.

## Say it to your companion

Explain child labour and workers' conditions in early industry to your companion in your own words: employment and housing at the end of the 19th century, children among the workers, and how the book compares workers' pay from sources. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
