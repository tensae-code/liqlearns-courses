# The three-sector model — adding government

## Do it first

Now let a government join the island: it takes a tax from every payment, buys desks from the workshops, helps one workshop with a subsidy, and gives a pension to an elder. Trace one taxed birr on its new journey.

## Sub lesson 1 — Government enters the circle

Adding government gives the three-sector model. Government activity splits into revenue and expenditure: it taxes households and firms, and it spends — the two sides of its life in the flow.

[Picture 24049 — the three-box diagram with tax arrows in and spending, subsidy and transfer arrows out of the government box]

## Sub lesson 2 — What government does with it

Government buys goods and services from firms, gives subsidies to firms and transfer payments to households. Taxes flow in; purchases, subsidies and transfers flow back out into the circle.

## Sub lesson 3 — Saving joins through the capital market

Household saving now also flows into the capital market and on to firms as investment — a leak from spending that returns as firms' building power. National income circulates among all three sectors.
