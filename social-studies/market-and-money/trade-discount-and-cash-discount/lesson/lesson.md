# Trade discount and cash discount

## Do it first

Goods are priced at Shs 10,000. The seller knocks 10% off for a trader, and then offers a further reduction if the trader pays promptly in cash. Are those two reductions the same discount doing the same job? Work out the price after the first one, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Trade discount — the price comes down

Kenya's Form 1 Business Studies book teaches the two discounts as worked calculations in shillings, both on the same starting price. The first is the **trade discount** — a percentage taken off the price, which brings down the price the buyer has to pay.

On the book's price of **Shs 10,000**, a **10% trade discount** takes off Shs 1,000, and the **payable price becomes Shs 9,000**. The trade discount is worked first, on the full price, and it sets what is owed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cash discount — the reward for paying promptly

The second is the **cash discount** — a further percentage taken off what is owed, given for **prompt cash payment**. It is worked after the trade discount, on the payable price.

The book works the cash discount two ways on the same example: **10% in one worked line, 5% in the other**. Either way its job is the same — the buyer who pays promptly in cash pays less than the payable price.

## Sub lesson 3 — Two discounts, two jobs

Set the two side by side and do not mix them. The **trade discount** comes first: it reduces the price itself — Shs 10,000 down to a payable Shs 9,000. The **cash discount** comes second: it reduces what is actually paid, as the reward for paying that payable price promptly in cash.

Same starting price, two different moments, two different jobs: trade discount sets the price to pay; cash discount rewards paying it promptly.
