# Personal finance planning

## Do it first

Imagine your first year of earning money. The money must cover living costs, and somehow also the future. Before you read on, list what you would want your money to do — every job you can think of for it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Earning, saving and investing

Chapter 15 of Albania's Grade 12 teacher guide is Financat personale — personal finance. It starts where all personal finance starts: **earning**. Income is the inflow everything else is built from, and the guide treats growing it — skills, work, enterprise — as part of the plan, not luck.

**Saving** comes next: part of income set aside before spending, not what is left after. Saving builds the fund that emergencies draw on and investment starts from.

**Investing** puts saved money to work — in education, a business, or financial assets — accepting that returns are not guaranteed. The order matters: earn, save, then invest; investing money you cannot afford to lose, or before any saving exists, reverses the plan.

## Sub lesson 2 — Credit and risk management

The guide's second pair is **credit** and **risk**. Credit — borrowing — can bring forward what income cannot yet buy, a home or training; used badly it eats future income in interest and repayment. The plan therefore treats credit as a tool with a price, matched to what the borrowing buys and what the income can carry.

**Risk management** is the plan's protection: keeping an emergency reserve, insuring against the losses that would break the plan, and not placing everything on one outcome.

Credit and risk are the two places a plan most often fails — borrowing without a repayment path, and one uninsured shock wiping out years of saving.

## Sub lesson 3 — The personal financial plan — and education's effect on lifetime income

The chapter closes by joining the parts into one **personal financial plan**: goals set in order, earning grown deliberately, saving made automatic, investing matched to the goal and the risk, credit used at a known price, and the whole protected against shocks — reviewed as life changes.

Its last teaching point reaches back to the start: **education choices affect lifetime income**. The years and the kind of study a person chooses change the earning the whole plan is built on, for decades. That is why the guide puts this chapter at the end of the course: the pupil's next big economic decision is their own education, and it is a financial decision too.
