# Fixed exchange rates

## Do it first

The government, not the market, sets the price of the birr — and one morning it announces a new price. What has it just done, and what grows in the shadows if the price is wrong?

## Sub lesson 1 — Set by decision — devaluation and revaluation

Under a fixed regime the government sets the rate and moves it by decision. Raising the domestic-currency price of foreign money is devaluation; lowering it is revaluation. The words belong to decree, not to the market.

## Sub lesson 2 — Undervalued and overvalued

A deliberately undervalued rate works as an import tax plus an export subsidy — exports cheap abroad, imports dear at home — though it also raises imported-input prices. An overvalued rate does the reverse, cheapening imports and taxing exporters.

## Sub lesson 3 — The parallel market

The regime's characteristic disease is the parallel, or black, market that opens when the official price is wrong: if the state sells dollars too cheaply, a second price grows beside the official board, and the gap between them measures the error.
