# Saving and rationalising consumption

## Do it first

A shop sells a laptop for 1,500 riyals today. Next month the same laptop will be on sale for 1,100 riyals. Before you read on, say: buy now, or wait — and why? Hold your answer — the lesson names the habit that answers it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Producer and consumer — who makes, who uses

In Saudi Arabia's Grade 4 social studies book, Unit 4 — Economics — **Lesson 15, Resources and consumption** — begins by naming the two people in every act of consumption.

The **producer** is the one who makes goods and provides services. The **consumer** is the one who uses those goods and services. The dates grower produces; the family that eats the dates consumes. Every learner is a consumer every day — and this lesson teaches two habits for consuming well.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saving — setting aside part of your money for future needs

The first habit is **saving (الادخار)**: setting aside part of one's money for future needs, instead of spending all of it now.

Money set aside is not lost. It waits — for a need that has not arrived yet, for something bigger than today's small wants. Saving turns part of today's money into tomorrow's security, and it is the consumer's own decision, made before the money is gone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rationalising consumption — using resources and money moderately

The second habit is **rationalising consumption (ترشيد الاستهلاك)**: using resources and money moderately — taking what is needed, and no more.

The book's examples are everyday ones. **Switching off unused devices** instead of leaving them running. **Using water as needed**, not letting it flow for nothing. And **choosing moderately priced goods**: the laptop at **1,500 riyals now** against the same laptop at **1,100 riyals on sale next month** — waiting for the moderate price is rationalising consumption with money, just as the switched-off device is rationalising it with electricity.

Producer and consumer, saving for future needs, and consumption kept moderate: that is Lesson 15's whole teaching on how a consumer handles money and resources well.

## Say it to your companion

Explain saving and rationalising consumption to your companion in your own words: who the producer and the consumer are, what saving is, and the book's three examples of rationalising consumption — including the laptop and its two prices. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
