# Electronic and mobile banking

## Do it first

Your salary lands in your account without you visiting a bank, and you pay a shop by tapping a card. Which of those two moved YOUR money, and could either of them overspend you? Say your answers first.

## Sub lesson 1 — E-banking and its three aspects

E-banking is electronic funds transfer, with three key aspects: ATMs, which give cash and account services beyond branch hours — for fees the lesson notes; direct deposit through the automated clearing house, landing salaries, tax refunds and benefits straight in the account; and debit cards. Mobile banking extends the same account to the phone.

## Sub lesson 2 — Debit versus credit — whose money?

The contrast that matters: a debit card spends your own money and cannot overspend the account; a credit card spends the bank's money and charges interest on it. Same plastic shape, opposite dangers — the debit card fails when the account empties, the credit card lets you spend into debt.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mobile banking weighed

Mobile banking brings anywhere-anytime access against two costs: security risks, and limited capability compared with a full bank. The book's snapshot — over 20% of US mobile owners had used mobile payment by its writing — marks how new the habit then was; the weighing of gain against risk is the permanent part.
