# How banks began — goldsmiths to banknotes

## Do it first

You own something precious and your house is not safe. Before you read on, say where you would leave it, what proof you would want that it is yours, and what could happen to that proof over time. Hold your answers — banks begin exactly there.

In Yemen's Grade 11 economics book (Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad), **Unit 5, Lesson 2 — Banks** — opens with the **emergence of banks**: where banks came from.

## Sub lesson 1 — Goldsmiths hold people's valuables

The story starts with **goldsmiths**: craftsmen of precious metal, whose workshops held the strong chests and safes of their time.

Because their keeping was safe, **people left their valuables with the goldsmiths** for safekeeping — the first service of banking, holding what is precious for others, existed before any bank did.

## Sub lesson 2 — Paper receipts for what was held

For what was left with him, the goldsmith **issued a paper receipt**: a written acknowledgement of the valuables in his keeping, which the owner could present to reclaim them.

The receipt turned a stored treasure into a document — and a document can travel in ways a chest of valuables cannot. That is the hinge of the whole story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Receipts become banknotes

People found they could pay with the **receipts themselves**: instead of reclaiming the valuables for every purchase, the receipt changed hands, and each new holder trusted it against the goldsmith's store.

Receipts circulating as money are **banknotes** in embryo. The goldsmith, keeper of valuables and issuer of trusted paper, has become a banker — and the bank has begun.
