# The state's silver rescate monopoly and the moneda feble

## Sub lesson 1 — The rescate — only the state may buy the silver

Miners were obliged by law to sell all their silver to the state for minting: the rescate. Only the state could buy silver, and it set the price — a monopoly over the country's chief product.

## Sub lesson 2 — A camouflaged tax — 26% and 18% below the world price

The state paid below the international price — 26% below in 1829 and 18% below in 1850 — a camouflaged, hidden tax on the miners. The Grade 10 book names the same mechanism as one reason mining was held back: state protectionism and implicit taxes raised costs. Alongside the rescate circulated the moneda feble, debased coinage.

## Sub lesson 3 — 1873 — free export ends the rescate

In 1873 free export of silver pastes was decreed, abolishing the rescate monopoly — and foreign investment flowed in. The Grade 10 book sets the end inside Mitre's phases of silver mining: recession to the mid-19th century, transition 1850–73, boom 1873–95.
