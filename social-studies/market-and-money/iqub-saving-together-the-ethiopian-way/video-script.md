(a case-study script telling one iqub round from first contribution to last payout)

Iqub (equb) is an indigenous Ethiopian revolving-savings practice, entirely outside the formal banking system — older than modern banking and rooted in community labour-exchange customs. Members save together by taking turns at one shared pot.

Groups of usually 10–30 trusted members contribute a fixed amount daily, weekly or monthly, under a small management of chairperson and secretary. At each meeting the pooled sum goes to one member, chosen by lottery or by urgency, with members naming guarantors. A round lasts from weeks to two years and ends when every member has received the pot once.

Historically iqub financed people who had no bank collateral to offer. It remains an important source of finance for both consumption and investment, especially for small and medium businesses.
