# The two-sector model — households and firms only

## Do it first

Imagine an island with only families and workshops: no government, no saving, no ships. Every birr a workshop receives came from a family. Where must every birr end up? Say it as an equation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who is in the model

The two-sector model is a private, closed economy of households and firms only. Households own the factors and supply their services; firms produce and sell everything to households.

[Picture 24048 — the two-box flow diagram with its assumption list pinned beside it]

## Sub lesson 2 — Its explicit assumptions

The model assumes: households spend all their income on consumption; there is no saving, no government, and no exports or imports. The assumptions are stated out loud so the model's simplifications are never mistaken for the world.

## Sub lesson 3 — What the model proves

Under those assumptions, total money receipts of firms therefore equal total household income: every birr flows round the full circle and comes back, because there is nowhere for it to leak out.
