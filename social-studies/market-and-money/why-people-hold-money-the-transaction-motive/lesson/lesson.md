# Why people hold money 1 — the transaction motive

## Do it first

Pay arrives on the 1st; spending happens every single day. Sketch the cash in a worker's pocket across the month — high after payday, low before. That sawtooth is the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — From the old view to Keynes

The older view held money is demanded to complete transactions, rising in booms and falling in slumps. Keynes reframed demand as liquidity preference — the demand for money to hold — and split it into motives, of which the transaction motive comes first.

## Sub lesson 2 — The income motive

The transaction motive's household face is the income motive: households bridge the gap between pay arriving weekly or monthly and spending happening daily. The amount held depends on income size, the pay interval and local payment methods.

## Sub lesson 3 — The business motive

The business motive is the firms' face of the same motive: firms keep ready cash for materials, transport, wages and current expenses. The amount a firm holds depends largely on its turnover — bigger flow, bigger bridge.
