# Transshipment and the container port — short answer

Answer in one or two sentences, from the lesson.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Why does cargo sometimes need a port in between its start and its destination?
**Answer:** Because there is no direct export–import route joining the two; the cargo is unloaded at the in-between port and loaded onto a second vessel that completes the journey.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What two measures does the book give for the Grand Bahama container port?
**Answer:** It handles over 10% of global container traffic, and it pays $25–30 million a year in local wages.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Is the transshipment port the cargo's home or its destination? Say what it is instead.
**Answer:** Neither — it is the crossing point where cargo changes vessels.
