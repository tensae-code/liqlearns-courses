## Sub lesson 1 — The intertemporal budget constraint

The **intertemporal budget constraint** sets **consumption now against consumption later** — saving moves spending into the future, borrowing pulls it into the present.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing now against later

The household picks its point on the constraint by its **preferences and the interest rate** — the price of consuming now rather than later.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why US saving rates are low

On **Bureau of Economic Analysis data**, **US saving rates are low** — the book asks why Americans choose so much consumption now over later.
