## Sub lesson 1 — Capital can be social, private or public

The senior book classifies Venezuela's industries a second way — not by what they make, but by **who owns them**. An industry's capital may be **social** (communities), **private** (one person) or **public** (the State).

Three owners, three kinds of capital. The forms in the next parts are what those ownership kinds look like as working enterprises.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mixed, social-property and social-distribution enterprises

Three forms follow. **Mixed enterprises** share their capital public/private — the book's example is Petrozumano S.A., with PDVSA and China capital. **Indirect social-property enterprises** are owned by the State in the community's name and are transferable to communities.

And **social distribution enterprises** reinvest their gains for social benefit. Ownership form decides where the gains go — that is the classification's point.

## Sub lesson 3 — Self-management, recovered enterprises and FONDEN

Two more forms complete the set: **self-management enterprises** — cooperatives using their own resources — and **recovered or nationalised enterprises controlled by their workers**.

Behind the forms stands a funder: **FONDEN** provides resources for industrial development. Venezuela's industry, in this chapter, is a family of ownership forms — and the State funds its growth.
