# Financial intermediaries — savers to borrowers

## Do it first

A farmer has Birr 5,000 spare after harvest; a tailor across town needs Birr 5,000 for a machine. They have never met. How does the money travel safely between them? Say your answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Standing between surplus and deficit

Financial intermediation is the standing-between: intermediaries take funds from surplus units — savers with more than they spend — and pass them to deficit units — borrowers who need more than they have. The Commercial Bank of Ethiopia is the book's example of the intermediary in the middle.

## Sub lesson 2 — The benefits to each side

Each side gains. Depositors get safety for their money plus interest on it. Borrowers and investors get funds, at interest, that they could not gather alone. Pupils are sent to list the intermediaries in their own town — the system is not abstract; it stands on their street.

## Sub lesson 3 — Finance, the blood of the economy

The unit's organising image: finance is the blood of the economy. As blood carries what each part of the body needs, intermediaries carry funds from where they idle to where they work. Block the flow and healthy parts starve — the image carries the whole of Unit 5.
