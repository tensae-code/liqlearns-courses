**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Mongolia, Palestine, Dominica, Cambodia, Yemen, Vietnam, Armenia). Zimbabwe is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere. Nepal is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 8 Teacher's Guide G8): Grade 8 economics is the money unit — it starts from barter in a story, moves to the media of monetary transaction (notes, cheques, ATM cards, online payment) and the central bank's sole right to issue notes, then to the financial institutions of the pupil's own locality, and outwards to tourism, foreign trade and foreign employment. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only, counted nowhere.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 9 Social Science student): the new-format Grade 9 book gives Economics a full theme, "State and Economy": systems first (with Smith, Marx and Keynes as the thinkers), then the market and where it fails, then Armenia's own financial system and the state's economic and social policies, closing with Lorenz/Gini inequality graphs, World Bank income groups and the SDGs.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 6 Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 6's financial literacy is a tour of Zimbabwe's own financial institutions by name — CBZ, Stanbic and FBC banks, NBS building society, Zimnat and Nicoz Diamond insurers — with microfinance explained through market stalls and sewing projects that big banks will not lend to.


Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 9 Social Studies 9): the labour market is taught with real Mongolian wage schedules in tögrög, a full payslip worked line by line, and national statistics pupils must compute rates from.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 12 Economics): the final-year course turns practical — the market is studied the way a Cambodian trader meets it (consumers, prices, banks, riel exchange), and macro policy is taught as coordination: monetary policy, exchange rates and public finance fitted together, closing with foreign trade and the world trading system.
