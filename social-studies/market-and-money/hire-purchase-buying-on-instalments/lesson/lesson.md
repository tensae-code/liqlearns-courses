# Hire purchase — buying on instalments

## Do it first

A colour TV costs Shs 19,000 and you have Shs 3,000 today. The shop offers you the TV now if you pay the Shs 3,000 and then a fixed sum every month. How many payments would you make in all, and what is each kind of payment called? Say it before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Buying now, paying over time

Kenya's Form 1 Business Studies book teaches **hire purchase** through a priced shop example. Hire purchase is a way of buying where the buyer takes the good and pays for it over time instead of paying the whole price at once: a first payment is made at the start, and the rest follows in regular payments.

The book's example is the **Akiba Hire-Purchase Shop** and its **colour TV, priced at Shs 19,000**. The whole lesson is that one offer, taken apart.

## Sub lesson 2 — The deposit — the first payment

The first payment in hire purchase is the **deposit** — the sum paid at the start, before the rest is spread out. In the Akiba example the deposit is **Shs 3,000**, paid on the Shs 19,000 colour TV.

The deposit is what separates hire purchase from paying cash in full: the buyer does not need the whole Shs 19,000 on the day. Paying the deposit starts the agreement, and the balance is left to the payments that follow.

## Sub lesson 3 — The instalments — the payments that follow

After the deposit come the **instalments** — the regular payments that clear the rest. In the Akiba example the instalments are **Shs 1,166.90 × 12**: twelve equal payments of Shs 1,166.90 each.

Count the shape, not just the numbers. One deposit first — Shs 3,000. Then twelve instalments — Shs 1,166.90 each, Shs 14,002.80 in all. Deposit plus instalments is how the hire-purchase buyer pays: part now, the rest over time, in fixed regular sums.

## Say it to your companion
