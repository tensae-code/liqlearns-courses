# Wages — nominal and real, piece-rate and time-rate

## Do it first

Two workers are each paid the same money sum at the end of the month. In the same month, the prices in the shops rise. Are the two workers really paid the same as last month? Say what changed, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nominal wage — the money sum

Belarus's Grade 9 book meets wages inside the chapter on the person as a consumer, and it starts from what a wage is: **wages are the price of labour** — the payment a worker receives for their work.

The first way of looking at that payment is the money itself. The **nominal wage** is the money sum the worker receives — the amount written on the pay, counted in money.

## Sub lesson 2 — Real wage — what the money buys

The second way of looking at the same payment is what it buys. The **real wage** is the goods and services the money wage can buy at a given price level.

That is why the same nominal wage is not always the same real wage. If prices rise while the money sum stays the same, the real wage falls — the worker's pay buys less. If prices fall, the same money sum buys more, and the real wage rises. To know what a worker is really paid, look past the money sum to what it buys.

## Sub lesson 3 — Piece-rate and time-rate — the two forms of pay

The book names two main forms of wage. Under the **piece-rate** form (здзельная), pay is counted **per unit produced** — the more units the worker makes, the more they are paid. Under the **time-rate** form (пачасовая), pay is counted **per hour actually worked** — the worker is paid for the time they worked.

On top of these forms, a worker's pay can also carry **bonuses** and a **share in profit** (profit-sharing). The form decides what the pay is counted by — units made, or hours worked.
