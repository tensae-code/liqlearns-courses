# Kinds of money — paper and credit money

## Sub lesson 1 — Paper money

The Class 12 money chapter sorts money into kinds, starting with **paper money**: **convertible** paper, which the issuer promises to convert, against **inconvertible** paper, which stands on trust and law alone; and paper as **limited** or **unlimited legal tender** — acceptable for small payments only, or for payments of any size.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cheques — traveller's, bearer, order and crossed

The chapter's credit instruments begin with **cheques**: the **traveller's cheque**, the **bearer cheque** (paid to whoever holds it), the **order cheque** (paid to the named person or their order) and the **crossed cheque** (paid only through a bank account).

## Sub lesson 3 — Bills of exchange, promissory notes, bank drafts and credit cards

The credit family closes with **bills of exchange** (including the **sight bill**, payable on sight), **promissory notes** (a written promise to pay), **bank drafts** and **credit cards** — written money that moves value without coins or notes changing hands.
