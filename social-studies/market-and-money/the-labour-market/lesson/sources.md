Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales; Bolivia Grade 10 Texto de Aprendizaje Ciencias Sociales Secundaria 4): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation. alongside the national story (Sucre's tax design, the silver cycle, the rubber boom), this year carries the wave's most textbook-like basic economics in the Bolivia set — supply and demand, the production–distribution–consumption cycle and the three sectors, all inside a unit on natural resources and Mother Earth.

United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

India's own way (India Grade 9 Economics): The whole first Economics course is built on Indian cases — a fictional UP village for production, two boys (Sakal who studies, Vilas who cannot) for human capital, Tendulkar poverty-line numbers, and the FCI/PDS machinery for food security.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 11 Economic & Legal Education): the macro topics are taught from Vietnam's own statistics and history — the CPI chart 2016–2021, the 775% inflation of 1986, COVID-era unemployment of 3.2%, the Q3 2022 labour-market bulletin — and the State's controlling role is quoted from the Law on the State Bank and the Labour Code.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 5 Year 5 Social Studies): A full primary money curriculum in one unit — work and wages first, then the Fiji dollar against other currencies, then a family budget, a personal saving plan and what banks do.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 6 Year 6 Social Science): The Year 5 money unit is deepened with employer-employee relations, a worked fortnightly family budget, and income tax and FNPF deductions explained through what the government pays for.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Bahamas' own way: labour is taught inside a "conservation" frame — human resources alongside natural resources — and the labour system is divided into the government sector and the private sector.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 9 Social Studies 9): the labour market is taught with real Mongolian wage schedules in tögrög, a full payslip worked line by line, and national statistics pupils must compute rates from.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 1 G1 Teacher CCSS) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide mirrors the student book lesson by lesson with teaching notes; the one guide-only Economics statement placed here is the basic principle of work stated for teachers — equal work, equal pay. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Honduras is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 9 G9 Student CCSS): Grade 9 closes the cycle with the world economy and Honduras inside it — work formal and informal, the lempira's devaluation named the country's biggest problem, and remittances weighed honestly: a strong boost to the economy that also demotivates production and drains the labour force.
