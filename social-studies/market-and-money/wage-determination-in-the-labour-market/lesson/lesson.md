# Wage determination in the labour market

## Do it first

Labour is bought and sold like grain — but the 'grain' can refuse, move towns, or stay home. What sets its price?

## Sub lesson 1 — The wage is a price

The wage settles where labour demand meets labour supply. Demand slopes downward from firms — at lower wages, more workers are worth hiring. Supply slopes upward — higher wages draw people from leisure, household production and other markets.

## Sub lesson 2 — The perfectly competitive labour market

The book's traits: many firms hiring identical labour, many identical workers, wage-taking on both sides, and free information and mobility. No single firm or worker can set the wage — the market hands it to both.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading the supply curve

The supply curve's height at any point reads as the marginal opportunity cost of the last hour worked: what that worker gave up — rest, home production, another job — to work it. Wages must beat that cost, hour by hour.
