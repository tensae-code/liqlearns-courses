# Fixed or floating — comparing regimes

## Do it first

You must choose a regime for a small trading country. Certainty for its firms, or freedom for its policies? There is no costless answer — say which cost you would pay, first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The case for fixed

Fixed rates give firms certainty, can suppress speculation if believed, and discipline government policy — a government that must defend a rate cannot print carelessly. The certainty is real while the promise holds.

## Sub lesson 2 — The price of fixed

But fixed rates cannot respond to shocks, demand large reserves to defend, invite speculative runs when a change is suspected, carry a deflationary bias, and can conflict with domestic targets. The discipline becomes a straitjacket exactly when the economy needs movement.

## Sub lesson 3 — The case and price of floating

Floating rates absorb external shocks, free domestic policy, and self-correct payments deficits. Their price: instability, the loss of that discipline, and speculation that can move rates away from trade fundamentals. The honest trade-off has no winner — only a choice of risks.
