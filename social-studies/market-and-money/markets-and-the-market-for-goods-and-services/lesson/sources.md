**Acceptance count: taught in 17 countries (Ethiopia, France, Laos, Mongolia, Rwanda, Palestine, Cambodia, Bangladesh, Yemen, Vietnam, Armenia, Nepal, Bulgaria, Iran, Iraq, India, Colombia). Guatemala is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Colombia's own way (Colombia Grade 5 Escuela Nueva G5 Cartilla 2): the fullest primary Economics unit in the set — work, goods, sectors, money, prices and the market are all taught from the child's own plaza de mercado, ending in a real market-day fieldwork and market survey.

India's own way (India Grade 7 Social and Political Life-II): Markets are taught as a chain you can walk — from a cotton farmer tied to a trader's loan, through ginning, spinning, putting-out weavers and an export factory, to a $26 shirt in a US store, with the money split counted at every step.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Economics): a full Western-style microeconomics course rooted in Iraq — economics itself is traced to Mesopotamia (the first banks at Nasiriyah, loan-interest rules, Hammurabi's laws), worked examples run in dinars on chickens, notebooks, dates and children's toys, and the monopoly and oligopoly examples are Iraq's white-oil distribution and its three mobile-phone companies.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 Social Studies & Human Value Education G4 trial): the new trial curriculum teaches the village economy as one circle — make it locally (bamboo craft, sugarcane gud, Dhaka cloth), sell it at the weekly haat bazaar, earn income from the work, and keep what is left in the child's khutruke (piggy bank) for future use.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 9 Social Science student): the new-format Grade 9 book gives Economics a full theme, "State and Economy": systems first (with Smith, Marx and Keynes as the thinkers), then the market and where it fails, then Armenia's own financial system and the state's economic and social policies, closing with Lorenz/Gini inequality graphs, World Bank income groups and the SDGs.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Contents misprint named here: the student book's contents page prints the title of Unit 3 as "Markerts" for Markets. The unit itself is Markets, as this lesson names it.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Guatemala's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the market is learned by running one in the classroom — pupil firms with stalls, set prices and sales strategies across three selling rounds, facing buyers handed different amounts of symbolic money.

Laos' own way (Laos Grade 11 Business student): the market is defined three ways at once — everyday (a place to buy and sell), economic (the group of buyers and sellers who exchange), and marketing (people with a need and the money to pay) — and costs are drilled with the SMOC sorting exercise (Salary, Material, Other, Capital).

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies 8): the economy is taught from the home district outward — pupils map the enterprises around their own school, work a flour price schedule, then write a real small-business plan.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 3 Technology and Entrepreneurship): Grade 3 turns the concepts into team projects with real selling — a charity Martenitsa bazaar and a farmers' market where each group must research its product, set its price and present it to clients.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 7 Social Studies): production and consumption are taught with Iranian anchors throughout — the historic bazaar as market and social centre, compulsory third-party car insurance, and the Silk Road crossing Iran as a bridge between East and West.
