**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, France, Laos, Mongolia, Rwanda, Palestine, Cambodia, Bangladesh, Yemen, Vietnam). Guatemala is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Contents misprint named here: the student book's contents page prints the title of Unit 3 as "Markerts" for Markets. The unit itself is Markets, as this lesson names it.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Guatemala's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the market is learned by running one in the classroom — pupil firms with stalls, set prices and sales strategies across three selling rounds, facing buyers handed different amounts of symbolic money.

Laos' own way (Laos Grade 11 Business student): the market is defined three ways at once — everyday (a place to buy and sell), economic (the group of buyers and sellers who exchange), and marketing (people with a need and the money to pay) — and costs are drilled with the SMOC sorting exercise (Salary, Material, Other, Capital).

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies 8): the economy is taught from the home district outward — pupils map the enterprises around their own school, work a flour price schedule, then write a real small-business plan.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.
