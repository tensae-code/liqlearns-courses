# Devaluation and inflation — Ethiopia 2010 and 2017

## Do it first

A devaluation is meant to help exporters. Within a year, shoppers are paying a third more for goods. Connect the two events with a chain of prices before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — August 2010 — 20% devaluation, 33% inflation

The National Bank of Ethiopia devalued by 20% in August 2010, from 13.63 to 16.35 birr per dollar — check: (16.35 − 13.63) / 13.63 ≈ 0.20. The aim was foreign-exchange earnings; inflation of about 33% followed in 2011, as imports priced in dollars became dearer in birr.

## Sub lesson 2 — October 2017 — 15% more

A further 15% devaluation came on 10 October 2017, to encourage exports, relieve the foreign-exchange shortage and suppress the black market. The World Bank had urged a devaluation of at least 10% on competitiveness grounds.

## Sub lesson 3 — The general lesson

Measured CPI inflation rose from 2.8% in 2010 to 20.2% in 2020 across the decade of the weaker birr, and the book generalises: in Ethiopia devaluation reliably feeds inflation. The export gain and the price pain arrive together — which is why this tool is never free.
