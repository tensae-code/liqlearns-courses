# Palestine's trade links — ports and crossings

## Do it first

Before you read on, write down what you already think this lesson's question is — palestine's trade relations across history, from its central location — in one sentence of your own. Keep it; you will check it at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — A central location, traded across history

Palestine's Grade 5 book teaches its trade from its map: **Palestine's central location gave it trade relations across history**. Where a country sits is its first trade fact — routes between neighbours and seas have passed through it for as long as there has been trade to carry.

## Sub lesson 2 — The links — Haifa, the Hejaz railway, Karama and Rafah

The book names the links one by one: **Haifa port on the Mediterranean**, the **Hejaz railway**, the **Karama crossing to Jordan**, and the **Rafah crossing to Egypt**. Sea, rail and two land crossings — Palestine's gateways to the world, each with a name and a direction.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gaza airport, and crossings under occupation control

Two facts close the lesson. **Gaza International Airport opened in 1998 and was destroyed by the occupation in 2001** — an air gateway built and then lost. And over all the links stands the present condition the book states plainly: **the occupation controls Palestine's crossings and transport routes**. The gateways are Palestine's; the control of them is not.
