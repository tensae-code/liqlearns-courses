# Actors of the economy and their roles

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold the lesson's one thing in mind and say what you already think it means: The economy's actors are producers (who invest capital, produce and supply goods and services, choosing what, how much and how to produce for maximum profit within limited resources), consumers (who buy to satisfy needs and so orient and motivate production, including the shift to green consumption), intermediaries (distributors, brokers and job-introduction centres who bridge production and consumption and supply market information) and the State. Hold your answer — the lesson takes it in parts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Producers and consumers — the two poles

**Producers** invest capital, produce and supply goods and services — and must choose **what to produce, how much and how**, for maximum profit within limited resources. **Consumers** buy to satisfy needs, and so **orient and motivate production** — including the shift the book names to **green consumption**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Intermediaries — the bridge

Between the poles stand the **intermediaries**: **distributors, securities and real-estate brokers, and job-introduction centres**. They **bridge production and consumption** and **supply market information** — the economy's go-betweens, without whom producers and consumers would meet blindly.

## Sub lesson 3 — The State — the fourth actor

The fourth actor is **the State**, which orients and regulates the whole economy (its full working-out is taught in the lesson on the State as an economic actor). The book's cast is complete: producers, consumers, intermediaries and the State — every economic act belongs to one of them.
