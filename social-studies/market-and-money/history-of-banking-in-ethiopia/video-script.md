# Video script — History of banking in Ethiopia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Banking opens with the Bank of Abyssinia, founded 1905/06 under Menelik II by agreement with the National Bank of Egypt on a 50-year concession, branching to Harar (1906), Dire Dawa (1908), Gore (1912) and Dessie and Djibouti (1920), financing the Franco-Ethiopian railway, and liquidated in 1931. The Bank of Ethiopia (1931), the first government-controlled bank, was liquidated at the Italian invasion of 1936; Italian banks served 1936–41, and Barclays Bank 1941–43.

The State Bank of Ethiopia (1943) combined central and commercial banking with 21 branches including Khartoum, until the 1963 split (Proclamation 206/1963, operating January 1964) created the National Bank of Ethiopia and the Commercial Bank of Ethiopia. Under socialism (1974–91) came nationalisation: Addis Bank formed in 1976 and merged into CBE in 1980, and finally just three banks stood — NBE, CBE and AIDB.

Liberalisation from 1991 changed the structure again: the Monetary and Banking Proclamation 83/1994 opened banking to private owners, and the modern private-bank era began. The timeline's shape is the lesson: one foreign-concession bank, state banks, a three-bank monopoly — then a flowering of many private banks after 1994.

Walk the timeline. 1905: the Bank of Abyssinia opens under Menelik the Second. 1931: the Bank of Ethiopia, the first government-controlled bank. Then Italian banks, then Barclays. 1943: the State Bank of Ethiopia, both central and commercial. 1963: it splits into the National Bank and the Commercial Bank. Under socialism only three banks stand. 1994: the law opens banking to private owners, and the modern era begins.
