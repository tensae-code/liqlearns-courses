# Video script — Exchange rates — the price of a currency

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An exchange rate prices one currency in another. The same leather jacket at 1,500 birr costs a foreign buyer 1,500 × 2 = 3,000 rupees at 2 rupees per birr, and 1,500 × 3 = 4,500 rupees at 3 rupees per birr. Nothing about the jacket changed — the currency's price did.

A stronger birr prices Ethiopian goods out of foreign markets: exports fall and imports, now cheaper in birr, rise — net exports fall and aggregate demand shifts left. A depreciation runs the film backwards, shifting AD right, which is why exchange rates move the whole economy, not just travellers.

Always ask which currency is being priced. If 1 US dollar exchanges for 30 birr, the rate is 30 birr per dollar in one direction and 1/30 ≈ 0.033 dollars per birr in the other. The two quotations are the same price read from opposite sides.
