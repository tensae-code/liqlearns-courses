# The money and commercial economy

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: working in diverse activities for money. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Working in diverse activities for money

Venezuela's Grade 4 book defines its second economy by its work: people work in **diverse activities — agriculture, livestock, mining, industry, commerce, services** — and receive **money** in exchange for their work.

That is the **monetary and commercial economy (economía comercial / dineraria)**: work is specialised, and the link between one person's work and another's product is money.

## Sub lesson 2 — Money buys what others produce

With that money, the book says, people **pay for services** and **buy food and objects produced by other people**.

Nobody in this economy makes everything they use — the opposite of the subsistence economy on the same page. Money is what lets a miner eat bread he did not bake and a baker use tools he did not forge.

## Sub lesson 3 — The cost to nature — and two economies coexisting

The book judges its own economy: the monetary and commercial economy **in many cases harms nature**, because nature is **exploited exaggeratedly to obtain many products to sell**.

And it closes with coexistence, not replacement: in many countries the **subsistence and monetary economies coexist** — the two economies of this chapter live side by side in one country.
