# How money evolved — commodity, paper, cheques, electronic

## Do it first

Line these up by the burden of carrying Birr 10,000: salt bars, gold coins, paper notes, a cheque book, a phone. Which end of the line is oldest? Arrange before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Commodity money and paper

The stages in order: commodity money — a valuable commodity itself, but heavy and hard to transport. Then paper currency, at first convertible into coin or precious metal, then fiat money — paper decreed legal tender by government, accepted only where the issuing authority is trusted and counterfeiting is hard.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cheques

Cheques are drawn on a bank account, payable on demand. They remove the need to carry bulk currency, can be cut to any amount up to the balance, are safer against theft, and give a receipt as they go.

## Sub lesson 3 — Electronic payment

Finally come electronic means of payment, where paperwork disappears altogether: value moves as signals between accounts. Each stage kept money's job and shed its weight — that is the pattern of the whole evolution.
