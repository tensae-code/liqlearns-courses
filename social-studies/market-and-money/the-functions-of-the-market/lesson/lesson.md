# The functions of the market

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold the lesson's one thing in mind and say what you already think it means: The market has three functions: recognition (it recognises the social usefulness of a good and the labour spent on it, through whether the good sells and at what price), information (it signals changes in social demand — quantity, quality, types, supply–demand structure, prices) and regulation (on that information producers and consumers adjust, so production and consumption are stimulated or restrained). Hold your answer — the lesson takes it in parts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Recognition — the market judges the good

The market's first function is **recognition**: it recognises the **social usefulness of a good and the labour spent on it** — through **whether the good sells, and at what price**. A good nobody buys has not been recognised, whatever labour went into it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Information — the market signals demand

The second function is **information**: the market **signals changes in social demand** — quantity, quality, types, supply–demand structure and prices. The book's case: a garment company **re-plans production from its sales reports** — the market speaking, the firm listening.

## Sub lesson 3 — Regulation — actors adjust

The third function is **regulation**: on that information, **producers and consumers adjust**, so production and consumption are **stimulated or restrained**. The book's case is pork: African swine fever first flooded, then emptied, the pork supply — and prices swung as the market regulated.
