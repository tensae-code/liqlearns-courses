# Money as a store of value — and liquidity

## Do it first

You are paid today but the rent is due in three weeks. In what do you 'keep' the rent money — cash, a goat, a sack of grain, a plot of land? Which can turn back into rent fastest?

## Sub lesson 1 — Storing purchasing power

Money stores purchasing power from when income is received until it is spent: the birr in a drawer on payday is next month's bread, held in portable form. Other assets — stocks, bonds, land, houses, jewellery — also store value, and may pay more.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why hold money then? Liquidity

The answer is liquidity: the relative ease and speed with which an asset can be turned into a medium of exchange — in which money is unmatched. A house is wealth that cannot buy tonight's dinner; money is wealth that already is dinner's price.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its weakness — and two classic definitions

The guide completes the point (teacher guide — evidence): money is a poor store of wealth because the purchasing power of money is subject to change. The guide's review answers also preserve two classic definitions (teacher guide — evidence): money as anything habitually and widely used as a means of payment and generally accepted in settlement of debt, and as anything commonly used and generally accepted as a medium of exchange or standard of value.
