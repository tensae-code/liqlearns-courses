# Video script — Financial institutions and the rules of the game

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Institutions are defined as the rules of the game, split into formal institutions — written laws and regulations — and informal ones — customs and conventions. Financial institutions operate under this rules layer: the game cannot be understood from the players alone; the rules shape every move they make.

Financial institutions come in three types: depository institutions (banks taking deposits), contractual institutions (insurance and pensions), and investment institutions (investment banks, underwriters, brokers). Banks' roles in development run deep: mobilising savings, identifying good investments, and exercising corporate control over firms they fund — and risk management stands as a core banking service, with efficiency depending on competition plus regulation.

Alongside the banks stand non-bank financial institutions: bodies without a full banking licence — insurance companies, pawn shops, cheque-cashing outlets, payday lenders, currency exchanges and microloan organisations. They serve customers the banks reach badly, under lighter rules — the street-level edge of the financial system.

## Grade 11 deepening from the Grade 10 teacher guide extension

The teacher guide's key names the financial system as five components working as one system: financial institutions — the banks and insurers themselves; financial markets — where funds trade; financial instruments — the assets and securities traded, such as shares and bills; financial services — the work done for savers and borrowers; and money itself, the medium the whole system moves. Any account of a financial system that leaves one of the five out is describing a part, not the whole.
