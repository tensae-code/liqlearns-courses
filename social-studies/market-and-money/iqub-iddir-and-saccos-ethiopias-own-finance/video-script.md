# Video script — Iqub, Iddir and SACCOs — Ethiopia's own finance

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iqub is the indigenous rotating savings club: members contribute Birr 100–10,000 by capacity, weekly, monthly or daily, and the pooled pot goes to one member in turn. Membership forms around workplaces, ethnicity, trade, schools and neighbourhoods — from shoe-shine boys to officials — and the pot serves weddings, funerals, sickness and house building.

The book's Aredo 1993 list of why people prefer Iqub: solidarity; forced contractual saving — the group makes you save; flexibility; low default; near-zero administration cost; no minimum threshold to join; and the attraction of the lottery draw that orders the turns. It is a bank's services rebuilt out of trust and routine.

Iddir is the funeral-focused mutual-aid association: monthly contributions, written by-laws, its own tents, chairs and utensils, its fund held mostly in a bank account — also helping with medical costs, fire and theft, with social sanctions enforcing attendance. SACCOs (savings and credit cooperatives) form the semi-formal tier beyond the central bank's control, dating to the late 1960s, stalled after 1974, then growing fast, with potential for short-term agricultural loans.
