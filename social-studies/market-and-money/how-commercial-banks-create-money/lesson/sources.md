Greece's own way: the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches. This lesson is built from that book's placement: Ch.8 §5 Creation of money by commercial banks — teaches: deposit multiplication through lending. The lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 10 Commerce Introductory Book 1974): a full British-style commerce course rebuilt for Ceylon — every concept is landed on Ceylon data: the 1953 census of occupations, Ceylon's trade balances 1954–59, the rice subsidy accounts, mint-par exchange worked in rupees, and Gresham's law and the trade cycle taught a decade before the later Business Studies books appear.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Economics Part II 1979): university-level national accounting taught entirely on Central Bank of Ceylon tables — Sri Lanka's 1974 national product by sector, its December 1974 money supply counted rupee by rupee, and its 1972–74 imported inflation priced in rice, flour and sugar per ton.
