# How commercial banks create money

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.8 §5 Creation of money by commercial banks** — teaches: deposit multiplication through lending. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — A deposit that is lent is not gone

Greece's Grade 12 textbook gives money creation its own section. When a **commercial bank** receives a deposit, it keeps a part as reserve and **lends the rest**. The loan is spent and lands as a **new deposit** in another bank — which lends again.

The original cash has not multiplied; what multiplies is **deposit money** — claims on banks that circulate as money while the reserves stay small beside them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The multiplication chain

Round by round the chain runs: deposit, reserve kept, loan made, new deposit, smaller loan — each round smaller than the last, because each bank keeps its reserve slice.

Added together, the rounds create total deposits several times the original reserve base. The chain's total size is governed by the reserve share banks keep: the smaller the slice kept, the longer and larger the multiplication.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the central bank stands over the chain

Because commercial banks together create money, the system's total is not left to chance: the **central bank** — the bank of issue of the previous lessons — steers it, through the reserves banks must hold and the terms on which it lends to them.

Money creation is thus a partnership the textbook keeps in order: commercial banks create deposit money by lending; the central bank bounds the process from above.
