## Sub lesson 1 — Interest and its kinds

The Class 11 book's interest section first sorts **interest** into its kinds — the payment for the use of money, told apart by what exactly is paid and kept. That sorting is the ground for the theory that follows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keynes's liquidity preference — why people hold money

Then the book teaches **Keynes's liquidity preference theory**: people demand money itself — liquidity — for three motives: the **transaction** motive, the **precautionary** motive and the **speculative** motive. The more interest they must give up, the less money they choose to hold idle.

## Sub lesson 3 — Supply of money, marginal efficiency of capital, and the rate

Against the demand for money stands the **supply of money**. The book sets the rate of interest where the two meet, with the **marginal efficiency of capital** — the expected return on new investment — deciding how much borrowing firms will do at that rate.
