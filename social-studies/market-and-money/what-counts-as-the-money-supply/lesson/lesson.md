# What counts as the money supply

## Do it first

A birr note sits in a bank's vault; another sits in a market woman's purse; a third exists only as a number in a current account. Which of the three is 'money supply' right now? Vote on each before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — The total, at a moment

The supply of money is the total of all forms of money held by the community at a moment: coins, currency notes issued by the currency authority, and chequable bank deposits. 'At a moment' matters — supply is a snapshot, not a flow.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's accounting

In the book's Ethiopian accounting, currency with the public is built from notes in circulation plus birr notes and coins and small coins, minus cash held by banks — the bulk of it notes issued by the National Bank of Ethiopia — plus deposit money in current accounts.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why currency dominates — and who steers supply

Currency dominates in developing countries because most transactions are cash rather than cheque. Overall, supply depends on the credit-control policies of the banking system — the supply of money is steered, not accidental.

## Going further — Older evidence — narrow, quasi and broad money (2011 guide)

An older guide fixes the aggregates in Ethiopian definitions: narrow money = currency in circulation outside the banks + demand deposits; quasi money = savings deposits + time deposits; broad money = narrow + quasi. Its key compares banking performance by regime: under the Derg, credit to the non-government sector declined and state banks performed poorly, obliged to lend to public enterprises, state farms, cooperatives and the central government; under the EPRDF, branch networks kept expanding and credit to the non-government sector increased.
