# How a payslip is worked out — nominal and real wages

## Do it first

A worker is promised 850,000 tögrög a month. Before you read on, say how much of it you think the worker actually takes home — and what stands between the promise and the pay.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nominal wage and real wage — real is nominal divided by the price level

Mongolia's Grade 9 book, in 3.2 How is a wage calculated?, teaches two wages at once. The **nominal wage** is the money sum itself. The **real wage** is what that sum is worth at the prices of the day — the book gives it as a division: **real wage = nominal wage divided by the price level**. The same nominal wage is a smaller real wage when prices are higher.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Mongolian payslip — 850,000 tögrög gross, less social insurance and income tax

Then the book works a **full Mongolian payslip, line by line**. It starts from a **gross pay of 850,000 tögrög**. From it come the deductions: **10% social insurance** and **10% income tax**. A payslip is the wage's own arithmetic — every line is either money in or money taken out, in order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Take-home pay — 695,500 tögrög

The payslip ends at **take-home pay: 695,500 tögrög** — what the worker actually receives after the deductions. That is the number the worker lives on, and the book's point stands behind it: judge a wage by the whole slip — gross, deductions, take-home — and by its real value at today's prices, not by the gross figure alone.
