# Offshore banking and insurance

## Do it first

A hurricane is coming toward the islands. Before you read on, list everything your family owns that could be harmed — and say who, if anyone, would pay to put it right.

## Sub lesson 1 — Financial services — the second industry

The Bahamas' Grade 5 Primary Social Studies Digital Textbook, in its Banks and Insurance Companies spread, ranks the country's industries. Tourism stands first — and **financial services are The Bahamas' second largest industry**.

Financial services means the work of banks and insurance companies: holding money, moving money, lending money and covering risks with money. That this office work — not a crop, not a catch — is the country's second industry is the spread's first teaching point.

## Sub lesson 2 — Offshore banking — a centre for the Americas

The spread names The Bahamas an **offshore banking centre for the Americas**. An **offshore bank** is a bank that does its business mainly for customers and companies from **outside** the country where it sits — money from abroad is held, managed and moved through The Bahamas because the centre is there.

So the country's banks work on two levels at once: the local banks of the islands, under the Central Bank, and the offshore banks whose business arrives from across the Americas. Both levels are financial services, and together they hold the industry's second place.

## Sub lesson 3 — Insurance — cover for life, home and hurricane

Beside the banks, the spread sets the **insurance companies**. Insurance is cover bought in advance: the customer pays the company, and the company pays when the covered harm happens.

The book names the covers: **life, health, home, vehicle and marine** cover — for a person's life and health, for houses, for vehicles, and for the boats and ships of a sea-going country. And it names the claim an island country knows best: **hurricane claims** — when a hurricane damages homes and property, insurance is the money that rebuilds. In The Bahamas, insurance is not an extra; it is part of how the country survives its own weather.
