# Factor payments — rent, wage, interest, profit

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika), **3.6 Rewards of the factors of production** — teaches: rent for land, wage for labour, interest for capital, profit for entrepreneurship. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Each factor has its payment

Greece's Grade 9 workbook closes its factors chapter with the **rewards of the factors of production** — each factor is paid in its own coin: **rent for land, wage for labour, interest for capital, profit for entrepreneurship**.

The four payments are the incomes of the economy's households in their four roles: landowner, worker, capital owner, entrepreneur. One person can receive several at once.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rent and wage

**Rent** is the payment for the use of **land** and natural resources — paid to the owner who lets others work what they own. **Wage** (and salary) is the payment for **labour** — for work done over a time or for a task.

Both are contracted before production: the tenant and the worker know their terms in advance, unlike the entrepreneur of the next sub lesson, who is paid last and by result.

## Sub lesson 3 — Interest and profit

**Interest** is the payment for **capital** — for the use of money or equipment over time, the price of waiting and of the risk of lending. **Profit** is the entrepreneur's reward: what **remains** after rent, wages and interest are paid — which can be large, small, or negative as a loss.

Profit's uncertainty is its defining mark: the other three payments are promised; profit is only hoped for, and it is the hope of it that moves entrepreneurs to organise the other factors at all.
