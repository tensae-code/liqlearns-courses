Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 10 G10 Business and Accounting Studies 1998): the second-year book teaches the services that make trade possible, each one through Sri Lanka's own institutions — the Central Bank, Bank of Ceylon and People's Bank, the CTB and Air Lanka, the Insurance Corporation of Sri Lanka — with insurance first explained as 100 people each paying Rs 100 a month into one shared pot.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 10 Commerce Introductory Book 1974): a full British-style commerce course rebuilt for Ceylon — every concept is landed on Ceylon data: the 1953 census of occupations, Ceylon's trade balances 1954–59, the rice subsidy accounts, mint-par exchange worked in rupees, and Gresham's law and the trade cycle taught a decade before the later Business Studies books appear.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Economics Part II 1979): university-level national accounting taught entirely on Central Bank of Ceylon tables — Sri Lanka's 1974 national product by sector, its December 1974 money supply counted rupee by rupee, and its 1972–74 imported inflation priced in rice, flour and sugar per ton.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Economics Source Book Part I): this is the Ceylon A/L source book — economics taught from its own history first: the feudal manor, the guilds, the Industrial Revolution, then capitalism, socialism and the welfare state, before the theory chapters (demand, the competitive producer, monopoly, wages, rent, interest and profit) — every theory chapter worked through Ceylon-flavoured examples (paddy lands, rupee accounts, the sterling exchange) and closed with a technical-terms glossary giving the English equivalents.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 13 Economics Teacher Guide 2018): the NIE teacher guide teaches the A/L macro course through Sri Lanka's own institutions and data throughout — CBSL's money-supply definitions (M1, M2, M2b, M4), the Colombo and National CPIs, the CBSL's own debt and balance-of-payments tables, Sri Lanka's trade structure 1977-2016, its policy eras 1948-2015, and its current economy (foreign employment and remittances, tourism, the knowledge economy) — the guide's worked examples are Sri Lanka's numbers, not imported ones.
