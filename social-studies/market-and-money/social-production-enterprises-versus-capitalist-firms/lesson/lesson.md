# Social production enterprises versus capitalist firms

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Big farm firms and where their capital comes from

Large farm firms need much capital — more than one farmer can raise. The Grade 5 book says that capital is provided by shareholders, national or foreign, who put money into the firm.

That is the capitalist firm in the countryside: production organised with other people's money, invested so the firm can farm at a large scale.

## Sub lesson 2 — Agroindustry — processing what the farm produces

The book adds a second kind of large firm: the **agroindustry**, which does not stop at growing or raising the product but processes it too.

Its examples are concrete: sugar mills that turn cane into sugar, and pig farms that go on to make sausages. Growing and processing under one roof is what makes the firm an agroindustry.

## Sub lesson 3 — Social production enterprises — a different purpose

Against the capitalist firm the book sets the **social production enterprise**. Its members do not enrich themselves: that is the first difference, and it is about purpose, not size.

What it does with its results is the second difference: it produces and sells more cheaply, distributes surpluses among its associates, and gives social aid to the community around it.
