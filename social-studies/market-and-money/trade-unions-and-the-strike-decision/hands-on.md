# Hands-on — Trade unions and the strike decision

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Rank the strike cards
Write the lesson's strike cards on pieces of paper: lost wages, rent to pay, eviction from the union, eviction from housing, solidarity, and the neighbouring factory's four-day strike that won higher pay. Rank them from the strongest reason to strike to the strongest reason not to, and say out loud why each card goes where it goes — the way the Icelandic pupils rank the cards of Worksheet 15.

## Draw it and check it
Draw the lesson's two sides from memory — what a strike costs on one side, what solidarity can win on the other — and label every part. Then open lesson/lesson.md, compare with the weighing there, and fix every label you missed.

## Teach it on the spot
Close the lesson. Teach the whole of sub lesson 1 aloud, as if to a younger learner, with no notes. Where you stop or guess, that is the part to re-read.
