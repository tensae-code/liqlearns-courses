# Start-up capital and working capital

## Do it first

Imagine you will open a small business next month. Before you read on, list everything you would have to pay for before opening day — and everything you would keep paying for after it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The capital needed to start a business

Both Laos Grade 11 books open their Business course with the same question: **what capital does a business need to start?** The Combined book's Ch. 1 §1 and the Business book's Ch. 1 §4 teach that the money a new business needs splits in two — money to set the business up, and money to keep it running. The next two sub lessons take the two halves in turn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Investment money — spent once, before opening

The first half is **investment money**: money **spent once, before opening**. The Combined book names what it buys — **land, buildings, machinery, equipment and licences**. These are the things the business must have in place before it can open its doors; they are bought once, at the start.

## Sub lesson 3 — Working capital — spent again and again, covering the wait

The second half is **working capital**: money **spent again and again to run** the business — **stock, wages and transport**. And the book adds the point that decides whether a new business survives its first weeks: working capital **must cover the wait before income arrives**. A business that has spent everything on setting up, with nothing left to run on while it waits for its first income, has split its capital wrong.

## Say it to your companion

Explain start-up capital and working capital to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, sort these into investment money and working capital: a licence, wages, machinery, stock, land, transport. Say which kind each one is.

2. A starter spends all her money on a building and equipment and has nothing left for stock and wages while she waits for her first customers to pay. Say what she got wrong, from the lesson.

3. Write the key words from memory — start-up capital, investment money, working capital — and write one sentence of your own for each.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
