# Payment instruments — cheques and orders

## Do it first

A business in Nairobi owes KSh 50,000 to a supplier in another town. Sending that much cash through the post would be slow and unsafe. What written orders could carry the payment instead? Name every one you can think of, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The cheque — an order to the bank

Kenya's Form 1 Business Studies book teaches the means of payment the way a business meets them: as filled specimen documents. The first is the **cheque** — a written order to a bank, telling the bank to pay the amount written on it to the person named.

The book's specimen is a **Kenya Commercial Bank cheque for KSh 50,000** — a real Kenyan bank, a real amount in shillings, filled in the way a business fills it. The cheque does not carry the money itself; it carries the order, and the bank moves the money.

## Sub lesson 2 — Money order and postal order — paying through the post

Two more instruments are bought and sent through the post. A **money order** is an order for a stated sum, bought at the post office and cashed by the person it is sent to. The book's specimen is a **Kenya inland money order — Garissa, Shs 800**: "inland" because the payment stays inside the country.

A **postal order** is the same family in a smaller, fixed amount. The book's specimen is a **Kenya postal order for ten shillings**. Where the cheque orders a bank to pay out of an account, the money order and postal order are paid for first, then sent — the paper itself carries the value.

## Sub lesson 3 — The bill of exchange — a written order to pay

The fourth specimen is the **bill of exchange** — a written order by one party telling another to pay a stated sum. The book's specimen bill is **drawn on Sukuma Bank Ltd**: the bank named on the bill is the one ordered to pay.

Set the four side by side and the family is clear. A cheque orders a bank to pay. A money order and a postal order are bought at the post and sent. A bill of exchange orders the party it is drawn on to pay. All four are **payment instruments** — paper orders that let a business pay without handing over cash.

## Say it to your companion

Explain payment instruments — cheques and orders — to your companion in your own words: what a cheque orders and whose specimen the book shows, how a money order and a postal order pay through the post, and what a bill of exchange is drawn on. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
