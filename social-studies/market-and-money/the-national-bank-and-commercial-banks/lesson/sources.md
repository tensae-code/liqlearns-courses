Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class XI Business and Entrepreneurship XI): Economics enters the business course through Bhutanese law and institutions — the Consumer Protection Act of Bhutan 2012 and the RMA/RSEBL financial system, with ngultrum–rupee parity explained.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG): secondary Social Science carries a full Economics strand — PNG's own tax system (about 75% of the budget from tax, collected by the IRC), the Bank of Papua New Guinea's direct and indirect monetary instruments, a public-service wage bill of over K200 million a fortnight, the Hiri-Motu traditional trade, and development judged against PNG's Five National Goals and Directive Principles. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales 11, Porras y Gamboa): a commercial textbook built on the same national programme as the CONED anthology but with far more economic detail — full PAE I/II/III measure lists, the CAT export-certificate fraud, state banking's six effects, and Costa Rica's services/call-centre economy measured against the region.

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales; Bolivia Grade 12 Texto de Aprendizaje Ciencias Sociales Secundaria 6): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation. the final year runs the whole arc in student form — 1952's nationalisations and reform, the inflation they fed, the stabilisation plans imposed from outside, hyperinflation, the neoliberal turn and its reversal in 2006 — and closes with a financial-education unit on money, the boliviano and the Central Bank.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 Social Studies Class 5 SNC): The Class 5 chapter deepens the Class 4 set with Pakistan's own institutions as the teaching examples — PIA, Pakistan Railways, Pakistan Television, Pakistan Post and WAPDA for public services, the State Bank of Pakistan (established 1948), and Dr Akhtar Hameed Khan's microfinance work for social entrepreneurship.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 12 Economics Class 12): the Class 12 book runs the standard macro course (national income, money, banks, public finance, trade) and then turns the same tools onto Pakistan itself for six chapters — Pakistan's sectors, national income, planning, transport and manpower, banking system, government finances and foreign trade — closing with the Islamic economic system (Zakat, Ushr, interest-free banking) taught as a full alternative system, not a footnote.


Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 9 Social Science student): the new-format Grade 9 book gives Economics a full theme, "State and Economy": systems first (with Smith, Marx and Keynes as the thinkers), then the market and where it fails, then Armenia's own financial system and the state's economic and social policies, closing with Lorenz/Gini inequality graphs, World Bank income groups and the SDGs.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). Bahamas' own way: the Central Bank of The Bahamas regulates all banks and issues Bahamian notes, and the Bahamian dollar exchanges one-to-one with the US dollar.

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 9 Social Studies 9): the labour market is taught with real Mongolian wage schedules in tögrög, a full payslip worked line by line, and national statistics pupils must compute rates from.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 10 Ekonomi Tingkatan 4): a full KSSM economics course in Malay with bilingual BM–BI glossaries, ringgit-denominated tables throughout, and Malaysian institutions (Bank Negara, KWSP/EPF, PERKESO) as the living examples.
