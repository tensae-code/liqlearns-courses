# The National Bank and commercial banks

## Do it first

Three jobs: printing birr notes, lending to a coffee exporter, and supervising a dam-building loan for five years. Which bank should do each? Assign them before the lesson confirms your sorting.

## Sub lesson 1 — The National Bank — referee and issuer

The National Bank of Ethiopia's role: issuing currency, bonds and treasury bills; keeping prices stable; authorising microfinance institutions; holding government deposits; lending to banks; and regulating money supply, interest, credit, reserves and foreign exchange. It referees the game the other banks play.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Commercial Bank — the public's bank

The Commercial Bank of Ethiopia does the everyday work: taking deposits, granting loans, safe custody of valuables, foreign-exchange services, letters of credit and traveller's cheques. Where the NBE faces the banks and the government, the CBE faces households and businesses across its branch network.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Development Bank — projects

The Development Bank of Ethiopia took its present name in the 1994 reform. It gives project-based development loans to agriculture and industry, and supervises the projects it finances — lending tied to a plan it then watches, unlike the CBE's general lending.

## Going further — Older evidence — two banks, two jobs (2011 guide)

An older guide's key carries the fullest institutional list. The National Bank of Ethiopia: exclusive right to mint coins and issue notes; issues government debt instruments; promotes balanced, accelerated growth; works to reduce unemployment and keep inflation low; supervises microfinance and commercial banks; acts as banker to the government; is lender of last resort; regulates money supply, interest rates and credit creation; manages the international reserves; regulates foreign exchange and organises the FX auction market. The Commercial Bank of Ethiopia: mobilises household saving; accepts savings, time and demand deposits; advances loans, credit and overdrafts with follow-up; keeps safe-deposit vaults; buys and sells foreign exchange; issues letters of credit and travellers' cheques; deals in bonds and treasury bills; transfers money; reports to the NBE; and accelerates the economy's monetisation. The same guide frames monetary policy's principal objective as price and exchange-rate stability in support of sustainable growth.
