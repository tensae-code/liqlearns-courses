# Insurance — a contract against risk

## Do it first

Think of the worst money accident that could hit one family this year — an illness, a fire, a crash. Before you read on, say how one family alone could ever pay for it — and what changes if a thousand families share the cost.

## Sub lesson 1 — What insurance is — a contract, a premium, a promised compensation

Iran's Grade 7 Social Studies book, in Lesson 6 "Insurance and coping with accidents", defines **insurance (بیمه)**: a **contract** in which the **insurer**, in return for a **premium (حق بیمه, defined in the book)**, **undertakes to compensate specified losses**. Three parts make the thing: the contract that binds, the premium that is paid in, and the compensation promised if the named loss happens.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why it exists — losses too big for one person to cover

The book gives the reason before the machinery. **Illness, disability, unemployment, retirement, accidents and fire** can each bring a **loss too big for one person to cover**. Insurance exists because those losses fall on one household at a time, but their cost can be spread across everyone who pays the premium — the many carry what would crush the one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Insurance in Iran — student, workers' accident and third-party vehicle cover

The book names the forms a pupil meets in Iran: **student insurance**, **workers' accident insurance**, and the **compulsory annual third-party vehicle insurance** — every vehicle must carry it, every year, so that the harm a driver does to others is covered whether or not the driver could pay. Compulsion here is part of the protection: the victim's compensation does not wait on the driver's purse.
