# Earthquakes — the slipping Earth

## Do it first

Bend a flexible ruler slowly until it slips out of your fingers. The bend was strain building; the snap was release. Write one sentence linking that to an earthquake before you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Strain and sudden slip

An earthquake — a temblor — is the sudden shaking when strained rock masses slip along a fault. Strain builds slowly as plates press and drag; the rock holds, bends, and then lets go all at once, releasing its stored strain energy as seismic waves.

Most damage-doing quakes come from crustal ruptures on faults. Seismographs detect even the near-continuous small ones that nobody feels — the Earth is slipping somewhere almost all the time.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring — the Richter scale

The Richter scale reads from 0, no movement, to 9, extremely severe. It is logarithmic: each whole step up multiplies the effect, so a step is never 'just one more'.

That is why the numbers must be read with care — the difference between a 6 and a 7 is not small change but a multiplication of the shaking's power.
