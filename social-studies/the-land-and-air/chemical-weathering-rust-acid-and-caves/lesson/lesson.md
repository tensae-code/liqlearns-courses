# Chemical weathering — rust, acid and caves

## Do it first

Think of an old iron tool left out in the rain. What has the rain done to it? Write it down; that change is the first process of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rust, acid and clay

Chemical weathering changes the rock's internal structure — the pieces are not the same material as the rock they came from. Rainwater carrying dissolved oxygen rusts iron minerals weak, the way iron tools rust.

Rainwater with dissolved carbon dioxide becomes carbonic acid, and it dissolves carbonate rocks — solution, or carbonation. Hydrolysis breaks other rocks down to clay and soluble salts, and plants and bacteria add organic acids and draw minerals out of the rock.

## Sub lesson 2 — Limestone country — caves and stone icicles

In limestone, solution carves whole cave systems. Ethiopia's named caves are the Weyb River caves and Sof Omar, hollowed out of limestone by acid water.

Inside, the dissolved rock is laid down again drip by drip: stalactites grow down from the roof, stalagmites grow up from the floor, and where the two meet they join into a pillar. Dissolving and building happen in the same cave, one drip apart.
