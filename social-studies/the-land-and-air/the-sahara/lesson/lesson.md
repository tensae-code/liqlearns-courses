# The Sahara

## Do it first

Picture a desert. Is it all sand dunes? Guess what else its surface could be — stone? gravel? bare rock? And where would people and plants gather? Keep your picture; this lesson corrects and completes it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The world's great hot desert on the Massif

The **Sahara** stretches across North Africa on the ancient **African Massif** — the greatest hot desert on Earth. Its character is **hyper-arid**: rain is not merely scarce, it is rare and unreliable, evaporation outruns it, and life concentrates only where water reaches the surface.

Its size is part of the lesson: this one desert covers a belt of the continent so wide that whole European countries would fit inside it many times over. On Africa's map it is the pale, empty quarter of the north — the Massif's dry crown.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dunes, hamada and oases — the desert's surfaces

The Sahara is not one sea of sand. Rolling **dune fields** cover parts of it, but wide areas are **hamada** — bare stony and rocky surfaces swept clean of soil — and gravel plains lie between. The traveller's picture of endless dunes is only one of the desert's faces.

Where groundwater reaches the surface, an **oasis** appears: palms, gardens and settlement in a green island. Oases are the desert's living points — proof that in the Sahara, water, not land, is what is scarce.

## Say it to your companion

Describe the Sahara to your companion: where it is, how dry, and its three kinds of surface. Finish with the oasis — your companion asks why people settle exactly there.
