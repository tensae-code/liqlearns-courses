# Lesson plan — The Sahara

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Land and Air · **Lesson:** The Sahara
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Landforms of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Sahara, on the ancient African Massif, is the world's great hot desert — a hyper-arid land of dune fields, stony hamada surfaces and rare oases, stretching across North Africa.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the African Massif — the Sahara is the massif's northern desert in detail.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The world's great hot desert on the Massif** — Extent and hyper-arid character.
2. **Dunes, hamada and oases — the desert's surfaces** — Not all sand.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Africa's four landform regions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Place the Sahara on the African Massif in North Africa.
2. Explain hyper-arid in plain words.
3. Name the desert's surfaces — dunes, hamada, gravel plains — and explain an oasis.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else, beyond what hands-on.md names for its activities.

## If the learner is stuck
If the Sahara becomes 'all sand' in your mind, list its faces on three fingers: dunes, hamada stone, gravel — then add the green exception: the oasis, where water reaches the surface.
