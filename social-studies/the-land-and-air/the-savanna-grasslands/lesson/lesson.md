# The Savanna grasslands

## Do it first

Picture grassland as far as you can see, with single flat-topped trees standing in it. In the wet months it is green; in the dry months, brown. What do the trees do to survive the dry months? Guess — the answer is the heart of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The grassland belt and its place

The **savanna** is the **tropical grassland belt** that lies between the equatorial rainforest and the Sahel. Grass is its true cover — wide grass seas in both hemispheres — with **scattered trees and shrubs** standing in it, never closing into forest. Heat lasts **all year**; what changes with the seasons is rain, not temperature.

Place it on the belt map: rainforest at the equator, then savanna, then Sahel, then desert — and the same order mirrored south of the equator. The savanna is the wide middle belt of the continent.

## Sub lesson 2 — How savanna trees survive the dry season

Savanna trees are **drought-resistant**: built for months without rain. Their clearest trick is to **shed their leaves in the dry season**. A tree loses most of its water through its leaves, so dropping them **cuts water loss** to almost nothing while the roots wait in the dry soil — then new leaves open when the rains return.

Read the savanna by its seasons: green and growing in the wet season; brown grass and bare-branched trees in the dry. The trees are not dead in the dry picture — they are waiting, with their water-saving done.
