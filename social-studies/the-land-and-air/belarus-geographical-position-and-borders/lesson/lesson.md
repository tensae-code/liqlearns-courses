# Belarus — Geographical Position and Borders

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Belarus's geographical position and borders: the size of its territory and its borders, the assessment of its geographical position, the formation of its territory, and its administrative-territorial division, as Belarus's Grade 10 book teaches them. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The size of Belarus's territory, its borders and the assessment of its geographical position

Belarus's Grade 10 book opens the study of its own country with the size of the territory and its borders, and an assessment of the country's geographical position (Theme I §1). Position comes first: before anything is said about nature or people, the learner fixes where the country is and how big it is.

## Sub lesson 2 — The formation of Belarus's territory and its administrative-territorial division

Theme I §2 follows with how the territory was formed and how it is divided today — the country's administrative-territorial division. The division into regions is the frame the book uses again at the end, when each region is studied in turn.

Belarus's own way: the whole course is one country studied whole — position and the history of its own geographical research first (including Belarusian place-names), then nature component by component, then its five natural provinces, population, the economy sector by sector, Belarus in the world, and finally each of its six regions in turn; every paragraph opens with a "recall" rubric and closes with questions and tasks.
