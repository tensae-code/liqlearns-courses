## Sub lesson 1 — Pangaea — the one land

In the Carboniferous, about 350 million years ago, all the land stood joined as one supercontinent: Pangaea, centred near the South Pole. There was one land and, around it, one great ocean.

In the Triassic, Pangaea split into two great lands: a northern land, Laurasia, and a southern land, Gondwanaland. Those two lands later broke further into the continents we know today.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wegener and the drift idea

In 1912 Alfred Wegener proposed the continental drift theory: the continents themselves move. Land is not fixed in place for ever — it travels, slowly, across the face of the Earth.

That one idea changed geography. If continents move, then coastlines, mountains and oceans all have a history of movement — and the rest of this unit follows where that history leads.
