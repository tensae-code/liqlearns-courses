# Lesson plan — Africa's climate types

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Land and Air · **Lesson:** Africa's climate types
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Climate of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's climates are equatorial, tropical wet-and-dry, tropical monsoon, semi-arid, desert and subtropical highland — temperate climates are rare, rainfall varies more than temperature, and the subtropical ridge's subsiding air makes the deserts the sunniest, driest places on Earth.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the world climate regions the unit opens with, narrowed to the continent's own list.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Africa's six climate types.
2. Explain why temperate climates are rare in Africa.
3. Explain 'rainfall varies more than temperature' and the subtropical ridge's desert-making role.

## If the learner is stuck
Anchor the list to the belt map: equatorial (rainforest), wet-and-dry (savanna), monsoon, semi-arid (Sahel), desert (Sahara/Kalahari), subtropical highland (high ground). Place first, name second — the names follow the belts.
