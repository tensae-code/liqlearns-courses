# Latitude — why the equator is hot and the poles are not

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: why does the same Sun heat one place fiercely and another weakly? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The angle of the Sun

Insolation — incoming solar radiation — is strongest where the Sun stands at 90 degrees, overhead at the equator. Toward the poles the angle falls toward 0 degrees at the horizon, and the same beam spreads over more ground, so each part gets less heat.

## Sub lesson 2 — Day length and seasons

The equator keeps 12-hour days and small seasons. The poles swing extremely: long summer days and long winter nights, as revolution, rotation and the Earth's axial tilt march the light between the hemispheres.

## Sub lesson 3 — The master control

Because it sets both the Sun's angle and the day's length, latitude is the first control every other climate control must work within. The next lessons add water, winds, currents and height — all standing on latitude's floor.
