**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Groundwater Wells Springs And Aquifers — Part Three, Chapter 1: groundwater is of three kinds by source — fresh, from rain on the land, melted snow and water seeping from river channels; mineral, the hot water that comes with volcanic eruptions; and salty, seeped in from neighbouring seas and oceans; rain is the greatest source of all: part of it sinks, and the greater part of what sinks fills the gaps and pores of sedimentary rocks and the cracks of igneous rocks, gathering in reservoirs inside the water-bearing layers called aquifers; Part Three, Chapter 1: people reach groundwater at wells and springs — ordinary wells are dug down to the reservoir, and artesian wells once threw their water up unaided, though after over-use thousands of them now need machines to lift it; a spring is groundwater issuing by itself at the surface, steadily or in spells, cold or hot, fresh or salty, and springs appear where a river has cut its valley below the water level, where streams lost in limestone country come back to the surface, and where water sunk in the gravels of desert wadis is dammed by a barrier into a natural store (any close wording).**
