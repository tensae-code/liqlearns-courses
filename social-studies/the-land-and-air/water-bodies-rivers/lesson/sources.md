**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Ethiopia, Iceland, Belarus, Guatemala, Eritrea, Slovenia, Uganda, Venezuela, Mauritania, Afghanistan, Libya, Moldova, Mozambique, Jordan, Laos, Iraq). Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Iceland's own way: a one-page map-labelling work card (vinnukort): pupils work directly on the outline map of their own country, its main rivers and lakes already drawn in for identification, with a 0–50 km scale bar.

Pairing warning: the Grade 8 teacher guide is an Addis Ababa bureau book and the student book is an Amhara bureau book; they are NOT edition-matched, and the guide in hand is truncated (printed pp. 1–47 only, Units 1–3). Pupil content in this lesson comes from the Amhara student book; anything from the guide is labelled (teacher guide — evidence) and never presented as pupil-taught.

Deliberate blanks kept as tasks: Table 5's rows 3–5 are blank in the book on purpose (its library completion-and-report task). This lesson keeps them as a task and never invents the missing data.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Eritrea's own way (ER G8 Social Studies): Grade 8 is the Eritrea-physical-geography year — relief, hydrology and water shortage, people-environment adaptation and Eritrea's strategic Red Sea location are taught as four full Geography units before the History units begin.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 3 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Social Science is organised in five strands (Community and Organization; Trade; Culture and Society; Environment and Resources; History and Origins) taught through guided lessons that start outdoors in the school grounds — children observe, draw and label the features, resources and maps of their own local community first. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 3 G3 CS Viva Venezuela): the second half is a state-by-state tour of Venezuela (territorial organisation, Nueva Esparta, Sucre, the Capital, Vargas, Zulia, the Andean states, the Llanos, Guayana) taught through local stories and children of each region — regional geography as a journey around the child's own country.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 4 geo-4af-m): A one-topic-per-spread observation book that moves from the child's own wilaya outward: the wilaya map first, then the landforms of home (watercourses, mountains, plains, plateaus, sand dunes), the weather elements and the two measuring instruments (thermometer, rain gauge), then the lived place — public places, archaeological sites, street names, my village, the capital of my wilaya — closing with integration exercises on the relief and climate of the wilaya capital.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 7 Geography (Dari)): the Dari MoE pattern — the world topic is taught first in the abstract (sphere, equator, latitude), then re-taught as Afghanistan's own geography (its position, relief, six climate regions, river basins, lakes, minerals, fuels), then widened to the continent (Asia). Every lesson ends with in-class group activity, questions and an out-of-class task naming the pupil's own locality.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 8 Geography (Dari)): the continent-by-continent march continues — after Asia in Grade 7, Grade 8 does Europe then the Americas, each continent in the same fixed order: position, mountains, rivers, lakes, seas, gulfs, climate, political geography, human geography (race, language, religion), economic geography, mines/industries, transport.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 9 Geography (Dari)): the continent march finishes with Africa, then Australia/Oceania and Antarctica — same fixed order per continent as Grade 8, with a data table of the independent countries of Africa appended to the Africa chapter.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 4 Social Studies (Dari)): primary social studies starts from the child's own world — family, village, city — and only then opens the map: directions, Afghanistan's boundaries and mountains and rivers, the continents and oceans, and finally the Earth's shape, equator, latitude/longitude and movements.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 6 LY G6 Social Studies): The geography half is "Principles of Physical Geography" in four units — surface shaping, climate, water, pollution — with Libyan examples inside world content (the Haruj volcanic mountains, Libyan springs such as Ain al-Sharshara). Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, History of Libya — the Ottoman State and Libya as an Ottoman province (printed pp. 12–18), Libya in the Ottoman era 1551–1912 including the Karamanli period (pp. 19–35), modern European colonialism and the Italian occupation of 1911 (pp. 36–43), Libyan resistance to the Italian occupation (pp. 44–54), Libya after the Italian era — liberation, the British/French military administrations, independence and the Libyan Kingdom (pp. 55–64).

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 5 G5 Geography): first Geography year built as a single Earth-systems arc — planet, envelopes (lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere/biosphere), human society, then a closing "Protecting the Earth" chapter; every paragraph opens with "you will be able to" outcomes and chapters close with evaluation tests, plus two practical applications (map work; a week-long weather calendar).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G9 ES T1): Earth Science at Grade 9 is minerals and water, anchored in Jordan — the enrichment case is potash drilling in the Dead Sea.

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 6 laos-g06-geography-teacher): a world-foundations year taught zone by zone — after Earth, maps, movements, atmosphere and waters, the last five lessons walk through how people live in each climate zone (hot, desert, savanna, temperate, cold); closes with a teaching-techniques section (printed p. 66) and a Q&A additional-knowledge appendix (printed p. 75), not placed separately.

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 6 laos-g06-geography-student): the student twin of Book 2 — same 9 parts / 16 lessons with full illustrated text, a reading passage (printed p. 90) and a glossary of terms (printed p. 95), not placed separately.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 5 Social Studies): the whole first unit is Iraq's own geography — location first (world, region, locality, then the astronomical location), then the land itself region by region off the relief map, then people and economic activity; history and national education follow as separate units.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 6 Social Studies): a whole unit walks Iraq governorate by governorate — all eighteen in one fixed pattern (geographical location, relief, climate, water resources, population with 2017 estimates, economic activity, then a short historical note) — so the child learns the country as its provinces, each with its own map; a second unit on Iraqi society (Civics) follows.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 8 Social Studies): the intermediate sequence widens ring by ring — after Iraq itself (G5–G6) and the ancient world (G7), Grade 8 gives the whole Arab world as one geographical unit (its position between two continents, relief, climates, waters, people and economy), worked constantly off outline maps of the Arab world; the history of the Arab world follows as Part 2.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies): Grade 9 returns to Iraq for the fullest treatment yet — the same national geography as Grade 5 but measured: the plain is 24% and about 600 km long, the desert plateau about 55%, the desert plant belt about 70%, two-thirds of the people live on the central and southern plain; every topic is pinned to numbered maps of Iraq.
