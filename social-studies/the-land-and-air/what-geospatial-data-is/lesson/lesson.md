# What geospatial data is

## Do it first

Before you read, describe your school so exactly that a stranger could map it: where it is, what it is like, and when you saw it. You have just made three kinds of information — the lesson names them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Data and information

Data are the raw records; information is what they tell you when organised. Geospatial work starts by keeping the two apart — a rain gauge's numbers are data until they become a rainfall map someone can decide from.

## Sub lesson 2 — Location, attribute and time

Every geographic record has three parts. Location information says where it is. Attribute information says what it is like. Temporal information says when it was true. The lesson's example is one mapped school carrying all three: a pin, a description, and a date stamp.

## Sub lesson 3 — Kinds of variables, kinds of world

Variables are qualitative — kinds, in words — or quantitative — amounts, in numbers. The world itself is modelled two ways: discrete objects with edges, like a school, and continuous data that exists everywhere, like elevation measured from sea level. Observations are what we collect; visualisation is how we show them so they can be read.
