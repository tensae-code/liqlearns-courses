# Ethiopia’s three great relief regions

## Do it first

Picture Ethiopia from the side, like a loaf cut open: high land, a deep valley, high land again. That is the whole lesson in one picture. Now read the names.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three regions

From its geological history, Ethiopia’s relief divides into three great regions. West of the Rift stand the Western Highlands, dropping to the western lowlands. East of the Rift stand the Southeastern Highlands, dropping to the southeastern lowlands. Between them runs the Rift Valley itself — the great split in the land. Highlands, Rift, highlands: that is the skeleton of the country’s relief.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the division matters

Every later relief lesson hangs on this frame. Each highland block, each lowland and each Rift section you will meet belongs to one of the three regions. When you can place a place — west, Rift, or southeast — you already know its height, its climate promise and the way its rivers must run.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three regions from west to east, and use the frame to place two places you both know.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the three-region cross-section from memory and label it.
2. Name the region each of these belongs to: the Simien Mountains, Lake Ziway, the Bale Mountains.
3. Say in one sentence why the Rift sits between the two highland regions.
