# Major soil types of Ethiopia

## Do it first

One book names six soils; the teacher’s book names ten. Both are about the same ground. Read how the two lists fit together.

## Sub lesson 1 — The student book’s types

The pupil text names the major types — including Nitosols, Vertisols, Leptosols, Luvisols and Cambisols, with the arid lowland soils — and describes each by colour, fertility, workability and distribution. Vertisols, the black clays, are praised in the guide’s keys as excellent black basaltic soils.

## Sub lesson 2 — The guide’s longer list (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide’s answer keys name ten soil types: fluvisols, luvisols, nithosols, vertisols, acrisols, cambisols, lithosols, regosols, xerosols and yermisols. The longer list uses older names alongside the student book’s — the naming difference is real, and the build shows it honestly rather than merging the lists silently.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion both soil lists, and why the guide’s list is longer and older-named.

## Do It Yourself

1. List the guide’s ten names from memory (as many as you can).
2. Name the soil praised as an excellent black basaltic soil.
3. Say why the two lists are not merged silently.
