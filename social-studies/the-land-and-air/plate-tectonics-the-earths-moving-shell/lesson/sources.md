**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, Micronesia, Barbados, Guatemala, Costa Rica, Indonesia, Dominican Republic, Samoa, Malaysia, United Kingdom, Cambodia, Fiji, Slovenia).**

Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Barbados's own way: this is the shared Caribbean CSEC examination textbook used in Barbados — every world process is re-taught through Caribbean case studies (the Caribbean plate, Montserrat's volcano, Jamaica's coasts and Kingston's urban problems, St Lucia's reefs, Bahamas/Jamaica/Puerto Rico karst, Guyana's rivers), map and field skills are folded into the chapters, and the course ends in a School-Based Assessment fieldwork project. That way is stocked here as the way Barbados teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error in the placements for this lesson is taught here. Figures are taught exactly as the placements report them, as the book's own record.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Costa Rica's own way: an almost purely geographical anthology — earth dynamics, water and climate change — built around risk: every world topic lands on Costa Rican vulnerability, risk management and territorial planning by sector (biodiversity, health, infrastructure, fisheries, energy, agriculture, water). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Searchable tags: plate tectonics, lithosphere, asthenosphere, plates, social studies, Guatemala, Costa Rica, CONED Antología, Estudios Sociales

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 10): This is the physical-geography year: the universe and Earth's layers, continental drift with volcanoes and earthquakes — including earthquake-prevention measures as a group task — water and winds across the continents, and a closing unit on political and economic geography that treats borders, geopolitical order and international climate agreements as geography, with hurricane-season citizen responsibility as the water unit's group activity.

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Geography): A full senior Geography course built on the Pacific itself — high and low island formation, island climates and ecosystems, then population of New Zealand, Asia and Samoa, and Samoan farming (bananas) beside Australian uranium mining.

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

United Kingdom's own way: A single exam-board (AQA) GCSE textbook organised as thematic units with numbered two-page spreads, every big topic anchored to a named case study (Typhoon Haiyan, Somerset Levels, Rio de Janeiro, Bristol, Nigeria, the Indus Basin, Lesotho Highlands) and a final unit on issue evaluation, fieldwork enquiries and cartographic/graphical/statistical skills.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 is the foundation for senior Geography and History — Fiji's own physical geography (tectonics, relief, rivers, climate, soils) taught in full, then its industries, population and urbanisation, all with Fiji data.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Years 11–12 Physical Geography): The physical volume is comparative — every theme (climate, relief, soil, vegetation, hazards) is taught twice, New Zealand first, then Fiji (Monsoon Asia for hazards), so Fiji's land is always read against a temperate and a monsoon case.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 9 Geografija 9): Grade 9 turns home — the whole book is Slovenia itself: first the country as a system (position and borders, relief and rocks, climate/vegetation/waters, settlement, population, settlements, economy, transport, environment), then its five natural regions one by one (Alpine, Pre-Alpine with the Ljubljana basin, Dinaric-Karst, Mediterranean, Pannonian). Named, not placed: the EU unit's history of the European Union and Slovenia-in-EU pros/cons (printed p. 7, Civics/History) and the state symbols inside the opening unit (p. 1, Civics).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.
