# Plate tectonics — the Earth's moving shell

## Do it first

Crack the shell of a boiled egg gently and look at the pieces. Each piece is whole, but the pieces can shift against each other. Hold that picture in mind as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lithosphere on asthenosphere

From the late 1960s, one theory has been accepted: plate tectonics. The lithosphere — the crust plus the upper mantle — slides as rigid plates over the weaker layer beneath it, the asthenosphere.

There are about a dozen large plates, plus smaller ones, and they move slowly: a few centimetres a year. Slow as that sounds, over millions of years a few centimetres a year moves continents across the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — What a plate can carry

A plate may carry continent, ocean floor, or both. Plate edges do not coincide with continent edges: the North American Plate, for example, includes half the North Atlantic floor as well as the continent.

The theory reaches beyond landforms. Plate processes influence the composition of the atmosphere and the oceans, the long-term climate, and the environment in which life evolves — the moving shell shapes far more than mountains.
