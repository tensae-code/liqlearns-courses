## Sub lesson 1 — When the crust breaks and shifts

Faulting happens when tectonic pressure pushes rock masses against, over or under each other, and the crust cracks. Along the crack — the fault — the blocks move against each other.

[GIF 11460 — a block sinking between two faults to form a valley]

## Sub lesson 2 — Block mountain, rift valley, horst

The block caught between two parallel faults and forced up is a block mountain. The block that sinks between faults is a rift valley. The unsunken ground on either side of the valley is a horst.

## Sub lesson 3 — One movement, three landforms

So a single faulting movement draws three landforms at once: horst up on one side, sunken rift valley in the middle, horst up on the other side. Ethiopia's own great valley, in the next lesson, was made exactly this way.
