**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Nauru, Tuvalu, Guatemala, Angola, Dominican Republic, Bahamas, Eritrea).**

Nauru's own way: Nauru uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisors Fay Itaia & Sharon Kam for Nauru) — Geography is taught as ocean stewardship: the Pacific as a connected "sea of islands", its animals, and the traditional fishing knowledge of Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue, ending in a plastic-waste action plan. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: Tuvalu uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisor Valisi Tovia for Tuvalu) — and Tuvalu itself is a case study inside it: Tuvalu's own traditional fishing and bird-catching (printed p. 14) is one of the three country fishing studies. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

Pairing warning: the Grade 8 teacher guide is an Addis Ababa bureau book and the student book is an Amhara bureau book; they are NOT edition-matched, and the guide in hand is truncated (printed pp. 1–47 only, Units 1–3). Pupil content in this lesson comes from the Amhara student book; anything from the guide is labelled (teacher guide — evidence) and never presented as pupil-taught.

Grade 11 depth honesty: the Grade 11 student book's ocean portraits (printed pp. 18–20) are taught as printed; no error is flagged for these pages in the placements.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 3): For third graders geography is the home island first: the planet and its components, then the Antilles and the relief of their own country, then countryside versus city, and finally where their own families came from — migration and a family census the children run themselves.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): The Grade 6 book is civics-led (government, elections, courts, constitution, finance ministry) but opens the primary course's widest geography: the world's continents and oceans, political and physical maps, global warming and natural disasters, human resources and the job market, worldwide transport and communication through Nassau's ports of entry, and a closing cultural comparison of The Bahamas with Africa, Australia, Brazil and Japan.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.
