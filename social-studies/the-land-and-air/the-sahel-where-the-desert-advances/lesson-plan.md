# Lesson plan — The Sahel — where the desert advances

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Land and Air · **Lesson:** The Sahel — where the desert advances
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Landforms of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Sahel is the semi-arid belt south of the Sahara — a fragile transition land where the desert advances through desertification, and protecting its grazing and farm land is the belt's central problem.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Sahara — the Sahel is the desert's southern edge, taught as its pair.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **A semi-arid transition belt** — South of the Sahara, between desert and savanna.
2. **Desertification — the desert advances** — The belt's central problem.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Sahara. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Place the Sahel south of the Sahara and describe it as semi-arid transition land.
2. Define desertification.
3. Explain why a thin-covered transition belt is fragile.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else, beyond what hands-on.md names for its activities.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep the pair straight: Sahara = desert by climate (fixed); Sahel = the edge that can tip — desertification is the tipping. If you can say which of the two can change, you have the lesson.
