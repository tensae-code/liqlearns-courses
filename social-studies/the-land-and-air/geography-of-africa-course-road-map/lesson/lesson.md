# Geography of Africa — course road map

## Do it first

Before you read on, say out loud everything you already know about Africa's land: its biggest desert, its longest river, its highest ground. Then guess: if you had to learn the whole continent in eight steps, which step would you put first? Keep your guess — this lesson shows the eight steps the course actually takes.

## Sub lesson 1 — The eight steps in order

The course walks the continent in eight steps. Step 1 is the **landforms of Africa** — the shape of the land itself. Step 2 is **Africa's climate** — the heat, rain and winds over that land. Step 3 is the **natural resource base** — rivers, soils, minerals, plants and animals. Step 4 is **population** — where Africans live and how their numbers change.

Step 5 is **major economic and cultural activities** — work, wealth, languages and faiths. Step 6 is **human–natural environment interactions** — how people and the land change each other. Step 7 is **geographic issues and public concerns** — cities, migration and polluted coasts. Step 8 is **geospatial information and data processing** — maps, data, graphs and GIS, the tools that tie all the steps together.

## Sub lesson 2 — World first, then Africa — and Ethiopia at home

Every step opens wide before it narrows. A step begins with the **world** — world landforms, world climates, world resources, world population — and only then comes to **Africa**, so the continent is always measured against the whole Earth, never studied in a box by itself.

And inside the African treatment, **Ethiopia is the home anchor**. The course keeps returning to Ethiopian ground: the Abbay Gorge, the Ethiopian Highlands' cool climate, Gore Town's rainfall, Addis Ababa's settlements, Ethiopian map data. You learn the continent outward from the ground you stand on.

## Sub lesson 3 — How each step is built

Every step is built the same way, so you always know where you are. It opens by recalling what came before, then gives **unit outcomes** — what you will be able to do — and a box of **key terms**. Numbered sections follow, each starting with 'At the end of this section you will be able to…' and filled with activities, maps and tables of African data.

Each step closes with a **unit summary** that gathers the whole step into one page, and **review questions** that test it. Learn the shape once, and every step after feels familiar: the ground changes, the machine does not.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the eight steps in order, without looking. Then explain why each step opens with the world before Africa, and name the Ethiopian place you would use as your own home anchor. Your companion names one step and asks: what does it study, and which Ethiopian example belongs to it?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight steps as a numbered road on one page, with one Ethiopian anchor beside each step.
2. Pick the step you know least about and write two questions you want it to answer.
3. Draw a simple outline of Africa and mark where you would start the journey and where you would end it.
