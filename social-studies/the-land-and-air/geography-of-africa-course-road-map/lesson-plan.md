# Lesson plan — Geography of Africa — course road map

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Land and Air · **Lesson:** Geography of Africa — course road map
**Place in the curriculum:** Course opening — Social Studies, The Land and Air (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's geography is learned in eight steps — landforms, climate, resources, population, economic and cultural life, people and environment together, today's issues, and geospatial data — always opening with the world, then Africa, with Ethiopia as the home anchor.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands at the door of the Grade 10 Africa course; it needs only the learner's Grade 9 study of Ethiopia's own land.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The eight steps in order** — The unit order of the whole course.
2. **World first, then Africa — and Ethiopia at home** — The pattern inside every step.
3. **How each step is built** — Outcomes, key terms, activities, summary, review.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: your Grade 9 study of Ethiopia's land and people. Recommended, never forced.

3. Explain the world-first, then-Africa pattern and Ethiopia's place as home anchor.
4. Describe how every step is built: outcomes, key terms, sections, summary, review.

## If the learner is stuck
If the eight steps blur together, walk them as a journey over one piece of ground: first its shape (landforms), then its sky (climate), then its riches (resources), then its people (population), then their work and cultures, then people and land together, then today's problems, then the maps and data that measure it all.
