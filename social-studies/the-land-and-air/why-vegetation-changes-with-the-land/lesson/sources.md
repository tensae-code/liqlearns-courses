Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas.

Malaysia's own way (MY T3 Geografi Tingkatan 3): Form 3 turns from skills (tables, graphs, pie charts) to living Malaysia — its natural vegetation and wildlife and the human pressure on both — then outward to the world's biomes, resources and economic cooperation, closing with forest management and recycling.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Years 11–12 Physical Geography): The physical volume is comparative — every theme (climate, relief, soil, vegetation, hazards) is taught twice, New Zealand first, then Fiji (Monsoon Asia for hazards), so Fiji's land is always read against a temperate and a monsoon case.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 8 Geografija 8): Grade 8 is pure world regional geography, continent by continent (Africa, Australia/Oceania, the Americas and polar regions), each region worked through the same grid — position/relief/waters, climate/vegetation, population, economy — and the book closes with two world-problems units (rainforest loss, desert spread, water conflicts, the Aral Sea).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 4 UG P4 SST Home-Study): Social Studies organised entirely around "our district": the child's own district's physical features, vegetation, settlement and work are the geography content. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Term Two social activities and ceremonies in the district (printed pp. 17–19); Term Three — Our Leaders in the District, Children's Rights, Responsibilities of People in Our District, Social Services in Our District and caring for social service centres (Civics, printed pp. 20–35).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG P5 Merged Home-Study SST): The whole SST course is Uganda itself: locating Uganda and its districts/neighbours, Uganda's physical features, climate and vegetation, then Uganda's population. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Mathematics section (PDF pp. 9–220); English section (PDF pp. 221–274); SST Term Two — The People of the Pre-Colonial Period, ethnic groups, explorers, colonial rule, Legislative Council and the independence struggle (History, SST printed pp. 36–54); SST Term Three — national symbols, democracy, The Government of Uganda, duties of citizens (Civics, SST printed pp. 56–70).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 9 SD Geography 9): A single-country book: the whole of Sudan taught in three units — its physical geography, its human geography, then its environmental problems — with map-work activities on Sudan's own maps (states, roads, the Nile) and an infographic "resources of Sudan" spread; the preface warns its statistics change and are only indicators.
