# Why vegetation changes with the land

## Do it first

From a plane window, Ethiopia’s plants change like bands on a blanket: bare tops, forest, thorn scrub, desert. Three controls make the bands. Read them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three controls

Natural vegetation is not scattered by chance. It varies with altitude (temperature falls as you climb), with rainfall (amount and season), and with soils. Ethiopia’s vegetation is therefore ordered in belts: read the land’s height, rain and soil, and you can predict its plants.

[Picture 21031 — a mountain transect with vegetation belts stacked by altitude]

## Sub lesson 2 — The order of the belts

The unit orders Ethiopia’s vegetation from the afro-alpine tops — cold, open, giant-lobelia country — down through forests and woodlands to the desert of the lowest, driest lands. Each belt is the answer its altitude, rain and soil give.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three controls, and walk them down a mountain from afro-alpine top to desert.

2. Say which belt sits at the very top.
3. Say what you read to predict a place’s plants.
