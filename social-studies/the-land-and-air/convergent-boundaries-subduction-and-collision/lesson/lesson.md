# Convergent boundaries — subduction and collision

## Do it first

Push two thick books slowly toward each other on a table until one slides under the other. Watch which one goes down, and what happens on top. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Subduction — one plate dives

Plates are built of two kinds of crust: lighter granitic continental crust, or denser basaltic oceanic crust. Where plates converge — move toward each other — one subducts: it dives beneath the other.

Where the Nazca Plate dives under South America, the land above crumples and melts feed a chain of volcanoes: the Andes. Where ocean meets ocean, the diving plate builds an arc of volcanic islands, like the Marianas.

## Sub lesson 2 — Collision — continent against continent

When both plates carry continents, neither can easily dive. Instead the land between them crumples upward, fold on fold, into great mountains.

That is how the Himalayas rose, where the Indo-Australian Plate met Eurasia. Convergent boundaries are destructive boundaries: crust is consumed in subduction zones even as mountains and volcanoes are built above them.
