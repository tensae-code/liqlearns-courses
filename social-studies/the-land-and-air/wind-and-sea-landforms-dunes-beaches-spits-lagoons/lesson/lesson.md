# Wind and sea landforms — dunes, beaches, spits, lagoons

## Do it first

Think of a beach you know or have seen pictured. List what the sea has piled up there and what it has taken away. The sea does both — keep both lists.

## Sub lesson 1 — Wind at work

In deserts and semi-deserts, wind is the great mover. It piles sand into dunes around obstacles, and shapes the crescent dune — the barchan — with its horns pointing downwind.

Finer material travels further. Wind-borne dust settles as loess, a fertile deposit — North China holds large loess deposits, farmland made by the wind.

## Sub lesson 2 — The sea at work — eroding and depositing

The sea both erodes and deposits. Its deposit along the coast is the beach, a band of mixed sediments. Where sediment is carried along the shore and out into the water, it builds a spit — a narrow sand and silt ridge attached to the land at one end and reaching into the sea.

Behind such banks, salt water can be cut off from the open sea: that sheltered, separated water is a lagoon. (In the book's figures, the dune panel is Figure 1.31 and the sea panel Figure 1.32.)
