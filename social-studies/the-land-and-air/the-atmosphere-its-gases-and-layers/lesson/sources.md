**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Angola, Dominican Republic, Samoa, Colombia, India, Cambodia, Fiji, Sri Lanka, Mauritania, Yemen). Myanmar is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Myanmar's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the year is split into five parallel strands revisited week by week — physical geography, social geography, environmental geography, neighbouring countries and cartography — each lesson plan built student-centred (Bloom steps, key questions, classroom assessment) around Myanmar and its five neighbours. That evidence is stocked here as the way Myanmar teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Pairing warning: the Grade 8 teacher guide is an Addis Ababa bureau book and the student book is an Amhara bureau book; they are NOT edition-matched, and the guide in hand is truncated (printed pp. 1–47 only, Units 1–3). Pupil content in this lesson comes from the Amhara student book; anything from the guide is labelled (teacher guide — evidence) and never presented as pupil-taught.

Guide figures differ: the teacher guide gives argon 0.93% and carbon dioxide 0.039% (guide printed p. 6) against the student book's 0.9% and 0.03% (student printed p. 22). This lesson teaches the student book's set, as the pupil text, and flags the guide's set in the lesson as teacher evidence.

Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 3): For third graders geography is the home island first: the planet and its components, then the Antilles and the relief of their own country, then countryside versus city, and finally where their own families came from — migration and a family census the children run themselves.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Geography): The final-year course is hazard- and water-centred for an island nation — volcanic eruptions and tropical cyclones (including Cyclone Heta on Niue), the hydrological cycle and water management, then atmosphere and climate change with Pacific consequences.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado06 Secundaria Activa): Grade 6 Secundaria Activa starts from the universe and the Earth as a "living machine" (rocks and wrinkles, water, air), then follows humans evolving, migrating from Africa to America and adapting to each continent's environment — geography as the stage for all the history that follows.

India's own way (Grade 6 The Earth Our Habitat): The first Geography year goes planet → globe → motions → maps → Earth's domains → landforms, and only then turns home: the last two chapters are India itself (location, neighbours, physical divisions, monsoon climate, vegetation and wildlife), so world basics are immediately applied to the home country.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 13 Geography): The final year integrates everything at advanced level (plate tectonics to urban models, largely from world texts such as Waugh) and then lands it in Chapter 8, South Pacific Islands Geography — Oceania's geology, island types, development prospects and regionalism.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 8 G8 English): a four-unit arc — Solar System, Earth's uniqueness, then regional geography taught as six South Asian "landscapes" (mountain, valley, coastal, rural, industrial-urban, desert), closing with Sri Lanka's own 1:50,000 topographic-sheet system (92 sheets) that Grade 9 continues.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 7 guide-geo-1as) (teacher guide — evidence): A competency-based guide (IPN 2025 reform) that fixes the whole Grade 7 course in three units — Earth and its main relief, Climate, Population and urbanisation — each unit ending in a class project, and every general topic anchored to Mauritania itself: the country's astronomical location, its relief map and Atlantic coast, rainfall differences between its wilayas, its age pyramid, and the growth of Nouakchott. Sample lesson plans (fiches) are given for one lesson per unit, plus model term exams with answers. That evidence is stocked here as the way Mauritania teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Mauritania is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 7 geo-1as-m): The student book of the course specified in Book 1 (the teacher guide): the same three units and 13 lessons, each lesson opening with expected outcomes and key concepts and closing with a concepts list per unit. General topics are worked with Mauritanian examples throughout, as the guide prescribes.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 11 geo-5as-lm): The senior Letters cycle re-runs physical geography at full depth in four units — Planet Earth, Climate, the Effects of Climate, the Earth (structure, erosion, coasts) — with a class project closing Units 1 and 4.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 10 G10 Geography of Man and the Environment): secondary geography is thematic, not regional — man and space, man representing his geographic field (remote sensing, projections, maps), man organising it (rural/urban), climate and water, the dynamic Earth, and the great environmental systems.
