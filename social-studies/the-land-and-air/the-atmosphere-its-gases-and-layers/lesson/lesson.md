# The Atmosphere — Its Gases and Layers

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try the opening task: the atmosphere is the blanket of air around the earth — say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The air blanket's recipe

The atmosphere is the blanket of air around the Earth. The student book gives its recipe: 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.9% argon, 0.03% carbon dioxide, and other gases. Nitrogen is nearly four-fifths of every breath you take.

[Picture 11472 — the air's recipe as a 100-part bar — 78, 21, and the small gases]

## Sub lesson 2 — More than gases (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide widens the recipe: the atmosphere is a mixture of gases, floating or suspended solids, and liquids — including a variable amount of water vapour averaging around 1%, plus suspended dust particles and condensed moisture droplets, called aerosols (teacher guide — evidence). Its dry-air figures differ slightly from the student book's — argon 0.93% and carbon dioxide 0.039% — and this lesson teaches the student book's set, flagged here.

## Sub lesson 3 — Two ways of slicing it

Judged by how temperature changes with height, the atmosphere divides into four layers — the next lesson's work. The teacher guide notes a second slicing: by composition as well as by temperature (teacher guide — evidence). One blanket, two ways of cutting it.
