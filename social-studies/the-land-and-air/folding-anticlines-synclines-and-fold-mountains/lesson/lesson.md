# Folding — anticlines, synclines and fold mountains

## Do it first

Lay a stack of cloth or paper flat and push its two ends slowly toward each other. Describe the waves that form: which part arches up, which part dips down?

## Sub lesson 1 — Anticlines and synclines

Sideways compression bends rock layers. An upfold — an arch — is an anticline; a downfold — a trough — is a syncline. The layers bend instead of breaking because the pressure comes slowly and the rock is buried deep.

Keep squeezing and the folds lean and deepen: first asymmetrical folds, tipped to one side; then overfolds; and at the limit, over-thrust folds, where one mass of rock is pushed bodily over another.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fold mountains, young and old

Whole mountain ranges are folds written large. Three mountain-building periods — orogenies — in the last 400 million years sort them by age.

Young fold mountains come from the Alpine orogeny: the Andes, Rockies, Alps, Himalayas, Atlas and Australian Alps — still high and rugged. Old fold mountains, 250–300 million years old, are lower and more weathered: the Scandinavian Caledonides, the Appalachians and the Urals.
