# Remote Sensing Images From Air And Space — short answer

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Remote Sensing Images From Air And Space — Part One, Chapter 2: remote sensing is "seeing what the naked eye cannot see" — a science that wins information and measurements about an area or a natural or human phenomenon by analysing data from a device that never touches the thing studied; satellites, aircraft, cameras and radar record Earth's phenomena by the waves they give off, from ultraviolet to radio waves, and the data are sent to ground stations and processed by computer into maps, images and digital data; Part One, Chapter 2: remote sensing at work — it eases the study of soil types, rock types and river drainage patterns and the search for mineral ores, petroleum and groundwater; it monitors the polluted water factories discharge into rivers, lakes and seas, the gases that harm the ozone layer, and oil-leak areas on coasts and in deep water, mapping the slicks and their movement; and its data are the fastest way to size up damage after hurricanes, floods, oil leaks and volcanoes, to map stricken places no one can reach, and to guide rescue and emergency services (any close wording).**
