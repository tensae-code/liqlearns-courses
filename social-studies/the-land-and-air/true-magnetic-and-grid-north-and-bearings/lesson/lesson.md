# True, magnetic and grid north — and bearings

## Do it first

Ask three norths where north is, and they point — almost — the same way. The small differences matter. Read the three norths and the bearing.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three norths

True or geographic north points to the earth’s geographic pole. Magnetic north is where the compass needle points, pulled by the earth’s magnetic field — it differs from true north by the declination. Grid north runs along the map’s Eastings, the grid’s own up. On a map the three arrows fan slightly apart.

[Picture 21135 — three north arrows fanning from one point, slightly apart]

## Sub lesson 2 — Bearings

A bearing is a direction measured as an angle clockwise from north: east is 090°, south 180°, west 270°. For map work, grid north along the Eastings is the convenient reference — bearings are taken from it with a protractor on the map itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three norths apart, and give the bearings of east, south and west.
