## Sub lesson 1 — Dating with numbers

Absolute age gives a number of years. It works because unstable parent isotopes decay into daughter products at a steady, measurable rate.

## Sub lesson 2 — The half-life rule

The rate is measured in half-lives. After one half-life, half the parent remains; after two half-lives, a quarter remains; after three, an eighth — the parent share halves at every step while the daughter share grows.

## Sub lesson 3 — The working pairs

The book tabulates the pairs scientists use: uranium-238 to lead-206, with a half-life of 4.5 billion years; potassium-40 to argon-40; and carbon-14 to nitrogen-14, with a half-life of 5,730 years — the short clock used for once-living things. Rocks and index fossils are the materials dated.
