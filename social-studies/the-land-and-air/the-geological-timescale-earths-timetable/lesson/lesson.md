# The geological timescale — Earth's timetable

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: how can anyone timetable 4.5 billion years on one chart? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — A timetable for a planet

The geological timescale organises Earth's whole history. Geologists built it by studying rocks and the fossils in them, reading which life lived when.

[Picture 27003 — a long timeline strip divided into eons, eras, periods and epochs, most of it Precambrian]

## Sub lesson 2 — The divisions, big to small

The largest divisions are eons. Eons divide into eras, eras into periods, and periods into epochs — like years dividing into months, weeks and days.

## Sub lesson 3 — The frame for everything else

This timetable frames everything else in the unit: the eras, the supercontinents and the dating methods all take their place inside it. Learn the frame first, and the rest of deep time has somewhere to sit.
