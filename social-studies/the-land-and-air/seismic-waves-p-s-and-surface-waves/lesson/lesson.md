## Sub lesson 1 — Focus, epicentre and body waves

Shock waves spread from the rupture point — the focus of the earthquake. The point directly above it on the surface is the epicentre, the place named in every news report.

Body waves travel through the Earth. P (primary) waves compress and expand the rock along their path, like a squeeze travelling. S (secondary) waves vibrate at right angles to their path, shaking the rock side to side as they go.

## Sub lesson 2 — Surface waves — and finding the quake

Surface waves — Love and Rayleigh waves — follow the body waves. They travel slower, but with larger amplitude, and they do most of the far-field destruction: the rolling and swaying that brings buildings down far from the focus.

Because the wave kinds travel at different speeds, their arrivals separate with distance. Comparing arrivals at recording stations lets scientists locate quakes thousands of miles away — the waves themselves report where they started.
