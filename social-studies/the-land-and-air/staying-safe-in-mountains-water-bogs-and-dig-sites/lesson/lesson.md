# Staying safe in mountains, water, bogs and dig sites

## Do it first

Four terrains, four ways to get hurt — and a rule that prevents each. Read the hazard rules terrain by terrain.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mountains and water (teacher guide — evidence)

In mountainous areas expect steep and loose rock, ice and snow, and fast weather change; carry a safety rucksack with first-aid kit, compass, whistle, torch, survival bags and extra food. In and near water: never drink dubious water, never go barefoot, and never wade deeper than knee-deep.

## Sub lesson 2 — Woods, bogs, digs and living hazards (teacher guide — evidence)

Woods and forests have their own care rules. Bogs and swamps hide water under vegetation rafts and sphagnum or peat carpets — probe ahead with a pole. In excavations, sand sections are far less stable than clay, and rubble is a risk. And everywhere: learn to recognise dangerous or poisonous species before you meet them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the hazard rules of the four terrains, rucksack included.
