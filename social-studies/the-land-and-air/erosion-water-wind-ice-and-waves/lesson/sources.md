**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ethiopia, India, North Macedonia, United Kingdom). Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grade 5 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Grade 5 Social Studies pairs a heritage year (Europeans and Africans, national heroes, Caribbean culture) with a physical-environment year — erosion, locating Jamaica and the Caribbean by compass and latitude/longitude, types of rainfall, the Solar System, plants and water pollution. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Pairing warning: the Grade 8 teacher guide is an Addis Ababa bureau book and the student book is an Amhara bureau book; they are NOT edition-matched, and the guide in hand is truncated (printed pp. 1–47 only, Units 1–3). Pupil content in this lesson comes from the Amhara student book; anything from the guide is labelled (teacher guide — evidence) and never presented as pupil-taught.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 10 mk-g10): the gymnasium course restarts Geography as a systems science — Theme 1 works through the four spheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) lesson by lesson, then natural disasters by sphere; Theme 2 turns to people in geographical space (world urbanisation, energy, farming, industry, transport and trade, tourism, environmental impact); Theme 3 treats the world as one global system (globalisation, transport corridors and sea straits/canals, global networks and navigation systems, international organisations, global challenges and climate-change solutions). 43 numbered lessons.

United Kingdom's own way: A single exam-board (AQA) GCSE textbook organised as thematic units with numbered two-page spreads, every big topic anchored to a named case study (Typhoon Haiyan, Somerset Levels, Rio de Janeiro, Bristol, Nigeria, the Indus Basin, Lesotho Highlands) and a final unit on issue evaluation, fieldwork enquiries and cartographic/graphical/statistical skills.
