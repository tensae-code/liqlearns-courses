## Sub lesson 1 — Worn rock, carried off

Erosion is worn rock being swept or washed from place to place by running water, ice, wind and ocean waves. Weathering breaks; erosion removes. The book shows it in its gully, sheet and wind erosion figures.

[Picture 11469 — four panels — a gully deepening, wind lifting sand, a glacier ploughing, waves cutting a coast]

## Sub lesson 2 — Water and wind at work

Water cuts flood channels that deepen and widen — from small gullies into great rivers. Wind carries sand far and drops it as loess, building new soil where it lands. The teacher guide adds wind's named loads: a sand dune and a crescent-shaped barchan are wind-built forms, and screes are piles of loose rock fragments at a cliff's foot (teacher guide — evidence).

[GIF 11485 — flood water deepening a gully]

## Sub lesson 3 — Ice and waves at work

Ancient glaciers ploughed Canada, western Europe and Russia, leaving thin-soiled land behind them. And sea and tidal waves both build new coastal landforms and change how people live along the shore.
