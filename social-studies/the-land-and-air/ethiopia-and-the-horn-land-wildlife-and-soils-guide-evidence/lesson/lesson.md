# Ethiopia and the Horn — land, wildlife and soils (guide evidence)

## Do it first

Before reading: write what you think makes Ethiopia's wildlife special compared with other countries. Then read what the guide itself states — and note the date of its figures as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Horn and its wildlife (teacher guide — evidence)

This lesson stands on the 2010–11 Grade 12 teacher's guide alone — evidence of what that course taught, era-labelled throughout. The guide states: the Horn of Africa covers about 1.8 million km², inhabited by more than 95 million people, a region of diversified physical and human character.

Ethiopia's wild-animal wealth, as the guide states it: about 103 species of mammals and 832 species of birds, of which 7 mammal and 25 bird species are endemic — found nowhere else. The wealth is grounded in the country's diversified climate, vegetation and relief, and wildlife is valued scientifically and educationally, economically through tourism, and aesthetically.

## Sub lesson 2 — Threats — and the soils (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide names the threats plainly: habitat destruction, illegal hunting, grazing-land expansion, wildfire, and migration to neighbouring countries; vegetation is likewise pressured by settlement expansion into forestland.

Its soils teaching contrasts two basaltic soils: Nitosols, the red basaltic soils, and Vertisols, the black basaltic soils — and prescribes management practices including crop rotation and rotation of grazing lands. Every figure in this lesson is the guide's own, from its own era: learn them as the guide's record, labelled as such.
