**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Malaysia, Fiji, Uganda, Pakistan). Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere. Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grade 5 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Grade 5 Social Studies pairs a heritage year (Europeans and Africans, national heroes, Caribbean culture) with a physical-environment year — erosion, locating Jamaica and the Caribbean by compass and latitude/longitude, types of rainfall, the Solar System, plants and water pollution. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Searchable tags: four-figure grid reference, eastings, northings, along the corridor, social studies, Jamaica, curriculum guide evidence

Malaysia's own way (MY T2 Geografi Tingkatan 2): the year pairs map skills (scale, topographic maps) with weather and climate, then transport and telecommunications as the human-geography core, an Asia regional study, and global warming with green technology as the environmental close.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 9 Social Science): Secondary geography opens with a full map-skills chapter — grid references, contours and cross-sections practised on Fiji maps — before environment use and conservation, including Pacific methods (shifting cultivation, rotational grazing, appropriate fishing methods).

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 6 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): in Senior Primary the strands are re-ordered and Environment and Resources comes first and largest — a full local-environment course (physical/human environment, environmental change, human impacts, sustainability, natural hazards) taught through sample guided lessons with benchmarks, before Civics, Culture, History and Trade. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 SS5): the Class 5 Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 51–85) deepens the Class 4 spiral — same BOLTS, landforms, weather and population topics, but now with scale types, latitude/longitude and time-zone arithmetic, worked population-density calculations, and graph-drawing exercises from real Pakistani city data.
