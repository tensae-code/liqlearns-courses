# Ethiopia's five zones in Köppen letters

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: can Ethiopia's own five zones be written in Köppen's letters? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fourteen categories (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide's answer key states that, under the modified Köppen scheme, Ethiopia's climate falls into fourteen categories. It names eight of them: BWh arid desert hot, BSh hot semi-arid, BSk cold semi-arid, Csa, Csb, Cwa, Cwb and Cwc — Cwc glossed as cold subtropical highlands (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — The guide's Köppen map of Ethiopia (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide supports the count with its own Köppen map of Ethiopia (its Figure 2.1, drawn from Beck et al.'s Köppen–Geiger maps). The student book teaches Köppen's letters and Ethiopia's five zones separately, and never joins them (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 3 — Two systems, one country (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide then bridges the two Ethiopian systems: it sets the Köppen map beside its own map of Ethiopia's agroecological zones on the Global 16-Class system (its Figure 2.2) and directs comparison of the two. The traditional zones answer altitude and crops; the letters answer measured temperature and rain (teacher guide — evidence).
