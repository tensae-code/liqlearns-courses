# Africa's rainforest and the Congo Basin

## Do it first

Imagine a forest where it rains through the whole year and the trees never stand bare. How tall, how dark, how layered would it be? Guess its structure before you read it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The equatorial belt centred on the Congo Basin

Africa's **tropical rainforest** belt sits on the **equator**, centred on the **Congo Basin** — the great low basin of the Congo River in the continent's centre, with forest reaching toward the Guinea coast and the lake highlands. Here rain and heat come together all year, and the forest answers with the densest growth on the continent.

Centre is the word to keep: other belts — savanna, Sahel, desert — arrange themselves north and south of this green equatorial core, mirrored about the equator.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dense and evergreen — the forest's structure

Rainforest trees are **evergreen**: with water in every month, they never need to shed all their leaves at once, so the forest stands green the whole year. Its growth is **dense** and layered — tall crowns catching the light above, smaller trees and shade plants below, the floor dim under the closed canopy.

Set it against its neighbours: the savanna tree drops its leaves and waits; the rainforest tree, never short of water, keeps them. One belt's structure is written by seasonal drought; the other's, by rain that never stops.

## Say it to your companion

Describe the rainforest to your companion: where it is, why it never stands bare, and its layers. Your companion asks: how is it different from the savanna beside it, and why?
