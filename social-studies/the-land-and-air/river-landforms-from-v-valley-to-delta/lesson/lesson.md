# River landforms — from V-valley to delta

## Do it first

Draw a river from mountain to sea as one wavy line. Mark where you think it cuts deepest and where it drops its load. Check your marks against the three courses.

## Sub lesson 1 — Upper and middle courses

In the upper course the river cuts a V-shaped valley, and where the bed steepens, a near-vertical gorge. Waterfalls form where hard rock resists while soft rock below is cut away; rapids race over steep bed stretches; and interlocking spurs force the young stream to wind around them.

In the middle course the signature landform is the meander — a zigzag bend thrown against alternating hard and soft rock. When a bend is cut off and abandoned, its crescent of water remains as an ox-bow lake.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lower course — plain and delta

The lower course lays down what the upper course tore away. Along its banks the river spreads a flood plain of deposited sediment, renewed whenever it floods.

At its mouth, where the river meets still water and drops the last of its load, the deposits build a triangular delta — named for the Greek letter delta (Δ), whose shape it copies. (Course order matters: meanders and ox-bow lakes belong to the middle course; flood plain and delta to the lower.)
