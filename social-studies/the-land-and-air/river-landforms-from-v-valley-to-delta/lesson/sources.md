**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Barbados, Malaysia, India, United Kingdom, Fiji, Slovenia, Sudan, Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Libya, Mozambique, Iraq, Bhutan).**

Barbados's own way: this is the shared Caribbean CSEC examination textbook used in Barbados — every world process is re-taught through Caribbean case studies (the Caribbean plate, Montserrat's volcano, Jamaica's coasts and Kingston's urban problems, St Lucia's reefs, Bahamas/Jamaica/Puerto Rico karst, Guyana's rivers), map and field skills are folded into the chapters, and the course ends in a School-Based Assessment fieldwork project. That way is stocked here as the way Barbados teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Course-order correction carried from the placements: the book's own summary lists flood plain and delta under 'lower course' while printing them against the middle-course figure; this lesson keeps the placements' corrected course order — meanders/ox-bow middle, flood plain/delta lower — and names the slip here only.

Searchable tags: river landforms, meander, ox-bow lake, delta, flood plain, waterfall, social studies

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

United Kingdom's own way: A single exam-board (AQA) GCSE textbook organised as thematic units with numbered two-page spreads, every big topic anchored to a named case study (Typhoon Haiyan, Somerset Levels, Rio de Janeiro, Bristol, Nigeria, the Indus Basin, Lesotho Highlands) and a final unit on issue evaluation, fieldwork enquiries and cartographic/graphical/statistical skills.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 13 Geography): The final year integrates everything at advanced level (plate tectonics to urban models, largely from world texts such as Waugh) and then lands it in Chapter 8, South Pacific Islands Geography — Oceania's geology, island types, development prospects and regionalism.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka G10 Tamil SB): the O/L year pairs every world topic with its Sri Lankan twin — world agriculture then Sri Lanka's land use, world industries then Sri Lanka's industries — and closes with map skills and an outline-map chapter; the syllabus (units 10.1–10.13) is printed at the back.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 7 Geography 7): a dedicated English-medium Geography book built entirely on Pakistan's own physical and economic geography — the Indus plain and delta, the canal system, Rabi/Kharif crops, Sui gas, Thar coal, Khewra salt and Tarbela power — with world concepts (river courses, settlement hierarchy, greenhouse effect, mining types) taught through Pakistani examples first.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 6 Geography 6): world physical Geography taught through Pakistan's own land — its fault lines and 1935/2005 earthquakes, Khewra salt and Thar coal among the rocks, K2 and the Karakoram among the mountains, Pakistan's four climatic regions, its six forest types from mangrove to alpine, and the Indus flood as the country's defining disaster.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 10 LY G10 Principles): Secondary geography opens as a two-part systematic course — physical geography first (astronomy, surface-shaping factors, climate, vegetation and wild animals), then human geography (population and settlement, economic resources) — and closes with a whole book on spatial-data technology: remote sensing, GPS and GIS, then the history of maps from Babylon and Ibn Hawqal.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 3): a systematic geomorphology course — rocks and Earth's interior first, then isostasy, continental drift and plate tectonics, then internal agents (folds, faults, earthquakes, volcanoes in four lessons), then external agents and the modelled landscapes of hot, temperate, cold and coastal environments, closing with the geological cycle.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Physical Geography): a pure physical-geography course in six chapters — landforms, weather and climate, water, the biosphere, soil, and the environment — with Iraqi anchors kept throughout (flood visits to Iraq's rivers, the hills of northern Iraq, groundwater and the desert caravans, soil salinity in the alluvial plain).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 5 Social Studies): Grade 5 spirals from the Earth and lat/long straight into Bhutan itself — its location, three physical zones, neighbours, major rivers and protected areas — so world geography is anchored in the home country's map first.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 9 Geography): The Intermediate level (IX–X) treats each world topic at full textbook depth and keeps the Bhutan case inside it — Soils of Bhutan, agro-ecological zones and farming practices of Bhutan, and common disasters in Bhutan (including GLOF) alongside the world disaster survey.
