Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

India's own way (Grade 8 Resources and Development): Grade 8 is a resources year, world-first but India-anchored throughout: resources and their conservation, then land/soil/water/vegetation, minerals and power, agriculture, industries and human resources — every world pattern is paired with its Indian distribution map and an Indian case study (Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad textiles, Bangalore IT, a farm in India set against a farm in the USA).

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 is the foundation for senior Geography and History — Fiji's own physical geography (tectonics, relief, rivers, climate, soils) taught in full, then its industries, population and urbanisation, all with Fiji data.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Years 11–12 Physical Geography): The physical volume is comparative — every theme (climate, relief, soil, vegetation, hazards) is taught twice, New Zealand first, then Fiji (Monsoon Asia for hazards), so Fiji's land is always read against a temperate and a monsoon case.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 9 Geography G9): every unit is taught twice at two scales — the Arab homeland first, then Syria — with parallel lesson pairs (relief, climate, water, soil, agriculture, industry, transport, tourism); Unit 3's framing is pointed: "huge human resources… and weak investment", and the subsoil lessons are titled "hidden treasures".
