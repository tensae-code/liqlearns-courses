Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 10 Geography): the literary track opens geography as a discipline in its own right — its thought from the Sumerians to GIS, how a geographer researches, where geographical data come from and how they are drawn, and only then the fields of the science; Iraq supplies the worked examples throughout (Baghdad research problem, Iraqi topographic maps, Iraq's gas time series, the Baghdad–Basra distance exercise).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Physical Geography): a pure physical-geography course in six chapters — landforms, weather and climate, water, the biosphere, soil, and the environment — with Iraqi anchors kept throughout (flood visits to Iraq's rivers, the hills of northern Iraq, groundwater and the desert caravans, soil salinity in the alluvial plain).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Earth Science): the scientific track's earth-science course — atmosphere, earthquakes and volcanoes, weathering and soil, environment and pollution, and fossils and geological time — bilingual with English technical terms throughout, and Iraqi touches kept (the air masses over Iraq, the yellow dust storms, Iraq's Natural History Museum).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 CCSS Módulo Autoformativo G7): The distance-learning parallel of the Grade 7 full-year book (Book 7): the same five Geography units rewritten for rural self-study, so every topic is anchored in the learner's own comunidad — its soils, its production lands, its road links and its risk zones. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I government institutions (printed p. 23, Civics); Unit VI first settlers (printed pp. 113–128, History, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans (printed pp. 131–141, History); Unit VIII independence process (printed pp. 145–154, History); Unit IX evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 157–168, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty (printed pp. 173–202, History); Unit XI triumph of the Revolution to the present (printed pp. 205–231, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 Estudios Sociales G8 full-year): Grade 8 widens the lens to the whole American continent: five Geography units on America's territorial patrimony, physical landscape, ethnic-cultural diversity, space-and-economy and environmental protection (closing with Nicaragua's own SINAPRED disaster system), then five History units. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI American origins (printed pp. 125–138, History); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans and colonial domination (printed pp. 145–165, History); Unit VIII emancipation processes (printed pp. 167–184, History); Unit IX changes in Latin American society (printed pp. 185–194, History); Unit X America in the 20th century (printed pp. 197–222, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 9 Geography): The Intermediate level (IX–X) treats each world topic at full textbook depth and keeps the Bhutan case inside it — Soils of Bhutan, agro-ecological zones and farming practices of Bhutan, and common disasters in Bhutan (including GLOF) alongside the world disaster survey.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 12 Geography): Class XII closes the Advanced course with specialist topics (surveying, geospatial technologies, glaciers and the "Third Pole", Köppen in full, fluvial processes, Davis's cycle of erosion) and Bhutan data chapters — fertility and mortality trends in Bhutan, migration trends, hydropower in Bhutan, and creative industries including Bhutanese film.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 OM Geografía G5) (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher guide teaches the same four units as the programme/student book but adds Cuba-specific complementary materials no other country set carries: the Tarea Vida state climate-change plan as classroom content, project-based-learning orientation, and a long section on mastering the changing world political map through country cases (Congo, Belarus, the Yugoslav successor states). That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Cuba is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 Geografía G5): The first Geography book a Cuban child meets opens with the history of geography itself (cuneiform maps, Hecataeus), builds all map skills before any content, and ends the planet tour with world population, the world political map and world heritage — with Cuba's own places (Llanura del Cauto, Trinidad) woven in as examples throughout.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 8 OM Geografía Económica y Social G8) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide's distinctive Cuban layer is territorial identity — learners characterise their own municipality's resources and the environmental problems of its economic sectors, and the world material (political map, population, resources, production) is always closed by comparison with Cuba. That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Cuba is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 9 Geografía de Cuba G9 (2012)): The 2012 edition follows the G9 programme chapter for chapter (archipiélago → naturaleza → panorama económico → desarrollo social → situación medioambiental → regiones) and is explicit about its own dating: data before 31 Dec 2010 use the 1975 provincial division, while Chapter 6 teaches the new 16-province organisation of 1 Jan 2011. NOTE — this is NOT the same book as the Biblioteca copy filed next (Book 16): that copy is the wholly rewritten 2025 Tercer Perfeccionamiento edition, so the two are placed separately.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 9 Geografía de Cuba G9 (2025)): The 2025 rewrite keeps the home-country arc but rebuilds it for the Tercer Perfeccionamiento: Chapter 2 now profiles every province inside the political-administrative division itself, the climate chapter adds the Tarea Vida state plan by name, the economy chapter is organised by primary/secondary/tertiary sectors with self-employment (trabajo por cuenta propia) and the Mariel development zone, and the social chapter faces an ageing, shrinking population (life expectancy 73.7 years, a "Vivir 120" programme) and closes with Cuba's Human Development Index. It is a different book from the 2012 edition (Book 15), not a parallel copy, and is placed separately.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Amangalieva NIS): the NIS (Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools) inquiry textbook: every lesson opens with a «Бұл сабақта» (In this lesson) goal box and closes with a «Сенің кезегің!» (Your turn!) task block; each section ends in a «Қорытынды» summary built round a concept map; the book closes with a trilingual Kazakh–Russian–English glossary. Country-studies section works through how a state improves its geographic position, with the UAE and Singapore as cases and Kazakhstan as the home example.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Egorina): the 2025 Atamura course is organised by numbered paragraphs (§1–§68) with coded learning goals (e.g. 7.3.2.4) and a «Негізгі ұғымдар» key-concepts box per paragraph; it gives Kazakhstani science its own paragraph (§3, research in the war and post-war years), a whole practical strand on building geographic databases (§§10, 18, 49, 54–55), and a country-studies block that classifies states by position — continental, archipelago, island and peninsular — with Kazakhstan's own economic-geographic position as the worked example.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Egorina 2017): the 2017 first edition of the Atamura course (the 2025 Book 2 is its revision): same six-section architecture but twelve numbered practical works, four of them database-building exercises on Kazakhstan itself — a geographic database for Kazakhstan's regions (§51) and for Kazakhstan's transport system (§52) — plus a practical on the natural complex of the pupil's own locality (§37).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Karatabanov): every paragraph is titled as a question («Как устроена Земля внутри?», «Все ли одинаково обеспечены водой?») and every section closes with a «Мои географические достижения» (My geographic achievements) self-check page; Part 2 asks what is known about "our country's soils" (§§37–38) and how to develop natural resources "without harm to nature and people" (§§55–57).
