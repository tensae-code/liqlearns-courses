**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (South Sudan).**

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 2 P2 Social Studies SB): "The Land of South Sudan" is taught through riddles and a word-search — Mount Kinyeti, the River Nile, the White Nile, the Nile Valley and the Sudd swamps — and location is taught by a song about compass directions.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 2 P2 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Features are located through the child's own payam and boma first, and the guide's answers stress what features give — hills, mountains and valleys make the land beautiful; a river or lake provides water. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 5 P5 Social Studies SB): Landforms are made, not just named — learners push a sheet of paper from both ends to make a fold mountain, and the home examples are the Ironstone Plateau and a farming landscape being changed by the farming itself.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 5 P5 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide insists on both sides of every feature — positive and negative effects of physical features on human activities — and on human agency: dumping material in a valley can cause flooding, and climate dictates which farming system a region of Africa can practise. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 6 P6 Social Studies SB): Tourism is taught as national homework — map the parks, weigh the landlocked disadvantage against South Africa, set up the anti-poaching unit — and the fishing unit ties grounds back to Unit 2's physical features and forward to roads that must be improved so fish can reach market.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 6 P6 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): An assessment-led guide: the checks that matter are map checks — can the pupil draw the map and locate the features, the parks and the fishing grounds on it? That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.
