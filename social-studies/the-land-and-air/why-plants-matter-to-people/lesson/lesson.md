## Sub lesson 1 — Why plants are important to humans and the environment

Two of Jamaica's Exploratory Core guides teach this thing. The Grade 4 guide (Social Studies Term 3 Unit 3, printed p. 217) teaches why plants are important to humans. The Grade 5 guide (Social Studies Term 3 Unit 3, printed p. 354) teaches the importance of plants to humans and the environment — the same thing, one year on, widened from humans to humans and the environment together. Both are curriculum guides, written for the teacher, not handed to the pupil: they are cited here as curriculum-guide evidence.

## Sub lesson 2 — Jamaica's way — the book's own teaching way

Jamaica's own way (Grade 4 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Social Studies is one of four subjects fused into a single Exploratory Core guide, organised by four attainment themes; Geography enters through Jamaica itself — Taino settlement maps, cardinal points, Jamaica's physical features, parishes, weather and the Earth in the Solar System — always with hands-on map and model work. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Jamaica's own way (Grade 5 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Grade 5 Social Studies pairs a heritage year (Europeans and Africans, national heroes, Caribbean culture) with a physical-environment year — erosion, locating Jamaica and the Caribbean by compass and latitude/longitude, types of rainfall, the Solar System, plants and water pollution. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
