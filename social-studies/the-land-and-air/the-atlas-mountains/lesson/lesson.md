# The Atlas Mountains

## Do it first

Think of the top edge of Africa, along the Mediterranean Sea. Is the land there flat like a plain, or crumpled like a pushed cloth? Guess which, and guess why. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Young fold mountains on the Mediterranean margin

The **Atlas Mountains** are the mountain system of north-west Africa, running along the **Mediterranean margin** through Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia. They are **young fold mountains**: rock layers squeezed from the sides until they bent and rose — the same folding force that raises ranges like the Alps, which is why this region is called Africa's **Alpine System**.

Young matters here. Against the ancient, worn African Massif to their south, the Atlas ranges stand high and sharp — a folded rim between the Mediterranean Sea and the Sahara.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four ranges — High, Middle, Rif and Tell

The system is not one wall but a family of ranges. In Morocco stand the **High Atlas** — the highest part of the system — and the **Middle Atlas** beside it. Along the northern coast runs the **Rif**, and eastward through Algeria toward Tunisia runs the **Tell Atlas**, the range nearest the Mediterranean shore.

Learn them as two pairs: High and Middle in the Moroccan west; Rif and Tell along the coast to the east. Together they make one system — the folded edge of Africa facing Europe across the sea.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what the Atlas is, why its mountains are called young, and the four range names in west-to-east order. Your companion asks: which pair stands in Morocco?
