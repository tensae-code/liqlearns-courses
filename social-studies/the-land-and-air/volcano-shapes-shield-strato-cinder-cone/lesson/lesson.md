# Volcano shapes — shield, strato, cinder cone

## Do it first

Sketch three hills: one very wide and gentle, one tall and steep, one small heap. Guess which is which volcano type as you read, then check.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shield and strato

Volcano form follows the lava and the eruption style. Shield volcanoes are broad and low — their sides slope at under 7° — because their fluid basalt lava runs far before it hardens. The Hawaiian Islands rose 5 km from the sea floor this way, flow on flow.

Strato volcanoes, the commonest type, are steep cones built of alternating layers: lava, then pyroclastic material, then lava again. Their viscous lava does not run far, and their eruptions are periodically explosive.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cinder cones

Cinder cones are the smallest of the three: hills of loose, cinder-sized fragments thrown out and piled around the vent.

The pile holds at its angle of repose — the steepest angle loose material can rest at. A cinder cone's shape is simply that angle, drawn by gravity on a heap of fragments.
