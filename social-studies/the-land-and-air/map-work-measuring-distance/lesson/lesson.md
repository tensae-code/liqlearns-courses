# Map Work — Measuring Distance

## Sub lesson 1 — What is really wanted: ground distance

Nobody walks the map — ground distance is what is really wanted. Every map measurement is two steps: measure on the map, then convert through the scale.

[Picture 11479 — a ruler on a straight road and a thread laid along a winding river]

## Sub lesson 2 — Straight lines: ruler, then convert

On a straight line, measure with a ruler, then convert through the scale. If the map says 5 cm and the scale is 1 cm to 2 km, the ground distance is 10 km.

[GIF 11486 — a thread being laid along a curve and then straightened against a ruler]

## Sub lesson 3 — Curved lines: the thread trick

On a curved line — a winding river or road — a ruler cannot follow the bends. Lay a thread along the curve, mark its end, straighten the thread, measure it with a ruler, then convert the same way. Your task: measure two point-pairs on the book's map, state your steps, and say how the two distances differ.
