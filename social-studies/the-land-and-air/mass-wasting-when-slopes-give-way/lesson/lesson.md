# Mass wasting — when slopes give way

## Do it first

Build a small heap of dry soil, then of wet soil. Which heap can stand steeper? Write the result down — it is the heart of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What holds a slope up

Mass wasting is the downslope movement of rock, regolith and soil under gravity. Material on a slope holds firm only between about 25° and 40° — steeper for larger, angular particles that lock together, gentler for fine, smooth ones.

The first governing factor is water. Rain soaking into the pores of slope material reduces its cohesion — the grains stop gripping each other — until the mass moves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Angle and human activity

The second factor is the angle of slope itself: beyond its holding angle, no material is safe, however dry.

The third factor is human activity. Farming and overgrazing steep, unstable slopes removes the cover and roots that bind them. The book gives Ethiopia as the home example, with repeated destructive mass-wasting events on farmed and grazed slopes.
