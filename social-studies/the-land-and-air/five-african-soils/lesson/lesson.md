# Five African soils

## Do it first

Before you read, think of two soils you know: a sandy one and a deep red one. They behave differently in rain and under a plough. Guess why geographers give soils names at all — then read the five names Africa's book teaches.

## Sub lesson 1 — Arenosols and Leptosols — sandy and shallow

Arenosols are sandy soils, easily eroded because their grains hold together weakly and hold little water. Leptosols are shallow soils lying over hard rock or gravel — there is simply not much soil there to farm or to hold roots.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cambisols and Ferralsols — developing and deeply weathered

Cambisols are moderately developed soils — young, but with a real profile forming. Ferralsols are strongly weathered soils, old and deeply worked by rain and heat; they are widespread across Central, East and Southern Africa.

## Sub lesson 3 — Calcisols — the lime soils

Calcisols are marked by accumulation of calcium carbonate — lime building up in the profile, the signature of dry country where evaporation pulls minerals upward. Read the five together and the pattern is plain: Africa's soil map is its climate and rock map, worked over long time.
