# What earthquakes do — shaking, fire and tsunami

## Do it first

List three ways an earthquake could harm a town besides buildings shaking. Then read the lesson and count how many of yours the book names.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ground, works and fire

Beyond the shaking itself, earthquakes shift groundwater, trigger landslides and mudflows, and wreck the works people depend on — buildings, bridges, pipelines, railways and dams.

Fire can out-damage the shaking itself. In San Francisco in 1906, fire burned 521 blocks for three days after the quake; Tokyo in 1923 burned the same way. Broken pipes and blocked streets leave a shaken city unable to fight the fires the quake starts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tsunami — the sea carries it

An underwater quake moves the sea floor, and the sea carries the energy away as a tsunami. In deep water the wave is low and fast — crossing oceans at up to 800 km/h.

As the water shallows near a coast, the wave grows: a tsunami can strike coasts up to 30 metres high. The quake lasts a minute; its wave can cross an entire ocean to do its damage.
