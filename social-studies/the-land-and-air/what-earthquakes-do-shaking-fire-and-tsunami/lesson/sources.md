Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

United Kingdom's own way: A single exam-board (AQA) GCSE textbook organised as thematic units with numbered two-page spreads, every big topic anchored to a named case study (Typhoon Haiyan, Somerset Levels, Rio de Janeiro, Bristol, Nigeria, the Indus Basin, Lesotho Highlands) and a final unit on issue evaluation, fieldwork enquiries and cartographic/graphical/statistical skills.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka G10 Tamil SB): the O/L year pairs every world topic with its Sri Lankan twin — world agriculture then Sri Lanka's land use, world industries then Sri Lanka's industries — and closes with map skills and an outline-map chapter; the syllabus (units 10.1–10.13) is printed at the back.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka G10 Tamil TG) (teacher guide — evidence): a 90-period competency syllabus (units 10.1–10.13, printed in full) with an expanded-content section and a timed activity set (செயற்பாடு) for every unit — quality inputs, process steps and assessment criteria — plus annexed readings (a poem on Earth and life, a 2004-tsunami reading) and term-test blueprints; the non-content parts (teaching-learning methods, pp. 18–23, and assessment, pp. 325–337) are pedagogy, named here but not placed. That evidence is stocked here as the way Sri Lanka teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Sri Lanka is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 6 LY G6 Social Studies): The geography half is "Principles of Physical Geography" in four units — surface shaping, climate, water, pollution — with Libyan examples inside world content (the Haruj volcanic mountains, Libyan springs such as Ain al-Sharshara). Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, History of Libya — the Ottoman State and Libya as an Ottoman province (printed pp. 12–18), Libya in the Ottoman era 1551–1912 including the Karamanli period (pp. 19–35), modern European colonialism and the Italian occupation of 1911 (pp. 36–43), Libyan resistance to the Italian occupation (pp. 44–54), Libya after the Italian era — liberation, the British/French military administrations, independence and the Libyan Kingdom (pp. 55–64).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 3): a systematic geomorphology course — rocks and Earth's interior first, then isostasy, continental drift and plate tectonics, then internal agents (folds, faults, earthquakes, volcanoes in four lessons), then external agents and the modelled landscapes of hot, temperate, cold and coastal environments, closing with the geological cycle.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 7 Geography): From Class VII Geography becomes a standalone subject organised in three strands (Time and Space; Physical Environment; People and Environment), every world topic closed with its Bhutan case — climate of Bhutan, vegetation types of Bhutan, minerals of Bhutan, and Bhutan's Himalayan disaster set (earthquake, flood, windstorm, fire, GLOF).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 11 Geography): The Advanced level (XI–XII) is full academic geography — map projections, remote sensing and GIS, insolation and pressure belts, climate classification — with Bhutan applied chapters on agriculture, manufacturing and tourism, the last taught on Bhutan's own sustainability principle ("high value, low volume" in the Bhutan Tourism Monitor data the book uses).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 9 S1 Geography SB): Geography is built around South Sudan itself — its own physical features, resources, floods/drought experience and the Nile — with paper-folding and paper-tearing practical experiments for folding, faulting and sea-floor spreading, and group discussion of local examples in nearly every topic.
