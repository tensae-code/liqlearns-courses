# Africa's vegetation belts

## Do it first

Lay two maps side by side in your mind: Africa's climates and Africa's plants. Guess how closely they match — not at all, a little, or almost exactly? Your guess is this lesson's main claim. Read on to test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Vegetation follows climate — the master control

**Vegetation** — the flora, the plant cover of a place — is distributed across Africa in belts, and its map **closely matches the climate map**. That match is the lesson: where climate gives rain all year, rainforest stands; where it gives seasons, savanna; where it gives almost nothing, desert plants. **Climate is the master control** of vegetation.

Beneath the master control, three more factors shape the details: **landform** — high ground carries its own mountain plants; **soils** — thin or poor soils thin the cover; and **biotic factors** — living things themselves, grazing, burning and human use, which can open, thin or change the plant cover of a place.

## Sub lesson 2 — The belts in order

Walk the belts as the course has built them. At the equator, **rainforest** — dense and evergreen. Around it, the **savanna** grasslands with scattered trees. Then the dry belts: thorn scrub and semi-desert plants of the **Sahel** kind, and at the dry extremes the sparse **desert** vegetation — plants built to wait, to store water, or to live fast between rare rains. On the high mountains, **mountain vegetation** changes with altitude, belt above belt up a single slope.

The order is the climate order — because the belts are the climate map, grown. Learn one map and you hold the other: name a place's climate and you can predict its plants; see its plants and you can read its climate.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why the two maps match, name the three detail factors, and walk the belts in order. Your companion names a climate; you answer with its vegetation — then swap: they name plants, you read the climate.
