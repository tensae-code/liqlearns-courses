# Measuring straight distances on a map

## Do it first

Ruler out. Two towns. One multiplication. Read the straight-distance method — and do it as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Measure

Lay the ruler between the two places on the map and read the map distance in centimetres, exactly, point to point. This is air distance — the straight line, as the crow flies.

[Picture 21124 — a ruler laid between two towns on a map]

## Sub lesson 2 — Convert

Convert with the scale: multiply the map distance by the scale’s ground value (at 1:50,000, each centimetre is 0.5 km). The answer is the ground or field distance — the real separation of the two places.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two steps — measure, convert — and work one aloud.

## Do It Yourself

1. Give the two steps in order.
2. At 1:50,000, what is 6 cm on the map on the ground?
3. Say what air distance means.
