# The Ethiopian and Eritrean Highlands

## Do it first

Look at the highland shading on a map of Ethiopia and Eritrea together. The dark high ground crosses the border without pausing. Read why.

## Sub lesson 1 — One highland system

The plateau country of northern Ethiopia continues north into Eritrea: the same uplifted dome and the same flood-basalt lavas built both. Relief does not read political borders — the Eritrean highlands are the northern continuation of the Ethiopian highland system, cut by the same rivers and escarpments.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading relief across borders

Reading the two together explains the north as one piece: the Tekeze system drains across the region, the eastern escarpment falls toward the Afar and Red Sea side, and the highland climates grade into the lowlands on every side. The three-region frame of Ethiopia is the south part of this bigger highland picture.

## The continental view — one of Africa's great highland masses (Grade 10 depth)

The Grade 10 book places the same highlands on the map of the whole continent: the high plateau block at the Rift's head — Ethiopia's and Eritrea's highlands together — stands as **one of Africa's great highland masses**. Read locally, it is home ground; read continentally, it is one of the largest high blocks in Africa, and the Rift begins at its edge.
