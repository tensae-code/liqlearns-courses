## Sub lesson 1 — Rodinia — the first gathering

The first solid rocks, formed between 4600 and 3900 million years ago, gathered into an early supercontinent called Rodinia. The student book's own figure places Rodinia at about 750 million years ago.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gondwana in the south

Later, Gondwana formed around the South Pole. Other great landmasses — Baltica, Siberia and Laurasia — stood as separate blocks while seas between them slowly closed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Pangaea — all land in one

Collisions closed the seas and built a pre-Pangaea. By about 255 to 210 million years ago, all the land stood joined as one supercontinent, Pangaea, which began breaking apart in the Triassic.
