Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography opens with representing the Earth itself — globe-to-flat projection problems, orientation practised on the ground with compass/GPS/Google Maps, and time-zone puzzles worked on Haiti's own links (midday shared with Québec, 18h in Paris, Port-au-Prince–Paris flights); every séance ends in a croquis, a glossary and field observation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

India's own way (Grade 6 The Earth Our Habitat): The first Geography year goes planet → globe → motions → maps → Earth's domains → landforms, and only then turns home: the last two chapters are India itself (location, neighbours, physical divisions, monsoon climate, vegetation and wildlife), so world basics are immediately applied to the home country.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 3 Amasomo Mbonezamubano SB): P3 moves the home ground up one rung of Rwanda's administrative ladder, from the cell to the SECTOR (Umurenge) — and it is here, at age 8–9, that formal map work begins: the sector map of Gikomero, the district map of Gasabo, symbols, scale, and the sectors that share a border with ours.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 6 Social Science): Year 6 turns outward to the whole Fiji map — map conventions, island groups, towns, rivers and roads — and to Fiji's climate and sea resources, with conservation taught through traditional taboos and harvest seasons.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 3 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Social Science is organised in five strands (Community and Organization; Trade; Culture and Society; Environment and Resources; History and Origins) taught through guided lessons that start outdoors in the school grounds — children observe, draw and label the features, resources and maps of their own local community first. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 4 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the same five-strand structure as Grade 3, widening the child's world from the local area to the district — district features, renewable versus non-renewable resources, and map scale. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 8 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the local-to-global ladder completes — after province (G5), nation (G7), Grade 8's Environment and Resources strand is global: global climate, global settlement and population, global farming, urbanization and logging impacts, global hazards and disaster resilience. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 5 SD Geography 5): The first-ever Geography book in the post-2019 curriculum starts from the child's own classroom and school: directions are learned by standing facing the sunrise, map elements through a pupil's-house map, and only then does the book step out to Sudan, Africa and the world — a local-to-global ladder.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 4 G4 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): geography is the first block ("Conóceme, soy Venezuela") and is built as widening circles — the child's house drawn to a stated scale (1 cm on the plan = 1 m), then Tomás's town, then Venezuela's territory and borders, then Latin America and the unequal world.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 9 G9 TG English 2018) (teacher guide — evidence): competency-based guide — every unit pairs a syllabus table (competency level, content, learning outcomes, periods) with lesson-planning notes and a specimen group activity; geography is taught region-first (Asia, then Sri Lanka itself in depth), closing with hands-on 1:50,000 topographic-sheet work (Matara sheet No. 91). That evidence is stocked here as the way Sri Lanka teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Sri Lanka is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 8 G8 English): a four-unit arc — Solar System, Earth's uniqueness, then regional geography taught as six South Asian "landscapes" (mountain, valley, coastal, rural, industrial-urban, desert), closing with Sri Lanka's own 1:50,000 topographic-sheet system (92 sheets) that Grade 9 continues.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 3 geo-3af-m): The youngest geography book in the set is built on five axes worked through observation pictures (I observe / I discuss / I conclude): the map key, position, location, landmarks, and a "time landmark" axis. Geography scope placed below; the time-landmark axis is local HISTORY and is NOT placed: History of my village (printed p. 37), History of the founding of the mosque (p. 39), History of the founding of the mahadra (p. 41), History of the founding of the school (p. 43), History of the founding of the dispensary (p. 45).

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 5 LY G5 Social Studies): Half the book is Islamic history; the geography half ("Principles of General Geography") is pure foundations — solar system, Earth's shape/movements, grid, compass/GPS, land-and-water, map scale and symbols — with the symbol lesson taught by reading Libya's own mineral, wind and road distribution maps. Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, Islamic History — the Arabs before Islam (printed pp. 11–12), the Emergence of Islam (pp. 13–20), the Rashidun Caliphs (pp. 21–30), the Umayyad State (pp. 31–35), the Abbasid State (pp. 36–40), the Fatimid State (pp. 41–43), the Ayyubid State (pp. 44–45), the Crusades on the Arab Homeland (pp. 46–47), the Mamluks repelling the Mongols and Crusaders (pp. 48–51).

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 5 G5 Geography): first Geography year built as a single Earth-systems arc — planet, envelopes (lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere/biosphere), human society, then a closing "Protecting the Earth" chapter; every paragraph opens with "you will be able to" outcomes and chapters close with evaluation tests, plus two practical applications (map work; a week-long weather calendar).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 5 np-g5): Grade 5 "Our Earth" is a first full geography of Nepal — physical features, climate and lifestyle, natural vegetation, soils, map-drawing with symbols, globe and maps, and the wider South Asian neighbours; economic geography is district-based (Sankhuwasabha's occupations mapped place by place, from yak and sheep rearing and herb collecting in the high Himalaya to paddy in the low valleys).
