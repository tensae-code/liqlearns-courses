Iran's book, the *Iran Grade 11 Geography (2)* (printed p. 44), teaches: Lesson 4: Weathering and erosion at work — in mountains, physical and chemical weathering together (day–night temperature change, water freezing in cracks); V-shaped valleys (the Colca valley in Peru, Madagascar) and mountain glaciers; in deserts, wind the great eroder (soft soils, sparse vegetation), kaluts (کلوت) formed by wind and water in the dry lands of the USA, Egypt, China and Iran, and the kinds of sand dune (nebkha, seif, crescent barchan); on rocky coasts, sea stacks, caves and arches.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 11 Geography (2)): the whole book is built on one organising idea — the region (ناحیه). Four chapters divide the world into regions four ways: what a region is, natural regions (climate, landforms, living, coasts, mountains — with Nepal as the highland case study), human regions (cultural, economic), and political regions (the state, borders, geopolitics — with the Persian Gulf and the European Union as case studies).
