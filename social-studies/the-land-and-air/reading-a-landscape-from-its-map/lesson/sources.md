Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Malaysia's own way (MY T2 Geografi Tingkatan 2): the year pairs map skills (scale, topographic maps) with weather and climate, then transport and telecommunications as the human-geography core, an Asia regional study, and global warming with green technology as the environmental close.

Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 8 Geografija 8): Grade 8 is pure world regional geography, continent by continent (Africa, Australia/Oceania, the Americas and polar regions), each region worked through the same grid — position/relief/waters, climate/vegetation, population, economy — and the book closes with two world-problems units (rainforest loss, desert spread, water conflicts, the Aral Sea).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 10 UG S3 Geography Home-Study): COVID-era self-study booklet: first half is East Africa's human geography (towns/ports, transport, trade) plus heavy practical map-work and photograph interpretation on Ugandan survey-map extracts (Tororo, Kakuto, Samia); second half is a "studies in development" comparison — the Rhinelands and landlocked Switzerland held up as models against East Africa.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 np-g4): Grade 4 "Our Earth" moves to the district (Kanchanpur read on a map), the origin and structure of the Earth, and Nepal's two neighbours India and China; resources and traffic rules sit inside the Civic Awareness unit.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 6 np-g6): Grade 6 has a full "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth) unit — Earth's introduction, latitude/longitude, outer-layer structure, then Nepal itself: geographical description, climate, rivers/glaciers/lakes, natural vegetation, natural resources and map study, closing with disaster management; a population unit follows (demography, the census). Economic activities are organised by region and resource: agriculture and industry, services and trade, tourism, and economic resources (land and water; forest, minerals and people).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 2 Conociendo mi Mundo G2): Geography begins hyper-locally — the child's own school, municipality and landscape — and the country is introduced through its two great lakes (Cocibolca and Xolotlán) as the "land of lakes and volcanoes" before any wider world content. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit II family, hygiene and food (printed pp. 9–32, home/health); Unit IV plant/animal/human-body topics (printed pp. 57–93, Natural Science); Unit V national symbols, heroes and traditions (printed pp. 113–143, Civics/History); Unit VI taxes (printed p. 151, Civics); efemérides throughout (History/Civics).
