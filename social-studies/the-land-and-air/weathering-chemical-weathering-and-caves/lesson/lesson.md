## Sub lesson 1 — Weathering that changes the rock itself

Begin with rusting iron at home: the rust is not the iron any more. Chemical weathering changes the rock's substance itself. It is strongest where it is hot and wet, and very slow where it is cold and dry.

## Sub lesson 2 — Oxidation and carbonation

Oxidation is oxygen joining minerals — it rusts copper and iron. Weak acids in rainwater dissolve rocks like limestone; that dissolving is carbonation. The rain needs no factory acid: the weak acid it carries is enough, given time.

[GIF 11484 — a drip building a stalactite downward drop by drop]

## Sub lesson 3 — Caves, stalactites, stalagmites, pillars

Rain sinking underground dissolves limestone into underground rivers, passages and caves. In the caves grow stalactites, hanging from the roof; stalagmites, rising from the floor; and pillars, where a hanging and a rising one meet.
