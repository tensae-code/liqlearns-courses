# Continents, oceans, islands, peninsulas

## Do it first

Look at a world map before you read. How much of what you see is water, and how much is land? Name every ocean you can. Then read, and check your guesses.

## Sub lesson 1 — Land and water on the world map

The Earth's surface is shared unevenly between land and water. Set out on the world map, the pattern is simple to state and worth fixing in memory: the **oceans together cover 71% of the surface**, and the continents — the great land masses — cover the rest. Geography begins here, with a water world carrying islands of land.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five oceans

There are **five oceans**. The **Pacific** is the largest and deepest of them, covering about **a third of the whole Earth** by itself. The others are the Atlantic, the Indian, the Arctic and the Southern Ocean. Learn the five as one set — every later lesson about coasts, currents and climates stands on this map.

## Sub lesson 3 — Coastal features — islands, peninsulas, isthmuses

Where land meets water, the meeting makes shapes, and the book defines three. An **island** is land completely surrounded by water. A **peninsula** is land reaching out into water, joined to the mainland and washed on three sides. An **isthmus** is a narrow strip of land joining two larger land areas, with water on both sides. Keep the three apart by asking one question of each shape: how much of it is surrounded by water — all of it (island), three sides (peninsula), or is it the narrow join itself (isthmus)?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the land–water split, the five oceans with the Pacific's rank, and the three coastal features with their one-question test.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sketch a world map from memory and label the five oceans.
2. Draw one island, one peninsula and one isthmus, and label each.
3. Write the 71% figure in a sentence about the Earth's surface.
