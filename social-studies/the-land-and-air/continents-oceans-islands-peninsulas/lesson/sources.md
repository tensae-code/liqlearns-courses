**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Guatemala, Palestine, Bahamas, Eritrea, North Macedonia, Syria). Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grade 6 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): the Grade 6 Social Studies year is built as three paired terms — culture and independence first, then the physical world (mountains, landmasses and water bodies), then climate, Earth movements and the atmosphere, closing on Caribbean cooperation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Pairing: the teacher guide was first published in 2022 and the student textbook in 2023, with the same writers and the same eight units in the same order; the student book is the learner text of record.

Built in the Geography fix build (2026-11-12) from the Grade 10 student placement named in the audit's NOT STOCKED list; no content beyond that placement's pages is taught.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Palestine's own way (Grade 6, PS G6P1): Grade 6 opens with how we know — the map and its elements as the first source of geographical knowledge — then walks the Arab homeland region by region (peninsula, Levant, Palestine, Iraq, North Africa), each time fixing location first, then surface and climate, then the ancient civilisation the land produced.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): The Grade 6 book is civics-led (government, elections, courts, constitution, finance ministry) but opens the primary course's widest geography: the world's continents and oceans, political and physical maps, global warming and natural disasters, human resources and the job market, worldwide transport and communication through Nassau's ports of entry, and a closing cultural comparison of The Bahamas with Africa, Australia, Brazil and Japan.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 7 mk-g7): regional geography taught continent by continent — Europe then Asia — each continent opened with its physical characteristics (position, coastline, relief, climate, hydrography), then its socio-geography (population, peoples and religions, science and culture, settlements), then region-by-region reviews with individual country lessons and a tourism-centres lesson per region; the home country is taught inside the Balkan/Southern Europe region alongside Albania.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 9 mk-g9): Grade 9 finishes the regional-geography cycle with the non-European continents — Asia again in greater depth (climate factors and phenomena, monsoons and typhoons, air pollution), then Africa, the Americas, and Australia with Oceania and the polar regions — each continent in the same pattern: position and coastline, relief, climate, hydrography, natural wealth and economy, population, a regional review, one or two spotlight countries (China, India; South Africa; USA, Brazil) and one problem lesson (food and water shortage in Africa; air pollution in Asia; global warming in the polar regions).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 8 Geography G8): Grade 8 leaves the Arab world and tours the "new" continents in a fixed six-lesson pattern (location and surface, natural environments, settlement/migration, population and cities, resources and economy, one big country study — USA, Brazil, Australia), gives Antarctica a whole unit as a land of ice and science, and closes with a unit of contemporary global challenges (water, food, pollution, warming, population, desertification).
