# Lesson plan — Continents, oceans, islands, peninsulas

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Land and Air · **Lesson:** Continents, oceans, islands, peninsulas
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Landforms of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Earth's surface is 71% ocean; the five oceans are named with the Pacific largest and deepest; and an island, a peninsula and an isthmus are defined and told apart.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the world-landforms lesson, narrowed to the land–water frame every later Grade 10 unit uses.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Land and water on the world map** — How the Earth's surface is shared between land and water.
2. **The five oceans** — The five oceans, with the Pacific first.
3. **Coastal features — islands, peninsulas, isthmuses** — The three coastal features defined.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Major and minor landforms — built up and worn down. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the land–water split of the Earth's surface.
2. Name the five oceans and the Pacific's rank among them.
3. Define island, peninsula and isthmus and tell the last two apart.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else, beyond what hands-on.md names for its activities.
