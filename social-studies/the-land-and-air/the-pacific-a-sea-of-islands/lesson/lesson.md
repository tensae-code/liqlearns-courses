# The Pacific — a Sea of Islands

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the Pacific as a sea of islands — one connected ocean home, crossed by star-guided voyaging, where Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu once formed one exchange community with no borders, as Nauru's Years 5–6 *People and the Sea in the Pacific* teaches it on printed p. 2. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A sea of islands

The reader opens the Pacific itself: not empty water between separate islands, but a sea of islands. Pacific voyagers crossed it guided by the stars, and the old community of Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu exchanged goods and knowledge across it with no borders — the ocean connected people; it did not divide them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Teaching the sea of islands — the teacher guide's notes (teacher guide — evidence)

This part stands on the teacher guide, written for the teacher, not handed to the pupil. Its notes for use of Book 1 teach Pacific people's connectedness with the ocean, and set a reflection point on real coral-reef degradation at Funafuti, Tuvalu — the connected sea can be damaged, and the damage is studied where it is happening.

Nauru's own way: Nauru uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisors Fay Itaia & Sharon Kam for Nauru) — Geography is taught as ocean stewardship: the Pacific as a connected "sea of islands", its animals, and the traditional fishing knowledge of Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue, ending in a plastic-waste action plan. And the guide's way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide teaches the readers through Pacific philosophy (head, heart and hands), reflection points on real reef degradation (Funafuti, Tuvalu), elder interviews, and a take-action project cycle, with formal-curriculum links country by country. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
