**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Nauru, Tuvalu). The teacher guides are cited as evidence for the teaching-notes parts, counted nowhere beyond the student books' countries.**

Nauru's own way: Nauru uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisors Fay Itaia & Sharon Kam for Nauru) — Geography is taught as ocean stewardship: the Pacific as a connected "sea of islands", its animals, and the traditional fishing knowledge of Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue, ending in a plastic-waste action plan. And the guide's way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide teaches the readers through Pacific philosophy (head, heart and hands), reflection points on real reef degradation (Funafuti, Tuvalu), elder interviews, and a take-action project cycle, with formal-curriculum links country by country. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

Tuvalu's own way: Tuvalu uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisor Valisi Tovia for Tuvalu) — and Tuvalu itself is a case study inside it: Tuvalu's own traditional fishing and bird-catching (printed p. 14) is one of the three country fishing studies. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing. And the guide's way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide's reef-degradation reflection point is Tuvalu's own Funafuti lagoon (Fongafale islet) — urbanisation, population density, nutrient and rubbish pollution and overfishing on Tuvalu's main atoll.
