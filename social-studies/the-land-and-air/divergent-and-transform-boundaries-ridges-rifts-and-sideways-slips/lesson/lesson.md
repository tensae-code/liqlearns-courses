# Divergent and transform boundaries — ridges, rifts and sideways slips

## Do it first

Tear a sheet of thick paper very slowly in two and watch the torn edge form. Then slide the two halves sideways past each other. Those are the two movements of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Divergent — apart, with new crust

Where plates diverge — move apart — magma rises into the gap and builds a ridge. Under the ocean this makes mid-ocean ridges, like the mid-Atlantic ridge, with sea-floor spreading and shallow earthquakes as new floor forms.

Divergence inside a continent works differently: the land stretches and splits open into a rift valley. The East African Rift is the named example — a continent in the act of tearing, on the way to a new ocean.

## Sub lesson 2 — Transform — sideways, in jerks

At a transform boundary, plates slide sideways past each other. They do not glide smoothly: they stick, strain builds, and then they slip in a sudden jerk — the San Andreas fault is the famous case.

Underneath every boundary type sits the engine: mantle convection. Hot material in the mantle slowly circulates, and the moving asthenosphere drags the plates riding on it.
