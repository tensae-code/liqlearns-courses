Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG P5 Merged Home-Study SST): The whole SST course is Uganda itself: locating Uganda and its districts/neighbours, Uganda's physical features, climate and vegetation, then Uganda's population. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Mathematics section (PDF pp. 9–220); English section (PDF pp. 221–274); SST Term Two — The People of the Pre-Colonial Period, ethnic groups, explorers, colonial rule, Legislative Council and the independence struggle (History, SST printed pp. 36–54); SST Term Three — national symbols, democracy, The Government of Uganda, duties of citizens (Civics, SST printed pp. 56–70).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 3 P3 Social Studies SB): The land unit is anchored on the child's own payam — Mount Kinyeti, the River Nile and the Sudd Swamp are the named examples — and features are taught as double-sided: benefits to us, and problems they can cause, like rivers that fill and flood.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 3 P3 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide adds the settlement half of migration — factors for migration and settlement together — and a practical question the pupil's book only implies: what can be done to minimise migration? That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — South Sudan is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 5 P5 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide insists on both sides of every feature — positive and negative effects of physical features on human activities — and on human agency: dumping material in a valley can cause flooding, and climate dictates which farming system a region of Africa can practise. That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — South Sudan is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 6 P6 Social Studies SB): Tourism is taught as national homework — map the parks, weigh the landlocked disadvantage against South Africa, set up the anti-poaching unit — and the fishing unit ties grounds back to Unit 2's physical features and forward to roads that must be improved so fish can reach market.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 7 P7 Social Studies SB): Everything is costed against home ground — Mount Imatong both blocks movement and feeds rivers and the Imatong forest industry; mining is debated head-on ("Mining is the backbone to the economy of South Sudan") with a field trip to a real mining sector.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 7 P7 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide starts the land unit outdoors — learners go and look for physical features neighbouring their school, beyond the ones in their books — and ends it with the balancing question: principles and strategies to protect the environment while industry and agriculture develop. That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — South Sudan is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).
