# Physical weathering — heat, ice, roots

## Do it first

Find a cracked stone or a cracked pavement slab near you. Write down what you think cracked it. Compare your answer with the three processes in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Heat and ice

Exogenic forces level the land through denudation — weathering plus erosion — and deposition. Physical weathering breaks rock into pieces of the same chemistry; nothing in the rock changes except its size.

Thermal expansion and contraction do the first kind of work: day heating and night cooling expand and shrink the surface until layers peel away like an onion — exfoliation. Frost action does the second: water enters cracks, freezes, expands, and wedges the rock apart, piling broken scree at the foot of slopes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plants and animals

The third process is living. Tree roots grow into cracks and force them wider, year by year, until blocks split away.

Burrowing animals — worms, rabbits and moles — break and loosen the ground from inside, opening the rock and soil to the other two processes. Heat, ice and roots together: the rock is broken by the weather, by water, and by life itself.
