# Running water at work — sheet, rill, gully and the river's courses

## Do it first

After rain, look at any bare slope you know: is the soil washed evenly, in small channels, or in a deep cut? Note which. That scale is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sheet, rill, gully

Running water is the most powerful external agent shaping the land. Its force rises with its speed — and falls where vegetation cover holds the soil against it.

Runoff erosion grades in three steps. Sheet erosion washes evenly and is easily missed. Rill erosion cuts small channels. Gully erosion lets floods cut deep — the same rain, concentrated, doing damage out of all proportion.

## Sub lesson 2 — The river's three courses

Along its course, a river changes character. The swift upper course cuts downward, deepening its valley. In the middle course the valley widens as tributaries join at confluences, adding their water and their load.

In the slow lower course the river can no longer carry everything, and it deposits its load. One river, three kinds of work: cutting, widening, laying down.
