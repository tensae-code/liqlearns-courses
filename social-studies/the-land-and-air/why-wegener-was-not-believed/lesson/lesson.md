# Why Wegener was not believed

## Do it first

Think of an idea you believe is true but others doubt. Write down why they doubt it: is it the idea itself, or something about who said it and how? Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The man and the map of science

The first reason drift was refused was about the man: Wegener was not a geologist by training, and the geologists of his day did not trust an outsider's theory about their own science.

The second reason was about where science sat. The influential geoscientists were northern, while Wegener's best data were southern — drawn from southern continents the northern establishment knew least and valued least.

## Sub lesson 2 — The timing and the missing engine

The third reason was his timing: Wegener placed the breakup late, in the Cenozoic, and that late timing was implausible against the evidence of much older separations.

The fourth reason was the deepest: he offered no mechanism and no direct evidence. Nothing in his theory could say what force could move a continent. The legacy of the refusal is the shift that followed anyway — from fixism, the idea that continents are fixed, to mobilism, the idea that they move. Once drift was finally accepted, geology itself had changed.
