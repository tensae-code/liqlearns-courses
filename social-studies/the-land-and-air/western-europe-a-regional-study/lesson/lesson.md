## Sub lesson 2 — Cuba's way — the book's own teaching way

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 8 Geografía 2 G8): This older edition teaches world economic geography as a story — first the political map's dynamics through history (colonial empires, independences, 1914, post-1945, the 1960s, the 1990s), then resources, then production, and it closes with full regional studies; Latin America is studied first and in most depth, and every world topic is measured against Cuba's own sugar, tobacco, nickel and trade.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 11 OM Geografía Regional G11) (teacher guide — evidence): Regional geography is taught with a fixed "plan tipo" (situation → physical character → socio-economic character → regions → political map → country studies) and heavy statistical work — the annexes teach teachers to build statistical materials, read demographic-indicator ranges and the HDI, and close the world tour with the Cuban archipelago and the Tarea Vida climate plan in schools. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Cuba is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 11 Geografía Regional G11): The capstone of Cuban school geography: five continents studied with one fixed plan (situation → physical character → socio-economic character → regions → political-map changes → countries-and-capitals → deep country monographs), then a full closing chapter on the Cuban archipelago longer than any continent chapter — the world tour exists to bring the learner home with the tools to characterise their own country.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grades 10-11 Cuaderno de mapas): The senior-cycle companion is pure map work — a full outline-map atlas the student fills in: world and continents first, then the "significant regions" exactly as the G11 Regional course carves them (Caribe, América Latina, América Anglosajona, África Subsahariana, Europa Occidental, Medio Oriente, Este y sureste de Asia), then one outline map per studied country, and it lands back home with the Cuban archipelago, the provinces and a street-level La Habana. It closes with a "Datos interesantes" page of world records. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
