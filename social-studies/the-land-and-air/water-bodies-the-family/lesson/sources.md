Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P1): Social Studies opens with a full Earth-science unit (shape, movements, surface, life components, disasters) taught through Palestinian examples — seasons read off Palestinian crops, surface forms read off Palestine's own map, which pupils draw with its borders.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 10): This is the physical-geography year: the universe and Earth's layers, continental drift with volcanoes and earthquakes — including earthquake-prevention measures as a group task — water and winds across the continents, and a closing unit on political and economic geography that treats borders, geopolitical order and international climate agreements as geography, with hurricane-season citizen responsibility as the water unit's group activity.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado06 Secundaria Activa): Grade 6 Secundaria Activa starts from the universe and the Earth as a "living machine" (rocks and wrinkles, water, air), then follows humans evolving, migrating from Africa to America and adapting to each continent's environment — geography as the stage for all the history that follows.

Eritrea's own way (ER G8 Social Studies): Grade 8 is the Eritrea-physical-geography year — relief, hydrology and water shortage, people-environment adaptation and Eritrea's strategic Red Sea location are taught as four full Geography units before the History units begin.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 4 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the same five-strand structure as Grade 3, widening the child's world from the local area to the district — district features, renewable versus non-renewable resources, and map scale. Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 4 UG P4 SST Home-Study): Social Studies organised entirely around "our district": the child's own district's physical features, vegetation, settlement and work are the geography content. Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies G3): the Grade 3 book widens in rings — Me, Me and You, My Safety, My Society, My Environment, My Homeland — and the Geography lives in the last three rings: road safety, Syrian-made products, ways of living, then a whole environment unit (sky lanterns, locating my position, discovering Syria, the weather bulletin, a water journey).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2): the same widening-rings design as Grade 3 (Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland), with the Geography carried by the society/environment/homeland rings: Earth's inhabitants, building in the homeland, heritage, and an environment unit built on the land's beauty and riches, diverse nature, water drops and a windy day.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 1 Social Studies G1): the Grade 1 book starts the same widening-rings series (Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland); Geography enters through street-crossing safety, then the environment unit — nature and the farm, the four friends (the seasons), beautiful nature, water "more precious than gold", a precious treasure, and a journey into space — and closes with the homeland's antiquities and pictures from across Syria.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 3 geo-3af-m): The youngest geography book in the set is built on five axes worked through observation pictures (I observe / I discuss / I conclude): the map key, position, location, landmarks, and a "time landmark" axis. Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 10 G10 Geography of Man and the Environment): secondary geography is thematic, not regional — man and space, man representing his geographic field (remote sensing, projections, maps), man organising it (rural/urban), climate and water, the dynamic Earth, and the great environmental systems.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G9 Geography T1): From Grade 9 Geography stands alone as its own book: the Earth's rock and water spheres first, then the geography of the Arab homeland — its natural features, its human features and its challenges.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 6 Estudios Sociales G6): The primary cycle closes at world scale: after the planet unit, Unit II treats the whole American continent and Unit III repeats the same template — position, political division, relief, resources, hydrography, climate, risk areas, economic activities — for Europe, Asia, Africa and Oceania; the remaining five units are world and Latin American History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV first human groups and the Neolithic (printed pp. 98–113, History); Unit V ancient civilisations to the medieval world (printed pp. 118–134, History); Unit VI the modern world (printed pp. 140–164, History); Unit VII transformations of the 18th–19th centuries (printed pp. 168–183, History); Unit VIII the world in the 20th century (printed pp. 188–204, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 Estudios Sociales G9 full-year): The classroom parallel of the Grade 9 Geography module (Book 12): five world-Geography units working continent by continent (Asia, Europe, Africa and Oceania in turn for position, relief, climate, hydrography, population and economies), closing on risk prevention with disaster impact in Nicaragua and the world; the second half is world History from human evolution to the modern age. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI evolution of human beings (printed pp. 137–144, History); Unit VII first agricultural civilisations (printed pp. 147–164, History, except the geographical-setting topics placed below); Unit VIII classical civilisations (printed pp. 165–189, History, except the topic placed below); Unit IX the medieval world (printed pp. 191–205, History); Unit X age of great changes (printed pp. 209–223, History, except the geographical discoveries placed below).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 CCSS Geografía Módulo G9): A pure-Geography self-study module (no History/Civics in this book): the Grade 9 world-Geography course taught continent by continent — position and political organisation, physical landscape, human diversity, production and consumption, and a closing planet-care unit on prevention and mitigation — with Nicaragua used as the home reference point throughout.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Geography student): grade-10 Geography is a methods-first course — it opens with the science's history, branches and GIS/GPS/aerospace methods, works through the lithosphere with a full natural-disasters lesson, and closes with resources measured by endowment; Armenia's own rivers fill the hydrology tables and Armenia's place in modern geopolitics closes the political-map theme.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 OM Geografía G5) (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher guide teaches the same four units as the programme/student book but adds Cuba-specific complementary materials no other country set carries: the Tarea Vida state climate-change plan as classroom content, project-based-learning orientation, and a long section on mastering the changing world political map through country cases (Congo, Belarus, the Yugoslav successor states). That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Cuba is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 Geografía G5): The first Geography book a Cuban child meets opens with the history of geography itself (cuneiform maps, Hecataeus), builds all map skills before any content, and ends the planet tour with world population, the world political map and world heritage — with Cuba's own places (Llanura del Cauto, Trinidad) woven in as examples throughout.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Amangalieva NIS): the NIS (Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools) inquiry textbook: every lesson opens with a «Бұл сабақта» (In this lesson) goal box and closes with a «Сенің кезегің!» (Your turn!) task block; each section ends in a «Қорытынды» summary built round a concept map; the book closes with a trilingual Kazakh–Russian–English glossary. Country-studies section works through how a state improves its geographic position, with the UAE and Singapore as cases and Kazakhstan as the home example.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Egorina): the 2025 Atamura course is organised by numbered paragraphs (§1–§68) with coded learning goals (e.g. 7.3.2.4) and a «Негізгі ұғымдар» key-concepts box per paragraph; it gives Kazakhstani science its own paragraph (§3, research in the war and post-war years), a whole practical strand on building geographic databases (§§10, 18, 49, 54–55), and a country-studies block that classifies states by position — continental, archipelago, island and peninsular — with Kazakhstan's own economic-geographic position as the worked example.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Egorina 2017): the 2017 first edition of the Atamura course (the 2025 Book 2 is its revision): same six-section architecture but twelve numbered practical works, four of them database-building exercises on Kazakhstan itself — a geographic database for Kazakhstan's regions (§51) and for Kazakhstan's transport system (§52) — plus a practical on the natural complex of the pupil's own locality (§37).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Karatabanov): every paragraph is titled as a question («Как устроена Земля внутри?», «Все ли одинаково обеспечены водой?») and every section closes with a «Мои географические достижения» (My geographic achievements) self-check page; Part 2 asks what is known about "our country's soils" (§§37–38) and how to develop natural resources "without harm to nature and people" (§§55–57).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Tolybekova): the Mektep course teaches geographic nomenclature continent by continent (§§6–8: Africa/Australia/Antarctica, the Americas, Eurasia) with a practical on completing an outline map, then works through the natural complexes belt by belt (§§39–47: arctic, temperate forest/steppe, desert, tropical, subequatorial, equatorial); Kazakhstan anchors the applied ends — its regions' flora and fauna (§33), its soil types and soil resources (§§34–35), its population (§52, Practical №3) and its own economic-geographic position (§62).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Tolybekova 2017): the 2017 first edition of the Mektep course (Book 5 is its 2025 revision): the same continent-by-continent nomenclature and belt-by-belt natural-complexes architecture, with a lithosphere field-research practical, a §52 «Человек — часть биосферы» (The human being as part of the biosphere), and a closing §60 «Этот многоликий мир» (This many-faced world) survey of the world's diversity.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 8 Abilmazhinova): the Grade 8 Mektep course deepens every Grade 7 line — relief classification and rock ages, global circulation and the climatic belts, natural zones of both hemispheres, demographic calculation and policy design (Practical №7 has pupils propose their own demographic-policy model), resource assessment, industry location factors, and the political map — closing with Kazakhstan's own place in political-integration processes (§54).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 8 Karatabanov): the Almatykitap Grade 8 course runs the same six-section architecture as the Mektep book (Book 7) but in Karatabanov's question-led style, closing Part 2 with §68 «Мой географический год» (My geographic year) — a pupil's own review of a year of geographic work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 8 Sakhova NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course phrases its lessons as questions and tasks — «Әр жерде неліктен уақыт әркелкі?» (Why is time different in different places?, 2.2), «Тау жыныстары қалай пайда болады?» (How do rocks form?, 3.1.5), «Өзеннің суы қалай өзгереді?» (How does a river's water change?, 3.3.4) — and adds a lesson on the culture of showing geographic names on the map (2.3).
