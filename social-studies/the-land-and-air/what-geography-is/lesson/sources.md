**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Bolivia, Angola, Dominican Republic, Slovenia, Vietnam, Uganda, Sudan, Syria, Venezuela, Mauritania, Kuwait).**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 7, Secundaria 1): The Bolivian Texto de Aprendizaje runs every unit through the same four moments — Práctica, Teoría, Valoración, Producción — and frames geography inside the plurinational, Madre Tierra worldview: continental drift and geological time sit beside the Laws of Mother Earth (071 and 300), the human right to water, and a closing general geography of Bolivia itself (regions, orography, political map, salt flats and lithium).

Bolivia's own way (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher compendium teaches the same four-moment lesson shape (Práctica, Teoría, Valoración, Producción) with QR-linked videos and local openings — a La Paz landslide video opens the geography unit — and it adds two geography units the sampled student books do not carry at those grades: the scientific development of geographic science with cartography and coordinates (year 3), and water as a human right with Andean water management (year 4). That evidence is stocked here as the way Bolivia teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Angola's own way (Grade 5): Geography opens as "geographic space" — the natural space and the human-built space (Luanda port, Benguela railway, Cambambe dam, Huambo housing) — before any physical topic; Angola's own position, borders, population figures (INE) and farm/fishery tables carry the world topics. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 6): The Libro Abierto primary books open with two full geography units before any history: geography as a science first (its concepts, principles and helper sciences), then a continent-by-continent tour that always pairs physical aspects with population and culture — and the year's history closes with the country's own lived geography, Hurricane Georges over the Dominican Republic.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Địa lí 10): a single Grade 10 course that runs the whole discipline — map craft, the Earth's spheres, then economic geography sector by sector — with Vietnam itself as the standing worked example (its power-plant and rice maps, the Red River's regime at Hanoi, Hanoi's climate chart, Vietnam's GDP structure, its internet and tourism figures) inside world-scale treatment.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide mirrors the learner book chapter for chapter, adding teacher instructions, possible responses and assessment for every activity — the same East-Africa-first, activity-led pedagogy, plus teacher-only content on plotting weather on maps and making simple weather instruments. That evidence is stocked here as the way Uganda teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Uganda is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 5 SD Geography 5): The first-ever Geography book in the post-2019 curriculum starts from the child's own classroom and school: directions are learned by standing facing the sunrise, map elements through a pupil's-house map, and only then does the book step out to Sudan, Africa and the world — a local-to-global ladder.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 9 G9 Geography Venezuela): a full national geography written as Geohistoria — every physical topic (position, climate, relief, hydrography) is paired with the historical stages that organised the same space (agrarian Venezuela, oil Venezuela, 21st-century Venezuela), and the Amazon rainforest closes the book as a defence-of-life argument.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 7 guide-geo-1as) (teacher guide — evidence): A competency-based guide (IPN 2025 reform) that fixes the whole Grade 7 course in three units — Earth and its main relief, Climate, Population and urbanisation — each unit ending in a class project, and every general topic anchored to Mauritania itself: the country's astronomical location, its relief map and Atlantic coast, rainfall differences between its wilayas, its age pyramid, and the growth of Nouakchott. Sample lesson plans (fiches) are given for one lesson per unit, plus model term exams with answers. That evidence is stocked here as the way Mauritania teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Mauritania is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 7 geo-1as-m): The student book of the course specified in Book 1 (the teacher guide): the same three units and 13 lessons, each lesson opening with expected outcomes and key concepts and closing with a concepts list per unit. General topics are worked with Mauritanian examples throughout, as the guide prescribes.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.
