# Paper maps vs digital data

## Do it first

Before you read, think of a paper map and the same map on a phone. List what each one can do that the other cannot. Say your list first — then check it against the book's five digital wins.

## Sub lesson 1 — Analogue — the hardcopy world

Analogue representation is the hardcopy world: paper maps and printed sheets. It can be read anywhere with no device — but it is one copy, at one scale, fading and tearing from the day it is printed, and it cannot be reworked without redrawing it by hand.

[Picture 21949 — a fading paper map beside the same map bright on a tablet]

## Sub lesson 2 — Digital — the five wins

Digital representation wins five ways: it works on devices; it copies and transmits fast; it stores densely in small space; it resists deterioration; and it is easy to transform, process and analyse. The same map can be enlarged, layered, queried and shared in ways paper never allowed.
