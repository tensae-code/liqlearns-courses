# Hands-on — Fieldwork And Official Statistics Geographic Sources

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Part One, Chapter 2: the field study (fieldwork) is among the most important steps of scientific research, showing the researcher what books, statistics and maps cannot; the field kit is a car or a strong jeep for desert areas, a compass or mobile phone, binoculars, a measuring tape of about 50 m, a small wooden drawing board, coloured pens and paper, and bags or jars for samples; in the field the researcher verifies collected information, gathers new data by questionnaire or random sample, interviews residents about a problem's causes and cures, and photographs the area's phenomena; Part One, Chapter 2: official statistics — the census defined (United Nations, 1980) as the whole process of collecting, classifying and publishing the demographic, economic and social data of every member of a society in a set area and period; states run it every ten years (Egypt, America) or five (Kuwait, Japan — Kuwait has lately moved to ten); beside it stand vital statistics (births, deaths, marriages, divorces), Kuwait's annual statistical abstract, issued since 1964 and covering climate, population, labour, education, income, trade and health, the civil-information guide built on identity-card data, and the ministries' own statistical bulletins. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
