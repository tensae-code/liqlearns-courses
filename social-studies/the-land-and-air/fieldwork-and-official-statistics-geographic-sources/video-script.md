Kuwait's book, the *Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1* (printed p. 38), teaches: Part One, Chapter 2: the field study (fieldwork) is among the most important steps of scientific research, showing the researcher what books, statistics and maps cannot; the field kit is a car or a strong jeep for desert areas, a compass or mobile phone, binoculars, a measuring tape of about 50 m, a small wooden drawing board, coloured pens and paper, and bags or jars for samples; in the field the researcher verifies collected information, gathers new data by questionnaire or random sample, interviews residents about a problem's causes and cures, and photographs the area's phenomena. Kuwait's book, the *Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1* (printed pp. 39–40), teaches: Part One, Chapter 2: official statistics — the census defined (United Nations, 1980) as the whole process of collecting, classifying and publishing the demographic, economic and social data of every member of a society in a set area and period; states run it every ten years (Egypt, America) or five (Kuwait, Japan — Kuwait has lately moved to ten); beside it stand vital statistics (births, deaths, marriages, divorces), Kuwait's annual statistical abstract, issued since 1964 and covering climate, population, labour, education, income, trade and health, the civil-information guide built on identity-card data, and the ministries' own statistical bulletins.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.
