# Geography of Ethiopia — course road map

## Do it first

Look at a map of Ethiopia. Name one mountain, one river and one lake you already know. This lesson shows you the road the course will take to study all of them — and much more.

## Sub lesson 1 — Eight units, one country

The course has eight units, and every one of them is about Ethiopia itself. Unit 1 tells the geological history and topography — how the land was made. Unit 2 is the climate. Unit 3 is the natural resource base: rivers, lakes, soils, minerals, vegetation and wildlife. Unit 4 is population. Unit 5 is the major economic and cultural activities. Unit 6 is how people and the natural environment change each other. Unit 7 is today’s issues and the Green Legacy. Unit 8, last, teaches the inquiry skills — maps and graphs — that the whole course has been using.

## Sub lesson 2 — How every unit is built

Every unit runs the same way, so you always know where you are. It opens by recalling what came before, gives its outcomes and a box of key terms, then takes its sections one by one. Each section opens with what you will be able to do at its end, and with questions to think about. Activities, figures, maps and tables with Ethiopian data are woven through — the Rift Valley, the Abbay and Wabe Shebelle rivers, Lake Tana, the climate zones, coffee exports. Each unit closes with a summary and a review exercise.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the eight units in order, and what each one studies. Then explain the rhythm inside a unit, from the opening recall to the closing review.
