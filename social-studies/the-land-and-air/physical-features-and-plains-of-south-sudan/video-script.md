South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 9 S1 Geography SB): Geography is built around South Sudan itself — its own physical features, resources, floods/drought experience and the Nile — with paper-folding and paper-tearing practical experiments for folding, faulting and sea-floor spreading, and group discussion of local examples in nearly every topic.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 9 S1 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide mirrors the student's book unit by unit (its printed contents list the same 8 units) and adds content maps, key inquiry questions, expected answers and practical activities — including the sea-floor-spreading paper practical — for every topic. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 10 S2 Geography SB): Each general topic is immediately re-taught through South Sudan — its named rivers (Sobat, Adar, Yabus, Daga), the Sudd swamplands, its oil, fishing and transport problems, and case-study research tasks set by a mock ministry brief.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 10 S2 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Mirrors the student's book's 8 units with content maps, expected answers and group-presentation case studies (agriculture solutions, fishing solutions, fold-mountain impacts), plus a field-observation exercise on the environment near the school. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 2 P2 Social Studies SB): "The Land of South Sudan" is taught through riddles and a word-search — Mount Kinyeti, the River Nile, the White Nile, the Nile Valley and the Sudd swamps — and location is taught by a song about compass directions.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 2 P2 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Features are located through the child's own payam and boma first, and the guide's answers stress what features give — hills, mountains and valleys make the land beautiful; a river or lake provides water. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 3 P3 Social Studies SB): The land unit is anchored on the child's own payam — Mount Kinyeti, the River Nile and the Sudd Swamp are the named examples — and features are taught as double-sided: benefits to us, and problems they can cause, like rivers that fill and flood.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 6 P6 Social Studies SB): Tourism is taught as national homework — map the parks, weigh the landlocked disadvantage against South Africa, set up the anti-poaching unit — and the fishing unit ties grounds back to Unit 2's physical features and forward to roads that must be improved so fish can reach market.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 7 P7 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide starts the land unit outdoors — learners go and look for physical features neighbouring their school, beyond the ones in their books — and ends it with the balancing question: principles and strategies to protect the environment while industry and agriculture develop. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; South Sudan is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.
