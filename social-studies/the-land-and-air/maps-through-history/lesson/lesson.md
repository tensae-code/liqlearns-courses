# Maps through history

## Do it first

The first maps were scratches showing the way to game and fish. Today satellites measure every metre. Read the two great stages between.

## Sub lesson 1 — Primitive maps

The first map-making was primitive: route sketches drawn to show hunting and fishing grounds and the ways to them — memory made visible, from observation, without measurement. Such sketches served their makers exactly: to return, and to tell others the way.

[Picture 21118 — a sketch route map beside a modern topographic sheet]

## Sub lesson 2 — Modern cartography

The second stage is measured, modern cartography: survey and measurement fix every feature’s position, and maps are drawn to true scale for whole countries and the world. The two stages are the two broad methods of map-making — from observation-sketch to measured survey.
