Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 5 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the five strands widen again — from district (Grade 4) to the physical environment of the province and nation; resources are taught through sustainable use (re-using water, reduce/reuse projects) and map work culminates in drawing sketch maps of the school and locality. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies G3): the Grade 3 book widens in rings — Me, Me and You, My Safety, My Society, My Environment, My Homeland — and the Geography lives in the last three rings: road safety, Syrian-made products, ways of living, then a whole environment unit (sky lanterns, locating my position, discovering Syria, the weather bulletin, a water journey).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2): the same widening-rings design as Grade 3 (Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland), with the Geography carried by the society/environment/homeland rings: Earth's inhabitants, building in the homeland, heritage, and an environment unit built on the land's beauty and riches, diverse nature, water drops and a windy day.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 2 G2 CS Viva Venezuela): population education (UNESCO population-education guides reworked) woven into story form — Tío Conejo tales, grandparents' memories and the child's own school and street carry population, housing, landscape and weather-sign content.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 SS5): the Class 5 Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 51–85) deepens the Class 4 spiral — same BOLTS, landforms, weather and population topics, but now with scale types, latitude/longitude and time-zone arithmetic, worked population-density calculations, and graph-drawing exercises from real Pakistani city data.
