**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Guatemala, Ecuador, Egypt, Bahamas, Colombia). Haiti is named here as teacher-sequence evidence only, counted nowhere. Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grade 4 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Social Studies is one of four subjects fused into a single Exploratory Core guide, organised by four attainment themes; Geography enters through Jamaica itself — Taino settlement maps, cardinal points, Jamaica's physical features, parishes, weather and the Earth in the Solar System — always with hands-on map and model work. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
Jamaica's own way (Grade 5 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Grade 5 Social Studies pairs a heritage year (Europeans and Africans, national heroes, Caribbean culture) with a physical-environment year — erosion, locating Jamaica and the Caribbean by compass and latitude/longitude, types of rainfall, the Solar System, plants and water pollution. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography opens with representing the Earth itself — globe-to-flat projection problems, orientation practised on the ground with compass/GPS/Google Maps, and time-zone puzzles worked on Haiti's own links (midday shared with Québec, 18h in Paris, Port-au-Prince–Paris flights); every séance ends in a croquis, a glossary and field observation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 3 book): the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Egypt's own way (Grade 4 Term 1): the very first Social Studies book starts from the child's own space — drawing a plan of the home and school, comparing walking routes by length and landmarks — before maps in general, then the child's own city and governorate, and only then Egypt as homeland; a robot mascot teaches alongside named child guides from different governorates.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado2_01): Escuela Nueva self-paced guides (parts A–D: explore, deepen, practise, apply) set in rural Colombia — veredas, resguardos, coffee regions; geography starts from the walk home-to-school, the classroom plan and the home municipality (Aracataca/Ciénaga worked example).

Colombia's own way (Insertos_sociales_03_Cartilla_01): a "Monopoly of Time and Space" board that drills Grade 3 space content — compass points, relief forms, Colombia's departmental capitals on a map — alongside time/clock questions.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado3_01): Grade 3's first cartilla is the orientation cartilla — cardinal points and maps as guides, a different map for each kind of information, then Colombia's political map with its departments and capitals explored department by department.
