Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 5 SD Geography 5): The first-ever Geography book in the post-2019 curriculum starts from the child's own classroom and school: directions are learned by standing facing the sunrise, map elements through a pupil's-house map, and only then does the book step out to Sudan, Africa and the world — a local-to-global ladder.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies G3): the Grade 3 book widens in rings — Me, Me and You, My Safety, My Society, My Environment, My Homeland — and the Geography lives in the last three rings: road safety, Syrian-made products, ways of living, then a whole environment unit (sky lanterns, locating my position, discovering Syria, the weather bulletin, a water journey).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 English): geography starts from the child's own school and home — location in the Grama Niladhari → Divisional Secretariat → District → Province hierarchy, a plan of the school garden, home-made weather instruments, and care of the home surroundings — before widening to Sri Lanka's location in the world in the final chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 Tamil 2007): dialogue-driven lessons (teacher–pupil conversations) that open with Sri Lanka's location and a comparison with the world's other islands, then work through the home and its surroundings, caring for that environment, and finish with map-making and scale.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 3 geo-3af-m): The youngest geography book in the set is built on five axes worked through observation pictures (I observe / I discuss / I conclude): the map key, position, location, landmarks, and a "time landmark" axis. Geography scope placed below; the time-landmark axis is local HISTORY and is NOT placed: History of my village (printed p. 37), History of the founding of the mosque (p. 39), History of the founding of the mahadra (p. 41), History of the founding of the school (p. 43), History of the founding of the dispensary (p. 45).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T1): Grade 8 widens the region a second time — from the Arab homeland (Grade 7) to the whole Islamic world — and reverses the balance: two History-led units on the Islamic state's rise and civilization come first, then two full Geography units (the natural environment; its effect on economy and society), a Civics unit on law in Kuwait, and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait". Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/religion — Unit 1, a historical glimpse of the Islamic state and its civilization: the heavenly religions and the Arab world's old civilizations, printed pp. 14–15; the rise of the Islamic state and the conquests movement, pp. 16–18; the concept and foundations of Islamic civilization, pp. 19–22 (the culture definition is placed below); its characteristics — faith and work, universality, tolerance, moderation — pp. 23–24. Civics/History — Unit 2 Lesson 1, the political and administrative manifestations: the caliphate, the ministry, the governorship, the diwans and the police, pp. 30–32. History of science — Unit 2 Lesson 4, the social, cultural and scientific manifestations: society's classes by profession, the status of women, the people of the covenant in farming, industry and trade, the scientific centres (the two Holy Mosques, Al-Azhar, Baghdad's House of Wisdom) and the translation of Muslim scholars' works in Europe, pp. 40–43 (the al-Idrisi map part is placed below). Civics — Unit 5, law as the basis of stability and building society in Kuwait: the definition, rules and aims of law; law and morality; the characteristics of the legal rule, pp. 85–96. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": the founding of Al-Mubarakiya school in 1911, Kuwait's first regular school, pp. 100–101; the etiquette of replies, pp. 102–103; the fragrance of neighbourliness, pp. 104–105; "What do you say, folks?" — consultation (shura) under Sheikh Salem Al-Mubarak, pp. 106–107 (the customs part is placed below).

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 3 Social Studies): a primary Social Studies book of 23 lessons in 7 chapters that works outward from the child — me, my family, my house, my school, from home to school. The Geography parts placed below are the resources lessons of Ch. 4 The family's needs (Lessons 11–13, printed pp. 37–55), the houses lessons of Ch. 5 Our house (Lessons 15–16, printed pp. 57–62), the school-map worksheet of Ch. 6 (printed p. 80) and the address and directions lessons of Ch. 7 From home to school (Lessons 20–21, printed pp. 81–85). The other parts are named here, not placed: Personal and family life (Civics) — Ch. 1 I am growing up (Lessons 1–3, printed pp. 1–15), Ch. 2 The family (Lessons 4–7, printed pp. 16–29), Ch. 3 Cooperation in the family (Lessons 8–9, printed pp. 30–37, with Lesson 10 of Ch. 4, How the family's needs are met, printed p. 38); Ch. 5 Lesson 14 I love my house and Lesson 17 Let us protect the house (printed pp. 56, 63); Ch. 6 Our school (Lessons 18–19, printed pp. 71–79); Ch. 7 Lesson 22 The post (printed p. 86) and Lesson 23 Safety in alley and street (printed p. 88).

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 4 Social Studies): a primary Social Studies book of 22 lessons in 6 chapters, working outward from the neighbourhood to the whole country. The Geography parts placed below are Ch. 1 Our neighbourhood (Lessons 2 and 4, printed pp. 5–23), Ch. 2 My city, my village (Lessons 5–7, printed pp. 24–37), Ch. 3 The arising of city and village (Lessons 8–9, printed pp. 38–46) and Ch. 5 My beautiful country (Lessons 15–19, printed pp. 74–98). The other parts are named here, not placed: Ch. 1 Lesson 1 Our neighbour (printed p. 2) and Lesson 3 Buying and selling in the neighbourhood (printed p. 10, Economics); Ch. 3 Lesson 10 How historians study the past (printed p. 47, History method); Ch. 4 A trip to the ancient cities (Lessons 11–14, printed pp. 51–70, History — Hamadan, Persepolis in two lessons, Kermanshah); Ch. 6 We are Iranians (Lessons 20–22, printed pp. 99–110, Civics — our national symbols, the calendar, important days).

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 3 G3 Social Studies): a combined Social Education book that starts from the child's own family, mosque and school, then widens to the neighbourhood/village and finally to phenomena around the child (directions, the Earth, seasons, time) — Geography enters last, through daily life.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 5 G5 Geography of Yemen): the first standalone Geography book is entirely about Yemen itself — map skills first, then Yemen's physical geography, then its population, then its economic activity sector by sector.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.
