# Parts of a research project

## Do it first

Every project begins by naming its problem well. A badly named problem wastes everything built on it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Problem, statement, objectives

The working order: identification and formulation of the research problem, through discussion with colleagues and experts and reading conceptual and empirical literature. The problem statement is sourced from professional experience, inference from theory, the professional literature, and technical or social change. Objectives follow: a general objective split into specific objectives that focus the study, prevent unnecessary data collection, and organise the work into phases.

## Sub lesson 2 — Questions, review, conclusion

Research questions are drawn from the problem statement, stated as questions, and point the study in a direction. Beyond them the project runs onward to literature review, and finally to conclusion and recommendation — the parts where the work answers its own questions and says what should be done. Walk your own locality problem through the five elements — problem, statement, objectives, questions, review — and you hold a project's skeleton.

## Say it to your companion

Explain parts of a research project to your companion in your own words: what it is, the one fact you would never want to forget from it, and one example from your own surroundings or from Ethiopia. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
