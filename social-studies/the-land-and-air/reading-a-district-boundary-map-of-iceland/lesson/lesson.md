# Reading a District Boundary Map of Iceland

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: A list tells you a country's districts; a boundary map shows you them — where each district ends and the next begins. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The district boundary map

The third page of Iceland's Umdæmisskipting work card is a map: Umdæmismörk sýslumanna — the boundaries of the sheriffs' districts, drawn over the outline of Iceland. To read it, follow a boundary line: everything inside one set of lines is one district, and the line itself is where that district ends.

## Sub lesson 2 — The sheriffs' seats on the map

The same map also marks the seats of the sheriffs — the places from which each sýslumaður works. So the map answers two questions at once: where does each district run, and where does its sheriff sit? Map work like this is how the work card turns the district list into ground the pupil can see.
