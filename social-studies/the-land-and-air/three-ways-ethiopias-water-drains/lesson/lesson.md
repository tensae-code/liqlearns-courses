# Three ways Ethiopia’s water drains

## Do it first

Stand on a highland in your mind in the rains. Water runs off you in every direction. There are only three ways it can end up. Read them.

## Sub lesson 1 — West and east

Ethiopia’s drainage falls into three systems. The western system carries water westward to the Nile and on to the Mediterranean. The eastern and south-eastern system carries water toward the Indian Ocean. Most of Ethiopia’s runoff volume travels by these two outward roads.

[Picture 21020 — a drainage map with three arrow colours leaving the highlands]

## Sub lesson 2 — The inward system

The third system drains inward: rivers that end inside the country, in the Rift lakes and closed basins, never reaching any sea. The Awash is its great example, ending in the Rift lakes of the Afar side. A highland country can thus be a water tower for its neighbours and still keep one system entirely its own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three drainage systems and where each one’s water ends.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name the three systems from memory.
2. Say which sea the western system finally reaches.
3. Name the inward system’s great example river.
