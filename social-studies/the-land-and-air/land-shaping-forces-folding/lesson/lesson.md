## Sub lesson 1 — When the crust is squeezed

When internal force squeezes the outer crust from the sides, the rock does not always break. Where it can, it bends and warps into folds — up-folds and down-folds, like a cloth pushed together on a table.

## Sub lesson 2 — Folding raises mountain ranges

Folding raises whole mountain ranges. The book names fold-built ranges on four continents: the Atlas and Cape ranges in Africa, the Alps in Europe, the Rockies in North America, the Himalayas in Asia and the Andes in South America.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading the fold figure

Look at the fold-types figure: the shapes were not carved — they were squeezed. Say, for each shape, what made it: sideways pressure from internal forces, bending the layers without breaking them.
