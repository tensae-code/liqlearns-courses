## Sub lesson 1 — Two families of forces

Geological processes are the natural forces shaping the planet's physical makeup. We study them to understand Earth's history, to locate resources such as metal ores, and to predict disasters before they strike.

They divide into two families. Endogenic forces come from inside the Earth — the heat and movement that fold, fault and erupt. Exogenic forces come from outside — sun, water, wind and ice working on the surface.

## Sub lesson 2 — Eruptions as the inside force at work

Eruption style shows the inside forces at work. Where ocean floor diverges, the lava is basaltic and fluid; where ocean plates collide, the erupted rock is basalt and andesite.

The warning example is 1883: the eruption of Krakatoa raised a tsunami that drowned over 36,000 people on Java and Sumatra. Inside forces build the land — and, in a day, can destroy on a scale no outside force matches.
