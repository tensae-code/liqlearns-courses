Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies SB): the P6 pupil's book is the fullest Geography book of the set — a whole unit on direction and location works Rwanda onto the East African and African maps by latitude and longitude and time zones, then physical features and climate, and a climate-change sequence (types, change, effects, overcoming) sits inside it; regional co-operation is taught bloc by bloc (COMESA, CEPGL, ECCAS, AU, NEPAD).

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Địa lí 10): a single Grade 10 course that runs the whole discipline — map craft, the Earth's spheres, then economic geography sector by sector — with Vietnam itself as the standing worked example (its power-plant and rice maps, the Red River's regime at Hanoi, Hanoi's climate chart, Vietnam's GDP structure, its internet and tourism figures) inside world-scale treatment.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 10 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 10 Geography is a population course — population pyramids in depth, world physical geography's effects on settlement, density and economic activity, then PNG-only migration (types, internal migration, routes and trends) and PNG's land-mobilisation questions (undeveloped land, Special Agricultural Business Leases, land grabbing). That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 11 Geography TG SBC) (teacher guide — evidence): the standards-based senior Geography course keeps the four-unit frame of the Grade 9–10 strand (Geography Skills; People and Places; People and Resources; People and Environment) as a standalone subject — a heavy map-skills unit (grid references, bearings, cross-sections, climate graphs) before climate regions, biomass, oceanography, and the structure of the Earth. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 6 Geography 6): world physical Geography taught through Pakistan's own land — its fault lines and 1935/2005 earthquakes, Khewra salt and Thar coal among the rocks, K2 and the Karakoram among the mountains, Pakistan's four climatic regions, its six forest types from mangrove to alpine, and the Indus flood as the country's defining disaster.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 6 LY G6 Social Studies): The geography half is "Principles of Physical Geography" in four units — surface shaping, climate, water, pollution — with Libyan examples inside world content (the Haruj volcanic mountains, Libyan springs such as Ain al-Sharshara). Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, History of Libya — the Ottoman State and Libya as an Ottoman province (printed pp. 12–18), Libya in the Ottoman era 1551–1912 including the Karamanli period (pp. 19–35), modern European colonialism and the Italian occupation of 1911 (pp. 36–43), Libyan resistance to the Italian occupation (pp. 44–54), Libya after the Italian era — liberation, the British/French military administrations, independence and the Libyan Kingdom (pp. 55–64).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 7 np-g7): Grade 7 Earth science is motion-first — Earth's daily and annual motion, surface form and the factors that change it, surface form's relation to human life — then Nepal's climate, the seven provinces of federal Nepal, climate change, disasters and map work; population is taught as structure, change and management.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 7 TG np-g7tg) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide teaches the same Grade 7 course as np-g7 with globe-and-ball demonstrations of rotation/revolution, chart work on Nepal's demographic structure (age, sex, religion, caste, language distributions), and discussion-led lessons; its geography content mirrors the student book lesson for lesson, so placements repeat with guide page references. That evidence is stocked here as the way Nepal teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Nepal is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 3): a systematic geomorphology course — rocks and Earth's interior first, then isostasy, continental drift and plate tectonics, then internal agents (folds, faults, earthquakes, volcanoes in four lessons), then external agents and the modelled landscapes of hot, temperate, cold and coastal environments, closing with the geological cycle.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 ES T1): the final year opens with fossil fuels and climate action, then structures and plate tectonics, closing with geological exploration — ending on uranium exploration in Jordan.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 Geography T1): the final-year Geography book is built in three blocks — the forces that shape Earth's surface, political geography (state power, borders, alliances, Jordan's national security), and population geography.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 CCSS Geografía 10): A distance-learning self-study module ("Geografía Dinámica") built entirely around Nicaragua itself — every physical topic (relief, climate, watersheds, coasts) is taught through Nicaraguan regions, and risk/vulnerability (hurricanes, Casita landslide, SINAPRED) closes the book. Non-Geography parts: Unit IV population-policy content (Hambre Cero, Usura Cero, housing projects) is Civics/social policy — named here, not placed beyond its population-geography topics.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 7 Geography): From Class VII Geography becomes a standalone subject organised in three strands (Time and Space; Physical Environment; People and Environment), every world topic closed with its Bhutan case — climate of Bhutan, vegetation types of Bhutan, minerals of Bhutan, and Bhutan's Himalayan disaster set (earthquake, flood, windstorm, fire, GLOF).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 11 Geography): The Advanced level (XI–XII) is full academic geography — map projections, remote sensing and GIS, insolation and pressure belts, climate classification — with Bhutan applied chapters on agriculture, manufacturing and tourism, the last taught on Bhutan's own sustainability principle ("high value, low volume" in the Bhutan Tourism Monitor data the book uses).
