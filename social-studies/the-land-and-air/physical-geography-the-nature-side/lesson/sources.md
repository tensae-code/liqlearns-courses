**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Dominican Republic, Slovenia, Uganda, Mauritania, Iran, Mozambique, Iraq, Cuba).**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Searchable tags: physical geography, geomorphology, climatology, biogeography, soil geography, oceanography, phytogeography, zoogeography, social studies

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 6): The Libro Abierto primary books open with two full geography units before any history: geography as a science first (its concepts, principles and helper sciences), then a continent-by-continent tour that always pairs physical aspects with population and culture — and the year's history closes with the country's own lived geography, Hurricane Georges over the Dominican Republic.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 8 geo-2as-m): A world-regional year: one unit of concepts (physical, human, economic geography), one unit walking the five inhabited continents by their natural and human features, and one unit of paired-country economic case studies (Egypt/South Africa, Brazil/Canada, India/Japan, Spain/Germany, Australia/New Zealand).

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 4 Social Studies): geography enters inside "Social Studies" through the child's own surroundings — phenomena are sorted from photographs first, then the local environment, then the map of Iraq's governorates, and only then the wider geographical environments (city, countryside); history and homeland/civics chapters follow in the same book.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Physical Geography): a pure physical-geography course in six chapters — landforms, weather and climate, water, the biosphere, soil, and the environment — with Iraqi anchors kept throughout (flood visits to Iraq's rivers, the hills of northern Iraq, groundwater and the desert caravans, soil salinity in the alluvial plain).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).
