# Evidence for continental drift

## Do it first

Take two neighbouring continents on a map — Africa and South America. List everything you can see that suggests they were once joined. Keep your list; check it against the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The fit and the matching rocks

The first evidence is the jigsaw fit of the coastlines: Africa fits against South America the way two torn pieces of paper fit. A fit that neat is hard to explain as chance.

The second evidence is what the rocks say. Rock types, structures and ages match across the ocean, and mountain belts continue across it — a belt that ends at one coast starts again at the matching coast, as if the ocean had been cut through one land.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fossils and past climates

The third evidence is fossils — above all Mesosaurus, an animal of about 250 million years ago, whose fossils are found on both sides of the Atlantic. A creature that lived in fresh water could not have crossed an ocean; the land it lived on must once have been one.

The fourth evidence is paleoclimatic: clues of past climates sit in the wrong places today — signs of ice where it is now warm, and of warmth where it is now cold. The climates did not move; the continents carrying the clues did.
