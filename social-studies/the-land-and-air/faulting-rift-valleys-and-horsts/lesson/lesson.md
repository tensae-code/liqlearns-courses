# Faulting — rift valleys and horsts

## Do it first

Snap a dry stick slowly and look at the break: the two sides no longer line up. In writing, say what 'displaced' could mean for a block of rock. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Faults and the sinking block

A fault is a crack in the rock where tension or compression displaces the rock — moves it up or down along the crack. Faulting is folding's sharper brother: where folding bends, faulting breaks.

Between two parallel faults, the middle block may sink. That sunken block is a rift valley, a graben — the East African Rift Valley is the great example, a whole strip of land dropped between faults.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rising block — horst

The opposite movement makes the opposite landform. A block pushed up between parallel faults stands above the land around it as a block mountain — a horst.

Ethiopia holds the named example: the Afar Horst. Graben and horst are a pair: the same faulting, read in two directions — one block down, one block up.
