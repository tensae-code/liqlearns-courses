**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Eritrea, Fiji, Venezuela, Mauritania, Pakistan, Libya, Moldova). Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Searchable tags: elements of weather, temperature, precipitation, humidity, pressure, wind, sunshine, cloud, social studies

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 4 Social Studies): Same four-strand frame as Year 3, now centred on the village/community — place and resources are taught through the child's own village, river, beachfront and village green ('rara').

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 4 geo-4af-m): A one-topic-per-spread observation book that moves from the child's own wilaya outward: the wilaya map first, then the landforms of home (watercourses, mountains, plains, plateaus, sand dunes), the weather elements and the two measuring instruments (thermometer, rain gauge), then the lived place — public places, archaeological sites, street names, my village, the capital of my wilaya — closing with integration exercises on the relief and climate of the wilaya capital.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 5 geo-5af-m): The whole book is Mauritania itself, in four "I get to know" axes: my country's location and borders, my country's climate, my society, my country's wealth. Climate is taught through the country's own three wind types and the effect of rain on where people, animals and plants live; farming includes the flood-recession farming of the Chamama plain after the Senegal River falls.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 6 LY G6 Social Studies): The geography half is "Principles of Physical Geography" in four units — surface shaping, climate, water, pollution — with Libyan examples inside world content (the Haruj volcanic mountains, Libyan springs such as Ain al-Sharshara). Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, History of Libya — the Ottoman State and Libya as an Ottoman province (printed pp. 12–18), Libya in the Ottoman era 1551–1912 including the Karamanli period (pp. 19–35), modern European colonialism and the Italian occupation of 1911 (pp. 36–43), Libyan resistance to the Italian occupation (pp. 44–54), Libya after the Italian era — liberation, the British/French military administrations, independence and the Libyan Kingdom (pp. 55–64).

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 10 LY G10 Principles): Secondary geography opens as a two-part systematic course — physical geography first (astronomy, surface-shaping factors, climate, vegetation and wild animals), then human geography (population and settlement, economic resources) — and closes with a whole book on spatial-data technology: remote sensing, GPS and GIS, then the history of maps from Babylon and Ibn Hawqal.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 8 G8 Geography): the home-country year — a full physical geography of the Republic of Moldova only (position and extreme points, structural units and minerals, relief units, climate and seasons, the Nistru/Prut river system, soils, steppe and forest-steppe zones), ending with its protected areas (national parks, scientific and landscape reserves, nature monuments) and Moldova in the context of sustainable development; hydrographic objects and protected areas are described "by algorithm" in set practicals.
