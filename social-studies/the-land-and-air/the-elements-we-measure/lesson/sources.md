**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Eritrea, Fiji, Venezuela, Mauritania, Pakistan, Libya, Moldova, Mozambique, Jordan, Cuba, South Sudan, Kazakhstan). Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 4 Social Studies): Same four-strand frame as Year 3, now centred on the village/community — place and resources are taught through the child's own village, river, beachfront and village green ('rara').

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 4 geo-4af-m): A one-topic-per-spread observation book that moves from the child's own wilaya outward: the wilaya map first, then the landforms of home (watercourses, mountains, plains, plateaus, sand dunes), the weather elements and the two measuring instruments (thermometer, rain gauge), then the lived place — public places, archaeological sites, street names, my village, the capital of my wilaya — closing with integration exercises on the relief and climate of the wilaya capital.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 5 geo-5af-m): The whole book is Mauritania itself, in four "I get to know" axes: my country's location and borders, my country's climate, my society, my country's wealth. Climate is taught through the country's own three wind types and the effect of rain on where people, animals and plants live; farming includes the flood-recession farming of the Chamama plain after the Senegal River falls.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 6 LY G6 Social Studies): The geography half is "Principles of Physical Geography" in four units — surface shaping, climate, water, pollution — with Libyan examples inside world content (the Haruj volcanic mountains, Libyan springs such as Ain al-Sharshara). Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, History of Libya — the Ottoman State and Libya as an Ottoman province (printed pp. 12–18), Libya in the Ottoman era 1551–1912 including the Karamanli period (pp. 19–35), modern European colonialism and the Italian occupation of 1911 (pp. 36–43), Libyan resistance to the Italian occupation (pp. 44–54), Libya after the Italian era — liberation, the British/French military administrations, independence and the Libyan Kingdom (pp. 55–64).

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 10 LY G10 Principles): Secondary geography opens as a two-part systematic course — physical geography first (astronomy, surface-shaping factors, climate, vegetation and wild animals), then human geography (population and settlement, economic resources) — and closes with a whole book on spatial-data technology: remote sensing, GPS and GIS, then the history of maps from Babylon and Ibn Hawqal.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 8 G8 Geography): the home-country year — a full physical geography of the Republic of Moldova only (position and extreme points, structural units and minerals, relief units, climate and seasons, the Nistru/Prut river system, soils, steppe and forest-steppe zones), ending with its protected areas (national parks, scientific and landscape reserves, nature monuments) and Moldova in the context of sustainable development; hydrographic objects and protected areas are described "by algorithm" in set practicals.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G8 SS T1): Grade 8 Term 1 pairs an environment unit (climate elements, the world's natural environments, hazards and disaster management) with population and development, framed by human rights and Mesopotamian history.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G6 SS T2): Term 2's geography is climate done properly — elements, effects on human activity, Jordan's own climate regions — plus economic concepts and three more governorate journeys (Mafraq, Balqa, Aqaba).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 ES T2): the last term spans galaxies and the origin of the universe, severe weather and how to stay safe in it, and the history of the Earth — with geological tourism in Jordan as the closing enrichment.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 Geography T2): the final Geography term is climate (atmosphere, elements, classifications, change), environment (components, problems, protection, Jordanian ecotourism), and economic geography (production, agriculture, industry, services, development).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 6 Cuaderno de actividades G6): The workbook is the practice twin of the G6 student book and the Atlas Escolar — nearly every activity is done ON a map (tracing coordinates, shading the insular shelf, locating features on outline maps) — and it is where the programme's fifth unit, "El paisaje de la localidad", actually lives: the student book has no locality chapter, but the workbook closes with sketching, describing and defending the child's own locality. Section 2 is pure tools: outline maps of the world and of Cuba plus graph paper.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 9 Geografía de Cuba G9 (2025)): The 2025 rewrite keeps the home-country arc but rebuilds it for the Tercer Perfeccionamiento: Chapter 2 now profiles every province inside the political-administrative division itself, the climate chapter adds the Tarea Vida state plan by name, the economy chapter is organised by primary/secondary/tertiary sectors with self-employment (trabajo por cuenta propia) and the Mariel development zone, and the social chapter faces an ageing, shrinking population (life expectancy 73.7 years, a "Vivir 120" programme) and closes with Cuba's Human Development Index. It is a different book from the 2012 edition (Book 15), not a parallel copy, and is placed separately.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 4 P4 Social Studies SB): Weather is learned by building the instruments — thermometer, rain gauge, wind vane and windsock made from local materials, then a school weather station — and map reading is taught on one worked map ("Upendo Area") with scale, symbol key and area measured by counting grid squares.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 4 P4 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide grounds every weather idea in farm and river work — wind for threshing and winnowing, rough water making fishing risky — and teaches that forests help in the formation of rain. That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — South Sudan is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Egorina 2017): the 2017 first edition of the Atamura course (the 2025 Book 2 is its revision): same six-section architecture but twelve numbered practical works, four of them database-building exercises on Kazakhstan itself — a geographic database for Kazakhstan's regions (§51) and for Kazakhstan's transport system (§52) — plus a practical on the natural complex of the pupil's own locality (§37).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Tolybekova): the Mektep course teaches geographic nomenclature continent by continent (§§6–8: Africa/Australia/Antarctica, the Americas, Eurasia) with a practical on completing an outline map, then works through the natural complexes belt by belt (§§39–47: arctic, temperate forest/steppe, desert, tropical, subequatorial, equatorial); Kazakhstan anchors the applied ends — its regions' flora and fauna (§33), its soil types and soil resources (§§34–35), its population (§52, Practical №3) and its own economic-geographic position (§62).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Tolybekova 2017): the 2017 first edition of the Mektep course (Book 5 is its 2025 revision): the same continent-by-continent nomenclature and belt-by-belt natural-complexes architecture, with a lithosphere field-research practical, a §52 «Человек — часть биосферы» (The human being as part of the biosphere), and a closing §60 «Этот многоликий мир» (This many-faced world) survey of the world's diversity.
