**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, Bangladesh, Samoa, Malaysia, Uganda, Syria, Mauritania, Iran, Nepal, Mozambique, Iraq, Armenia, Kazakhstan). Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Pairing honesty: the 2023 student book's own teacher guide is still unsecured; the secured Grade 12 teacher guide is the 2010–11 edition for a different student book (see the action-research lesson's sources). No teacher evidence is claimed for this lesson.

Bangladesh's own way (Grades 9-10): the shared SSC book's big chapter "Living Together Safely" is a genuine geography course inside the combined book — land-use change read off 1988-vs-2017 Khulna maps, world population and density, remote sensing and GIS with its five components, settlement and urbanisation, desertification, and Bangladesh's own squeeze of limited land, dense population and climate risk.

Samoa's own way (Year 11, Book 1): The marine environment is the Geography core — Samoa's own reefs and fisheries, taught through perception, traditional and national management law (with SPREP input), and a student investigation.

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Geography): A full senior Geography course built on the Pacific itself — high and low island formation, island climates and ecosystems, then population of New Zealand, Asia and Samoa, and Samoan farming (bananas) beside Australian uranium mining.

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

Malaysia's own way (MY T2 Geografi Tingkatan 2): the year pairs map skills (scale, topographic maps) with weather and climate, then transport and telecommunications as the human-geography core, an Asia regional study, and global warming with green technology as the environmental close.

Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

Malaysia's own way (MY T3 Geografi Tingkatan 3): Form 3 turns from skills (tables, graphs, pie charts) to living Malaysia — its natural vegetation and wildlife and the human pressure on both — then outward to the world's biomes, resources and economic cooperation, closing with forest management and recycling.

Malaysia's own way (MY T5 Geografi Tingkatan 5): the final form is the world's systems book — advanced graphs and photo interpretation, then weather and climate at world scale, vegetation and wildlife by climate zone, energy, and the environmental bill for economic activity.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 11 Geography TG SBC) (teacher guide — evidence): the standards-based senior Geography course keeps the four-unit frame of the Grade 9–10 strand (Geography Skills; People and Places; People and Resources; People and Environment) as a standalone subject — a heavy map-skills unit (grid references, bearings, cross-sections, climate graphs) before climate regions, biomass, oceanography, and the structure of the Earth. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grades 11–12 Geography TG OBE) (teacher guide — evidence): an outcomes-based guide — it prescribes no lesson scripts; for each syllabus unit it lists suggested learning activities and assessment tasks built around the local environment (soil profiles, local hazard sites, a PNG mine and logging case study, a transect along latitude 6°S), with fieldwork and geographical inquiry as the core skills. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide mirrors the learner book chapter for chapter, adding teacher instructions, possible responses and assessment for every activity — the same East-Africa-first, activity-led pedagogy, plus teacher-only content on plotting weather on maps and making simple weather instruments. That evidence is stocked here as the way Uganda teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Uganda is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Home-Study): A home-based compression of the Senior One course: fieldwork is done around the learner's own home, photographs and statistics (Kilembe Mines data) replace classroom map work, and the booklet ends on East Africa's location, size and relief.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 11 geo-5as-lm): The senior Letters cycle re-runs physical geography at full depth in four units — Planet Earth, Climate, the Effects of Climate, the Earth (structure, erosion, coasts) — with a class project closing Units 1 and 4.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 13 geo-7as-lm): The final-year Letters book is an exam-facing synthesis: Mauritania's natural and human features and its economic activities in full (including the structural problems of the Mauritanian economy and a lesson on how to write a geography essay), then the Arab world and Africa as economic powers (oil and minerals as the lenses), closing with the two great powers — the American and Chinese economies, their foundations, features and problems.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 12 np-g12): Grade 12 geography is federal and applied — each of Nepal's provinces studied four ways (administration, population, social-cultural life, economy) plus inter-provincial relations; disasters in Nepal with the national disaster-management system and plan-making; GIS/geo-information in social mapping; and a full urbanisation-and-migration unit (village–city relations, safe and dignified migration, migration effects).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 1): the second cycle opens with the discipline itself — geographic thought, its history from Greek antiquity to the determinist/possibilist schools, then cosmography (celestial sphere, solar system, Earth's shape and revolution) before any regional content.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 10 Geography): the literary track opens geography as a discipline in its own right — its thought from the Sumerians to GIS, how a geographer researches, where geographical data come from and how they are drawn, and only then the fields of the science; Iraq supplies the worked examples throughout (Baghdad research problem, Iraqi topographic maps, Iraq's gas time series, the Baghdad–Basra distance exercise).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 8 Geography student): a pure regional-geography year — the world is divided into regions following the UN scheme, each region and chosen state is studied in the same fixed order (position, natural conditions and resources, population, economy), and Armenia's neighbours (Georgia, Azerbaijan, Turkey, Iran) get full country lessons of their own.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 9 Geography of Armenia student): the whole grade-9 year is the home country — first the Armenian Highlands as a physical whole, then the Republic section by section (nature, population, economy), finishing marz by marz (all 10 provinces) so every learner ends by studying their own province.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Geography student): grade-10 Geography is a methods-first course — it opens with the science's history, branches and GIS/GPS/aerospace methods, works through the lithosphere with a full natural-disasters lesson, and closes with resources measured by endowment; Armenia's own rivers fill the hydrology tables and Armenia's place in modern geopolitics closes the political-map theme.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Amangalieva NIS): the NIS (Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools) inquiry textbook: every lesson opens with a «Бұл сабақта» (In this lesson) goal box and closes with a «Сенің кезегің!» (Your turn!) task block; each section ends in a «Қорытынды» summary built round a concept map; the book closes with a trilingual Kazakh–Russian–English glossary. Country-studies section works through how a state improves its geographic position, with the UAE and Singapore as cases and Kazakhstan as the home example.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Egorina): the 2025 Atamura course is organised by numbered paragraphs (§1–§68) with coded learning goals (e.g. 7.3.2.4) and a «Негізгі ұғымдар» key-concepts box per paragraph; it gives Kazakhstani science its own paragraph (§3, research in the war and post-war years), a whole practical strand on building geographic databases (§§10, 18, 49, 54–55), and a country-studies block that classifies states by position — continental, archipelago, island and peninsular — with Kazakhstan's own economic-geographic position as the worked example.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Egorina 2017): the 2017 first edition of the Atamura course (the 2025 Book 2 is its revision): same six-section architecture but twelve numbered practical works, four of them database-building exercises on Kazakhstan itself — a geographic database for Kazakhstan's regions (§51) and for Kazakhstan's transport system (§52) — plus a practical on the natural complex of the pupil's own locality (§37).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Karatabanov): every paragraph is titled as a question («Как устроена Земля внутри?», «Все ли одинаково обеспечены водой?») and every section closes with a «Мои географические достижения» (My geographic achievements) self-check page; Part 2 asks what is known about "our country's soils" (§§37–38) and how to develop natural resources "without harm to nature and people" (§§55–57).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Tolybekova): the Mektep course teaches geographic nomenclature continent by continent (§§6–8: Africa/Australia/Antarctica, the Americas, Eurasia) with a practical on completing an outline map, then works through the natural complexes belt by belt (§§39–47: arctic, temperate forest/steppe, desert, tropical, subequatorial, equatorial); Kazakhstan anchors the applied ends — its regions' flora and fauna (§33), its soil types and soil resources (§§34–35), its population (§52, Practical №3) and its own economic-geographic position (§62).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Tolybekova 2017): the 2017 first edition of the Mektep course (Book 5 is its 2025 revision): the same continent-by-continent nomenclature and belt-by-belt natural-complexes architecture, with a lithosphere field-research practical, a §52 «Человек — часть биосферы» (The human being as part of the biosphere), and a closing §60 «Этот многоликий мир» (This many-faced world) survey of the world's diversity.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 8 Abilmazhinova): the Grade 8 Mektep course deepens every Grade 7 line — relief classification and rock ages, global circulation and the climatic belts, natural zones of both hemispheres, demographic calculation and policy design (Practical №7 has pupils propose their own demographic-policy model), resource assessment, industry location factors, and the political map — closing with Kazakhstan's own place in political-integration processes (§54).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 8 Karatabanov): the Almatykitap Grade 8 course runs the same six-section architecture as the Mektep book (Book 7) but in Karatabanov's question-led style, closing Part 2 with §68 «Мой географический год» (My geographic year) — a pupil's own review of a year of geographic work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 9 Tolybekova): the whole Grade 9 year is Kazakhstan itself — Part 1 walks the country's own relief, climate, rivers, lakes, natural zones and protected areas (opening with the research of Kazakhstani geographers, Shokan Ualikhanov first), and Part 2 its population, industries, economic regions and place in the world economy, ending with the geographic position of the Republic of Kazakhstan (§64).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 9 Usikov): the Atamura Grade 9 course pairs every Kazakhstan topic with its research base — sections close with "исследования, географические карты и базы данных" (research, geographic maps and databases) for the lithosphere (§15), climate (§24), inland waters (§31), social geography (§47), nature use (§51) and Kazakhstan in the world economy (§64); it also names the country's own naming heritage — Kazakh oronyms (§10), hydronyms (§27), climate in Kazakh folk terminology (§23) and choronyms (§67).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 9 Karatabanov): the one-volume Almatykitap Grade 9 course compresses the same Kazakhstan year into paired paragraphs and adds a naming thread of its own — Kazakh oronyms (§12), Kazakh hydronyms (§§26–27) and the nomination of atmospheric and climatic phenomena (§22) — closing with a complex country-study characterisation of Kazakhstan (§67) and «Мой географический год» (§68).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 9 Sakhova NIS): the NIS trial Grade 9 course is organised by problems rather than by the standard sections — forecasting lithospheric cataclysms and safety measures (3.1.2–3.1.3), the causes of water scarcity (3.3.4), the noosphere and its development in Kazakhstan (3.4.1–3.4.2), and a research lesson on the very concept of sustainable development (5.1.4).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 8 Sakhova NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course phrases its lessons as questions and tasks — «Әр жерде неліктен уақыт әркелкі?» (Why is time different in different places?, 2.2), «Тау жыныстары қалай пайда болады?» (How do rocks form?, 3.1.5), «Өзеннің суы қалай өзгереді?» (How does a river's water change?, 3.3.4) — and adds a lesson on the culture of showing geographic names on the map (2.3).
