# Video script — Natural Regions of Ecuador — Costa, Sierra, Oriente, Galápagos

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Ecuador is four countries in one. The Costa is the low Pacific coast; the Sierra is the high Andean spine, stacked in climatic floors (pisos climáticos) that change with altitude; the Oriente is the Amazonian east of rainforest and great rivers; and Galápagos is the island region far out in the Pacific. The books teach the four regiones naturales at three depths — named with their characteristics in Grade 2, set out as a topic in Grade 3, and re-read with their climatic floors in Grade 5 — because almost everything else in Ecuadorian geography (weather, farming, products, wildlife) follows from which region a place sits in.

Ecuador learns this inside its own books' way: (Grade 2 book) Geography is taught inside three thematic sections that always start from the child's family, school and province and widen to Ecuador and South America; heavy use of hazard maps (floods, landslides, volcanoes) and emergency plans from Grade 2, reflecting Ecuador's volcanic / El Niño risk profile. (Grade 3 book) the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation. (Grade 5 book) the 2024 Grade 5 book is History-led (five of eight sections are national history, from the roots of society to the Republic); Geography is concentrated at the end, where the country is re-read spatially — Andean relief and rivers, natural regions and climatic floors, population diversity and migration, territorial division, provincial products, and the protected-area system taught together with climate-change action. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
