Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Samoa's own way (Year 9, Book 2): Place is taught through Samoan lived places — ordinary, sacred (tapu), natural and political places such as Mulinu'u — and migration is taught through Samoa's own movement story and its New Zealand diaspora, closing with a full mapping-skills unit.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado2_01): Escuela Nueva self-paced guides (parts A–D: explore, deepen, practise, apply) set in rural Colombia — veredas, resguardos, coffee regions; geography starts from the walk home-to-school, the classroom plan and the home municipality (Aracataca/Ciénaga worked example).

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

India's own way (Grade 6 The Earth Our Habitat): The first Geography year goes planet → globe → motions → maps → Earth's domains → landforms, and only then turns home: the last two chapters are India itself (location, neighbours, physical divisions, monsoon climate, vegetation and wildlife), so world basics are immediately applied to the home country.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): from P5 the medium is English and the organising frame is the child's own PROVINCE — its location, services, environment, climate, population and infrastructure — before the frame widens to the whole country at P6. Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 5 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the five strands widen again — from district (Grade 4) to the physical environment of the province and nation; resources are taught through sustainable use (re-using water, reduce/reuse projects) and map work culminates in drawing sketch maps of the school and locality. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grades 11–12 Geography TG OBE) (teacher guide — evidence): an outcomes-based guide — it prescribes no lesson scripts; for each syllabus unit it lists suggested learning activities and assessment tasks built around the local environment (soil profiles, local hazard sites, a PNG mine and logging case study, a transect along latitude 6°S), with fieldwork and geographical inquiry as the core skills. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide mirrors the learner book chapter for chapter, adding teacher instructions, possible responses and assessment for every activity — the same East-Africa-first, activity-led pedagogy, plus teacher-only content on plotting weather on maps and making simple weather instruments. That evidence is stocked here as the way Uganda teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Uganda is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 10 UG S3 Geography Home-Study): COVID-era self-study booklet: first half is East Africa's human geography (towns/ports, transport, trade) plus heavy practical map-work and photograph interpretation on Ugandan survey-map extracts (Tororo, Kakuto, Samia); second half is a "studies in development" comparison — the Rhinelands and landlocked Switzerland held up as models against East Africa.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 5 SD Geography 5): The first-ever Geography book in the post-2019 curriculum starts from the child's own classroom and school: directions are learned by standing facing the sunrise, map elements through a pupil's-house map, and only then does the book step out to Sudan, Africa and the world — a local-to-global ladder.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 5 G5 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): the Geography block is a journey ("Viajemos por Venezuela") through work and wealth — farmland and its natural supports, climate, biodiversity, relief, oil basins and minerals — always tied to how the oil economy rearranged the country's population and space.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 English): geography starts from the child's own school and home — location in the Grama Niladhari → Divisional Secretariat → District → Province hierarchy, a plan of the school garden, home-made weather instruments, and care of the home surroundings — before widening to Sri Lanka's location in the world in the final chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 Tamil 2007): dialogue-driven lessons (teacher–pupil conversations) that open with Sri Lanka's location and a comparison with the world's other islands, then work through the home and its surroundings, caring for that environment, and finish with map-making and scale.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 3 geo-3af-m): The youngest geography book in the set is built on five axes worked through observation pictures (I observe / I discuss / I conclude): the map key, position, location, landmarks, and a "time landmark" axis. Geography scope placed below; the time-landmark axis is local HISTORY and is NOT placed: History of my village (printed p. 37), History of the founding of the mosque (p. 39), History of the founding of the mahadra (p. 41), History of the founding of the school (p. 43), History of the founding of the dispensary (p. 45).

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 10 G10 Geography of Man and the Environment): secondary geography is thematic, not regional — man and space, man representing his geographic field (remote sensing, projections, maps), man organising it (rural/urban), climate and water, the dynamic Earth, and the great environmental systems.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 SS5): the Class 5 Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 51–85) deepens the Class 4 spiral — same BOLTS, landforms, weather and population topics, but now with scale types, latitude/longitude and time-zone arithmetic, worked population-density calculations, and graph-drawing exercises from real Pakistani city data.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 5 LY G5 Social Studies): Half the book is Islamic history; the geography half ("Principles of General Geography") is pure foundations — solar system, Earth's shape/movements, grid, compass/GPS, land-and-water, map scale and symbols — with the symbol lesson taught by reading Libya's own mineral, wind and road distribution maps. Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, Islamic History — the Arabs before Islam (printed pp. 11–12), the Emergence of Islam (pp. 13–20), the Rashidun Caliphs (pp. 21–30), the Umayyad State (pp. 31–35), the Abbasid State (pp. 36–40), the Fatimid State (pp. 41–43), the Ayyubid State (pp. 44–45), the Crusades on the Arab Homeland (pp. 46–47), the Mamluks repelling the Mongols and Crusaders (pp. 48–51).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 1 np-g1): Geography begins as "Our Earth" at home scale — the land around the home and school, then a map of home, school and classroom; picture-led lessons ("look and talk about the pictures") with almost no text at Grade 1.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 2 np-g2): "Our Earth" grows from living place to village/town and the village in the map; economic geography is comparative — things made in the village vs things made in the city.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 3 np-g3): Grade 3 introduces Nepal's three ecological regions — Himalayan (cold, Gaurishankar), Hilly (Kathmandu valley) and Terai (flat, hot, Dhangadhi) — through three children's home places, with rivers (Tamakoshi, Narayani), landforms and climate tied to each child's village/town.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 np-g4b): The newer (2078 BS) trial book reorganises the same ground by units ending in "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth): Earth as our common home, our needs from the Earth, our local level, and drawing a map / making a map of our village; economic life is taught through production, income-generating work and the haat bazaar (periodic market).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 5 np-g5): Grade 5 "Our Earth" is a first full geography of Nepal — physical features, climate and lifestyle, natural vegetation, soils, map-drawing with symbols, globe and maps, and the wider South Asian neighbours; economic geography is district-based (Sankhuwasabha's occupations mapped place by place, from yak and sheep rearing and herb collecting in the high Himalaya to paddy in the low valleys).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 5 TG np-g5tg) (teacher guide — evidence): Guide to the new-curriculum Grade 5 book (companion to the trial-edition line, not to the older English np-g5): its "Hamro Prithvi" unit teaches the solar system and Earth's structure, then Nepal's natural form, climate, vegetation, population and social life, local disasters, and map-making — learners draw their own province's map showing their district and local level and study the Nepal map. Economic unit works from local economic activities and locally produced goods (and the markets that sell them) up to the country's major economic activities across Himalayan, Hilly and Terai regions. That evidence is stocked here as the way Nepal teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Nepal is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 8 TG np-g8tg) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 8 economics is monetary — monetary transactions, Nepal's financial institutions, tourism, foreign trade and foreign employment; the Earth unit pairs Earth's motions with weather/climate elements, climate change, Nepal's bordering neighbours and SAARC, disaster management (with the 2072 earthquake as case material) and a map-making practical (draw Nepal, fill in geographical facts). Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 4 Social Studies): geography enters inside "Social Studies" through the child's own surroundings — phenomena are sorted from photographs first, then the local environment, then the map of Iraq's governorates, and only then the wider geographical environments (city, countryside); history and homeland/civics chapters follow in the same book.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 6 Cuaderno de actividades G6): The workbook is the practice twin of the G6 student book and the Atlas Escolar — nearly every activity is done ON a map (tracing coordinates, shading the insular shelf, locating features on outline maps) — and it is where the programme's fifth unit, "El paisaje de la localidad", actually lives: the student book has no locality chapter, but the workbook closes with sketching, describing and defending the child's own locality.
