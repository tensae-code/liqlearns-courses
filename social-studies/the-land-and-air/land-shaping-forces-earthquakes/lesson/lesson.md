## Sub lesson 1 — Strain, slip, shaking

Think first of what you felt when a heavy vehicle passed close by — that tremor is a small taste of an earthquake. An earthquake begins where two plates strain against each other: friction holds them, pressure builds, and a sudden slip releases it all as shaking.

[Picture 11464 — a cross-section with focus below and epicentre marked on the surface above it]

## Sub lesson 2 — Focus, epicentre, Richter

The starting point underground is the focus. The point directly above it on the surface — where shaking is strongest — is the epicentre. Shock waves spread out in all directions, and the strength of an earthquake is measured on the Richter scale.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where most earthquakes sit (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide adds the location frame: most earthquakes occur along the rim of the Pacific Ocean, called the Ring of Fire (teacher guide — evidence). The plates strain hardest where they meet — and the Pacific rim is ringed by such meetings.
