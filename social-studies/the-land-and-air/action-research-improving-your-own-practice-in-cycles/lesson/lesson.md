# Action research — improving your own practice in cycles

## Do it first

A teacher notices her class cannot read contour lines. She does not wait for a university to study it — she studies it herself, while teaching. (Teacher guide — evidence, 2010–11 edition.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Research on your own practice

Action research is practitioner-led research aimed at improving one's own action and practice, defined against scientific/academic research in the guide's comparison table. Where academic research seeks general knowledge, action research seeks a better next lesson, a better next term — knowledge with a user who is also its maker.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cycle — plan, act, observe, reflect

Action research is characteristically cyclical: plan, act, observe, reflect — then re-plan and turn the wheel again. It is staged step by step so teachers and learners can run it on selected problems in their own school and locality, and the old course's outcomes included learners acquiring basic research skills expressly to conduct it. A problem small enough to hold, a cycle honest enough to repeat: that is action research.
