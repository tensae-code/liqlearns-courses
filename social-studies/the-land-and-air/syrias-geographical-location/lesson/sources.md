**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Syria).**

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 9 Geography G9): every unit is taught twice at two scales — the Arab homeland first, then Syria — with parallel lesson pairs (relief, climate, water, soil, agriculture, industry, transport, tourism); Unit 3's framing is pointed: "huge human resources… and weak investment", and the subsoil lessons are titled "hidden treasures".

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 4 Social Studies G4): the whole primary book is one-country geography of Syria — nature, then life (Badia, countryside, city), then work (agriculture, industry, trade, tourism) — with map tools (map symbols, four directions) and a tree-cutting/environment strand built into the opening unit's goals.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies G3): the Grade 3 book widens in rings — Me, Me and You, My Safety, My Society, My Environment, My Homeland — and the Geography lives in the last three rings: road safety, Syrian-made products, ways of living, then a whole environment unit (sky lanterns, locating my position, discovering Syria, the weather bulletin, a water journey).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 1 Social Studies G1): the Grade 1 book starts the same widening-rings series (Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland); Geography enters through street-crossing safety, then the environment unit — nature and the farm, the four friends (the seasons), beautiful nature, water "more precious than gold", a precious treasure, and a journey into space — and closes with the homeland's antiquities and pictures from across Syria.
