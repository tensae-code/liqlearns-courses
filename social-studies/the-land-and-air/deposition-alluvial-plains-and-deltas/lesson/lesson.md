## Sub lesson 1 — Where the load is laid down

Deposition is the end of weathering and erosion: broken rock, sand and soil carried by water, wind, ice, sea and gravity are laid down somewhere new. Nothing carried travels forever — every load lands.

[Picture 11470 — a river slowing at its mouth and fanning into a delta]

## Sub lesson 2 — Mass movement and alluvial plains

Gravity can move masses suddenly — that is mass movement, a slope's load falling at once. More gently, new soil builds alluvial plains: at river mouths, along sea coasts, and at the feet of hills and mountains.

[GIF 11471 — a river dropping its load as it slows]

## Sub lesson 3 — Deltas of the great rivers

Great rivers build wide deltas where they meet the sea — the book names the Nile, the Mississippi and the Amazon. Some of a river's load travels far out to sea before it settles. The teacher guide names the dropped loads piece by piece: dunes, barchans, loess and screes are deposition's own vocabulary (teacher guide — evidence).
