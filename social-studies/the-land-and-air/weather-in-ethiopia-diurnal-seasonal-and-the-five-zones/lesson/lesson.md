# Weather in Ethiopia — Diurnal and Seasonal Variation and the Five Agro-Climatic Zones

## Sub lesson 1 — Bega days and nights, Kiremt rains (teacher guide — evidence)

Because Bega (winter) skies are clear, days grow hotter and nights colder, giving Ethiopia a high diurnal — daily — temperature range. Regular solar radiation through the year keeps the annual range low. In Kiremt (summer) most highlands receive rain, and the western part of the country receives rain all year (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — The five agro-climatic zones (teacher guide — evidence)

An agro-climatic zone is land described by its major climate and growing period, suited to a certain range of crops. Ethiopia has five traditional zones: Bereha, Kola, Woina Dega, Dega and Wurch — from hottest to coldest (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 3 — Working like a weather station (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's pupils calculate the mean and the range of temperature, draw climatic graphs, sketch-map the five zones, and collect real data on a field visit to a nearby meteorological station, writing a field report (teacher guide — evidence). The worked script shows a daily range: highest 28, lowest 12 — range 16, mean 20.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bereha and Kolla — the hot lowlands (Grade 11 depth)

Grade 11 depth, from the student book itself: Bereha is desert lowland below 500 metres, over 30 degrees, pastoralist country — the Afar and Somali lowlands. Kolla runs 500 to 1,500 metres: warm semi-arid lowlands growing sorghum, sesame and groundnuts.

## Sub lesson 5 — Woina-Dega and Dega — the temperate and cold highlands (Grade 11 depth)

Grade 11 depth: Woina-Dega, 1,500 to 2,300 metres, is the temperate highland zone of teff, wheat and maize. Dega, 2,300 to 3,300 metres, is cold highland country: wheat, highland pulses and oil seeds.

## Sub lesson 6 — Wurch — and mapping your own kebele (Grade 11 depth)

Grade 11 depth: Wurch, above 3,300 metres, is afro-alpine country, where barley is the crop that still ripens. The student book's own task closes the zones: map the zones of your own kebele and woreda, zone by zone, from the ground you know.
