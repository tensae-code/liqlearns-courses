# Your own enquiry — a locality research project

## Do it first

Four years of geography — land, people, maps, issues — end in a single order: go and enquire yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The course funnels into one project

Unit 8 is designed as a doing-unit, not only a reading-unit. Its outcomes require you to undertake a small research project on your own locality with basic tools: choose a real problem where you live, state it, set objectives and questions, gather data, and present what you find. The whole Grade 12 course funnels into this one piece of independent geographical enquiry.

## Sub lesson 2 — GIS inside your project

Within the project you use GIS to locate your place and navigate routes — your locality on the machine's map, your field sites pinned, your journeys planned. Basic tools suffice: the project's worth is measured by the honesty of its problem, the care of its data and the clarity of its presentation, not by the price of its equipment. Begin where you stand: the geographer's first laboratory is home ground.

## Say it to your companion

Explain your own enquiry — a locality research project to your companion in your own words: what it is, the one fact you would never want to forget from it, and one example from your own surroundings or from Ethiopia. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
