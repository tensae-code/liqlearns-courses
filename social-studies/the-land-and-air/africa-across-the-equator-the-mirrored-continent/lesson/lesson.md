# Africa across the equator — the mirrored continent

## Do it first

Fold a map of Africa along its middle line — the equator. What lands on what? Which northern desert would land on a southern one? Guess the pairs before you read them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The equator divides Africa almost equally

Africa **straddles the equator**: the line crosses the continent close to its middle, so Africa extends about as far north of the equator as south of it. The continent holds **54 sovereign states** — but its physical pattern is simpler than its political map: one line, the equator, organises it.

Because the same climate machinery works in both hemispheres — wet at the equator, drier toward each tropic, mild at each far edge — the belts that form north of the line form again, in reverse order, south of it. Africa is a **mirrored continent**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The mirror pairs — Kalahari–Sahara, Karoo–Maghreb, Cape–Mediterranean

The book's own pairs teach the mirror. The southern **Kalahari** mirrors the northern **Sahara** — dry lands at similar distances from the equator on opposite sides. The **Karoo** matches the **Maghreb** — the dry interior country behind the southern and northern coastal rims. And the **Cape** at the southern tip matches the **Mediterranean north** — mild far edges of the continent, as far from the equator as Africa reaches.

Use the pairs as a study tool: learn a northern belt, and you have already half-learned its southern twin. Forget a southern feature, and its northern mirror will remind you.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the mirror to your companion: why it happens, then the three pairs. Your companion names a southern place — Kalahari, Karoo, Cape — and you answer with its northern mirror and why they match.
