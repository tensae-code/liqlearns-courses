# Lesson plan — Africa across the equator — the mirrored continent

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Land and Air · **Lesson:** Africa across the equator — the mirrored continent
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Landforms of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Because the equator divides Africa almost equally, its physical and climatic belts repeat in mirror image north and south — Kalahari mirroring Sahara, Karoo matching the Maghreb, the Cape matching the Mediterranean north — across a continent of 54 sovereign states.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the belt lessons — savanna, Sahel, Sahara, rainforest — whose order this lesson explains.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The equator divides Africa almost equally** — The straddle that explains the mirror.
2. **The mirror pairs — Kalahari–Sahara, Karoo–Maghreb, Cape–Mediterranean** — Reading the continent by pairs.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Sahara; The Southern Africa Platform. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain Africa's equatorial straddle and its 54 sovereign states.
2. Explain why belts repeat mirrored north and south.
3. Give the three mirror pairs: Kalahari–Sahara, Karoo–Maghreb, Cape–Mediterranean.

## If the learner is stuck
Fold the map in your mind along the equator. Whatever a northern place lands on is its mirror. The three pairs in the lesson are the fold's three clearest landings.
