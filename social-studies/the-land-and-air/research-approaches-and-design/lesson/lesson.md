# Research approaches and design

## Do it first

Some problems want counting; some want listening; most real problems want both. The approach is chosen before any tool is picked up.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three approaches — numbers, stories, both

The approach is the general way of treating the problem. Quantitative research uses designs and measures producing discrete, replicable numbers. Qualitative research uses methods without quantitative measurement — historical/narrative, ethnographic and action research among its types. Mixed research uses both, interrelated, under a flexible outlook. Method is narrower than approach: the techniques used — questionnaires, interviews and the rest.

## Sub lesson 2 — Methodology and design

Methodology is the philosophy and logic of how research is done scientifically, starting from how the problem is identified. The research design is the blueprint decided after the problem is defined: the arrangement for data collection, measurement and analysis running from hypothesis to final analysis. At minimum, a design must state the problem clearly — a blueprint that cannot say what it is for will guide nothing.
