# Anatomy of a drainage basin

## Do it first

Before you read, pour a cup of water slowly onto sloped ground and watch: tiny streams join into bigger ones and all the water leaves at one low point. That is a basin in miniature. Say what the joining points and the exit are called once you have read — first guess now.

## Sub lesson 1 — One basin, one outlet

A drainage basin is the whole area whose surface water converges — gathers — to a single outlet. Rain that falls anywhere inside the basin ends up leaving by that one exit, which is why a basin is managed as one piece of land.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tributaries and confluences

The small streams that feed the main river are its tributaries. Where two streams meet is a confluence. Tributary joins tributary, confluence follows confluence, and the river grows all the way to the outlet.

## Sub lesson 3 — The watershed — the dividing ridge

Basins are divided from each other by a watershed: the high ground around the basin's rim. Rain falling just outside the rim belongs to the next basin. Catchment and river basin are given as synonyms for the drainage basin itself.
