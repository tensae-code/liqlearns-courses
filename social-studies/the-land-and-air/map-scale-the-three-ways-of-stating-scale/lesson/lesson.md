## Sub lesson 1 — What scale is

Scale is the method that lets a place's real features be fitted onto a map. Scale = map distance ÷ ground distance. Map distances are read in centimetres; ground distances in metres or kilometres. Without scale, a map is only a picture; with scale, it is a measure.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fraction and statement

A scale is stated three ways. As a fraction or ratio, unit-free: 1:200,000 — one unit on the map stands for 200,000 of the same unit on the ground. As a statement in words, with units: '1 cm to 1 km'.

## Sub lesson 3 — The drawn line — and a worked task

As a drawn line or graphic scale: a line divided into units, its first section finely divided, from which ground distances can be read off directly. Worked task: the ground distance is 100 km and the map distance is 10 cm — the scale is 10 cm ÷ 100 km = 1:1,000,000, found step by step in the video script.
