# The geographer's questions — what geography asks

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: what questions does a geographer ask about any place on Earth? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — What geography is (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide frames geography as the holistic bridge between natural and social science. It studies spatio-temporal variability — how things change over space and time — and human–environment relations, aiming at analysis of locations and distributions, environmental analysis, and analysis of spatial organisation (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — The two sides of the subject (teacher guide — evidence)

Geography holds a nature side and a people side together. Its content concepts, as the guide lists them, include location, direction and distance; size and scale; physical attributes and cultural attributes; interrelations between places; and place similarity and regions (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 3 — The geographer's questions (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide lists the key questions: What is it? Where is it? How did it come to be? How does it relate to what affects it? How is it part of a functioning whole? Why do patterns develop? How does its location affect people's lives? Behind them run the recurring concepts of all Grade 11 inquiry: location, place, relationships and interdependence, movement, regions, cultural understanding and diversity, and scale (teacher guide — evidence).

## Say it to your companion
