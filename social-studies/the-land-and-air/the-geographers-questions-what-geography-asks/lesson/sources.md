Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography begins as a daily practice, not a chapter: pupils draw the landscape around their school, the plan of their quarter and Haiti with their school placed on it, read landscape forms and functions from their own croquis, then trace one everyday object (a coffee cup, a phone) from raw material to shop shelf to see the world from the local. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Sub lessons 1–3 rest on the Grade 11 teacher guide's general introduction (guide printed pp. 1–3) and are labelled (teacher guide — evidence). Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 9 G9 Geography Venezuela): a full national geography written as Geohistoria — every physical topic (position, climate, relief, hydrography) is paired with the historical stages that organised the same space (agrarian Venezuela, oil Venezuela, 21st-century Venezuela), and the Amazon rainforest closes the book as a defence-of-life argument.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 8 Student Book): widening-circles design — the Americas in depth at secondary level — maps and location first, then the continent's regions, resources, population, migration and demography. Every lesson runs the same 'Sembrar y cosechar' activity cycle.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 8 Teacher Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): widening-circles design — the Americas in depth at secondary level — maps and location first, then the continent's regions, resources, population, migration and demography — the teacher guide mirrors the student book lesson-for-lesson (same 38 lessons and blocks) and adds didactics, theory summaries and answer keys; its geography lessons are placed here at the guide's own printed pages. Every lesson runs the same 'Sembrar y cosechar' activity cycle. Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 1): the second cycle opens with the discipline itself — geographic thought, its history from Greek antiquity to the determinist/possibilist schools, then cosmography (celestial sphere, solar system, Earth's shape and revolution) before any regional content.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 7 Geography): From Class VII Geography becomes a standalone subject organised in three strands (Time and Space; Physical Environment; People and Environment), every world topic closed with its Bhutan case — climate of Bhutan, vegetation types of Bhutan, minerals of Bhutan, and Bhutan's Himalayan disaster set (earthquake, flood, windstorm, fire, GLOF).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 Geografía G5): The first Geography book a Cuban child meets opens with the history of geography itself (cuneiform maps, Hecataeus), builds all map skills before any content, and ends the planet tour with world population, the world political map and world heritage — with Cuba's own places (Llanura del Cauto, Trinidad) woven in as examples throughout.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 6 Geografía de Cuba G6): The child's first geography book is the home country itself — one chapter of tools (coordinates, orientation, scale), then Cuba whole (situation, relief, coasts, climate, population, economy), then its two great regions landscape by landscape; the political-administrative division taught is the pre-2011 one (Ciudad de La Habana appears as a province), and History episodes woven into the regional chapters (the Santa Clara battle, the Granma landing) are named here but not placed — they belong to the History course.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 9 Geografía de Cuba G9 (2012)): The 2012 edition follows the G9 programme chapter for chapter (archipiélago → naturaleza → panorama económico → desarrollo social → situación medioambiental → regiones) and is explicit about its own dating: data before 31 Dec 2010 use the 1975 provincial division, while Chapter 6 teaches the new 16-province organisation of 1 Jan 2011. NOTE — this is NOT the same book as the Biblioteca copy filed next (Book 16): that copy is the wholly rewritten 2025 Tercer Perfeccionamiento edition, so the two are placed separately.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 9 Geografía de Cuba G9 (2025)): The 2025 rewrite keeps the home-country arc but rebuilds it for the Tercer Perfeccionamiento: Chapter 2 now profiles every province inside the political-administrative division itself, the climate chapter adds the Tarea Vida state plan by name, the economy chapter is organised by primary/secondary/tertiary sectors with self-employment (trabajo por cuenta propia) and the Mariel development zone, and the social chapter faces an ageing, shrinking population (life expectancy 73.7 years, a "Vivir 120" programme) and closes with Cuba's Human Development Index. It is a different book from the 2012 edition (Book 15), not a parallel copy, and is placed separately.
