Palestine's own way (Grade 6, PS G6P1): Grade 6 opens with how we know — the map and its elements as the first source of geographical knowledge — then walks the Arab homeland region by region (peninsula, Levant, Palestine, Iraq, North Africa), each time fixing location first, then surface and climate, then the ancient civilisation the land produced.

Samoa's own way (Year 9, Book 2): Place is taught through Samoan lived places — ordinary, sacred (tapu), natural and political places such as Mulinu'u — and migration is taught through Samoa's own movement story and its New Zealand diaspora, closing with a full mapping-skills unit.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): from P5 the medium is English and the organising frame is the child's own PROVINCE — its location, services, environment, climate, population and infrastructure — before the frame widens to the whole country at P6. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Rwanda is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 3 Amasomo Mbonezamubano SB): P3 moves the home ground up one rung of Rwanda's administrative ladder, from the cell to the SECTOR (Umurenge) — and it is here, at age 8–9, that formal map work begins: the sector map of Gikomero, the district map of Gasabo, symbols, scale, and the sectors that share a border with ours.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies SB): the pupil's book teaches the province through named Rwandan things — the five provinces with Kigali City as the case study, Rwanda's four seasons by their Kinyarwanda names (Umuhindo, Urugaryi, Itumba, Icyi), and the country's two international airports (Kigali and Kamembe) in the transport-places lesson.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 5 Social Studies): Geography is Fiji-first — the child locates Fiji among Pacific neighbours, works on the map of Fiji's islands and divisions, and learns land care through cane farming, river-farm flooding and village land use.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 9 Social Science): Secondary geography opens with a full map-skills chapter — grid references, contours and cross-sections practised on Fiji maps — before environment use and conservation, including Pacific methods (shifting cultivation, rotational grazing, appropriate fishing methods).

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 6 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): in Senior Primary the strands are re-ordered and Environment and Resources comes first and largest — a full local-environment course (physical/human environment, environmental change, human impacts, sustainability, natural hazards) taught through sample guided lessons with benchmarks, before Civics, Culture, History and Trade. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 5 SD Geography 5): The first-ever Geography book in the post-2019 curriculum starts from the child's own classroom and school: directions are learned by standing facing the sunrise, map elements through a pupil's-house map, and only then does the book step out to Sudan, Africa and the world — a local-to-global ladder.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 SS5): the Class 5 Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 51–85) deepens the Class 4 spiral — same BOLTS, landforms, weather and population topics, but now with scale types, latitude/longitude and time-zone arithmetic, worked population-density calculations, and graph-drawing exercises from real Pakistani city data.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G4 SS T2): Term 2 ends with Jordan's region — the world around me (solar system, Earth's movement, the world's heat zones), geographic maps, then Bilad al-Sham and Iraq and the Arabian Peninsula as Jordan's neighbourhood.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 4 Social Studies): geography enters inside "Social Studies" through the child's own surroundings — phenomena are sorted from photographs first, then the local environment, then the map of Iraq's governorates, and only then the wider geographical environments (city, countryside); history and homeland/civics chapters follow in the same book.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 10 Geography): the literary track opens geography as a discipline in its own right — its thought from the Sumerians to GIS, how a geographer researches, where geographical data come from and how they are drawn, and only then the fields of the science; Iraq supplies the worked examples throughout (Baghdad research problem, Iraqi topographic maps, Iraq's gas time series, the Baghdad–Basra distance exercise).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 7 Geography): From Class VII Geography becomes a standalone subject organised in three strands (Time and Space; Physical Environment; People and Environment), every world topic closed with its Bhutan case — climate of Bhutan, vegetation types of Bhutan, minerals of Bhutan, and Bhutan's Himalayan disaster set (earthquake, flood, windstorm, fire, GLOF).
