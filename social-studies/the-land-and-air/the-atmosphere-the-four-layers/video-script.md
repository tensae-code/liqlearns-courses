The troposphere is the lowest layer: all weather happens here — rain and cloud form here — it holds most of the air, and temperature falls with height; its thin top boundary is the tropopause.

The mesosphere, above the stratopause, is the coldest layer; temperature falls with height, and its top is the mesopause.

The teacher guide subdivides the thermosphere into the ionosphere, a layer of electrically charged particles, and the exosphere, the outermost fringe, rich in hydrogen and helium; it fixes the mesosphere's top at about 80/85 km, puts jet streams — high-velocity winds — at the top of the troposphere, and calls the thermosphere the hottest, least dense layer (teacher guide — evidence).
