## Sub lesson 1 — Troposphere and stratosphere

The troposphere is the lowest layer: all weather happens here — rain and cloud form here — it holds most of the air, and temperature falls with height; its thin top boundary is the tropopause. Above it, the stratosphere holds the thin ozone layer, which absorbs the Sun's dangerous ultraviolet rays; temperature rises with height there, and its top is the stratopause.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mesosphere and thermosphere

The mesosphere, above the stratopause, is the coldest layer; temperature falls with height, and its top is the mesopause. The thermosphere is the last layer, and temperature rises with height. So the temperature line zig-zags: down, up, down, up. Your paired task: explain why temperature rises in two layers and falls in the other two.

## Sub lesson 3 — Inside the top layer (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide subdivides the thermosphere into the ionosphere, a layer of electrically charged particles, and the exosphere, the outermost fringe, rich in hydrogen and helium; it fixes the mesosphere's top at about 80/85 km, puts jet streams — high-velocity winds — at the top of the troposphere, and calls the thermosphere the hottest, least dense layer (teacher guide — evidence). The ozone layer is being thinned by chlorofluorocarbons — CFCs — from human activity, which endangers all life; CFCs are not 'carbon emissions', whatever the student book's wording suggests (see sources).
