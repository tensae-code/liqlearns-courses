Greece's own way (Greece Grade 6 Geography Teacher Guide (old ed.)) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 6 turns outward from Greece to the whole Earth — Earth in space, the natural environment, Earth as people's living space (how humans live in deserts, polar lands, rainforests, temperate zones), then a continent-by-continent tour (Europe in depth first, then Asia, Africa, North and South America, Oceania). The guide works from children's own prior mental models (e.g. flat-Earth models, taught with oranges and globes) and gives per-chapter objectives, alternative pupil ideas, method, activities and workbook/assessment answers (printed pp. 100–103). That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Greece is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 6 Geography Workbook (old ed.)): a worksheet for every one of the 45 student chapters plus 10 assessment sheets — one per unit and then one per continent — so the world tour is practised continent by continent.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 7 Geology-Geography Workbook): the Gymnasium workbook is a project book, not drills — pupils build a solar-system model, draw a relief profile from the Vouraikos topographic map, argue the Aral Sea case, retell ecosystems as comics, build an imaginary city, study the Ruhr, and "fly over" each continent from satellite images; it ends with blank outline maps to photocopy and enlarge.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 6 Geography Student Book (old ed.)): the student twin of Book 4's 45 chapters, with one addition the guide only implies — every continent section opens with a one-page "identity" profile of the continent (area, extremes, population at a glance) before its position, environment, states and peoples chapters.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 7 Geology-Geography Student Book): Gymnasium Geography is organised by Earth's spheres — maps first, then the natural environment sphere by sphere (atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, biosphere), then the human environment (population, cities, resources), closing with one "snapshot" chapter per continent, Antarctica included. Lessons open with art and literature (a Little Prince extract introduces coordinates) and state their learning goals up front ("In this chapter you will learn…").

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 7 Geology-Geography Teacher Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide opens with the philosophy of the whole package — against memorising lists of countries and numbers: geography as systems, change over time and cross-curricular links (the ΔΕΠΠΣ framework), with "mental maps" as the goal. Per lesson it gives objectives, visual material, teaching approach and worked answers (e.g. the coordinate games solved for Quito, Athens, Manila, Lusaka; Greece's own 1821–1999 birth/death-rate table under population). Its second half guides the workbook's project activities, and it closes with cross-curricular question sets and 32 suggested assessment topics. Europe gets no separate unit here — it is the whole of the Grade 8 course. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Greece is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.
