# Areas of irregular shapes — counting squares

## Do it first

A lake has no formula. But it can be covered in squares, and squares can be counted. Read the grid-square method on the book’s lake example.

## Sub lesson 1 — Count the squares

Lay a grid over the irregular shape. Count the full squares inside it. Then combine the part squares — pairing and estimating them into whole-square equivalents. Full plus combined parts gives the shape’s area in grid squares.

## Sub lesson 2 — Convert, or use a planimeter

Multiply the square count by one square’s ground area (found by the areal scale) for the ground area. A planimeter — an instrument traced around the boundary — gives the area directly, the book’s alternative for the same job.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the grid-square method from first square to ground area.
