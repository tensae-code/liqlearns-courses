Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Social Studies G6 T1): Kuwait's intermediate Social Studies is organised as four "learning units" per term, each with its own field label (Geography / History / Civics) printed in the unit's standards page, and every unit closes with a project and a full assessment. Term 1 of Grade 6 takes the whole Arab Gulf as its region — Kuwait first among equals: Unit 1 (Geography) covers the natural features of Kuwait and the Gulf States, Unit 2 (History) the civilizational features, Unit 3 (Geography/History) population origins and migrations, Unit 4 (Geography) the old economic activities; a values unit ("Stations with Good Kuwait") and a maps appendix close the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 2 Lessons 2–5: the historical importance of the Gulf's location and the Dilmun, Tarut, Hili and Magan civilizations, printed pp. 47–50; civilizations in Kuwait (Ubaid at Al-Subiya, Hellenistic Failaka), pp. 50–52; Islamic civilization in the Gulf region, pp. 52–55; Islamic civilization in Kuwait (Kazma, Umm al-Aish, Al-Shuqaya; Dar al-Athar al-Islamiyya), pp. 55–57. History — Unit 3 Lessons 3–5: the Utub migration and the founding of Kuwait, pp. 68–70; the naming of Kuwait and the choice of Al Sabah, pp. 70–73; the founding of the other Gulf States, pp. 74–76. Values/Civics — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": social solidarity (the Failaka story), pp. 101–104; patience, pp. 104–106; visiting the sick, pp. 106–108; the gift of water (Sabil bin Duaij), pp. 108–110.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 9 Social Studies G9 T1): Grade 9 turns outward to the whole world through a single theme — the environment. Four full Geography units work it from both directions: what the environment is and how it is divided; the environment's effect on humans (where people live, what they do, state power, disasters); the human effect on the environment (ecological balance, pollution of air, water, soil and by noise); and the other environmental problems (radioactive pollution, global warming, desertification, water scarcity) with Kuwait's and the world's efforts against them. A Civics unit on law and crime follows, and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics/law — Unit 5, law protects society from crime: the concept of crime and its two elements — a legal rule forbidding the act, and an act causing harm — against wrong behaviour and school infractions, printed pp. 90–92; the three pillars of crime — the legal pillar ("no crime and no punishment except by text"), the material pillar (the act actually done; the law does not punish intentions) and the moral pillar (intent against error), pp. 93–95; classifying crimes by seriousness, the worse the harm the graver the crime — drug crimes, electronic blackmail and violent assault as the gravest examples, pp. 96–98; drug crimes — use and possession defined, their effects on the individual (addiction, poisoning, death, falling school and work performance) and on society (more theft and violence, burdens on health and law services), and Kuwait's penalties under Decree-Law 159 of 2025, pp. 99–102; unit assessment, pp. 103–105. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": welcoming the guest — honouring the guest as an old Kuwaiti duty owed no return, the Amir joining the ardha folk dance at state receptions, pp. 108–109; trust and dhimma in old Kuwait — people dealing on word and honour, with few bonds and little work for lawyers, pp. 112–113; the replies in condolence — "Azzam Allah ajrakum" answered by "Ajrana wa ajrakum", mourners received for three days after the burial, pp. 114–115 (the diwaniya station is placed below).

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 7 Geography 7): a dedicated English-medium Geography book built entirely on Pakistan's own physical and economic geography — the Indus plain and delta, the canal system, Rabi/Kharif crops, Sui gas, Thar coal, Khewra salt and Tarbela power — with world concepts (river courses, settlement hierarchy, greenhouse effect, mining types) taught through Pakistani examples first.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 6 Geography 6): world physical Geography taught through Pakistan's own land — its fault lines and 1935/2005 earthquakes, Khewra salt and Thar coal among the rocks, K2 and the Karakoram among the mountains, Pakistan's four climatic regions, its six forest types from mangrove to alpine, and the Indus flood as the country's defining disaster.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 7 Amangalieva NIS): the NIS (Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools) inquiry textbook: every lesson opens with a «Бұл сабақта» (In this lesson) goal box and closes with a «Сенің кезегің!» (Your turn!) task block; each section ends in a «Қорытынды» summary built round a concept map; the book closes with a trilingual Kazakh–Russian–English glossary. Country-studies section works through how a state improves its geographic position, with the UAE and Singapore as cases and Kazakhstan as the home example.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 9 Usikov): the Atamura Grade 9 course pairs every Kazakhstan topic with its research base — sections close with "исследования, географические карты и базы данных" (research, geographic maps and databases) for the lithosphere (§15), climate (§24), inland waters (§31), social geography (§47), nature use (§51) and Kazakhstan in the world economy (§64); it also names the country's own naming heritage — Kazakh oronyms (§10), hydronyms (§27), climate in Kazakh folk terminology (§23) and choronyms (§67).
