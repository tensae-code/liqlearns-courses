# Africa's six great river basins

## Do it first

Rain falls on a huge area of land. Where does it all end up? Trace in your mind how small streams join bigger ones. Then guess: into how many great river systems does the whole of Africa drain? Read on for the six.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a basin is — the words first

A **drainage basin** — also called a catchment or river basin — is the whole area whose surface water converges to **one outlet**. Its working words: **tributaries** are the streams that feed the main river; a **confluence** is where streams join; the **outlet** is the single point where the basin's water leaves; and a **watershed** is the raised divide separating one basin from the next.

Test every basin with the same question: if rain falls here, which outlet does it finally reach? The answer draws the basin's true border — not a political line, but a water line.

## Sub lesson 2 — The six great basins of Africa

Six basin systems carry the continent's water. The **Nile** drains the north-east to the Mediterranean. The **Congo** drains the equatorial centre to the Atlantic. The **Niger** sweeps through West Africa to the Atlantic. The **Zambezi** drains the south-central lands to the Indian Ocean. The **Orange** drains the south-west to the Atlantic. The **Chad** basin gathers water into the interior — a basin whose outlet is a great shallow lake, not the sea.

Learn them by direction: Nile north, Congo centre, Niger west, Zambezi south-east, Orange south-west, Chad inland. These six are the skeleton on which Africa's water resources — farming, fishing, transport, power — all hang.

## Say it to your companion

Name the six basins to your companion with each one's direction. Then define the four basin words. Your companion drops a pin on an imaginary map and asks: which basin takes this rain?
