# Major and minor landforms — built up and worn down

## Do it first

Before you read, look at any landscape you know — a hill, a flat field, a deep valley. Sort what you see into big shapes and small shapes. Say your sort out loud first, then read on and check it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The major landforms

The major landforms are the big shapes of the land: mountains, plateaus and plains. They are the frame every landscape hangs on — the high ground, the raised flat ground, and the low flat ground.

[Picture 21942 — a mountain, plateau and plain trio with a gorge cut through one]

## Sub lesson 2 — The minor landforms

Inside and between the majors sit the minor landforms: hills, gorges, valleys and basins. A gorge is a deep, narrow cut; a valley is a gentler low line between higher ground; a basin is a hollow the land drains into.

## Sub lesson 3 — Built up and worn down

Two families of processes make all of it. Internal processes — plate tectonics and vulcanism — push land up. External processes — erosion by water and wind — cut it down. The worked example is the Abbay Gorge, about 1,000 metres deep between Goha Tsiyon and Dejen, sawn into the plateau by the river itself.
