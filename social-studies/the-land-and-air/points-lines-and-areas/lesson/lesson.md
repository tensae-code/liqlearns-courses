# Points, lines and areas

## Do it first

Before you read, take three things near you — a well or a tap, a path or a road, a field or a lake. Shrink each one onto paper: what shape does each become? Guess first, then read the three shapes of every map.

## Sub lesson 1 — Points — features too small to draw true

A point marks a feature whose size does not matter at the map's scale: a well, a school, an airport. The point carries the feature's exact location, and its attributes say what it is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lines — features that run

A line models a feature that runs from place to place: a road, a river, a boundary. The line follows the feature's path across the map, joining point to point.

## Sub lesson 3 — Areas — features with extent

An area, or polygon, models a feature with extent: a lake, a park, a region. Every feature of all three kinds is defined the same way — by its location in a coordinate system, plus its attributes, the facts that describe it.
