# Areas of regular shapes on maps

## Do it first

A rectangular field on a map is a geometry exercise wearing a geography coat. Read the regular-area method.

## Sub lesson 1 — Formulae on the map

For regular shapes, measure the needed lengths on the map and use the geometry formulae: square and rectangle — length × width; triangle — half base × height; circle — πr². The book’s formula table gives them side by side.

[Picture 21127 — a rectangular field on a map with its sides measured]

## Sub lesson 2 — The areal scale

Then convert: areas convert by the areal scale — the linear scale squared. At 1:50,000, one map centimetre is 0.5 km, so one square centimetre on the map is 0.25 km² on the ground. Areas punish those who convert with the linear scale only.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the formulae and the areal conversion, working the example aloud.

## Do It Yourself

1. Give the rectangle and triangle formulae.
2. At 1:50,000, what ground area is 4 cm² on the map?
3. Say why the scale must be squared.
