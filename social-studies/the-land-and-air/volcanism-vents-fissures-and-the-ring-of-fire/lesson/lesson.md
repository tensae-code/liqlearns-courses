## Sub lesson 1 — Vents and fissures

Volcanism forces magma, gas and solids to the surface. Where magma rises through a pipe-like vent, eruptions pile up a cone volcano around the exit. Where it rises through a long crack — a fissure — lava floods out and spreads into a lava plateau, with little explosive activity.

The exit decides the landform: a point builds a cone; a line builds a plateau.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where volcanoes live — and their three states

Of roughly 1,900 historically active volcanoes, almost 90% sit in the Pacific Ring of Fire, with others on oceanic ridges and along the Great Rift Valley of East Africa.

By activity they are extinct — Mount Zuqualla in Ethiopia; dormant — Kilimanjaro; or active, erupted within the last 500 years — Erta Ale in Ethiopia. Ethiopia, sitting on the Rift, holds examples at both ends of the scale.
