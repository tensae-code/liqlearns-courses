Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 10): This is the physical-geography year: the universe and Earth's layers, continental drift with volcanoes and earthquakes — including earthquake-prevention measures as a group task — water and winds across the continents, and a closing unit on political and economic geography that treats borders, geopolitical order and international climate agreements as geography, with hurricane-season citizen responsibility as the water unit's group activity.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Geography): The final-year course is hazard- and water-centred for an island nation — volcanic eruptions and tropical cyclones (including Cyclone Heta on Niue), the hydrological cycle and water management, then atmosphere and climate change with Pacific consequences.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 3): a systematic geomorphology course — rocks and Earth's interior first, then isostasy, continental drift and plate tectonics, then internal agents (folds, faults, earthquakes, volcanoes in four lessons), then external agents and the modelled landscapes of hot, temperate, cold and coastal environments, closing with the geological cycle.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G11 ES T2): Term 2 is fossil fuels — their origin, kinds, and Jordan's own (oil shale, gas) with the petrochemical industry — then earthquakes and volcanoes.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Physical Geography): a pure physical-geography course in six chapters — landforms, weather and climate, water, the biosphere, soil, and the environment — with Iraqi anchors kept throughout (flood visits to Iraq's rivers, the hills of northern Iraq, groundwater and the desert caravans, soil salinity in the alluvial plain).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Earth Science): the scientific track's earth-science course — atmosphere, earthquakes and volcanoes, weathering and soil, environment and pollution, and fossils and geological time — bilingual with English technical terms throughout, and Iraqi touches kept (the air masses over Iraq, the yellow dust storms, Iraq's Natural History Museum).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 11 Geography): The Advanced level (XI–XII) is full academic geography — map projections, remote sensing and GIS, insolation and pressure belts, climate classification — with Bhutan applied chapters on agriculture, manufacturing and tourism, the last taught on Bhutan's own sustainability principle ("high value, low volume" in the Bhutan Tourism Monitor data the book uses).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Geography student): grade-10 Geography is a methods-first course — it opens with the science's history, branches and GIS/GPS/aerospace methods, works through the lithosphere with a full natural-disasters lesson, and closes with resources measured by endowment; Armenia's own rivers fill the hydrology tables and Armenia's place in modern geopolitics closes the political-map theme.
