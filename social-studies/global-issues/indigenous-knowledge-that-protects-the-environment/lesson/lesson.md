# Indigenous knowledge that protects the environment

## Do it first

A practice kept for two hundred years has passed an examination no laboratory can set: it worked, every year, or it was dropped.

## Sub lesson 1 — Knowledge tested by generations

Indigenous knowledge is locally held, experience-tested knowledge transmitted across generations — and it is a working environmental technology, not a museum piece. The section sets out indigenous knowledge for environmental issues generally: reading land, water, seasons and species through long local experience, and keeping the practices that experience proved.

[Picture 22320 — An Ethiopian terraced hillside farmed under local practice]

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's own practices

The section's home evidence is Ethiopia's own environmentally friendly practices — the terraced hillsides, closures, rotations and local rules by which communities protected land and water for generations. The lesson they teach the whole unit: solutions to sustainability problems are not only invented in laboratories and treaties; some are inherited, and the wise course keeps and learns from them.
