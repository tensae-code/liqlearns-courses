# Rapid Population Growth — Effects

## Do it first

Think of one classroom, one clinic and one forest near you, each serving twice as many people as ten years ago. What breaks first in each? Write the three answers before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The family and social costs

**Family costs:** food shortage; rising food, health, clothing and school costs; parental economic and psychological strain; and higher mother and child deaths. **Social costs:** pressure on **schools, health services, housing, water and electricity** — every shared service stretched by the same growth.

## Sub lesson 2 — The economic and environmental costs

**Economic costs:** capital is diverted from productive investment to social services, so **per-capita product and income fall** — the country spends on coping instead of growing. **Environmental costs:** forest is felled for **building, fuel and farmland**, and then **erosion and air pollution** follow the felling.

## Sub lesson 3 — Demographic overload — the engine (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide names the mechanism inside the environmental cost. When population grows rapidly, **trees are cut in great quantity because the majority of Ethiopia's population depends on wood for fuel, house construction and tools and equipment** — so **demographic overload causes deforestation**, and uncontrolled growth depletes and destroys the Earth's natural resources where no population-control mechanism exists. Its keyed problem list runs: **deforestation and desertification, decrease of farmland, water pollution, depletion of the ozone layer and the greenhouse effect**. (Teacher guide — evidence: this framing is the guide's, labelled as such; the fourfold cost above is the student book's own.)

## Say it to your companion

Name the four cost families. Your companion gives one cost inside each; then swap, and finish with the overload chain: growth → wood demand → deforestation.
