# Human Trafficking

## Do it first

A broker comes to a village promising good city work to the young. What questions should a young person ask before believing him? Write your questions — they are the start of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The modern slavery

**Brokers lure rural youth to cities for labour exploitation** — and across borders it is worse. **Trafficking is moving people by force or false promises, hiding them in coastal shelters, and putting them to forced work for the trafficker's profit.** It is called **the symbol of modern slavery** because victims are kept from visible work: **women forced into hidden domestic service and forced marriage, children into begging, youth into war**; in the worst cases **organs — kidney, heart — are taken for sale, or people are sold alive like animals**.

The causes group **social, economic and political**: weak government, poverty, corruption, conflict and instability, family breakup, human-rights violations, lack of education and jobs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's own wound

In Ethiopia the main victims are **poor youth migrating illegally**: forced to work, **wages stolen, kept sleepless, beaten** — some kill themselves under the suffering. The book sets three tasks that are this lesson's heart: read the photographs of Ethiopians on the migration route; **interview someone who knows migration's horror and write a one-page report**; and answer — **'if you were Ethiopia's leader, how would you solve migration?'**

## Sub lesson 3 — Child trafficking and the health bill (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide defines **child trafficking** separately: **any person under 18 illegally recruited, transported, harboured or received by threats or force for exploitation**. And it itemises what victims suffer: **inhumane living conditions, poor sanitation, inadequate nutrition, poor personal hygiene, physical and emotional attacks by traffickers, dangerous workplace conditions and occupational hazards** — with severe health consequences and social crisis the reason governments and international organisations now attend to it.
