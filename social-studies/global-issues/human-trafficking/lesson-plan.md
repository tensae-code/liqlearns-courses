# Lesson plan — Human Trafficking

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** Human Trafficking
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the eighth lesson of the title, after Terrorism (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** trafficking moves people by force or false promises into forced work for the trafficker's profit — called the symbol of modern slavery — and in Ethiopia its main victims are poor youth migrating illegally; the guide adds the under-18 definition of child trafficking and the victim's health bill.
**This lesson's own level:** sub lessons 1–2 are the Grade 7 student book (the definition and the broker's trade, then Ethiopia's own wound). Sub lesson 3 climbs with the Addis Ababa guide's child-trafficking definition and health inventory, as labelled teacher evidence. This lesson carries a full video script — the warning script the placement names.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The modern slavery** — define trafficking, say why it is called the symbol of modern slavery, name who is hidden in what work, and group the causes.
2. **Ethiopia's own wound** — name the main victims in Ethiopia and what they suffer, and carry the book's interview and leader tasks.
3. **Child trafficking and the health bill (teacher guide — evidence)** — state the under-18 definition and itemise the victim's sufferings and health consequences.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Terrorism — the chapter's two human-made harms sit side by side. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define trafficking: moving people by force or false promises into forced work for profit.
2. Say why it is called the symbol of modern slavery, and who is hidden where.
3. Group the causes social, economic and political, as the book does.
4. Describe Ethiopia's own wound: who the main victims are and what they suffer.
5. State the guide's child-trafficking definition (under 18) and the victim's health bill (teacher guide — evidence).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks trafficking needs a border crossing → brokers lure rural youth to cities inside the country too; the guide's Ethiopia note names internal and external forms.
- Thinks victims chose freely → the definition turns on force or false promises; a choice built on a lie is the trafficker's tool, not consent.
