# Lesson plan — The UN — the League's successor, built to last

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** The UN — the League's successor, built to last
**Place in the curriculum:** Global Issues — the world organisations thread; the Grade 10 book's opening post-1945 lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the United Nations, established in 1945 as the League's successor, gathers the world's states (193 members at the book's writing) under a Charter of peace and welfare, working through its main organs — Assembly, Security Council, Secretariat and Court.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the League of Nations lesson — the UN is the second attempt at the same house. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1945 — a successor on the Charter** — the UN established in 1945 on the Charter's goals and ideals, succeeding the League, with 193 member states at the book's writing.
2. **The main organs — who does what** — General Assembly, Security Council, Secretariat and International Court of Justice — the organ chart the book sets pupils to design.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The League of Nations — collective security's first try. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Date the UN's founding and name what it succeeded.
2. State the Charter's mission in your own words.
3. Name the four main organs and one job of each.
4. Say how the UN was meant to be stronger than the League.

## If the learner is stuck
Four organs, four jobs: Assembly — all members speak; Council — peace and security, the strong room; Secretariat — the Secretary-General's working machine; Court — law between states. Everything else is commentary.
