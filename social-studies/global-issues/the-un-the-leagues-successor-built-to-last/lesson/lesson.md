# The UN — the League's successor, built to last

## Do it first

The second house of peace was built while the second world war was still burning. Its builders had all watched the first house fail — over Ethiopia, over Manchuria, over everything. This lesson is what they built differently.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1945 — a successor on the Charter

The United Nations was established in 1945, as the Second World War ended, succeeding the League of Nations. Its founding document is the Charter, setting out the organisation's goals and ideals: to maintain international peace and security, to develop friendly relations among nations, and to cooperate on the world's economic, social and humanitarian problems — the League's two fields, peace and welfare, taken up again with the war's lessons in hand.

Where the League had begun with a fraction of the world, the UN grew toward universality: at the book's writing it counts 193 member states — effectively every state on earth inside one house, including Ethiopia, a founding member whose earlier plea to the League had gone unanswered.

## Sub lesson 2 — The main organs — who does what

The Charter divides the work among main organs. The General Assembly is the house's great hall: every member state sits there with one vote, debating and recommending on the whole range of the Charter's concerns. The Security Council is the strong room: a small body charged with maintaining peace and security, whose decisions members agree to carry out — the enforcement hand the League had lacked.

The Secretariat is the working machine, headed by the Secretary-General, administering the organisation's programmes and carrying its voice between governments. And the International Court of Justice settles legal disputes between states by law instead of by war. Assembly, Council, Secretariat, Court: voice, enforcement, administration, law — the four legs the new house stands on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: founded 1945 on the Charter, succeeding the League; then the four organs with one job each. Finish by naming the organ that holds the enforcement hand.
