# Protecting the Environment Where You Live

## Do it first

Look at one damaged place near you — a bare slope, a fouled stream bank, a waste heap. What would 'protecting' it actually mean, in actions, this month? Write the actions before you read the guide's list.

## Sub lesson 1 — What protection is, and who does it (teacher guide — evidence)

**Environmental protection is the practice of conserving the natural environment**, valued for **conserving what exists, repairing damage and reversing trends**. It is not one office's job: **individuals, organisations and governments** are all engaged in it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The community's forms, and two words kept apart (teacher guide — evidence)

The community-participation forms the guide names are **afforestation, reforestation, educating people, and reducing, reusing and recycling materials**. The guide keys the two plantings precisely, and this lesson keeps them apart: **afforestation is establishing a forest where there was no previous tree cover**; **reforestation is regenerating or replanting forest areas destroyed or damaged**. The guide closes by turning the lesson on the learner: pupils must **reflect on their own roles and identities as environmental actors in their localities**. (All of this lesson is teacher guide — evidence.)

## Say it to your companion

Define protection and its three values. Your companion names the community's forms; then each of you gives one hillside and says — afforestation or reforestation? — and why.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the two definitions side by side: afforestation / reforestation, keyed exactly.
2. Name one place near you for each: a no-cover place (afforestation) and a damaged forest place (reforestation).
3. Write your own role line: 'As an environmental actor in my locality, I…' and finish it with one dated action.
