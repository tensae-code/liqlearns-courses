**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Nauru, Tuvalu, Niue, Mongolia). Ethiopia is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere. Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grade 4 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Social Studies is one of four subjects fused into a single Exploratory Core guide, organised by four attainment themes; Geography enters through Jamaica itself — Taino settlement maps, cardinal points, Jamaica's physical features, parishes, weather and the Earth in the Solar System — always with hands-on map and model work. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 8): the Grade 8 year is the LOCAL year — every chapter is re-anchored to the child's own орон нутаг (local area/homeland): its environment and problems, its heritage, its economy and market, its governance. The book's Law/Civics parts (Chapters IV–V, printed pp. 63–94) are named in the placements, not placed. That way is stocked here as the way Mongolia teaches this thing.

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — Geography is taught as Pacific peoples' lived relationship with the ocean: voyaging, reef knowledge, tapu/fono closed seasons and village fishing practice in Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue itself, ending in a pupil action plan against plastic. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

Nauru's own way: Nauru uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisors Fay Itaia & Sharon Kam for Nauru) — Geography is taught as ocean stewardship: the Pacific as a connected "sea of islands", its animals, and the traditional fishing knowledge of Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue, ending in a plastic-waste action plan. And, from the land-side companion reader: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. The Nauru parts above are student-book parts, pupil-taught in Nauru; they are stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing, and the acceptance count above now counts Nauru for them — the Ethiopia teacher-guide position stated below is unchanged.

Tuvalu's own way: Tuvalu uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisor Valisi Tovia for Tuvalu) — and Tuvalu itself is a case study inside it: Tuvalu's own traditional fishing and bird-catching (printed p. 14) is one of the three country fishing studies. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing. And, from the land-side companion reader: Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes.

This whole lesson is the Addis Ababa guide's §7.1.2, a standalone protection lesson the Amhara student book has no equivalent of (its remedies sit inside the desertification lesson). The Addis Ababa Grade 7 teacher guide is NOT edition-matched to the Amhara student books (different bureau, different year, different language of instruction); guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence, never as pupil content from the student book. The lesson therefore stands on teacher-guide evidence only, is labelled so in every row above, and counts in no country's pupil-taught acceptance. There is NO Grade 8 teacher-guide coverage for Chapter 7: the Grade 8 guide in hand holds only Units 1–3, so no teacher evidence is claimed for any Grade 8 part of this lesson.
