**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Nauru). Ethiopia is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Nauru's own way: Nauru uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisors Fay Itaia & Sharon Kam for Nauru) — Geography is taught as ocean stewardship: the Pacific as a connected "sea of islands", its animals, and the traditional fishing knowledge of Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue, ending in a plastic-waste action plan. And, from the land-side companion reader: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. The Nauru parts above are student-book parts, pupil-taught in Nauru; they are stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing, and the acceptance count above now counts Nauru for them — the Ethiopia teacher-guide position stated below is unchanged.

This whole lesson is the Addis Ababa guide's §7.1.2, a standalone protection lesson the Amhara student book has no equivalent of (its remedies sit inside the desertification lesson). The Addis Ababa Grade 7 teacher guide is NOT edition-matched to the Amhara student books (different bureau, different year, different language of instruction); guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence, never as pupil content from the student book. The lesson therefore stands on teacher-guide evidence only, is labelled so in every row above, and counts in no country's pupil-taught acceptance. There is NO Grade 8 teacher-guide coverage for Chapter 7: the Grade 8 guide in hand holds only Units 1–3, so no teacher evidence is claimed for any Grade 8 part of this lesson.
