# Video script — Natural and Human-Made Disasters Near Home

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe's ECD A teacher guide, the *PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 35), teaches: Natural and human-induced disasters / Fire related disasters. Zimbabwe's ECD B teacher guide, the *PlusOne Social Science ECD B Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 46), teaches: Natural and human induced disasters (incl. local water bodies, fire rescue drills). Zimbabwe's Grade 1 teacher guide, the *PlusOne Social Science Grade 1 Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 46), teaches: Unit 23: Water related disasters. Zimbabwe's Grade 2 teacher guide, the *PlusOne Social Science Grade 2 Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 39), teaches: Unit 21: Floods.

Zimbabwe's own way (ECD A): Heritage-Based Curriculum organised by cross-cutting topics repeated at every level (Identity, National history, Heritage, Work and leisure, Transport and communication, Shelter, Global issues…); teaching is play-, song- and role-play-based with workbook colouring/cutting tasks; local examples throughout (totems, pada hopscotch, veld fires, Zimbabwe outline map). Zimbabwe's own way (ECD B): Same HBC topic spiral as ECD A, one step deeper — transport starts from traditional/indigenous means (walking, animal-drawn) before modern vehicles; heritage sorted hands-on into animals / plants (baobab, maize) / artefacts (ngoma, hosho, mbira). Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 1): From Grade 1 the guide is unit-numbered (41 units across the same 14 HBC topics) with suggested answers to the Learner's Book; geography grows outward from home/school — my country and its ethnic groups and neighbours (South Africa, Zambia, Botswana), then transport, shelter, and Zimbabwe-specific hazards (veldfires, water disasters). Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 2): The spiral widens from family to community — ethnic groups and languages of Zimbabwe (Kalanga, Sotho and others), production in the community, shelter types in the neighbourhood (granary, silos, community hall, clinic), and global issues introduced as floods and deforestation.
