# The gap between rich and poor countries

## Sub lesson 1 — The gap and its causes

Economic inequality between countries and regions — the rich–poor gap — stands at its highest in decades. It is conceptualised through per-capita GDP and trade balances: what each person produces on average, and whether a country sells more than it buys.

The section traces causes and effects of trade imbalance — how a persistent gap between exports and imports both reflects inequality and deepens it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring it — Gini, Lorenz, McLoone

Inequality has named instruments. The Gini coefficient summarises a whole distribution in one number, drawn as the Lorenz curve: a line of equality, and the actual curve bowing away from it — the further the bow, the wider the gap.

The McLoone Index is the second named measure. The book's figures rank selected African countries by World Bank Gini estimates and map the world's 2019 Gini coefficients, closing on the widening current imbalance and what would significantly reduce poverty.
