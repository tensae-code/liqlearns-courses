Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): World inequality is questioned through the words used for it (Nord/Sud, développé/sous-développé) and through Haiti's own case — a rich-country/poor-country comparison (Canada or USA vs Haiti), the independence debt, food insecurity and Covid vaccination in Haiti — with every claim built from authenticated statistics, graphs and maps crossed from local to world scale. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error in the placements for this lesson is taught here. Instruments and figures are taught exactly as the placements report them.

Searchable tags: inequality, Gini coefficient, Lorenz curve, McLoone Index, per-capita GDP, social studies, Haiti, Inégale répartition des richesses, teacher sequence evidence

Palestine's own way (Grade 9, PS G9P2): the course closes at world scale — the diversity of natural environments, peoples and economies, then the age's challenges (population explosion, famines, poverty) framed as matters for international cooperation.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 8 Social Studies): Grade 8 Geography is a regional tour — one full country-profile lesson per Southeast Asian neighbour (location, area, population, economy, from map work), then a developing-vs-developed countries comparison.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 12 Geography): the final Geography year is economic geography — Cambodia's population, inequalities, farm/industry/energy/trade/tourism economy and urbanisation first, then the world's economic poles studied one by one: the USA, Japan and the European Union.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 4 G4 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): geography is the first block ("Conóceme, soy Venezuela") and is built as widening circles — the child's house drawn to a stated scale (1 cm on the plan = 1 m), then Tomás's town, then Venezuela's territory and borders, then Latin America and the unequal world.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 8 G8 Geography of the Continents): Grade 8 tours the continents one by one — natural features, population, economic activities — with one country studied in depth per continent (Indonesia, Eritrea, Germany, the USA, Brazil), and closes on contemporary world issues.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 7): industry taught as a long history — from Palaeolithic tool-making through manufacture and mercantilism to the Industrial Revolution and its consequences — before location, types and industrial landscapes; commerce likewise from barter and the invention of coin to trade blocs and unequal world flows.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Social Science student): grade-11 Social Science is the state year — law, the economy's state, elections, parties, international law and relations — with the economy theme measuring countries by GDP, growth, inflation and unemployment before judging state intervention.
