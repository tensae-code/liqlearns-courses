Costa Rica's own way: a distance-education anthology organised around "the human footprint on geographic space" — every unit opens with a generating question and objectives, and Costa Rica is used as the home case inside world topics (tropical forest conservation, food production problems). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 8): the year's Geography is one long chapter, "Protect the Bond between Nature and People": pupils model the world's biomes, compare world forest cover in 2000 and 2020, map Bangladesh's own forests and disaster-prone areas, and read a long inquiry-reader on biomes, sea resources and the Sundarbans mangrove.

Samoa's own way (Year 9, Book 1): Geography is taught through Samoa's own resources first — environment, land, rainforest, water and marine resources of Samoa itself, then how resources are shared out in Samoan community life.

Samoa's own way (Year 10, Book 2): Identity and cultural interaction are taught from the Samoan self outward, and place change is taught through one big Pacific-region case — deforestation in Indonesia, read off South-West Pacific maps.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 2) (teacher guide — evidence): The spiral widens from family to community — ethnic groups and languages of Zimbabwe (Kalanga, Sotho and others), production in the community, shelter types in the neighbourhood (granary, silos, community hall, clinic), and global issues introduced as floods and deforestation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Malaysia's own way (MY T3 Geografi Tingkatan 3): Form 3 turns from skills (tables, graphs, pie charts) to living Malaysia — its natural vegetation and wildlife and the human pressure on both — then outward to the world's biomes, resources and economic cooperation, closing with forest management and recycling.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 8 Geografija 8): Grade 8 is pure world regional geography, continent by continent (Africa, Australia/Oceania, the Americas and polar regions), each region worked through the same grid — position/relief/waters, climate/vegetation, population, economy — and the book closes with two world-problems units (rainforest loss, desert spread, water conflicts, the Aral Sea).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 4 UG P4 SST Home-Study): Social Studies organised entirely around "our district": the child's own district's physical features, vegetation, settlement and work are the geography content. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Term Two social activities and ceremonies in the district (printed pp. 17–19); Term Three — Our Leaders in the District, Children's Rights, Responsibilities of People in Our District, Social Services in Our District and caring for social service centres (Civics, printed pp. 20–35).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG P5 Merged Home-Study SST): The whole SST course is Uganda itself: locating Uganda and its districts/neighbours, Uganda's physical features, climate and vegetation, then Uganda's population. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Mathematics section (PDF pp. 9–220); English section (PDF pp. 221–274); SST Term Two — The People of the Pre-Colonial Period, ethnic groups, explorers, colonial rule, Legislative Council and the independence struggle (History, SST printed pp. 36–54); SST Term Three — national symbols, democracy, The Government of Uganda, duties of citizens (Civics, SST printed pp. 56–70).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 9 G9 Geography Venezuela): a full national geography written as Geohistoria — every physical topic (position, climate, relief, hydrography) is paired with the historical stages that organised the same space (agrarian Venezuela, oil Venezuela, 21st-century Venezuela), and the Amazon rainforest closes the book as a defence-of-life argument.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 10 Geography (Dari)): the upper-secondary pattern changes from continent tours to themes studied in depth through Afghanistan itself: a full map-skills chapter (map types, scale, colour, projection, satellite photos), Afghanistan's climate and each of its river basins one by one (Amu, Kabul, Helmand–Sistan, Harirud, the closed basins), an environment-protection chapter aimed at Kabul's air and streets, a natural-hazards chapter, then earth-and-space and atmosphere chapters.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 12 Geography (Dari)): the final year is pure human geography — Afghanistan's borders side by side (north, north-east, east/south, west), its population and ethnic make-up, religion and language, transport and communications, then the same themes for the world: population, races, religions, languages, global communications, and global environmental problems to close the whole twelve-year course.
