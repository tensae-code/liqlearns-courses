# Forests — Shrinking and Care

## Do it first

Find the nearest grove or single old tree. How many services does it give your street — shade, fuel, fruit, soil-holding, homes for birds? List them; then imagine the ground bare. That difference is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why forests fall, and what follows

**Forests are gatherings of vegetation where trees abound.** Once, wide parts of Europe, most of northern Asia, North America, Africa and Latin America were forest-covered; **now very little forest cover remains** — because of **farm and grazing expansion, city growth, and cutting for the various services trees give**.

The ecological disturbance brings the listed world problems: **climate disturbance, accelerated soil degradation, faster silt accumulation, heavy flooding, fuel-wood shortage, the loss of individual plant species, and forests failing to remain the breeding places of plant species**. The chapter's review stock fixes one trap: groundwater does **not** increase when forests shrink — it falls.

## Sub lesson 2 — The seven care practices

Forests have a **high capacity to reproduce and replace themselves** — care works with that power. The seven practices: **reforestation** — re-covering with forest a place that was forest and is now bare; **coppicing** — cutting trees for use in a way that makes shoots sprout from the uncut stump; **forest protection** — wholly banning cutting where overuse threatens the ecological balance; **using proper tree-cutting methods**; **controlling forest fires**; **controlling forest diseases** — using medicines when disease appears and multiplying resistant species for the future; and **controlling pests** — clearing and burning the fallen trunks, branches and leaf-litter that harbour bark- and trunk-eating organisms like the qinqin as printed. Over them all, **land-use planning** is applied.
