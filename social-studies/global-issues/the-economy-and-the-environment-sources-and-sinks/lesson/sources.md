**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Nauru, Niue, Tuvalu, France, Rwanda, Dominica, Uzbekistan, Fiji, Vietnam, Iran). Bolivia is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 11 Economic & Legal Education, Study Topics): the environment topic is taught entirely from national reports (Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment state-of-environment data) — extraction tables, Red River levels, Mekong Delta salinity — and closes with the State's own remedy plans, ending on green growth and the circular economy.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 5 ESD Reader 3 People and the Natural Environment in the Pacific): A regional Pacific reader used in Fiji — farming is taught as a spectrum from subsistence through semi-subsistence to cash crops for export, set against the cost of chasing overseas demand.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 7 Social Studies): production and consumption are taught with Iranian anchors throughout — the historic bazaar as market and social centre, compulsory third-party car insurance, and the Silk Road crossing Iran as a bridge between East and West.
