# Famine and food security — availability is not enough

## Do it first

Television shows a feeding camp and calls it famine. The book corrects: the camp is the end of a long process, not the thing itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Famine is a process, not an event

Famine is a sequence of processes and events that reduce food availability or food entitlements until morbidity and mortality rise substantially — which is why media images of feeding camps (Ethiopia and southern Sudan named) mislead as a definition. Famine must also be separated from the chronic hunger affecting as much as a third of the world's people: hunger is the standing condition; famine is the collapse.

## Sub lesson 2 — The food-security frame

Food security is access by all people, at all times, to enough food for an active, healthy life. It rests on availability plus the ability to acquire food — the entitlement pillar. Food insecurity is chronic (a household's persistent inability to buy or produce enough) or transitory (a temporary decline from price instability, production failure or income loss) — and famine is transitory insecurity at its worst. A country can hold grain and still starve households that cannot acquire it.
