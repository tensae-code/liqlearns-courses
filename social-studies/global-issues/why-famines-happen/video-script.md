# Video script — Why famines happen

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The first master cause is a decline in food availability. Its natural precipitants: agricultural drought, floods, high winds, unseasonable cold or frost, crop disease, and pest invasions — locusts, army worms and quelea birds. Its human precipitants: conflict stopping planting, weeding, harvesting and selling, or destroying standing crops; external economic shocks to oil and fertiliser prices or export earnings; internal macro-economic mismanagement, such as pricing policies that discourage surplus production and overtaxed exports; and forced state procurement and over-export pushing national availability below need. The second master cause is a reduction in people's ability to acquire food — the entitlement failure that can starve households while food exists in the same country, even the same market. Either root alone can produce famine; together they are deadliest. The section closes with the mechanisms by which people and systems cope with famine's effects — selling assets, migrating, eating less and differently — each coping step buying time at a price paid later.
