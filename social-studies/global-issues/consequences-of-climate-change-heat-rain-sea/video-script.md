# Video script — Consequences of climate change — heat, rain, sea

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Consequences begin with temperature: more frequent, intense heat waves raise heat-related deaths — partly offset by fewer cold-related illnesses. Lower latitudes face more drought and unpredictable rainfall. Precipitation changes unevenly: generally up over northern mid- and high-latitude land, down over some subtropics, with the heaviest events intensifying while soils dry faster between rains — a double stress on agriculture, which needs rain that is both enough and on time. Sea level rose about 17–19 cm across the twentieth century — about half from melting land ice adding water, and half from thermal expansion, the same water swelling as it warms. Since 1993 it has risen about twice as fast, roughly 3.4 cm per decade. For coastal millions that means higher storm surges, damaged reefs, and salt water contaminating groundwater that farms and towns drink from.
