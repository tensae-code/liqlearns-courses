# Lesson plan — The Arab-Israeli conflict — a war that never quite ends

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** The Arab-Israeli conflict — a war that never quite ends
**Place in the curriculum:** Global Issues — the post-1945 thread; the Grade 10 book's Middle East lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Middle East's defining post-1945 conflict: rooted in Zionism and the creation of Israel, running through successive Arab-Israeli wars into a durable unresolved question of land, statehood and refugees, with the superpowers' rivalry drawn in.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the post-1945 lessons — the conflict is one of the Cold War's regional theatres. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Roots — Zionism and the creation of Israel** — Zionism defined, Israel created, and the conflict's frame set: two peoples' claims on one land.
2. **Wars without an ending** — successive Arab-Israeli wars, superpower rivalry drawn into the region, and the durable unanswered questions of land, statehood and refugees.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Bandung to Belgrade — the third camp refuses both blocs. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define Zionism as the book's glossary defines it.
2. Tell the conflict's roots in two sentences.
3. Name the three open questions the lesson leaves: land, statehood, refugees.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
Hold three nouns: land, statehood, refugees. Every war and every failed peace in this story is an attempt to answer one of those three — and the lesson's point is that none has been answered to all sides' acceptance.
