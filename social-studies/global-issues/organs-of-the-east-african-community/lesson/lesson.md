## Sub lesson 1 — Organs of the East African Community

Uganda Excel P6 SST (printed p. 16), teaches: the Co-ordinating Committee of permanent secretaries, the Council of Ministers, and the East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) as the EAC's parliament that approves its budget. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Organs of the East African Community

Uganda Shine P6 SST (printed p. 17), teaches: the Secretariat at Arusha, headed by the Secretary General who runs the Community's daily affairs and finances, and the East African Court of Justice, which promotes human rights and settles disputes among member states. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Uganda's way — the book's own teaching way (Uganda Excel P6 SST)

Uganda's own way: P6 looks outward and inward at once — the East African Community's own organs and symbols beside the member states' flags, then the citizen: what citizenship is, the child's rights, what violating rights looks like (mob justice named plainly), and how elections actually run. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Uganda's way — the book's own teaching way (Uganda Shine P6 SST)

Uganda's own way: a second publisher teaches the same P6 civics — the EAC's organs (Secretariat at Arusha, Court of Justice) and symbols, then each member state's flag and arms and why symbols matter.
