# The United Nations and Human Rights — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's part teaches: why the world built the United Nations — the Second World War's destruction (Hiroshima and Nagasaki named), the 1944 Dumbarton Oaks talks and the 1945 San Francisco conference of fifty nations, whose Charter puts the protection of human rights at its head.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's part teaches: the regional answers — Europe's convention with its Strasbourg commission and court, the American declaration of 1969 and the African charter of 1981 — and the UN's specialised agencies for health, food, education, labour and children protecting rights in daily life, with the United Nations as the centre of the whole effort.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's part teaches: where a citizen goes — a petition to the Supreme Court within one month (Article 126), the Ombudsman (the Parliamentary Commissioner for Administration) who investigates the officials' wrongs, and the Human Rights Commission set up under Act No. 21 of 1996.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's part teaches: no state may deny or set aside the Declaration's rights on any ground whatever, and Sri Lanka, as a member of the United Nations, gives the world declaration its full attention, with steps taken at home to put these rights into practice.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on Sri Lanka's student books only, named in lesson/sources.md.**

**Answer: False — it stands on Sri Lanka's student books only, named in lesson/sources.md.**
