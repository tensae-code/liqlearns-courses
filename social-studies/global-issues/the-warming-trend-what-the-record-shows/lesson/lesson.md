## Sub lesson 1 — What the thermometers say

The observed record is plain. Average global surface temperature has risen about 0.8 °C in a century, and about 0.6 °C in just three decades — the warming is speeding up. The IPCC's 2001 Third Assessment Report found the 1990s the warmest decade and 1998 the warmest year since instrumental records began in 1861, and judged the twentieth century likely the warmest in 1,000 years. Most of the warming of the past 50 years was found to be human-caused.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where the trend points

If emissions keep rising, projections run to a rise of +1.4 to 5.8 °C by the year 2100 — several times the change already seen. The shifts are not waiting for 2100: sea level, snow cover, ice sheets and rainfall patterns are already shifting now. A trend is a direction proven by a long record, and this record — thermometers since 1861, checked against a thousand years — points one way: up.
