Palestine Grade 9 Social Studies Part 2, printed p. 70, teaches: facing famines is a collective, preventive responsibility that calls for joining national, regional and international efforts: on the Arab level the Arab League created the Arab Organization for Agricultural Development, aiming at Arab agricultural development to face the agricultural problems the Arab homeland suffers, famines among them, and at coordinating with the related regional and international organisations; on the international level, organisations seek solutions for famine victims by providing international aid, such as the FAO, which aims to expand agricultural development, prospect for water sources to solve the water deficit, resist desertification and drought, and control population growth rates in the countries suffering their rise, and a number of international and regional organisations provide urgent aid to famine victims, like the International Committee of the Red Cross, the Red Crescent, and UNICEF, which gives its urgent and emergency aid to children in areas exposed to wars, siege or natural disasters; and there is a social responsibility every person must bear — the moral outlook of solidarity (takaful) that everyone in the one state should show, to offer help to the victims of these disasters (the famine concept and its natural and human causes, printed pp. 67–68, are named in this book's Contents map, not placed).

Palestine's own way: the course ends at world scale and comes home — war is classified and costed, heritage is identity with a UNESCO date (full membership, 31 October 2011), and racial discrimination is defined by the very convention Palestine joined in 2014 without reservations, then answered by its own Basic Law and its protected minorities, the Samaritans of Mount Gerizim first.
