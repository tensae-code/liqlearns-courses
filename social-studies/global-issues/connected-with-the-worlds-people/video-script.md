# Video script — Connected with the Worlds People

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Japan Grades 1–2 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 170), teaches: The 2020 Tokyo Olympics and Paralympics, where people from every country gather across their differences; things from other countries in our own daily life; and greetings that differ by country — Japan's bow, America's handshake, India's namaste, Mongolia's sain bainaa.
Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 176), teaches: Seven billion people on one earth, many languages, ways of life and ideas.
Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 178), teaches: What war brings to human society, and praying for peace (reading).
Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 180), teaches: Friendship across borders through the 1967 World Women's Volleyball Championship in Tokyo (reading).

Japan's own way (Book 1): MEXT's own moral reader for the youngest pupils is built from short stories and picture tales around four value topics per unit — orderly living, doing your own work, doing willingly what is right, and honesty — each story followed by talk-it-over pages for school, home and neighbourhood.

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination.
