# Non State Actors in World Politics — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: the actors states no longer monopolise — the non-state actor defined (any participant acting across borders without government part or support); the kinds, each with its example: international organisations; international NGOs — Transparency International, whose Corruption Perceptions Index judges governments including Mongolia's; transnational corporations — Oyu Tolgoi LLC, whose copper goes wholly to China's market, so that Mongolia's foreign-trade income itself rides on one company's decisions and international law must regulate such power; influential individuals — the Dalai Lama, the Pope, Bill Gates, George Soros, and, on the dark side, Osama bin Laden; religious movements; the international media — CNN's live war reporting of 1991 made the media a true actor, and citizens now speak into world affairs through social networks; and criminal and terrorist networks, fought jointly through Interpol; then the jurist's questions — how an international organisation differs from an INGO (in structure, aims and founders: states create the one, citizens and groups the other), how each becomes a subject of international law with its own rights and duties (the UN Charter's Article 104; the 1982 Law of the Sea Convention), the common ground (both serve shared human aims), and the six schools of theory — realist, liberal, Marxist, neo-Marxist, neo-realist, neo-liberal — set in one table by what each takes the true actor to be.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: True**
