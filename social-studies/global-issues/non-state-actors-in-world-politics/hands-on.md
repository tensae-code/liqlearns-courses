# Hands-on — Non State Actors in World Politics

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Topic 6.1 "Non-state actors in international relations"
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The actors states no longer monopolise — the non-state actor defined (any participant acting across borders without government part or support); the kinds, each with its example: international organisations; international NGOs — Transparency International, whose Corruption Perceptions Index judges governments including Mongolia's; transnational corporations — Oyu Tolgoi LLC, whose copper goes wholly to China's market, so that Mongolia's foreign-trade income itself rides on one company's decisions and international law must regulate such power; influential individuals — the Dalai Lama, the Pope, Bill Gates, George Soros, and, on the dark side, Osama bin Laden; religious movements; the international media — CNN's live war reporting of 1991 made the media a true actor, and citizens now speak into world affairs through social networks; and criminal and terrorist networks, fought jointly through Interpol; then the jurist's questions — how an international organisation differs from an INGO (in structure, aims and founders: states create the one, citizens and groups the other), how each becomes a subject of international law with its own rights and duties (the UN Charter's Article 104; the 1982 Law of the Sea Convention), the common ground (both serve shared human aims), and the six schools of theory — realist, liberal, Marxist, neo-Marxist, neo-realist, neo-liberal — set in one table by what each takes the true actor to be. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
