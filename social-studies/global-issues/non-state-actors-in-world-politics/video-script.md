# Video script — Non State Actors in World Politics

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Grade 11 Social Studies, printed p. 168, teaches: The actors states no longer monopolise — the non-state actor defined (any participant acting across borders without government part or support); the kinds, each with its example: international organisations; international NGOs — Transparency International, whose Corruption Perceptions Index judges governments including Mongolia's; transnational corporations — Oyu Tolgoi LLC, whose copper goes wholly to China's market, so that Mongolia's foreign-trade income itself rides on one company's decisions and international law must regulate such power; influential individuals — the Dalai Lama, the Pope, Bill Gates, George Soros, and, on the dark side, Osama bin Laden; religious movements; the international media — CNN's live war reporting of 1991 made the media a true actor, and citizens now speak into world affairs through social networks; and criminal and terrorist networks, fought jointly through Interpol; then the jurist's questions — how an international organisation differs from an INGO (in structure, aims and founders: states create the one, citizens and groups the other), how each becomes a subject of international law with its own rights and duties (the UN Charter's Article 104; the 1982 Law of the Sea Convention), the common ground (both serve shared human aims), and the six schools of theory — realist, liberal, Marxist, neo-Marxist, neo-realist, neo-liberal — set in one table by what each takes the true actor to be.

Mongolia's own way (Book 9): the final year goes deep on law as the citizen's toolkit — property, harm, crime, the complaint against the state itself, and the national and UN machinery that guards rights — with sociology closing on how societies change and what moves them.
