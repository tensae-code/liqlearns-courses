# Variability is not change — the discipline of a data record

## Do it first

A price that jumps up and down all year around the same level is wobbling, not rising. Climate data must be read with the same discipline.

## Sub lesson 1 — Wobble is not a shift

Variability is the climate's natural wobble — from internal processes or external forcing, with or without humans — around a level that stays, on average, where it was. One hot year, one failed rain, one cold spell: each is variability speaking, and none of them, alone, proves anything about the climate. The discipline of a data record is to refuse to call a wobble a change.

## Sub lesson 2 — Change is a persistent shift

Climate change is a persistent, decades-long shift in the climate system's average and variability, from natural and/or human (anthropogenic) drivers. The test is persistence: the new level must hold across decades, the way the Grade 12 record shows temperature up about 0.8 °C in a century. Read any climate claim this way: ask for the record's length, ask whether the shift persists, and never let a single season argue against a century of thermometers.
