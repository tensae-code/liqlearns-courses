# Water — Pollution and Shrinking Clean Water

## Do it first

Where does your drinking water come from, and what passes it on its way to you — fields, streets, waste places? And when the tap or the well runs low, what do people do? Write both answers before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Water pollution (the Grade 7 depth)

**Water pollution** is the pollution of **lakes, rivers, seas, oceans and groundwater** by human-made or natural substances. Polluted water **changes its nature, cannot renew itself, and loses its physical quality, biological content and economic use**. The book's own audit follows: pupils examine their **kebele's rivers, ponds, wells and springs**, and how household waste is disposed of near them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why clean water runs short (the Grade 8 climb)

One year on, the book returns to water from the other side: not spoiled water, but **shrinking clean water**. Water is essential to everything alive, and **71% of the Earth is covered by it**, kept in amount by a reliable natural cycle — divided equally, there would be drinking water enough for all. But the stores are **unevenly spread**, water is **wasted**, **consumption climbs yearly**, and **quality is lost** — so clean-water shortage is widespread, recorded in **80 of the world's countries**, Ethiopia's cities among the places it shows plainly. Falling amount and quality harm crop production and biodiversity, and the book quotes the saying: **'the Abay's child dies of thirst'** — great rivers near, clean drinking water still unreached.

## Sub lesson 3 — Results, and the three ways out (the Grade 8 climb)

The results listed: **falling crop products; rising death and migration of domestic and wild animals; vegetation shrinking and desert spreading; and rising human death and migration** from drinking-water shortage.

The solutions come in three: **first, saving water** — which every person on Earth can do: reduce consumption, stop leaks, replace pipes in time, use water reasonably. **Second, establishing clean-water storage**, with improved filtering and treatment technologies. **Third, and most promising as printed, converting salt water into clean water — desalination**.
