**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Bolivia, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Cambodia). Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Jamaica's own way (Grade 5 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Grade 5 Social Studies pairs a heritage year (Europeans and Africans, national heroes, Caribbean culture) with a physical-environment year — erosion, locating Jamaica and the Caribbean by compass and latitude/longitude, types of rainfall, the Solar System, plants and water pollution. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

There is NO Grade 8 teacher-guide coverage for Chapter 7: the Grade 8 guide in hand holds only Units 1–3, so no teacher evidence is claimed for any Grade 8 part of this lesson. The Addis Ababa Grade 7 teacher guide is NOT edition-matched to the Amhara student books (different bureau, different year, different language of instruction); guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence, never as pupil content from the student book.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 7, Secundaria 1): The Bolivian Texto de Aprendizaje runs every unit through the same four moments — Práctica, Teoría, Valoración, Producción — and frames geography inside the plurinational, Madre Tierra worldview: continental drift and geological time sit beside the Laws of Mother Earth (071 and 300), the human right to water, and a closing general geography of Bolivia itself (regions, orography, political map, salt flats and lithium).

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.
