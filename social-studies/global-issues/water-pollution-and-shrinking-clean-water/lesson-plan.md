# Lesson plan — Water — Pollution and Shrinking Clean Water

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** Water — Pollution and Shrinking Clean Water
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the fourth lesson of the title, after Air Pollution (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** water is spoiled two ways — polluted until it cannot renew itself or be used, and shrunk by uneven spread, waste and climbing consumption until clean water runs short in 80 of the world's countries — and the book gives the results and the three ways out.
**This lesson's own level:** the Grade 7 treatment (water pollution) sits at the lower depth as sub lesson 1; the Grade 8 treatment climbs from it in sub lessons 2–3 (why clean water runs short, its results and solutions) — the two chapters sequenced inside one lesson, as TITLES.md prescribes for a title spanning both books.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Water pollution (the Grade 7 depth)** — define water pollution and state what polluted water loses: its nature changed, no self-renewal, physical quality, biological…
2. **Why clean water runs short (the Grade 8 climb)** — explain shortage from uneven spread, waste, climbing consumption and lost quality, against 71% cover and shortage in 80…
3. **Results, and the three ways out (the Grade 8 climb)** — list the results and give the three solutions — saving water, clean-water storage, desalination — in the book's order.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Air Pollution — pollution of air and of water are taught side by side in the Grade 7 chapter. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define water pollution and say what polluted water loses.
2. Explain why clean water runs short even though 71% of the Earth is water-covered.
3. List the results of water shortage.
4. Give the three solutions: saving water, clean-water storage, desalination.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks 71% water cover means no shortage → cover is not supply: the stores are unevenly spread, wasted, and losing quality; shortage is recorded in 80 countries.
- Mixes pollution with shortage → pollution spoils the water that is there; shortage is clean water running low. Both strike the same tap.
