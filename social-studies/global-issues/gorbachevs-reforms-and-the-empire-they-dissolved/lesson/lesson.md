# Gorbachev's reforms — and the empire they dissolved

## Do it first

A reformer takes over a tired empire and opens two windows: let people speak, let the economy breathe. Fresh air rushes in — and the house, held together by silence and habit, comes apart. This lesson is how reform dissolved an empire.

## Sub lesson 1 — Glasnost and perestroika

Mikhail Gorbachev became the Soviet leader in 1985, inheriting a stagnating superpower: an economy that could not match the West's, and a system held together by control. His answer was reform in two named parts. Glasnost means openness: transparency in government, and freer speech and press — the right to say what everyone already knew. Perestroika means restructuring: opening the economy to some private ownership, a hybrid between the communist command system and capitalist markets.

The reforms were meant to strengthen the Soviet Union by curing it. Instead they licensed its peoples to judge it. Once speech was free, the demands the system had suppressed for decades — for rights, for national independence, for an end to shortages and lies — were spoken aloud, and then organised.

## Sub lesson 2 — 1989 — and the union dissolves

The end came fast. The reforms encouraged people to demand more rights; protests spread through the Soviet Union and its bloc. In 1989 anti-communist movements swept the Eastern Bloc — the puppet governments built in 1945–49 fell one after another, and Moscow, reformed and overstretched, did not send the tanks that an earlier era would have sent.
[Picture 23105 — the Cold War map and the union breaking apart]

The pressures underneath were material as well as moral: the USSR could no longer match United States military spending, and the Eastern Bloc's economies were failing around it. In the early 1990s the Cold War ended the way no planner had scripted: the Soviet Union itself disintegrated into over a dozen independent nations. The superpower rivalry that had organised the world since 1945 was over — ended not by war, but by reform, protest and exhaustion.
