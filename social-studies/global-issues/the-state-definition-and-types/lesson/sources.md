**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Egypt, Afghanistan, Eritrea, Mexico, Belarus, Syria, Bangladesh, Iraq). Haiti is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 8): sociology is taught as a toolkit for changing one's own society — socialisation, control and change are each defined, then immediately used: the class stops a dowry in a cartoon case, studies Martin Luther King as a movement model, and finishes by setting new norms for itself to practise through the civic club.

Mexico's own way (Grade 8 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades): the second year turns from living together to judging and acting — pupils weigh ethical judgements on real injustice, learn how laws are made and changed in Mexico's own Congress, practise mediation, and end as watchdogs: citizen participation is taught as the duty to see public money administered with honesty, transparency and legality.

Egypt's own way (Grade 12): a full political-geography course to close school geography — the state first (definition, unitary/federal/confederal types, capitals chosen for stated reasons, the natural then the human components of a state's power, electoral geography), then borders taught with the border/frontier distinction and the Great Wall of China as the historical case, then blocs and alliances, ending with international relations and the new world order; activities, exam models and a maps appendix fill the back half of the book.

Haiti's own way (EC1 7AF nation/État/identité) (teacher guide — evidence): Citizenship is built from Haiti's own foundations — the nation defined by shared identity, history, territory, values and institutions and the will to live together (unity, solidarity, social cohesion), read through the Haitian Constitution itself in French and Creole, and practised on the symbols and memory places of the pupils' own locality. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Haiti's own way (EC1 8e Fondements de la nation et du territoire) (teacher guide — evidence): The Grade 7 nation/state sequence is deepened by returning to the territory itself: pupils draw Haiti from memory, then test their drawing against the Constitution's own definition of the territory (article 8) and against the island shared with a second state. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): government is taught as a growth story — from clan and tribal administration run by appointed, removable leaders in the common interest, through class society and city-states (Athens beside Eritrea's own Adulis, Coloe and Toconda), to Eritrea's present unitary state with Regional Administration.

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 6): civics is taught inside the Arab-world regional tour — the state lesson interrupts the geography of Africa to define the state from Syria's own case (sovereignty won 17 April 1946, land, nation, authority), and the family/institutions unit teaches society as a ladder of organised groups, from the family cell to the cultural institutions the Ministry of Culture runs.
