# Desertification — Causes and Defence

## Do it first

Look at a slope, a grazing field or a wooded patch near you. Could it look like desert in twenty years? What would have to happen, year by year, for that to come true? Write your chain before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Desert spreading

**Desertification** is the desert spreading — land that carried grass, trees and farms drying and degrading until it behaves like desert. On the figures the book prints from the UN Environment Programme (1991), desertification **threatens 35% of the world — 4.5 billion hectares, the area of the two Americas — with 850,000,000 people affected**.

**Africa is the worst-hit continent**: two-thirds of Africa's land is already desert, and the spreading presses on the land that remains.

## Sub lesson 2 — The causes

The book lists the causes plainly: **overgrazing beyond the land's capacity**, **deforestation**, **backward ploughing**, **unbounded chemical fertiliser and pesticide use**, **city growth**, **air and soil pollution**, and **population beyond what resources can carry**. Every cause on the list is something people do — which is why the book treats desertification as answerable, not as fate.

## Sub lesson 3 — The defence

The defence begins at home: the book sets pupils to **list their own woreda's desertifying activities and propose remedies** — graze within the land's capacity, protect and replant trees, plough and farm in ways that hold the soil, and keep waste and chemicals out of land and water. A cause you can name in your own place is a cause you can start answering there.

## Say it to your companion

Name the seven causes from memory. Your companion names a woreda activity for each one; then swap. Finish by saying the three UN figures together.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the UN (1991) figures from memory: the share threatened, the hectares, the people affected.
2. List three desertifying activities in your own woreda, as the book's task asks.
3. Beside each activity, write one remedy your community could actually carry out.
