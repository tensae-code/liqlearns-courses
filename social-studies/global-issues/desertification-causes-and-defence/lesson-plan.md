# Lesson plan — Desertification — Causes and Defence

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** Desertification — Causes and Defence
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the second lesson of the title, after What a Desert Is (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** desertification is desert spreading into land that was not desert — threatening 35% of the world on the book's UN figures, with Africa worst hit — and it is driven by named human causes that can be answered.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What a Desert Is — the learner knows what a desert is; now they learn how desert is made. Grade 7 depth throughout; the Grade 8 book's 'deserts are spreading' question is the same teaching returned to in the climate lessons.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Desert spreading** — state the UN (1991) figures as printed and say Africa is worst hit, two-thirds of its land already desert.
2. **The causes** — list the book's causes: overgrazing, deforestation, backward ploughing, unbounded fertiliser/pesticide use, city growth, air and…
3. **The defence** — name a desertifying activity in one's own woreda and propose a remedy for it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What a Desert Is — desertification is a desert growing, so the definition comes first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what desertification is and how it differs from a natural desert.
2. Give the book's UN (1991) figures: 35% of the world threatened, 4.5 billion hectares, 850,000,000 people affected.
3. Say why Africa is the worst-hit continent.
4. List the causes and match each with a defence.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses desert with desertification → a desert is a place; desertification is a process — the spreading of desert-like dryness into land that was not desert.
- Mems the figures loosely → anchor them: 35% of the world, 4.5 billion hectares (the two Americas), 850,000,000 people.
