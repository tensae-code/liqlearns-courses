# Consequences — poles, ecosystems, people

## Do it first

At the poles, warming cuts both ways. Read which side is winning.

## Sub lesson 1 — The poles melt unevenly

In Antarctica, warming brings more interior snow even as coastal ice melts faster. Greenland's ice sheet is shrinking, and Arctic sea ice hit record summer lows in 2007 and 2012 — it may fall under 10% summer cover by mid-century. The poles are not a side story: their ice holds the sea level of every coast on Earth, and their white surface is part of the planet's own cooling.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ecosystems shift, people feel it

Extra CO₂ fertilises some plants, but it can let dominant species eliminate others, thinning the variety life depends on. Tropical developing regions may lose crop yield while high latitudes gain longer growing seasons; wildfires spread in dry high-latitude forests. The IPCC Fifth Assessment summary the unit closes with is stark: 2005 CO₂ levels stood far beyond the 800,000-year ice-core range of 180–300 ppm, and species-distribution changes are already hitting human well-being through disease and food supply — more losers than winners, worsening later in the century.
