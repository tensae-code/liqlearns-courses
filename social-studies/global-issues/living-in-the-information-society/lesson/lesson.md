# Living in the Information Society

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: information machines are convenient, but never give out friends' names and phone numbers to a stranger who calls asking — as Japan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — 情報モラル: コンピュータやけい帯電話などをどのように使えばよいのでしょうか (Information morals: how should we use computers and mobile phones?)

Japan Grades 3–4 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 170), teaches: Information machines are convenient, but never give out friends' names and phone numbers to a stranger who calls asking. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — 情報社会に生きる私たち (We who live in the information society)

Japan Grades 5–6 Watashitachi no Doutoku (printed p. 184), teaches: Using the internet well — too much gaming, personal information, and what cannot be said without meeting. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Japan's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 2): the middle pupils' reader widens in the same four steps as the other two Doutoku books — raise yourself, relate to people, feel life, relate to everyone — and plants the first civics inside moral teaching: society's rules are introduced through a banned swing the class wins back by making rules, and fairness is taught as never shutting anyone out. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Japan's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Japan's own way (Book 3): the older pupils' reader moves from self-discipline to society in four widening units — polish yourself, connect with people, cherish life, connect with everyone — and teaches civics inside moral stories: rights are introduced as the other face of duties, the Diet appears as the place where rules are made, and fairness is taught through a story of a Vietnamese classmate facing discrimination. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
