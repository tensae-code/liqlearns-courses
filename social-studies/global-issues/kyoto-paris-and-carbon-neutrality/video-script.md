# Video script — Kyoto, Paris and carbon neutrality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The Kyoto Protocol (adopted 1997, in force 16 February 2005) set mandatory cuts for developed countries — at least 5% below 1990 levels over 2008–2012 — and participating industrialised nations were on course for over 22%. But the USA never ratified, and big developing emitters such as China carried no reduction duty, so global emissions still rose over 25% from 1990 to 2012. Binding law for some could not hold a sky shared by all. The Paris Agreement (2015) replaced compulsion with voluntary national targets and regular progress reporting — though the USA announced withdrawal intent in mid-2017. Alongside the treaties, Costa Rica, Iceland and Norway and many businesses pledge carbon neutrality: emissions fully offset, for example by tree planting. The unit closes by tying emission cuts to co-benefits — slowed greenhouse enhancement, less acid rain, haze and photochemical smog, and health gains — with renewable energy (sun, wind, rain, tides, waves, geothermal) as the replacement path.
