# Kyoto, Paris and carbon neutrality

## Do it first

A treaty binds some countries and not others. Guess its weakness before you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kyoto — binding targets, partial world

The Kyoto Protocol (adopted 1997, in force 16 February 2005) set mandatory cuts for developed countries — at least 5% below 1990 levels over 2008–2012 — and participating industrialised nations were on course for over 22%. But the USA never ratified, and big developing emitters such as China carried no reduction duty, so global emissions still rose over 25% from 1990 to 2012. Binding law for some could not hold a sky shared by all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Paris — everyone pledges

The Paris Agreement (2015) replaced compulsion with voluntary national targets and regular progress reporting — though the USA announced withdrawal intent in mid-2017. Alongside the treaties, Costa Rica, Iceland and Norway and many businesses pledge carbon neutrality: emissions fully offset, for example by tree planting. The unit closes by tying emission cuts to co-benefits — slowed greenhouse enhancement, less acid rain, haze and photochemical smog, and health gains — with renewable energy (sun, wind, rain, tides, waves, geothermal) as the replacement path.
