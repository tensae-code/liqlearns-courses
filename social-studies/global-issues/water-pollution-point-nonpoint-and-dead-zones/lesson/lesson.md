# Water pollution — point, nonpoint and dead zones

## Do it first

One pipe pouring waste into a river is easy to point at. A thousand fields washing fertiliser into the same river is not. The book separates the two.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two ways filth enters water

Water pollution is harmful substances — chemicals or microorganisms — entering water bodies. It comes as point-source pollution, from one identifiable source, or nonpoint-source pollution, from diffuse sources spread over an area. Its loads include industrial and municipal wastewater chemicals and heavy metals. Point sources can be licensed and inspected; nonpoint sources must be managed across whole landscapes of farms and streets.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dead zones in the sea

In the oceans, pollution meets nutrient overload and warming to create near-lifeless low-oxygen 'dead zones'. The book's map shows coastal sites where oxygen has fallen to 2 mg/l or below — water that cannot support the life it should. A dead zone is pollution's end point: not dirty-looking water, but water emptied of breath.
