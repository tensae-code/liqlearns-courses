# Video script — Water pollution — point, nonpoint and dead zones

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Water pollution is harmful substances — chemicals or microorganisms — entering water bodies. It comes as point-source pollution, from one identifiable source, or nonpoint-source pollution, from diffuse sources spread over an area. Its loads include industrial and municipal wastewater chemicals and heavy metals. Point sources can be licensed and inspected; nonpoint sources must be managed across whole landscapes of farms and streets. In the oceans, pollution meets nutrient overload and warming to create near-lifeless low-oxygen 'dead zones'. The book's map shows coastal sites where oxygen has fallen to 2 mg/l or below — water that cannot support the life it should. A dead zone is pollution's end point: not dirty-looking water, but water emptied of breath.
