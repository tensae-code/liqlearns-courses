**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Egypt, Bhutan).**

Egypt's own way (Grade 12): a full political-geography course to close school geography — the state first (definition, unitary/federal/confederal types, capitals chosen for stated reasons, the natural then the human components of a state's power, electoral geography), then borders taught with the border/frontier distinction and the Great Wall of China as the historical case, then blocs and alliances, ending with international relations and the new world order; activities, exam models and a maps appendix fill the back half of the book.

Bhutan's own way: the primary course closes by putting the whole state in front of the child — what a government is, Bhutan's own change from monarchy to Democratic Constitutional Monarchy in 2008, its three branches told through the courts' own ladder and the kabney/patang ranks the King bestows, the Article 7 fundamental rights quoted from Bhutan's Constitution, and a social-problems chapter that names Bhutan's own helping agencies (Royal Bhutan Police, RENEW, NCWC) for every problem.
