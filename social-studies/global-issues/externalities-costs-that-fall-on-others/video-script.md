# Video script — Externalities — costs that fall on others

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The guide's answer to the student book's closing air question gives the course its first use of the word (teacher guide — evidence): city air pollution is named a negative externality of a business organisation. An externality is an effect of production that lands on people outside the deal.

Opening its cost section, the guide defines an external cost (teacher guide — evidence): a cost not borne by the firm but incurred by other members of society. It stands beside explicit and implicit costs as the third cost a full accounting must see — the one no ledger of the firm records.

The guide rules air is a free resource because no price is paid to breathe it (teacher guide — evidence); yet although the air itself is unpaid, people may pay for treatment of the health harm the pollution causes. Free air, priced harm — the externality lives exactly in that gap.
