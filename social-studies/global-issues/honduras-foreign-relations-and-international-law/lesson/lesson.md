## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 31 Honduras y sus relaciones internacionales

Honduras Grade 9 G9 Student CCSS, printed p. 194, teaches: Public international law studies and regulates the behaviour of states and other international subjects to guarantee peace and international cooperation; Honduras's foreign policy is directed from the Secretariat of Foreign Relations (Cancillería); its borders were fixed by international awards — King Alfonso XIII's 1906 award for the Nicaragua line and the Hughes tribunal award for Guatemala — and the country has ratified treaties and conventions with international organisms to protect its citizens. ## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 31, teoría adicional

Honduras Grade 9 G9 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 128, teaches: Honduras's own foreign-relations history — the Federation's official dissolution in 1839 made each province an individual State, and the 1839 Constitution (Art. 51) ordered the creation of the Ministry of Relations for all political affairs abroad and at home; the first official diplomatic relations were with Great Britain in the 19th century, through Consul Frederick Chatfield; the 1969 war with El Salvador did not end the border dispute — the International Court of Justice at The Hague, seized of it from 1986, ruled in Honduras's favour, granting two-thirds of the six disputed territories and fixing a border of some 420 km², and the two States disputed Isla Conejo in the Gulf of Fonseca again in 2014. ## Sub lesson 4 — Honduras's way — the books' own teaching way

Honduras's own way: the cycle closes with vocation and rights — three full lessons on the generations of human rights (with Honduras's own record: the most-violated civil right is physical integrity; Garífuna and Miskito peoples organising for third-generation rights), then the UN from Honduras's seat and the country's foreign relations told through its border awards. the final guide deepens the pupil book's rights cycle into doctrine and machinery — the UN treaty bodies and the three pillars, the ancient sources of rights from Hammurabi to the Iroquois constitution, the 1966 Covenants — and widens Bloque 4 from the pupil book's UN seat to Honduras's own regional house: the OAS, ODECA and SICA, and a foreign-relations history that ends at the 1992 Hague judgment and the 2014 Isla Conejo dispute. the Telebásica twin of Grade 9 teaches the same cycle at a distance learner's pace, each sequence paired with its television programme — and it is the more Honduras-specific of the two books on rights: CONADEH's creation decrees dated one by one, women's suffrage won by Decree 29 of 1955 with the literacy limit named, and the protective laws listed with their La Gaceta dates.
