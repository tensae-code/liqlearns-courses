# Video script — Mongolia And the Worlds Non State Actors

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mongolia Grade 11 Social Studies, printed p. 176, teaches: Mongolia's own framework — since 1990 an open, peace-loving, independent and multi-pillared foreign policy; the Foreign Policy Concept's six pillar directions, diagrammed: balanced friendly relations with the two neighbours; active participation in Asia-Pacific political and economic integration; friendly relations with the developed countries of West and East; realistic cooperation with developing countries toward shared goals; cooperation with the UN and its specialised agencies, the IMF, the World Bank and the ADB; and friendly relations with the post-socialist and CIS countries — the fourth direction being the one that runs through non-state actors (APEC, ASEAN channels, the Non-Aligned Movement, the UN system); and the Concept's standing commitments — strengthening democracy; fighting terrorism and organised crime together with others; joining UN peacekeeping; adapting to climate change and guarding against disaster and epidemic — as the work a small state does inside the world's common institutions.

Mongolia's own way (Book 9): the final year goes deep on law as the citizen's toolkit — property, harm, crime, the complaint against the state itself, and the national and UN machinery that guards rights — with sociology closing on how societies change and what moves them.
