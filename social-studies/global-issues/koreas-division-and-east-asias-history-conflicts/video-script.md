South Korea's Grade 10 Cheonjae Integrated Society, printed p. 260; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society, printed p. 256; Grade 10 Bisang Integrated Society, printed p. 248; Grade 10 Donga Integrated Society, printed p. 238; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society 2 (2022), printed p. 120, teaches: VIII.03 남북 분단과 동아시아의 역사 갈등, VIII.3 남북 분단 및 동아시아 역사 갈등과 국제 평화 기여 방안, VIII.3 동아시아 갈등과 국제 평화, VIII.05 남북 분단과 동아시아 역사 갈등의 해결과 국제 평화에 기여하는 방안은?, 세계화와 평화 3 남북 분단 및 동아시아 역사 갈등과 세계 평화를 위한 노력 — teaches: the North–South division and East Asia's history conflicts, and the road to settling them peacefully; analysing the North–South division and East Asia's history conflicts, and Korea's contribution to international peace; East Asia's conflicts and international peace; resolving the North–South division and East Asia's history conflicts, and contributing to international peace; the North–South division and East Asia's history conflicts, and efforts toward world peace.

South Korea's own way: Cheonjae's Integrated Society teaches the same nine-unit national frame as MiraeN's but ends the book with the Constitution itself and a standard labour contract (표준 근로 계약서) as appendices — pupils finish the course holding the two documents. Jihaksa splits every section into two numbered lessons, so its civics teaching is the most granular of the three publishers — human rights alone gets five lessons (meaning/change, modern society, the constitution's role, law-abiding participation, problems and solutions), and its justice unit ends on the institutions and practical plans for a just society. Bisang's Integrated Society binds its justice unit shut with a practice section (불평등의 해결과 정의의 실현 — solving inequality and realising justice) and its culture unit with creative succession of traditional culture; its peace unit is framed as East Asia's conflicts and international peace rather than the two Koreas alone. Donga writes every lesson as a question ("Why is civil disobedience ever needed?", "What does universal ethics do in understanding a culture?") and is the only one of the five publishers to teach civil disobedience (시민 불복종) beside the law-abiding spirit, and to split the conditions of happiness into two lessons — living environment/economic stability, then democracy's development and moral practice. the 2022 curriculum splits Integrated Society into two volumes; volume 2 is the civics-heavy one — human rights and the constitution, justice and inequality, then globalisation and peace — and it closes by naming the pupil's own role outright: "life as a global citizen in the future society" (세계시민으로서의 삶). Its enrichment boxes (사회 고수 되기) add the proportionality principle in limiting basic rights, redistributive justice for the socially weak, and the death penalty debated as a human-rights question.
