## Sub lesson 1 — A geohistory of risk — Haiti's vulnerable territory

Haiti's Grade 7 risk sequence reads the country's disaster history as geography: a typology and a chronology of disasters from Quisqueya through Saint-Domingue to Haiti, drawn into a historical risk map — the territory's vulnerability has a history, and the history can be mapped.

## Sub lesson 2 — Haiti's way — read the territory through its disasters

This is a teacher sequence, written for the teacher, not handed to the pupil. In Haiti's Projet Nectar sequence the past is not a separate subject here: the island's three names mark three ages of the same ground, and each age's disasters are placed on the map so pupils see vulnerability being built over time, not falling from the sky.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Risk is taught as geography first: pupils survey and map the hazards of their own locality, build Haiti's vulnerability factor by factor (anarchic urbanisation, deforestation, waste, poverty), read the country's disaster history from Quisqueya to the 2010 earthquake, and finish by making prevention posters and safety icons for their own school from the national risk plan (PNGRD 2019-2030). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
