# Video script — Three kinds of drought

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Meteorological drought is rainfall significantly below the long-term average for a place — a shortage in the sky. Hydrological drought is water in streams, reservoirs and aquifers falling short — a shortage in the stores. Agricultural drought is the soil-water supply agriculture needs becoming scarce — a shortage in the field, striking crops even where rain statistics look less severe. The three forms usually follow each other but can arrive independently. Hydrological drought can follow a meteorological drought — or arrive on its own, as when geological change cuts off part of an aquifer: the Ethiopian case the book gives. Naming the kind correctly matters, because the responses differ: you cannot refill an aquifer with a rain dance, nor save a crop with a full reservoir it cannot reach.
