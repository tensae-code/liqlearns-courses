# Three kinds of drought

## Do it first

'Drought' names three different shortages that usually — wrongly — are treated as one word. Separate them now.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sky, water, soil — three shortages

Meteorological drought is rainfall significantly below the long-term average for a place — a shortage in the sky. Hydrological drought is water in streams, reservoirs and aquifers falling short — a shortage in the stores. Agricultural drought is the soil-water supply agriculture needs becoming scarce — a shortage in the field, striking crops even where rain statistics look less severe.

## Sub lesson 2 — One can arrive without the others

The three forms usually follow each other but can arrive independently. Hydrological drought can follow a meteorological drought — or arrive on its own, as when geological change cuts off part of an aquifer: the Ethiopian case the book gives. Naming the kind correctly matters, because the responses differ: you cannot refill an aquifer with a rain dance, nor save a crop with a full reservoir it cannot reach.
