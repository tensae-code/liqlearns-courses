Costa Rica's own way: an almost purely geographical anthology — earth dynamics, water and climate change — built around risk: every world topic lands on Costa Rican vulnerability, risk management and territorial planning by sector (biodiversity, health, infrastructure, fisheries, energy, agriculture, water). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 5 book): the 2024 Grade 5 book is History-led (five of eight sections are national history, from the roots of society to the Republic); Geography is concentrated at the end, where the country is re-read spatially — Andean relief and rivers, natural regions and climatic floors, population diversity and migration, territorial division, provincial products, and the protected-area system taught together with climate-change action. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Honesty note: the ministry's 2024 transición-curricular PDFs, as held, reuse one body per sublevel — the Grade 5 body also carries the Grade 6 and Grade 7 covers (Subnivel Media), and the Grade 8 body also carries the Grade 9 and Grade 10 covers (Subnivel Superior). That content is credited once, under its true Grade 5 / Grade 8 identity, exactly as the placements state; the genuine Ecuador Grade 6, 7, 9 and 10 Estudios Sociales books are NOT held (library debt).

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Egypt's own way (Grade 9 Term 1): preparatory school closes with the “New World” — the Americas and Australia taught continent by continent (position, then surface: Rockies and Appalachians, Andes and the Amazon plain), the ecosystem sequence once more, and for the first time a full lesson on climate change itself (greenhouse-gas shares from a pie chart, human and natural causes, effects on ecosystems, water, farming and transport) — with the Panama Canal read against Suez as the strategic-position comparison.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Geography): The final-year course is hazard- and water-centred for an island nation — volcanic eruptions and tropical cyclones (including Cyclone Heta on Niue), the hydrological cycle and water management, then atmosphere and climate change with Pacific consequences.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado8 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria guide teaches Grade 8 through the student's own environment and land — ecosystems where you live, how you relate to your environment economically and culturally, and migration/displacement from "our land" — inside a mostly historical course.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 9 mk-g9): Grade 9 finishes the regional-geography cycle with the non-European continents — Asia again in greater depth (climate factors and phenomena, monsoons and typhoons, air pollution), then Africa, the Americas, and Australia with Oceania and the polar regions — each continent in the same pattern: position and coastline, relief, climate, hydrography, natural wealth and economy, population, a regional review, one or two spotlight countries (China, India; South Africa; USA, Brazil) and one problem lesson (food and water shortage in Africa; air pollution in Asia; global warming in the polar regions).

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 10 mk-g10): the gymnasium course restarts Geography as a systems science — Theme 1 works through the four spheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) lesson by lesson, then natural disasters by sphere; Theme 2 turns to people in geographical space (world urbanisation, energy, farming, industry, transport and trade, tourism, environmental impact); Theme 3 treats the world as one global system (globalisation, transport corridors and sea straits/canals, global networks and navigation systems, international organisations, global challenges and climate-change solutions). 43 numbered lessons.
