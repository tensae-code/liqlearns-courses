# Climate Change — Effects and Responses

## Do it first

Whose smoke warms your sky — only your town's? If gases from far away change your rains, whose problem is your harvest? Write your answer before the book gives its own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Four harms that cross borders

The harms **cross borders — they do not stay where they were caused**. **(a) Falling farm output:** most Ethiopians live by farming while population grows fast and demands more food; meeting it presses the environment, farm productivity falls, and feeding the growing population becomes hard. **(b) Soil degradation:** Ethiopia's mountainous, hilly and lowland terrain, with great rivers rising in the highlands, fits the land for erosion — soil routinely washes into neighbouring countries, driven by running water and, in the dry season and hot areas, whirlwinds; the change worsens drought, spreads desert, and unbalances nature.

## Sub lesson 2 — Health and water — and the remedies

**(c) Human and animal health:** diseases once confined to Ethiopia's lowland and desert parts — **malaria and trypanosomiasis (ገንዲ)** are named — are **moving into the highlands**; diseases spread faster, new ones likelier appear, and animal products, foreign earnings and home supply all shrink. **(d) Surface and groundwater shrink:** spreading desert and repeated drought draw down both. Beyond these, the change strikes the whole economy and can worsen instability, migration, hunger and war.

The stated remedies: **cut the things burned for various services, plant many trees, care for the dense forests, and encourage inventions that can draw carbon dioxide out of the air**.
