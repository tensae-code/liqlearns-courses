# Hands-on — The Six Organs of the United Nations

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Ch. 2 The six principal organs
Work through this part with your hands: the UN drawn as a temple standing on six pillars — the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Economic and Social Council, the Trusteeship Council, the International Court of Justice and the Secretariat. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Ch. 2 The General Assembly
Work through this part with your hands: every member state inside it with one vote each; its work — debating the principles of peace and disarmament, taking up what the Security Council sends it, approving the budget and sharing out its costs, and electing the Secretary-General on the Council's recommendation; it sits each year from September. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Ch. 2 ECOSOC and the Trusteeship Council
Work through this part with your hands: the Economic and Social Council, 54 members serving three years in turns, steering the UN's economic and social work and guarding human rights; and the Trusteeship Council walking its eleven trust territories toward self-government until the last of them, Palau, stood free in 1994. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Ch. 2 The Court and the Secretariat
Work through this part with your hands: the International Court of Justice at The Hague, the UN's own judicial organ, judging the disputes states bring before it and advising the Assembly and the Council; and the Secretariat under the Secretary-General, the organisation's chief administrative officer, chosen for five years, who carries threats to the peace before the Council. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Sri Lanka's way — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: the combined book's second half is a full civics course in disguise — democracy first as an idea with its thinkers and its six conditions, then Sri Lanka's own 1978 constitution opened article by article: the rights chapter with its Ombudsman and Human Rights Commission, the vote's road from McCallum to Donoughmore, the preferential presidential election, Parliament's 225 seats, the cabinet that falls with its bill, provinces and pradeshiya sabhas, and a court ladder that ends in village mediation boards.. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
