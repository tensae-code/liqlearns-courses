# The Six Organs of the United Nations — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's part teaches: the UN drawn as a temple standing on six pillars — the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Economic and Social Council, the Trusteeship Council, the International Court of Justice and the Secretariat.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's part teaches: every member state inside it with one vote each; its work — debating the principles of peace and disarmament, taking up what the Security Council sends it, approving the budget and sharing out its costs, and electing the Secretary-General on the Council's recommendation; it sits each year from September.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's part teaches: the Economic and Social Council, 54 members serving three years in turns, steering the UN's economic and social work and guarding human rights; and the Trusteeship Council walking its eleven trust territories toward self-government until the last of them, Palau, stood free in 1994.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's part teaches: the International Court of Justice at The Hague, the UN's own judicial organ, judging the disputes states bring before it and advising the Assembly and the Council; and the Secretariat under the Secretary-General, the organisation's chief administrative officer, chosen for five years, who carries threats to the peace before the Council.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on Sri Lanka's student books only, named in lesson/sources.md.**
