# Video script — The Six Organs of the United Nations

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000 (printed p. 38), teaches: The UN drawn as a temple standing on six pillars — the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Economic and Social Council, the Trusteeship Council, the International Court of Justice and the Secretariat.

Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000 (printed p. 39), teaches: Every member state inside it with one vote each; its work — debating the principles of peace and disarmament, taking up what the Security Council sends it, approving the budget and sharing out its costs, and electing the Secretary-General on the Council's recommendation; it sits each year from September.

Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000 (printed pp. 40–41), teaches: The Economic and Social Council, 54 members serving three years in turns, steering the UN's economic and social work and guarding human rights; and the Trusteeship Council walking its eleven trust territories toward self-government until the last of them, Palau, stood free in 1994.

Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000 (printed p. 42), teaches: The International Court of Justice at The Hague, the UN's own judicial organ, judging the disputes states bring before it and advising the Assembly and the Council; and the Secretariat under the Secretary-General, the organisation's chief administrative officer, chosen for five years, who carries threats to the peace before the Council.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 11 Social Education and History 2000): the combined book's second half is a full civics course in disguise — democracy first as an idea with its thinkers and its six conditions, then Sri Lanka's own 1978 constitution opened article by article: the rights chapter with its Ombudsman and Human Rights Commission, the vote's road from McCallum to Donoughmore, the preferential presidential election, Parliament's 225 seats, the cabinet that falls with its bill, provinces and pradeshiya sabhas, and a court ladder that ends in village mediation boards.
