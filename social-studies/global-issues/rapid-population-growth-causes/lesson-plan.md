# Lesson plan — Rapid Population Growth — Causes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** Rapid Population Growth — Causes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the fifth lesson of the title, after Water — Pollution and Shrinking Clean Water (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rapid growth means population beyond what natural resources can carry and beyond the jobs the economy can create — and in Ethiopia the book sorts its drivers cultural, economic and political, with a wider world list beside them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the population lessons of Population, Transport and Maps, where growth was counted; here the Grade 7 chapter asks why it runs fast. Its pair, Rapid Population Growth — Effects, follows in this title.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What rapid growth means** — define rapid growth as population beyond what resources can carry and beyond the jobs the economy can create.
2. **Ethiopia's drivers, sorted** — place the cultural drivers (honour, disdain, 'a child grows by its luck'), the economic drivers (income, old-age security) and…
3. **The world list** — name the world drivers, including high child mortality and unexpanded birth-control services.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Population, Transport and Maps — Population: Ethiopia's Growth, where the rate itself is taught. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define rapid population growth in the book's terms.
2. Sort Ethiopia's drivers into cultural, economic and political.
3. Give the saying 'a child grows by its luck' as the book prints it, and what it shows.
4. Name world drivers beyond Ethiopia's list.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts 'children as old-age security' as cultural → the book places it as economic: it is about support and income in old age.
- Thinks rapid growth just means 'many babies' → the definition is relational: beyond what resources can carry AND beyond the jobs the economy can create.
