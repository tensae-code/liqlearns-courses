That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Jamaica's own way (Grade 6 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): the Grade 6 Social Studies year is built as three paired terms — culture and independence first, then the physical world (mountains, landmasses and water bodies), then climate, Earth movements and the atmosphere, closing on Caribbean cooperation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P1): Social Studies opens with a full Earth-science unit (shape, movements, surface, life components, disasters) taught through Palestinian examples — seasons read off Palestinian crops, surface forms read off Palestine's own map, which pupils draw with its borders.

Angola's own way (Grade 6): a resources-and-environment year — every world topic (air, water, soils, forests) is closed with the Angolan case: Angola's fresh-water use, Angola's farm land, Angola's forest areas. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

India's own way (Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): Contemporary India-II is the human/economic book, matching Part I's physical book: India's resources, forests, water, agriculture, minerals and energy, manufacturing and the "lifelines" (transport, communication, trade) — taught throughout as development-versus-environment argument, with movements and case studies (Chipko, Narmada Bachao, rooftop rainwater harvesting in the Thar) carrying the criticism.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 9 mk-g9): Grade 9 finishes the regional-geography cycle with the non-European continents — Asia again in greater depth (climate factors and phenomena, monsoons and typhoons, air pollution), then Africa, the Americas, and Australia with Oceania and the polar regions — each continent in the same pattern: position and coastline, relief, climate, hydrography, natural wealth and economy, population, a regional review, one or two spotlight countries (China, India; South Africa; USA, Brazil) and one problem lesson (food and water shortage in Africa; air pollution in Asia; global warming in the polar regions).

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 English): geography starts from the child's own school and home — location in the Grama Niladhari → Divisional Secretariat → District → Province hierarchy, a plan of the school garden, home-made weather instruments, and care of the home surroundings — before widening to Sri Lanka's location in the world in the final chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 Tamil 2007): dialogue-driven lessons (teacher–pupil conversations) that open with Sri Lanka's location and a comparison with the world's other islands, then work through the home and its surroundings, caring for that environment, and finish with map-making and scale.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 12 Geography (Dari)): the final year is pure human geography — Afghanistan's borders side by side (north, north-east, east/south, west), its population and ethnic make-up, religion and language, transport and communications, then the same themes for the world: population, races, religions, languages, global communications, and global environmental problems to close the whole twelve-year course.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 6 LY G6 Social Studies): The geography half is "Principles of Physical Geography" in four units — surface shaping, climate, water, pollution — with Libyan examples inside world content (the Haruj volcanic mountains, Libyan springs such as Ain al-Sharshara). Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, History of Libya — the Ottoman State and Libya as an Ottoman province (printed pp. 12–18), Libya in the Ottoman era 1551–1912 including the Karamanli period (pp. 19–35), modern European colonialism and the Italian occupation of 1911 (pp. 36–43), Libyan resistance to the Italian occupation (pp. 44–54), Libya after the Italian era — liberation, the British/French military administrations, independence and the Libyan Kingdom (pp. 55–64).

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 12 LY G12 Environmental Geography): The geography course closes with a full environmental-geography book: the environment as a system of living and non-living parts, the world's great environments (water, then land biome by biome), the social environment and its balance with nature, the problems of settlements, the five pollutions (air, water, soil, food, noise) — and protection ending in sustainable development.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 7): industry taught as a long history — from Palaeolithic tool-making through manufacture and mercantilism to the Industrial Revolution and its consequences — before location, types and industrial landscapes; commerce likewise from barter and the invention of coin to trade blocs and unequal world flows.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 8): transport and communications taught as twin evolutions — transport from foot/animal traction to the Industrial Revolution's bulk cargo, communications from primitive signals to telematics (1970) and videoconference (1984) — with transport's environmental impact given its own lesson before tourism closes the module.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 ES T1): the final year opens with fossil fuels and climate action, then structures and plate tectonics, closing with geological exploration — ending on uranium exploration in Jordan.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Physical Geography): a pure physical-geography course in six chapters — landforms, weather and climate, water, the biosphere, soil, and the environment — with Iraqi anchors kept throughout (flood visits to Iraq's rivers, the hills of northern Iraq, groundwater and the desert caravans, soil salinity in the alluvial plain).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Earth Science): the scientific track's earth-science course — atmosphere, earthquakes and volcanoes, weathering and soil, environment and pollution, and fossils and geological time — bilingual with English technical terms throughout, and Iraqi touches kept (the air masses over Iraq, the yellow dust storms, Iraq's Natural History Museum).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Geography student): the senior Geography course is two big themes only — the world economy in depth (structure, division of labour, every branch, transport systems, trade) and then the environment (ecological balance, pollution of air and water, land degradation, biodiversity, ecosystem services), i.e. economy first, its ecological bill second.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).
