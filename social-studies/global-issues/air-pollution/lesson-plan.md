# Lesson plan — Air Pollution

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** Air Pollution
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the third lesson of the title, after Desertification — Causes and Defence (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** clean air is by nature without smell, taste or colour; air pollution is toxic substances mixing into the air and changing it against living things — and on the WHO study the book cites, only one person in ten breathes clean air.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the resource lessons of People and Resources, where air's trouble was named as pollution; here the Grade 7 chapter teaches it in full. The Grade 8 climb — greenhouse gases and climate — sits later in this title and stands on this lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What clean air is, and what spoils it** — describe clean air (no smell, taste or colour) and define air pollution as toxic substances mixing into air against living things.
2. **How little clean air is left** — state the WHO figure the book cites — one person in ten breathes clean air — and name main causes and defences.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe clean air as the book does: without smell, taste or colour.
2. Define air pollution in the book's terms.
3. State the WHO figure the book cites: only one person in ten breathes clean air.
4. Name main causes and defences from the lesson.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks air pollution must be visible → clean air has no colour, and polluted air's first everyday sign the book gives is a smell, not a colour.
- Reads 'one in ten' backwards → one in ten breathes CLEAN air; nine in ten do not.
