# Lesson plan — Terrorism

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** Terrorism
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the seventh lesson of the title, after Rapid Population Growth — Effects (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** terrorism is a planned act attacking life and property without distinction to create fear for a political aim — known by its mark, that it strikes unarmed people and civil places — and its bill, itemised by the teacher guide, touches every part of life.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing earlier in the title except the learner's own knowledge of public places. Sub lesson 1 is the Grade 7 student book's definition and central case; sub lesson 2 is the Addis Ababa guide's impact inventory, folded in as labelled teacher evidence.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The definition, its mark, and the September 11 case** — define terrorism, state its mark against ordinary war, and give the September 11, 2001 case with about 3,000 dead.
2. **The bill, itemised (teacher guide — evidence)** — list the guide's impacts across economic, social, cultural and political life, including fear, anxiety and depression.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: none in this title — this lesson stands alone. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define terrorism in the book's terms.
2. State its distinguishing mark against ordinary or guerrilla war.
3. Tell the September 11, 2001 case as the book gives it, with its death figure.
4. List the guide's itemised impacts (teacher guide — evidence).

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls any violence terrorism → the book's test has three parts: planned, without distinction, to create fear for a political aim — and the mark is striking the unarmed and the civil places.
- Dates September 11 to 2201 → that is the book's caption misprint; the body text gives September 11, 2001, and that is what is taught.
