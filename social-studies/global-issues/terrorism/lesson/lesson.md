## Sub lesson 1 — The definition, its mark, and the September 11 case

**Terrorism** is a **planned act attacking life and property without distinction, to create fear in society for a particular political aim**. Its distinguishing mark against ordinary or guerrilla war is that **it strikes unarmed people and civil institutions — public transport, schools, markets**.

The book's central case is the **September 11, 2001 destruction of the World Trade Center's twin towers in New York, with about 3,000 dead**. Pupils discuss causes, prevention, and whether attacks have occurred in Ethiopia.

## Sub lesson 2 — The bill, itemised (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide keeps the same core — violence targeting civilians for political or ideological aims — and makes the impacts the lesson's substance: terrorism **touches every aspect of economic, social, cultural and political life**, bringing **loss of lives, social disruption, environmental loss, destruction of property, damage to infrastructure, loss of confidence, and fear, anxiety and depression**. The guide's review key adds the legal shape: unlawfully and intentionally causing, attempting or threatening death or serious bodily injury, or serious damage to public or private property, public places, state facilities, transport systems, infrastructure or the environment.
