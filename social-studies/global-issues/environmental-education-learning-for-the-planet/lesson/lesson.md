# Environmental education — learning for the planet

## Do it first

Three small words carry the whole definition: education in the environment, about it, and for it. Hold all three.

## Sub lesson 1 — In, about and for

Environmental education is most simply education in, about and for the environment: an approach engaging learners, communities and policy makers to build ecological literacy, values and opportunities for action. It runs formal (in school) and informal/non-formal (public settings, media, workplaces), and it also travels as tacit knowledge — what people absorb by living and working in a place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lifelong and interdisciplinary

Its defining characteristics: it is lifelong — youth are a critical audience, but employees, clergy, activists and officials count too — and interdisciplinary: geography and ecology among the sciences, but social at its core, working on behaviour, critical thinking and citizen participation. Its roots are traced to the 18th century and Jean-Jacques Rousseau. The unit's practical outlet is close to home: school environmental clubs, where learning becomes planting, protecting and teaching others.
