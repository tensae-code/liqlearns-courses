# Environmental movements — people organising for nature

## Do it first

One person plants a tree; a movement changes what a whole society expects. The difference is organisation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Organised collective action

Environmental movements are organised collective action for the environment — people joining, persisting and pressing together rather than acting alone. The section builds the concept and classifies movement types, from local campaigns over a single forest or river to national and global movements over the climate itself.

[Picture 22319 — A community tree-planting march]

## Sub lesson 2 — Bright greens and dark greens

At one pole stand the 'bright greens': sustainability through better design, new technology and social innovation — the problem is real, and better tools can solve it. At the other stand the 'dark greens': environmental problems are inherent to industrial capitalist civilisation itself, demanding radical political change, not better gadgets. The section closes with environmental movements in Ethiopia, where community and national action — including great tree-planting campaigns — show the spectrum at work at home.
