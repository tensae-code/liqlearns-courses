## Sub lesson 1 — From old diseases to worldwide ones

A pandemic is the worst case in infectious disease: an outbreak gone worldwide. Communicable diseases are as old as hunter-gatherer life — people have always caught diseases from each other.

What changed everything was farming, settlement and inter-regional contact. Crowded, settled, trading peoples gave diseases the roads they needed: malaria, tuberculosis, leprosy, influenza and smallpox are among the early arrivals that travelled them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Eleven pandemics — and malaria's toll

About eleven accelerated pandemics are counted up to the most famous: the 1918 Spanish Flu, named from wire-service reports of a Madrid outbreak — the name records where the news came from, not necessarily where the flu began.

The stakes are quantified with malaria: infecting millions, and killing a child in Africa roughly every 30 seconds, as the book reports it — fought today by partnerships such as the Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria. Disease is a geographic force, and fighting it is organised geographically too.
