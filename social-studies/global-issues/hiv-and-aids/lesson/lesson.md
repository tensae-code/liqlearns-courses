# HIV and AIDS

## Do it first

Write what you have heard about how HIV passes from person to person — and one thing you have heard that you are not sure is true. The lesson will sort routes from non-routes; keep your unsure item ready to test.

## Sub lesson 1 — The virus and the signs

**HIV** is the virus that **weakens the body's defence**; **AIDS** is the condition that follows. The book states that **no cure for AIDS has been found** — its own framing, carried in the honesty note in this lesson's sources. The signs printed are six: **night sweats, mouth and throat sores, fatigue and anxiety, muscle pain and skin rashes, fever and swollen glands**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Routes, non-routes and prevention

The chapter opens by setting **transmission routes and non-routes and prevention** as its first task, and closes with them as its outcome. The review stock fixes the non-route the lesson must hold firm: **HIV does not pass through shared eating** — and its trap to reject: HIV is not 'curable if treated in time' in the book's framing. Prevention is the chapter's practical teaching: because there is no cure to rely on, **knowing the routes and guarding against them is the defence**.

## Say it to your companion

Name the six signs in order. Your companion states one route and one non-route; swap until the shared-eating non-route has been said plainly by both of you.
