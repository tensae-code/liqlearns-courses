# Video script — Building Safely in Disaster-Prone Areas

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe's Grade 5 teacher guide, the *PlusOne Social Science Grade 5 Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 70), teaches: Unit 25: Building materials suitable for constructing buildings in disaster-prone areas. Zimbabwe's Grade 4 teacher guide, the *PlusOne Social Science Grade 4 Teacher's Guide* (printed p. 77), teaches: Unit 25: Materials used to make different shelter (climate and availability factors).

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 5): Grade 5 transport is taught as a landlocked country's system — goods by rail/road/air/sea, animal transport (riding, leading, herding), and the roadports, seaports and airports Zimbabwe depends on; shelter links heritage sites (Matobo Hills) and map-drawing of Zimbabwe to building safely in disaster-prone areas. Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 4): Grade 4 heritage turns practical — Indigenous Knowledge Systems (aloe, mubvamaropa, moringa) and conservation of natural resources (gully filling, afforestation); transport names Zimbabwe's major roads and railways and their economic importance; shelter compares indigenous types (tsapi granary) with shelters outside Zimbabwe (igloo, pyramid) and the materials and climate factors behind them.
