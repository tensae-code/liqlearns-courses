# Lesson plan — What a Desert Is

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** What a Desert Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the first lesson of the title (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a desert is defined by rain, not heat — a place receiving under 250 mm of rain a year — and the world's deserts come in four types by location, the two largest being cold polar deserts.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing in this title — it opens Global Issues. Inside the lesson the Grade 7 book's treatment is the whole depth; the Grade 8 book returns to spreading deserts inside its climate and forest chapters, and those deeper lessons sit later in this title.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Rain, not heat** — define a desert as under 250 mm of rain a year and state that about a third of the Earth's land is desert.
2. **The four types by location** — name polar, subtropical, cold-winter and cool coastal deserts.
3. **The greatest deserts** — rank Antarctic (14,200,000 km²), Arctic (13,900,000 km²) and Sahara (9,200,000 km²), and place the Great Basin and the Gobi as…

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: People and Resources — Resource Depletion in Ethiopia, where dry land first appears as a resource problem. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a desert by its rainfall: under 250 mm a year.
2. Say why heat is not the defining mark of a desert.
3. Name the four desert types by location.
4. Rank the three largest deserts and give their areas as the book prints them.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says a desert must be hot → the two largest deserts are polar ice; the mark is rain under 250 mm a year, not temperature.
- Says two-thirds of the world is desert → the book's figure is about one-third of the land; two-thirds is the figure for Africa's land that is desert, in the next lesson.
