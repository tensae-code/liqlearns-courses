# What a Desert Is

## Do it first

Think of the driest place you know. Is it hot? Now think: could a place be bitterly cold and still be a desert? Write your guess — what makes a desert a desert — before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rain, not heat

A **desert** is a place receiving **under 250 mm of rain a year** — too little for plant growth. The defining mark is **low rainfall, not heat**. A desert can be hot sand, but it can also be ice.

About **a third of the Earth's land** is desert. Hold that number carefully: the chapter's own review questions test the trap that two-thirds of the world is desert — that is false; the book's figure is one-third.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four types by location

Deserts sort into **four types by location**: **polar deserts**, **subtropical deserts**, **cold-winter deserts**, and **cool coastal deserts**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The greatest deserts

The two largest deserts on Earth are the cold polar ones: the **Antarctic desert, 14,200,000 km²**, and the **Arctic desert, 13,900,000 km²**. Third comes the **Sahara, 9,200,000 km²**. The book's ten-greatest table runs down to the **Great Basin, 492,098 km²**, and Asia's **Gobi** is counted a cold desert.

## Say it to your companion
