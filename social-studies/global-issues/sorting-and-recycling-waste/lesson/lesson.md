# Sorting and Recycling Waste

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: saving nature's resources: the recycling station, sorting everyday waste (paper, food scraps, plastic, metal, glass, batteries) and saving electricity, as Sweden's Grade 1 teacher guide notes the textbook. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The recycling station — sorting everyday waste

Sweden's Grade 1 book ends its nature teaching at the pupil's own home: waste is sorted at the recycling station. The teacher guide's notes for the textbook's pages 60–61 name the sorts: paper, food scraps, plastic, metal, glass and batteries.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saving electricity — saving nature's resources

Sorting is taught as saving nature's resources — and so is saving electricity. The lesson joins the two: what the household throws away, sorted rightly, and what it uses, used carefully.

Sweden's own way: Geography starts from the child's own surroundings — Sweden's place on the globe, the home town, Swedish nature and its animals, and sorting the household's own waste — taught with big pictures and sorting games for pupils who read and write only a little.
