The Addis Ababa Grade 7 teacher guide is NOT edition-matched to the Amhara student books (different bureau, different year, different language of instruction); guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence, never as pupil content from the student book. The lesson therefore stands on teacher-guide evidence only, is labelled so in every row above, and counts in no country's pupil-taught acceptance. There is NO Grade 8 teacher-guide coverage for Chapter 7: the Grade 8 guide in hand holds only Units 1–3, so no teacher evidence is claimed for any Grade 8 part of this lesson. Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): The Grade 6 book is civics-led (government, elections, courts, constitution, finance ministry) but opens the primary course's widest geography: the world's continents and oceans, political and physical maps, global warming and natural disasters, human resources and the job market, worldwide transport and communication through Nassau's ports of entry, and a closing cultural comparison of The Bahamas with Africa, Australia, Brazil and Japan.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 8 Social Studies G8 T2): Term 2 is the challenges book for the Islamic world. Unit 1 (Geography) is a full environmental unit — pollution in its four kinds, global warming, waste and recycling, and the answer through sustainable development. Unit 2 mixes Geography and Civics — wars, political borders, Muslim minorities, the population explosion, poverty and illiteracy. Units 3–4 are Civics: the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, its organs and summits, its subsidiary bodies, and Kuwait's own humanitarian role. The values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Note: this book counts the OIC at 57 member states (Unit 3), while Book 5 counted 58 Islamic states — organisation membership against a geographic count. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics/human rights — Unit 2 Lesson 3's detail on the challenges facing Muslim minorities: Arakan (Rakhine) in Myanmar, whose Muslims are at least 15% of the country's people and face killing, displacement and the erasing of their identity, printed pp. 45–48; and Xinjiang in China, home of some 11.5 million Uyghur Muslims (2020 census), p. 47 (the migration cause of minority formation is placed below). Education — Unit 2 Lesson 5, the illiteracy problem: adult literacy in OIC states about 73%, Qatar highest at 97%, Kazakhstan's literacy-eradication rate 99.8% against Sierra Leone's 6% (2022 table), pp. 53–55. Civics/institutions — Unit 3, Kuwait and the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation: founded at Rabat in 1969 after the burning of Al-Aqsa Mosque, 57 member states, headquarters Jeddah, renamed in 2011; its principles and political, economic and cultural-social goals; its organs (Islamic Summit, Council of Foreign Ministers, General Secretariat, International Islamic Court of Justice); and its summits — the first (Rabat 1969), fifth (Kuwait 1987), thirteenth (Istanbul 2016) and fourteenth (Makkah 2019), pp. 60–73. Civics/humanitarian — Unit 4, the role of the OIC's organisations and Kuwait's aid: the Union of OIC News Agencies (1972, Jeddah), the Islamic Development Bank (1975, Jeddah — its project support led by transport at 40.1%), ICESCO (1982, Rabat; 40% of the world's 757 million illiterate people live in Islamic states), the Kuwait Red Crescent Society (1966) and the International Islamic Charitable Organization (1986, Kuwait; 32,000 small development projects in 27 Islamic states), pp. 78–92. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": national unity (mixed crews on the diving ships; standing together in epidemics and in the 1990 resistance), pp. 98–99; a ray of light from selflessness — the Al-Hailaq famine of 1868, which ran from the middle Euphrates to Al-Ahsa and the Gulf's eastern coast, and the two Yusufs who fed Kuwait's hungry, pp. 100–101; the home clinic — Kuwaiti women as the family's folk doctors, pp. 104–105 (the Al-Haddama flood part is placed below).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 9 Social Studies G9 T1): Grade 9 turns outward to the whole world through a single theme — the environment. Four full Geography units work it from both directions: what the environment is and how it is divided; the environment's effect on humans (where people live, what they do, state power, disasters); the human effect on the environment (ecological balance, pollution of air, water, soil and by noise); and the other environmental problems (radioactive pollution, global warming, desertification, water scarcity) with Kuwait's and the world's efforts against them. A Civics unit on law and crime follows, and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): Civics/law — Unit 5, law protects society from crime: the concept of crime and its two elements — a legal rule forbidding the act, and an act causing harm — against wrong behaviour and school infractions, printed pp. 90–92; the three pillars of crime — the legal pillar ("no crime and no punishment except by text"), the material pillar (the act actually done; the law does not punish intentions) and the moral pillar (intent against error), pp. 93–95; classifying crimes by seriousness, the worse the harm the graver the crime — drug crimes, electronic blackmail and violent assault as the gravest examples, pp. 96–98; drug crimes — use and possession defined, their effects on the individual (addiction, poisoning, death, falling school and work performance) and on society (more theft and violence, burdens on health and law services), and Kuwait's penalties under Decree-Law 159 of 2025, pp. 99–102; unit assessment, pp. 103–105. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": welcoming the guest — honouring the guest as an old Kuwaiti duty owed no return, the Amir joining the ardha folk dance at state receptions, pp. 108–109; trust and dhimma in old Kuwait — people dealing on word and honour, with few bonds and little work for lawyers, pp. 112–113; the replies in condolence — "Azzam Allah ajrakum" answered by "Ajrana wa ajrakum", mourners received for three days after the burial, pp. 114–115 (the diwaniya station is placed below).
