# Global Warming, Ozone, Waste and Biodiversity

## Do it first

Name every environmental problem you can think of in one minute — write them all. The guide counts five major ones. See how many of yours match its five before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The five problems and their definitions (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide defines environment as **the real world of living and non-living surroundings**, and lists the major environmental problems as **pollution, global warming, ozone layer depletion, waste disposal and loss of biodiversity**. Its keyed definitions: **pollution** is introducing harmful materials into the environment; **global warming** is the long-term heating of the Earth's climate system, primarily from fossil-fuel burning raising heat-trapping greenhouse gases; **waste disposal** is the collection, processing, recycling or deposition of human society's waste; **biodiversity** is the variety of life, ecosystems and living organisms.

## Sub lesson 2 — Human impacts and their harvest (teacher guide — evidence)

The keyed human impacts are **overpopulation, pollution, burning fossil fuels and deforestation** — triggering **climate change, soil erosion, poor air quality and undrinkable water**. The guide's Unit 7 review key adds the effects line: **skin cancer, breathing problems, loss of a person's acquired immunity and rising air temperature** — the harvest, in the guide's keying, of a damaged atmosphere and a warming world. ## Say it to your companion

Name the five problems. Your companion gives the keyed definition of the one you point to; swap until all five have been defined aloud.
