# Human Rights Institutions That Protect Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: human rights are inherent and irrenunciable, and states must recognise, spread, protect and guarantee them; the institutions that watch over them — Guatemala's Procurador de los Derechos Humanos, designated by Congress, and the United Nations, founded in 1945 to keep peace and security — as Guatemala's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1 "Instituciones de Derechos Humanos"

Guatemala Grade 5 Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana (2025), printed pp. 31–33, teaches: human rights are inherent and irrenunciable, and states must recognise, spread, protect and guarantee them; the institutions that watch over them — Guatemala's Procurador de los Derechos Humanos, designated by Congress, and the United Nations, founded in 1945 to keep peace and security. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 4 "Instituciones de derechos humanos y justicia"

Guatemala Grade 6 Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana (2025), printed p. 190 (same lesson first needed by Book 7), teaches: public, autonomous institutions watch over compliance with the conventions, treaties, laws and agreements on justice and people's rights — the Procurador de los Derechos Humanos and the Archbishop's Human Rights Office for rights; the Supreme Court of Justice and the Constitutional Court for justice. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Guatemala's way (Book 7) — the book's own teaching way

Guatemala's own way: the fifth-grade book is the series' citizenship book proper — a pupil meets democracy four times over (definition, citizenship, duties, participation) and every right is tied to the article of Guatemala's own Constitution that grants it.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Guatemala's way (Book 8) — the book's own teaching way

Guatemala's own way: the sixth-grade book widens the lens — democracy is traced from Athens to Guatemala's own authoritarian past, and citizenship ends at the world: Guatemala's Belize claim put to its own citizens in the 2018 popular consultation on the International Court of Justice.. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
