Guatemala Grade 5 Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana (2025), printed pp. 31–33, teaches: human rights are inherent and irrenunciable, and states must recognise, spread, protect and guarantee them; the institutions that watch over them — Guatemala's Procurador de los Derechos Humanos, designated by Congress, and the United Nations, founded in 1945 to keep peace and security.

Guatemala Grade 6 Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana (2025), printed p. 190 (same lesson first needed by Book 7), teaches: public, autonomous institutions watch over compliance with the conventions, treaties, laws and agreements on justice and people's rights — the Procurador de los Derechos Humanos and the Archbishop's Human Rights Office for rights; the Supreme Court of Justice and the Constitutional Court for justice.

Guatemala's own way: the fifth-grade book is the series' citizenship book proper — a pupil meets democracy four times over (definition, citizenship, duties, participation) and every right is tied to the article of Guatemala's own Constitution that grants it.
Guatemala's own way: the sixth-grade book widens the lens — democracy is traced from Athens to Guatemala's own authoritarian past, and citizenship ends at the world: Guatemala's Belize claim put to its own citizens in the 2018 popular consultation on the International Court of Justice.
