**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Egypt, Costa Rica, Maldives, Micronesia, Philippines, Bahamas, Bhutan, Mexico, Uganda, India).**

Uganda's own way (Uganda Excel P5 SST): P5 civics is the independent state itself — what a nation is, its symbols and how to honour them, democracy and elections in Uganda's own terms, then the government: central and local levels, the three arms, and the constitution the people made in 1995.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 P6 SST Home-Study): democracy is taught regionally — East Africans choosing their own leaders through their own parties and electoral commissions, with equality before the law as its stated fruit.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Excel P6 SST): P6 looks outward and inward at once — the East African Community's own organs and symbols beside the member states' flags, then the citizen: what citizenship is, the child's rights, what violating rights looks like (mob justice named plainly), and how elections actually run.

Mexico's own way (Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades): one combined volume per grade under the Nueva Escuela Mexicana, built as teacher-written narrative articles grouped by discipline area; civics is taught as rights-in-action — discrimination, violence and public service are each opened from the pupil's own school and community, and Mexico's pluricultural nationhood is anchored in Article 2 of its own Constitution.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 is the state year — government type, elections, courts, the constitution and citizenship are taught as one civics block closing primary school.

Maldives's own way: belonging widens in rings — me, family, school, island, atoll, country, world — and citizenship is taught through the island itself: its councilors, its roads, its flag and its crafts.
