## Sub lesson 1 — Major risks around us — mapping local hazards

Haiti's Grade 7 risk sequence opens in the pupils' own locality: a field survey of the hazards around them, sorted into natural risks and technological risks, and drawn into a local risk map — the risks are found outside first, then mapped.

## Sub lesson 2 — Haiti's way — survey your own locality first

This is a teacher sequence, written for the teacher, not handed to the pupil. In Haiti's Projet Nectar sequence risk is taught as geography first: before any definition is given, the class surveys and maps the hazards of its own environment, so the map of risk begins with the ground the pupils walk every day.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Risk is taught as geography first: pupils survey and map the hazards of their own locality, build Haiti's vulnerability factor by factor (anarchic urbanisation, deforestation, waste, poverty), read the country's disaster history from Quisqueya to the 2010 earthquake, and finish by making prevention posters and safety icons for their own school from the national risk plan (PNGRD 2019-2030). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
