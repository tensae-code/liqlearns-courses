Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Indonesia's own way (Grade 11 teacher guide — evidence): a Kurikulum Merdeka teacher guide — a general part sets out the Fase F learning outcomes (Capaian Pembelajaran), Pancasila-student-profile targets per chapter, teaching strategies and assessment design; a special part then guides each student-book chapter activity by activity, with prerequisite concepts, pre-learning assessment, competence + character scoring rubrics for every activity, answer keys and reflection. Note: its Bab IV is retitled "Perubahan Iklim dan Kebencanaan" (climate change and disasters), widening the student book's disaster chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above beyond Indonesia's student-book count.

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — Geography is taught as Pacific peoples' lived relationship with the ocean: voyaging, reef knowledge, tapu/fono closed seasons and village fishing practice in Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue itself, ending in a pupil action plan against plastic. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing.

Nauru's own way: Nauru uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisors Fay Itaia & Sharon Kam for Nauru) — Geography is taught as ocean stewardship: the Pacific as a connected "sea of islands", its animals, and the traditional fishing knowledge of Tonga, Tuvalu and Niue, ending in a plastic-waste action plan. That way is stocked here as the way Nauru teaches this thing.

Tuvalu's own way: Tuvalu uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (local advisor Valisi Tovia for Tuvalu) — and Tuvalu itself is a case study inside it: Tuvalu's own traditional fishing and bird-catching (printed p. 14) is one of the three country fishing studies. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 2 book): Geography is taught inside three thematic sections that always start from the child's family, school and province and widen to Ecuador and South America; heavy use of hazard maps (floods, landslides, volcanoes) and emergency plans from Grade 2, reflecting Ecuador's volcanic / El Niño risk profile. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Angola's own way (Grade 5): Geography opens as "geographic space" — the natural space and the human-built space (Luanda port, Benguela railway, Cambambe dam, Huambo housing) — before any physical topic; Angola's own position, borders, population figures (INE) and farm/fishery tables carry the world topics. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6): an inquiry-based combined History and Social Science course built around a class teacher character and group investigations; Geography is taught through the landform of Bengal itself — rivers, silt and hills explaining settlement, ancient states, ports and even the 1971 war — plus hands-on map-making (home-to-school route maps, a world-tour board game) and a pupil-run nature conservation club.

Colombia's own way (Insertos_sociales_05_Cartilla_02): the Grade 5 review game is a road trip across Colombia's own map — Sierra, coffee zone, Llanos capybara country, Amazon toucan country — drilling capitals, cordilleras, páramos, regional economies and biodiversity.

India's own way (Grade 8 Resources and Development): Grade 8 is a resources year, world-first but India-anchored throughout: resources and their conservation, then land/soil/water/vegetation, minerals and power, agriculture, industries and human resources — every world pattern is paired with its Indian distribution map and an Indian case study (Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad textiles, Bangalore IT, a farm in India set against a farm in the USA).

India's own way (Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): Contemporary India-II is the human/economic book, matching Part I's physical book: India's resources, forests, water, agriculture, minerals and energy, manufacturing and the "lifelines" (transport, communication, trade) — taught throughout as development-versus-environment argument, with movements and case studies (Chipko, Narmada Bachao, rooftop rainwater harvesting in the Thar) carrying the criticism.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Years 5–6 ESD Reader 1): Geography through the ocean itself — Pacific identity as a "sea of islands", reef ecology, and traditional Fijian/Pacific fishing and tabu (no-take) practice as the model of sustainable resource use.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 8 Geografija 8): Grade 8 is pure world regional geography, continent by continent (Africa, Australia/Oceania, the Americas and polar regions), each region worked through the same grid — position/relief/waters, climate/vegetation, population, economy — and the book closes with two world-problems units (rainforest loss, desert spread, water conflicts, the Aral Sea).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 5 G5 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): the Geography block is a journey ("Viajemos por Venezuela") through work and wealth — farmland and its natural supports, climate, biodiversity, relief, oil basins and minerals — always tied to how the oil economy rearranged the country's population and space.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 English): geography starts from the child's own school and home — location in the Grama Niladhari → Divisional Secretariat → District → Province hierarchy, a plan of the school garden, home-made weather instruments, and care of the home surroundings — before widening to Sri Lanka's location in the world in the final chapter.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 7 Geography 7): a dedicated English-medium Geography book built entirely on Pakistan's own physical and economic geography — the Indus plain and delta, the canal system, Rabi/Kharif crops, Sui gas, Thar coal, Khewra salt and Tarbela power — with world concepts (river courses, settlement hierarchy, greenhouse effect, mining types) taught through Pakistani examples first.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G10 Geography T1): Grade 10 Geography opens with physical geography (atmosphere, biosphere, biodiversity), then tourism and transport as Jordanian economic geography, then geographic technologies.
