**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (North Macedonia, Costa Rica, Bahamas). Sierra Leone is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 is the state year — government type, elections, courts, the constitution and citizenship are taught as one civics block closing primary school.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 10 mk-g10): the gymnasium course restarts Geography as a systems science — Theme 1 works through the four spheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) lesson by lesson, then natural disasters by sphere; Theme 2 turns to people in geographical space (world urbanisation, energy, farming, industry, transport and trade, tourism, environmental impact); Theme 3 treats the world as one global system (globalisation, transport corridors and sea straits/canals, global networks and navigation systems, international organisations, global challenges and climate-change solutions). 43 numbered lessons.

Costa Rica's own way: a geography-and-climate anthology that closes by lifting the child's own country into world governance — international treaties are defined first, then the Paris Agreement is taught point by point (the 2 °C goal, national pledges, five-year review, the Green Fund) with Costa Rica's own payment-for-environmental-services programme as the home case.

Searchable tags: international organisations, North Macedonia, Costa Rica, social studies, global-issues, Sierra Leone, Bahamas

Sierra Leone's own way (SL SS BECE JSS3, teacher guide — evidence): the whole syllabus is taught through Sierra Leone's own peoples and institutions — culture is taught ethnic group by ethnic group (Mende, Temne, Limba, Krio…: dress, music, dance, craft, secret societies), and global bodies are taught through what they have done for Sierra Leone itself (UN Special Court, Sierra Leone as the UN's 100th member in 1961).

Sierra Leone's own way (SL General Paper Class 6, teacher guide — evidence): primary civics is taught as Sierra Leone's own citizenship — the flag's green-white-blue meanings, the coat of arms, anthem and pledge, and the country's own provinces, districts and 190 chiefdoms.
