South Korea's Grade 8 Bisang Social ②, printed p. 108; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society, printed p. 249; Grade 10 Jihaksa Integrated Society 2 (2022), printed p. 114; Grade 8 Cheonjae Middle Social Studies ② (2015), printed p. 112; Grade 8 Middle Social Studies ②, Park Hyung-jun edition (2015 curriculum), Unit VI, teaches: VI.2 국제 사회의 모습과 갈등·협력, VIII.2-01 국제 갈등과 협력 및 국제 사회의 행위 주체, 세계화와 평화 2 국제 사회의 모습과 평화를 위한 노력, Ⅵ 국제 사회와 국제 정치 (국제 사회의 특성과 주체), Ⅵ 국제 사회와 국제 정치 — teaches: the face of international society — conflict and cooperation between states and other actors; international conflict and cooperation, and the actors of international society; the face of international society and the efforts made for peace in it; international society and its politics — the unit wrap-up names the logic of power, multinational firms and cooperation, under the principle of sovereign equality; international society and international politics.

South Korea's own way: the middle-school Social Studies ② volume opens with civics, not geography — human rights and the Constitution first, then the three state institutions one by one, and the full text of the Constitution of the Republic of Korea printed as an appendix for pupils to read; it closes with global-citizenship units (global problems, reducing inter-regional inequality). Jihaksa splits every section into two numbered lessons, so its civics teaching is the most granular of the three publishers — human rights alone gets five lessons (meaning/change, modern society, the constitution's role, law-abiding participation, problems and solutions), and its justice unit ends on the institutions and practical plans for a just society. the 2022 curriculum splits Integrated Society into two volumes; volume 2 is the civics-heavy one — human rights and the constitution, justice and inequality, then globalisation and peace — and it closes by naming the pupil's own role outright: "life as a global citizen in the future society" (세계시민으로서의 삶). Its enrichment boxes (사회 고수 되기) add the proportionality principle in limiting basic rights, redistributive justice for the socially weak, and the death penalty debated as a human-rights question. this Cheonjae edition reorganises the same national frame into twelve units of its own — human rights get a whole unit that ends in remedies (국가인권위원회, 헌법 소원) and labour rights, and the book closes with 더불어 사는 세계 ("the world we live in together"): hunger, biodiversity, appropriate technology and fair trade as the pupil's own global agenda. a second Cheonjae author team's telling of the same frame — its Unit I wrap-up drills the exact constitutional mechanics of rights (restriction only by law, for national security, order or public welfare, never touching the essential content), and its labour pages speak directly to teenagers: young workers hold the same legal protections, and wages, hours, rest and leave are guaranteed by statute.
