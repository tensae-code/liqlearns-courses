---

By the UNCCD definition, desertification is human-induced land degradation in dry and sub-humid regions — the IPCC's land report is aligned with it. It is not natural deserts expanding; it is productive dryland losing its life where it stands, and it can only occur on land vulnerable to it. The world woke to it at the 1977 UN Conference on Desertification in Nairobi, after the early-1970s Sahel drought. The drylands at risk cover about 5.45 billion hectares of arid, semi-arid and sub-humid land — 47% of the land in the World Atlas of Desertification accounting the book cites. Roughly a quarter of the world's land is already degraded, and up to 3 billion people are exposed. Mapping the vulnerability is hard because the processes are multiple and complex — no single line on a map can hold them.
