Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 140–142), teaches: Sustainability means using the environment so enough resources are left for future generations — reduce, reuse, recycle — and a class environmental action plan against soil erosion (plant trees, keep drains clean) and land pollution (recycle waste). ## Sub lesson 5 — Guyana's Environmental Protection Agency

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 97–98 (NEW slug first proposed in Book 8 above)), teaches: Guyana's National Environmental Action Plan (NEAP, from 1994) protects the environment while considering social and economic well-being, and the Environmental Protection Agency (E.P.A) was established under the Environmental Protection Act of 1996 with four functions — natural resource management, environmental management through regulations, standards, quality control, monitoring, enforcement and permits, information, education and awareness, and co-ordinating environmental activities across institutions. ## Sub lesson 6 — Possible solutions to pollution problems

Guyana Grade 9 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 3 (printed pp. 104–105), teaches: Pollution problems can be reduced if not completely resolved — update old laws and regulations, enforce the rules and deal severely with repeated offences, make the public aware through the mass media, monitor regularly, and require community efforts: suitable dump sites and regular clearing of garbage, factory filters, set hours for machinery and loud music, treated chemical and sewage waste, and greater use of solar and wind energy, organic farming and biological pest control. ## Sub lesson 7 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: the newest ministry series teaches citizenship from the child's own roles outward — family duties, community roles, youth responsibilities at home and across the Caribbean, then power, privilege and human rights in plain language, with Guyanese and Caribbean case studies (Annette Arjoon-Martins, Mia Mottley) and a value component woven into every unit. the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught. the secondary book teaches the same citizenship at national scale — CARICOM as the region's own institution, the family as society's oldest institution, women's equality written into the 1980 Constitution and the Acts of the 1990s, and the state answering pollution through the Environmental Protection Agency.
