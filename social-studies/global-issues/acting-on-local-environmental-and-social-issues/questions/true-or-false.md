# Acting on Local Environmental and Social Issues — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: Governments, organisations and individuals act on current issues — the Guyana Mangrove Restoration Project (Government of Guyana and the European Union), environment management through regulations and monitoring, and community foundations such as the Linden Care Foundation.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: Making a plan of action against negative social, economic and environmental issues — sustainable solutions to poverty and small-loans programmes, community resource centres, action on juvenile delinquency, recycling programmes with local authorities, replanting trees against deforestation, and an action plan to reduce child abuse.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: Mining and logging have changed the Amerindian environment — mercury from gold mining pollutes the rivers, disease and lost soil richness follow, and communities are displaced from the land they depend on.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson's fourth part teaches: Sustainability means using the environment so enough resources are left for future generations — reduce, reuse, recycle — and a class environmental action plan against soil erosion (plant trees, keep drains clean) and land pollution (recycle waste).
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** This lesson's fifth part teaches: Guyana's National Environmental Action Plan (NEAP, from 1994) protects the environment while considering social and economic well-being, and the Environmental Protection Agency (E.P.A) was established under the Environmental Protection Act of 1996 with four functions — natural resource management, environmental management through regulations, standards, quality control, monitoring, enforcement and permits, information, education and awareness, and co-ordinating environmental activities across institutions.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** This lesson's sixth part teaches: Pollution problems can be reduced if not completely resolved — update old laws and regulations, enforce the rules and deal severely with repeated offences, make the public aware through the mass media, monitor regularly, and require community efforts: suitable dump sites and regular clearing of garbage, factory filters, set hours for machinery and loud music, treated chemical and sewage waste, and greater use of solar and wind energy, organic farming and biological pest control.
**Answer: True**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on Guyana's student book, named in lesson/sources.md.**
