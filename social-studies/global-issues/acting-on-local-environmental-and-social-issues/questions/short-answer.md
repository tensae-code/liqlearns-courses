# Acting on Local Environmental and Social Issues — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Ways to combat local, environmental and global issues — governments, organisations and individuals act on current issues — the Guyana Mangrove Restoration Project (Government of Guyana and the European Union), environment management through regulations and monitoring, and community foundations such as the Linden Care Foundation (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: A plan of action — making a plan of action against negative social, economic and environmental issues — sustainable solutions to poverty and small-loans programmes, community resource centres, action on juvenile delinquency, recycling programmes with local authorities, replanting trees against deforestation, and an action plan to reduce child abuse (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Changes harming Amerindian communities — mining and logging have changed the Amerindian environment — mercury from gold mining pollutes the rivers, disease and lost soil richness follow, and communities are displaced from the land they depend on (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Sustainability and an environmental action plan — sustainability means using the environment so enough resources are left for future generations — reduce, reuse, recycle — and a class environmental action plan against soil erosion (plant trees, keep drains clean) and land pollution (recycle waste) (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does the fifth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Guyana's Environmental Protection Agency — Guyana's National Environmental Action Plan (NEAP, from 1994) protects the environment while considering social and economic well-being, and the Environmental Protection Agency (E.P.A) was established under the Environmental Protection Act of 1996 with four functions — natural resource management, environmental management through regulations, standards, quality control, monitoring, enforcement and permits, information, education and awareness, and co-ordinating environmental activities across institutions (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** What does the sixth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Possible solutions to pollution problems — pollution problems can be reduced if not completely resolved — update old laws and regulations, enforce the rules and deal severely with repeated offences, make the public aware through the mass media, monitor regularly, and require community efforts: suitable dump sites and regular clearing of garbage, factory filters, set hours for machinery and loud music, treated chemical and sewage waste, and greater use of solar and wind energy, organic farming and biological pest control (any close wording).**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way (any close wording).**
