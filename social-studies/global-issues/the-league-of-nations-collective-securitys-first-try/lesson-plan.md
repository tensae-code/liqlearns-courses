# Lesson plan — The League of Nations — collective security's first try

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** The League of Nations — collective security's first try
**Place in the curriculum:** Global Issues — the world organisations thread; the Grade 10 book's League lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** founded in 1919 after the First World War, the League of Nations tried to keep peace by collective security — all members defending any member attacked — and failed its great test in the 1930s, Ethiopia's unanswered plea among them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the First World War lessons — the League was the peace the war's victors designed. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Founded 1919 — peace by collective security** — the League's founding, its aim of peace through collective security and negotiated diplomacy, and its welfare work.
2. **The failure — aggression unanswered** — the League cannot stop the Axis aggressions of the 1930s; Ethiopia's plea is the book's own case; the second war it existed to prevent arrives.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What WWI changed — empires down, America and communism up (the Versailles settlement). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the League's founding year — 1919 — and its purpose.
2. Explain collective security in one sentence.
3. Tell how the League failed in the 1930s, with Ethiopia as the case.
4. Say what succeeded the League after 1945.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
One idea holds the lesson: collective security means an attack on one is an attack on all. The League failed when the great powers would not act on that idea — Ethiopia 1935 is the proof the book itself gives.
