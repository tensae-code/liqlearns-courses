# The League of Nations — collective security's first try

## Do it first

In 1936 an emperor stood before the assembled nations in Geneva and warned them: what Ethiopia suffers today, you will suffer tomorrow if this goes unanswered. The hall applauded. Nothing was done. This lesson is about the organisation that listened — and about why listening was all it could do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Founded 1919 — peace by collective security

The League of Nations was founded in 1919, in the settlement that ended the First World War. Its idea was new in the world's history: instead of balance-of-power alliances — the trap that had detonated 1914 — peace would be kept by collective security. An attack on any member would be treated as an attack on all; the members together would face the aggressor down, and disputes would be settled by negotiation before they became wars.

The League also worked beyond war: it sought to improve global welfare — health, labour conditions, the care of refugees and the fight against disease and trafficking. In these quieter fields it did real good, and its machinery became the model its successor would inherit.

## Sub lesson 2 — The failure — aggression unanswered

The test came in the 1930s, and the League failed it. When Fascist Italy invaded Ethiopia in 1935, Ethiopia appealed to the League exactly as the system promised — and the League's leading members, unwilling to risk war or lose Italy as an ally against Germany, answered with half-measures. The Emperor's plea met, in the Grade 10 book's own words for the invasion lesson, deaf ears. The occupation proceeded.

Ethiopia was not the only unanswered aggression of the decade; the pattern repeated until the conclusion was unavoidable: the League had failed its principal goal. It did not prevent the next world war. When that war ended, the world built a successor — the United Nations — on the League's model but, it hoped, with stronger hands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: founded 1919; the idea — collective security; the test — Ethiopia 1935; the verdict — failed its principal goal. Then name its successor.
