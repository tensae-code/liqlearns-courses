# Land degradation and poisoned soils

## Do it first

Degradation is not one event; it is a trend — the land getting worse year by year under human pressure.

## Sub lesson 1 — Land sliding downhill in condition

Land degradation is a negative trend in land condition from direct or indirect human causes. It is among the world's most pressing environmental problems, affecting about 3.2 billion people — especially rural communities whose living is the land itself. It feeds back into climate change through released carbon, as degraded soils and vegetation give up what they stored. The answers named are GEF programmes and voluntary Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN) targets built into planning: lose no more good land than you restore.

## Sub lesson 2 — Poison in the soil

Soil contamination is the presence of harmful substances in soil — disposed of directly, or arriving via polluted water and air. The book's figure names discarded dry-cell batteries as the most common contaminant. Contamination harms plants, animals and humans, and susceptibility varies by age and general health — the young and the already unwell are hit first and hardest.
