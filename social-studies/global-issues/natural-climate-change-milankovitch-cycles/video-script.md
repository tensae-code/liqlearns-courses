---

Climate can be changed from outside by three levers: the incoming solar radiation, the atmosphere's composition, and the Earth's surface — and from inside by circulation patterns that redistribute energy. The Milankovitch theory ties the ice ages to three slow orbital cycles acting together: eccentricity, the orbit's shape stretching and rounding; precession, the axis wobbling like a spinning top; and obliquity, the axis tilt leaning more or less. Together they vary the solar energy the Earth receives and pace the alternation of glacial and interglacial times. A feedback is a change in one element that alters many others, which then alter the first again. Melting ice, for example, exposes darker ground, which absorbs more heat, which melts more ice. Feedbacks make the climate system hard to read whole: you cannot follow one thread alone. The book also notes that today's forcing sits outside the natural Milankovitch cycle's range — the present warming is faster and differently caused than the slow orbital pacing of the ice ages.
