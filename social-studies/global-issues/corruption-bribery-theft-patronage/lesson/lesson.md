## Sub lesson 1 — The concept

Corruption is the abuse of public position for private gain. The book analyses it in a North–South frame: it predominantly affects states, particularly in the Global South — but it implicates both North and South, as payer and as receiver.

Every type that follows is a form of the same abuse: a public trust converted into private benefit.

[Picture 22443 — Three types of abuse]

## Sub lesson 2 — The three types — and the scales

First, bribery — the most familiar type: payments to sway administrative decisions, reaching across the whole scope of government. Second, theft of public assets: unilateral embezzlement by officials, or organised 'state capture' — with kick-backs as the bribery form of asset theft.

Third, patronage: favours distributed through loyalty networks, rewarding connection over merit. And corruption runs at two scales: petty — small, everyday abuses; and grand — theft and capture at the top.
