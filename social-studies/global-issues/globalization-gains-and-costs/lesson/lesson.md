# Globalization — gains and costs

## Do it first

Name three things in your home that come from other countries, and one thing from your area that goes to the world. That web is globalization — now weigh it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The connected world

Globalization is the deepening worldwide interconnection of production, trade, finance, culture and media — the book's Figure 5.1 draws it as a network of global interaction. The process is centuries old, but it has now accelerated far beyond its earlier pace.

Its carriers are multinational corporations, companies operating across many countries at once. Its politics are framed as Global North against Global South — the wealthy industrial world and the poorer world it trades with, invests in and buys from.

## Sub lesson 2 — The weighing

The section's task is the weighing its outcomes demand. On one side: expanded production of goods and services, wider markets, and cultural exchange across the whole network.

On the other side: dependency, unequal gains — the network's rewards pooling at one end — and the charge that economic globalization is a new form of imperialism, rule by market instead of by flag. The book teaches both sides; the weighing is the learner's work.
