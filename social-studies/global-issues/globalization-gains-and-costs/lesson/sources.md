**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Costa Rica, Bolivia, Samoa, Colombia, Malaysia, North Macedonia, Cambodia, Fiji, Sudan, Syria, Venezuela). Haiti is named here as teacher-sequence evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography begins as a daily practice, not a chapter: pupils draw the landscape around their school, the plan of their quarter and Haiti with their school placed on it, read landscape forms and functions from their own croquis, then trace one everyday object (a coffee cup, a phone) from raw material to shop shelf to see the world from the local. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error in the placements for this lesson is taught here. The definition and the weighing are taught exactly as the placements frame them. Stacking note: the standing Global Issues lesson on Ethiopia in a connected world carries the home case; this is the Grade 12 world statement — nothing overwritten.

Costa Rica's own way: the CONED 11 anthology opens with a Geography unit on globalisation read "from the geographic viewpoint" (spatial flows of production and consumption, global cities, space-time compression), then turns to Costa Rica's own 20th-century history; its commercial workbook twin, Porras y Gamboa's *Estudios Sociales 11*, teaches the same ministry programme as a fixed cycle (problem question, basic concepts, my previous ideas, widening my knowledge, I apply my knowledge, problem-based learning, exam preparation) with heavy map-work and Costa Rica-specific data: ICE internet-coverage map, services-export league table, Costa Rica's geo-strategic bridge position between North/South America and the Caribbean. That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 12, Sexto Año): The final year opens with the census itself — the censista's work as civic-geographic practice — and later teaches geopolitics critically: Haushofer and geopolitical thought, geographic scales, and natural resources as a product of human knowledge (Sunkel), alongside Bolivia's own resource politics: the Gas War, hydrocarbon nationalisation and the urea plant that industrialises gas.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Geography): The final-year course is hazard- and water-centred for an island nation — volcanic eruptions and tropical cyclones (including Cyclone Heta on Niue), the hydrological cycle and water management, then atmosphere and climate change with Pacific consequences.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado9 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria Grade 9 guide is organised as questions about campesino life — globalisation, why a resource-rich country has poverty, how Afro-descendant, indigenous and campesino communities face environmental change, and how rural communities administer and defend their territory.

Malaysia's own way (MY T3 Geografi Tingkatan 3): Form 3 turns from skills (tables, graphs, pie charts) to living Malaysia — its natural vegetation and wildlife and the human pressure on both — then outward to the world's biomes, resources and economic cooperation, closing with forest management and recycling.

Malaysia's own way (MY T5 Geografi Tingkatan 5): the final form is the world's systems book — advanced graphs and photo interpretation, then weather and climate at world scale, vegetation and wildlife by climate zone, energy, and the environmental bill for economic activity.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 10 mk-g10): the gymnasium course restarts Geography as a systems science — Theme 1 works through the four spheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) lesson by lesson, then natural disasters by sphere; Theme 2 turns to people in geographical space (world urbanisation, energy, farming, industry, transport and trade, tourism, environmental impact); Theme 3 treats the world as one global system (globalisation, transport corridors and sea straits/canals, global networks and navigation systems, international organisations, global challenges and climate-change solutions). 43 numbered lessons.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 12 Geography): the final Geography year is economic geography — Cambodia's population, inequalities, farm/industry/energy/trade/tourism economy and urbanisation first, then the world's economic poles studied one by one: the USA, Japan and the European Union.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 8 Social Science): Year 8 turns fully outward — "Exploring the World": world climate zones read from Fiji's tropical position, then global warming, resource management (including money and time) and globalisation from a Fiji/Pacific viewpoint.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 6 G6 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): the whole Geography block is one argument — "Nuestro espacio geográfico en tiempos del petróleo": how oil remade population (2011 census), cities (metropolisation), industry location, internal migration and landscapes; learners start by learning how to *look* at a piece of oil-era Venezuela.
