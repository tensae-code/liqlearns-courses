Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography begins as a daily practice, not a chapter: pupils draw the landscape around their school, the plan of their quarter and Haiti with their school placed on it, read landscape forms and functions from their own croquis, then trace one everyday object (a coffee cup, a phone) from raw material to shop shelf to see the world from the local. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error in the placements for this lesson is taught here. The definition and the weighing are taught exactly as the placements frame them. Stacking note: the standing Global Issues lesson on Ethiopia in a connected world carries the home case; this is the Grade 12 world statement — nothing overwritten.

Costa Rica's own way: the CONED 11 anthology opens with a Geography unit on globalisation read "from the geographic viewpoint" (spatial flows of production and consumption, global cities, space-time compression), then turns to Costa Rica's own 20th-century history; its commercial workbook twin, Porras y Gamboa's *Estudios Sociales 11*, teaches the same ministry programme as a fixed cycle (problem question, basic concepts, my previous ideas, widening my knowledge, I apply my knowledge, problem-based learning, exam preparation) with heavy map-work and Costa Rica-specific data: ICE internet-coverage map, services-export league table, Costa Rica's geo-strategic bridge position between North/South America and the Caribbean. That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 12, Sexto Año): The final year opens with the census itself — the censista's work as civic-geographic practice — and later teaches geopolitics critically: Haushofer and geopolitical thought, geographic scales, and natural resources as a product of human knowledge (Sunkel), alongside Bolivia's own resource politics: the Gas War, hydrocarbon nationalisation and the urea plant that industrialises gas.
