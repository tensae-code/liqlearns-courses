## Sub lesson 1 — Symbols of the East African Community

Uganda Excel P6 SST (printed p. 17), teaches: the EAC flag, anthem, constitution, motto and emblem; the anthem sung at assemblies, official ceremonies and parliamentary sessions, promoting regional unity. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Symbols of the East African Community

Uganda Shine P6 SST (printed p. 20), teaches: the EAC flag (six colours, blue for Lake Victoria, the rest taken from member states' flags) and where it is raised; the motto “One People One Destiny”; and the EAC anthem, a hybrid song from the three founding countries' composers. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Uganda's way — the book's own teaching way (Uganda Excel P6 SST)

Uganda's own way: P6 looks outward and inward at once — the East African Community's own organs and symbols beside the member states' flags, then the citizen: what citizenship is, the child's rights, what violating rights looks like (mob justice named plainly), and how elections actually run. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Uganda's way — the book's own teaching way (Uganda Shine P6 SST)

Uganda's own way: a second publisher teaches the same P6 civics — the EAC's organs (Secretariat at Arusha, Court of Justice) and symbols, then each member state's flag and arms and why symbols matter.
