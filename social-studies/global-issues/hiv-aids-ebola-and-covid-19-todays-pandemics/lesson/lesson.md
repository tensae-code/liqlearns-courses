## Sub lesson 1 — HIV/AIDS and Ebola

HIV/AIDS was first identified in 1981. The virus weakens the immune system by destroying t-cells; it was first observed in America, and treatment has slowed it but not cured it. About 35 million deaths have followed since its discovery, and the book's Figure 5.7 maps adult HIV prevalence in 2011.

Ebola's index case was an 18-month-old boy in a Guinean village in December 2013, believed infected by bats; Figure 5.8 maps its African spread from that single village outward.

## Sub lesson 2 — COVID-19 — and the geographic lesson

COVID-19 is the most recent of the three crises — the pandemic every living learner has lived through.

Together the three teach the unit's closing point: health crises are geographic events, with origins, routes, maps and socioeconomic consequences — not only medical ones. Where a disease starts, how it travels, and whom it hits hardest are geography questions, and this course has trained you to ask them.
