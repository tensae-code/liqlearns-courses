# Living Things Depend on Each Other

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: ecosystems and interdependence — how living things depend on each other, in a bush ecosystem where fruit bats depend on the trees that feed them, as Nauru's Grade 5 *People and the Natural Environment in the Pacific* teaches it on printed p. 2. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ecosystems and interdependence

The natural environment is not a pile of separate things: it is ecosystems, where living things depend on each other. The reader's bush ecosystem makes it plain — fruit bats live from the trees that feed them, and the trees' life is bound up with the bats; change one part and every part feels it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Teaching interdependence — the teacher guide's notes (teacher guide — evidence)

This part stands on the teacher guide, written for the teacher, not handed to the pupil. Its notes for use of Book 3 set out the ecological, socio-cultural and economic pillars of living sustainably with the land, and build activities on the bat ecosystem — interdependence is taught by doing, around the same bats and trees.

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. And the guide's way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide teaches the readers through Pacific philosophy (head, heart and hands), reflection points on real reef degradation (Funafuti, Tuvalu), elder interviews, and a take-action project cycle, with formal-curriculum links country by country. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
