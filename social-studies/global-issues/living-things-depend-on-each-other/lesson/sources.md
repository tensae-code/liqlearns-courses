**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Nauru, Tuvalu, Niue, Angola). The teacher guides are cited as evidence for the teaching-notes parts, counted nowhere beyond the student books' countries.**

Niue's own way: Niue uses the shared Pacific ESD reader suite (Niue advisor Birtha Togahai) — land Geography is taught through Pacific farming itself: taro, pulaka and the coconut "tree of life", with Niue's own crops, exports and name-story ("behold the coconut") as anchor examples, and shifting cultivation/fallowing taught as sound science. That way is stocked here as the way Niue teaches this thing. And the guide's way (teacher guide — evidence): its "Links to formal learning" section carries a dedicated Niue chapter (what sustainability means for Niueans; Niue curriculum links to SDG 4.7).

Nauru's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — the natural environment, ecosystems and resources are taught through two Pacific case-study plants (taro/pulaka and the coconut, the "tree of life") across Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu, then traditional farming is set against modern farming's damage. And the guide's way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide teaches the readers through Pacific philosophy (head, heart and hands), reflection points on real reef degradation (Funafuti, Tuvalu), elder interviews, and a take-action project cycle, with formal-curriculum links country by country. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

Tuvalu's own way: the land-side companion to the sea reader — and Tuvalu's own pulaka pits and coconut toddy/biofuel are two of its case studies, including pulaka's vulnerability to drought, heavy rain and salt water as the climate changes. That way is stocked here as the way Tuvalu teaches this thing. And the guide's way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide's reef-degradation reflection point is Tuvalu's own Funafuti lagoon (Fongafale islet) — urbanisation, population density, nutrient and rubbish pollution and overfishing on Tuvalu's main atoll.
