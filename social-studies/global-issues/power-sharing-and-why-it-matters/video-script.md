The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

South Sudan Primary 8 P8 Social Studies SB, printed p. 63, teaches: power sharing is the distribution of power among the legislature, executive and judiciary; it reduces conflict between social groups, ensures stability and participation, gives minorities a fair share and provides checks and balances — in South Sudan through an all-inclusive cabinet of different tribes and genders; its methods are autonomy (symmetrical and asymmetrical federalism) and the group building-block (consociational) approach with devolution to ethnic groups.

South Sudan's own way: The final primary book is a civics of unity for a country at war with itself — power sharing is taught from the 2016 Transitional Government of National Unity cabinet photograph, the constitution's Section 33 equality clause is quoted in full, and Kenya's 2007–08 crisis and Kofi Annan's mediation are the worked example of how a neighbour's unity process shapes South Sudan's own.
