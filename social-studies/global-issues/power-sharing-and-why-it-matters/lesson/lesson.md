# Power Sharing and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: power sharing is the distribution of power among the legislature, executive and judiciary; it reduces conflict between social groups, ensures stability and participation, gives minorities a fair share and provides checks and balances — in South Sudan through an all-inclusive cabinet of different tribes and genders; its methods are autonomy (symmetrical and asymmetrical federalism) and the group building-block (consociational) approach with devolution to ethnic groups — as South Sudan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.
