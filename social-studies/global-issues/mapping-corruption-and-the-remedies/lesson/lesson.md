# Mapping corruption — and the remedies

## Do it first

Guess which countries you think score cleanest against corruption. Write three names. Two of the book's four may surprise you — check at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — The map of scores

Transparency International's scores let corruption be mapped. Only a few countries score clean — Canada, Singapore, Australia and New Zealand are named. Most of the world sits at moderately high corruption.

The worst scores concentrate in parts of Africa and the Middle East, in East, South and Southeast Asia (Japan, South Korea and Singapore excepted), and in totalitarian states — Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan and North Korea are named. The book's Table 5.3 distributes the world's population by their state's corruption degree, and Figure 5.5 maps the 2016 scores.

## Sub lesson 2 — Causes, consequences, remedies

A map alone does not fix anything, so the section closes analytically: with the causes of corruption, with its consequences for the states it weakens, and with the remedial measures against it.

The geography matters for the remedy: where corruption concentrates, and what the clean few do differently, are the two facts any honest remedy must start from.
