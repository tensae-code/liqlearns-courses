Kuwait's own way (Grade 9 Term 2): The last Kuwaiti schoolbook widens citizenship to the planet — a Kuwaiti pupil is taught global citizenship with Kuwaiti evidence (women's rights under Kuwait's own practice, Kuwait as the model of cultural cooperation, a WHO prize named for its late Amir), then the machinery of world order — international relations, world organisations, the UN — and finally the future's challenges, where identity and bullying sit beside poverty as things a citizen must face.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 1, Term 2) (teacher guide — evidence): term 2 completes the widening circles — after self, family and neighbourhood come the homeland (its flag raised every morning, its Sultan named with a prayer, its Renaissance told as one man's building) and then the whole world, where the Omani child is taught that difference itself is what makes a good citizen. That evidence is stocked here as the way Oman teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 4, Term 2) (teacher guide — evidence): the primary civics course ends at the ballot box — a nine-year-old in Oman learns to elect who represents her, to be an active member of her community, and that her vote is an أمانة, a trust, given four full periods; then the same word — trust — is carried to the whole planet. That evidence is stocked here as the way Oman teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 2, Term 2) (teacher guide — evidence): the world unit is taught as three verbs of the heart — communicate, interact, empathise — while the homeland unit grounds belonging in heritage, folk tales and the Omani personalities who shaped history. That evidence is stocked here as the way Oman teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
