Jamaica's own way (Grades 7–9 Social Studies, curriculum guide — evidence): one guide carries all three secondary grades, each year mixing Civics, heritage and a strong resources-and-environment Geography spine — natural and human resources, movements of the Earth, Caribbean hazards, climate change, green technology and tourism — taught through Jamaican case material (bauxite communities, Caribbean disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

There is NO Grade 8 teacher-guide coverage for Chapter 7: the Grade 8 guide in hand holds only Units 1–3, so no teacher evidence is claimed for any Grade 8 part of this lesson. Held as printed: the book's CFC wording elsewhere in the Grade 8 book (its §1.3.1, 'carbon emission (CFC)') is a flagged error — CFCs are chlorofluorocarbons, not carbon emissions — but that section belongs to an earlier chapter and is not used in this lesson; nothing here repeats it.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Geography): The final-year course is hazard- and water-centred for an island nation — volcanic eruptions and tropical cyclones (including Cyclone Heta on Niue), the hydrological cycle and water management, then atmosphere and climate change with Pacific consequences.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 13 Geography): The final year integrates everything at advanced level (plate tectonics to urban models, largely from world texts such as Waugh) and then lands it in Chapter 8, South Pacific Islands Geography — Oceania's geology, island types, development prospects and regionalism.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 12 Geography TG SBC) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 12 Geography closes with global population and mega-cities, a full migration unit, and a seven-topic climate-change unit that ends at home — the impact of climate change in PNG and the regions affected. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 7 np-g7): Grade 7 Earth science is motion-first — Earth's daily and annual motion, surface form and the factors that change it, surface form's relation to human life — then Nepal's climate, the seven provinces of federal Nepal, climate change, disasters and map work; population is taught as structure, change and management.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 7 TG np-g7tg) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide teaches the same Grade 7 course as np-g7 with globe-and-ball demonstrations of rotation/revolution, chart work on Nepal's demographic structure (age, sex, religion, caste, language distributions), and discussion-led lessons; its geography content mirrors the student book lesson for lesson, so placements repeat with guide page references. That evidence is stocked here as the way Nepal teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Nepal is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G10 Geography T2): Grade 10 Term 2 turns to the planet's problems and futures — climate change, impact assessment, sustainable development, energy — then tours the New World continent by continent.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 ES T1): the final year opens with fossil fuels and climate action, then structures and plate tectonics, closing with geological exploration — ending on uranium exploration in Jordan.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 Geography T2): the final Geography term is climate (atmosphere, elements, classifications, change), environment (components, problems, protection, Jordanian ecotourism), and economic geography (production, agriculture, industry, services, development).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 4 Estudios Sociales G4): Units I–V are a full national Geography of Nicaragua — coordinates, time zones and seasons first, then the country's position, borders, political division and hydrography, and a closing landscape unit on climate, natural resources, relief by region, earthquakes and hurricanes; the second half of the book is national History. Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 CCSS Geografía Módulo G9): A pure-Geography self-study module (no History/Civics in this book): the Grade 9 world-Geography course taught continent by continent — position and political organisation, physical landscape, human diversity, production and consumption, and a closing planet-care unit on prevention and mitigation — with Nicaragua used as the home reference point throughout.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.
