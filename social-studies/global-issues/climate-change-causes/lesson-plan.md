# Lesson plan — Climate Change — Causes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Global Issues · **Lesson:** Climate Change — Causes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 14 — Global Issues (CURRICULUM.md) — the twelfth lesson of the title, after Protecting the Environment Where You Live (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** climate change is caused by heat-trapping (greenhouse) gases accumulating excessively in the atmosphere — above all from humankind's wide burning of coal, petroleum and natural gas, releasing carbon dioxide and disturbing the air's former natural state.
**This lesson's own level:** this is the Grade 8 book's opening of its Chapter 7 — a deeper return to the air the learner met in Air Pollution (Grade 7 depth, earlier in this title). The Grade 8 chapter's three issues are climate change, shrinking water and shrinking forests; the water climb is already built into the water lesson of this title, and the forest climb closes the title.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the Grade 8 chapter's three issues.
2. State the cause of climate change: greenhouse gases accumulating excessively.
3. Name the three fossil fuels and the gas their burning releases.
4. Say nature's contribution — volcanoes and natural wildfires — and where the book puts the danger.

## If the learner is stuck
- Blames volcanoes as the main cause → the book grants nature a contribution, then stresses the human-made part as the danger: wide fossil-fuel use releasing carbon dioxide.
- Drops 'excessively' → gases in the air are not new; the cause is their accumulating excessively and disturbing the air's former natural state.
