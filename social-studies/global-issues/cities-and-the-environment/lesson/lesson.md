# Cities and the environment

## Do it first

By 2045 six billion people will live in cities. Growth that cleans a rich city first dirties a poor one. Where does the harm land hardest inside a fast-growing city?

## Sub lesson 1 — The urban majority grows

Over 50% of the world is already urban, and the urban population will grow 1.5 times to 6 billion by 2045. Urbanisation follows the same rise-then-fall pollution pattern as the Kuznets curve — but its early stages strain everything at once.


[Picture 25305 — an informal settlement under haze, clean technology at its edge]
## Sub lesson 2 — Strain, concentrated on the poor

Early urban growth strains water, sanitation, housing, power and transport past capacity. Congestion, vehicle and industrial emissions and smoky household stoves concentrate the harm on the urban poor in slums and informal settlements, whose malnutrition and poor services lower resistance to environmental hazards.

## Sub lesson 3 — The offered direction — clean technology

The book's offered direction: clean technologies that by design pollute less and use resources more efficiently. Not cleaning up after growth, but growing differently from the start.
