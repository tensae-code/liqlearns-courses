South Korea's Grade 8 Bisang Social ②, printed p. 208; Grade 8 Cheonjae Middle Social Studies ② (2015), printed pp. 200–214; Grade 8 Middle Social Studies ②, Park Hyung-jun edition (2015 curriculum), Unit XI, teaches: XI.3 국토 통일과 통일 한국의 미래, ⅩⅠ 세계 속의 우리나라 (통일 부분), ⅩⅠ 세계 속의 우리나라 3 통일 한국의 미래 — teaches: national unification and the future of a unified Korea; why unification is needed and how life and the land change after it — continental land routes and a developed unified Korea; the need for unification and the future of a unified Korea — changes in daily life after unification.

South Korea's own way: the middle-school Social Studies ② volume opens with civics, not geography — human rights and the Constitution first, then the three state institutions one by one, and the full text of the Constitution of the Republic of Korea printed as an appendix for pupils to read; it closes with global-citizenship units (global problems, reducing inter-regional inequality). this Cheonjae edition reorganises the same national frame into twelve units of its own — human rights get a whole unit that ends in remedies (국가인권위원회, 헌법 소원) and labour rights, and the book closes with 더불어 사는 세계 ("the world we live in together"): hunger, biodiversity, appropriate technology and fair trade as the pupil's own global agenda. a second Cheonjae author team's telling of the same frame — its Unit I wrap-up drills the exact constitutional mechanics of rights (restriction only by law, for national security, order or public welfare, never touching the essential content), and its labour pages speak directly to teenagers: young workers hold the same legal protections, and wages, hours, rest and leave are guaranteed by statute.
