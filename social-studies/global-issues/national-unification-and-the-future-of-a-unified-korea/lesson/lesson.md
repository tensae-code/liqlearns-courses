## Sub lesson 2 — South Korea's way — the books' own teaching way

South Korea's own way: the middle-school Social Studies ② volume opens with civics, not geography — human rights and the Constitution first, then the three state institutions one by one, and the full text of the Constitution of the Republic of Korea printed as an appendix for pupils to read; it closes with global-citizenship units (global problems, reducing inter-regional inequality). this Cheonjae edition reorganises the same national frame into twelve units of its own — human rights get a whole unit that ends in remedies (국가인권위원회, 헌법 소원) and labour rights, and the book closes with 더불어 사는 세계 ("the world we live in together"): hunger, biodiversity, appropriate technology and fair trade as the pupil's own global agenda. a second Cheonjae author team's telling of the same frame — its Unit I wrap-up drills the exact constitutional mechanics of rights (restriction only by law, for national security, order or public welfare, never touching the essential content), and its labour pages speak directly to teenagers: young workers hold the same legal protections, and wages, hours, rest and leave are guaranteed by statute. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
