# Preventing and Preparing for Natural Hazards

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: preventing and preparing for natural hazards — prevention measures, and natural risks with contingency plans — as Ecuador's Grade 2 *Estudios Sociales 2do EGB* teaches it in Medidas de prevención ante amenazas y catástrofes naturales on printed p. 52, its Grade 3 *Estudios Sociales 3ro EGB (SM 2016)* teaches it in Medidas de prevención con los fenómenos naturales on printed p. 42, and its Grade 8 *Estudios Sociales 8vo EGB* teaches it in Riesgos naturales y planes de contingencia on printed p. 99. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Prevention measures and contingency plans

Ecuador teaches preparation before the event, at every age: Grade 2 names prevention measures against natural threats and catastrophes, Grade 3 teaches prevention measures to use with natural phenomena, and Grade 8 raises the same habit into natural risks and contingency plans — a written plan for what a community does when a risk becomes an event. The through-line is one idea: hazards cannot always be stopped, but being ready can be chosen in advance.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ecuador's way — the book's own teaching way

Ecuador learns this inside its own books' way: (Grade 2 book) Geography is taught inside three thematic sections that always start from the child's family, school and province and widen to Ecuador and South America; heavy use of hazard maps (floods, landslides, volcanoes) and emergency plans from Grade 2, reflecting Ecuador's volcanic / El Niño risk profile. (Grade 3 book) the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation. (Grade 8 book) at Superior level the book turns thematic — history of work and social transformation, then Latin America and Ecuador today; Geography enters as a full cartography tema (map studies explicitly tied to Earth's formation, continents, oceans, climates, natural risks and contingency plans) and as the economic-sectors tema on Ecuador. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
