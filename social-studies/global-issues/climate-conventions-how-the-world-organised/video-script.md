# Video script — Climate conventions — how the world organised

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The timeline: the First World Climate Conference (1979) declares climate change an urgent world problem and sets up the World Climate Programme. The Toronto Conference (1988) calls for a global framework convention; the UN General Assembly takes up climate change (Resolution 43/53, 'a common concern of mankind'), and WMO/UNEP create the IPCC. The IPCC's First Assessment (1990) drives negotiations, and the Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee (1991–92) produces the Framework Convention — the treaty under which all later climate talks sit. The COP sequence the unit teaches: COP 2, Geneva 1996, recognises discernible human influence on climate; the Bonn Agreements and Marrakesh Accords (2001) set the working machinery — emissions trading, the Clean Development Mechanism, sink accounting and compliance; COP 8, New Delhi 2002, opens the implementation phase; COP 9, Milan 2003, adds CDM forestry; COP 10, Buenos Aires 2004, adds the adaptation programme. Science first, then a treaty, then machinery — that is the order the world organised in.
