# Hands-on — Food And Water Security In The Dry Gulf

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Unit 3 Lesson 5, Economic Challenges and Ways to Achieve Development: food security — the Gulf States depend on imports for their food needs (population growth, heat harming farm production, moving sand and urban expansion), so food security is fragile; water security — fresh water is a rare wealth in the Gulf States, limited by the dry desert location and the absence of fresh water sources; Unit 1 Lesson 5: among Kuwait's sustainable-development work is ending hunger — providing healthy, sustainable food and supporting local agriculture to achieve food security and reduce dependence on imports; the lesson's map shows Kuwait's agricultural areas, with the northern agricultural area and the Al-Sulaibiya farms — this extends Book 2's NEW food-and-water-security lesson; Unit 4 Lesson 5: Kuwait's model against the food crisis, despite its limited farm and water resources — the Public Authority for Agriculture Affairs and Fish Resources (at Al-Omariya, Farwaniya) supporting farmers and encouraging farming of all kinds to develop local production; a strategic stockpile of basic foods (wheat, rice, sugar and oils) to cover the people's needs in world crises; and a share in international humanitarian relief for peoples suffering famine, through the charities and the Kuwait Fund; the world's organisations work on solutions — the FAO developing smart farming techniques such as vertical farming and biotechnology to help poor states reach self-sufficiency, the World Food Programme (headquarters Rome) giving urgent food aid to regions stricken by wars and disasters, and Sustainable Development Goal 2, "zero hunger" by 2030 — this extends Book 2's NEW food-and-water-security lesson.
