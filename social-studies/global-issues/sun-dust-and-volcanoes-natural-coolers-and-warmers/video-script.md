# Video script — Sun, dust and volcanoes — natural coolers and warmers

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Solar output varies slightly — about 0.1% — with the roughly 11-year sunspot cycle. Sunspots are dark patches, yet the bright areas around the spots outweigh them, so a spotty sun is, on balance, a slightly brighter sun. These are small changes: a tenth of a percent, rising and falling in a regular rhythm of about eleven years. Atmospheric particles — aerosols from wildfires, dust storms and volcanoes — mostly cool the surface by blocking sunlight. The strongest natural cooler is a sulfur-rich eruption: its gases rise into the stratosphere and form a reflective sulfuric-acid haze that can persist for years, shading the whole planet. Nature, then, holds both a small warmer (the sun's bright phases) and a strong cooler (the volcanic veil) — and both are temporary compared with the gases humans keep adding.
