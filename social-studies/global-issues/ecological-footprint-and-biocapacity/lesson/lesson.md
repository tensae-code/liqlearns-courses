# Ecological Footprint and Biocapacity

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: what the ecological footprint is, and the ecological footprint of countries at world scale, as Costa Rica's Grade 7 *Antología CONED 7* teaches it in Unit II Tema II A–B on printed p. 94. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the ecological footprint is — and countries compared

Costa Rica's Grade 7 CONED anthology asks how much of the Earth each way of living uses up. In Unit II Tema II A–B it introduces the ecological footprint — the area of land and water needed to produce what people consume and to absorb their waste — and sets it against biocapacity, what the Earth can renew. The lesson then compares the footprint of countries at world scale, so a learner can see which ways of living take more than one Earth and which live inside it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Costa Rica's way — the human footprint on geographic space

Costa Rica learns this inside its own book's way: a distance-education anthology organised around "the human footprint on geographic space" — every unit opens with a generating question and objectives, and Costa Rica is used as the home case inside world topics (tropical forest conservation, food production problems). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
