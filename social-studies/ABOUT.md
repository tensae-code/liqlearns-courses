# About the Social Studies course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this part is built from
Ethiopia's own Social Studies books for Grades 7 and 8, read in
full, page by page. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'.

## Where the course comes from

Social Studies is one world course: the Earth, its peoples and
their past are the same everywhere, and each country's way of
teaching them shows inside as examples. The first way stocked is
Ethiopia's: the books run geography → history → today's world,
activity first, and that way is kept as teaching throughout this
part of the course. Inside the course the path is organised by
**levels, not grades** — the school grades the books come from
appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the
course's organisation.

**The six-part lesson rhythm (both guides).** Every lesson in
both guides is built on the same scaffold: competencies →
contents → overview → suggested teaching aids → suggested
methods → pre-lesson preparation → presentation — the lesson
opens with brainstorming, its body runs in small-group
discussion with group presentations, and a "stabilization"
step reviews the key ideas — → evaluation and follow-up →
expected answers for the textbook activity. The recurring
methods are brainstorming (pupils' ideas listed on the board,
the teacher's points added while clarifying), explanation,
group discussion, demonstration with pictures, maps and video,
and questions-and-answers; the teacher facilitates, harmonises
and gives constructive feedback, and performance is observed
and recorded. Two orderings are the Grade 7 guide's own
evidence about the course's way: human evolution opens the
people unit, before population, and Ethiopian history is
placed after world and African history, as the capstone
history unit. These budgets and this rhythm are filed here as
the guides' pacing and method reference (teacher evidence):
they shape how this course's lessons are paced and checked,
and they are counted nowhere as pupil-taught content.

### The Grade 10 guide's unit pedagogy summaries (teacher evidence)

Filed by the History fix (2026-11-12) — the audit found these six
summaries standing nowhere. Each is the Grade 10 teacher guide's own
account of how its unit is taught (teacher evidence; counted nowhere
as pupil-taught):

- **Unit 1 (guide placement 2, printed pp. 2–19).** Six periods. The
  guide teaches capitalism **definition-first**: features first, then
  the Industrial Revolution's role in establishing the system, then
  why capitalism first emerged in Western Europe — and nationalism
  through its two unifications, the American Civil War and the
  Eastern Question, in the student book's order.
- **Unit 3 (guide placement 6, printed pp. 34–52).** **Fourteen
  periods — joint-largest budget in the course.** The guide follows
  the student book's five lessons in order (trade and cottage
  industry; the making of the modern state; external aggressions;
  the ruling elites' power struggle; Fascist aggression and
  patriotic resistance) and loads the state-making lesson with
  document-level feedback detail.
- **Unit 4 (guide placement 8, printed pp. 53–68).** Seven periods —
  **the world wars in seven periods**: the guide follows the student
  book's six lessons one for one (WWI; the October Revolution; the
  League; the worldwide crisis; Fascism/Nazism/militarism; WWII),
  with the same compare-and-contrast demand on Fascism and Nazism
  and the wars' Ethiopian echoes (the League's failure) as local
  anchoring.
- **Unit 6 (guide placement 10, printed pp. 88–114).** Fourteen
  periods — joint-largest. **Structural compression:** the guide
  teaches the student book's whole closing movement — the
  Ethio-Somalia war, foreign influences, the question of Eritrea,
  and the fall of the Derg — as **one two-period lesson (its
  §6.4)**, where the student book splits the war and Eritrea into
  §§6.4.1–6.4.2 and carries the fall in its summary and Unit 8.
- **Unit 7 (guide placement 12, printed pp. 115–128).** Nine
  periods — **the course's largest compression**: the guide teaches
  the whole of the student book's §7.1 (North, West, East/Central
  and Southern liberation, apartheid, Pan-Africanism and the OAU
  with its successes and failures — seven subsections,
  §§7.1.1–7.1.7) as **a single §7.1 lesson**, "Rise of Independent
  States in Africa". Its unit title also differs by a plural:
  "Africa Since 1960s".
- **Unit 9 (guide placement 14, printed pp. 137–150).** Eight
  periods, with the specific-systems lesson (§9.1.3) given two of
  them; the guide's contents for that lesson list the same systems
  as the student book — Mada'a and Dagu of the Afar; Jaarsummaa,
  Sinqe and Gumaa; Shimgelina; the Luwa system — framed, as the
  student book frames them, as conflict-management practices,
  indigenous laws and rights through which elders lead settlement.
  No system appears in the guide that the student book lacks.

### The Grade 9 guide's unit pedagogy summaries (teacher evidence)

Filed by the History fix — the audit found these summaries standing
nowhere (guide placements 2, 5, 7, 11, 13, 15):

- **Unit 1 (placement 2, printed pp. 1–24).** Seven periods across
  six lessons (prehistory/history 1, discipline of history 2,
  evolution 1, Africa and evolution 1, Stone Age 1, emergence of
  state 1). Signature methods: opening questions as motivation, a
  **collected set of ancient-ruin pictures** used to interrogate the

  textbook's figures as the main aids.
- **Unit 3 (placement 7, printed pp. 44–68).** **Eleven periods —
  the joint-second-largest budget.** Follow-up assignments extend
  lessons outward (the contribution of early states to today's
  Ethiopia; the nature of early Ethiopia's interaction with the
  outside world), and **grading of participation and quiz results
  is enjoined after every activity**.
- **Unit 5 (placement 11, printed pp. 88–107).** Seven periods, and
  a **structural compression**: the guide teaches the student
  book's §§5.7, 5.8 and 5.9 (intra/inter-continental relations,
  Trans-Saharan trade, early contacts) as **one lesson** (its §5.7,
  printed pp. 103–107, with activities 5.8 and 5.9 inside it).
  Methods: map of Africa, documentaries on the Trans-Saharan trade,
  **jigsaw group projects**, and a pointed classroom question — why
  Europeans considered Africans uncivilised when African heritages
  such as Great Zimbabwe show otherwise.
- **Unit 6 (placement 13, printed pp. 108–120).** Eight periods,
  with **slavery given three of the eight** — the heaviest
  single-lesson weighting in the world units. Evaluation is
  pointed: **why did Africans engage in the slave trade, and which
  part of Africa was the greatest source of enslaved people, and
  why**; pupils must sketch both the Trans-Atlantic and
  Trans-Saharan route maps.
- **Unit 7 (placement 15, printed pp. 121–144).** **Thirteen
  periods — the largest budget in the course**, marking this as the
  year's centrepiece unit. The guide follows the student book's
  eight sections one for one, including separate lessons for Gadaa,
  for Moggasa/Guddifacha, and for the egalitarian-governance
  survey.

Open with discussion questions that separate demand from mere desire or want ("do we always buy what we want?"), teach general-to-particular, and assess with a live computation — the market supply of 100 identical wheat suppliers from one supplier's Qs = 3p − 2 (the 100-identical-suppliers assessment computation).
- **Unit 3 pedagogy** (teacher placement 7; printed pp. 29–33): 8 periods, split evenly between production and cost. Cost is taught by assigning different topics to different groups to present back to the class; assessment asks pupils to state the law of diminishing marginal productivity and its implication for Ethiopia's agriculture sector — the guide's own instruction to land the theory on Ethiopian farming.
- **Unit 4 pedagogy** (teacher placement 9; printed pp. 38–46): 8 periods, two per market structure, every structure taught through the same four lenses — degree of competition, type of product, demand curve, and the price/revenue/average-revenue functions — with assessment by oral review of characteristics and sources of monopoly power.
- **Unit 5 pedagogy** (teacher placement 13; printed pp. 52–67): 10 periods, taught as a practical unit: pupils are asked whether they hold bank accounts and who in their family borrows, every section closes by working the student text's activity questions in open discussion with the teacher recording achievements, and the signature method is the bank-visit field task — the class is sent to visit banks in the town and report back what they observed.
- **Unit 6 pedagogy** (teacher placement 16; printed pp. 71–81): 9 periods. Opening framing: differences in living conditions come from differences in growth, which come from natural endowment and from efficiency (education, innovation, skill, work culture, saving culture) — under the guide's motto that poverty is not a curse and prosperity not a blessing where a committed government allocates factors well. Signature task: pupils calculate Ethiopia's growth over the past five years from real data.
- **Unit 7 pedagogy** (teacher placement 20; printed pp. 85–99): 8 periods, whose stated outcome includes pupils writing a brief report on the Ethiopian economy. Signature method: the locality price-collection deflator exercise — pupils collect prices and quantities of real goods in their own locality and compute nominal GDP, real GDP (base year 2021 in the guide's template) and the deflator themselves.
- **Unit 8 pedagogy** (teacher placement 24; printed pp. 103–112): 6 periods. Innovation is taught by making pupils associate the types with innovations and startups around them; business-plan teaching is reduced largely to the student text (the guide's Activity 8.4 key answers the components question with "please refer from the text book!"), and the section objectives repeat the student book's, including developing a business plan and undertaking cost-benefit analysis.

### The Grade 11 guide's way (teacher evidence)

**The guide's frame** (*Economics, Teacher Guide, Grade 11*, printed pp. I–II, 1, the front matter and unit openings throughout; teacher placement 1) — replacing the earlier merged pointer sentence with the full filing. The guide re-walks the student book's seven units section by section. Period budgets: Units 1–6 allotted 16, 18, 18, 16, 17 and 18 periods respectively (Unit 1's sections run 3-4-6-3 periods, the budget line taking the largest share; Unit 7's budget stands in the guide's unit openings). Its front matter sets the method for the whole grade: teacher resources (charts, flow charts, task cards, guest speakers, mentors, ICT tools), student resources, and activity formats — individual (directed question-and-answer, mind maps), pair/group (assignments, current-events items), whole-class (debates, displays, role play, and field visits/excursions to markets, economic enterprises, production sites, industrial parks and nearby universities, meeting local entrepreneurs) — under an active-learning, student-centred, constructivist approach with people-first inclusive language and no more than 10–15 minutes of teacher talk at a stretch. ### The Grade 12 (2011) guide's way (teacher evidence)

**The 2011 guide's frame** (*Economics, Teacher's Guide, Grade 12* (2011 GEQIP), printed pp. i–iii, 1, 80, 142 and unit openings throughout; teacher placement 1) — replacing the earlier merged pointer note with the full filing. **The words stand as the placement gives them: the course it serves totals 136 periods** (Units 1–8: 15, 24, 18, 18, 16, 14, 15, 16). Every section of every unit is built identically: Periods Allotted, Competency, Sub-contents, a short content Overview (the guide's real content payload), then a Teaching-Learning Process in five fixed moves — Suggested Teaching Aids (CSA and Ethiopian Economic Association reports, PASDEP documents, ministry bulletins, newspapers), Suggested Teaching Methods, Pre-Lesson Preparation, Presentation of the Lesson (introduction/body), and Evaluation and Follow-Up — and every unit closes with Answers to Activities, Answers for Content Checks and Answers to the Review Exercise. The Introduction (printed pp. i–iii) states the method doctrine of the era: a paradigm shift from teacher-centred to student-centred teaching — brainstorming to surface prior knowledge without punishing wrong answers, motivation as the teacher's primary duty, explanation that must not occupy the whole period, "powerful questions" (closed and open), students making their own notes from highlights rather than copying the board, and continuous assessment with records analysed and released to students. Two worked sample lesson plans (45-minute periods, printed pp. 80 and 142) show the template filled in. The eight-unit course it serves is the old syllabus's Ethiopian-economy course: Main Sectors of the Ethiopian Economy; Agriculture; Industry; Services; Trade; Fiscal Policy and Public Development; Monetary Policy and Financial Sectors; Economic Reform Program. All of this is teacher evidence for how Ethiopian-economy economics was taught in the 2011 curriculum — a different course from the 2023 Grade 12 book, preserved here as such. The 2011 guide is NOT the 2023 student book's companion, and nothing from it is taught as that book's answer key.

**Grade 12 (2011) guide — per-unit pedagogy summaries (teacher-guide evidence):**

- **Unit 1 pedagogy** (teacher placement 5; printed pp. 1–10): 15 periods split 4/6/5 across the three sections. Teaching aids are documentary: Ethiopian Economic Association quarterly reports, CSA reports, PASDEP and ten-year-perspective-plan documents. Signature follow-up: a three-column comparison table (pre-1974 / 1974–1991 / EPRDF plan characteristics) built when students have not understood, plus take-home assignments graded against it.
- **Unit 2 pedagogy** (teacher placement 13; printed pp. 11–32): 24 periods (sections 3/3/2/2/2/–/2/2) — the guide's largest unit. It leans on named scholarship (Dessalegn Rahmato's *The Agrarian Reform in Ethiopia*), photographs of the three farming systems as the recommended aid (Afar pastoralists, the Tendaho plantation), and locality work — students identify which production system dominates their own area and report on it.
- **Unit 3 pedagogy** (teacher placement 22; printed pp. 33–57): 18 periods (2/2/3/2/2/2/3/2). Distinctively debate-driven: students argue the five arguments for industry, list their own local firms' contributions, and are sent to explore surrounding manufacturing establishments and report their size, ownership and structure against the textbook classification.
- **Unit 4 pedagogy** (teacher placement 29; printed pp. 58–80): 18 periods (2/2/2/3/3/3/3). The unit carries the guide's first sample lesson plan (printed p. 80): a 45-minute period on the service sector in the standard grid. Field work is prescribed (visits to local service providers, the Road Transport Authority) and current-affairs aids (the *Capital Ethiopia* newspaper, MoFED and NBE reports) replace purely textual references.
- **Unit 5 pedagogy** (teacher placement 34; printed pp. 81–99): 16 periods (3/4/4/5). Interview- and data-based: students interview government bodies and elders on their locality's inter-regional trade, identify formal and informal trade activities around them, and evaluate sector performance from the student book's tables in groups; the guide's answer keys do unusual amounts of content work in this unit (Activities 5.1–5.18).
- **Unit 6 pedagogy** (teacher placement 39; printed pp. 100–114, 142): 14 periods (2/2/3/3/2, with 2 unaccounted in the unit total against the printed section allotments). Aids are fiscal documents themselves: the Ministry of Finance's Federal Budget Grant Distribution Formula to Regional States (May 2009) and MoFED bulletins; the second sample lesson plan (printed p. 142) is built on this unit.
- **Unit 7 pedagogy** (teacher placement 45; printed pp. 115–129): 15 periods (4/6/3/2). The most demonstration-based unit in the guide: a class visit to a nearby bank with group reports, the teacher presenting a 1-birr paper note beside the newly minted 1-birr coin to open discussion of denominations, and the student book's Table 7.5 interrogated figure by figure.
- **Unit 8 pedagogy** (teacher placement 49; printed pp. 130–144): 16 periods (6/5/5). Field-based again: an educational tour to financial institutions with a GDP-focused assignment, and group interviews with microfinance authorities reported back as judgements on the adjustment policy. The unit closes the guide's body with the book's reference list (printed pp. 143–144) — CSA abstracts, EEA reports, NBE annual reports, SDPRP and PASDEP documents, the 1995 Constitution, and the era's fiscal proclamations (VAT No. 285/2002, Income Tax No. 286/2002, Excise Tax No. 307/2002, Investment Amendment No. 116/98).
