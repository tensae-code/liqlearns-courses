# The trade balance — exports minus imports

## Do it first

A country's trucks carry coffee out worth Birr 80 and bring machinery in worth Birr 110. Surplus or deficit — and by how much? Compute before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — The visible balance

The trade balance (visible balance) is receipts for exported goods minus payments for imported goods — trade in things that can be seen crossing frontiers. 'Visible' is literal: goods you could watch crossing a border post.

[Picture 24072 — trucks of goods leaving and entering a border post, with two money totals compared underneath]

## Sub lesson 2 — Surplus and deficit

A surplus means export receipts exceed import payments; a deficit means the reverse. The balance's sign is a headline number for any economy — earned more from the world than it spent with it, or spent more than it earned.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ethiopia's standing example

Ethiopia is given as the standing example of an economy experiencing a trade deficit — its export receipts falling short of import expenditure. The book returns to that fact when the balance of payments widens the lens in the next lesson.
