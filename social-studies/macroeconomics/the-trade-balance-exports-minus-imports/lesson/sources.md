**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Kuwait, Cambodia, Egypt, Vietnam).**

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 12 Economic & Legal Education): the macro course is built on Vietnam's national accounts — the expenditure formula GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), GDP/GNI tables against China and Singapore, the 8.12% growth of 2022, sector-share charts 2018 vs 2022, and the 50/30/20-style family budget rule (50% essentials / 20% non-essentials / 30% saving, investment and reserve).

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Term 1, split filing): Economics at this level is the pre-oil economy remembered — the Gulf's old trades — and the single concept taught is trade itself: what the Gulf sold to the world and what it bought back, including the Musabala practice of buying now and paying the next year.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 9 Social Studies): the national economy is taught inside Geography as one whole-country lesson — sectors first, then transport, then trade, closing with a GDP data table pupils must read.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7 Term 2, egypt-placements-b.md): Africa's economies are taught sector by sector — farming, mining and energy, industry, tourism, transport and trade — each tied to sustainable development and to Egypt's place in Africa.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).
