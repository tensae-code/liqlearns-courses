**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Cambodia, Iraq).**

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 12 Economics): the final-year course turns practical — the market is studied the way a Cambodian trader meets it (consumers, prices, banks, riel exchange), and macro policy is taught as coordination: monetary policy, exchange rates and public finance fitted together, closing with foreign trade and the world trading system. This lesson is built from that book's placement: Ch. 3, Lesson 4: Public finance — teaches: public finance as its own subject — how the state raises, holds and spends public money. The lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
