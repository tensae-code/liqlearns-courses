# The saving function

## Do it first

At a very low income a household spends more than it earns. Draw where its saving would sit on a graph — above zero, at zero, or below it — before the lesson derives the saving function.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saving is income not consumed

Saving is disposable income not consumed. Substituting the consumption function into that definition derives the saving function: saving equals income minus consumption, part by part.

[Picture 24904 — a saving line starting below zero and crossing into positive territory as income grows]

## Sub lesson 2 — Starting negative — dissaving

The saving function starts negative: at low incomes the household dissaves — borrowing or drawing down wealth — to finance the autonomous consumption that continues even at zero income. Saving turns positive only once income passes the break-even level.

## Sub lesson 3 — The slope, and saving versus savings

The saving function rises with a slope equal to one minus MPC — the share of each extra birr that is saved. Keep two words apart: saving is the act, a flow per period; savings is the accumulated stock built up by past saving. Saving itself rises either by raising income or by cutting consumption.
