# Foreign railway capital and the guaranteed seven percent

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Capital exported in the imperialism phase

In the **last quarter of the 19th century**, the republican history book says, capitalism reached its **economic-imperialism phase**, and the great powers **exported their capital surpluses**.

The destinations were colonial and semicolonial areas, and the purposes are named without softening: control of **cheap raw materials** and **juicy super-profits**. Venezuela's railways were built inside that movement of capital.

## Sub lesson 2 — The State guarantees 7% a year

Venezuela's State did not merely allow the foreign railway companies in — it **guaranteed them 7% annual interest on the capital invested**.

Do the book's arithmetic: at 7% a year, the companies could **recover their capital in about fourteen years** from the guarantee alone. The risk of the railway business was carried not by the companies but by the Venezuelan State.

## Sub lesson 3 — Inflated capital — the guarantee covers that too

The book adds the abuse the guarantee invited: companies **frequently inflated the initial capital**.

Why inflate it? Because the increase was **also covered by the guaranteed interest** — a bigger stated capital meant a bigger guaranteed payment every year. A guarantee written on the companies' own figure pays them to exaggerate the figure.
