# Venezuela's monoexport economy — cacao, coffee, oil

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: cacao — the colonial export. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cacao — the colonial export

The Grade 5 book tells Venezuela's whole economic story as a succession of single exports. First came **cacao** in colonial times.

Each export crop concentrated population where it grew: cacao drew people to the colonial Costa-Montaña, the coastal-mountain region where it was produced for export. Where the export grew, there the people gathered.

## Sub lesson 2 — Coffee — the 19th-century export

In the 19th century **coffee** took cacao's place as the leading export. It gave jobs in the valleys where it grew and work in the export ports that shipped it.

The product had changed; the shape of the economy had not. Venezuela still earned its income from one product sold abroad — the pattern the book is building towards naming.

## Sub lesson 3 — Oil — one product still carries the income

From the early 20th century came **oil**, and from **1926** oil exports displaced coffee from first place. The book's verdict is the lesson's name: the product changed, but the **monoexport economy** continued.

One single product — oil — has since generated most of the national income. The book ends by asking its pupils directly whether an economy should depend on a single product. That question is the lesson.
