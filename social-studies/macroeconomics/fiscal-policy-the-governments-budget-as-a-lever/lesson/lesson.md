# Fiscal policy — the government's budget as a lever

## Do it first

A government spends and taxes. Which of its payments buys something, and which simply hands money over? Sort pensions, roads, soldiers' pay and debt interest before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — The budget as a policy tool

Fiscal policy is the government's expenditure-and-revenue policy — the budget used deliberately as a lever on the economy. Spending moves demand directly; taxes move it through households' pockets.

## Sub lesson 2 — Four kinds of spending

Government spending has four components: purchases of goods and services; transfer payments — pensions and unemployment benefits, payments with nothing bought in return; grants in aid to lower governments; and net interest on government debt. Only the first buys output directly.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where the revenue comes from

Revenue comes from personal taxes, social-insurance contributions, taxes on production and imports, and corporate taxes. Taxes move disposable income, and so consumption and real GDP — the indirect half of the fiscal lever.
