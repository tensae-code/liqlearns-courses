Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 12 Economics Class 12): the Class 12 book runs the standard macro course (national income, money, banks, public finance, trade) and then turns the same tools onto Pakistan itself for six chapters — Pakistan's sectors, national income, planning, transport and manpower, banking system, government finances and foreign trade — closing with the Islamic economic system (Zakat, Ushr, interest-free banking) taught as a full alternative system, not a footnote.


United States' own way (United States Grade 13 American Government 3e): economics appears inside civics as the machinery of public policy — goods classified by who can be excluded, and the federal budget as the arena where Keynesian ideas meet mandatory spending.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Ekonomi): national income worked in rupiah with Indonesian provincial data; cooperatives taught as the “soko guru” (main pillar) of the Indonesian economy with SHU surplus-sharing calculations; the state/regional/private ownership ladder (BUMN–BUMD–BUMS) as the map of who owns the economy.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Panduan Guru Ekonomi Edisi Revisi) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide works with real Indonesian data — provincial per-capita PDRB tables, BPS poverty lines, the open unemployment rate (TPT), provincial minimum wages (UMP) — and adds online-loan (pinjol) financial-literacy teaching. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Indonesia is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 9 Social Science student): the new-format Grade 9 book gives Economics a full theme, "State and Economy": systems first (with Smith, Marx and Keynes as the thinkers), then the market and where it fails, then Armenia's own financial system and the state's economic and social policies, closing with Lorenz/Gini inequality graphs, World Bank income groups and the SDGs.
Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Social Science student): Grade 11 macroeconomics is anchored in Armenian official statistics — learners compute with the Statistical Committee's data, meet the 425-item consumer basket Armenia uses for inflation, the Central Bank's 3% ±1 inflation target, and the Armenian-born Nobel laureate Daron Acemoglu's "Narrow Corridor" on institutions and prosperity.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 8 G8 Student CCSS): Grade 8 teaches Latin America's development problem head-on — development vs underdevelopment, the integration ladder, and the debt lesson built around Honduras's own fiscal-deficit chart and the structural-adjustment era.
