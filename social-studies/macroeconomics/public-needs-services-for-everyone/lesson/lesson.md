# Public needs — services for everyone

## Do it first

Look out of the window or picture your street. Before you read on, list the things on it that no single family could provide by itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Needs no one can meet alone

Bulgaria's Grade 4 book teaches: **public needs are people's needs that cannot be satisfied by the efforts of one single person**. Its examples are **national defence, security and safety on the roads, in the street and at home, education, healthcare and transport**. No family can pave the road, guard the border or run the fire service by itself — these needs belong to everyone together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Services the state and the municipality offer

Most public needs, the book teaches, are **satisfied through services the state or the municipality offers**: the **army, police, fire service, street signs, marking and lighting, parks and playgrounds**. The money for them is provided in the form of **taxes** — money citizens and firms must set aside from their income and profit, whether or not they have used these services.

## Sub lesson 3 — Private services you pay for when you use them

Part of the public needs, the book continues, are **satisfied by private firms' services** — **private schools, hospitals, cinemas, theatres, museums and transport vehicles** — **for which citizens pay only when they use them**. That is the dividing line of the lesson: state and municipal services are paid for through taxes by everyone; private services are paid for at the moment of use, by the user alone.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the lesson to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, define a public need and give three examples.

2. Name the services the state or municipality offers in the book, and say where their money comes from.

3. Give two private services that meet public needs, and say how paying for them differs from taxes.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
