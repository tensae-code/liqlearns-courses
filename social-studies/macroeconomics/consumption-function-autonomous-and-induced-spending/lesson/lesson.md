# The consumption function — autonomous and induced spending

## Do it first

Your family has a month with no income at all. Would spending fall to zero? List what would still be bought, and where the money for it would come from.

## Sub lesson 1 — Disposable income — what can be spent or saved

Income is either spent or saved. What households can dispose of is gross income minus personal taxes — disposable income. Keynes's consumption function makes consumption rise as disposable income rises.

[Picture 24900 — an upward consumption line starting above the origin, its intercept and slope labelled]

## Sub lesson 2 — Autonomous consumption — spending at zero income

One part of consumption does not depend on current income at all: autonomous consumption, the survival spending that continues even at zero income. It is financed from past savings or by borrowing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Induced consumption — the part income pulls up

The second part is induced consumption: the fraction of each extra birr of income that gets spent. Things that are not income — illness, or a holiday season — do not move you along the function; they shift the whole function up or down. The teacher guide works the function fully: with autonomous consumption 60 and MPC 0.8, the function is C = 60 + 0.8Y (teacher guide — evidence).
