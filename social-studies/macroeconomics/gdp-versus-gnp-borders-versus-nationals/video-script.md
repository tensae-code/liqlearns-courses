# Video script — GDP versus GNP — borders versus nationals

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Gross national product counts instead what a country's nationals produce, wherever in the world they are. The Ethiopian-owned apartments in New York are the book's example: their rent counts in Ethiopia's GNP — Ethiopians produced it — and in the USA's GDP, because that is where the production stands.

The bridge between the two measures is net foreign factor income: what citizens and companies earn abroad minus what foreigners earn at home. Add it to GDP and you get GNP — GNP = GDP + NFFI. If a country's people earn less abroad than foreigners earn inside it, NFFI is negative and GNP is smaller than GDP.

The section also fixes a distinction used everywhere in economics: a flow is measured per unit of time — so much per year — while a stock is measured at a point in time, like water in a tank on one day. GDP is a flow: it is always the production of a period, never of an instant.
