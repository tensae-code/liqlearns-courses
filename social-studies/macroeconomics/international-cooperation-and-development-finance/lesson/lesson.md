# International cooperation and development finance

## Do it first

A poor country needs money for roads, schools and a balance-of-payments gap it cannot fill at home. Before you read on, say: who in the world lends money to whole countries — and what might those lenders ask in return?

## Sub lesson 1 — The lenders — the IDB, the World Bank and the IMF

Honduras's books name the **international cooperation and financing organisms**. The Grade 7 book's Lesson 38 "Organismos cooperantes y de financiamiento en Honduras" teaches that the **IDB (Inter-American Development Bank), the World Bank and the IMF lend to Honduras**. The Grade 9 book adds the honest frame: these international economic organisms, directed by the rich countries, were **created to finance underdeveloped countries**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Loans with conditions — supervision and structural adjustment

The loans are not free of conditions. The Grade 7 book teaches that **IMF loans come with supervision of the country's economic policy**, and that **structural-adjustment plans (PAE)** have shaped Honduras's economy since the 1990s. The Grade 8 book's debt lesson adds the World Bank's side: it **lends to countries with balance-of-payments problems** — countries whose payments abroad outrun what they earn abroad.

## Sub lesson 3 — Debt forgiveness — and opening the door to foreign investment

The Grade 8 book also names the release valve: **debt forgiveness (condonación)** — a country is exempted from repaying a debt, in whole or in part. And the Grade 9 book closes the circle at home: governments **pass laws easing foreign investment**, removing tariff barriers on imports, so outside money comes in as investment as well as loans.
