# Causes of poverty

## Do it first

Before you read on, write down what you already think this lesson's question is — poverty's causes, as palestine's grade 9 book lists them — in one sentence of your own. Keep it; you will check it at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Household causes — low, irregular income, unemployment and large families

Palestine's Grade 9 book, having defined poverty against its shekel poverty lines, asks what causes it. At household level: **low and irregular income (seasonal and day labour)**, **widespread unemployment**, and **large families on limited income**. A family can be poor because too little comes in, because it comes irregularly, because there is no work, or because the same income must stretch across many people.

## Sub lesson 2 — State and regional causes — spending, debt, fragmentation, dependence and corruption

At state and regional level the causes widen: **low government spending on health and education under accumulated debt**, the **Arab world's regional fragmentation and lack of economic integration**, the **Arab economy's dependence on the West and the flooding of its markets with foreign products**, and **corruption**. Poverty here is not only a household's arithmetic; it is also a budget's, a region's and a market's.

## Sub lesson 3 — In Palestine — the occupation deepens poverty

In Palestine the occupation deepens every cause: **closures, siege, land confiscation and control of ports, crossings and trade policy**. The book's measure of the result: **about a third of Palestinians live under the national poverty line**. The causes lesson therefore ends where the pupil lives — with the controls named as causes, not as background.
