# Neoclassical economic thought

## Sub lesson 1 — The marginal revolution

In the late 1800s a new school rebuilt economics around one idea: the margin. Value and choice, they argued, are decided not by totals but by the next one — the next glass of water, the next hour, the next unit. Walras, Jevons and their fellows made this the marginal revolution.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Austrian and mathematical wings

The school spread in two wings. Pareto and Edgeworth gave it mathematics — curves and equilibria that pupils meet elsewhere in economics. Böhm-Bawerk, Wieser, Mises and Hayek, the Austrian wing, pressed the role of individual choice, time and free markets, and argued against planning the economy from above.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rebuilding on marginal choice

The lesson's summary is in its own phrase: economics rebuilt on marginal choice. The classical school's value puzzle — why water, so useful, is cheap and diamonds dear — is answered at the margin: price follows the value of the next unit, which for water is small because water is plentiful.
