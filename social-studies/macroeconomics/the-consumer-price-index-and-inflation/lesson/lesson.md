# The consumer price index and inflation

## Do it first

A family's usual shopping basket cost 300 birr in the base year and 360 birr now. Compute the price index for the basket, and the percentage rise. What would that percentage be called, in the news?

## Sub lesson 1 — Pricing a fixed basket

The consumer price index prices a fixed basket of goods and services bought by a typical consumer, and compares its cost with the same basket's cost in the base year. The book works it with a basket of 10 bananas and 3 coffees. Its sibling, the producer price index, does the same for the goods bought by firms.

## Sub lesson 2 — Deflator versus CPI — three differences

The two headline price measures differ three ways. The deflator covers all domestic production; the CPI covers only consumer purchases. Imports appear in the CPI — consumers buy them — but not in the deflator, which covers only what is produced at home. And the CPI's basket is fixed, while the deflator's basket moves with GDP's changing composition.

## Sub lesson 3 — Inflation — the CPI's growth

The inflation rate is the percentage growth of the CPI between two periods: how much faster the typical basket is getting more expensive. A CPI rising from 100 to 112 over a year is inflation of 12% — the number households feel directly, since the CPI is built from the prices they actually pay.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Ethiopian Calendar basket — the worked example

Grade 12 works the CPI on an Ethiopian basket of wheat and edible oil, priced in Ethiopian Calendar years 2000–2003 EC. With the base year (2000 EC) set at 100, basket costs of 200, 280, 240 and 400 birr give CPI values of 100, 140, 120 and 200. Check the last: 400/200 × 100 = 200. The basket never changes — only its price tag does, and that sameness is the method.

## Sub lesson 5 — The corrected oil price — 440, not 400

Read the Grade 12 book's own price table carefully: it prices 2003 EC edible oil at 24 birr, but the printed solution computes with 20 birr. With the table's 24 birr, the 2003 basket costs 440 birr, not 400, and its CPI is 440/200 × 100 = 220. Trust the table, check the solution: that is the habit this sub lesson teaches.
