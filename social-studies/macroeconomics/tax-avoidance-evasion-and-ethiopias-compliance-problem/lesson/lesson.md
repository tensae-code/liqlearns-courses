# Tax avoidance, evasion and Ethiopia's compliance problem

## Do it first

Two taxpayers pay less tax than expected. One used the law's own exemptions; the other falsified a return. Only one is a criminal. Which — and why does the legal one still hurt?

## Sub lesson 1 — Avoidance — legal, but erosive

Avoidance arranges affairs within the law — exemptions, deductions, incentives — to pay less. It is legal, but it erodes revenue: every birr lawfully avoided is a birr the budget planned on and lost.

## Sub lesson 2 — Evasion — the crime

Evasion falsifies returns or does not file at all: a crime. It is fed by an underground economy that exists both because some activities are illegal and because high marginal rates reward hiding income.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why compliance fails

Administrators face growing workloads, complex legislation, taxpayer attitudes and non-compliance, service quality and collection costs. Perceived corruption and unfairness, and low tax knowledge, depress the voluntary compliance the whole system depends on — complexity itself pushes transactions underground.
