# Cambodia's reform and development programme

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one Cambodian example of this lesson's subject from your own life — a family business, a market price, a job, or a government programme you have heard named. Say in one sentence what you already think about it. The lesson will test that sentence.

## Sub lesson 1 — A national programme

A reform and development programme is the state's plan for changing and growing the economy: what will be reformed, what will be built, and in what order. Cambodia's book closes its state-and-economy chapter with Cambodia's own programme, so the plan pupils judge is their own country's.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reform and development

The programme joins two words that are not the same. Reform changes how things are run — rules, institutions, the state's own working. Development grows what the country has — production, incomes, services. A programme that only grows without reforming can stall; one that only reforms without growing leaves people waiting.

## Sub lesson 3 — Judging a programme

Pupils meet the programme as citizens, not only as students: a plan can be read, its promises listed, and its results checked against them later. That is the book's closing move for the chapter — the state's plan for Cambodia's economy, placed in the hands of the people who will live under it and judge it.
