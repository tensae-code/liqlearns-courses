Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class X Economics X): Class X turns from concepts to Bhutan itself — the Bhutanese economy's features, public finance, GNH as the official development measure alongside GDP, Bhutan's balance of payments and rupee-crisis case material.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grades 9–10 Business Studies TG): the old OBE Business Studies course carries its Economics inside Unit 9.1 — production and trading of PNG-made goods, shell money and pigs as traditional exchange against modern money, and why domestic trade keeps money inside the country — plus a wise-consumer unit and a Taxation option taught with real IRC forms. Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 11 New Economics SS2): SS2 pairs every theory block with its Nigerian application in the same theme — wage policy and trade unions, agricultural policy and marketing, revenue allocation in the federation, the Nigerian Stock Exchange and inflation in Nigeria.

Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales CONED 7): Economics is taught inside history and geography — the Neolithic surplus as the first economy, pre-Columbian markets and tribute, then Costa Rica's own ecological footprint/ecological debt (Estado de la Nación 2016 data) and its world-famous payment-for-environmental-services (PSA/Fonafifo) forest scheme as the national case studies.

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales; Bolivia Grade 7 Texto de Aprendizaje Ciencias Sociales Secundaria 1; Bolivia Grade 8 Texto de Aprendizaje Ciencias Sociales Secundaria 2): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation. the student counterpart of the compendium's Year 1 — economics enters through the agricultural revolution's surplus and through the Andean and Mesoamerican worlds the students inherit (ayllu, calpulli, Tiahuanaco's trade). the student Year 2 lives inside the colonial extraction machine — encomienda, mita, repartimiento, obraje, tribute and monopoly — each named as a system for taking wealth out, with Potosí's silver as the engine.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 Social Studies Class 5 SNC): The Class 5 chapter deepens the Class 4 set with Pakistan's own institutions as the teaching examples — PIA, Pakistan Railways, Pakistan Television, Pakistan Post and WAPDA for public services, the State Bank of Pakistan (established 1948), and Dr Akhtar Hameed Khan's microfinance work for social entrepreneurship.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 12 Economics Class 12): the Class 12 book runs the standard macro course (national income, money, banks, public finance, trade) and then turns the same tools onto Pakistan itself for six chapters — Pakistan's sectors, national income, planning, transport and manpower, banking system, government finances and foreign trade — closing with the Islamic economic system (Zakat, Ushr, interest-free banking) taught as a full alternative system, not a footnote.


India's own way (India Grade 8 Social and Political Life-III): The government's economic role is taught through Chennai's water — who gets how much, who pays, and what happens when a basic need is left to profit — and through Bhopal, where cheap labour and weak safety enforcement are priced in workers' lives.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 7 Social Studies & Human Value Education G7): economics is taught from documents — a farmer's letter about commercial maize and lentils, a newspaper editorial on livestock self-sufficiency, a World Consumer Rights Day leaflet, a DDC milk news item — each followed by the concept the document carries.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 10 Social Studies): Grade 10 economics is the money-and-state unit, taught as documents a pupil could meet in life — a student's study report for energy, a first-person story for foreign employment, a central-bank officer's school visit for financial education, a mother's letter for insurance, a street-corner conversation with a businessman for revenue, and the Sixteenth Plan's own target table for planning.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Ekonomi): growth measured on Indonesia's own PDB series; trade taught through Indonesia's nickel-export case; budgets are Indonesia's real APBN/APBD with village funds (Dana Desa) as the regional-budget story.
