**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Guatemala, Uganda, Bahamas, Laos, Dominican Republic). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 5 Ciencias Sociales 5° Primaria): the sectors are taught twice — once as a general classification (including a quaternary sector of technology and research) and once through the Dominican economy itself, province by province and industry by industry, with larimar, tobacco and Punta Cana tourism as national examples.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 7 Ciencias Sociales 1° Secundaria): at secondary level the tax lesson becomes concrete Dominican fiscal practice — the ITBIS rate a pupil sees added at the shop till, its reduced rate and its exemptions for the family food basket, water, electricity, health and education.

Bahamas' own way: Grade 6 teaches the state's economic machinery — the Ministry of Finance suggesting, increasing and enforcing various taxes, alongside making the national budget.

No matching teacher guide exists in this band: the book recorded as this text's teacher guide proved to be the 2011 GEQIP Grade 12 teacher's guide for the old syllabus, not this book's companion, and supplies no answer keys for it (proof in the placements file's verification block).

Guatemala's own way (Guatemala Grade 4 Ciencias Sociales G4): taxes are taught as Guatemala's own payments — the municipal arbitrios such as the IUSI property levy beside the State's taxes, with the IVA at 12% as the named example.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Senior 1 Entrepreneurship Textbook (NCDC Prototype)): an Entrepreneurship textbook that ends by teaching public finance — the Senior One learner meets government revenue, taxation and toll roads (the Entebbe Express Highway) as the last chapter of learning how businesses and the state fit together; this placement keeps that way: a tax is defined inside the government-revenue chapter, as the state's most important revenue source.

Laos' own way (Laos Grade 10 Business student): Economics is taught as the theory inside a "become a businessperson" course, drawn from village life — weaving, market stalls and repair shops sorted into producing, trading and services; the circular flow is drawn between households and the village business, and "produce for whom?" is answered with a Rolls-Royce.
