# Video script — Comparative advantage — David Ricardo

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Ricardo showed that mutually beneficial trade survives even when one country has an absolute advantage in both goods: each should specialise where its advantage is greatest — or its disadvantage smallest — judged by opportunity cost.

The book reworks Ricardo's England–Portugal cloth-and-wine example as Ethiopia and Germany producing automobiles and wine. Germany needs fewer labour hours for both goods, yet its opportunity cost is lower in automobiles and Ethiopia's is lower in wine — so Ethiopia should export wine and Germany automobiles.

The teacher guide works a second case in full (teacher guide — evidence): against India, Ethiopia holds both absolute and comparative advantage in coffee (opportunity cost 0.42), while India's opportunity cost of computers (1.29 against Ethiopia's 2.4) gives India comparative advantage in computers; complete specialisation yields 240 units of coffee and 140 computers against a no-trade 210 coffee and 120 computers — a net world gain of 30 coffee and 20 computers. The theory's noted criticisms: the labour theory of value, homogeneous labour, and constant costs.
