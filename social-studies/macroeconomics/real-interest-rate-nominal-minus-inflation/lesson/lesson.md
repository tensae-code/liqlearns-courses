# Real interest rate — nominal minus inflation

## Do it first

A bank poster offers 15% a year, while prices rise 8% a year. Is the saver really 15% better off after a year? Work out the true gain before the lesson names the two rates.

## Sub lesson 1 — The nominal rate — the rate as quoted

The nominal rate is the rate as quoted, before inflation is taken into account — the number on the bank's poster. On its own it overstates what saving truly gains while prices are rising.

[Picture 24913 — a 15 percent bank poster with an 8 percent slice eaten away by inflation, leaving 7 percent]

## Sub lesson 2 — The real rate — the true cost and gain

The real rate removes inflation's effect and measures the true cost of borrowing and the true gain from saving: real rate = nominal rate − inflation rate.

## Sub lesson 3 — The worked example — 15, 8 and 7

Worked example: a nominal rate of 15% with inflation of 8% gives a real return of 15 − 8 = 7%. The saver's money grows 15% in number, but only 7% in what it can buy.
