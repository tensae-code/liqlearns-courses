## Sub lesson 1 — The free-traders' programme

Bolivia's founding trade fight opened with the librecambistas: they declared raw-metal exports duty-free, cut tariffs on foreign cloth and ended the quina monopoly — a programme serving the mining oligarchy.

## Sub lesson 2 — The protectionists' programme

Against them, the protectionists wanted state control of silver and quina exports to secure the treasury's income — the young republic's revenue depended on what its mines and bark could earn abroad.

## Sub lesson 3 — Santa Cruz the protectionist — and the fight's outcome

The Grade 10 student book names the protectionist in power: Santa Cruz was clearly mercantilist and highly protectionist — he prohibited imports of products that competed with national ones, such as tocuyo cloth, to protect local producers, while exports were incentivised through the port of Cobija. In the longer fight of the century, the compendium records, free trade won.
