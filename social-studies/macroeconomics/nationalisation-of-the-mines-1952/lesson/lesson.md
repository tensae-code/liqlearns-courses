## Sub lesson 1 — Catavi, 31 October 1952 — the decree

Under pressure from the FSTMB and the COB, formed days after the 1952 Revolution, President Paz Estenssoro signed the nationalisation decree at Catavi on 31 October 1952 — to eliminate the tin barons' 'Super Estado Minero' and, in Paz's words, stop the constant drain of the country's wealth. The COB and Marxist parties demanded nationalisation without indemnisation.

## Sub lesson 2 — COMIBOL — 163 mines, 29,000 workers

COMIBOL, created on 2 October 1952, would administer 163 mines in 13 companies with 29,000 workers producing 27,000 tonnes of tin. It became the country's largest employer, its first taxpayer (US$100 million) and its first provider of foreign exchange (US$260 million). The miners stopped depending on private firms and passed to dependence on the state.

## Sub lesson 3 — Worker control with right of veto

The nationalisation was accompanied by worker control (Control Obrero) with right of veto: the FSTMB and the district unions elected representatives to COMIBOL's directorates who could veto decisions contrary to the miners' or the nation's interests — creating a duality of power between the COB and the MNR.
