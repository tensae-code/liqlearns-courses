# Frictional, structural and cyclical unemployment

## Do it first

Three jobless people: a seasonal worker between seasons, a graduate whose training fits no local vacancy, and a builder idled because all building stopped in a slump. Same label, three causes — sort them before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Frictional — between jobs

Frictional unemployment is brief spells between jobs: seasonal work ending, voluntarily switching jobs for a better one, new entrants and re-entrants to the labour force. It is the friction of a moving labour market, and some of it exists even in healthy times.

## Sub lesson 2 — Structural — the mismatch

Structural unemployment is a mismatch between seekers' skills or locations and the vacancies' requirements or locations, arising when the pattern of demand changes the structure of production. The book's example: an agricultural graduate seeking work at a construction site.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cyclical — the missing vacancies

Cyclical unemployment is joblessness from deficient overall demand in the downswing, recession or depression phase of the trade cycle — a shortage of vacancies themselves. The workers are ready and matched; the jobs, economy-wide, are not there.
