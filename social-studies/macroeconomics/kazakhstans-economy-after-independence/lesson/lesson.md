# Kazakhstan's economy after independence

## Do it first

Kazakhstan became independent in 1991. Before you read on, write what you think happened to its shops, factories and markets in the first years after that — and who had to rebuild them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The post-Soviet crisis — rebuilding markets after 1991

Kazakhstan's Grade 10 books open the national story with a historical excursion. Aganina's 1.2 Historical Excursion into the Economy of Kazakhstan teaches **the post-Soviet crisis and the rebuilding of markets after 1991**: the old system ended, and a market economy had to be built in its place. Duisenkhanov's Lesson 2 Historical Overview of the Economy of Kazakhstan teaches the same opening — **the history of entrepreneurship in the Republic of Kazakhstan**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Small and medium business in the rebuilding — and the state's support

In that rebuilding, the books give a central place to **small and medium business**. Aganina's 1.2 teaches **the role of small and medium business** in the new economy. Duisenkhanov's Lesson 2 adds the other half: **the state's support for domestic business** — the new state backing its own entrepreneurs as the markets were rebuilt.

## Sub lesson 3 — Schumpeter's entrepreneur — the engine of economic development

Aganina's 1.2 closes the excursion with the thinker the course returns to: **Schumpeter's entrepreneur as the engine of economic development**. In Schumpeter's account, as the book teaches it, it is the entrepreneur — bringing new combinations into economic life — who drives development forward. That is why a course for entrepreneurs opens with the country's own economic history.
