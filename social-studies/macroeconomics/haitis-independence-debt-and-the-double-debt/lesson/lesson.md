# Haiti's independence debt and the double debt

## Do it first

A country wins its independence, and another country agrees to recognise it — for a price. Before you read on, say: who owes whom, once that price is set? Hold your answer — the lesson follows Haiti's own chronology.

## Sub lesson 1 — The 1825 ordinance — an indemnity imposed for recognition

In Haiti's Grade 9 book, the story opens with the **1825 ordinance**: France imposed an **indemnity** — a sum Haiti had to pay — **in exchange for recognition** of Haiti's independence.

The debt was not borrowed for a project. It was the price set on recognition itself, negotiated and imposed — the book's own séance title says it: **La dette de l'indépendance, négociation et imposition**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1838 treaties — the debt renegotiated

The chronology continues with the **1838 treaties**: the indemnity debt was renegotiated between Haiti and France.

Renegotiation changed the terms; it did not end the debt. Haiti went on owing, and went on paying — and paying required money Haiti had to find somewhere.

## Sub lesson 3 — The double debt — borrowing to pay the indemnity

Where the money was found is the book's central idea: **De la dette à « la double dette »** — from the debt to the "double debt". To pay the indemnity, **Haiti had to borrow from French banks**.

So the first debt bred a second: the indemnity itself, plus the **loans taken to pay it** — debt plus loans, followed by **successive national and international borrowing**. That doubling is the double debt, and it is why the book teaches the indemnity not as one payment but as the start of a long chain of borrowing.
