# Latin America and globalisation

## Sub lesson 1 — Globalisation — from the word globe

The Grade 6 book starts from the word itself: **globalisation** comes from "globe" — the whole world becoming one field of activity.

Its economic form is dated: economic globalisation took off at the **end of the 20th century**. A word, a date, and then the book's hard question — who holds the power inside it.

## Sub lesson 2 — A small group of powers holds most markets

Inside globalisation, the book says, a **small group of powers holds most of the world's markets**.

That is the fact Latin America faces: the game is world-wide, but the strongest players are few. The book does not present globalisation as a level field — it presents it as a field tilted towards the powers that hold the markets.

## Sub lesson 3 — Shared problems and the case for regional union

Latin America's countries share **economic and social problems** — the book treats the region as one case, not twenty separate ones.

From shared problems it draws its conclusion: the case for **regional union**. If the powers act together at world scale, Latin America's answer is to unite at regional scale — the same argument the senior book makes with ALBA and ALCA.
