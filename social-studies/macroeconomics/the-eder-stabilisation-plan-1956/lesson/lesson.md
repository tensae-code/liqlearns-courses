# The Eder stabilisation plan, 1956

## Sub lesson 1 — Inflation at 900% — the plan arrives

The National Revolution's inflation spiral — scarcity, wage demands, inorganic money emission, speculation, vanished confidence — left inflation reaching 900%. Siles Zuazo's government brought in Jackson Eder's plan, by decree of 15 December 1956, to stop it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The measures — one exchange rate, no controls, credit held tight

The plan's listed measures: stabilisation of the peso at a single exchange rate, elimination of official price controls and of subsidies, and rigid controls on bank credit. The compendium's fuller list adds: cut government spending 40%, suspend the pulpería subsidies, reduce and suspend tariffs while raising internal taxes 200%, level the exchange rates, and freeze wages after a limited rise.

## Sub lesson 3 — A relative success — bought with austerity, conditioned by a loan

The plan was a relative success bought with fiscal austerity — and it was a condition for a US$25 million US loan. Its tariff cuts bankrupted small national industries, and the wage rise did not compensate the price rises: stabilisation's bill was paid by workers and small industry.
