# Video script — Classical economic thought — Smith and Ricardo

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The classical school is the first great system of economics, built by Adam Smith and David Ricardo and their fellows. Instead of tying wealth to treasure, they asked how nations actually grow rich — through production, work and exchange. Their books founded economics as a science of the whole economy.

Smith's great book asks what makes a nation wealthy and answers: the work of its people, divided and specialised, producing goods for exchange. Left to trade freely, people's own efforts are guided as if by an unseen hand to serve others too. The market system pupils met earlier in the book is Smith's system, named and argued.

Ricardo sharpened the school's tools: he argued about what gives goods their value, and he showed with numbers that two countries both gain when each specialises in what it does relatively best and trades freely — the argument for free trade that economies still use. Classical thought thus ends as a system: wealth, value and trade in one frame.
