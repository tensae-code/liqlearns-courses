# The optimal investment of natural resources

## Do it first

A farmer has one small field, a little rain, and a family to feed this year — and children who will need the same field after him. Before you read on, say: what would using his land and water *well* mean — this year, and for the children?

## Sub lesson 1 — Using resources for the greatest benefit

Jordan's Grade 6 book gives this idea its own name: the **optimal investment of natural resources**. It means using resources for the **maximum benefit** — getting the most good out of them — **without harming them** and without harming the **future generations** who will need them too.

A resource used badly gives a little now and nothing later. A resource invested well keeps giving: the same water, land and materials serve the people alive today and the people who come after them. That balance — greatest benefit now, no harm to the resource, no theft from the future — is the whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Jordan's ways — water, land and energy

The book teaches the idea through Jordanian ways, because Jordan is a dry country that cannot afford waste. For **water**: **smart irrigation** that puts water where the plants need it, and **water harvesting** — catching and storing the rain that falls instead of letting it run away. For **land**: careful farm land use, so the soil keeps producing. For **energy**: **renewable energy** from the sun and the wind, which does not run out the way oil and gas do.

Each way follows the same rule from sub lesson 1: take the greatest benefit the resource can give, in a way the resource can survive.

## Sub lesson 3 — Investing in people and recycling

Two more ways complete the book's list. **Recycling projects** turn used materials back into useful things, so a resource gives its benefit twice instead of once. And **investing in human capital** — teaching, training and keeping people healthy — invests in the resource that does all the other investing: a country whose people know more can farm, build and save better with the same land and water.

So the optimal investment of natural resources is a habit with five Jordanian faces: harvest the water, irrigate smartly, farm the land carefully, recycle what you use, take energy from sun and wind — and invest in people.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the optimal investment of natural resources to your companion in your own words: the rule — maximum benefit, no harm, nothing stolen from future generations — and two Jordanian ways that follow it. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name one resource in your own town and write the wasteful way and the optimal-investment way of using it, side by side.

2. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.

3. Retell the five Jordanian ways to someone at home, and ask them which one your own home could start this week.
