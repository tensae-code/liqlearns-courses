# Video script — Aggregate demand — C + I + G + (X − M)

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Aggregate demand is total spending on the economy's output: AD = C + I + G + (X − M) — household consumption, investment by firms, government spending, and net exports (exports minus imports). It is the same four buyers as the national accounts, now seen as demand.

The AD curve slopes downward against the price level: at lower prices, the same money balances buy more, borrowing is cheaper, and home goods look cheap abroad. A change in the price level moves the economy along the curve — it does not shift it.

AD shifts when a component changes for a reason other than the price level: income and wealth move C; interest rates and expectations move I; policy moves G; exchange rates and foreign income move X − M. Naming the component is the first step of every AD problem.
