# Poverty in the community — causes, effects and ways out

## Do it first

A family near you has money for either food this week or school fees, but not both. Before you read on, say what you would call their situation, what could have caused it — and what could lift them out of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What poverty is

Zimbabwe's Grade 5 guide, Unit 26, defines **poverty** plainly: not having enough money for food, clothes or school. Poverty is measured at the family's table and the school gate — the meal that is not there, the clothes that cannot be bought, the schooling that cannot be paid for.

That definition matters because it names what a way out must restore: enough for the essentials of life and of learning.

## Sub lesson 2 — Causes and effects of poverty

The unit names poverty's **causes**: unemployment, limited access to education, natural disasters, and civil wars. A family falls into poverty when work disappears, when schooling was never reached, when a disaster destroys what it had, or when war breaks its life apart.

And it names the **effects** poverty does once it is there: poor health and malnutrition, school dropouts, early marriages, and crime. Poverty harms the body, ends schooling early, pushes children into marriage too young, and tempts people into crime — which is why it cannot be left to cure itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ways of reducing poverty

The unit closes with the **ways of reducing poverty**: creating jobs, income-generating projects, and access to education. Work gives the family its own income; a project — the guide's wider course names poultry, piggery, gardening and the like — lets a family earn where no job exists; and education opens the door to both.

Each way out answers a cause: jobs answer unemployment, projects answer the missing income, and schooling answers the limited access to education that helped cause the poverty in the first place.

## Say it to your companion

Explain poverty to your companion in your own words: the definition, the causes and effects, and the ways out — and how each way out answers a cause. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
