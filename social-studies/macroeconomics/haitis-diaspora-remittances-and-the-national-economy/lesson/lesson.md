# Haiti's diaspora remittances and the national economy

## Do it first

A Haitian family receives money sent home by a relative who works abroad. Before you read on, say: is that money part of Haiti's economy, or only of that family's money? Hold your answer — the lesson weighs the whole country's answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Les Haïtiens de l'extérieur — mapping where the diaspora lives

In Haiti's Grade 8 book, Séance 4 is called **Les Haïtiens de l'extérieur** — the Haitians outside. Its pupils begin by **mapping where Haitians abroad live**: the **diaspora**, Haitians living outside Haiti, spread across other countries and regions.

The map comes first because the money follows the people. You cannot weigh what the diaspora sends home until you know where the diaspora is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Transfers weighed in the country's accounts

From the map, the pupils work from **statistics on the diaspora's money transfers** — the money Haitians abroad send home — studied **region by region**: how much comes from where.

Then they weigh it in the country's own accounts: the **foreign currency** the transfers bring in carries a real **weight in the country's accounts** — money earned outside the country flowing into it, the kind of inflow a current account records. Haiti's transfers are not small change at the edge of the economy; weighed properly, they are a pillar of it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The lifeline — graphing the diaspora's contribution

Last, the pupils **build a graph of the diaspora's contributions to the national economy** — the transfers made visible as one rising or falling line the whole class can read.

And they read **press analysis calling the diaspora the Haitian economy's lifeline** — the claim, argued in print, that the money sent home by Haitians abroad is one of the things keeping the national economy alive. The graph is how pupils test that claim with numbers instead of only repeating it.

That is the whole lesson: map the diaspora, weigh its transfers region by region in the country's accounts, and graph its contribution to the national economy.

## Say it to your companion

Explain Haiti's diaspora remittances to your companion in your own words: where the work starts (the map), what is weighed (the transfers and their foreign currency), and what the graph shows. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
