# How economic policies work together

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one Cambodian example of this lesson's subject from your own life — a family business, a market price, a job, or a government programme you have heard named. Say in one sentence what you already think about it. The lesson will test that sentence.

## Sub lesson 1 — No policy stands alone

Governments hold several policies at once — monetary, fiscal, exchange-rate and more. This lesson's claim is simple: the policies touch each other. A move in one changes what the others do, so no policy can be judged as if it worked alone in an empty room.

## Sub lesson 2 — How policies touch

The touching is concrete: state spending affects the money and credit the central bank manages; the exchange rate changes what fiscal choices cost; interest moves alter what trade policy achieves. Cambodia's lesson traces these relations policy by policy, so pupils see the wires, not only the switches.

## Sub lesson 3 — Judging policies together

The working rule follows: judge a policy by what it does to the whole set, not only by its own aim. A policy that hits its own target while breaking another policy's work has not succeeded. Coordination — the previous lesson's subject — is possible only for pupils who first see the relations.
