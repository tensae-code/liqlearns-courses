# Why countries trade

## Do it first

Ethiopia could try to build its own computers. It imports them instead. Work out why before the lesson gives the basis of international trade.

## Sub lesson 1 — Price differences — the immediate cause

The immediate cause of international trade is price differences between nations: a good flows from where it is cheap toward where it is dear. Ethiopia imports computers because producing them at home would cost far more than the world price.

[Picture 24917 — a world map with unequal resource icons — coffee here, machines there — and trade arrows between them]

## Sub lesson 2 — Unequal endowments underneath

Underneath the price differences sit unequal resource endowments, technology and know-how: countries are differently stocked by nature and differently skilled by history, so the same good costs different amounts to produce in different places.

## Sub lesson 3 — The gains from trading

The listed gains: efficient worldwide use of resources through specialisation, more and more varied output, harmonised political relations, efficiency pressure from foreign competition, economies of scale from wider markets, technology learning, and cultural exchange.
