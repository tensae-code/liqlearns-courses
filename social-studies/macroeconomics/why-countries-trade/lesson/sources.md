Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 8 Social Science TG): Grade 8 Trade is entirely international trade — PNG's raw-material exports (gold, copper, gas, oil, copra, cocoa, coffee, palm oil) exchanged for Japanese machinery and vehicles, the challenges of trading, and the PNG authorities (IPA, Customs, NAQIA) that police the trade procedures. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG): secondary Social Science carries a full Economics strand — PNG's own tax system (about 75% of the budget from tax, collected by the IRC), the Bank of Papua New Guinea's direct and indirect monetary instruments, a public-service wage bill of over K200 million a fortnight, the Hiri-Motu traditional trade, and development judged against PNG's Five National Goals and Directive Principles. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 11 Economics TG): the standalone Senior High Economics course re-runs the five-unit frame at full depth — human resources and human capital first, then price mechanism and inflation, economic ideologies (conservatism, liberalism, protectionism, free market), international trade, and a closing unit on the communist economy mirroring Grade 10's capitalism unit. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 12 New Economics SS3): SS3 is Nigeria's national-economy story as a course — petroleum and NNPC/OPEC, planning and the Structural Adjustment Programme, NEEDS and Vision 2020, bank consolidation, privatisation and the anti-corruption agencies — with the Asian Tigers and Japan taught explicitly as lessons for Nigeria.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 Social Studies Class 5 SNC): The Class 5 chapter deepens the Class 4 set with Pakistan's own institutions as the teaching examples — PIA, Pakistan Railways, Pakistan Television, Pakistan Post and WAPDA for public services, the State Bank of Pakistan (established 1948), and Dr Akhtar Hameed Khan's microfinance work for social entrepreneurship.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 12 Economics Class 12): the Class 12 book runs the standard macro course (national income, money, banks, public finance, trade) and then turns the same tools onto Pakistan itself for six chapters — Pakistan's sectors, national income, planning, transport and manpower, banking system, government finances and foreign trade — closing with the Islamic economic system (Zakat, Ushr, interest-free banking) taught as a full alternative system, not a footnote.


Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Ekonomi): growth measured on Indonesia's own PDB series; trade taught through Indonesia's nickel-export case; budgets are Indonesia's real APBN/APBD with village funds (Dana Desa) as the regional-budget story.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Panduan Guru Ekonomi) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide's worked answers run on the student book's Indonesian data — PDB growth series, Indonesia's trade and balance-of-payments cases, the APBN/APBD. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Indonesia is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 10 Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 economics is economic geography of Fiji itself — village, semi-subsistence and commercial farming, the sugar industry's markets and problems, then industry as primary, secondary and tertiary activity with location factors and trade.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 Term 1): Trade is taught from Aqaba port and the Silk Road into Jordan's real 2022 export/import charts, and the economy lesson ends in entrepreneurship — Jobs and Apple beside Jordanian start-up projects the class must hunt down itself.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 4 G4 Student CCSS): Grade 4 widens the lens to Central America — regional production and diversification, tourism as an earner, and a first named look at capitalism and globalisation.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 5 G5 Student CCSS): Grade 5 turns outward to the Americas — Honduras's trade network and treaties, its free-trade zone at Choloma, tourism, and the integration blocs (MCCA, CARICOM, CELAC) as the road to development.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.
