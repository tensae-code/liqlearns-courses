# Private and public investment

## Do it first

A new dam, a new school, a pension payment and a new bakery oven: which of these would you leave to private firms, and which must the state do itself? Decide before the lesson classifies them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Private investment

Private investment is firms' and households' spending on machinery, factories and inventories — capital built by the private sector for its own production.

[Picture 24912 — a factory going up beside a dam, a school and a pension payment — the three public classes captioned]

## Sub lesson 2 — Public investment — and its three kinds

Public investment is the state's, made where vital services cannot be left to the private sector — defence, electricity, water. It divides into physical investment (infrastructure), human investment (education and skills) and consumption investment (pensions and social security).

## Sub lesson 3 — Sharing the work — partnerships

The book notes the modern pressure toward privatisation and public–private partnerships: some of what the state once built alone is now built with, or handed to, private partners.
