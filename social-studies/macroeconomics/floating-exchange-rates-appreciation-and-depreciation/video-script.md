# Video script — Floating exchange rates — appreciation and depreciation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Under a floating system the market's supply and demand set the rate, day by day. Most real systems are managed floats, where the central bank still steers the price — and the National Bank of Ethiopia follows the birr's rate in line with managed floating.

Market-driven moves take the other names from official ones. If the dollar's birr price rises from 45 to 50, the birr has depreciated: each birr now buys less foreign currency than before.

If the dollar's birr price falls from 45 to 30, the birr has appreciated: each birr buys more foreign currency. Appreciation and depreciation are the market's words; devaluation and revaluation are the government's, under a fixed rate.
