## Sub lesson 1 — First, reforms that raised the taxes

The Bourbon reforms began by squeezing: production taxes in America were raised — fuelling the great rebellions — while the Crown tried to simplify and standardise taxation and improve collection. Only then came the loosening of the trade monopoly.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1778 Reglamento — 13 ports and 22 ports

The 1778 Reglamento de Comercio Libre opened 13 metropolitan and 22 American ports to trade between Spain and its colonies. It was extended to Mexico in 1789 and to neutral ships in 1797 — and it showed the colonies they could live without Spain.

## Sub lesson 3 — Register ships replace the fleet and galleons

The student book tells the same loosening from the ships' side: the old fleet-and-galleon system was replaced by licensed register ships (navíos de registro), trading under the Casa de Contratación's inspection — more ports enabled, rules loosened, trade freer than before, still watched.
