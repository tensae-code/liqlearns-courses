# Video script — Investment — gross and net

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Investment is spending on goods not consumed now but accumulated to produce future income; in the strict sense it adds to the stock of capital — machines, buildings and stocks that will produce later.

Gross investment is total capital spending before depreciation. Part of it — replacement investment — merely offsets capital worn out in production: it keeps the stock where it was rather than growing it.

Net investment is gross investment minus depreciation. Only positive net investment expands the economy's productive capacity — if gross spending only matches wear, capacity stands still.
