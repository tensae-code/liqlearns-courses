# Internal and external public debt

## Do it first

A government owes a large total. Before you read on, say: does it matter *to whom* the money is owed — people inside the country, or lenders outside it? Hold your answer; Honduras's Grade 8 book splits the debt exactly that way.

## Sub lesson 1 — A country's total debt — split in two

In Lesson 36 "La Deuda en América Latina", the book teaches that a country's **total debt splits into two parts**: **internal debt** and **external debt**. The split is by lender — where the money was borrowed from — not by size or by year.

## Sub lesson 2 — Internal debt — owed to its own citizens

**Internal debt** is the part owed **to the country's own citizens** — borrowed at home, from its own people and institutions. The government owes it, but the lenders are inside the same economy the government serves.

## Sub lesson 3 — External debt — owed abroad, read off Honduras's chart

**External debt** is the part owed **abroad** — including **bilateral debt**, owed to foreign governments and central banks. The book has pupils read the split off **Honduras's own central-government fiscal deficit chart, 2017–2020**: the deficit years are where the borrowing that feeds both parts of the debt comes from.
