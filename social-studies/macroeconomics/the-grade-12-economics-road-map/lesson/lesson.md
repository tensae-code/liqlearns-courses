# The Grade 12 economics road map

## Do it first

Look at the unit titles in order. Which do you think are about one buyer or one firm, and which are about the whole country? Say your answers first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Units 1–3 — the whole economy, its balance, its failures

Unit 1 Fundamental Concepts of Macroeconomics tells how macroeconomics was born with Keynes, names its five recurring challenges — growth, inflation, unemployment, the business cycle and the trade balance — and sets out six schools of thought. Unit 2 Aggregate Demand and Aggregate Supply builds AD = C + I + G + (X − M), explains the upward slope with sticky wages and worker misperception, and works shocks. Unit 3 Market Failure and Consumer Protection takes public goods, externalities and asymmetric information to Ethiopia's Consumer Proclamation No. 813/2013.

## Sub lesson 2 — Units 4–6 — policy levers, taxes, poverty

Unit 4 Macroeconomic Policy Instruments works fiscal, monetary, income and foreign-exchange policy, each split expansionary and contractionary, and closes with the birr's exchange-rate history from 1945 to the 2017 devaluation. Unit 5 Tax Theory and Practice runs from Adam Smith's canons to Ethiopia's Schedules A–E, VAT, TOT, excise and surtax in birr terms. Unit 6 Poverty and Inequality hears the poor's own voices, measures poverty by headcount and gap, builds the MPI, and works Lorenz and Gini on a 20-household table, with Idir, Iqub and Debo as Ethiopia's own anti-poverty institutions.

## Sub lesson 3 — Units 7–8 — Ethiopia's reforms, then the climate economy

Unit 7 Macroeconomic Reforms in Ethiopia runs the Imperial five-year plans, the Derg, ADLI, SDPRP, PASDEP, GTP I and II with their performance tables, the 2021–2030 ten-year plan, the home-grown reforms and fiscal decentralization. Unit 8 Economy, Environment and Climate Change closes the course with the poverty–environment nexus, mitigation and adaptation, the green economy, and Ethiopia's CRGE and Green Legacy. Every unit follows the Grade 11 rhythm — introduction, Key Concepts, Learning Outcomes, sections opening with their own outcomes and a Start-up Activity, numbered Activities, a Unit Summary, and Review Questions in fixed parts, with a Workout where numbers allow — the define–work it in birr–summarise–review pattern, with worked Ethiopian examples throughout: a CPI basket of wheat and edible oil, a leather jacket bought across the birr–rupee rate, National Bank reserve-requirement cuts with their dates, and an employment-income-tax workout on 10,000 birr a month. Grade 11 is named as the micro foundation this macro course builds on.
