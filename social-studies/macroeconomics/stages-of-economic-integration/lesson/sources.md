**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Guatemala, South Sudan, Malaysia, Dominican Republic, Honduras, Indonesia, Nepal, Nicaragua, Colombia, Nigeria). Argentina is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 12 New Economics SS3): SS3 is Nigeria's national-economy story as a course — petroleum and NNPC/OPEC, planning and the Structural Adjustment Programme, NEEDS and Vision 2020, bank consolidation, privatisation and the anti-corruption agencies — with the Asian Tigers and Japan taught explicitly as lessons for Nigeria.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 Geografía 9° Grado): the distance-education twin of the Grade 9 world-economy unit, organised around technology as the difference between continents — US centre-pivot irrigation against Latin America's rain-fed traditional farming — and closing with the integration stages ladder and the international bodies (down to FAO's Nicaragua country programme) that soften globalisation's impact.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 12 Social Studies & Life Skills): Grade 12 economics is the development unit with real tables — sector shares of GDP, the trade growth and direction tables, Nepal's growth rates through the COVID year, the HDI bands, and Nurkse's vicious circle drawn as two loops, supply side and demand side.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Ekonomi): growth measured on Indonesia's own PDB series; trade taught through Indonesia's nickel-export case; budgets are Indonesia's real APBN/APBD with village funds (Dana Desa) as the regional-budget story.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 4° Secundaria): at this level the analysis turns Marxian in vocabulary — capitalism is explained through the separation of the worker from the means of production and the owner's appropriation of surplus labour (plusvalía), and imperialism through cartels, monopoly centralisation and the export of capital.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 6 G6 Student CCSS): Grade 6 goes global — world tourism, the maldistribution of world wealth, and integration blocs with the common market explained, while Honduras's own trade (US as main market) anchors it home.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 8 G8 Student CCSS): Grade 8 teaches Latin America's development problem head-on — development vs underdevelopment, the integration ladder, and the debt lesson built around Honduras's own fiscal-deficit chart and the structural-adjustment era.
