# Public revenue and spending in the early Islamic state

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — The revenues — zakat, kharaj, the fifth, fay', jizya, ushr and rikaz

Palestine's Grade 7 book teaches the Islamic state's economy as **public finance first**. Its revenues were named, separate streams: **zakat**, the **kharaj** land tax, **the fifth of spoils**, **fay'**, **jizya** paid by protected non-Muslims, the **ushr** tithe on crops such as wheat and olives, and **rikaz** on extracted minerals.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Bayt al-Mal — where the revenues gather

Those revenues did not scatter. They were **gathered in the Bayt al-Mal** — the state's treasury. The lesson's shape is the treasury shape: many named revenues flowing into one public store, from which the state then spends.

## Sub lesson 3 — The spending — pay, the poor and public services

From the Bayt al-Mal the state spent on **pay of soldiers and officials**, **care of the poor**, and **public services**. Revenue, treasury and spending are taught as one chain — what comes in, under named heads, is what pays for defence, administration, welfare and services.
