# Marxist economic thought

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — A critique of capitalism

Marxist thought begins as a critique: Marx and Engels studied the capitalist system from inside its own factories and slums and argued that its wealth rests on the labour of workers who own nothing but their ability to work. The school is taught in Cambodia's book as one of the great answers to the classical school.

## Sub lesson 2 — Workers, owners and surplus

In the Marxist account, owners hold the means of production and workers sell their labour; the value workers create beyond their wages is taken as the owners' gain. From this split — owners and workers, each with opposed interests — the school explains capitalism's crises and conflicts.

## Sub lesson 3 — How capitalism must change

The school does not stop at critique: it argues capitalism carries the seeds of its own end and must change into a system where the means of production are held in common. Lenin carried the argument into the age of empires. Whether a pupil accepts the account or not, the book teaches it whole — it shaped the century Cambodia lived through.
