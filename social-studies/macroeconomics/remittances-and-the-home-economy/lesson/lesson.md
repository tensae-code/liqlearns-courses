# Remittances and the home economy

## Do it first

A family receives money every month from a relative working abroad. Before you read on, say: is that money only good for the family and the country — or can it also do harm? Hold both sides; Honduras's Grade 9 book weighs exactly this.

## Sub lesson 1 — Remittances — money sent home by Hondurans abroad

Inside Lesson 37 "El fenómeno de la migración", the book teaches **remittances** — the money sent home by Hondurans working abroad. The flow is large because the people are many: **some 1.8 million Hondurans abroad** send money back to the families and the economy they left.

## Sub lesson 2 — The boost — a strong lift to the national economy

The book's first verdict is positive: remittances are **a strong boost to the national economy**. Money earned abroad flows into Honduran homes and spending, lifting the economy from outside in a way few other flows match.

## Sub lesson 3 — The cost — production demotivated, the labour force drained

The book's second verdict is the warning, and it teaches both together: remittances also **demotivate production** — when money arrives without local work, the push to produce at home weakens — and they **drain the productive labour force**, because the senders themselves are workers the home economy has lost. A strong boost that also weakens the base it lands on: that double judgement is the lesson.

Explain remittances and the home economy to your companion in your own words, giving both of the book's verdicts. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
