# Eritrea's post-independence development programmes

## Do it first

A country has just become independent. Its land, roads and farms have been through a long war, and it must now build itself. Before you read on, say: what three goals would you write at the top of its development plan? Keep your list — Eritrea's Grade 8 book gives the country's own.

## Sub lesson 1 — The National Charter goals — self-reliance, fair shares, participation

Eritrea's Grade 8 Social Studies teaches the country's development as a programme with stated goals, drawn from the **National Charter**. The book names three.

**Self-reliance**: the country builds itself, depending on its own resources and work. **Fair distribution of wealth**: what the country gains is shared fairly, not gathered in a few hands. **People's participation**: the people themselves take part in the building, not only watch it.

These are the programme's compass — the land reform and the campaigns that follow are taught as ways of carrying them out.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1995 land reform — state land, equal use rights

The programme's land settlement is the **1995 land reform**, and the book gives its rules plainly.

**All land is state property.** No one owns land privately; the state holds it for the whole people. On that basis, **use rights are equal for men and women** — a woman's right to use land stands equal to a man's. And **investment is permitted**: a person may invest in the land he or she uses, even though the land itself remains the state's.

Hold the three rules together: state ownership, equal use rights, investment allowed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Building, feeding — and the Warsai-Yikaalo campaign

From goals and land, the book moves to the work itself: **infrastructure** drives — building what the country needs to run — and the drive for **food self-sufficiency**, growing enough to feed the country from its own fields.

Its named campaign is the **Warsai-Yikaalo** recovery campaign of **2002**, the post-war rebuilding drive. And the book is careful about its money: the campaign was **financed from internal resources and loans** — raised at home first, in line with the Charter's self-reliance, with loans beside it.

So Eritrea's programme, whole: Charter goals of self-reliance, fair distribution and participation; the 1995 land reform; infrastructure and food-self-sufficiency drives; and the Warsai-Yikaalo campaign, financed from internal resources and loans.
