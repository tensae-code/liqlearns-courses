Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 Ciencias Sociales 8° Grado (Módulo Autoformativo, Primera Parte)): Economics arrives as "Space and Economy" for the whole continent — technology, globalisation, integration and free-trade treaties read critically from Central America's position, with the maquila and CAFTA debated from Nicaraguan experience, closing on sustainable development and quality of life.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 Estudios Sociales 7): "What we produce" — the three sectors plus a fourth information sector, taught from Nicaraguan work itself: free-zone garment workers, seasonal coffee pickers, street vendors of the informal sector, and a maize yield comparison (tractor 60 qq, oxen 40 qq, planting stick 20 qq per manzana) that makes technology's gain and its job losses concrete.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Ekonomi): growth measured on Indonesia's own PDB series; trade taught through Indonesia's nickel-export case; budgets are Indonesia's real APBN/APBD with village funds (Dana Desa) as the regional-budget story.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 12 Economic & Legal Education, Study Topics): integration is taught as national history with milestones (Đổi mới 1986, ASEAN 1995, APEC 1998, WTO 2007) and the CPTPP as the worked case, and development is measured with Vietnam's own HDI, income and Gini figures.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 12 Economic & Legal Education): the macro course is built on Vietnam's national accounts — the expenditure formula GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), GDP/GNI tables against China and Singapore, the 8.12% growth of 2022, sector-share charts 2018 vs 2022, and the 50/30/20-style family budget rule (50% essentials / 20% non-essentials / 30% saving, investment and reserve).

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 9 Social Science Year 9): With no economics strand at Year 9, economics appears inside other topics — industrial society in a sociology survey, globalisation in universal culture, a bottled-water tax fight as a conflict case study, and SPARTECA in regional organisations.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 11 Venezuela G11 Geografía de Venezuela SB Spanish): the senior geography book is an economic survey of the whole country — land, oil, industry by ownership form, commerce under a fair-prices law, and integration as the Bolivarian alternative to free trade. This lesson teaches the placements' content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Term 2): the modern economy is taught as resources-and-development of the Gulf states — oil and gas first, with the danger of depending on oil named from the start, then farming, livestock/fisheries, industry and trade, ending in GCC economic integration.

Uganda's own way: the Excel ladder widens from Uganda to the region — Primary Six teaches the East African Community as a working market: common currency aim, fewer trade barriers, free movement, wider markets, and the landlocked country's trade costs, Uganda being landlocked itself.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 5 G5 Student CCSS): Grade 5 turns outward to the Americas — Honduras's trade network and treaties, its free-trade zone at Choloma, tourism, and the integration blocs (MCCA, CARICOM, CELAC) as the road to development.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 6 G6 Student CCSS): Grade 6 goes global — world tourism, the maldistribution of world wealth, and integration blocs with the common market explained, while Honduras's own trade (US as main market) anchors it home.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 8 G8 Student CCSS): Grade 8 teaches Latin America's development problem head-on — development vs underdevelopment, the integration ladder, and the debt lesson built around Honduras's own fiscal-deficit chart and the structural-adjustment era.
