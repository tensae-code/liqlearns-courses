(a worked-example script walking the coffee-and-cloth numbers from autarky to post-trade consumption)

Adam Smith argued that trade is positive-sum: each country should specialise where it produces at absolutely lower cost, and import where it is at an absolute disadvantage — under laissez faire, with government merely keeping markets free.

The model assumes two countries, two goods, and labour as the only factor of production — labour that is homogeneous, fully employed, and internationally immobile. Pricing follows the labour theory of value, technology is fixed, there are no transport costs, and exchange is by barter.

The worked example gives Ethiopia absolute advantage in coffee (4 kg per labour hour against China's 1) and China in cloth (5 yards against 2). Before trade the pair produce 5 kg of coffee and 7 yards of cloth; after complete specialisation, 8 kg and 10 yards. Exchanging 4-for-4 then leaves both countries consuming more than they could alone.
