# Video script — Mercantilism — the gold view of trade

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The mercantilist writers of 1500–1800 Europe held that national wealth and power consist of holdings of gold and silver. A rich nation, on this view, is a nation whose vaults are full of precious metal.

So a country should export more than it imports, pocketing the difference in precious metals, and should use tariffs, quotas and regulation to suppress imports. Trade on this view is zero-sum — one nation's gain is another's loss, since the world's gold only changes hands.

The classical refutation: wealth is productive capacity, not metal — a nation is rich by what it can make, not by what it hoards. Mercantilism still echoes twice in modern debate: jobs-based suspicion of imports, and faith in state regulation of economic life.
