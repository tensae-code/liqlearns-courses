# Video script — Measuring GDP — value added at every stage

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Much production passes through stages, each stage buying the previous stage's output as its input. The book's chain runs from the farmer's wheat to the miller's flour to the baker's bread to the consumer. Adding every stage's full selling price would count the wheat's value three times over.

The fix is to count only each stage's value added: the gap between its selling price and the intermediate inputs it bought in. The farmer adds the wheat's whole value; the miller adds only flour-price minus wheat-cost; the baker adds only bread-price minus flour-cost. Each stage's true contribution, no more.

Sum the value added at every stage of every chain in the economy and the result is GDP — identical to the expenditure total and the income total. Three different counting roads, one destination: that agreement is the national accounts' built-in check on itself.
