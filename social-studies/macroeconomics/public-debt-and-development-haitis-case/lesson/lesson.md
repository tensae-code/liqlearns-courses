# Public debt and development — Haiti's case

## Do it first

A country owes a large debt, and year after year money leaves the country to pay it and the loans taken to pay it. Before you read on, say: what does that do to the country's development — its schools, roads and services? Hold your answer — the lesson opens exactly that question, the Haitian book's way: as a debate.

## Sub lesson 1 — Debt and development — what a country's debt does to its development

In Haiti's Grade 9 book, Séance 1 is titled **La dette et le développement d'Haïti, un débat contemporain ?** — Haiti's debt and development: a contemporary debate?

Its work is **analysis**: pupils analyse **the effects of a country's debt and its successive loans on its development**. Money owed must be serviced and repaid; loans taken to pay earlier debt add their own burden; what a country can build and provide is shaped by what it owes. The séance teaches pupils to trace those effects on Haiti itself, not to memorise a slogan about debt in general.

## Sub lesson 2 — Neocolonialism by debt — the argument

The book's own title names the argument pupils must weigh: **le néocolonialisme par la dette** — **neocolonialism by debt**.

The argument is this: a country can be formally independent and still be bound by its debt — the indemnity imposed at recognition, and the successive loans taken to pay it, keep wealth flowing out and keep the country's choices constrained long after independence. Pupils learn the argument as an argument: built from the chronology of the indemnity and the loans, and open to be tested against it.

## Sub lesson 3 — A live debate — restitution and reparations

The book does not close the question. The enquiry runs from **President Aristide's 2003 restitution speech** and **current press investigations**, and it ends with the pupils **arguing the restitution and reparations debate themselves**: should the independence debt be given back — restitution — and repaired — reparations?

That is the book's final move: debt and development is taught as a **live contemporary debate**, and the learners take their places in it with the chronology and the analysis in hand.
