# Industrialisation in the Dominican Republic

## Do it first

A country decides to stop importing shoes, beer and food and make them at home instead. Before you read on, write down what has to exist before the first factory can open — and who you think should own the factories.

## Sub lesson 1 — A timid import substitution begins

In the Dominican Republic's Grade 9 Social Studies book, the unit Geografía económica de América ends by bringing industrialisation home. Under **Trujillo**, a **timid import-substitution process** began with the **Second World War economic boom**: from **1937**, **shoe, beer and food factories** opened — the country starting to make at home things it had imported.

## Sub lesson 2 — Monopoly factories, a working mass and stronger public finances

More factories followed: the **sack and cordage factory**, the **cement factory** and the **industrial slaughterhouse**. But these firms were **monopolies owned by Trujillo himself** — industrialisation and the dictator's own property grew together. The factories still changed the country: they created an **important urban working mass** and **strengthened public finances**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Wartime agriculture — from rice importer to rice exporter

The war years transformed farming too. Wartime agriculture turned the country from a **rice importer into a rice exporter of 630,000 quintales**, as **irrigated land rose 91.8% between 1936 and 1944**. The same war boom that started the factories made the fields produce for export.
