How does a country start making things? In the Dominican Republic, under Trujillo, it began timidly, with the Second World War boom: shoe, beer and food factories from 1937, then sacks and cordage, cement, a big slaughterhouse. One catch — the firms were monopolies, and they belonged to Trujillo himself. Still, they raised an urban working mass and strengthened public finances. And in the fields, war did the same work: irrigated land rose 91.8 percent between 1936 and 1944, and a rice importer became a rice exporter of 630,000 quintales.
