# When a household has no money

## Do it first

Before you read on, write what you already think this lesson's title means — when a household has no money — in one sentence of your own. Keep it; you will check it against the lesson at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Barriers that stop a family getting money

Some families cannot get money. The book names the barriers: **unemployment**, **laziness**, **disasters**, **epidemics**, **many children**, **waste** and **conflict**. Each barrier stops the family's money coming in or uses it up.

## Sub lesson 2 — What lacking money leads to at home

When a household has no money, the book teaches, it leads to **debt**, **theft**, **untreated illness** and **conflict** at home.

## Sub lesson 3 — Money, health and peace at home

Lacking money is not only about coins: illness goes **untreated**, the family falls into **debt**, and **conflict** grows — so the barriers in sub lesson 1 harm health, honesty and peace together.

## Say it to your companion

Explain when a household has no money to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, say in your own words what when a household has no money teaches, part by part.

3. Check your Do it first sentence against the lesson: fix anything the lesson teaches differently.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
