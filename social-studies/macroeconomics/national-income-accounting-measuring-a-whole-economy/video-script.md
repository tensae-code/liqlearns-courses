# Video script — National income accounting — measuring a whole economy

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

National income accounting is the official system for measuring an economy's aggregate activity — the whole country's production, income and spending, counted together. Its results are summarised by the two headline measures of the unit, GDP and GNP.

The accounts are used first to know the economy's output level — how much was actually produced — and to observe its long-run trend: whether production is rising, stagnating or falling across the years, which no single year's figure can show.

The accounts also give evidence for policy formulation — governments steer by them — and they make cross-country comparison possible, since every country counting the same way can be set beside every other. The rest of this unit builds the measures themselves, approach by approach.
