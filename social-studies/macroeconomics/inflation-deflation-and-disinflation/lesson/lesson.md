# Inflation, deflation and disinflation

## Do it first

Prices rose 40% last year and 10% this year. Prices are still rising — so what fell? Say your answer before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading the change off the CPI

Inflation is the percentage change in the CPI from one year to the next. From the basket example: CPI 100 to 140 is +40% inflation; 140 to 120 is a fall of 20 points on 140, which is −14.3%.

[Picture 25203 — price-level line rising, dipping, rising, with the three labels]

## Sub lesson 2 — Deflation — the level itself falls

When the CPI falls, the general price level falls: that is deflation, the −14.3% year above. Then the index climbs from 120 to 200, a rise of 80 on 120, which is +66.6% inflation — the sharpest rise in the example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Disinflation — rising more slowly

Disinflation is different: prices still rise, but the inflation rate falls — from +40% to +10%, say. Deflation means the level goes down; disinflation means the rate goes down while the level still goes up. Confusing the two misreads every headline.
