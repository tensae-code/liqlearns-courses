# Lesson plan — Sources of government revenue

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Macroeconomics · **Lesson:** Sources of government revenue
**Place in the curriculum:** Macroeconomics (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Economics expansion (Uganda, 2026-11-16).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Government revenue is the government's income — the total money a government and local authorities receive to finance the country's sectors — and it comes from one main source, taxation, and a long list of non-tax sources: grants, loans, donations, fines, licences, fees, tourism, sale of minerals and electricity, earnings from government enterprises, the national lottery, rent and rates, and road and bridge tolls.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on what the learner already knows about what a tax is, and widens it: a tax is one source among many, and this lesson maps them all, with what the money pays for.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

2. **Tax sources — taxes, the main source**
3. **Non-tax sources — grants, loans, donations, fines, licences and more**
