# Resistance economy — building a resilient economy

## Do it first

Imagine your country's banks are suddenly cut off from the world's banks, and money owed to your country's sellers cannot be collected. Before you read on, say what an economy could do to keep standing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why resistance — sanctions set against a country's progress

Iran's books teach the setting first. After the Islamic Revolution, **imposed war and economic sanctions were set against Iran's progress**. The Grade 10 Economics book works the pressure through its running family story: the workshop's **export orders from Oman and Iraq are blocked by US banking sanctions** — the goods are sold, but the money cannot be collected — and import costs multiply. An economy under that pressure needs a doctrine for standing, and Iran's answer is named in both books: the **resistance economy (اقتصاد مقاومتی)**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The principles — least waste, own feet, national production

The Grade 9 book's box "Resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) gives the policy's principles, adopted **on the Leader's guidance**: in hard conditions have the **least waste of cost**, **stand on your own feet**, **support national production**, **avoid extravagance** and **use resources correctly**. The Grade 10 book's Lesson 10, "Making the economy resilient" (مقاومسازی اقتصاد), has the students build the principles of a resistance economy themselves.

## Sub lesson 3 — Using domestic capacity — making at home what was imported

The Grade 10 lesson names the working side: **using domestic capacity**. Where an input was imported, Iran's own **industrial universities** set out to produce it at home — the workshop's imported inputs are the book's small case of a national pattern the book names in **missiles, nuclear technology, laser and medicine**. Resilience, in this doctrine, is not only spending less; it is being able to make what the economy needs when the outside road is closed.
