## Sub lesson 1 — Programas de Ajuste Estructural

From 1982 Costa Rica became a neoliberal-adjustment state — PAE II (Arias, 1986–1990) lists opening to the world market, tariff cuts to boost exports, shrinking the state and selling state institutions, financial reform opening private banking, and changing the CNP's farm price-fixing role.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planes o Programas de Ajuste Estructural (PAES)

The PAEs defined as sets of measures imposed in public administration to remove the obstacles to free trade and production — often the state itself and the unions — pushed by the World Bank and IMF, which lent money only if the measures were implemented, aiming at less import dependence, more foreign exchange, contained internal demand and fiscal and monetary discipline.

## Sub lesson 3 — El PAE I y sus medidas

PAE I (Monge, 1982–86; US$80 million from the World Bank) in full — mini-devaluations of the colón, a farm-promotion law, import taxes cut or eliminated for non-traditionals, CENPRO created, CAT tax-credit certificates for exporters, public spending cuts in education and health, sale of CODESA firms, less tariff protection, pension age raised to 65 — plus the paradox that the state intervened to promote exports, and the CAT fraud that cost the treasury 196 million colones.

## Sub lesson 4 — Los PAE II y III

PAE II (Arias, signed 1987; US$200 million from the World Bank and Japan) — strengthen private banking, fix basic-grain prices, sell CODESA firms, restrict state-bank credit — and PAE III (Figueres Olsen, 1994; US$250 million from the World Bank) — privatisation of state firms, a tax reform to raise government income, opening state monopolies to competition, modernising state banking through mixed banking, and the sales of CEMPASA and FERTICA.
