## Sub lesson 1 — Keynes and the slump

John Maynard Keynes (1883–1946) wrote in the shadow of the 1930s slump, when factories stood idle and millions had no work though nothing was wrong with their skills. The old schools expected the economy to right itself; Keynes asked why it had not, and his answer founded a new school.

## Sub lesson 2 — Demand drives the economy

Keynes's new idea: spending — total demand — drives the economy. When households and firms stop spending, production falls and jobs vanish, however able the workers are. In a slump the problem is not lazy workers or broken machines; it is missing demand.

## Sub lesson 3 — The state must manage it

If missing demand is the disease, the state holds a cure: when private spending falls, public spending and policy can take up the slack and steady the economy. This is why Keynesian thought closes the book's history chapter — it hands pupils the argument behind the fiscal and monetary policies they studied earlier.
