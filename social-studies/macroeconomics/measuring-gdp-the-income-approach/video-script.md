# Video script — Measuring GDP — the income approach

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Production generates incomes as surely as it generates goods: every birr of output value is paid out to someone who helped produce it. The income approach therefore measures GDP from the receiving side — adding up the incomes production creates.

The factor incomes are wages and salaries paid for labour, rent paid for land and buildings, interest received by households, and profits earned by firms. These four sum to national income — the part of GDP that lands in factor owners' hands.

Two non-factor items are added back to national income to reach GDP. Indirect business taxes are part of market prices but are no factor's income; depreciation — the capital consumption allowance — is the value of capital used up in production, likewise not paid out as income. National income plus these two gives GDP, the same total the expenditure approach found.
