**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Malaysia, Dominica, Bangladesh, Uzbekistan, Egypt, United States, Pakistan, Costa Rica). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales CONED 11): the full national economic story in one book — agro-export dependence, the first state-intervention reforms of 1914, import substitution under the welfare state, then the 1980s crisis and the PAE adjustment that opened the economy and courted Intel and the free zones.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 12 Economics Class 12): the Class 12 book runs the standard macro course (national income, money, banks, public finance, trade) and then turns the same tools onto Pakistan itself for six chapters — Pakistan's sectors, national income, planning, transport and manpower, banking system, government finances and foreign trade — closing with the Islamic economic system (Zakat, Ushr, interest-free banking) taught as a full alternative system, not a footnote.


United States' own way (United States Grade 13 American Government 3e): economics appears inside civics as the machinery of public policy — goods classified by who can be excluded, and the federal budget as the arena where Keynesian ideas meet mandatory spending.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

No matching teacher guide exists in this band: the book recorded as this text's teacher guide proved to be the 2011 GEQIP Grade 12 teacher's guide for the old syllabus, not this book's companion, and supplies no answer keys for it (proof in the placements file's verification block).

The classification workout is computed and checked in the lesson: 300/4,000 = 7.5% and 1,400/20,000 = 7%, a falling average rate — regressive — despite the larger absolute payment.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).
