# Progressive, proportional and regressive taxes

## Do it first

Income 4,000 birr, tax 300. Income 20,000 birr, tax 1,400. The richer person paid more birr — but is this tax progressive, proportional or regressive? Compute the shares first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three shapes of the average rate

Judge a tax by its average rate as income rises. Progressive: the average rate rises with income. Proportional: the same share at every level. Regressive: the share falls, so the rich pay a smaller fraction than the poor.

## Sub lesson 2 — The workout — check it

The unit's figures: 300 on 4,000 is 300/4,000 = 7.5%; 1,400 on 20,000 is 1,400/20,000 = 7%. The richer income pays more birr but a smaller share — the average rate falls, so this schedule is regressive. Always divide before you classify.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ability-to-pay in a schedule

These three shapes are how ability-to-pay is put into a schedule: vertical equity normally argues for the progressive shape, which is why most income taxes, Ethiopia's Schedule A included, rise in steps.
