# The labour force and the unemployment rate

## Do it first

A student, a retired farmer, a shop worker and a graduate searching for work walk past. Which of them can be counted as unemployed? Decide before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who counts — the working-age frame

Unemployment is only counted inside the working-age population. The book gives Ethiopia's working-age band as 14–60 years, with 18 the minimum age for formal employment. Children, students in full-time study and retirees outside the band are not unemployed, however little they work.

[Picture 25205 — a population pyramid with the 14–60 band highlighted]

## Sub lesson 2 — The labour force

The labour force is everyone of working age who is employed plus everyone without work who is available for and seeking work. People neither working nor seeking — full-time students, for example — are outside the labour force entirely.

## Sub lesson 3 — The unemployment rate

The unemployment rate is the unemployed divided by the labour force, times 100 — never divided by the whole population. That denominator is why a country can have many jobless people and a modest rate, or the reverse.
