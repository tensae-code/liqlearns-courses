# Video script — Costa Rica's 1980s economic crisis

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The second oil shock and the fall in coffee prices pushed GDP per capita down (negative growth from 1978–79); by 1982 inflation ran near 80% a year with consumer prices up 225%, international reserves were lost and intraregional trade fell by half.

The oil shock met a state that had stopped receiving tax income while spending far more than it received (health, education, a swollen state payroll).

It borrowed from international banks until the external debt passed US$1,000 million and reached US$3,700 million by 1983, inflation climbed from 7.8% (1979) to 81% (1982), and President Carazo broke with the IMF and declared a moratorium on the external debt.
