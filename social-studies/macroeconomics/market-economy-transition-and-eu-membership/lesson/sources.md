| Bulgaria part — Economics expansion — Lesson 56 "Bulgaria's economy — general characteristics" (Стопанство – обща характеристика): Bulgaria's economy developed in stages — craft centres and trade before 1878 (the first textile factory, at Sliven in 1834); a small agricultural country after the Liberation, with banking developing (the Bulgarian National Bank) and the greatest commodity exchange with Austria-Hungary, Germany, the United Kingdom and Russia; the socialist model of 1946–1989, with nationalisation of the factories and mines and land united in cooperatives; and after 1989 the transition to a market economy — equality of private, state, cooperative and mixed ownership, commodity exchanges and labour exchanges formed (because of the increased unemployment), the land returned to its owners, foreign investments attracted, and the service activities best developed; since 2007 Bulgaria is a member of the European Union, whose community gives stability and help through the European funds, free movement of labour resources, goods, services and capital, and travel in the EU countries with an identity card, without visas | Bulgaria, *Bulgaria Grade 7 Geography and Economics*, Lesson 56 "Bulgaria's economy — general characteristics" (Стопанство – обща характеристика) — printed pp. 125–127 |

**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Bulgaria).**

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 7 Geography and Economics): the economy of a country is taught as three families of activities — extractive, processing and serving — and Bulgaria's own story is told in four stages, from the craft towns and the first Sliven textile factory of 1834 through nationalisation and the cooperatives to the post-1989 market transition and EU membership in 2007.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 10 Geography and Economics): at Grade 10 the pupil meets the whole national economy at once — its three sectors with the 2021 employment shares (services 74.2%, processing 21.9%, extractive 3.9%), GDP and GNI defined in a box, and the market economy explained through Bulgaria's own post-1989 reform: privatisation, commodity and labour exchanges, and prices set on the market.
