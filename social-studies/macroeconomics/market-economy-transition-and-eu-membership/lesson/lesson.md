# Bulgaria — market-economy transition and EU membership

## Do it first

Before you read on, write what you think changed in Bulgaria's economy in 1989 — and what changed again in 2007.

## Sub lesson 1 — Before socialism — crafts, the first factory and the National Bank

Bulgaria's Grade 7 book tells the first stages: **craft centres and trade before 1878 — the first textile factory, at Sliven in 1834**; then, **a small agricultural country after the Liberation, with banking developing (the Bulgarian National Bank) and the greatest commodity exchange with Austria-Hungary, Germany, the United Kingdom and Russia**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The socialist model, then the transition to a market economy

Next, the book's third stage: **the socialist model of 1946–1989, with nationalisation of the factories and mines and land united in cooperatives**. Then, **after 1989, the transition to a market economy**. Grade 10 tells the reform in detail: **a process of equal standing of ownership begins — private, state, cooperative, mixed — and the country passes from a centralised planned economy to a market economy; privatisation is carried out with the participation of foreign capitals, and as a consequence unemployment is formed and the percentage of the employed and the quantity of production decrease, while the country loses many markets; commodity exchanges, labour exchanges and employment offices, stock exchanges and privatisation funds are formed; the agrarian reform returns the land to its owners, restitution of the large city property is carried out, the denationalisation of the large industrial enterprises proceeds and foreign investments are attracted; price and quality become the basic requirements in forming the internal market**. Grade 7 adds the same stage's marks: **equality of private, state, cooperative and mixed ownership, commodity exchanges and labour exchanges formed (because of the increased unemployment), the land returned to its owners, foreign investments attracted, and the service activities best developed**.

## Sub lesson 3 — EU membership, 2007 — stability, funds and free movement

Grade 7 closes the stages: **since 2007 Bulgaria is a member of the European Union, whose community gives stability and help through the European funds, free movement of labour resources, goods, services and capital, and travel in the EU countries with an identity card, without visas**. Membership is told as part of the economy's story — the market opened outward, to the whole Union.
