# The discount rate

## Do it first

A commercial bank runs short of reserves overnight. There is one lender it can always approach. What does that lender charge, and why does the charge matter to everyone else?

## Sub lesson 1 — The price of borrowing from the central bank

The discount rate is the interest rate the central bank charges banks that borrow from it. Raise it, and banks borrow less and lend less; cut it, and borrowing from the centre gets cheaper.

## Sub lesson 2 — From the window to the street

Cheaper borrowing at the discount window lets banks lend more to the public; dearer borrowing does the reverse. The rate signals the central bank's stance even to banks that never borrow.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ethiopia's discount window — March 2001

Ethiopia's discount-window facility for commercial banks started in March 2001. The date matters: it anchors this tool in Ethiopian practice — the NBE lending directly to banks at a set rate — not in theory alone.
