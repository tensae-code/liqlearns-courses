# Food security and food sovereignty

## Do it first

A town grows plenty of food, yet some of its people go hungry. Before you read on, say: is that town food-secure? What else, besides food existing, must be true? Hold your answer — the lesson works it the Guatemalan book's way, through the idea's own history and its two definitions in full.

## Sub lesson 1 — Food and nutrition security — how the idea grew

In Guatemala's Grade 9 entrepreneurship guide, the first experience opens with **food and nutrition security** — *Seguridad Alimentaria y Nutricional* (SAN) — taught as a concept **with its history**.

The idea was **born in the 1980s around production and availability** — whether enough food is produced and there to be had. It **widened to physical and economic access** — whether people can actually reach the food and pay for it. And it was **completed in the 1990s** with **food safety, cultural preferences and its standing as a human right**. A town can grow plenty and still fail the completed idea: access, safety, culture and right are part of security, not extras.

## Sub lesson 2 — The definitions — INCAP and the FAO, 1996

The experience gives the definitions **in full**. **INCAP's** definition: food and nutrition security is **a state in which all people enjoy, in good time and permanently, physical, economic and social access to the food they need, in quantity and quality, for proper consumption and biological use**.

The **FAO's** definition, from the **1996 World Food Summit** in Rome: **all people, at all times, with physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food for an active and healthy life**.

Both definitions agree on the completed idea: **all people, at all times, real access — physical and economic — to food that is sufficient, safe and fitting to eat**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Food sovereignty — the second activity

The experience does not stop at security. **Food sovereignty is the experience's second activity** — set after the security concept, its history and its definitions, as the next thing to be understood in its own right.

So the experience's order is itself the teaching: first **what food and nutrition security is** — its history and its two definitions in full — then **food sovereignty**, taken up separately, as its own activity.
