# Inflation — a general, lasting rise in prices

## Do it first

Teff costs more this month — one good, one jump. Is that inflation? What would have to be true about prices in general, and over time, before the word applies?

## Sub lesson 1 — The definition, word by word

Inflation is a sustained, persistent and appreciable increase in the general price level, measured by a price index. A small or one-off rise is not inflation, and not all prices rise together or in the same proportion — the level rises, unevenly, and keeps rising.

## Sub lesson 2 — Deflation — the opposite

Deflation is inflation's opposite: a general fall in prices. Both are movements of the level, not of one price — one good cheapening is a bargain, all goods cheapening is deflation.

## Sub lesson 3 — What causes it

The listed causes: growth of the money supply, rising input costs, imported inflation, a weaker exchange rate, and declining productivity. The next lesson sorts the first moves into two great families — demand pulling and costs pushing.
