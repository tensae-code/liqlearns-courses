**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Ethiopia, Mongolia, Malaysia, Dominica, Bangladesh, Fiji, Vietnam, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Iran, Pakistan, Bolivia, Costa Rica, Nigeria, Greece, Samoa). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere. Papua New Guinea is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Samoa's own way (Year 9 Business Studies Book 1): Economics opens with producers, not definitions — a Samoan is first a fisher, planter or weaver in one of three industries, and money itself is introduced through Samoa's own history (fine mats and tapa, German marks, pounds, the tala of 1967).

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Economics): A full NZ-style supply/demand course rebuilt on Samoan ground — Apia milk and taro-fertiliser markets, VAGST, the tala/yen foreign-exchange market, Samoan public/merit goods, and money-supply definitions for Samoa's banks.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 11 Economics TG): the standalone Senior High Economics course re-runs the five-unit frame at full depth — human resources and human capital first, then price mechanism and inflation, economic ideologies (conservatism, liberalism, protectionism, free market), international trade, and a closing unit on the communist economy mirroring Grade 10's capitalism unit. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 11 New Economics SS2): SS2 pairs every theory block with its Nigerian application in the same theme — wage policy and trade unions, agricultural policy and marketing, revenue allocation in the federation, the Nigerian Stock Exchange and inflation in Nigeria.

Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales CONED 11): the full national economic story in one book — agro-export dependence, the first state-intervention reforms of 1914, import substitution under the welfare state, then the 1980s crisis and the PAE adjustment that opened the economy and courted Intel and the free zones.

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales; Bolivia Grade 12 Texto de Aprendizaje Ciencias Sociales Secundaria 6): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation. the final year runs the whole arc in student form — 1952's nationalisations and reform, the inflation they fed, the stabilisation plans imposed from outside, hyperinflation, the neoliberal turn and its reversal in 2006 — and closes with a financial-education unit on money, the boliviano and the Central Bank.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 Social Studies Class 4 SNC): Economics enters at Class 4 as one named chapter of Social Studies, taught in Urdu with English concept labels, through Pakistani examples — the State Bank of Pakistan, a National Bank of Pakistan cheque, and Pakistan's own import/export goods.


Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Ekonomi): national income worked in rupiah with Indonesian provincial data; cooperatives taught as the “soko guru” (main pillar) of the Indonesian economy with SHU surplus-sharing calculations; the state/regional/private ownership ladder (BUMN–BUMD–BUMS) as the map of who owns the economy.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 11 Economic & Legal Education): the macro topics are taught from Vietnam's own statistics and history — the CPI chart 2016–2021, the 775% inflation of 1986, COVID-era unemployment of 3.2%, the Q3 2022 labour-market bulletin — and the State's controlling role is quoted from the Law on the State Bank and the Labour Code.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 2): International and development topics are worked with Fiji data — a Fiji CPI table to a 2008 base year, Fiji's January 2010 balance of payments, and Fiji unemployment with cane-cutting seasonal work and government job actions.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 9 Social Studies 9): the labour market is taught with real Mongolian wage schedules in tögrög, a full payslip worked line by line, and national statistics pupils must compute rates from.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika): a workbook-style first economics course adapted from Cyprus, built around fill-in worksheets and Greek data — the labour-force shift table is Greece's own movement from farms to services.
