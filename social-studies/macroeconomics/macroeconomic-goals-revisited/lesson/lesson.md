# Macroeconomic goals revisited

## Do it first

A government can chase only one this year: more exports than imports, steadier prices, faster growth, or more jobs. Which would you pick for Ethiopia, and what might it cost the other three? Say your choice first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Trade and prices — two goals

The current account — exports minus imports — is healthier in surplus, when a country earns more abroad than it spends. Inflation, a general rise in prices, comes in two forms: cost-push, driven by rising production costs, and demand-pull, driven by demand outrunning supply. Both goals are about balance with the outside world and with the price level.

## Sub lesson 2 — Growth and jobs — two more

Growth — more real output — is desirable in itself and as the funder of everything else. Unemployment is sorted into types, now including seasonal unemployment: the drought-time idleness of farmers, whose work vanishes with the rains rather than with any failing of theirs. Each type asks a different policy answer.

## Sub lesson 3 — Optimisation — the best attainable

Optimisation is defined as getting the best attainable outcome — not a perfect one. With four goals pulling against each other and resources limited, economic policy is optimisation work: the best combination actually reachable. The word closes the goals review and opens the growth measurement that follows.
