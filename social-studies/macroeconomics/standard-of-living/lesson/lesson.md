# Standard of living

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika), **5.4 Standard of living** — teaches: the standard of living as more than income: health, education and living conditions. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — More than income

Greece's Grade 9 workbook separates two ideas students merge: income per head, and the **standard of living** — how well people actually live. The standard of living is **more than income**: it includes **health, education and living conditions** alongside what money buys.

Two places with the same income per head can house very different lives — in clinics reached, years schooled, water clean, homes sound.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why income alone misleads

Income is an average of money; living is not made of money alone. Public services, a clean environment, security and time all enter real living standards without passing through a wage.

The workbook therefore teaches the standard of living right after per-capita income — as its necessary correction: the average share tells what could be bought; the standard of living asks what life is actually like.

## Sub lesson 3 — Judging living standards

To judge a standard of living, the lesson's method is to look at several measures together — income, health, education, conditions — and refuse any single number's verdict.

That habit is the bridge to the workbook's next step, GDP and the standard of living, where GDP per head alone is shown not to settle how well people live.
