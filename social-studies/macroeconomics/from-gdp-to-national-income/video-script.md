---

Not all of GDP is net gain: producing it used up capital. Net domestic product is GDP minus the capital consumption allowance — the depreciation of machines, buildings and equipment worn out in the year's production. NDP is the honest 'after wear and tear' measure of output.

National income is the sum of payments to the resource owners who produced the output. Its first components are compensation of employees — wages and salaries with their supplements — and proprietors' income, the earnings of unincorporated businesses such as small shops and farms run by their owners.

Corporate profits enter in three pieces: dividends paid out to shareholders, retained earnings kept by the company, and profit taxes paid to the government. Personal rental income and net interest complete the five components of national income.
