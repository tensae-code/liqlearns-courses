# Why government intervenes — regulation and consumer protection

## Do it first

Two medicine sellers compete. One makes honest medicine and charges what it costs. The other makes cheap, weak medicine and charges less. Before you read on, say who wins the customers if nobody watches — and what happens to the buyers.

## Sub lesson 1 — When no one watches — the low-quality firm drives out the honest producer

Iran's Grade 10 Economics book, in Lesson 6 "What is the government's role in the economy?", gives the case plainly. **Without state supervision, a low-quality, low-price drug firm drives out the honest producer**: buyers cannot see inside the medicine, they buy the cheaper one, and the honest maker — whose real medicine costs more to make — loses the market. The market's own working, unwatched, rewards the worse good.

## Sub lesson 2 — Endangered consumers — why supervision exists

The same section names who pays for that result: **consumers are endangered**. A weak or false medicine is not a bargain; it is a harm sold as a saving. State supervision exists so that what is sold as medicine must be medicine — the consumer is protected against a danger they cannot test with their own eyes before buying.

## Sub lesson 3 — Laws that help exchange — the state's body of rules

The book's wider claim completes the lesson: **the body of state laws exists to facilitate and encourage exchanges that benefit buyers and sellers**. Regulation, in this account, is not the enemy of the market. It is the frame that keeps exchange honest, so that the exchanges which benefit both sides can go on and the ones built on cheating cannot.
