# Video script — Why government intervenes — regulation and consumer protection

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iran's Grade 10 Economics book, in Lesson 6 "What is the government's role in the economy?", gives the case plainly. **Without state supervision, a low-quality, low-price drug firm drives out the honest producer**: buyers cannot see inside the medicine, they buy the cheaper one, and the honest maker — whose real medicine costs more to make — loses the market. The market's own working, unwatched, rewards the worse good. The same section names who pays for that result: **consumers are endangered**. A weak or false medicine is not a bargain; it is a harm sold as a saving. State supervision exists so that what is sold as medicine must be medicine — the consumer is protected against a danger they cannot test with their own eyes before buying. The book's wider claim completes the lesson: **the body of state laws exists to facilitate and encourage exchanges that benefit buyers and sellers**. Regulation, in this account, is not the enemy of the market. It is the frame that keeps exchange honest, so that the exchanges which benefit both sides can go on and the ones built on cheating cannot.
