# PDVSA — Venezuela as a world oil power

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: the world's greatest holder of black gold. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The world's greatest holder of black gold

The senior book's oil chapter opens with the country's standing: Venezuela is consolidated as the **world's greatest holder of "black gold"**, with reserves estimated to last about **300 years**.

It is also **one of the main oil exporters worldwide**. Holder of the largest reserves and a leading exporter — that double standing is the chapter's foundation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Oil in everyday products

Most everyday products come from oil, the chapter says — and lists them so pupils see the barrel inside their own homes: **plastics from polyethylene, fuels, nylon, paints**.

Oil in this book is not only an export figure. It is the material of ordinary life, which is why the industry that produces it matters to every Venezuelan, exporter or not.

## Sub lesson 3 — PDVSA — an industry committed to the people

The chapter's subject is **PDVSA** — Petróleos de Venezuela S.A., the **state oil industry**.

It is presented in a defined way: as **an industry committed to the people**. The world's largest reserves, in the book's telling, are held by a state industry whose commitment is to the country's own population — the sovereignty argument of the whole senior book, applied to its biggest asset.
