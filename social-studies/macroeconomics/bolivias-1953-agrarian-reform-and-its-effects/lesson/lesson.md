## Sub lesson 1 — 1953 — 4.5% of owners hold 70% of the land

In 1953 agriculture contributed 33% of GDP, while the hacendados controlled 92% of cultivable land and 4.5% of rural owners held 70% of it, across 8,137 great and medium haciendas where pongueaje — unpaid service — persisted despite its 1945 abolition. That was the order the reform struck.

## Sub lesson 2 — 'The land is for who works it'

The reform abolished the latifundio and servitude and turned hacienda colonos and pongos into small proprietors, incorporating almost 2,000,000 Bolivians into the national economy under the banner 'la tierra es para quien la trabaja' — the land is for who works it. But land alone was not enough to generate wealth: the state did not back production with infrastructure or services, and the cooperative experiments all ended in failure.

## Sub lesson 3 — Minifundio, surcofundio, and the road to the cities

The compendium follows the reform's decay: the minifundio shrank into 'surcofundio' — plots of a few furrows — driving migration to the cities, descampesinación, and an urban informal sector the cities could not absorb; while in the east, extensive fertile land and oil-royalty capital generated an agroindustry and an agrarian bourgeoisie.
