# The business cycle

## Sub lesson 1 — Fluctuations around a trend

The business cycle is the pattern of irregular, unpredictable fluctuations around an economy's trend — not truly cyclical, despite the name: no timetable governs it. Output, jobs and spending swing above and below the trend line as confidence and investment rise and fall.

## Sub lesson 2 — Boom and recession

Boom or prosperity tops the wave: spending and investment feed each other through the multiplier, and the economy overheats. Recession follows as overheating breeds caution — firms cut investment, hiring slows, and the wave turns down. The turn, not any single bad month, marks the phase change.

## Sub lesson 3 — Depression and recovery

Depression, slump or trough bottoms the wave: confidence at its lowest, investment and spending at their weakest. Recovery or expansion climbs out: confidence returns, investment resumes, and output grows back toward — and past — the trend. The four phases are one wave, and policy's art is reading which stretch the economy is in.

## Sub lesson 4 — Expansion, peak, contraction, trough — the Grade 12 names

Grade 12 names the same wave with four phase words: expansion, peak, contraction (recession) and trough, ridden around the trend. The cycle is the frame that makes unemployment and inflation move together through time — and the frame that countercyclical policy, later in the Grade 12 course, tries to smooth.
