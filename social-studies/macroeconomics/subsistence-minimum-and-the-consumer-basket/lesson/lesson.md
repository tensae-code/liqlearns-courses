# The subsistence minimum and the consumer basket

## Do it first

How would you measure whether a family's life is decent — not rich, not poor, but enough? Write down what you would count, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Consumption — using goods and services to meet needs

Belarus's Grade 9 book meets this inside the chapter on the person as a consumer. **Consumption is using goods and services to meet needs** — eating the food, wearing the clothes, using the services a person lives by.

The **level of consumption is set by personal income**: what a person's income allows is what their consumption can reach. A higher income can meet more needs; a lower income meets fewer.

## Sub lesson 2 — The consumer basket — the typical goods and services

To measure that level, the book uses a basket. The **"consumer basket"** (спажывецкі кошык) is the set of **typical goods and services** a person consumes — the standard list of what a life ordinarily uses.

Because the basket is the same kind of list everywhere, it can be priced and compared: what the typical basket costs in one place can be set against what it costs in another.

## Sub lesson 3 — The subsistence minimum — the yardstick, and where the minimum wage comes from

The **subsistence minimum** (пражытачны мінімум) is the minimum level a person needs to live on, measured through the consumer basket. Together, the subsistence minimum and the consumer basket are the **yardstick of living standards across countries** — the measure by which one country's level of life is compared with another's.

And the yardstick is used, not just read: **from the subsistence minimum, the state sets the minimum wage** — the lowest pay is tied to what a person needs to live on.
