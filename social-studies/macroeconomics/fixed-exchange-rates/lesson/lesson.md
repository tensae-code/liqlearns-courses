# Fixed exchange rates

## Do it first

A central bank promises the dollar will cost exactly 45 birr — today, next month, next year. What must it be ready to do, every day, to keep that promise? Answer before the lesson names the system.

## Sub lesson 1 — The peg — and defending it

Under a fixed system the government or central bank sets the rate and defends it by buying and selling foreign currency from its reserves: selling reserves when the currency is pushed up, buying when it is pushed down.

[Picture 24927 — a rate pegged to a post by the central bank, with foreign-currency reserves as sandbags around it]

## Sub lesson 2 — The system's benefits

The benefits of fixing: currency stability, certainty for exporters and importers who can plan on the rate, and help in holding inflation low, since import prices cannot drift upward with a drifting rate.

## Sub lesson 3 — Devaluation and revaluation

An official raising of the foreign currency's price is a devaluation — it makes imports dearer and exports cheaper abroad. The opposite official move, lowering the foreign currency's price, is a revaluation.
