**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, France, Dominican Republic, Dominica, Bangladesh, Vietnam).**

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 12 Economic & Legal Education): the macro course is built on Vietnam's national accounts — the expenditure formula GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), GDP/GNI tables against China and Singapore, the 8.12% growth of 2022, sector-share charts 2018 vs 2022, and the 50/30/20-style family budget rule (50% essentials / 20% non-essentials / 30% saving, investment and reserve).

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 12 Ciencias Sociales 6° Secundaria): the school-leaving book ends with the national economy measured — GDP, openness, the balance of payments and per-capita income are all taught on Central Bank tables for 2010–2022, and the unit's own conclusion is critical: GDP per capita doubled while the average worker's income stagnated, so other sectors appropriated the increase.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.
