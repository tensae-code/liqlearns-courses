# Per capita GDP

## Do it first

Two countries have the same GDP, but one has ten times the people. Are their people equally well off on average? What calculation settles it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Dividing by population

Per capita GDP divides GDP by the population, giving the average income per person. It can be computed in nominal form, at current prices, or in real form, at base-year prices — the same nominal-real choice as GDP itself, carried through the division.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the average is for

Totals impress; averages inform. Per capita GDP lets countries of very different sizes be compared person against person, and lets one country's progress be followed even while its population grows — a rising total with a faster-rising population can still mean a falling average.

## Sub lesson 3 — Market price versus factor cost

GDP is usually valued at market price — what buyers pay. Market price equals factor cost — what the factors of production receive — plus net indirect taxes: indirect taxes minus subsidies. A tax on a good raises its market price above factor cost; a subsidy lowers it below.
