# Tourism in the Bahamian economy

## Do it first

Ask anyone in The Bahamas what the country lives on, and one word comes first. Before you read on, say that word — and then list three jobs that would disappear if the visitors stopped coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tourism — the number one industry

In The Bahamas' Grade 4 Primary Social Studies Digital Textbook, the Tourism spread opens with the country's first economic fact:

**Tourism is The Bahamas' number one industry.**

Most of the country's **revenue** — the money the economy takes in — comes from tourism. Visitors come to the islands, and their spending is the largest single stream of income the country has. That is why the book teaches tourism first among the country's industries, before farming and fishing.

## Sub lesson 2 — The jobs tourism carries — over half the workforce

Tourism's weight is measured in jobs as much as in money. The book teaches that **over half the workforce is employed in tourism, directly or indirectly**.

Directly means the jobs a visitor sees: the people who work where visitors stay, eat, travel and are entertained — the spread names **the kinds of tourism jobs** as its own teaching point. Indirectly means the jobs behind them: everyone whose work supplies, builds for, transports or serves the tourism trade without meeting a visitor face to face. When over half of all workers stand in those two rings together, a change in tourism is a change in the whole country's work.

## Sub lesson 3 — International and domestic tourism — a year-round business

The Grade 5 book's International and Domestic Tourism spread splits the industry in two. **International tourism** is visitors coming from abroad — in The Bahamas, resort tourism on the scale of **Atlantis**. **Domestic tourism** is Bahamians travelling and holidaying inside their own country. Both kinds spend money into the same economy.

And the Grade 4 book closes the story with a person: **Sir Stafford Sands**, who made tourism a **year-round business** — not a winter season that ends, but an industry that works in every month. A number one industry, carrying over half the workforce, fed from abroad and from home, running all year: that is tourism in the Bahamian economy.
