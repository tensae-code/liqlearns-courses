# Lesson plan — Malthus — population versus food

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Macroeconomics · **Lesson:** Malthus — population versus food
**Place in the curriculum:** Macroeconomics (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Economics expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-16).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Thomas Malthus's theory: population grows geometrically (1, 2, 4, 8, 16) while food production grows arithmetically (1, 2, 3, 4, 5), so the imbalance brings poverty and misery — population growth pressures natural resources per person, saving and investment rates fall, and economic development is delayed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on two number lines the learner can write out by hand — the doubling line of people and the adding line of food.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.
