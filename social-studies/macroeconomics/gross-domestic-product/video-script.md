# Video script — Gross domestic product

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Gross domestic product is the market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a year. Every word carries weight: market value, so things are added in money; final, so intermediate stages are not double-counted; within the borders, whoever owns the resources; in a year — GDP is specific to a period of time.

The test for GDP is place, not ownership. Production by a foreign-owned factory standing inside Ethiopia counts in Ethiopia's GDP; production by an Ethiopian firm abroad does not. That border rule is what the next lesson contrasts with GNP's nationality rule.

The same GDP equally equals the total income earned in the economy, or the total expenditure on its output — because every birr spent on a final good becomes someone's income from producing it. That three-way equality is why the next three lessons can measure GDP from spending, from incomes, or from value added, and must all arrive at the same number.
