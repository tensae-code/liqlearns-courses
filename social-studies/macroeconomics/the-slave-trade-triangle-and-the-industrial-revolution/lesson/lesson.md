# The slave-trade triangle and the Industrial Revolution

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Three sides of one triangle

Bolivia's teacher compendium draws the triangle that linked three shores: the slave trade in America, the supply of cotton to Europe, and the textile industry that powered the Industrial Revolution. Each side fed the next.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cotton — grown by enslaved hands, spun in mills

Slaves sold in America worked the cotton plantations. Their cotton crossed the Atlantic to Europe's mills, where the textile industry turned it into cloth — the industry at the front of the Industrial Revolution ran on plantation cotton.

## Sub lesson 3 — Industrial demand deepens the slave economies

The traffic ran both ways: industrial demand for cotton deepened the slave economies of the Americas. The more cloth the mills could sell, the more cotton was demanded — and the more enslaved labour the plantations consumed.
