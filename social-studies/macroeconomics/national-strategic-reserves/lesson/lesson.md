# National strategic reserves

## Do it first

A drought has ruined the harvest, and the shops are running out of grain. Before you read on, say where the country could find grain quickly — and who should have been keeping it ready.

## Sub lesson 1 — What strategic reserves are

Zimbabwe's Grade 5 guide, Unit 13, teaches **national strategic reserves**: stockpiles of essential commodities that a government keeps for emergencies. The emergencies named are natural disasters, war, and economic crises — times when the normal supply of essentials can fail.

A reserve is kept before it is needed. The stockpile stands ready so that when disaster, war or crisis cuts the ordinary supply, the essentials are still there to draw on.

## Sub lesson 2 — Zimbabwe's keepers of the reserves

The unit names Zimbabwe's own reserve institutions, one for each essential. The **Grain Marketing Board** keeps grain, for food security. **NOCZIM** keeps petroleum. **ZINWA** keeps water. **ZETDC** keeps electricity. And the **Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe** stands behind the money itself, issuing local currency to banks.

Each essential a country cannot do without has a named keeper, and the keeper's job is the reserve: holding the stock, and releasing it when the emergency comes.

## Sub lesson 3 — What the reserves do for the economy

The keepers do more than store. The unit teaches that these institutions **manage supply and demand of essentials** — releasing stock when supply runs short, holding it when supply is plentiful — and in that way **keep economic stability**.

That is the point of a strategic reserve: the essentials keep flowing at steady terms even in a disaster, a war or a crisis, so one failed harvest or one broken supply line does not become a national emergency.
