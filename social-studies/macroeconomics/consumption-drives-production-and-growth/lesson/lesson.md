# Consumption drives production and growth

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold the lesson's one thing in mind and say what you already think it means: Consumption is society's "order" to production — its purpose and driving force: every positive change in consumption contributes to economic development, higher living standards and social progress, and as incomes rise, high-end consumption grows, stimulating new market segments, diversifying the market structure, raising total demand and the market's attractiveness to investment, creating jobs and increasing state budget revenue — while luxury consumption far beyond income is waste. Hold your answer — the lesson takes it in parts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Society's "order" to production

The book likens **consumption to society's "order" to production**: it is production's **purpose and driving force**. What people consume tells production what to make — and every positive change in consumption **pushes production to develop**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rising consumption develops the economy

Positive change in consumption contributes to **economic development, higher living standards and social progress**. As **incomes rise, high-end consumption grows** in type, character and scale — stimulating **new market segments**, **diversifying the market structure**, **raising total demand** and the market's **attractiveness to investment**, **creating jobs** and **increasing state budget revenue**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The limit — luxury beyond income is waste

The lesson keeps its balance: consumption drives development when it grows with income — but **luxury consumption far beyond income is waste**. The order to production must be a real order, payable from real income.
