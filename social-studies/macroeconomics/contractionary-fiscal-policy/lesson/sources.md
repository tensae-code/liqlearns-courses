**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Ethiopia, Indonesia, Costa Rica). Bolivia is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales 11, Porras y Gamboa): a commercial textbook built on the same national programme as the CONED anthology but with far more economic detail — full PAE I/II/III measure lists, the CAT export-certificate fraud, state banking's six effects, and Costa Rica's services/call-centre economy measured against the region.

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Ekonomi): national income worked in rupiah with Indonesian provincial data; cooperatives taught as the “soko guru” (main pillar) of the Indonesian economy with SHU surplus-sharing calculations; the state/regional/private ownership ladder (BUMN–BUMD–BUMS) as the map of who owns the economy.

No matching teacher guide exists in this band: the book recorded as this text's teacher guide proved to be the 2011 GEQIP Grade 12 teacher's guide for the old syllabus, not this book's companion, and supplies no answer keys for it (proof in the placements file's verification block).
