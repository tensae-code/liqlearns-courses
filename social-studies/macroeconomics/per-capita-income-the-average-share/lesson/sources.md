**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Dominica, Bangladesh, Egypt, Honduras, Indonesia, India, Greece).**

India's own way (India Grade 10 Understanding Economic Development): Development economics taught entirely on Indian data and institutions — per-capita and HDI comparisons with Sri Lanka and Kerala against Haryana, MGNREGA, Sonpur village credit, SEZs, and COPRA consumer courts.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Ekonomi): national income worked in rupiah with Indonesian provincial data; cooperatives taught as the “soko guru” (main pillar) of the Indonesian economy with SHU surplus-sharing calculations; the state/regional/private ownership ladder (BUMN–BUMD–BUMS) as the map of who owns the economy.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Panduan Guru Ekonomi Edisi Revisi) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide works with real Indonesian data — provincial per-capita PDRB tables, BPS poverty lines, the open unemployment rate (TPT), provincial minimum wages (UMP) — and adds online-loan (pinjol) financial-literacy teaching. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Indonesia is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 7 G7 Student CCSS): Grade 7 carries the set's first real Economics block — it starts from the community economy and the Banco Central's own activity classification, then builds Honduras's national story: sectors, formal/informal work (70.8% informal per INE), indicators, poverty, globalisation and the foreign lenders behind structural adjustment.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika): a workbook-style first economics course adapted from Cyprus, built around fill-in worksheets and Greek data — the labour-force shift table is Greece's own movement from farms to services.
