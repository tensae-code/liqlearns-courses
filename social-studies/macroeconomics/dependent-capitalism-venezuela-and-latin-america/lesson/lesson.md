# Dependent capitalism — Venezuela and Latin America

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: a dependent capitalist economy — what the words mean. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — A dependent capitalist economy — what the words mean

Venezuela's Grade 6 book names its own system: Venezuela and Latin America have a **dependent capitalist economy**.

The meaning is precise: their economies are conditioned by the development interests of stronger capitalist countries. Dependence is not poverty alone — it is having the direction of your economy set from outside, by interests that are not yours.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two patterns compared — dependent and developed

The book compares the two patterns in a table. The dependent pattern: high population growth, low industrial development, high poverty, and monoexport foreign trade.

The developed countries show the opposite pattern on every line. And the dependence is practical, not abstract: Venezuelan industry depends on imported machinery and technology, foreign financial capital and imported raw materials.

## Sub lesson 3 — Development redefined, and the endogenous road

The book then redefines the goal. Development is now measured by **gains in a people's standard of living** and the **least environmental damage** — the UN's human development measure.

And it names a road out: **endogenous development** — development from inside — with social property, presented as the way to economic independence. Diagnosis and prescription are both in the pupil's own book.
