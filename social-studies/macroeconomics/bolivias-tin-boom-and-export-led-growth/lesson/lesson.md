## Sub lesson 1 — Tin exports quintuple, 1900–1929

A classic case of export-led growth: tin exports quintupled between 1900 and 1929, and Bolivia supplied about a quarter of world tin production. The whole economy rode on one metal sold abroad.

## Sub lesson 2 — Three barons — 70% of exports, 3% for the country

Yet the three barons — Patiño, Aramayo, Hochschild — exported 70% of all Bolivia's exports while the country received only 3% of the gains: the rosca minero feudal, the mining-feudal ring, held the boom in three hands.

## Sub lesson 3 — Profits abroad — Patiño's loan and the low taxes

Patiño lent the government £600,000 on condition of no tax rises for five years; mining taxes stayed low and were spent on railways benefiting the miners; and the profits were not reinvested in Bolivia but went abroad. Growth led by exports, gains led out of the country.
