# The reserve requirement

## Do it first

A bank takes 100 birr of deposits. If the rules let it lend 85, then the rules change and it may lend 90 — what changed, and who changed it?

## Sub lesson 1 — A fraction locked away

Banks must hold a set fraction of deposits as reserves — the reserve requirement. Lowering the ratio releases lending capacity from the same deposits; raising it locks liquidity away. It is the bluntest of the monetary tools, because it binds every bank at once.

## Sub lesson 2 — The NBE's January 2012 cut

In January 2012 the National Bank of Ethiopia cut the minimum deposit reserve from 15% to 10% and the liquid-assets-to-deposits ratio from 25% to 20%, expressly to encourage lending, particularly to the export sector.

## Sub lesson 3 — Work the numbers

On 1,000 birr of deposits, a 15% reserve locks 150 and frees 850 for lending; at 10%, it locks 100 and frees 900. The 50 birr difference, multiplied across the banking system, is the policy.
