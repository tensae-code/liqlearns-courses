# Video script — Marginal propensity to save

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The marginal propensity to save (MPS) is the change in saving per unit change in disposable income — the slope of the saving function. Like MPC it lies between 0 and 1.

Because each extra birr can only be consumed or saved, MPS equals 1 − MPC. An MPC of 0.6 means an MPS of 0.4 at once — the two marginal propensities always add to 1.

MPS rises with income: the rich save a larger share of each extra birr than the poor, whose extra birr is mostly claimed by necessities. This is the mirror image of MPC falling as income rises.
