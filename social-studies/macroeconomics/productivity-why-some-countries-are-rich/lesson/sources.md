**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Malaysia, Dominica, Armenia, Nicaragua). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 Ciencias Sociales 8° Grado (Módulo Autoformativo, Primera Parte)): Economics arrives as "Space and Economy" for the whole continent — technology, globalisation, integration and free-trade treaties read critically from Central America's position, with the maquila and CAFTA debated from Nicaraguan experience, closing on sustainable development and quality of life.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 Estudios Sociales 7): "What we produce" — the three sectors plus a fourth information sector, taught from Nicaraguan work itself: free-zone garment workers, seasonal coffee pickers, street vendors of the informal sector, and a maize yield comparison (tractor 60 qq, oxen 40 qq, planting stick 20 qq per manzana) that makes technology's gain and its job losses concrete.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 Estudios Sociales 8): the same "Space and Economy" unit as the distance-education module, but fuller — after the sectors and the continent's three regional economies, Nicaragua's own economy is toured sector by sector (agroindustry, commerce, fishing, tourism, telecoms, mining, energy, free zones, organic production, forestry, and the MIPYMEs that are over 90% of its firms), and human development is set against GDP per capita.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 Geografía 9° Grado): the distance-education twin of the Grade 9 world-economy unit, organised around technology as the difference between continents — US centre-pivot irrigation against Latin America's rain-fed traditional farming — and closing with the integration stages ladder and the international bodies (down to FAO's Nicaragua country programme) that soften globalisation's impact.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Social Science student): Grade 11 macroeconomics is anchored in Armenian official statistics — learners compute with the Statistical Committee's data, meet the 425-item consumer basket Armenia uses for inflation, the Central Bank's 3% ±1 inflation target, and the Armenian-born Nobel laureate Daron Acemoglu's "Narrow Corridor" on institutions and prosperity.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 10 Ekonomi Tingkatan 4): a full KSSM economics course in Malay with bilingual BM–BI glossaries, ringgit-denominated tables throughout, and Malaysian institutions (Bank Negara, KWSP/EPF, PERKESO) as the living examples.
