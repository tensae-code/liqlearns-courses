# Productivity — why some countries are rich

## Do it first

Crusoe alone on his island catches fish with his hands. He spends a week weaving a net instead. Why might the hungry week make him richer for years? Say your answer first — it is the whole lesson in one story.

## Sub lesson 1 — Productivity — the one-word explanation

Productivity is the one-word explanation of living standards. The gaps are large: the US, Japan and Germany stand over ten times India, Indonesia and Nigeria in income per person; East Asia grew about 7% a year, doubling income in a decade, and China about 12%. Crusoe catching more fish with a better method is the whole theory in miniature — and GDP = total income = total expenditure anchors what is being explained. Pupils calculate Ethiopia's own growth from GDP data.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four determinants

Four determinants set productivity: physical capital per worker — tools and structures; human capital per worker — skill and knowledge in the worker; natural resources per worker — US farmland, Kuwaiti and Saudi oil, though Japan grew rich with few resources, through trade; and technological knowledge — knowing the best methods at all. A country short in one can lean on the others, as Japan shows.

## Sub lesson 3 — Kuznets's five characteristics

Kuznets's five characteristics of modern economic growth describe the full picture: rising per-capita income; rising factor productivity; structural transformation with a rising entrepreneurial class; rising saving and investment with modern financial services; and the application of scientific knowledge. Growth, when it is modern, changes how a society is built — not only how much it makes.
