# Average propensity to consume

## Do it first

One household earns 4,000 birr and spends 3,000 of it. Another earns far less and spends every birr — and borrows a little more. Work out each household's share of income spent before the lesson names it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The average share of income consumed

The average propensity to consume (APC) is total consumption divided by total disposable income — the average share of income that is consumed. Worked example: consumption of 3,000 birr out of income of 4,000 birr gives an APC of 3,000 ÷ 4,000 = 0.75.

[Picture 24901 — two households, one poor with an APC bar over 100%, one richer below it]

## Sub lesson 2 — APC falls as income rises

Keynes held that the APC falls as income rises: richer households consume a smaller share of their income, because basic needs take a shrinking fraction of a growing income.

## Sub lesson 3 — When APC passes 1 — spending beyond income

At very low incomes the APC can exceed 1, because basic necessities force spending beyond income. The household is then dissaving — spending more than it earns, from past savings or borrowing.
