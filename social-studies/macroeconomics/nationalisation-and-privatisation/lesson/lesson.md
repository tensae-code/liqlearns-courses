# Nationalisation and privatisation

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one Cambodian example of this lesson's subject from your own life — a family business, a market price, a job, or a government programme you have heard named. Say in one sentence what you already think about it. The lesson will test that sentence.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nationalisation

Nationalisation is the state taking an enterprise into its own ownership: a business that private owners ran becomes a state enterprise, run under the state's decisions. The move changes who owns, who decides, and who receives what the enterprise earns.

## Sub lesson 2 — Privatisation

Privatisation is the opposite move: the state sells an enterprise back into private ownership. New owners put in their own money, make the decisions and carry the results. Countries privatise to bring in private capital and management, or because the state judges the enterprise better run outside its hands.

## Sub lesson 3 — What each move changes

The two moves are mirror images, and the lesson weighs what each changes: ownership, decisions, who gains and who carries losses move from one side to the other. Neither move is judged good or bad by its name — each is judged by what it does to the enterprise and the economy, the same evaluating habit the book taught for the market system itself.
