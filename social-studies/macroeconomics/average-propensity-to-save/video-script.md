# Video script — Average propensity to save

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The average propensity to save (APS) is total saving divided by total income — the average share of income saved. Worked example: saving of 500 birr out of income of 2,000 birr gives APS = 500 ÷ 2,000 = 0.25.

By construction APS = 1 − APC, since every birr of income is either consumed or saved. Add the two average propensities for any household and they must total exactly 1.

The two propensities are two readings of one split. If a household's APC is 0.75, its APS is 0.25 at once — the same 4,000 birr income divided the same two ways as the earlier worked examples.
