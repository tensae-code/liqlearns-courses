# Globalisation — what it is and its challenges

## Do it first

Think of this topic the way the Egypt Secondary 12 book opens it — Ch. 6, International economic relations: العولمة (Globalisation). What do you already know about it before you read on?

## Sub lesson 1 — What globalisation is — one knitted-together world economy

**Globalisation** is the deepening knitting-together of the world's economies into one world economic system: production, trade, money and information cross borders ever more freely, so what happens in one country's economy is felt in the others. The Egypt book teaches it as the latest stage in the development of that world economic system.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its realities — knowledge first, rules loosened, capital on the move

The book names globalisation's realities. **Knowledge and information** have become the main input of production — worth more than raw material alone. **Deregulation** loosens the state's rules over markets and trade. And **capital movements grow**: money flows between countries in ever larger amounts, seeking return, tying national economies to decisions taken far away.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its challenges — what developing countries face, and where Egypt and Africa stand

For **developing countries** globalisation is a challenge as much as a chance: they must compete inside a system whose main input is knowledge they are still building, face capital that can arrive and leave quickly, and find a place in world trade from a weaker starting point. The book closes the chapter at home — with **Egypt's and Africa's position** inside globalisation as the question the pupil must weigh.
