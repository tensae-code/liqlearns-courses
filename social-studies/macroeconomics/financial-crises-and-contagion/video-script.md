# Video script — Financial crises and contagion

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Financial crises spread from country to country: contagion. They travel through trade (a crashed partner stops buying), capital flight (investors pull money from anything that looks similar) and confidence (fear itself freezes lending).

Mexico's 1995 collapse cut its GDP by 8.1%. The Asian crisis of 1997–98 followed, then crises in Russia and Argentina — each wave teaching investors to flee faster the next time. The chain, not any single crash, is the lesson.

Because contagion is real, macroeconomic policy cannot watch only its own economy. Reserves, debt in foreign currency and dependence on flighty capital decide how hard a foreign crisis hits at home.
