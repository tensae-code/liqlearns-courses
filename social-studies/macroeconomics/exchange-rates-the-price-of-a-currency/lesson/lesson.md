# Exchange rates — the price of a currency

## Do it first

At the time this book went to press, one US dollar cost close to 45 birr. What makes that price — who is demanding dollars, and who is supplying them? List both crowds before the lesson opens the market.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forex market

The foreign-exchange market, where currencies are traded, sets exchange rates from demand for foreign currency — from tourists, importers and investors abroad — and supply of it — from export earnings, tourist spending and foreign investment.

[Picture 24925 — a currency-exchange board showing USD 45 and EUR 53 against the birr, stamped at time of going to press]

## Sub lesson 2 — The nominal rate — the price as quoted

The nominal exchange rate is quoted as the domestic price of foreign currency. At the time of going to press, 1 US dollar cost close to 45 birr and 1 euro close to 53 birr.

## Sub lesson 3 — Quoting it the other way

Quoted the other way, 1 birr bought about 0.022 dollars — the same price read from the other side. Checking: 1 ÷ 45 ≈ 0.022, so the two quotes agree.
