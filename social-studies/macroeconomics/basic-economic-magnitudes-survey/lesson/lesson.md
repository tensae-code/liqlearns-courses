In Greece's book (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika), **Ch.5 Basic economic magnitudes (chapter as a whole)** — teaches: GDP, per-capita income, living standard, unemployment and inflation taught together as the economy's headline numbers. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — The economy's headline numbers

Greece's Grade 9 workbook devotes a whole chapter to the **basic economic magnitudes** — the headline numbers by which an economy is sized and judged, taught **together as a set**: **GDP, per-capita income, the standard of living, unemployment and inflation**.

Each number answers a different question, and the chapter's point is the set: read together, they give the economy's portrait; read alone, each can flatter or frighten falsely.

## Sub lesson 2 — What each number answers

**GDP**: how much the economy produces in total. **Per-capita income**: the average share per person. **Standard of living**: how well people actually live, beyond income. **Unemployment**: how much of the labour force that wants work has none. **Inflation**: how fast prices rise and money's value falls.

Five questions — size, share, welfare, work, prices — and a number built for each. Mixing the questions is the classic error the survey exists to prevent.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading the set together

An economy can grow (GDP up) while unemployment also rises, or incomes average higher while living standards stall — the magnitudes correct each other, which is why the workbook surveys them as one chapter.

The survey is a map for the rest of the course: every later topic — distribution, the labour force, inflation — is one of these numbers studied in depth.
