# Waqf — the charitable endowment

## Do it first

Think of a school, a bridge or a hospital that was paid for not by the state and not by a business, but by one person's gift — a gift arranged to keep helping people for centuries. Before you read on, say how such a gift could be made to last forever.

## Sub lesson 1 — What waqf is — benefactors building for all to use

Iran's Grade 8 Social Studies book, in Lesson 2 "Cooperation (2)", teaches **waqf (وقف)** — an **old Iranian institution of giving**. Its **benefactors** built for everyone to use: **schools, bridges, caravanserais, water reservoirs and public baths**. The gift is not money handed out once; it is a thing built, or an asset set aside, whose use or income keeps serving people.

## Sub lesson 2 — Income directed to the needy — and an asset that may never be taken back

The book gives the second form: a benefactor **directed the income of an asset — farmland, a production workshop — to the needy**. And it states the rule that makes waqf last: **endowed assets (موقوفات) may never be appropriated and must stay as dedicated forever**. Once a thing is made waqf, no one may take it back or turn it to private use; the dedication is permanent.

## Sub lesson 3 — Waqf today — hospitals, clinics and university research

The book brings the institution into the present. Today **more than 2,500 hospitals and clinics in Iran are waqf-funded**, and **university research is financed from waqf capital**. The centuries-old form still pays for care and knowledge: the endowment's income, not the endowment itself, is what gets spent.
