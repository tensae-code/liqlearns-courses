# GDP — what a country produces inside its borders

## Do it first

A bakery's flour was bought this year and sold as bread this year. If we count both the flour's price and the bread's full price, what did we count twice? Say it before reading — GDP has a rule against exactly that.

## Sub lesson 1 — The definition

Gross Domestic Product is the total value of currently produced final goods and services produced within a country's boundary in a given period, usually a year. Four words carry loads: total value, currently produced, final, and within the boundary.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rules that protect the count

Current production only — last year's goods do not enter. Final goods only: intermediate goods, used up making other products in the same period, are excluded to avoid double counting. Location is what counts, whoever produces it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Valued at market prices

GDP is valued at market prices: GDP = Σ Pi × Qi over final outputs — each final good's price times its quantity, summed. Prices are the common measure that lets bread and bridges be added into one number.
