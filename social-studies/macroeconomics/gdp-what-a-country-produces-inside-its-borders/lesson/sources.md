**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, France, Malaysia, Dominica, Cambodia, Uzbekistan, Bulgaria, Iran, India, Costa Rica, Greece, Samoa).**

Samoa's own way (Year 10 Social Studies Book 1): Economics appears inside skills work — pupils meet GDP and trade first as Samoan and Pacific data to graph, not as definitions to memorise.

Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales 11, Porras y Gamboa): a commercial textbook built on the same national programme as the CONED anthology but with far more economic detail — full PAE I/II/III measure lists, the CAT export-certificate fraud, state banking's six effects, and Costa Rica's services/call-centre economy measured against the region.

India's own way (India Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): The Class X geography book doubles as economic geography of India — land reform and Bhoodan, manufacturing's stuck 17% GDP share, and trade read as the country's "economic barometer".

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Dated claim NOT taught as fact: the teacher guide's Activity 7.1 answer asserts Ethiopia is "the least developed country with the lowest GDP figure", contrasted with Botswana as "the fifth most developed in Africa" — with no year, figures or source given (placements, teacher guide printed p. 75). Following the placements' instruction, this claim is recorded here as dated teacher evidence needing verification and is NOT built into the lesson as fact.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 9 Social Studies): the national economy is taught inside Geography as one whole-country lesson — sectors first, then transport, then trade, closing with a GDP data table pupils must read.


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 10 Geography and Economics): at Grade 10 the pupil meets the whole national economy at once — its three sectors with the 2021 employment shares (services 74.2%, processing 21.9%, extractive 3.9%), GDP and GNI defined in a box, and the market economy explained through Bulgaria's own post-1989 reform: privatisation, commodity and labour exchanges, and prices set on the market.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 9 Social Studies): productivity is framed with Qur'anic and religious warrants (no extravagance, moderation, consultation) and capped by the resistance-economy doctrine; world inequality is read off Iran's own rising Human Development Index chart.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.
