# Rural exodus to the oilfields

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: rural and urban — who lives where and does what. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rural and urban — who lives where and does what

The Grade 5 book draws the line by size and by work. Rural population lives in places under **2,500 inhabitants** and works in agricultural activities.

Urban population lives in the towns and cities and works in industry, commerce and services. Two populations, two kinds of work — and in 1830 almost all Venezuelans were on the rural side of the line.

## Sub lesson 2 — From about 90% rural to about 90% urban

Venezuela flipped. In 1830 about **90%** of its people were rural; today about **90%** are urban — one of the sharpest countryside-to-city moves a book can show in two numbers.

A change that big needs a cause strong enough to pull millions of people out of the countryside. The book names it in the next part: oil.

## Sub lesson 3 — 1914 — oil pulls the countryside to Zulia

From **1914**, foreign oil companies in **Zulia** needed workers. Poor peasants left the countryside seeking jobs in the oilfields — the movement the book calls the **rural exodus** (éxodo campesino).

The book is honest about the result: leaving did not necessarily improve their living conditions. The exodus filled the cities and emptied the countryside, whether or not the people who moved ended up better off.
