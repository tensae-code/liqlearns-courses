# Nominal and real GDP

## Do it first

A country produces exactly the same harvest two years running, but prices rise 20% the second year. Its money GDP jumps. Did the country actually produce more? Say what rose and what did not.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nominal GDP — current prices

Nominal GDP values output at current prices — the prices of the very year being measured. It therefore rises when quantities rise and also when only prices rise, mixing the two together. Under inflation, nominal growth overstates how much better the economy actually performed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Real GDP — base-year prices

Real GDP values output at constant base-year prices: every year is priced with the same, fixed price list. Prices can no longer move the total, so real GDP rises only when quantities rise. In the base year itself, nominal and real GDP are equal, because current prices and base prices are the same list.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where real growth comes from

Since prices are frozen in the calculation, real GDP growth can come only from producing more: more capital and more labour put to work, or productivity gains — more output squeezed from the same inputs. The book works the formula on a two-good apples-and-oranges economy, where the arithmetic of both measures can be followed by hand.
