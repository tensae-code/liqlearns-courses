# Video script — Schools of macroeconomic thought

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The classical school holds that markets self-correct: wages and prices adjust, and government intervention mostly gets in the way; its modern supply-side branch draws the Laffer curve, arguing lower tax rates can raise revenue. The neoclassical school builds on the same faith with formal models of rational choosers.

The Keynesian school answers the Depression: demand fails, so government must manage it with spending and taxes. The monetarist school, led by Friedman, distrusts that discretion and prescribes a constant money-growth rule instead.

The new classical school argues policy ineffectiveness: fully anticipated policy cannot move real output, because people adjust in advance. The new Keynesian school replies that nominal rigidities — sticky prices and wages — make demand policy effective again. The whole course keeps returning to this argument.
