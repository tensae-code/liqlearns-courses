# Disguised unemployment — employed, but adding nothing

## Do it first

Six workers weed a plot; the harvest would be identical with three. How many are 'unemployed' in disguise — and why does no statistic show them as jobless?

## Sub lesson 1 — Employed, but redundant

Disguised (hidden) unemployment is workers in redundant work that adds minimal or no productivity — too many workers filling too few jobs. Every person is busy; the work does not need them all.

[Picture 24071 — six workers on a plot whose harvest stays the same when three step away]

## Sub lesson 2 — The removal test

The test: removing some of the workers would not reduce output. If output stands unchanged with fewer hands, the removed hands were disguisedly unemployed all along — employed in form, unemployed in contribution.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hidden, and common where labour is surplus

It does not show up as open joblessness — nobody is searching, because everybody 'has work'. It is tied to labour surplus and is presented as very common in developing countries such as Ethiopia.
