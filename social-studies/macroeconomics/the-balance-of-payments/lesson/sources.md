**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Malaysia, Dominican Republic, Egypt, Fiji, Indonesia, Pakistan, Costa Rica, Nigeria, Greece, Samoa, Bhutan). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class X Economics X): Class X turns from concepts to Bhutan itself — the Bhutanese economy's features, public finance, GNH as the official development measure alongside GDP, Bhutan's balance of payments and rupee-crisis case material.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class XI Economics XI): Class XI re-teaches the Class IX foundations at full analytical depth (elasticity calculation, equilibrium shifts, BoP correction) and adds Bhutan-specific system, Five-Year Plan and trade-policy chapters.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Economics Guide): Year 13 economics is anchored on Samoa itself — the Central Bank of Samoa (not a generic central bank), one oil supplier as a Samoan monopoly, Samoan government revenue/expenditure tables, and a closing national-economy session built from the Central Bank Governor's 2002 address.

Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 12 New Economics SS3): SS3 is Nigeria's national-economy story as a course — petroleum and NNPC/OPEC, planning and the Structural Adjustment Programme, NEEDS and Vision 2020, bank consolidation, privatisation and the anti-corruption agencies — with the Asian Tigers and Japan taught explicitly as lessons for Nigeria.

Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales CONED 11): the full national economic story in one book — agro-export dependence, the first state-intervention reforms of 1914, import substitution under the welfare state, then the 1980s crisis and the PAE adjustment that opened the economy and courted Intel and the free zones.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 12 Economics Class 12): the Class 12 book runs the standard macro course (national income, money, banks, public finance, trade) and then turns the same tools onto Pakistan itself for six chapters — Pakistan's sectors, national income, planning, transport and manpower, banking system, government finances and foreign trade — closing with the Islamic economic system (Zakat, Ushr, interest-free banking) taught as a full alternative system, not a footnote.


Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Ekonomi): growth measured on Indonesia's own PDB series; trade taught through Indonesia's nickel-export case; budgets are Indonesia's real APBN/APBD with village funds (Dana Desa) as the regional-budget story.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Panduan Guru Ekonomi) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide's worked answers run on the student book's Indonesian data — PDB growth series, Indonesia's trade and balance-of-payments cases, the APBN/APBD. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Indonesia is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 2): International and development topics are worked with Fiji data — a Fiji CPI table to a 2008 base year, Fiji's January 2010 balance of payments, and Fiji unemployment with cane-cutting seasonal work and government job actions.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 12 Ciencias Sociales 6° Secundaria): the school-leaving book ends with the national economy measured — GDP, openness, the balance of payments and per-capita income are all taught on Central Bank tables for 2010–2022, and the unit's own conclusion is critical: GDP per capita doubled while the average worker's income stagnated, so other sectors appropriated the increase.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).
