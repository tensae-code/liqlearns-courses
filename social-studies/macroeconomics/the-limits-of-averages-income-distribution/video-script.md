# Video script — The limits of averages — income distribution

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

GDP and even per capita GDP are averages: they divide a total by a headcount and report one number. That number cannot show who earns what — it is silent about the distribution of income across the population that produced it.

Two economies can carry identical per-capita figures while distributing income in opposite ways: one spreading income evenly, the other concentrating it in a few large slices with many tiny ones. Judged by the average alone they are twins; judged by their people's lives they are not.

This limitation is why inequality measures, poverty analysis and other development indicators now accompany GDP in serious policy analysis. The averages open the story; they cannot finish it. The development unit later in this course takes up exactly where this limit leaves off.
