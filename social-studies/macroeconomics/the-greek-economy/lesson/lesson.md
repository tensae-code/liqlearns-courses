# The Greek economy

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.11 §7 The Greek economy** — teaches: the structure and story of Greece's own national economy. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — A textbook that ends at home

Greece's Grade 12 textbook closes its final chapter with **the Greek economy itself** — the structure and story of the national economy the students live in, after the EU section and the whole theory course behind it.

The placement is the lesson: theory is completed by turning it on home ground — the sectors, trade and European membership of Greece read with the course's own instruments.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading a national economy's structure

To read a national economy is to ask the course's questions of one country: what does it produce (its sectors), whom does it trade with and in what, how is it placed inside its integration bloc — for Greece, the **European Union** of the previous section.

Greece's students meet the same structure the Grade 9 book showed in motion: a labour force that shifted over time from the primary sector toward services — the national story told in the economy's own proportions.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why every course needs this lesson

A principles course that never lands home leaves students able to analyse everywhere except where they live. The Greek book's order — world theory first, the home economy last — makes the home economy the course's final examination of itself.

Other countries' books in this expansion do the same in their own way; Greece's version is distinctive only in its place: one section, at the very end, where the whole Lykeio course is finally pointed at Greece.
