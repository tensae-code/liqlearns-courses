## Sub lesson 1 — Latin America — capital flight and collapsing export prices

When the 1929 crisis crossed the Atlantic, Latin America felt it as capital flight and an abrupt fall in export prices: the money left, and what the continent sold abroad suddenly earned far less.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bolivia — minerals unsold, credit suspended

In Bolivia, the industrial countries' falling imports depressed mineral consumption and prices — the tin economy's market shrank — while the international financial crisis suspended external credit and capital left the country to service the external debt.

## Sub lesson 3 — A relatively quick recovery — public policies

Latin America's recovery was relatively quick, the compendium notes, thanks to proactive public policies — states that acted, rather than waiting for the market to heal itself. The New Deal in the United States, taught on the next pages, is the famous case of the same answer.
