# Video script — Two kinds of inflation — demand-pull and cost-push

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Demand-pull inflation arises when aggregate demand grows faster than productive capacity at or near full employment — 'too much money chasing too few goods'. Demand pressure from government, entrepreneurs or households bids prices up against a supply that cannot grow as fast.

Cost-push (supply-side) inflation arises when aggregate supply continuously falls — through bad weather, wage or input-price rises, raw-material shortages, power breakdowns or strikes — so less output faces the same demand and prices rise.

Ask which moved first: if spending surged against full shelves' capacity, it is demand-pull; if supply shrank under rising costs while demand stood still, it is cost-push. The first mover is the diagnosis.
