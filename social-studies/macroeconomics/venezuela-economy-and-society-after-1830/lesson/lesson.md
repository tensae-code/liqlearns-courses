# Venezuela — economy and society after 1830

## Sub lesson 1 — A poor new republic and its debt

After independence, the Grade 5 book says, Venezuela faced poverty and a **scarce national treasury** — too little to drive the economy, and too little even to pay its share of Gran Colombia's external debt.

The new republic was politically free and economically weak at the same time. That tension runs through everything the book says next about the century.

## Sub lesson 2 — Free trade, land grants and the coffee economy

Free-trade laws **favoured merchants and export landowners**, while others stayed poor — the book names winners and losers, not just policies.

Land grants to officers created new latifundistas, and coffee became the main export. The book gives the evidence as a data table: export weights in 1830–31 against 1845–46 for indigo, cacao, coffee and hides — coffee's rise is in the numbers.

## Sub lesson 3 — Moneylenders and the 1834 Law of Freedom of Contracts

World coffee prices decided whether Venezuela had a **bonanza or a crisis** — the monoexport pattern again, now in prices.

Producers borrowed from moneylenders to keep going, until the **1834 Law of Freedom of Contracts** reshaped the borrowing. A country whose income hangs on one world price borrows in the bad years; the book makes its pupils see the chain.
