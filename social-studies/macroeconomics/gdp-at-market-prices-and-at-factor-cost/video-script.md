# Video script — GDP at market prices and at factor cost

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Greece's Grade 12 textbook measures GDP two ways. At market prices, output is valued at what buyers actually pay. At factor cost, it is valued at what the factors of production receive for making it.

An indirect tax (like VAT) raises the buyer's price above what the producer's factors receive. A subsidy does the opposite: the state pays part, so buyers pay less than factors receive.

Use market prices to value what the economy's output costs its buyers and to compare spending. Use factor cost to see what production actually earned for labour, land and capital — the incomes generated.
