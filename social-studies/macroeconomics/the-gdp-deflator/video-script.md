# Video script — The GDP deflator

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The GDP deflator is nominal GDP divided by real GDP, times 100. Because both totals measure the same year's output, the quantities cancel out of the ratio and only the price level remains: the deflator is an economy-wide price measure, expressed relative to the base year, where it equals exactly 100.

The book works the formula on nominal GDP of 950 million against real GDP of 750 million: 950 divided by 750, times 100, gives a deflator of about 127. Read it as: prices account for a rise of some 27% over the base year — over a quarter of the nominal total is price increase, not extra production.

The deflator also runs backwards: dividing nominal GDP by the deflator (and multiplying by 100) converts a current-price total into its real, base-price equivalent — stripping the price rises back out. The unit's activity applies the same calculation to the National Bank of Ethiopia's actual 2019/20 figures.
