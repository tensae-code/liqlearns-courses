# The role of the state in the economy

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one Cambodian example of this lesson's subject from your own life — a family business, a market price, a job, or a government programme you have heard named. Say in one sentence what you already think about it. The lesson will test that sentence.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the state does

The state is an economic actor in its own right: it makes the laws business runs under, provides public services and works, collects taxes and spends, and steps in where the market alone fails. Cambodia's book sets these jobs out as the state's role in economic activity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the role is needed

The role is needed because the market, for all its strengths, does not do everything: some goods everyone needs cannot be sold one by one, some failures leave people unprotected, and the whole economy sometimes needs steering. The state exists in the economy to do what the market alone will not.

## Sub lesson 3 — State and market together

The lesson's picture is not state instead of market, or market instead of state. It is the two together: the market organising most production and exchange, the state setting the frame, providing what the market cannot, and answering for the whole economy's direction — the picture the next lessons on intervention, nationalisation and reform fill in.
