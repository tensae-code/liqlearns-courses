**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Syria, Jordan, Bulgaria). Zimbabwe is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 6 Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 6's financial literacy is a tour of Zimbabwe's own financial institutions by name — CBZ, Stanbic and FBC banks, NBS building society, Zimnat and Nicoz Diamond insurers — with microfinance explained through market stalls and sewing projects that big banks will not lend to.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 7 Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 7 teaches the country's own economic story outright — land reform as redistribution for economic empowerment, human capital built through VTCs and colleges, self-reliance through cooperatives and vending, and tourism counted in foreign currency and jobs — and its financial-literacy unit reaches gross and net profit and the profit-and-loss account.


Syria's own way (Syria Grade 4 Social Studies): Grade 4 carries a whole unit named Pillars of the Economy in Syria — the population's economic activities (agriculture, industry, trade, and services such as education, health, transport and tourism) — and tourism closes the unit as its fourth pillar, taught from Syria's own coast, mountains, forests, desert and sites such as Palmyra. This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 5 Term 2): Grade 5 gets the first formal market lesson in the whole set — supply and demand open with frost-damaged vegetables and Ramadan meat prices, trade is read off Aqaba port and Jordan's real export/import lists, and services are toured through Jordan's own schools platform, hospitals and tourist sites.


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 7 Geography and Economics): the economy of a country is taught as three families of activities — extractive, processing and serving — and Bulgaria's own story is told in four stages, from the craft towns and the first Sliven textile factory of 1834 through nationalisation and the cooperatives to the post-1989 market transition and EU membership in 2007.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 6 Geography and Economics): each continent's economy is read the same way — what its extractive activities lead the world in, how developed its processing and services are, and which economic union its countries have built — so a Bulgarian pupil can line the continents up side by side.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 10 Geography and Economics): at Grade 10 the pupil meets the whole national economy at once — its three sectors with the 2021 employment shares (services 74.2%, processing 21.9%, extractive 3.9%), GDP and GNI defined in a box, and the market economy explained through Bulgaria's own post-1989 reform: privatisation, commodity and labour exchanges, and prices set on the market.
