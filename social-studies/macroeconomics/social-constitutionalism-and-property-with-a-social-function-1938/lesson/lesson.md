## Sub lesson 1 — 1938 — from liberal to social constitutionalism

The 1938 Convention abandoned liberal constitutionalism — a limited state that does not intervene in the economy — for social constitutionalism: the state must watch over its members' wellbeing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Property — a right with a social function

In the new order, property is a right granted by the state, retained only while it fulfils a legitimate social function. Own — but own usefully for society, or the right itself lapses.

## Sub lesson 3 — 'The problem of the Indian is the problem of the land'

In the same debate the Convention heard the sentence that frames Bolivia's land question: 'the problem of the Indian is the problem of the land' — indigenous poverty rooted not in culture but in who holds the land, the question the 1953 agrarian reform would try to answer.
