# Income policy — wage and price controls

## Do it first

Fiscal and monetary restraint are slow. Could a government simply order wages and prices to stop rising? What would you expect to happen next?

## Sub lesson 1 — Controlling wages and prices directly

Income policies are economy-wide wage and price controls, running from voluntary guidelines to mandatory freezes. They attack inflation at the price tag itself, rather than through demand.


[Picture 25253 — a wage slip and a price tag under a glass cover]
## Sub lesson 2 — When they are used

They are used against inflation when fiscal and monetary restraint fails, or as their complement — a direct brake while the indirect brakes take hold. A freeze that outlives its welcome distorts every price it holds.

## Sub lesson 3 — The developing-country twist

In developing countries the book also notes the reverse use: keeping real wages growing with productivity, as a sustainable engine of domestic demand. Rising real wages, earned by rising output per worker, spend the economy forward.
