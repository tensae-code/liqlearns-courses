# What decides investment?

## Do it first

A firm can borrow cheaply, expects strong sales, and faces a new tax on profits. Two forces push it to invest and one pushes back — name them before the lesson lists the determinants and takes them to Ethiopia.

## Sub lesson 1 — Interest, expectations and taxes

Investment falls as the interest rate — the cost of borrowed funds — rises. It rises with optimistic expectations of future sales, and falls when business taxes cut expected after-tax profit. Rising national income also induces more of it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's firm challenges

Grounded in Ethiopia, CSA and World Bank surveys list firms' chief challenges as infrastructure (electricity, roads, land, water, communications), access to finance, rules and regulations, lack of raw materials, informal-sector practices and lack of markets.

## Sub lesson 3 — The ease-of-doing-business rank

The challenges are summarised in the ease-of-doing-business index, on which Ethiopia ranked 159th of 190 countries in 2019 — a ranking that prompted government reforms to make investing easier.
