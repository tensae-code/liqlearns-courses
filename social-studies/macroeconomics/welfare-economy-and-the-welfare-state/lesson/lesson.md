# Welfare economy and the welfare state

## Do it first

Imagine a country where the state itself guarantees that everyone's basic needs are met well, and where a person who loses their job still receives an allowance. Who pays for that? Say what you think before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The welfare economy — basic needs guaranteed by the state

In a **welfare economy**, the state guarantees quality basic needs for everyone, and endless employment opportunity. Meeting people's basic needs well is not left to chance or to the market alone — the state takes it as its own job.

## Sub lesson 2 — Work, safety nets and unemployment allowances

The welfare state runs **social safety nets** and **unemployment allowances**: people who lose work or fall into difficulty are supported, so that losing a job does not mean losing the basic needs of life.

## Sub lesson 3 — Progressive tax pays for it — the Scandinavian example

The money comes from a **progressive tax on the rich**, redistributed to essential sectors. The book's example is the **Scandinavian countries**, where this welfare system is the model in practice.
