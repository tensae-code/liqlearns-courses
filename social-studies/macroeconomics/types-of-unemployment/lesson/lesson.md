# Types of unemployment

## Do it first

Three people have no job: one quit last week to find better work, one lost work when coffee buyers stopped ordering, one trained for a trade nobody hires any more. Same problem, or three different ones?

## Sub lesson 1 — Frictional — the gap between jobs

Frictional unemployment is the short, normal gap while a worker moves between jobs or enters work for the first time. It never falls to zero, even in a healthy economy, because people always move.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cyclical — when demand falls

Cyclical unemployment rises when demand falls across the economy. The book's example: a drop in overseas demand for Ethiopian coffee idles coffee workers — their skills are fine; the buyers vanished.

## Sub lesson 3 — Structural — and the hidden kinds

Structural unemployment is a mismatch: the worker's skills or place no longer match any vacancy, and retraining, not recovery, is the fix. Around these three sit underemployment (too little or too low work), open unemployment, and disguised unemployment — workers who look employed but add almost nothing, put at 30–35% in underdeveloped economies.
