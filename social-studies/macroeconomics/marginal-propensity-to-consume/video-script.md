# Video script — Marginal propensity to consume

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is the change in consumption per unit change in disposable income — the slope of the consumption function. It lies strictly between 0 and 1. Worked example: income up 1,000 birr, consumption up 600 birr, so MPC = 600 ÷ 1,000 = 0.6 — and the other 40% is saved.

MPC falls as income rises, so the poor have a higher MPC than the rich: a poor household spends most of any extra birr on necessities, while a rich household can save most of it.

A fuller worked example builds consumption of 640 birr: autonomous consumption of 80 birr plus MPC 0.7 applied to income of 800 birr — 80 + (0.7 × 800) = 80 + 560 = 640 birr. The 80 is the autonomous part and the 560 is the induced part.
