# Tariffs and quotas — restricting trade

## Do it first

A government wants fewer imported shoes sold at home. It could tax each imported pair, or cap the number of pairs allowed in. Which tool works through price, and which through quantity? Decide before the lesson defines both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Trade policy, for and against

Trade policies encourage or restrict trade. Restrictions come in two families — price form and quantity form — and this lesson's two tools are their standard examples.

[Picture 24923 — two gates — one charging a toll (tariff), one with a counter that closes after a fixed number of crates (quota)]

## Sub lesson 2 — The tariff — a tax at the border

A tariff is a customs or import duty: it raises the import's domestic price, cuts its demand, and raises government revenue for the state that levies it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The quota — and why governments restrict

An import quota fixes the maximum volume allowed in, allotted among importers. The stated reasons for restricting trade at all: protecting domestic jobs, protecting infant industries, maintaining living standards, equalising production costs, national security, and cultural or sociological concerns.
