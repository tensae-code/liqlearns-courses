Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 11 Economics TG): the standalone Senior High Economics course re-runs the five-unit frame at full depth — human resources and human capital first, then price mechanism and inflation, economic ideologies (conservatism, liberalism, protectionism, free market), international trade, and a closing unit on the communist economy mirroring Grade 10's capitalism unit. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 12 Economics TG): Grade 12 Economics turns analytical — demand and supply with graphs, elasticity coefficients, the incidence of a sales tax drawn between producer and consumer, market structures illustrated with PNG firms, and a full unit on trade relations and foreign aid, the economy PNG actually lives in. Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 12 New Economics SS3): SS3 is Nigeria's national-economy story as a course — petroleum and NNPC/OPEC, planning and the Structural Adjustment Programme, NEEDS and Vision 2020, bank consolidation, privatisation and the anti-corruption agencies — with the Asian Tigers and Japan taught explicitly as lessons for Nigeria.

Colombia's own way (Colombia Grade 5 Escuela Nueva G5 Cartilla 1): Economics arrives as the history of Colombian work itself — campesinos, coffee labourers, artisans threatened by free trade, and the first unions and strikes.

United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

India's own way (India Grade 10 Understanding Economic Development): Development economics taught entirely on Indian data and institutions — per-capita and HDI comparisons with Sri Lanka and Kerala against Haryana, MGNREGA, Sonpur village credit, SEZs, and COPRA consumer courts.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 Geografía 9° Grado): the distance-education twin of the Grade 9 world-economy unit, organised around technology as the difference between continents — US centre-pivot irrigation against Latin America's rain-fed traditional farming — and closing with the integration stages ladder and the international bodies (down to FAO's Nicaragua country programme) that soften globalisation's impact.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 Ekonomi): growth measured on Indonesia's own PDB series; trade taught through Indonesia's nickel-export case; budgets are Indonesia's real APBN/APBD with village funds (Dana Desa) as the regional-budget story.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 2): International and development topics are worked with Fiji data — a Fiji CPI table to a 2008 base year, Fiji's January 2010 balance of payments, and Fiji unemployment with cane-cutting seasonal work and government job actions.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 3 G3 Student CCSS): Grade 3 scales up from family to country — occupations and production for the whole nation, social classes by income, the production-distribution-exchange-consumption chain, and exports/imports through customs with the arancel explained.
