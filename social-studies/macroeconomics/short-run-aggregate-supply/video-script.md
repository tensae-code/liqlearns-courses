# Video script — Short-run aggregate supply

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Short-run aggregate supply, SRAS, slopes upward: at higher prices firms supply more in the short run, because some of their costs — above all wages set by contract — have not yet risen with prices, so each extra unit earns more.

SRAS shifts with input prices (wages, oil), productivity, taxes and subsidies on production, and expectations. A wage rise or an oil-price rise shifts SRAS left; a productivity gain shifts it right. These are the supply-side counterpart to AD's shifters.

The short run is the period while some prices, especially wages, are still stuck. In the long run all prices adjust, and supply no longer depends on the price level at all — the long-run curve stands vertical at potential output, as the next lessons show.
