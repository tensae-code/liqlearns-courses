# What moves saving?

## Do it first

Two neighbours earn the same and face the same prices, yet one has a full savings jar and the other an empty one. List the forces that could explain the difference before the lesson counts seven.

## Sub lesson 1 — Income — level and distribution

Saving responds first to the level of income: more income makes more saving possible. Its distribution matters too — greater inequality raises total saving, because the rich save a larger share of their income than the poor.

[Picture 24907 — seven labelled levers beside a savings jar, the miser and spendthrift at either end]

## Sub lesson 2 — Expectations, interest, wealth and tax

Expectations about future prices and income steer saving today; the rate of interest rewards it; the level of wealth already held changes how much more a household feels it must add; and the level of direct taxes decides how much income is left to save from.

## Sub lesson 3 — The saver's own nature

Last comes the individual's own nature — the book's own contrast is the miser, who saves at almost any income, against the spendthrift, who saves at almost none. The same income and the same prices can therefore produce opposite saving in two people.
