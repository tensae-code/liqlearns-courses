## Sub lesson 1 — The whole-economy branch

Macroeconomics is the branch concerned with the economy as a whole and its aggregates — national income and output, total employment, the general price level. Its fundamental problem is full employment of resources, and its tools are aggregate demand and aggregate supply.

## Sub lesson 2 — Adding up — and the scope

The guide defines the method (teacher guide — evidence): macroeconomics is adding up the economic activity of all households and all businesses in all markets to get the economy's overall demand and supply. Its scope of aggregative analysis is four factors: national income, employment, the general price level and economic growth.

## Sub lesson 3 — Micro and macro compared — in Ethiopian examples

The comparison table is anchored in Ethiopian examples: the price of teff or of an orange and the saving of a named person are micro; the GDP of Ethiopia and Ethiopia's general price level are macro. The two branches are complementary, not rivals — one economy, two heights of view.

## Sub lesson 4 — Born from the Depression — Keynes, 1936

Grade 12 opens the subject again at full scale: macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole — total output, total employment, the general price level — rather than single markets. The book dates the field's birth to Keynes's 1936 General Theory, written because classical self-correction visibly failed in the 1930s Depression. Its focus ever since: the aggregates, and the policies that move them.

The Grade 12 road ahead runs through eight units in order: fundamental concepts of macroeconomics; aggregate demand and supply; market failure and consumer protection; policy instruments; tax theory and practice; poverty and inequality; macroeconomic reforms in Ethiopia; and the economy, environment and climate change — all of it standing on Grade 11's micro foundations.
