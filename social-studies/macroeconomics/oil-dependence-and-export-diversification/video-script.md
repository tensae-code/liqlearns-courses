# Video script — Oil dependence and export diversification

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iran's Grade 6 Social Studies book opens its energy chapter with Lesson 7 "Black gold" (طلای سیاه). Oil is a **natural heritage that is used up**: every barrel taken out is gone, and what the present generation extracts cannot be extracted again by the next. The book sets the class a debate on exactly this: **whether the present generation may extract all the oil and gas without thinking of future generations**. The first well of the story is named — the **Masjid Soleiman well, about 100 years ago**. The same lesson tells who took the profit for most of that century: **foreign oil companies extracted and refined Iran's oil and took great profits under contracts**, until **after the Islamic Revolution Iranian specialists took over**. The Grade 8 book widens the picture to the whole region: most countries of **Southwest Asia** hold oil and gas reserves yet **depend economically on oil exports**, while **importing machinery, food, medicine and consumer goods** from other countries — selling one thing, buying everything else. Both books draw the same answer. Iran **tries not to depend on oil exports and to sell other goods abroad** — to spread what the country earns across many exports instead of resting it on the one heritage that runs out. That spreading is **export diversification**: the deliberate move from one export that finishes to many exports that can continue.
