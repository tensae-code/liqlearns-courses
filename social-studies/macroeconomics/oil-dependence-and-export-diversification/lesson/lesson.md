# Oil dependence and export diversification

## Do it first

Imagine a country that earns almost all its money from one thing under the ground — and that thing will one day run out. Before you read on, say what dangers you can already see for that country.

## Sub lesson 1 — Black gold — a natural heritage that is used up

Iran's Grade 6 Social Studies book opens its energy chapter with Lesson 7 "Black gold" (طلای سیاه). Oil is a **natural heritage that is used up**: every barrel taken out is gone, and what the present generation extracts cannot be extracted again by the next. The book sets the class a debate on exactly this: **whether the present generation may extract all the oil and gas without thinking of future generations**. The first well of the story is named — the **Masjid Soleiman well, about 100 years ago**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The foreign-companies era — and dependence today

The same lesson tells who took the profit for most of that century: **foreign oil companies extracted and refined Iran's oil and took great profits under contracts**, until **after the Islamic Revolution Iranian specialists took over**. The Grade 8 book widens the picture to the whole region: most countries of **Southwest Asia** hold oil and gas reserves yet **depend economically on oil exports**, while **importing machinery, food, medicine and consumer goods** from other countries — selling one thing, buying everything else.

## Sub lesson 3 — Selling other goods instead — export diversification

Both books draw the same answer. Iran **tries not to depend on oil exports and to sell other goods abroad** — to spread what the country earns across many exports instead of resting it on the one heritage that runs out. That spreading is **export diversification**: the deliberate move from one export that finishes to many exports that can continue.
