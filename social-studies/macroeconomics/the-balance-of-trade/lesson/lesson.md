# The balance of trade

## Do it first

In 2020 one country sold about 535 billion dollars more than it bought; another bought about 10.7 billion dollars more than it sold. Which position would you rather defend as a finance minister?

## Sub lesson 1 — Exports minus imports of goods

The balance of trade compares what a country earns from exports with what it spends on imports of goods. Exports greater than imports give a surplus; imports greater than exports give a deficit. (Trade in services belongs to the wider balance on goods and services, and to the balance of payments.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's deficit, China's surplus

For 2020 the book contrasts Ethiopia's trade deficit of about US$10.7 billion with China's surplus of about US$535 billion. Same measure, opposite worlds: one country must find foreign currency to cover the gap every year.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the balance matters

A lasting deficit must be financed — by reserves, borrowing or aid — and it is the external challenge that foreign-exchange policy, later in this course, tries to answer. A surplus brings foreign currency in, but can rest on holding home spending down.
