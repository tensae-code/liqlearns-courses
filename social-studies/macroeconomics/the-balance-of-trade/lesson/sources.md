Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 8 Social Studies): Grade 8 teaches the Arab world as one economic space — its crops, animals, oil and minerals, the conditions that make industry succeed, and its trade, foreign and domestic — after a first term spent on the same resources governorate by governorate at home.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 12 Social Studies & Life Skills): Grade 12 economics is the development unit with real tables — sector shares of GDP, the trade growth and direction tables, Nepal's growth rates through the COVID year, the HDI bands, and Nurkse's vicious circle drawn as two loops, supply side and demand side.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 12 Ciencias Sociales 6° Secundaria): the school-leaving book ends with the national economy measured — GDP, openness, the balance of payments and per-capita income are all taught on Central Bank tables for 2010–2022, and the unit's own conclusion is critical: GDP per capita doubled while the average worker's income stagnated, so other sectors appropriated the increase.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Term 2): the modern economy is taught as resources-and-development of the Gulf states — oil and gas first, with the danger of depending on oil named from the start, then farming, livestock/fisheries, industry and trade, ending in GCC economic integration.

No matching teacher guide exists in this band: the book recorded as this text's teacher guide proved to be the 2011 GEQIP Grade 12 teacher's guide for the old syllabus, not this book's companion, and supplies no answer keys for it (proof in the placements file's verification block).

WORKED-ANSWER / DEFINITION ERROR CORRECTED: §1.2.5 of the book defines the balance of trade over 'goods and services'; that wider measure is the balance on goods and services. The lesson teaches the corrected definition — the balance of trade covers goods — and names the wider measure separately.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 5 Term 2): Grade 5 gets the first formal market lesson in the whole set — supply and demand open with frost-damaged vegetables and Ramadan meat prices, trade is read off Aqaba port and Jordan's real export/import lists, and services are toured through Jordan's own schools platform, hospitals and tourist sites.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 Term 1): Trade is taught from Aqaba port and the Silk Road into Jordan's real 2022 export/import charts, and the economy lesson ends in entrepreneurship — Jobs and Apple beside Jordanian start-up projects the class must hunt down itself.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 3 G3 Student CCSS): Grade 3 scales up from family to country — occupations and production for the whole nation, social classes by income, the production-distribution-exchange-consumption chain, and exports/imports through customs with the arancel explained.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 5 G5 Teacher CCSS) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide mirrors the student book; the one additional-theory fact placed here is Honduras's trade history — free-trade treaties and economic expansion, with a mostly trade-deficit record since 2010. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Honduras is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.
