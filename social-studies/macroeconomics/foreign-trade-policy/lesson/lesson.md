# Foreign trade policy

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — A policy for trade with others

Foreign trade policy is the set of choices a state makes about trade across its borders: which goods may flow freely, which are encouraged, which are restricted, and on what terms. Every trading country has one, whether written as a plan or grown from its laws and taxes at the border.

## Sub lesson 2 — Encouraging and restricting

The policy works with two hands. It encourages what the country wants more of — exports that earn, imports the economy needs — and restricts what it wants less of, through border taxes, limits and rules. Each encouragement and each restriction is a choice with a reason behind it, and the lesson asks for the reasons.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why a state steers trade

A state steers trade to protect its producers while they grow, to earn foreign currency, to secure needed goods, and to bargain with partners from strength. Cambodia's chapter closes the book by widening from this policy to the world trade system itself — the frame inside which every national policy must work.
