# What GDP misses — short answer

Answer in one or two sentences, from the lesson.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Explain "The unpaid and the uncounted" in your own words.
**Answer:** GDP excludes non-market home production — the chef cooking at home adds nothing to it.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Explain "Environment and the underground" in your own words.
**Answer:** GDP misses environmental quality and black-market activity.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Explain "Distribution — the same average, opposite lives" in your own words.
**Answer:** The same GDP per person can come from 100 people earning $50,000 or 10 earning $500,000 and 90 earning nothing.

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Explain "Grade 11 — the measurement problems in full" in your own words.
**Answer:** GDP measurement problems include the large informal sector of a developing country like Ethiopia — street trade, home repairs and unpaid family work.

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Explain "Grade 11 — the fixes the book prescribes" in your own words.
**Answer:** The book's remedies include estimating informal and own-consumption activity into GDP.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** Explain "Why GDP understates output in developing countries" in your own words.
**Answer:** In developing countries GDP misses subsistence output, underreported production, the non-monetary sector, price distortion and pollution costs.
