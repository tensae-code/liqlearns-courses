## Sub lesson 1 — The encomienda — tribute and personal service

The encomienda was the colony's most widespread institution for extracting economic surplus: the king 'entrusted' a set number of indigenous people to a conqueror who, in exchange for supposed spiritual care, could collect tribute from them at the rates fixed in the Tasa. The student book adds the two payment forms — tribute in money or kind, and personal service in domestic, farm and herding labour — with the Tasa specifying goods owed, for example 250 sheep, 200 pigs and 200 fanegas of maize a year; the Leyes de Burgos (1512) and the New Laws (1542) regulated it on paper without stopping the abuse.

## Sub lesson 2 — The mita distorted — Toledo's mining draft for Potosí

Viceroy Toledo turned the Inca turn-work, the mita, into an obligatory mining draft: married men aged 18 to 50 from 16 provinces, a seventh part serving in turns. Mitayos bore the cost of the mine owners' and the Crown's great profits with labour of the lowest possible cost; inside the mine they worked up to 36 hours for 4 reales a day, buying their own candles when the azogueros failed to provide them — and mitayos were sold between mine owners who did not need them, and fled or paid bribes to escape recruitment.

## Sub lesson 3 — The obrajes — semi-slave textile workshops

The obrajes were pre-industrial textile workshops — wool, cotton, coarse cloth — owned by Spaniards or creoles, in Bolivia at La Paz, Oruro and Cochabamba. Forbidden to compete with Spain's textiles, they worked cheaper, lower-quality cloth on rustic techniques and the cheapest possible indigenous labour: a semi-slave system of exploitation.
