# The transition to capitalism in the Dominican Republic

## Do it first

A government needs money but prints none of its own. Before you read on, write down who it could borrow from, what that borrowing would cost — and what the lenders would do with the profit.

## Sub lesson 1 — The liberal experiment from the late 1870s

In the Dominican Republic's Grade 11 Social Studies book, the turn into the 20th century is told as a **tránsito al capitalismo** — a transition to capitalism. From the **late 1870s** the **liberal experiment** rested on **developing the productive forces** and **inserting the country further into international transactions** through **new export lines**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Merchant credit companies charging 24–36%

The experiment ran on borrowed money. **Governments borrowed from merchants instead of issuing notes**, and the **port merchant elites formed credit companies (compañías de crédito)** charging **exorbitant interest of 24–36% a year**. The companies' **profits were invested in productive activities** such as **cattle farms, cacao and sugar mills** — merchant money turning into capitalist production.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sugar leads, cacao and coffee bring the peasantry in

The book closes the story by crop. **Sugar production stood out in the capitalist sector**, while **cacao and coffee marked the peasantry's entry into the market economy** — the small producers joining the market through their own two crops as the mills joined it through sugar.
