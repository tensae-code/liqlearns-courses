# Human capital formation

## Do it first

A country counts its treasures: its land, its sea, its money. Before you read on, say whether the trained skill inside its people belongs on that list — and why.

## Sub lesson 1 — Human capital — the investment carried in people

In The Bahamas' Grade 6 Primary Social Studies Digital Textbook, the Conservation – Human Resources spread places people inside conservation — the same frame the book uses for natural resources. People, trained and able, are a resource the country holds.

The spread's name for that stock is **human capital**: the capability carried in people themselves — what trained workers know and can do. Like any capital, it is **an investment**: it costs time and teaching to form, and it returns work and income for years after. A country forms human capital every time it trains its people.

## Sub lesson 2 — Getting the most from the investment

The spread's first rule for the stock is to **maximise the investment from human capital** — to get the most that the investment can return.

Training returns most when trained people are placed where their training counts: when skills meet the jobs that need them, the country's earlier spending on teaching comes back as work done well, services offered and income earned. Human capital left unused returns nothing, however much it cost to form.

## Sub lesson 3 — Holding down the economic and social risk

The spread's second rule stands beside the first: **minimise the economic and social risk** around human capital.

The risk is real on both sides. Economically, training that finds no matching work is an investment lost. Socially, people whose skills cannot find a place carry the loss into their households and communities. Forming human capital well therefore means both halves together — the fullest use of the investment, with the least risk of wasting it.
