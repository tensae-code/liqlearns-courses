# Globalisation — trade and interdependence

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold the lesson's one thing in mind and say what you already think it means: Globalisation is the integration of the world's economy (and culture) through trade and transactions, capital and investment movements, migration and knowledge, driven by transport and telecommunications — and small Pacific economies feel its effects sharply, answering through their regional forum and donor aid. Hold your answer — the lesson takes it in parts.

## Sub lesson 1 — What globalisation is — one integrated world economy

In Fiji's Year 8 book, **globalisation** is **international integration** — the world's economies knitting together through **trade and transactions, capital and investment movements, migration and the spread of knowledge**.

The Year 9 book says the same thing from the culture side: globalisation is the integration of the world's **culture and economy**, and its agents include **trade, banking and money, multinational companies, transport, ICT and tourism**. What drives it all, in the Year 8 book, is **transport and telecommunications** — moving goods, money, people and ideas ever faster.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its impact in the Pacific — small and vulnerable economies

The Year 8 book then brings globalisation home: its second lesson is **the impact of globalisation in the Pacific**.

Pacific economies are **small and vulnerable** — a small island economy tied into world trade and investment feels the world's changes with little cushion. Globalisation reaches the islands as opportunity and as exposure at the same time.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Pacific answer — the regional forum and donor aid

The Pacific does not face globalisation island by island. The book teaches the **regional forum responses** — the islands acting together through their regional forum.

And it teaches **donor aid**: aid given for **poverty reduction and sustainable development**, including **Australia's aid to the Pacific** — outside help aimed at the very weaknesses that make small economies vulnerable.
