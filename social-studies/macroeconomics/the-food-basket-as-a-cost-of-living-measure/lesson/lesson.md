# The food basket as a cost-of-living measure

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — An indicator, not a shopping list

Venezuela's Grade 7 book teaches the **food basket** (canasta alimentaria) as a **statistical indicator** — a measuring instrument, published by the INE, the national statistics institute.

It determines a number: the **monthly cost in bolívares** of a set of foods. Before it is a list of foods, it is a way of measuring what it costs a family to eat.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the basket must cover

The set of foods is not random. It must cover **all the energy and nutrient requirements** of a **typical Venezuelan family**.

That is what makes the basket a cost-of-living measure and not just a price list: it prices a standard of eating — enough energy and nutrients for a family — and watches that price month by month.

## Sub lesson 3 — How the basket is built — habits, availability, lowest cost

Three considerations build the basket: the country's **consumption habits**, the **national production availability** — what the country actually produces — and the **lowest possible cost**.

Real habits, real supply, lowest cost: an indicator built that way can be priced every month and compared across time, which is exactly what a cost-of-living measure is for.
