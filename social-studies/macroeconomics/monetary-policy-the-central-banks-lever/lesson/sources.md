**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Malaysia, Dominica, Cambodia, Uzbekistan, Fiji, Armenia, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Pakistan, Samoa, Bhutan). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class XI Economics XI): Class XI re-teaches the Class IX foundations at full analytical depth (elasticity calculation, equilibrium shifts, BoP correction) and adds Bhutan-specific system, Five-Year Plan and trade-policy chapters.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Economics Guide): Year 13 economics is anchored on Samoa itself — the Central Bank of Samoa (not a generic central bank), one oil supplier as a Samoan monopoly, Samoan government revenue/expenditure tables, and a closing national-economy session built from the Central Bank Governor's 2002 address.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 12 Economics Class 12): the Class 12 book runs the standard macro course (national income, money, banks, public finance, trade) and then turns the same tools onto Pakistan itself for six chapters — Pakistan's sectors, national income, planning, transport and manpower, banking system, government finances and foreign trade — closing with the Islamic economic system (Zakat, Ushr, interest-free banking) taught as a full alternative system, not a footnote.


Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 11 Ekonomi): national income worked in rupiah with Indonesian provincial data; cooperatives taught as the “soko guru” (main pillar) of the Indonesian economy with SHU surplus-sharing calculations; the state/regional/private ownership ladder (BUMN–BUMD–BUMS) as the map of who owns the economy.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 Social Science student): Grade 11 macroeconomics is anchored in Armenian official statistics — learners compute with the Statistical Committee's data, meet the 425-item consumer basket Armenia uses for inflation, the Central Bank's 3% ±1 inflation target, and the Armenian-born Nobel laureate Daron Acemoglu's "Narrow Corridor" on institutions and prosperity.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 1): A full direct-approach course built on the circular flow, with Fiji examples throughout — ginger supply curves, Fiji import tables, quarry and land-rent income, the Attu family budget, Fiji credit facilities, sugar-industry production stages and Reserve Bank of Fiji policy tools.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

No matching teacher guide exists in this band: the book recorded as this text's teacher guide proved to be the 2011 GEQIP Grade 12 teacher's guide for the old syllabus, not this book's companion, and supplies no answer keys for it (proof in the placements file's verification block).

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 12 Economics): the final-year course turns practical — the market is studied the way a Cambodian trader meets it (consumers, prices, banks, riel exchange), and macro policy is taught as coordination: monetary policy, exchange rates and public finance fitted together, closing with foreign trade and the world trading system.
