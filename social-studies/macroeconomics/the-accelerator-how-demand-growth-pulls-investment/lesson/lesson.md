# The accelerator — how demand growth pulls investment

## Do it first

Shoppers want 10% more clothes this year. Does the clothing factory order 10% more machines — or far more? Commit to a guess before the accelerator principle answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Investment's double role

Investment is both a component of aggregate demand and the thing that grows the capital stock, shifting the production possibilities curve outward. But revenue from capital investment arrives only over years — the spending is now, the returns are spread out.

[Picture 24915 — a small rise in shoppers' demand becoming a large rise in machine orders]

## Sub lesson 2 — The accelerator principle

The accelerator theory says investment is driven by changes in output: investment equals an accelerator coefficient times the change in GDP, I = v × ΔGDP. A rise in demand for consumer goods therefore triggers a proportionately larger rise in demand for capital goods as firms expand capacity — or run down inventories first.

[GIF 24971 — demand rising a little, investment rising a lot, in step]

## Sub lesson 3 — Why investment swings

Because investment answers to the change in demand, not its level, it swings harder than the demand that drives it: a small quickening of demand calls forth a large burst of machine orders, and a mere slowing can cut investment sharply.
