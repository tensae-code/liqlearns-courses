# Venezuela 1830 — a dependent monoexport economy and its debt

## Sub lesson 1 — Devastated fields, scarce exports

The republican history book opens Venezuela's economic life in **1830** with the damage plain: agriculture and livestock were **devastated**, and exports were **scarce**.

The new country inserted itself into the world market under the colonial scheme — as **supplier of tropical products**, mainly coffee, also cacao and tobacco. Independence changed the flag; the insertion did not change.

## Sub lesson 2 — Politically independent, economically dependent

The trade balances tell the dependence story by partner: **positive** with the United States and Germany, **negative** with England, from which Venezuela imported **all kinds of manufactures**.

English and German commercial houses — Blohm, Boulton, Brandt, Römer — controlled exports and imports through La Guaira, Puerto Cabello and Maracaibo while investing very little in production. The book's verdict is a phrase to remember: **politically independent but economically dependent** — a backward **monoproductora and monoexportadora** economy, the mark of semicolonial countries.

## Sub lesson 3 — The debt takes 40% — military spending over 50%

The State received **low tax and customs income** — and what it received was spoken for. Paying the inherited external debt absorbed **almost 40% of state income**: Venezuela had assumed **28.5% of Gran Colombia's debt**.

Military spending took **over 50%** more. Debt and army together left **almost nothing to increase production** — the arithmetic of dependence, in the book's own percentages.
