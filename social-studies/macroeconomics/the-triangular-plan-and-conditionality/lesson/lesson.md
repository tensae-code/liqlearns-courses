# The Triangular Plan and conditionality

## Do it first

Before you read on, write what you already think this lesson's title means — the Triangular Plan and conditionality — in one sentence of your own. Keep it; you will check it against the lesson at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Credits to rehabilitate the nationalised mines

The Triangular Plan brought US, West German and IDB credits to rehabilitate the nationalised mining industry — outside money to rescue COMIBOL, the state miner created in 1952.

## Sub lesson 2 — The conditions — closures, dismissals, overseers

The money had strings: closing deficit mines, dismissing a fifth of COMIBOL's workforce, and letting the financiers' advisers oversee the plan. Conditionality — the lenders' terms written into Bolivia's own industry.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Soviet smelter offer Bolivia had to refuse

One more condition stood outside the mines: Bolivia had to abstain from accepting the Soviet offer to build a tin smelter with a US$150 million credit. The Cold War was priced into the loan.
