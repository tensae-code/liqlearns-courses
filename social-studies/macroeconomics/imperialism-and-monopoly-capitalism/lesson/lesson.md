# Imperialism and monopoly capitalism

## Sub lesson 1 — Why industrial powers reached abroad

Contemporary **imperialism** arose at the **end of the 19th century**, the Grade 8 book says, when industrial powers needed two things abroad: **cheap raw materials** and **places to invest large capitals**.

Need, not adventure, drives the expansion. An industrial economy that has outgrown its home supplies and its home investment outlets reaches for other people's countries to secure both.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colonies and neocolonial pressure

The powers guaranteed their supply by **exploiting Asia and Africa** — directly, by **colonies**, or indirectly, by **neocolonial pressure** for laws favouring their monopolist firms.

Two grips, one purpose: formal rule where it was taken, and favourable laws where formal rule was not. The book teaches pupils to recognise the second grip as imperialism too.

## Sub lesson 3 — Fusing capital — monopoly capitalism and transnational firms

Economic imperialism is also called **monopoly capitalism**, and the book explains the name: expansion needs **more capital than one businessman has**.

The solution is to **fuse the capital of several businessmen** to finance transnational firms — which in the 20th century became the multinational firms in mining, agriculture, industry and transport.
