# From evidence to a written history — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A primary source is —
a) written long after the event  b) contemporary with the event  c) always a book
**Answer: b) contemporary with the event** — Made at the time.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Silver coins of King Endubis are —
a) a secondary source  b) a primary source  c) an opinion
**Answer: b) a primary source** — Struck in the reign itself.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Oral traditions are classed as —
a) primary sources  b) secondary sources  c) forgeries
**Answer: b) secondary sources** — Passed mouth to mouth, they derive and must be cross-checked.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Primary sources are tested for —
a) authenticity — forgery or real  b) popularity  c) length
**Answer: a) authenticity — forgery or real** — Authenticity first.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Oral data must be cross-checked against documents because —
a) elders always lie  b) retelling distorts  c) documents are older
**Answer: b) retelling distorts** — Each retelling can bend the story.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Historians select a problem because —
a) the past is endless and cannot all be written  b) sources are boring  c) bias is required
**Answer: a) the past is endless and cannot all be written** — Selection is unavoidable — so it is declared.
**7. (Sub lesson 4)** What is external criticism?
a) testing whether what a source says can be believed
b) testing whether a source is genuine and what it is
c) a detail belonging to a different lesson in this course
**Answer: b) testing whether a source is genuine and what it is**

**8. (Sub lesson 5)** What is objectivity?
a) a detail belonging to a different lesson in this course
b) writing history so the evidence, not the wish, carries the judgement
c) bending the past to flatter a power — named and warned against
**Answer: b) writing history so the evidence, not the wish, carries the judgement**
