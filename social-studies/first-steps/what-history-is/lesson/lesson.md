# What history is

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — History — inquiry into the human past

The word history comes from the Greek istoria, which means inquiry. History is the organised, critical study of the human past, built from evidence — not a pile of old stories, but a discipline that asks of every claim: how do we know?

It covers two great stretches. Pre-history is the long age before writing, before about 4000 BC, known from tools, bones and ruins. History proper begins where written records begin — and the historian works across both, tracing change and continuity, and asking what caused what.

## Sub lesson 2 — Change, continuity, cause and effect

Some things change: empires rise, tools improve, cities grow. Some things continue: farming, family life and trade run through every age. The historian's craft is to hold both together — to show what changed, what stayed, and why.

That is why history is organised and critical, not just remembered. A chronicle lists; history explains. Every statement in it must be able to point to the evidence it stands on.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the past needs a method

Because the past cannot be visited, it must be reconstructed — and reconstruction without rules becomes invention. So history sets its rules early: find the evidence, test it, and only then tell the story.

This lesson opens the whole Grade 11 road: method first, then the ancient world, Ethiopia and the Horn, Africa, and the age of revolutions — each part held to the same standard of proof.
