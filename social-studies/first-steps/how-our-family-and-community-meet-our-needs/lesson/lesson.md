# How Our Family and Community Meet Our Needs

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: family members work together to meet the family's needs — parents work to buy food, clothing and books, children help with chores, families grow crops and rear animals — and when the family needs help, community groups provide the services: doctors and nurses, farmers and butchers, pharmacists and businesses — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meeting the family's needs

Guyana Grade 1 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 67–69), teaches: Family members work together to meet the family's needs — parents work to buy food, clothing and books, children help with chores, families grow crops and rear animals — and when the family needs help, community groups provide the services: doctors and nurses, farmers and butchers, pharmacists and businesses. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: the series opens at Grade 1 inside the home — the family that cooks and gardens together, the chores shared out fairly, the groups a small child already belongs to, and rules taught as the way a home and a classroom stay in harmony. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
