# Loving and Helping Family Members with Special Needs

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: what a family is (nuclear, single-parent and extended), every member's role and need to be loved, respected and heard, and how to help members with special needs — children, the elderly, people with disabilities, blindness, deafness, mental illness and albinism — without denying them their rights — as Tanzania's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Loving Family Members

Tanzania Standard Six tz-civic6-en (printed p. 1), teaches: What a family is (nuclear, single-parent and extended), every member's role and need to be loved, respected and heard, and how to help members with special needs — children, the elderly, people with disabilities, blindness, deafness, mental illness and albinism — without denying them their rights. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Loving Yourself and Others

Tanzania Standard Five tz-civic5-en (printed p. 1), teaches: Love is caring for yourself and others; people with special needs — the aged, disabled, poor and orphans — need more love because of their social and physical conditions, and we must not hurt them by our acts. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tanzania's way — the books' own teaching way

Tanzania's own way (Standard Six, Civic and Moral Education Standard Six Pupil's Book): TIE teaches civics as a good-governance wheel (the cover itself: transparency, accountability, participation, human rights, integrity, efficiency, reconciliation, democracy, equality, rule of law around “GOOD GOVERNANCE”) and builds the state from the child's own levels — street, village, ward, district, region, nation — with the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar as the founding fact and Kiswahili, unity and humanism (utu) as named national treasures. Tanzania's own way (Standard Five, Civic and Moral Education Standard Five Pupil's Book): Standard Five civics teaches the state from the ward up — Ward Development Committee, district council and its heads of departments, regional commissioner — and the nation through its symbols in detail (the emblem's thirteen signs, the shilling, the Uhuru torch first lit 9 December 1961, national festivals from Zanzibar Revolution Day to Farmers' Day); democracy is taught with political parties' duties and political tolerance, and peace as peaceful coexistence that condemns segregation on any ground. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
