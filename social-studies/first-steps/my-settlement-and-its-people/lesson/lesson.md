# My Settlement and Its People

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the people who live in one settlement are its residents and fellow citizens, each with defined rights and duties; much is shared and belongs to all — street, park, river, school, bus — and residents meet, agree and organise the work their settlement needs, from a village feast's invitations to the House of Culture — as Serbia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — У насељу (In the settlement)

Serbia Grade 2 Свет око нас 2 (printed pp. 12–13), teaches: the people who live in one settlement are its residents and fellow citizens, each with defined rights and duties; much is shared and belongs to all — street, park, river, school, bus — and residents meet, agree and organise the work their settlement needs, from a village feast's invitations to the House of Culture. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Serbia's way — the books' own teaching way

Serbia's own way: the second year widens the circles again — family rules are now made by all members together, the settlement's people become "fellow citizens" with shared things (street, park, river, school, bus are all "ours"), and the state enters through its symbols: flag, coat of arms and the anthem "Боже правде", with the behaviour owed while it plays. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
