# Protecting Wild Animals

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the tradition is to let wild animals live and multiply and to take them only for real need; birds must not be frightened off their eggs ("a bird cannot fold its young away — do not make it abandon its nest"); the hunting bans: no hunting when animals are fattening or weak by season, no pregnant animal or one followed by young, no young male of the herd; birds and fish, heaven's and water's creatures, were not hunted at all, and game taken for medicine was asked permission of the mountain — set against the two hunters of Lodoidamba's story "The Marmot Hunter", the one who cherishes his rich khangai and the one who only shoots — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 15 "From the traditional custom of cherishing wild animals" (Ан амьтныг хайрлан хамгаалах уламжлалт ёсноос)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 44, teaches: The tradition is to let wild animals live and multiply and to take them only for real need; birds must not be frightened off their eggs ("a bird cannot fold its young away — do not make it abandon its nest"); the hunting bans: no hunting when animals are fattening or weak by season, no pregnant animal or one followed by young, no young male of the herd; birds and fish, heaven's and water's creatures, were not hunted at all, and game taken for medicine was asked permission of the mountain — set against the two hunters of Lodoidamba's story "The Marmot Hunter", the one who cherishes his rich khangai and the one who only shoots. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
