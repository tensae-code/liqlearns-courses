## Sub lesson 1 — My Role Model

Iraq Grade 1 Moral Education (printed pp. 66–71), teaches: A role model (qudwa) is the one whose path I follow: be a good role model, and let your morals be what people talk about; in the park game "What do you want to be when you grow up?", Muhammad will finish his studies and become a soldier defending his homeland and his people, Lana an engineer building her homeland and making it beautiful, Yusuf a farmer planting his homeland's land and making it beautiful, and Sara a nurse like her mother, an angel of mercy staying up for the sick and spreading hope in their hearts; the professions are many — soldier, pilot, farmer, carpenter, writer, artist, engineer, dentist — they are human professions, and all of us build the homelands; and every profession has its importance. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: moral education is a separate subject from Grade 1, taught entirely as stories about five named children (Muhammad, Yusuf, Zahraa, Lana, Sara) whose small school and family incidents carry each value; every lesson ends in the child's own voice — "What I learned" — and the second unit's family tree is drawn with the father's help, branch by branch, until the big family shades them all. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
