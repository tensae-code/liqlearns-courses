# Life in the Village and the Nomads

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the village is small, its air always clean, and its people mostly know one another and are kin; from early morning they are busy with farming, gardening and animal husbandry; and a few people move from place to place in the seasons to graze their animals — these are the nomads (عشایر), who live in tents, are kin to one another, and produce meat, dairy, kilims and jajims — as Iran's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Life in the Village and the Nomads

Iran Social Studies Grade 4 (special needs) (student book, Persian), printed p. 56, teaches: the village is small, its air always clean, and its people mostly know one another and are kin; from early morning they are busy with farming, gardening and animal husbandry; and a few people move from place to place in the seasons to graze their animals — these are the nomads (عشایر), who live in tents, are kin to one another, and produce meat, dairy, kilims and jajims. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iran's way — the book's own teaching way

Iran's own way: the Grade 4 special-needs book widens the circle from self to country — my identity booklet, my address, my neighbourhood and its people, city against village, and finally Iran itself as "our big home" divided into provinces. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
