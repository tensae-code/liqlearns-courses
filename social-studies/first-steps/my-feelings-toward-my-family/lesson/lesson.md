## Sub lesson 1 — My Feelings Toward My Family

Iraq Grade 1 Moral Education (printed pp. 61–65), teaches: The most important feelings I should feel toward my family are love, happiness, joy and pride, and I express my feelings toward my family: Sara is proud of her mother the nurse, whose work — treating the sick and staying up for their comfort — is called the work of the angels of mercy, and proud of her father, who took his wife's place in caring for the children during her absence at the hospital; my presence inside the family is of great importance; every individual in the family has a profession he should be proud of; and responsibility inside the family is shared and exchanged — there are no works special to one member without the other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: moral education is a separate subject from Grade 1, taught entirely as stories about five named children (Muhammad, Yusuf, Zahraa, Lana, Sara) whose small school and family incidents carry each value; every lesson ends in the child's own voice — "What I learned" — and the second unit's family tree is drawn with the father's help, branch by branch, until the big family shades them all. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
