# Lesson plan — Heritage — what it is and why it matters

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage — what it is and why it matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Unit 5 — Peoples and States of Africa to 1500 (the heritage case) (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Heritage** is what a people inherits from its past and **chooses to keep**: its buildings and ruins, its objects, its languages, its ceremonies and skills — the things and practices handed down because they carry the people's memory of itself.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner having met Great Zimbabwe's stone city in the Africa to 1500 title — this lesson asks what such a place MEANS to the people who inherit it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks heritage means only very old buildings → include practices, languages, ceremonies and skills; if it is handed down and chosen, it is heritage.
- Thinks the 'who built it' argument is just pride → it is an evidence question: materials, dates and trade goods decide it, as the sources lesson taught.
