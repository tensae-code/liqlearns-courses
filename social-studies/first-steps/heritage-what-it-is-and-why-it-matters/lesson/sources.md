Mongolia's own way (Grade 9): Social Studies is a five-chapter spiral (Individual & Society, Individual & Culture, Individual & Economy, Human Rights & Law, Public Administration) continued from Grade 8; there is no physical-geography chapter — Mongolia's geography is a separate subject (Газарзүй) — so this book's Geography-wave content is its culture and economics teaching, grounded in Mongolian surveys, wage tables and heritage inventories. The book's Sociology parts (Chapter 1, printed pp. 6–13) and Law/Civics parts (Chapters 4–5, printed pp. 62–92) are named in the placements, not placed. Mongolia's own way (Grade 8): the Grade 8 year is the LOCAL year — every chapter is re-anchored to the child's own орон нутаг (local area/homeland): its environment and problems, its heritage, its economy and market, its governance. The book's Law/Civics parts (Chapters IV–V, printed pp. 63–94) are named in the placements, not placed. That way is stocked here as the way Mongolia teaches this thing.

Scope honesty, stated plainly: the Grade 9 placements give no UNESCO World Heritage List content and no general heritage chapter — the 'Heritage — things and practices we choose to keep' and 'Ethiopia on the World Heritage List / Ethiopia's UNESCO heritages' names in the coordinator's list have no Grade 9 placement behind them. This lesson is therefore built ONLY from what the Grade 9 books do give: the teacher guide's heritage question (Unit 5, printed pp. 88–107, labelled evidence) and the student book's Great Zimbabwe account (printed pp. 94–96), stacked into the single heritage lesson the Grade 9 material can honestly stock. The UNESCO-list lesson is logged as unbuilt in the build log, with the reason. No heritage fact is claimed beyond those two placements.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 2 book): Geography is taught inside three thematic sections that always start from the child's family, school and province and widen to Ecuador and South America; heavy use of hazard maps (floods, landslides, volcanoes) and emergency plans from Grade 2, reflecting Ecuador's volcanic / El Niño risk profile.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 3 book): the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 7, Secundaria 1): The Bolivian Texto de Aprendizaje runs every unit through the same four moments — Práctica, Teoría, Valoración, Producción — and frames geography inside the plurinational, Madre Tierra worldview: continental drift and geological time sit beside the Laws of Mother Earth (071 and 300), the human right to water, and a closing general geography of Bolivia itself (regions, orography, political map, salt flats and lithium).

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 7) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 7 turns analytical — factors behind transport-mode choice, up-to-date maps/charts (routes, contours, scale-and-distance work) for domestic, regional and international travel, transport hazards from floods/fog/earthquakes, shelter shortages and siting factors, and desertification with land rehabilitation (afforestation, gully filling). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): Grade 7 turns fully to skills and Eritrea's land — map scale, area and population arithmetic first, then Eritrea's natural heritage (Red Sea coast, wildlife) and Eritrea's own disaster set (drought, locusts, earthquakes, volcanism) as the environmental core.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 5 Social Studies): the same four-chapter frame as Grade 4, spiralling up — culture and land use return at a higher level, and the past chapter adds Khmer art motifs and traditional instruments as living heritage.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 6 Social Studies): the primary Social Studies spiral ends with its first real map work — maps, the globe and the equator/parallels/meridians grid taught inside the “society today” chapter, anchored on a world map with Cambodia marked.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 9 Social Science): Secondary geography opens with a full map-skills chapter — grid references, contours and cross-sections practised on Fiji maps — before environment use and conservation, including Pacific methods (shifting cultivation, rotational grazing, appropriate fishing methods).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 9 Geografija 9): Grade 9 turns home — the whole book is Slovenia itself: first the country as a system (position and borders, relief and rocks, climate/vegetation/waters, settlement, population, settlements, economy, transport, environment), then its five natural regions one by one (Alpine, Pre-Alpine with the Ljubljana basin, Dinaric-Karst, Mediterranean, Pannonian). Named, not placed: the EU unit's history of the European Union and Slovenia-in-EU pros/cons (printed p. 7, Civics/History) and the state symbols inside the opening unit (p. 1, Civics).
