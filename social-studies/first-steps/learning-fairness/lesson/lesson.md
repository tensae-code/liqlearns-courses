# Learning Fairness

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: fairness (шударга ёс) means one rule for all — everyone under the same rules, the same responsibility and the same punishment for breaking them, no one treated better or worse than another; good deeds are encouraged and bad ones never supported; worked through cases — is it fair that only the girls wash up after the picnic, is the teacher's punishment of the boy who drew on the desk fair, should only the best players vote for the team leader? — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 19 "Let's learn fairness" (Шударга ёсонд суралцъя)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 72, teaches: Fairness (шударга ёс) means one rule for all — everyone under the same rules, the same responsibility and the same punishment for breaking them, no one treated better or worse than another; good deeds are encouraged and bad ones never supported; worked through cases — is it fair that only the girls wash up after the picnic, is the teacher's punishment of the boy who drew on the desk fair, should only the best players vote for the team leader?. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
