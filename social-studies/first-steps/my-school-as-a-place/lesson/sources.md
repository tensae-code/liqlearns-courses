**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Fiji, Ecuador, Yemen, Libya, Afghanistan, Eritrea, Serbia, Venezuela). Latvia and Mauritius are named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 3 Social Studies): Expanding-circles ("cyclic") approach — starts with self/family and widens to community/nation/world in later years; place is learned through the child's own home and school first.

Latvia's own way (teacher guide — evidence): social studies is taught integrated with the Latvian language itself (CLIL) for linguistically mixed classes — every topic is framed as a question ("What does it mean to be a pupil?") and the social content is carried by the vocabulary and courtesies of school life. That evidence is stocked here as the way Latvia teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Mauritius’s own way (teacher guide — evidence): Values taught through the child’s widening circles — self, people around me, then "I love my country" — with Mauritius’s own flag colours, anthem words, and its three islands (Mauritius, Rodrigues, Agalega) as the identity content, and unity taught through the country’s many festivals. That evidence is stocked here as the way Mauritius teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Ecuador's own way (Estudios Sociales 2do EGB): the transition-curricular Grade 2 book is a workbook of songs, stories and activity spreads organised in three sections that always start from the child — family first, then school and community, then Ecuador and South America — with rights taught from the family outward to universal human rights. That evidence is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing; it is counted in the acceptance count above (student book).

Yemen's own way (G3 Social Studies): the combined Social Education book starts from the child's own family, mosque and school, then widens to neighbours and the neighbourhood/village — the remaining strands are taught as lived duties (kindness to parents, care of the home, good neighbourliness, school order) before any geography appears. That evidence is stocked here as the way Yemen teaches this thing; it is counted in the acceptance count above (student book).

Eritrea's own way (ER G6 Citizenship Education): a standalone Citizenship Education subject built entirely from Eritrean stories, poems and dialogues — responsibility is taught through a shop-queue drama and a family Tandoor day, the state through the 1993 referendum story, and patriotism as practical service (keeping the school clean, planting trees at the new clinic) that even a child can do.


Serbia's own way (Grade 1 Свет око нас): the integrated subject opens with the child herself (me, my feelings, my family) and widens circle by circle — family, home, neighbours, school, street — with rights taught from the first year as inseparable from duties ("every right seeks a duty"), and Serbia's own school slava, Saint Sava's Day on 27 January, taught as every school's feast.
Fiji's own way (Grade 3): The whole primary course starts from the child in the family and widens outward year by year; Year 3 is built as a UNESCO-backed Family Tree project, so family, culture and heritage are researched by the child from their own household.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 1 G1 CS Viva Venezuela): the child's own world first — school, family, classroom and house are taught by direct observation and riddles before any map; rights are taught as the child's own rights (to a house with a flag window, to Venezuelan foods, to traditional games, to school, health and respect), each paired with the duty that goes with it.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 3 G3 CS Viva Venezuela): coexistence is the spine of the primary book — respect, rules, family and community are each defined in plain words, then practised: the class votes in assembly, community leaders must learn to agree, and solidarity is taught from Lincoln's collection for an enslaved sailor to Caracas's free children's heart hospital serving the whole continent.

Libya's own way (National Education G4): the whole book is written in the first person — every lesson is "I and …" (I and my family, I and my school, I and the neighbourhood) — citizenship starts as the child's own relationships, and even the environment unit is framed as the child's own behaviour: what I throw away, what I plant, which beach I clean.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 4 Social Studies): the child's world widens in rings — social life, family, mosque, school, village, city, homeland, world, Earth — and every ring carries its duties with it: respect for elders at home, duties and order at school, reverence for the flag, obedience to traffic rules.
