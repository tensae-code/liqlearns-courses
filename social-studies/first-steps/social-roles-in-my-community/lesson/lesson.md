# Social Roles in My Community

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: everyone assumes social roles, each with expected behaviours, ideals and norms (child, parent, teacher, community workers); roles relate to each other and carry social responsibility — what happens if family members stop fulfilling theirs; women's roles have changed (owning property, voting, public office without social censure) — as Colombia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 10 Los roles sociales

Colombia Grade 3 CS_Grado3_02, printed pp. 63–87, teaches: everyone assumes social roles, each with expected behaviours, ideals and norms (child, parent, teacher, community workers); roles relate to each other and carry social responsibility — what happens if family members stop fulfilling theirs; women's roles have changed (owning property, voting, public office without social censure). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colombia's way (Book 11) — the book's own teaching way

Colombia's own way: culture is taught through named Colombian expressions (Pasto's Carnaval de Negros y Blancos, Riosucio's Carnaval del Diablo, the Madre Monte myth) and through imagining life inside another Colombian culture — speaking Pamié or Tucano — while emotions and affection get a full primary unit of their own. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
