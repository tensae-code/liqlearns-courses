# Video script — The Family Its Members and Their Roles

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Honduras Grade 1 G1 Student CCSS, printed p. 138, teaches: A family descends from a couple, lives in harmony, and each member has responsibilities by age; parents protect and decide, grandparents guide; when each does their part everyone benefits (André's coffee-farming family in Teupasenti).

Honduras Grade 7 G7 Student CCSS, printed p. 8, teaches: The family is society's smallest unit and society works well if the family works well; its functions include material and emotional security and socialisation; Honduras's Family Code (Decree 76-84, 1984) guarantees each member's rights, and the lesson closes on rights and duties inside the family and on Garífuna community family life (children share fishing and yuca, rice and banana work; solidarity and respect for elders).

Honduras Grade 7 G7 Student CCSS, printed p. 14, teaches: Family types by size, members and residence — the extensive family (parents, children, grandparents, uncles, cousins under one roof) against smaller forms; Honduran families have moved from mostly rural (to 2007) to mostly urban (UN Population Fund); maltreatment is named in its kinds — physical, psychological and sexual abuse, negligence and abandonment — and must be avoided.

Honduras Grade 7 G7 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 8, teaches: The family is a community of persons joined by kinship ties, with a name, a home and the duty of moral and material solidarity, above all between parents and children; in modern societies it has shrunk to the nuclear family, with the extended family, friends and schoolmates arriving later; the parents' own example — how they ask for things, yield a seat, share, renounce and defend others — is the core of values education, with effort, constancy, compassion, recognition of the other, justice and tolerance of frustration.

Honduras Grade 7 G7 Teacher CCSS, printed p. 12, teaches: The International Convention on the Rights of the Child gives every child the right to a family, which links the child to a history and protects against violations and maltreatment — though sometimes the family itself is the aggressor; parents are the first who can act for their children: they decide their care and medical treatment (no practice without their consent except in emergency) and hold the essential duty of educating them, including moral, civic and religious education, so they can live in society with autonomy.

Honduras's own way (Book 1): civics begins inside the child's own circles — feelings, family norms and tasks, classroom rules and school authorities, a rehearsal student-government election with a paper ballot, and only then the community's elected authorities (alcaldesa, regidores, patronato, junta de agua).

Honduras's own way (Book 7): secondary civics starts from the family as a legal institution — the Family Code (Decree 76-84) enters in Lesson 1 — and builds to the State itself: its elements (population, territory, power), Montesquieu's separation, and the full legislative process ending in publication in La Gaceta.
