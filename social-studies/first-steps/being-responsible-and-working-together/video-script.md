The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 72, teaches: No one lives alone — we depend on each other as passengers depend on the driver and the bank's customers on its clerks, so each person consciously doing their own duty is how everyone lives in peace; failing a duty brings its fruits — fines, lost trust, others refusing to deal with you — while doing it brings reward and pride; and decisions are made together: in the team-leader's guide, know each member, hear everyone one by one, weigh every opinion, decide by discussion and let the decision be fair — with the story of class chair Khaliun, whose meeting went wrong when proposals were not heard.

Mongolia's own way (Book 4): the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other.
