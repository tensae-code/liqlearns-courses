# Video script — Types of Pacific Islands — Continental, Volcanic, Coral, Atoll

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Fiji's book, the *Fiji Year 7 Social Science* (printed p. 46), teaches: Strand 3 Place and Environment, 3.1 Features of Places: the South Pacific and its four island types (continental, volcanic, coral limestone, atoll) and their size, soils and life. Fiji's book, the *Fiji Year 10 Social Science* (printed p. 55), teaches: Geography of Fiji: types of islands and their characteristics (volcanic high islands, coral islands/islets such as Nukulau and Wailagilala, reefs). Fiji's book, the *Fiji Year 13 Geography* (printed p. 230), teaches: Chapter 8: Oceania's three island groups (continental islands, volcanic high islands, coral raised limestone and atoll islands) and how each is formed.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 7 Social Science): Year 7 widens the lens to the whole South Pacific — island types, "islandness" and vulnerability, Pacific hazards, and resources owned by tribes and clans; Fiji is taught as the hub of the South West Pacific.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 is the foundation for senior Geography and History — Fiji's own physical geography (tectonics, relief, rivers, climate, soils) taught in full, then its industries, population and urbanisation, all with Fiji data.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 13 Geography): The final year integrates everything at advanced level (plate tectonics to urban models, largely from world texts such as Waugh) and then lands it in Chapter 8, South Pacific Islands Geography — Oceania's geology, island types, development prospects and regionalism.
