# Me Myself

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: I am a boy or a girl, my name, my class, the colour of my hair; the things I like and can do, and the feelings they bring — happiness, sadness, anger — matched to their pictures; and how I have grown: the day I was born, the day I walked, the day I talked, the day I went to school — as Iran's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Me Myself

Iran Social Studies Grade 3 (special needs) (student book, Persian), printed p. 10, teaches: I am a boy or a girl, my name, my class, the colour of my hair; the things I like and can do, and the feelings they bring — happiness, sadness, anger — matched to their pictures; and how I have grown: the day I was born, the day I walked, the day I talked, the day I went to school. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iran's way — the book's own teaching way

Iran's own way: the special-needs track teaches the same social world as the regular book but rebuilt in one-sentence pages through two named children, Sara and Saman — self, family, school, neighbourhood — with every rule and every act of cooperation stated as a single pictured example. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
