# My House, My Home

## Do it first

Look at the house you live in. Before you read on, draw it from memory on a blank page — the whole house as it looks from outside. Under your drawing, write its name in your mind: this is my house, my dwelling place, the place where I live.

## Sub lesson 1 — My house: draw it and write about it

In Suriname, Grade 3 children start Geography from themselves, in a book called the Ik-boek — the "I-book". It is a personal portfolio: its pages start blank, and the pupil fills each one in. One page is headed Mijn huis — My house.

On that page there is nothing printed to copy. The work is yours: you draw your own house and you write about it. Your house is your dwelling place — the place where you live. Drawing it and writing about it is how you start to know the place closest to you.

## Sub lesson 2 — A dwelling place for Botje

Suriname's Grade 3 workbook asks the same thing in a drawing task, its very first lesson: Teken een woonplek voor Botje — Draw a dwelling place for Botje. The page says: "This is Botje. Where would he live?"

So a dwelling place is not only for you. Every living thing needs a place where it lives. When you draw a dwelling for Botje, you think about what a dwelling must give the one who lives in it — a place of their own to be in.

## Say it to your companion

Explain your house to your companion in your own words: what your dwelling looks like, who lives there with you, and what a dwelling place must give the one who lives in it. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
