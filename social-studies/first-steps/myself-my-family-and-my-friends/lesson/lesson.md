# Myself My Family and My Friends

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: naming yourself and your gender, identifying family members, and talking about your friends — the child's first social map, taught through the naming game 'De de dzengera uyo ndiani?' — as Zimbabwe's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1: Identity: Family history and local heritage

Zimbabwe, *Best Approach to Family and Heritage Studies Teacher's Resource Book ECD 'A'* (printed p. 1), teaches: Naming yourself and your gender, identifying family members, and talking about your friends — the child's first social map, taught through the naming game 'De de dzengera uyo ndiani?'. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Topic 1: Advantages of living in a family / Relatives

Zimbabwe, *Ventures Primary Family and Heritage Studies Learner's Book Grade 2* (printed pp. 1–4), teaches: People live in a family to help one another, work together, share ideas and learn to care and love; relatives are the extended family (grandparents, aunts, uncles) shown on a family tree, who help in sickness and death and celebrate at weddings. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 10) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 10) (teacher guide — evidence): a second publisher's ECD A book for the same syllabus, laid out as weekly lesson grids (objective / skill / activities / media); its heritage unit teaches culture through languages, attire, food and truth-telling songs, and its rights unit is built almost entirely on recognising and dramatising abuse and being assured help exists. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 12) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 12): the learner's-book voice of the same syllabus — the community is "like a big family" that meets at the dare, shares the tap, sports grounds and clinics, and feeds its elderly, orphans and poor from the chief's own field (zunde ramambo/isiphal senkosi); children's roles are taught as changed by equality — in the past cooking was girls' work and herding boys', now both do the same roles. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
