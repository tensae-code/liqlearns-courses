# The Fruit of Good Deeds

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a good deed and its fruit — love (хайр) and a compassionate heart (нинжин сэтгэл); Bilguun, who told his tale honestly and admitted his own fault in it; the girl who warmed the freezing poor old woman and was herself warmed; the boy who saved the drowning dog; and the tale of the Three Gold Coins, in which the youngest brother spends his coins saving a pheasant, a golden fish and an otter — and is saved by them in return; good deeds are sorted into deeds of body, thought and speech — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 3 "The benefit of good deeds" (Сайн үйлийн ач тус)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 11, teaches: A good deed and its fruit — love (хайр) and a compassionate heart (нинжин сэтгэл); Bilguun, who told his tale honestly and admitted his own fault in it; the girl who warmed the freezing poor old woman and was herself warmed; the boy who saved the drowning dog; and the tale of the Three Gold Coins, in which the youngest brother spends his coins saving a pheasant, a golden fish and an otter — and is saved by them in return; good deeds are sorted into deeds of body, thought and speech. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 3 turns from the family to the whole moral order — deeds and their fruits, the moving camp, the spirits of land and water, the state's banner and emblem — and ends with the child's first political ideas: deciding together, equal rights, and a government the citizens elect and pay for. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
