# Greetings for Each Season

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Mongolians greet each season differently, because the greeting speaks of the season's work and wishes it well — in spring, "Are the lambs and kids thriving?"; in autumn at a felt-making household, a blessing answer about being white as snow and strong as felt; the reply to a seasonal greeting is itself a blessing (ерөөл), tied to that household's labour — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 1 "Seasonal greetings" (Улирлын мэнд)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 5, teaches: Mongolians greet each season differently, because the greeting speaks of the season's work and wishes it well — in spring, "Are the lambs and kids thriving?"; in autumn at a felt-making household, a blessing answer about being white as snow and strong as felt; the reply to a seasonal greeting is itself a blessing (ерөөл), tied to that household's labour. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
