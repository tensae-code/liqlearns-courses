# Lesson plan — Turkey's national days — Republic Day and remembering Atatürk

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Turkey's national days — Republic Day and remembering Atatürk
**Place in the curriculum:** First Steps — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The 29 October Republic Day poem (the homeland's form of government became a republic, and the nation decks itself in flags); the Atatürk Week (10–16 November) poem (the 09:05 moment of grief, the crowds running to Anıtkabir, Atatürk as patriotic, honest, determined and hardworking); and the 15 July Democracy and National Unity Day poem (the night the nation-army fought for democracy and won).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: a seven-year-old learns the Republic's birthday, its founder's death-day and its newest day of unity as three poems to recite. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Republic Day, 29 October, and say what became a republic that day.
2. Say what Atatürk Week (10–16 November) remembers — the 09:05 moment of grief and the crowds at Anıtkabir.
3. Say what 15 July Democracy and National Unity Day remembers.
4. Explain, in their own words, how Turkey teaches its youngest children the nation's story — as poems for special days, and the Student Oath at the front of the book.
