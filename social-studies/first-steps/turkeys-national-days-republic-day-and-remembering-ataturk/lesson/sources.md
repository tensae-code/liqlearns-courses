**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Turkey).**

Turkey's own way (Hayat Bilgisi 1. Sınıf, 1. Kitap): the Grade 1 year opens school itself as the first society: a six-year-old first learns to introduce herself and listen, then the school's rooms and workers, then rules the class itself votes on, and only then the family as the second society — nuclear and extended family, the values that hold it together, and jobs shared like a team.

Turkey's own way (Hayat Bilgisi 1. Sınıf, 2. Kitap): the second Grade 1 book turns from school and family to the homeland: the flag and the anthem are taught as the symbols of independence a six-year-old stands for at the school ceremony, the five national days are learned as one family of celebrations, and the religious bayrams are taught through visiting elders and helping each other — the state and the faith calendar side by side in one learning area.

Turkey's own way (Hayat Bilgisi 2. Sınıf, 2. Kitap): local government is taught to seven-year-olds as the answer to a child's question — "who put the new play equipment in our park?" — and the class then visits a real muhtarlık, belediye or kaymakamlık and interviews the manager with a form it wrote itself.

Turkey's own way (Hayat Bilgisi 3. Sınıf, 1. Kitap): Grade 3 is where Hayat Bilgisi turns explicitly civic: an eight-year-old is taught that rights and responsibilities are a pair, and then reads the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child itself — in a child-worded card set (definition of the child, no discrimination, best interests, life and development) — two years before any civics course exists.

Turkey's own way (Hayat Bilgisi 3. Sınıf, 2. Kitap): the republic is taught by research the children run themselves — interviewing family and school staff and searching the net to answer "how is our country governed, who serves in it, and what do we citizens contribute?" — and national unity is taught through one girl's memory notebook of the year's bayrams.


Turkey's own way: before any history course exists, the state teaches national memory to seven-year-olds as poems for the year's special days — Republic Day, Atatürk Week, July 15 — plus the daily Student Oath (Andımız) printed at the front of the book. That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing. The book's stories are filed here as plot summaries in the course's own words only — never transcribed.
