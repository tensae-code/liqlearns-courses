# Turkey's national days — Republic Day and remembering Atatürk

## Do it first

Three days in the Turkish school year are not ordinary days. On one, the whole country hangs out flags. On another, at five past nine in the morning, everything stops and goes quiet. On a third, children hear how ordinary people defended their country one summer night. Before you read on, guess: what is each day remembering? Hold your guesses; the poems will answer them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three days a child learns by heart

Turkey's Grade 2 life-studies book carries the country's memory in its "Belirli Gün ve Haftalar" (Special Days and Weeks) poem spreads, and three of the poems are pure national history.

The Republic Day poem tells how "the season was autumn, the calendars showed 29 October" — the day the homeland's form of government became a republic — and how the nation decks itself in flags. The Atatürk Week poem, for 10–16 November, remembers the 09:05 moment of grief when Atatürk died, the crowds running to Anıtkabir, his tomb in Ankara, and praises him as patriotic, honest, determined and hardworking. The 15 July poem retells the night "the nation-army fought for democracy and won" — the newest of the three days, Democracy and National Unity Day.

And every school day begins inside this memory: the Student Oath (Andımız) is printed at the front of the book, so a seven-year-old recites the nation's story before the lessons even start.
