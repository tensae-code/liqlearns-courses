# Video script — I Am Special and So Are Others

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Oman Grade 1 Identity & Citizenship TG T1, printed pp. 29–38, teaches: Each child is distinct — the class discovers similarities and differences between children and learns to accept them; the unit's named values are respect, acceptance, pride in oneself and in the homeland.

Oman Grade 2 Identity & Citizenship TG T1, printed pp. 29–40, teaches: The child expresses her traits creatively, practises activities that show self-acceptance, and — the standard's own words — proposes advice to others to accept themselves; accepting one's own traits and respecting them is taught as the root of accepting others.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 1, Term 1): citizenship is its own school subject from age six, built as widening circles — the child first names herself, her feelings and her family, and only then her neighbourhood; the state prints its map and anthem at the front of the teacher's guide, and the fourth unit-component is always participation, not obedience.

Oman's own way (Identity and Citizenship Grade 2, Term 1): Grade 2 turns Grade 1's circles into verbs — accept, communicate, cooperate, consult, belong — and plants two purely Omani institutions in the child's civic world: the sablah, the village meeting hall where the community gathers, and the folk games of the neighbourhood.
