# Being Proud of Your School and Loving Your Country — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does part 1 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Being Proud of Your School and Loving Our Country — pride in school through its motto and song (the motto motivates pupils, teachers and community toward the school's vision), acts that bring honour to a school, and loving Tanzania by honouring its national treasures (Kiswahili, patriotism, unity, accountability, love, respect, humanism), national symbols and public holidays (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does part 2 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Being Proud of Our School and Country — pride in school and country is shown through the national symbols and festivals — the constitution as a symbol of the nation, the national festivals (Zanzibar Revolution Day 12 January, Union Day 26 April, Workers' Day 1 May, Farmers' Day 8 August, Nyerere Day 14 October, Independence Day 9 December) on which leaders report achievements and citizens raise their concerns (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does part 3 of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Tanzania's way — the books' own teaching way — Tanzania's own way (Standard Six, Civic and Moral Education Standard Six Pupil's Book): TIE teaches civics as a good-governance wheel (the cover itself: transparency, accountability, participation, human rights, integrity, efficiency, reconciliation, democracy, equality, rule of law around “GOOD GOVERNANCE”) and builds the state from the child's own levels — street, village, ward, district, region, nation — with the Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar as the founding fact and Kiswahili, unity and humanism (utu) as named national treasures. Tanzania's own way (Standard Five, Civic and Moral Education Standard Five Pupil's Book): Standard Five civics teaches the state from the ward up — Ward Development Committee, district council and its heads of departments, regional commissioner — and the nation through its symbols in detail (the emblem's thirteen signs, the shilling, the Uhuru torch first lit 9 December 1961, national festivals from Zanzibar Revolution Day to Farmers' Day); democracy is taught with political parties' duties and political tolerance, and peace as peaceful coexistence that condemns segregation on any ground. (any close wording).**
