# Video script — Staying Safe at Home

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Syria Grade 1 Social Studies (Syria Grade 1 Social Studies G1, printed p. 31), teaches: Sorting the things around the house by colour code — safe to use, use with care, dangerous (scissors, sockets, the stove, sharp garden tools)

Syria Grade 2 Social Studies (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2, printed p. 20), teaches: Protecting yourself in high and closed places — Karam on the castle wall's edge, the ladder and the open window, the heater in Saeed's shut room (fresh air is needed when the heater burns), the sick friend playing too close; the child writes their own safety rules for high places and for closed places (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1)

Syria Grade 2 Social Studies (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2, printed p. 42), teaches: Being careful with tools and materials — Ghayth helps his father mend the bicycle; the dangerous things in the tool cupboard are marked, the pliers are carried by the handle with the point down, and the harms are named: fires, poisoning, skin burns, wounds (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1)

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 1): the whole primary series opens with widening rings — Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland — taught almost entirely through picture scenes with a bear mascot, one "I can…" outcome per spread; rights arrive at age six as lived scenes (the child shut out of play has lost a right; the state gives every child the vaccine free).

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 2): Grade 2 is the honesty-and-safety year — the right-decision lesson turns on a shopkeeper who offers a boy an expensive sweet free if he tells no one, the careful lesson warns that things found on the road may be explosives, and rights arrive as a shipwreck game: children who trash a dream island invent rules, and discover the balance — "my right to enjoy nature, my duty to protect it".
