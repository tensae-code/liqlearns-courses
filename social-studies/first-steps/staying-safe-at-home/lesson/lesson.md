# Staying Safe at Home

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: sorting the things around the house by colour code — safe to use, use with care, dangerous (scissors, sockets, the stove, sharp garden tools) — as Syria's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Staying Safe at Home 1

Syria Grade 1 Social Studies (Syria Grade 1 Social Studies G1, printed p. 31), teaches: Sorting the things around the house by colour code — safe to use, use with care, dangerous (scissors, sockets, the stove, sharp garden tools). ## Sub lesson 2 — Staying Safe at Home 2

Syria Grade 2 Social Studies (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2, printed p. 20), teaches: Protecting yourself in high and closed places — Karam on the castle wall's edge, the ladder and the open window, the heater in Saeed's shut room (fresh air is needed when the heater burns), the sick friend playing too close; the child writes their own safety rules for high places and for closed places (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1). ## Sub lesson 3 — Staying Safe at Home 3

Syria Grade 2 Social Studies (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2, printed p. 42), teaches: Being careful with tools and materials — Ghayth helps his father mend the bicycle; the dangerous things in the tool cupboard are marked, the pliers are carried by the handle with the point down, and the harms are named: fires, poisoning, skin burns, wounds (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Syria's way — the books' own teaching way

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 1): the whole primary series opens with widening rings — Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland — taught almost entirely through picture scenes with a bear mascot, one "I can…" outcome per spread; rights arrive at age six as lived scenes (the child shut out of play has lost a right; the state gives every child the vaccine free). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 2): Grade 2 is the honesty-and-safety year — the right-decision lesson turns on a shopkeeper who offers a boy an expensive sweet free if he tells no one, the careful lesson warns that things found on the road may be explosives, and rights arrive as a shipwreck game: children who trash a dream island invent rules, and discover the balance — "my right to enjoy nature, my duty to protect it". This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
