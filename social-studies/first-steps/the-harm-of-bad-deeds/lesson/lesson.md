# The Harm of Bad Deeds

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a bad deed brings suffering to its doer and to others; the bad mind is greed (шунал), anger and pride (уур, омог), ignorance (мунхаг) and envy (атаа) — against them, speak gently, share others' joy, never envy another's goods or success, and keep a patient heart; the old woman of the tale undoes herself by halves — lying by halves, stealing by halves, slandering by halves — until her own life is halved — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 4 "The harm of bad deeds" (Муу үйлийн хор уршиг)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 18, teaches: A bad deed brings suffering to its doer and to others; the bad mind is greed (шунал), anger and pride (уур, омог), ignorance (мунхаг) and envy (атаа) — against them, speak gently, share others' joy, never envy another's goods or success, and keep a patient heart; the old woman of the tale undoes herself by halves — lying by halves, stealing by halves, slandering by halves — until her own life is halved. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 3 turns from the family to the whole moral order — deeds and their fruits, the moving camp, the spirits of land and water, the state's banner and emblem — and ends with the child's first political ideas: deciding together, equal rights, and a government the citizens elect and pay for. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
