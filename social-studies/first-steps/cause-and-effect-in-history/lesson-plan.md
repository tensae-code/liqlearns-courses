# Lesson plan — Cause and effect in history

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Cause and effect in history
**Place in the curriculum:** First Steps — built in the History expansion (Saudi Arabia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every historical event has causes that led to it and results that followed, practised on examples the children choose and sort — nothing in the past "just happened".
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: an event with no cause is a story with no beginning, and an event with no result is a story with no ending — every event sits in the middle, pushed from behind and pushing forward. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Every event has causes** — Before anything in the past happened, something led to it — the causes are what pushed it into happening.
2. **Every event has results** — After anything in the past happened, something followed from it — the results are what the event pushed in turn.
3. **Sorting causes and results** — The children practise on examples they choose themselves, sorting each one into what caused it and what came of it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What history is, and From evidence to a written history. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, what a cause is — what led to an event — and what a result is — what followed from it.
2. Given a simple past event, name at least one cause that led to it.
3. Given the same event, name at least one result that followed from it.
4. Sort a small set of chosen examples into causes and results, and explain one sorting choice.
