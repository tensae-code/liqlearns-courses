# Video script — Our School Family

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Guyana Grade 7 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 1 (printed pp. 20–21), teaches: The members of the school make up one family which we call the school family — the Head Teacher, other teachers, the P.T.A., ancillary staff, the Prefect Body, the Student Council and the students; our school is like a second home, and the process of education which begins in the home continues at school; like a family, each school has an organisational structure, with the Headteacher as head or leader of the school family, assisted by a Deputy Headteacher and senior teachers, all answerable for the smooth and proper running of the school.

Guyana Grade 7 New Horizons in Social Studies Book 1 (printed p. 22 (NEW slug first proposed in the line above)), teaches: We belong to our home as well as to our school, and in some ways our school is like our home: just as we learn to obey our parents at home, so we obey our teachers at school; the Headteacher plans activities and sets goals with the teachers, as parents set family goals at home; at school we are helped and trained by our teachers to be good members of the school family and to grow up to earn a decent livelihood, and every member of the school family has a special part to play so that success may be achieved.

Guyana's own way: Book 1 opens secondary Social Studies where the primary books end — the child as citizen of nested communities (family, school, village or town, country), national symbols learned part by part with their meanings, and local government taught through the rates-and-taxes bargain: services exist because citizens pay for them and care for what they buy.
