Ecuador's own way (Estudios Sociales 2do EGB): the transition-curricular Grade 2 book is a workbook of songs, stories and activity spreads organised in three sections that always start from the child — family first, then school and community, then Ecuador and South America — with rights taught from the family outward to universal human rights.

Fiji's own way (Grade 3): The whole primary course starts from the child in the family and widens outward year by year; Year 3 is built as a UNESCO-backed Family Tree project, so family, culture and heritage are researched by the child from their own household.

Fiji's own way (Grade 7): Identity is taught as "what makes you who you are" — family, friends, school, church, province and culture — and valuing cultures starts from respecting your own roots.

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 1): the whole primary series opens with widening rings — Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland — taught almost entirely through picture scenes with a bear mascot, one "I can…" outcome per spread; rights arrive at age six as lived scenes (the child shut out of play has lost a right; the state gives every child the vaccine free).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 1 G1 CS Viva Venezuela): the child's own world first — school, family, classroom and house are taught by direct observation and riddles before any map; rights are taught as the child's own rights (to a house with a flag window, to Venezuelan foods, to traditional games, to school, health and respect), each paired with the duty that goes with it.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 2 G2 CS Viva Venezuela): the family is taught through Venezuela's own Family Protection Law and through how families actually form and grow (courtship, weddings, births, learning from babyhood); equality is taught straight from the 1999 Constitution — equal rights whatever your skin, religion, sex or disability, in a mestizo population.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 3 G3 CS Viva Venezuela): coexistence is the spine of the primary book — respect, rules, family and community are each defined in plain words, then practised: the class votes in assembly, community leaders must learn to agree, and solidarity is taught from Lincoln's collection for an enslaved sailor to Caracas's free children's heart hospital serving the whole continent.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 4 G4 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): the closing block builds citizenship in steps — family first (a community with its own norms, duties and rights), then the homeland's symbols, then humanity's rights (the 1948 UN Declaration read article by article), then the needs those rights protect and the taxes that pay for the services, and finally Venezuela's own 1999 Constitution — participatory democracy and its four referendums — as the nation's rulebook for living well.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 CS Patria y Ciudadanía): the first secondary civics book builds belonging as a ladder — family community, school community, municipal, regional and national community, with the Communal Council standing beside them — and participation itself is the lesson: the Constitution gives everyone a broad right to participate in forming, executing and controlling public management, as both a right and a common duty.
