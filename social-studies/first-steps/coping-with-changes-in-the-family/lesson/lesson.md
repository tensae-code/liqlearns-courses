# Coping with Changes in the Family

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: family changes (death, moving house, divorce, new baby, parent working away) can make children sad or confused; cope by talking to trusted adults, playing, singing, drawing, helping at home and seeing a counsellor if very sad — as Zimbabwe's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 3) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum infant guide teaches civics through the child's own duties — rights arrive paired with responsibilities (homework, cleaning, respecting elders) and protection is taught as naming who to tell (grandmother, teacher, police), with community taught as shared things (borehole, dip tank, hall) people take turns to use. ## Sub lesson 6 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 1) (teacher guide — evidence): at ECD the civics ladder starts inside the family — rights are taught as things every child must have (name, food, love, play) and protection as a spoken routine ("No, Stop, Tell someone") with named community helpers (teacher, parent, police officer, doctor/nurse); care is practised on the family's own vulnerable members (baby, Gogo, child on crutches). ## Sub lesson 7 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 2) (teacher guide — evidence): ECD B widens ECD A's family civics one ring — rights gain the right to identity (your name, clan and totem) and to clean water, responsibilities are framed as being a "model citizen" of the school, and sharing-and-helping is taught in three widening places: school, community, family.
