# Video script — Coping with Changes in the Family

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe Grade 1 zw-g1 PlusOne Social Science Grade 1 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 50), teaches: Family changes (death, moving house, divorce, new baby, parent working away) can make children sad or confused; cope by talking to trusted adults, playing, singing, drawing, helping at home and seeing a counsellor if very sad.
Zimbabwe ECD A zw-ecda PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide (printed p. 42), teaches: A new baby at home is small, sleeps and cries a lot and needs much help; the older child learns patience and helping mother and father, and that they are still very loved.
Zimbabwe ECD B zw-ecdb PlusOne Social Science ECD B Teacher's Guide (printed p. 52), teaches: A newborn baby changes home life — who holds the baby, where the baby sleeps, less play time with mother/father, new noises and new feelings — and the older child learns the changes are normal.
Zimbabwe Grade 2 zw-g2 PlusOne Social Science Grade 2 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 42), teaches: Naming the changes that can come to a family and how a Grade 2 child copes with them, continuing the ECD coping ladder.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 3) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum infant guide teaches civics through the child's own duties — rights arrive paired with responsibilities (homework, cleaning, respecting elders) and protection is taught as naming who to tell (grandmother, teacher, police), with community taught as shared things (borehole, dip tank, hall) people take turns to use.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 1) (teacher guide — evidence): at ECD the civics ladder starts inside the family — rights are taught as things every child must have (name, food, love, play) and protection as a spoken routine ("No, Stop, Tell someone") with named community helpers (teacher, parent, police officer, doctor/nurse); care is practised on the family's own vulnerable members (baby, Gogo, child on crutches).

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 2) (teacher guide — evidence): ECD B widens ECD A's family civics one ring — rights gain the right to identity (your name, clan and totem) and to clean water, responsibilities are framed as being a "model citizen" of the school, and sharing-and-helping is taught in three widening places: school, community, family. Honesty item, as filed: no ISBN is printed for ECD B — the imprint page repeats the ECD A title line and ECD A's ISBN 978-1-77932-134-3 (publisher copy error, confirmed on cover render: the cover correctly says ECD B).

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 4) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 2 is where the state first appears in the child's civics — government as the payer of school fees (BEAM), builder of schools and playgrounds and feeder of learners — and where the school itself is taught as an agent of socialisation where children create their own class rules. Note: Unit 35's printed objectives are a publisher copy error (Islam objectives pasted in); its teaching notes are genuinely the voluntary-participation lesson.
