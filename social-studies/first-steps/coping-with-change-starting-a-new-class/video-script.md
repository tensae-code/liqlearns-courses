# Video script — Coping with Change Starting a New Class

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe Grade 1 zw-g1 PlusOne Social Science Grade 1 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 48), teaches: Starting Grade 1 brings bigger classrooms, pencil writing, new teachers, stricter rules and longer days; cope by following instructions, listening, doing homework, making friends and sharing feelings.
Zimbabwe ECD A zw-ecda PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide (printed p. 41), teaches: Change means something is different now (a friend moves school, a new book, new rules); big changes can make children sad, and kindness helps — hug gently, invite the sad friend to play, sit quietly beside them.
Zimbabwe ECD B zw-ecdb PlusOne Social Science ECD B Teacher's Guide (printed p. 54), teaches: School changes (new classroom, new routine, a new friend joining) are normal and anxiety about them is normal; deal with them by talking about feelings and joining in.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 3) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum infant guide teaches civics through the child's own duties — rights arrive paired with responsibilities (homework, cleaning, respecting elders) and protection is taught as naming who to tell (grandmother, teacher, police), with community taught as shared things (borehole, dip tank, hall) people take turns to use.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 1) (teacher guide — evidence): at ECD the civics ladder starts inside the family — rights are taught as things every child must have (name, food, love, play) and protection as a spoken routine ("No, Stop, Tell someone") with named community helpers (teacher, parent, police officer, doctor/nurse); care is practised on the family's own vulnerable members (baby, Gogo, child on crutches).

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 2) (teacher guide — evidence): ECD B widens ECD A's family civics one ring — rights gain the right to identity (your name, clan and totem) and to clean water, responsibilities are framed as being a "model citizen" of the school, and sharing-and-helping is taught in three widening places: school, community, family.
