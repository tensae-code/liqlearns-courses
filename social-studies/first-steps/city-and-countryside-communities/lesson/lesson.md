# City and Countryside Communities

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the urban community (the city, where Lía lives) and the rural community (fields and farm zones) — how each looks and how people live in them — as Dominican Republic's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unidad 2 La vida en mi comunidad

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 2° Primaria (printed p. 30), teaches: The urban community (the city, where Lía lives) and the rural community (fields and farm zones) — how each looks and how people live in them. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unidad 3 ¡Venimos del campo y la ciudad!

Dominican Republic — Ciencias Sociales 3° Primaria (printed p. 48), teaches: The ways of life of rural and urban dwellers — work, institutions, streets, transport and services in the city against life in the campo, compared in a pupil model. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dominican Republic's way — the book's own teaching way

Dominican Republic's own way: the youngest Libro Abierto volume builds citizenship from the child's own ground — my community and its public spaces, my family and its tree, then rights and duties told through stories (José, the boy with nowhere to live) and a class "poetry of values". Grade 3 ends the book with a whole annex most countries never give 8-year-olds — the Constitución Dominicana Infantil, the country's constitution rewritten for children, grouped as civil, social and cultural rights; and its democracy unit starts from the school's own consejo de curso election. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
