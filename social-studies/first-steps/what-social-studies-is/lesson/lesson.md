# What Social Studies Is

## Do it first

Look at the place you live. The land under your feet, the river or the road, the rain that comes or does not come — studying that is the first lens: geography.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three lenses: land, past, today

Now look back. The people who lived here before you, the kings and farmers and traders of long ago — studying that is the second lens: history.

Now look around you today: the market, the roads, the problems people argue about and solve together — that is the third lens: today's world. Social Studies looks through all three lenses, in that order. In this course you will meet seven chapters of geography, history and today's world in the first year's book, and seven more in the second year's book — the same woven subject, one year on.

## Sub lesson 2 — What belongs in Social Studies

Try a sort first. Which of these belong in Social Studies: the world people live in? Human relationships? Building and roads? Nutrition? Researching new drugs? Events in your town, your country and the world? Decide each one, and say why, before you read on.

Here is the definition. Social Studies is selected, age-fitted content drawn from the social sciences — geography, history, economics, philosophy, law and others like them. It studies how people interact with each other, and with the natural and human-made things around them. It gives a general grasp of local, social, natural, national and world matters.

So the world people live in belongs. Human relationships belong. Building and roads belong — people made them and live by them. Events near and far belong. Nutrition and drug research, on their own, belong to other sciences — Social Studies touches them only where they touch people's lives together.

## Sub lesson 3 — How this course works: activity first

In every lesson here, you do something first. You sort, you observe, you judge, you draw, you debate with a partner — and only then does the lesson state the idea in plain words. That is not an accident; it is how these books teach.

Each part of the course opens by telling you what you should be able to do when it ends, and gives you the key words up front. Each part closes with a summary and questions in five shapes: true or false, matching, multiple choice, short answer, and challenging questions.

Dates in this course are given in the European calendar. Two Amharic short forms appear with old dates: one means "before Christ" and one means "after Christ" — the lesson tells you which is which when you first meet them.

One more partner matters: the people at home. Learning built on what you bring from your own surroundings grows stronger when home and school pull together — so show them what you are doing.

## Say it to your companion

Explain what social studies is to your companion in your own words: what it is, the one figure or fact you would never want to forget from it, and one example from your own surroundings. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
