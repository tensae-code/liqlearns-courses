# Traffic Signs and Road Safety for Pedestrians

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: keeping my body safe on the road — traffic signals and signs, crossing carefully, the seat belt, and the signs that protect pedestrians — as Libya's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — My Body's Safety on the Road — Grade 4

Libya Grade 4 National Education G4 (printed p. 34), teaches: keeping my body safe on the road — respecting traffic signals and signs, crossing carefully, wearing the seat belt; and no one has the right to harm another by word or deed (the lesson's home-safety body is the Health course's, named in the Contents map). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traffic Signs — Grade 6

Libya Grade 6 National Education G6 (printed p. 73), teaches: respecting traffic signs keeps my body safe — no waiting, no horn, pedestrian crossing ahead, a place reserved for the cars of people with disabilities, no entry, no parking (the lesson's home-safety body is the Health course's, named in the Contents map). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Libya's way — the book's own teaching way

Libya's own way: the whole book is written in the first person — every lesson is "I and …" (I and my family, I and my school, I and the neighbourhood) — citizenship starts as the child's own relationships, and even the environment unit is framed as the child's own behaviour: what I throw away, what I plant, which beach I clean. Grade 6 defines the citizen by a chain of relations — rights paired with duties, freedom paired with responsibility, then one lesson per circle the child belongs to (family, school, neighbourhood) — and grounds the rights lesson in named world instruments: "human rights" as the UN defines them, the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child, and UNICEF. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
