# Video script — Traffic Signs and Road Safety for Pedestrians

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Libya Grade 4 National Education G4 (printed p. 34), teaches: keeping my body safe on the road — respecting traffic signals and signs, crossing carefully, wearing the seat belt; and no one has the right to harm another by word or deed (the lesson's home-safety body is the Health course's, named in the Contents map).

Libya Grade 6 National Education G6 (printed p. 73), teaches: respecting traffic signs keeps my body safe — no waiting, no horn, pedestrian crossing ahead, a place reserved for the cars of people with disabilities, no entry, no parking (the lesson's home-safety body is the Health course's, named in the Contents map).

Libya's own way: the whole book is written in the first person — every lesson is "I and …" (I and my family, I and my school, I and the neighbourhood) — citizenship starts as the child's own relationships, and even the environment unit is framed as the child's own behaviour: what I throw away, what I plant, which beach I clean.

Libya's own way: Grade 6 defines the citizen by a chain of relations — rights paired with duties, freedom paired with responsibility, then one lesson per circle the child belongs to (family, school, neighbourhood) — and grounds the rights lesson in named world instruments: "human rights" as the UN defines them, the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child, and UNICEF.
