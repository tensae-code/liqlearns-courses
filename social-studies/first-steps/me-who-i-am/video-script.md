# Video script — Me Who I Am

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Syria Grade 1 Social Studies (Syria Grade 1 Social Studies G1, printed p. 5), teaches: Introducing yourself — name, age, governorate, where you live and what you love — and filling in your own identity card (name, gender, age)

Syria Grade 2 Social Studies (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2, printed p. 8), teaches: Describing yourself and guessing classmates from descriptions — hair, eyes, skin and temperament — then filling your own personal card: name, age, gender, skin, eye and hair colour, and the personal traits that mark you out (the NEW lesson proposed at Grade 1, Book 1)

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 1): the whole primary series opens with widening rings — Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland — taught almost entirely through picture scenes with a bear mascot, one "I can…" outcome per spread; rights arrive at age six as lived scenes (the child shut out of play has lost a right; the state gives every child the vaccine free).

Syria's own way (Social Studies Grade 2): Grade 2 is the honesty-and-safety year — the right-decision lesson turns on a shopkeeper who offers a boy an expensive sweet free if he tells no one, the careful lesson warns that things found on the road may be explosives, and rights arrive as a shipwreck game: children who trash a dream island invent rules, and discover the balance — "my right to enjoy nature, my duty to protect it".
