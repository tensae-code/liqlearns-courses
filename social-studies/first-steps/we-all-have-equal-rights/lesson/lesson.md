# We All Have Equal Rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: every person is different, yet all have equal rights (тэгш эрх): a right (эрх) is the chance to do what one wishes and loves — but one person's right and freedom are limited by another's, which is called limiting a right (эрхийг хязгаарлах): I love loud music, but the law forbids noise after 23:00 because the neighbours have a right to peaceful rest; people differ in language, culture, custom, food, dress and ability — some children cannot join every game — yet by law all are equal, and no one may be mocked, bullied or left out for being different; the tale of the owl who, mocked by the fox and monkey for her weak eyes, saves them both in the dark — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 15 "We have equal rights" (Бид тэгш эрхтэй)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 3, printed p. 54, teaches: Every person is different, yet all have equal rights (тэгш эрх): a right (эрх) is the chance to do what one wishes and loves — but one person's right and freedom are limited by another's, which is called limiting a right (эрхийг хязгаарлах): I love loud music, but the law forbids noise after 23:00 because the neighbours have a right to peaceful rest; people differ in language, culture, custom, food, dress and ability — some children cannot join every game — yet by law all are equal, and no one may be mocked, bullied or left out for being different; the tale of the owl who, mocked by the fox and monkey for her weak eyes, saves them both in the dark. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 3 turns from the family to the whole moral order — deeds and their fruits, the moving camp, the spirits of land and water, the state's banner and emblem — and ends with the child's first political ideas: deciding together, equal rights, and a government the citizens elect and pay for. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
