# Keeping Safe in a Disaster

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a natural disaster strikes without warning — "a disaster does not come announced" — so everyone stays prepared: what to do before, during and after earthquake, fire, flood and storm (do not panic, get under a strong table, stay away from windows; check yourself for injury and signal others afterwards); and an emergency (онц байдал) is when the state itself acts to protect the citizens — in earthquake, storm, drought, zud, flood, fire or epidemic — with what a family must keep ready — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 21 "How do I protect myself in natural disasters and emergencies?" (Байгалийн гамшиг, онц байдлын үед өөрийгөө хэрхэн хамгаалах вэ?)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 5, printed p. 61, teaches: A natural disaster strikes without warning — "a disaster does not come announced" — so everyone stays prepared: what to do before, during and after earthquake, fire, flood and storm (do not panic, get under a strong table, stay away from windows; check yourself for injury and signal others afterwards); and an emergency (онц байдал) is when the state itself acts to protect the citizens — in earthquake, storm, drought, zud, flood, fire or epidemic — with what a family must keep ready. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: the last primary civics book completes the customs syllabus (the guest, the genealogy book, the ger's building order, the meaning of every garment) and then turns the same lens on the modern state — the local Khural and governor's office are explained exactly as customs were, as the settled way citizens and the state behave toward each other. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
