# My Relatives And My Name

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: relatives on the father's side are авга and on the mother's side нагац, drawn on the family tree; my birth certificate carries my clan name (овог), my father's name and the name my parents gave me; and the Mongolian saying that a name breaks before a bone does — a person's name carries the honour of the whole clan — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 1 "My kin" (Миний ураг төрөл)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 2, printed p. 5, teaches: Relatives on the father's side are авга and on the mother's side нагац, drawn on the family tree; my birth certificate carries my clan name (овог), my father's name and the name my parents gave me; and the Mongolian saying that a name breaks before a bone does — a person's name carries the honour of the whole clan. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
