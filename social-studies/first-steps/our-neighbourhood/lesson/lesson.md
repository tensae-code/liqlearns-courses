# Our Neighbourhood

## Do it first

Think of the homes nearest to yours. Who lives in them? The families who live near you are your neighbours, and the place where you all live, close together, is your neighbourhood. Before you read on, name three things you can see in your own neighbourhood — a building, a tree, a shop, a road.

## Sub lesson 1 — Many families live in a neighbourhood

In Guyana's first social-studies book, two children, Ron and Sue, show you their world. Ron does not live alone with his family. Many families live near Ron's home. They are his neighbours.

That is what a neighbourhood is: the homes and families that live near each other, around your own home. Your neighbours are the people in those homes — the families on your street, near your house.

## Sub lesson 2 — Green Valley: naming our neighbourhood

Ron and Sue's neighbourhood has a name: Green Valley. A neighbourhood is not only houses. It has buildings and features you can name — the homes, the school, the trees, the roads and the other places people use every day.

Try it with your own neighbourhood: walk it in your mind and name its buildings and features, one by one, the way the book names Green Valley. Naming the parts of your neighbourhood is the first step to knowing it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Neighbours help each other

Neighbours are not only people who live near you. Neighbours help each other, and many neighbours are the workers of the neighbourhood. In Ron and Sue's neighbourhood, a father works as a carpenter, building and mending things made of wood, and a mother works as a nurse, caring for people who are sick.

Look at your own neighbourhood the same way: who are its workers, and how does each one's work help the neighbours around them?

## Say it to your companion

Explain your neighbourhood to your companion in your own words: who your neighbours are, three buildings or features you can name, and one worker whose work helps the neighbourhood. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
