## Sub lesson 1 — Rules of Our Classroom

Vanuatu Citoyenneté Sociale Guide Years 1–6 (printed p. 18), teaches: The class designs its own fair set of duties and rules — every child helps, with different tasks matched to different abilities, and the class votes on the set it prefers. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Class Rights and Responsibilities

Vanuatu Citoyenneté Sociale Guide Years 1–6 (printed p. 27), teaches: Every right carries a matching responsibility (the right to be safe ↔ the duty not to harm); responsibilities agreed by consensus become the class rules and hold longer than rules imposed by others. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Vanuatu's way — the book's own teaching way

Vanuatu's own way: social citizenship is taught as roles before rules — the Year 1 child maps who they are in family and village (chief, pastor, nurse, police, women's and youth group leaders), learns that roles change and are not fixed by gender, and only in Years 5–6 reaches rights, and how a community's rules are made and by whom. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
