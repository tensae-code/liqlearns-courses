## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: are our needs only food, clothing and housing? — no: we also have non-material needs — to have friends and be loved, to talk with others and receive their help; no one can meet these needs alone — even the nursing baby, who cannot drink milk or be bathed and dressed by itself, needs its mother's embrace and love; people need one another, as the pictures show — the boy in the wheelchair, the volleyball team, the gift given with a smile — as Iran's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Needs That Are Not Things

Iran Social Studies Grade 10 (special needs, Kardanesh) (student book, Persian), printed p. 15, teaches: are our needs only food, clothing and housing? — no: we also have non-material needs — to have friends and be loved, to talk with others and receive their help; no one can meet these needs alone — even the nursing baby, who cannot drink milk or be bathed and dressed by itself, needs its mother's embrace and love; people need one another, as the pictures show — the boy in the wheelchair, the volleyball team, the gift given with a smile. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iran's way — the book's own teaching way

Iran's own way: the special-needs secondary book is a citizen's primer in the plainest form — needs first (things, then love and company), then the circles that meet them: family, groups, institutions — and at the top, government and the Majlis, explained through a ship and its captain and a school and its rules. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
