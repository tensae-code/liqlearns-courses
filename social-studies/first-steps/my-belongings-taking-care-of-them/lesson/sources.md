Vietnam's own way (Book 2): Grade 2 widens the circle from family to homeland and public space: love of homeland is proven in small deeds (a donation box for neighbours in difficulty, flowers planted along the village road, a class visit to a Heroic Vietnamese Mother), and the civics core is rules in public places, taught place by place — school, hospital, bus stop, library, cinema, zoo, historic site.

Vietnam's own way (Book 7): the local community is introduced through two big picture scenes — the home village (health station, post office, market, school) against the city (hospital, supermarket, cinema, traffic) — and every job, from farmer to traffic policeman, is summed up in one sentence: any work that benefits society is precious.

Nepal's own way (Book 1): from Grade 1 the subject is values-first — the youngest child's social studies is seniors respected, juniors loved, the needy and disabled helped, and duties at home and school, all taught through pictures the teacher talks through.

Nepal's own way (Book 2): Grade 2's civic sense is property and patience — our common property, protecting public property, and waiting one's turn — beside neighbourliness taught through festivals and living styles.

Nepal's own way (Book 3): Grade 3 names caste directly — "Abolish Untouchability" is a named lesson — and local government arrives as the child's own ward and what it does.
