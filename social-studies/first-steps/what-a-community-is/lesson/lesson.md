# What a Community is

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a community is the people who live together in one place, and its shared needs — a school and playing fields for the children, services and cleanliness, peace and security, and care for the area's trees and plants — worked for together — as Eritrea's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a Community is

Eritrea Grade 4 ER G4 Social Studies (printed p. 85), teaches: a community is the people who live together in one place, and its shared needs — a school and playing fields for the children, services and cleanliness, peace and security, and care for the area's trees and plants — worked for together. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Eritrea's way — the book's own teaching way

Eritrea's own way: the primary book builds the whole idea of community from the child's own neighbourhood — what a community is, how one forms around a factory or a railway, how Eritrea's nine peoples live together in one, what the local administration does for it, how village and town feed each other, and how a village changes — Dehab's village, ten kilometres from Asmara, is the running example. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
