Eritrea's own way (ER G4 Social Studies): the primary book builds the whole idea of community from the child's own neighbourhood — what a community is, how one forms around a factory or a railway, how Eritrea's nine peoples live together in one, what the local administration does for it, how village and town feed each other, and how a village changes — Dehab's village, ten kilometres from Asmara, is the running example.

Guyana's own way (Book 3 — Social Studies For Our Children Book 3): the whole book is one widening idea — a community is people living and working together who share services; three invented communities (Riverview, Mountain Gap, Runsvale) carry every lesson, and the book ends not on facts but on duties: courtesy, respect for national symbols, and protecting the community.

Guyana's own way (Book 8 — Guyanese in the World, Social Studies Grade 6): the newest ministry series teaches citizenship from the child's own roles outward — family duties, community roles, youth responsibilities at home and across the Caribbean, then power, privilege and human rights in plain language, with Guyanese and Caribbean case studies (Annette Arjoon-Martins, Mia Mottley) and a value component woven into every unit.

Guyana's own way (Book 9 — Guyanese in the World, Social Studies Grade 2): the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught.

Guyana's own way (Book 13 — New Horizons in Social Studies Book 1): Book 1 opens secondary Social Studies where the primary books end — the child as citizen of nested communities (family, school, village or town, country), national symbols learned part by part with their meanings, and local government taught through the rates-and-taxes bargain: services exist because citizens pay for them and care for what they buy.
