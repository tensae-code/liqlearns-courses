# Riding a Bicycle Safely

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: safe riding places are traffic-free — playgrounds, parks, cycle paths — with helmet and pads; the age rules (under 9 only in pedestrian zones under supervision, from 9 near school, from 12 on public roads), checking brakes and wheels, both hands on the handlebars, and signalling turns by hand — as Serbia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Вожња ролера, тротинета и бицикла (Riding rollers, a scooter and a bicycle)

Serbia Grade 3 Природа и друштво 3 (printed pp. 38–39), teaches: safe riding places are traffic-free — playgrounds, parks, cycle paths — with helmet and pads; the age rules (under 9 only in pedestrian zones under supervision, from 9 near school, from 12 on public roads), checking brakes and wheels, both hands on the handlebars, and signalling turns by hand. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Serbia's way — the books' own teaching way

Serbia's own way: the third-grade book widens "my region" into the whole republic — its many peoples named one by one as equal citizens, work divided into making and serving, and the environment taught as a duty: soil, water and parks protected by rules every child can keep. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
