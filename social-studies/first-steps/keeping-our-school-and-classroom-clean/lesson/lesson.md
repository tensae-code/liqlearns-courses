## Sub lesson 3 — Keep the school hygienic

Vietnam Grade 2 Tự nhiên và Xã hội 2, printed pp. 36–39, teaches: The school is your second home and keeping it clean is how you show love for it — picking up the litter left after the flag ceremony, planting and watering trees, and the schoolyard cleaning session with its group assignments and tools, ending in the class project "greening our school" (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 1 of this file; counted here as an existing target). ## Sub lesson 4 — Keep safety and hygiene at school

Vietnam Grade 3 Tự nhiên và Xã hội 3, printed pp. 30–33, teaches: Surveying the school in groups — classroom, playground, washrooms — for whatever is unsafe or unhygienic (a broken windowpane, a wobbling desk, a wet floor), reporting it and proposing the repair, then cleaning the school and the area around it together: keeping the school safe and hygienic is also a way of loving your second home (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 1 of this file; counted here as an existing target). ## Sub lesson 5 — Vietnam's way — the book's own teaching way

Vietnam's own way: Đạo đức (Ethics) is Vietnam's standalone primary moral-education subject; every lesson runs the same four moves — a song to open, picture stories to explore, practice pictures to judge, a role-play to apply — and closes with a two-line rhyme that states the duty; the family is taught three-generational (ông bà – cha mẹ – anh chị em) and school rules are taught as the child's first shared rules. the local community is introduced through two big picture scenes — the home village (health station, post office, market, school) against the city (hospital, supermarket, cinema, traffic) — and every job, from farmer to traffic policeman, is summed up in one sentence: any work that benefits society is precious. work is met first inside the family — the adults' occupations, paid and unpaid, with Hồ Chí Minh's saying "labour is glory" — and traffic is taught as a system of kinds (road, rail, air, water) whose signs fall into three colour-coded families every road user must obey. kinship is mapped on both sides of the family with the exact word of address for every relative (and a note that regions address them differently), and the school's link to its community is made of named joint deeds — flood relief, a rubbish-for-plants fair, traffic-safety campaigning and tree planting. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
