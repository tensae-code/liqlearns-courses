**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Vietnam).**

Vietnam's own way (Book 1): Đạo đức (Ethics) is Vietnam's standalone primary moral-education subject; every lesson runs the same four moves — a song to open, picture stories to explore, practice pictures to judge, a role-play to apply — and closes with a two-line rhyme that states the duty; the family is taught three-generational (ông bà – cha mẹ – anh chị em) and school rules are taught as the child's first shared rules.

Vietnam's own way (Book 7): the local community is introduced through two big picture scenes — the home village (health station, post office, market, school) against the city (hospital, supermarket, cinema, traffic) — and every job, from farmer to traffic policeman, is summed up in one sentence: any work that benefits society is precious.

Vietnam's own way (Book 8): work is met first inside the family — the adults' occupations, paid and unpaid, with Hồ Chí Minh's saying "labour is glory" — and traffic is taught as a system of kinds (road, rail, air, water) whose signs fall into three colour-coded families every road user must obey.

Vietnam's own way (Book 9): kinship is mapped on both sides of the family with the exact word of address for every relative (and a note that regions address them differently), and the school's link to its community is made of named joint deeds — flood relief, a rubbish-for-plants fair, traffic-safety campaigning and tree planting.
