# Hands-on — Knowing My Lineage

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Topic 2 "Let's cherish our lineage" (Удам угсаагаа хайрлая)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: A people who know their own descent and history — "among the Mongols there is not one person who does not know his lineage"; the five generations above me are named (father, grandfather, great-grandfather, and the элэнц and хуланц forefathers), exactly as the Secret History names five generations above Chinggis Khan, and the mother's line likewise. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Topic 3 "Let's keep our genealogy record" (Ургийн бичгээ хөтөлье)
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The ancestors kept the genealogy book (ургийн бичиг) from ancient times — it is the family's own history, and keeping it keeps kinship known and the hearth pure; the record is kept nine generations up, father's side and mother's side branching separately, with its own signs (a square for a man, a circle for a woman, a line for marriage, a line down for children) — and the first rule of the record is to write it truly. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Section 3.1 "The value of knowing your genealogy"
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: The genealogy book (үгийн бичиг) is how a nation remembers itself — Rashid al-Din recorded the Mongols' clan genealogies in the 14th century because the Mongols themselves kept them so exactly; to marry inside nine generations of one's own clan was forbidden, so knowing the line — bone kin and blood kin, each generation with its own name, counted on the joints of the hand — protected the family and the nation; the great Setsen Khan Sholoi genealogy of 1905 holds 11,996 names over 328 years on a 5.3-metre cloth scroll, the largest genealogy book in the world; clan surnames were abolished in 1925 and restored by government resolution in 1997, and the pupil builds their own family's tree with the book's own signs. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
