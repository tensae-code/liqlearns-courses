# Knowing My Lineage — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: a people who know their own descent and history — "among the Mongols there is not one person who does not know his lineage"; the five generations above me are named (father, grandfather, great-grandfather, and the элэнц and хуланц forefathers), exactly as the Secret History names five generations above Chinggis Khan, and the mother's line likewise.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson's second part teaches: the ancestors kept the genealogy book (ургийн бичиг) from ancient times — it is the family's own history, and keeping it keeps kinship known and the hearth pure; the record is kept nine generations up, father's side and mother's side branching separately, with its own signs (a square for a man, a circle for a woman, a line for marriage, a line down for children) — and the first rule of the record is to write it truly.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This lesson's third part teaches: the genealogy book (үгийн бичиг) is how a nation remembers itself — Rashid al-Din recorded the Mongols' clan genealogies in the 14th century because the Mongols themselves kept them so exactly; to marry inside nine generations of one's own clan was forbidden, so knowing the line — bone kin and blood kin, each generation with its own name, counted on the joints of the hand — protected the family and the nation; the great Setsen Khan Sholoi genealogy of 1905 holds 11,996 names over 328 years on a 5.3-metre cloth scroll, the largest genealogy book in the world; clan surnames were abolished in 1925 and restored by government resolution in 1997, and the pupil builds their own family's tree with the book's own signs.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This lesson stands on a student book, with no teacher guide.
**Answer: True**
