Indonesia's own way (PP-G1 student): Pancasila values are taught from the child's own body, friends and family outward — five recurring child characters from Papua, Bali, Jakarta, Sulawesi and Kalimantan carry every chapter, so national unity is shown as named children from different islands.

Indonesia's own way (PP-G2 student): Grade 2 turns Pancasila from a symbol to copy into behaviour at home — each principle (sila) is matched to a picture-symbol on the Garuda shield and then to a concrete act a 7-year-old can do in the family.

Indonesia's own way (PP-G2 teacher): the guide organises Grade 2 by Fase A (the Kurikulum Merdeka phase for Grades 1–2), so civics outcomes are stated as phase competences, not yearly facts.

Indonesia's own way (IPAS-G3 student): IPAS hides its civics inside a story — a mother explains norma and nilai (norms and values) to her children over tidying toys, so abstract civic words arrive as named goodnesses a child already does.

Indonesia's own way (IPAS-G3 teacher): the guide states the chapter's learning outcome in civic terms — pupils must apply "nilai dan norma di lingkungan rumah" (values and norms in the home environment) while doing the family's varied activities.
