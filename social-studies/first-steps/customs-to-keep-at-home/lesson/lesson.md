# Customs to Keep at Home

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the ger is honoured — a guest enters and moves to the right, the place of honour (хоймор) faces the door, and the taboos guard the house: never step on the threshold (it is said to bring poverty), never sit on the threshold or lean on the posts, close the door properly, sit by age — elders above, children below the posts — and on leaving, the eldest goes first — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 6 "From the customs to keep at home" (Гэрт баримтлах ёсноос)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 21, teaches: The ger is honoured — a guest enters and moves to the right, the place of honour (хоймор) faces the door, and the taboos guard the house: never step on the threshold (it is said to bring poverty), never sit on the threshold or lean on the posts, close the door properly, sit by age — elders above, children below the posts — and on leaving, the eldest goes first. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
