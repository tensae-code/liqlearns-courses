# Lesson plan — Historiography — history of history-writing

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Historiography — history of history-writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 9 History, Unit 1 — The Discipline of History and Human Evolution (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked into this title in the Ethiopia Wave 4 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Historiography** studies how knowledge of the past is attained and transmitted — how history itself has been written, age by age.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner knowing what history is — an account of the past built on evidence — from the opening lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses historiography with history → history studies the past; historiography studies how that studying itself was done, age by age.
- Thinks Ethiopia's history-writing began in the 1960s → the chronicle tradition is indigenous and old; the 1960s date the academic discipline.
