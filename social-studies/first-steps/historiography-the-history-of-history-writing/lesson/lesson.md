# Historiography — history of history-writing

## Do it first

Every history book was itself written by somebody, somewhere, in some century. Who wrote the very first histories? And when did history become a subject with rules, taught at universities? The story of history-writing itself has a name: historiography. Walk its shelf.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first historians

**Historiography** studies how knowledge of the past is attained and transmitted — how history itself has been written, age by age. The oldest written history is placed in **China**, where records were kept **before 1000 BC** — earlier than any other written history the book names.

[Picture 22504 — a shelf from Chinese records and Herodotus to an Ethiopian royal chronicle]

From Greece come the two founding names. **Herodotus** is named the **father of history** — the great collector and teller of the past. **Thucydides** is named its **first critical writer** — the one who weighed evidence and questioned accounts. Between them they set the two poles history-writing still swings between: gather everything, and test everything.

## Sub lesson 2 — History becomes a discipline — and Ethiopia's own shelf

For most of its life, history was written by chroniclers, travellers and kings' scribes. Only in the **later 19th century** did it become an **academic discipline** — a subject with training, standards and universities — and **Leopold von Ranke** is named the **father of modern historiography**, the man of the archives who demanded history be built from documents.

Ethiopia's shelf is older than its universities. Ethiopia had its **own indigenous chronicle tradition** — royal chronicles written at court across the centuries. That tradition **changed in the early 20th century**, and **Ethiopian academic history dates from the 1960s**, when the discipline took university form. Two shelves, then: a long indigenous one, and a young academic one — and this course reads from both.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grade 11 depth: How history-writing itself changed

The Grade 11 telling widens the road: historiography is the study of how history itself has been written — from Herodotus, the father of history, who asked and travelled and doubted, through the long chronicle traditions in which courts recorded their kings, to the modern professional history of universities, archives and criticism. Each age wrote the past in its own image; historiography is how we catch it doing so.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw historiography's shelf as a timeline: China before 1000 BC, Herodotus, Thucydides, Ranke in the later 1800s, Ethiopian academic history in the 1960s.
2. Write two sentences: one a chronicler of a royal court might write about his king, one a modern academic might write about the same king. Say what changed.
3. Ask an elder or teacher: what is the oldest written account of your town or family that anyone knows of? Note who wrote it and when.
