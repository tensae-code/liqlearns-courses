# Video script — Leisure Activities in the Community

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe Grade 2 zw-g2 PlusOne Social Science Grade 2 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 24), teaches: The leisure activities people share in the community as part of community life.
Zimbabwe Grade 3 zw-g3 PlusOne Social Science Grade 3 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 45), teaches: Leisure is what people do in free time to relax; community leisure facilities include community halls (meetings, concerts, events that foster community spirit), swimming pools, recreational clubs (sports, games, hobbies, well-being), cultural villages (crafts, music and dance that preserve traditional culture) and art and craft centres.
Zimbabwe Grade 4 zw-g4 PlusOne Social Science Grade 4 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 59), teaches: Leisure time is free time meant for relaxation, enjoyment and self-development; using it responsibly means productive, healthy activities — taking part in community events such as festivals and clean-up days, spending time with family (strengthening bonds and sharing values and traditions), and reading.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 4) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 2 is where the state first appears in the child's civics — government as the payer of school fees (BEAM), builder of schools and playgrounds and feeder of learners — and where the school itself is taught as an agent of socialisation where children create their own class rules. Note: Unit 35's printed objectives are a publisher copy error (Islam objectives pasted in); its teaching notes are genuinely the voluntary-participation lesson.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 5) (teacher guide — evidence): rights teaching is concrete and local — support services are named institutions a child can find (BEAM school-fees support, community care workers, police, health workers), government services are named ministries and ZUPCO buses, and the needy are helped by named Zimbabwean homes and trusts (Harare Children's Home, Capernaum Trust, Angel of Hope Foundation).

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 6) (teacher guide — evidence): child rights are sourced, not just listed — the Constitution of Zimbabwe Section 81, the African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child and the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child; cultural beliefs are tested against those rights (FGM, child marriage and corporal punishment named as violations), and local welfare includes the traditional Zunde Ramambo / Isiphala Senkosi (the chief's granary) alongside councils, BEAM and church feeding schemes.
