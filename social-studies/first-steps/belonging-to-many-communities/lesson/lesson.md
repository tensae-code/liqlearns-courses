# Belonging to Many Communities

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a child belongs to many communities at once — the family community, the school community, religious communities (churches, mandirs, mosques), sports communities and business communities — as Guyana's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The communities we belong to

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 2–5), teaches: A child belongs to many communities at once — the family community, the school community, religious communities (churches, mandirs, mosques), sports communities and business communities. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Belonging to many communities

Guyana Grade 2 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 11–13), teaches: A worked story of one child, Hannah, who belongs at the same time to her family, her school and her church communities, and what she does in each. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Belonging to many groups

Guyana Grade 1 Guyanese in the World (printed pp. 24–27), teaches: A person can belong to many groups at the same time — Adam is in the school choir, the cricket team and the Red Cross — and belonging brings help for one another, confidence, friends and learning to help people in need. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Guyana's way — the book's own teaching way

Guyana's own way: the Grade 2 book of the new series starts citizenship at the child's own doorstep — the many communities a child belongs to at once, the councils that serve them, the peoples and festivals that fill them, then decisions, conflict and rights, all before any map or market is taught. the series opens at Grade 1 inside the home — the family that cooks and gardens together, the chores shared out fairly, the groups a small child already belongs to, and rules taught as the way a home and a classroom stay in harmony. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
