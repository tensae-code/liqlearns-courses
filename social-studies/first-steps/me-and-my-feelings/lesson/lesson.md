# Me and My Feelings

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: all people pass through different feelings — joy, love, anger and sadness — and we must express our feelings and not keep them inside us, choosing the right way to express them and the suitable time for each matter: in the mother's feelings-cards game, Rami learns that we all live moments of anger, but you can control your anger and choose another way to express it besides crying and shouting — such as talking with your father or mother about what angers you — because crying and shouting do not change the thing that angered you; and Lana, sad because she pushed her friend Zahraa hard while playing, goes to her house and apologises to her — as Iraq's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Me and My Feelings

Iraq Grade 1 Moral Education (printed pp. 41–48), teaches: All people pass through different feelings — joy, love, anger and sadness — and we must express our feelings and not keep them inside us, choosing the right way to express them and the suitable time for each matter: in the mother's feelings-cards game, Rami learns that we all live moments of anger, but you can control your anger and choose another way to express it besides crying and shouting — such as talking with your father or mother about what angers you — because crying and shouting do not change the thing that angered you; and Lana, sad because she pushed her friend Zahraa hard while playing, goes to her house and apologises to her. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Iraq's way — the book's own teaching way

Iraq's own way: moral education is a separate subject from Grade 1, taught entirely as stories about five named children (Muhammad, Yusuf, Zahraa, Lana, Sara) whose small school and family incidents carry each value; every lesson ends in the child's own voice — "What I learned" — and the second unit's family tree is drawn with the father's help, branch by branch, until the big family shades them all. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
