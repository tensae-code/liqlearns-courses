# Video script — Me and My Feelings

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iraq Grade 1 Moral Education (printed pp. 41–48), teaches: All people pass through different feelings — joy, love, anger and sadness — and we must express our feelings and not keep them inside us, choosing the right way to express them and the suitable time for each matter: in the mother's feelings-cards game, Rami learns that we all live moments of anger, but you can control your anger and choose another way to express it besides crying and shouting — such as talking with your father or mother about what angers you — because crying and shouting do not change the thing that angered you; and Lana, sad because she pushed her friend Zahraa hard while playing, goes to her house and apologises to her.

Iraq's own way: moral education is a separate subject from Grade 1, taught entirely as stories about five named children (Muhammad, Yusuf, Zahraa, Lana, Sara) whose small school and family incidents carry each value; every lesson ends in the child's own voice — "What I learned" — and the second unit's family tree is drawn with the father's help, branch by branch, until the big family shades them all.
