# School Cooperative How Pupils Run It — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This part teaches: Morocco Grade 5 Dalil Moufid Ijtiamiat (Teacher Guide), printed p. 136, teaches: cooperative work, its meaning and benefits; forming the class cooperative by a real election — candidacy, campaign, voting with a polling booth and ballot box, counting the votes, and writing the cooperative's official record (محضر) for the school year — a child's first election, run properly.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This part teaches: Morocco Grade 6 Al-Massar Ijtiamiat, printed p. 121, teaches: founding a democratic office takes enough time for real electoral operations; the six stages drawn as a strip — candidate list, election campaign, voting, counting, announcing the results, writing the record; a girl candidate's programme (responsibility, cooperation, equality, involving everyone, encouraging cooperative work and solidarity with pupils in difficulty, respecting the environment); and the conditions for honouring the commitments made to the programme.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** This part teaches: Morocco's own way: Moroccan primary Social Studies is one integrated subject (History + Geography + Civic Education) built around "my homeland"; its civics third is a full civic-education programme in its own right — civic behaviour defined as the citizen's relation to society and to the state through its institutions, civic values (freedom, dignity, solidarity, equality, tolerance, rejecting discrimination) learned by practice not memorisation, and the classroom itself run as a small democracy (a negotiated class charter and an elected class cooperative with ballot box and vote count).
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** This part teaches: Morocco's own way: the Grade 6 civics course is a working apprenticeship in democracy — it opens with law, freedom and responsibility (Rousseau: no freedom without responsibility), runs the class as a republic (charter, elected cooperative), then walks out of the school gate: local democracy is learned by studying the elected communal council from the 2011 Constitution (Articles 135 and 139), visiting the commune headquarters, simulating a full council meeting with agenda, president, budget rapporteur and association representatives, and finally pleading a real solidarity proposal before that council.
**Answer: True**
