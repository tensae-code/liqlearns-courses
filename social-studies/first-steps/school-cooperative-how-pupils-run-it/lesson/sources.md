Book 1 is a teacher guide — its part above is evidence only; Morocco is counted here on its student book(s), named above.

Morocco's own way (Book 1, Dalil Moufid Ijtiamiat 5e): Moroccan primary Social Studies is one integrated subject (History + Geography + Civic Education) built around "my homeland"; its civics third is a full civic-education programme in its own right — civic behaviour defined as the citizen's relation to society and to the state through its institutions, civic values (freedom, dignity, solidarity, equality, tolerance, rejecting discrimination) learned by practice not memorisation, and the classroom itself run as a small democracy (a negotiated class charter and an elected class cooperative with ballot box and vote count).

Morocco's own way (Book 2, Al-Massar Ijtiamiat 6e): the Grade 6 civics course is a working apprenticeship in democracy — it opens with law, freedom and responsibility (Rousseau: no freedom without responsibility), runs the class as a republic (charter, elected cooperative), then walks out of the school gate: local democracy is learned by studying the elected communal council from the 2011 Constitution (Articles 135 and 139), visiting the commune headquarters, simulating a full council meeting with agenda, president, budget rapporteur and association representatives, and finally pleading a real solidarity proposal before that council.
