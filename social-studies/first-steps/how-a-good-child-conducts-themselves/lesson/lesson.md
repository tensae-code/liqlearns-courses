# How a Good Child Conducts Themselves

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: a person's worth is read from their conduct (явдал мөр) — their deeds, words and bearing; the good boy and good girl of the old teaching verses (heed the father's teaching, love learning, be compassionate) against their opposites, and the proverbs that sum a person up by what they do, not what they say — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 3 "Judging a boy's and a girl's conduct" (Хөвгүүн, охины явдал мөрийг шинжихүй)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 4, printed p. 10, teaches: A person's worth is read from their conduct (явдал мөр) — their deeds, words and bearing; the good boy and good girl of the old teaching verses (heed the father's teaching, love learning, be compassionate) against their opposites, and the proverbs that sum a person up by what they do, not what they say. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 4 widens "custom" (ёс) into the whole inherited order — greetings, food, dress, herding, tales, games and the calendar are all taught as rules a good person keeps — and then sets beside it the made rules of the state: taxes, the pupils' council, laws from the Great Khural, and fairness as the same rule for everyone. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
