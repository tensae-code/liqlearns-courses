# Learning Together the Class as a Collective

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the class as a collective — a group joined by common work and goals — with the rules of pair and group work, class self-government and an elected school council, and a collective's strength in its unity — as Kazakhstan's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Learning Together the Class as a Collective

Kazakhstan Grade 1 Poznanie mira (919/929) (printed p. 21), teaches: a collective as a group of people joined by common work; the rules of pair work and group work — both work, one speaks and the other listens, disagree politely, work quietly, everyone works for the common result — as mutual help and respect. Kazakhstan Grade 2 Poznanie mira (970/980) (printed p. 40), teaches: a collective as a group of people with common interests who can work or study together, the school class as such a collective, and how to communicate in class, judged through pictured situations. Kazakhstan Grade 2 Poznanie mira NIS (1031/1019) (printed p. 28), teaches: a collective as a group of people with a common goal; each class is a small collective and all classes together are the school collective, whose members are drawn as a chart — administration (the director and deputies), teachers, librarian, guard, cook, cleaner, doctor, janitor and the pupils — and a collective's strength is in its unity. Kazakhstan Grade 3 Poznanie mira (992/999) (printed p. 20), teaches: the class as a collective with common goals whose strength is that several people can solve what one cannot; self-government (самоуправление) as independence in managing your own affairs — the class takes collective decisions (the whole collective joins the holiday preparations) and draws up common rules of self-government so that differing opinions do not turn into quarrels. Kazakhstan Grade 4 Poznanie mira (110/88) (printed p. 28), teaches: the school as a small model of society, where independence and initiative for adult life are learned; a working system of pupil self-government — an elected school council (школьный совет) that carries pupils' problems to the director, class councils, and the committees of knowledge, culture and sport standing over the class collectives. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kazakhstan's way — the books' own teaching way

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 1): the primary course opens inside the child's own circles — class, family, school rules — and only then widens to the state: independence is taught as a checklist of a real state (territory, people, president and government, constitution, symbols, capital, tenge), and Kazakh family culture (the Tusau keser first-steps rite, the dastarkhan, hospitality) carries the family theme. Kazakhstan's own way (Book 2): Grade 2 turns the Grade 1 circles outward — the child meets "society" itself, etiquette as society's rules and the golden rule of politeness, then rights and duties read straight out of the Constitution, and service to society taught through volunteers and four named Kazakh figures (Abai, Akhmet Baitursynov, Kanysh Satpaev, Bauyrzhan Momyshuly). Kazakhstan's own way (Book 3): the NIS telling names things the Atamura book leaves general — citizenship by birth with rights and duties quoted from the Constitution itself (including Article 34's duty to respect the state symbols), the Kazakh custom of asar (the whole village doing unpaid work too heavy for one person) set beside modern volunteering, and the school collective drawn as a full staff chart from director to janitor. Kazakhstan's own way (Book 4): Grade 3 gives the child the Constitution itself — its preamble read aloud ("We, the people of Kazakhstan…"), the citizen's rights drawn as a diagram and Constitution Day named — and roots the family in the Kazakh shezhire, the genealogy every Kazakh was expected to know to the seventh generation (Zheti ata). Kazakhstan's own way (Book 5): Grade 4 is where the primary course turns civic in earnest — the republic's own map of 14 oblasts and three cities of republican significance, an elected school council drawn as a working model of the state, the Constitution's rights quoted article by article, and the country's face to the world built from the UN seat, the closed Semipalatinsk test site and EXPO-2017. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the books' text itself is never transcribed.
