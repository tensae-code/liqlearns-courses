# Hands-on — My Feelings and How I Express Them

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Guía 1 ¿Cómo es mi forma de ser?
Work through this part with your hands: Colombia Grade 1 CS_Fichas, printed p. 7, teaches: feelings (joy, sadness, anger, love) are felt by everyone but expressed differently, and children name how they want to be most of the time. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Guía 8 Y tú, ¿qué sientes?
Work through this part with your hands: Colombia Grade 3 CS_Grado3_02, printed pp. 30–42, teaches: emotions are innate ways of reacting (fear, anger, joy), grouped in types (anger/resentment, pain/sadness, joy), shown in faces and behaviour; it is healthy to express them — telling someone how you feel and how their acts affect you — and families handle emotions together (worked through Little Red Riding Hood's and the wolf's feelings). Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Colombia's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: Colombia's own way: Grade 1 worksheet "fichas" built around Alejo and Mariana; social studies starts from the child's own body, name and family, widening to neighbours and region; care is taught as a right and as men's and boys' work too, and no family type is ranked above another. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.

## Colombia's way (Book 11) — the book's own teaching way
Work through this part with your hands: Colombia's own way: culture is taught through named Colombian expressions (Pasto's Carnaval de Negros y Blancos, Riosucio's Carnaval del Diablo, the Madre Monte myth) and through imagining life inside another Colombian culture — speaking Pamié or Tucano — while emotions and affection get a full primary unit of their own. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
