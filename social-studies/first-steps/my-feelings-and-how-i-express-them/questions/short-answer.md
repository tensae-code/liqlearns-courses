# My Feelings and How I Express Them — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Guía 1 ¿Cómo es mi forma de ser? — Colombia Grade 1 CS_Fichas, printed p. 7, teaches: feelings (joy, sadness, anger, love) are felt by everyone but expressed differently, and children name how they want to be most of the time. (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Guía 8 Y tú, ¿qué sientes? — Colombia Grade 3 CS_Grado3_02, printed pp. 30–42, teaches: emotions are innate ways of reacting (fear, anger, joy), grouped in types (anger/resentment, pain/sadness, joy), shown in faces and behaviour; it is healthy to express them — telling someone how you feel and how their acts affect you — and families handle emotions together (worked through Little Red Riding Hood's and the wolf's feelings). (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Colombia's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way — Colombia's own way: Grade 1 worksheet "fichas" built around Alejo and Mariana; social studies starts from the child's own body, name and family, widening to neighbours and region; care is taught as a right and as men's and boys' work too, and no family type is ranked above another. (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Colombia's way (Book 11) — the book's own teaching way — Colombia's own way: culture is taught through named Colombian expressions (Pasto's Carnaval de Negros y Blancos, Riosucio's Carnaval del Diablo, the Madre Monte myth) and through imagining life inside another Colombian culture — speaking Pamié or Tucano — while emotions and affection get a full primary unit of their own. (any close wording).**
