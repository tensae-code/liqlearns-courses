# My Feelings and How I Express Them

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: feelings (joy, sadness, anger, love) are felt by everyone but expressed differently, and children name how they want to be most of the time — as Colombia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 1 ¿Cómo es mi forma de ser?

Colombia Grade 1 CS_Fichas, printed p. 7, teaches: feelings (joy, sadness, anger, love) are felt by everyone but expressed differently, and children name how they want to be most of the time. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 8 Y tú, ¿qué sientes?

Colombia Grade 3 CS_Grado3_02, printed pp. 30–42, teaches: emotions are innate ways of reacting (fear, anger, joy), grouped in types (anger/resentment, pain/sadness, joy), shown in faces and behaviour; it is healthy to express them — telling someone how you feel and how their acts affect you — and families handle emotions together (worked through Little Red Riding Hood's and the wolf's feelings). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Colombia's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Colombia's own way: Grade 1 worksheet "fichas" built around Alejo and Mariana; social studies starts from the child's own body, name and family, widening to neighbours and region; care is taught as a right and as men's and boys' work too, and no family type is ranked above another. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Colombia's way (Book 11) — the book's own teaching way

Colombia's own way: culture is taught through named Colombian expressions (Pasto's Carnaval de Negros y Blancos, Riosucio's Carnaval del Diablo, the Madre Monte myth) and through imagining life inside another Colombian culture — speaking Pamié or Tucano — while emotions and affection get a full primary unit of their own. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
