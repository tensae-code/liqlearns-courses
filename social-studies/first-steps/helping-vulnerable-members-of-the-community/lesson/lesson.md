# Helping Vulnerable Members of the Community

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: vulnerable members (orphans, the elderly, people with disabilities, the underprivileged) need extra support; help by visiting and talking, giving food/clothes/toys, helping across the road and showing kindness — as Zimbabwe's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 8 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 3) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 3) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum infant guide teaches civics through the child's own duties — rights arrive paired with responsibilities (homework, cleaning, respecting elders) and protection is taught as naming who to tell (grandmother, teacher, police), with community taught as shared things (borehole, dip tank, hall) people take turns to use. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 9 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 1) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 1) (teacher guide — evidence): at ECD the civics ladder starts inside the family — rights are taught as things every child must have (name, food, love, play) and protection as a spoken routine ("No, Stop, Tell someone") with named community helpers (teacher, parent, police officer, doctor/nurse); care is practised on the family's own vulnerable members (baby, Gogo, child on crutches). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 10 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 2) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 2) (teacher guide — evidence): ECD B widens ECD A's family civics one ring — rights gain the right to identity (your name, clan and totem) and to clean water, responsibilities are framed as being a "model citizen" of the school, and sharing-and-helping is taught in three widening places: school, community, family. ## Sub lesson 11 — Zimbabwe's way (Book 12) — the book's own teaching way

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 12): the learner's-book voice of the same syllabus — the community is "like a big family" that meets at the dare, shares the tap, sports grounds and clinics, and feeds its elderly, orphans and poor from the chief's own field (zunde ramambo/isiphal senkosi); children's roles are taught as changed by equality — in the past cooking was girls' work and herding boys', now both do the same roles.
