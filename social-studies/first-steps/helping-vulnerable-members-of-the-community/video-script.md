# Video script — Helping Vulnerable Members of the Community

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This script is full: the placements name a video-script need for this lesson. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zimbabwe Grade 1 zw-g1 PlusOne Social Science Grade 1 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 80), teaches: Vulnerable members (orphans, the elderly, people with disabilities, the underprivileged) need extra support; help by visiting and talking, giving food/clothes/toys, helping across the road and showing kindness.
Zimbabwe ECD A zw-ecda PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide (printed pp. 69–70), teaches: Vulnerable means needing extra help and special care to be safe and healthy — a new baby, a very old grandmother, a child on crutches — each needs specific gentle acts (soft voice and feeding; a hand to walk; space and no pushing).
Zimbabwe ECD A zw-ecda PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide (printed p. 71), teaches: Caring for sick family members as part of the family's duty to its vulnerable members.
Zimbabwe ECD A zw-ecda PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide (printed p. 72), teaches: Caring for elderly family members (Gogo) with patience, respect and practical help as the child's own part in the family.
Zimbabwe ECD B zw-ecdb PlusOne Social Science ECD B Teacher's Guide (printed p. 83), teaches: Vulnerable members named and cared for beyond the family, in the community and at school.
Zimbabwe Grade 2 zw-ventures-g2 Ventures Primary Family and Heritage Studies Learner's Book Grade 2 (printed p. 46), teaches: The community works the chief's field (zunde ramambo/isiphal senkosi) whose harvest feeds the elderly, orphans, the poor and visitors; helping people with disabilities (pushing a wheelchair), and visiting the sick in hospital to cheer them up.
Zimbabwe Grade 3 zw-g3 PlusOne Social Science Grade 3 Teacher's Guide (printed p. 150), teaches: The needy are those who require assistance because of poverty, orphanhood, disability or old age; orphanages such as Sunrise Children's Home, Ponesai Vanhu and Harare Children's Home provide shelter, food and education, and voluntary organisations such as Capernaum Trust and Angel of Hope Foundation help through donations and services.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 3) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum infant guide teaches civics through the child's own duties — rights arrive paired with responsibilities (homework, cleaning, respecting elders) and protection is taught as naming who to tell (grandmother, teacher, police), with community taught as shared things (borehole, dip tank, hall) people take turns to use.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 1) (teacher guide — evidence): at ECD the civics ladder starts inside the family — rights are taught as things every child must have (name, food, love, play) and protection as a spoken routine ("No, Stop, Tell someone") with named community helpers (teacher, parent, police officer, doctor/nurse); care is practised on the family's own vulnerable members (baby, Gogo, child on crutches).

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 2) (teacher guide — evidence): ECD B widens ECD A's family civics one ring — rights gain the right to identity (your name, clan and totem) and to clean water, responsibilities are framed as being a "model citizen" of the school, and sharing-and-helping is taught in three widening places: school, community, family. Honesty item, as filed: no ISBN is printed for ECD B — the imprint page repeats the ECD A title line and ECD A's ISBN 978-1-77932-134-3 (publisher copy error, confirmed on cover render: the cover correctly says ECD B).

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 12): the learner's-book voice of the same syllabus — the community is "like a big family" that meets at the dare, shares the tap, sports grounds and clinics, and feeds its elderly, orphans and poor from the chief's own field (zunde ramambo/isiphal senkosi); children's roles are taught as changed by equality — in the past cooking was girls' work and herding boys', now both do the same roles.

Zimbabwe's own way (Book 5) (teacher guide — evidence): rights teaching is concrete and local — support services are named institutions a child can find (BEAM school-fees support, community care workers, police, health workers), government services are named ministries and ZUPCO buses, and the needy are helped by named Zimbabwean homes and trusts (Harare Children's Home, Capernaum Trust, Angel of Hope Foundation).
