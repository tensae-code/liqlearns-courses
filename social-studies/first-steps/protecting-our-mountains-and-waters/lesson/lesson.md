# Protecting Our Mountains And Waters

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: for thousands of years Mongolians have lived by their livestock, and mountain, water, plants and soil are the animals' pasture — so nature is worshipped and guarded: mountains and waters have their master-spirits (савдаг, лус), the first of the tea and food is offered to them, and the taboos forbid digging up the earth, felling trees, breaking branches, picking plants by the root, hunting beyond need, and playing with fire — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 9 "Let's protect our mountains and waters" (Уул, усаа хамгаалья)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 2, printed p. 29, teaches: For thousands of years Mongolians have lived by their livestock, and mountain, water, plants and soil are the animals' pasture — so nature is worshipped and guarded: mountains and waters have their master-spirits (савдаг, лус), the first of the tea and food is offered to them, and the taboos forbid digging up the earth, felling trees, breaking branches, picking plants by the root, hunting beyond need, and playing with fire. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
