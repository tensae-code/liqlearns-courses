# My Town, My District and My Village

## Do it first

Your house does not stand alone. Before you read on, name the place your house stands in: is it a town, or a village? Then name the wider place around it. Write both names down — you will record them properly in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — My town and my village

In Suriname, Grade 3 children learn Geography from themselves outward, in the Ik-boek — the "I-book", a personal portfolio whose blank pages the pupil fills in. After the page for your house, the book widens: Mijn stad, mijn district en mijn dorp — My town, my district and my village.

The first wider place is the one your house stands in. Some children live in a town (stad); some live in a village (dorp). Either way, that place has a name, and it is yours to record: on the portfolio page you write and draw your own town or your own village — the place around your house, where your neighbours live too.

## Sub lesson 2 — My district: the wider place

Around your town or village lies a still wider place: your district (district). Your town or village sits inside its district, the way your house sits inside your town or village.

So the places hold each other, widening outward: house, then town or village, then district. The Ik-boek gives the district its own place in the same pages, because knowing where you live means knowing each of these places — not only your house, but the town or village around it and the district around that.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion your places in widening order: your house, your town or village, your district — and one thing you know about each. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
