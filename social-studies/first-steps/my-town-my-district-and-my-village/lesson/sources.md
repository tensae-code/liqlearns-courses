**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Suriname, Somalia, Serbia).**

Suriname's own way: OJW (Oriëntatie op Jezelf en de Wereld) starts Geography from the child outward — a blank-page personal portfolio the pupil fills in: self, family, house, then town/district/village, widening place by place. This lesson keeps that way: one placement (the book's combined town/district/village pages) is taught as its two widening steps — the town or village around the house, then the district around that — and the learner records their own places, exactly as the book's blank pages ask. The lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed. The book's non-Geography parts (Who am I, My family, What am I good at, What do I like, What do I want to become, What I liked best last year) are named in the placements file and are not placed in this lesson.


Serbia's own way (Grade 2 Свет око нас): the second year widens the circles again — family rules are now made by all members together, the settlement's people become "fellow citizens" with shared things (street, park, river, school, bus are all "ours"), and the state enters through its symbols: flag, coat of arms and the anthem "Боже правде", with the behaviour owed while it plays.
Searchable tags: my town, my district, my village, stad, district, dorp, Ik-boek, OJW, Suriname, Serbia, social studies, Somalia

Somalia's own way (Grade 3): Grade 3 turns from people to the working nation — its lessons are built on daily experience and field visits (to farms, cooperatives and self-help workplaces), because a pupil of this age learns society by visiting it; politics is taught as one of the three growth directions of the pupil, alongside the social and the economic.
