# Adults Work and Children Learn — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What does the first part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 2 Lección 1 Las personas adultas trabajan — The social landscape is built by people's work; adults work to meet our needs, and work is an activity for adults, not for children, whose main activity is learning (any close wording).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does the second part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 2 Lección 1 Las personas adultas trabajan — Adults' work builds the social landscape and meets our needs; work belongs to adults and learning to children (NEW lesson proposed in Book 1) (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does the third part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 2 Lección 1 Los adultos trabajan — Adults' work and the social landscape it builds (NEW lesson proposed in Book 1) (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does the fourth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 3 Lección 5 ¿Somos personas productivas? — Work is the main activity people do to earn a salary and meet basic needs; employment makes goods or services, unemployment is a social problem, and Article 37 of El Salvador's Constitution protects work (NEW lesson proposed in Book 1) (any close wording).**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What does the fifth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 3 Lección 3 Las y los trabajadores de América Central — The women and men who work in Central America and the work they do (NEW lesson proposed in Book 1) (any close wording).**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** What does the sixth part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Unidad 4 Lección 2 El trabajo en América — Work in the Americas (NEW lesson proposed in Book 1) (any close wording).**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** What does the seventh part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: El Salvador's way — the books' own teaching way — the Cipotas y Cipotes series teaches society from the child's own ground — adults' work in the local landscape, the family, the school and its institutions, then living together with rights and duties. Grade 2 widens Grade 1's frame to the whole society — "¡La sociedad somos todos!" — individuals, families, schools, public and private institutions and communities together form Salvadoran society, and its main institutions are named one by one. the same six-unit Cipotas y Cipotes frame deepens — society now "organises itself" (La sociedad se organiza) and the child's country closes the book (Mi país El Salvador). Grade 4 turns to the whole country — its media, its work, its rights — and closes with El Salvador's own internet story: first connection in December 1995 at ANTEL, first web pages at the UCA and Don Bosco universities. Grade 5 widens the frame from El Salvador to Central America — its workers, its families and its values are taught as a shared regional life. Grade 6 reaches the Americas, and its civics peak is El Salvador's own government-ethics law — public servants, their principles, and the 2006 Ley de Ética Gubernamental against corruption. (any close wording).**
