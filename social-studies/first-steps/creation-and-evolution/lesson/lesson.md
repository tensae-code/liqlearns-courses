# Creation and evolution — two accounts of human origin

## Do it first

Where did human beings come from? Stand in a classroom anywhere on earth and you will hear two answers. One says: we were created. The other says: we evolved, slowly, from earlier forms of life. This lesson sets the two accounts side by side, fairly — the way the book sets them for debate.

## Sub lesson 1 — The creation account

The **creationist view** holds that **humans and all living things were created by God, or a supernatural being**. In this account, life does not build itself up from other life across ages; it is made — each kind brought into being by a creator. It is the account held in the faith traditions of very many people, and for most of human history it was the only account there was. Hold it in its own terms first: a created world, with humankind placed in it by God's act.

## Sub lesson 2 — The evolution account

The **evolutionist — scientific — view** was formulated by **Charles Darwin (1809–1882)**. It holds that living things, **including humans, arose by gradual succession and variation from earlier forms**: across long ages, living things changed a little at a time, and new forms descended from older ones. The fossil lessons of this course — the Afar finds, Lucy, the long line of hominids — are this account's evidence, read bone by bone.

The book closes the comparison honestly: the **scientific view dominates scientific thinking**, while **large numbers of people worldwide hold the creationist view**. The two accounts are set side by side for debate — and a good debater can state the other side's account fairly before arguing their own.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grade 11 depth: Two accounts of human origins, side by side

The Grade 11 book sets the two families of explanation side by side and lets them be compared fairly: the creation accounts, held by faith traditions, and the scientific theory of evolution, in which Darwin's natural selection explains how species change across immense time. The classroom rule — confirmed by the teacher guide as evidence — is that pupils weigh the two on the evidence as presented, each account stated in its own strongest form.

## Say it to your companion

Tell the whole story of Creation and evolution back to your companion, part by part, in order — names, dates and places included. Your companion listens with the lesson closed and stops you at any name or date they cannot place; open the lesson together and repair that part.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write each account in exactly two sentences, in words its own holders would accept as fair.
2. List three pieces of evidence the evolution account points to (use the fossil lessons), and one question a creationist might ask about each.
3. Prepare a one-minute debate speech: argue your own view, but spend the first half stating the other view fairly.
