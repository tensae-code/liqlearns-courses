**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Serbia).**

Serbia's own way (Grade 2 Свет око нас): the second year widens the circles again — family rules are now made by all members together, the settlement's people become "fellow citizens" with shared things (street, park, river, school, bus are all "ours"), and the state enters through its symbols: flag, coat of arms and the anthem "Боже правде", with the behaviour owed while it plays.

Serbia's own way (Grade 3 Природа и друштво): the third-grade book widens "my region" into the whole republic — its many peoples named one by one as equal citizens, work divided into making and serving, and the environment taught as a duty: soil, water and parks protected by rules every child can keep.

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 2): Grade 2 turns the Grade 1 circles outward — the child meets "society" itself, etiquette as society's rules and the golden rule of politeness, then rights and duties read straight out of the Constitution, and service to society taught through volunteers and four named Kazakh figures (Abai, Akhmet Baitursynov, Kanysh Satpaev, Bauyrzhan Momyshuly).

Kazakhstan's own way (Book 3): the NIS telling names things the Atamura book leaves general — citizenship by birth with rights and duties quoted from the Constitution itself (including Article 34's duty to respect the state symbols), the Kazakh custom of asar (the whole village doing unpaid work too heavy for one person) set beside modern volunteering, and the school collective drawn as a full staff chart from director to janitor.

Angola's own way (Book 5): Grade 2 widens each Grade 1 topic by one step — the family gains kinship names and needs, the school gains its parts and the care of school materials, and the book closes with two short themes that are pure community life: work, and transport and communications.

Angola's own way (Book 6): Grade 3 is where Estudo do Meio turns outward — the child's own locality is inventoried place by place (school, hospital, market, bank, water fountain), and Angola's customs are taught region by region: dress, mother tongue, food, and festivals such as efico and circumcision celebrations.

Angola's own way (Book 7): Grade 4 names the idea itself — instituições públicas: buildings that belong to no private person but serve everyone — and defines quality of life as a checklist a community can judge itself by (education, health, housing, food, hygiene, culture, leisure), ending on the shared duties of sanitation and water.
