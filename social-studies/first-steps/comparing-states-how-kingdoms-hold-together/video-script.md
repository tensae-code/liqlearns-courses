# Video script — Comparing states: how kingdoms hold together

Story-style script in the animated-map spirit: the map opens, peoples and armies move, routes light up. Super-simple voice; made later, when videos are made.

---

[Scene 1 — One vocabulary for every state. The map and the scene build as the voice speaks.]
The Grade 11 book describes its states in one shared vocabulary — territory, tribute, trade and legitimacy — from Egypt and Aksum to the Sudanic empires, the sultanates, the Omotic kingdoms and the Gibe states.

That is deliberate: one vocabulary invites comparison, and comparison is this lesson's work.. Watch the names appear one by one: vocabulary, legitimacy, comparison. This is the part of the story to hold.

[Scene 2 — Five states on one table. The map and the scene build as the voice speaks.]
Set them out: Aksum held together by Red Sea trade and a Christian crown; Mali by gold, Islam and a travelling emperor; the sultanates by caravan taxes and councils; Kafa by a sacral king and closed forests; Jimma by coffee wealth and a merchant king.

Different fuels, same engine-parts. Watch the names appear one by one: table, sacral king, strain. This is the part of the story to hold.

[Scene 3 — The comparison habit. The map and the scene build as the voice speaks.]
Whenever this course meets a new state, ask the four questions: What is its territory? Who pays it tribute, and for what protection? What trade feeds it? Why do its people accept its rule?

Four questions, asked of every kingdom, turn a list of states into an understanding of statehood itself.. Watch the names appear one by one: statehood, protection, accept. This is the part of the story to hold.
