Bahrain's own way (Bahrain Grade 4 BH Citizenship G4): citizenship is taught outwards from the child — homeland love and public property first, then the services of Bahrain's own government, the child's own rights under the UN Convention Bahrain ratified, choosing leaders from the class council up to the King, Crown Prince and Prime Minister, and finally Bahrain inside the Gulf Cooperation Council, its own regional family.

Philippines's own way: the whole third unit of Grade 4 is one civics arc — the national government and its three branches first, then levels down to the local government, then what government does for citizens (health, education, peace, infrastructure, rights), closing on local government working with community servants; the Bill of Rights is taught straight from Article III of the Philippine Constitution.

Somalia's own way (Grade 1, teacher guide — evidence): this subject was created by the 1969 Revolution to form the new Somali person — personality (qofnimo) is built, not born: family, food, dress, schooling and society shape it, and the school's job is to graft onto the child Somali-ness, straight morals, a living conscience, patriotism, responsibility and tolerance, in that order, from the flag outward to the society the pupil lives in.
