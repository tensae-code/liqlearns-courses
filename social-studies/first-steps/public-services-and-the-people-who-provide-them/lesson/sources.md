Sweden's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the guide frames the whole of primary civics for the adapted school as the services and jobs of everyday life — part 1 the businesses a pupil meets (bakery, shop, pharmacy, hairdresser…), part 2 the people who work in them — opened through discussion pictures and real visits, with difficult words (kollektivtrafik/public transport, återvinningscentral/recycling centre) explained concretely. The pupil book is book 3 of a five-book "Så funkar samhället" series (traffic; culture and leisure; recycling; healthcare and blue-light services are the other parts). That evidence is stocked here as the way Sweden teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Bahrain's own way (Bahrain Grade 4 BH Citizenship G4): citizenship is taught outwards from the child — homeland love and public property first, then the services of Bahrain's own government, the child's own rights under the UN Convention Bahrain ratified, choosing leaders from the class council up to the King, Crown Prince and Prime Minister, and finally Bahrain inside the Gulf Cooperation Council, its own regional family.

Philippines's own way: the whole third unit of Grade 4 is one civics arc — the national government and its three branches first, then levels down to the local government, then what government does for citizens (health, education, peace, infrastructure, rights), closing on local government working with community servants; the Bill of Rights is taught straight from Article III of the Philippine Constitution.

Haiti's own way (EC2 7AF citoyenne/citoyen dans un État démocratique) (teacher guide — evidence): The democratic state is taught as resting on its citizens: rights are learned by exercising them at school (debate, a school charter of rights and duties), and Haitian texts (1987 amended Constitution, the 1801/1805 constitutions, laws on paternity and on violence against women) are read beside the international declarations. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Ecuador's own way (Estudios Sociales 2do EGB): the transition-curricular Grade 2 book is a workbook of songs, stories and activity spreads organised in three sections that always start from the child — family first, then school and community, then Ecuador and South America — with rights taught from the family outward to universal human rights. That evidence is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing; it is counted in the acceptance count above (student book).

Yemen's own way (G4 Social Studies): Grade 4 turns from the child's surroundings to the homeland's institutions — education, health, media and traffic police are taught one ministry at a time, each with the supervising ministry named, its real Yemeni buildings pictured (the Ministry of Education and al-Thawra Hospital in Sana'a among them), and the government's duty to keep improving the service. That evidence is stocked here as the way Yemen teaches this thing; it is counted in the acceptance count above (student book).

Yemen's own way (G5 National Education): a standalone National Education book that teaches the state through Yemen's own map and constitution-era institutions — the country is divided into governorates and districts the child can name, the three authorities are taught one per lesson, and democracy is introduced through shura (consultation), with Yemenis presented as early practitioners of it. That evidence is stocked here as the way Yemen teaches this thing; it is counted in the acceptance count above (student book).
