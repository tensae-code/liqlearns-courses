**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: I love my homeland — love of homeland shown in deeds at a child's scale — giving books to poorer children in the hamlet, incense at the martyrs' memorial, drawing the homeland, cleaning and planting, visiting a museum with a veteran guide, writing to grandparents far away (any close wording).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Proud of my homeland Vietnam — the homeland's beauty in land and people (Tuyên Quang's terraced fields, Hạ Long Bay, Hội An old town, Tây Nguyên gong culture, a Trường Sa soldier, garment workers, the class donation box for Central Vietnam) and its development (oil lamp to electric light, old school to new, ferry landing to bridge), and the deeds that show love of country — protecting nature, gratitude to those who served it, studying well, introducing it to visitors (the slug was proposed as NEW in Book 2 of this file; counted here as an existing target) (any close wording).**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What does this part of this lesson teach?
**Answer: Vietnam's way — the book's own teaching way — Grade 2 widens the circle from family to homeland and public space: love of homeland is proven in small deeds (a donation box for neighbours in difficulty, flowers planted along the village road, a class visit to a Heroic Vietnamese Mother), and the civics core is rules in public places, taught place by place — school, hospital, bus stop, library, cinema, zoo, historic site. Grade 3 opens the Ethics course with the state itself — the country's full official name, its flag and its anthem are taught as things a child must know and honour standing still — before widening to neighbours, promises and quarrels; traffic safety returns as a full closing theme, now split into walking rules and rules for riding in vehicles.
