# Mine And Other Peoples Property

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: everyone has their own things and their own creations — your notebook, book and pencil are yours alone, and a family's fence, house, livestock and car are the family's private property; a scholar's book or invention is intellectual property; you may not take what others made by their labour without permission, and what you find you return to its owner — worked through Bat and Bold's quarrel over a pen taken without asking — as Mongolia's books teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 16 "Mine and others' property" (Миний болон бусдын өмч)

Mongolia Civic Education Grade 2, printed p. 47, teaches: Everyone has their own things and their own creations — your notebook, book and pencil are yours alone, and a family's fence, house, livestock and car are the family's private property; a scholar's book or invention is intellectual property; you may not take what others made by their labour without permission, and what you find you return to its owner — worked through Bat and Bold's quarrel over a pen taken without asking. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's way — the books' own teaching way

Mongolia's own way: Grade 2 civics is taught through custom (ёс) and story — the respect and friendship customs come as a folk tale and a taboo list, property as a quarrel over a borrowed pen — and the state first appears as the soum office and its governor, with the child's own address as the first civic fact. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
