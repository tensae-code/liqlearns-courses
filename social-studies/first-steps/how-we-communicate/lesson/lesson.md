## Sub lesson 13 — El Salvador's way — the books' own teaching way

El Salvador's own way: the Cipotas y Cipotes series teaches society from the child's own ground — adults' work in the local landscape, the family, the school and its institutions, then living together with rights and duties. Grade 2 widens Grade 1's frame to the whole society — "¡La sociedad somos todos!" — individuals, families, schools, public and private institutions and communities together form Salvadoran society, and its main institutions are named one by one. the same six-unit Cipotas y Cipotes frame deepens — society now "organises itself" (La sociedad se organiza) and the child's country closes the book (Mi país El Salvador). Grade 4 turns to the whole country — its media, its work, its rights — and closes with El Salvador's own internet story: first connection in December 1995 at ANTEL, first web pages at the UCA and Don Bosco universities. Grade 5 widens the frame from El Salvador to Central America — its workers, its families and its values are taught as a shared regional life. Grade 6 reaches the Americas, and its civics peak is El Salvador's own government-ethics law — public servants, their principles, and the 2006 Ley de Ética Gubernamental against corruption. Grade 8's democracy unit is El Salvador's and Central America's own institutionality — the State, electoral rights, and municipal governments in El Salvador itself. the final book's civics is law for working life — labour legislation and intellectual property — and rights pushed to their third and fourth generations. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
