# Legal forms of business

## Do it first

Look at the businesses you know. One person runs the small grocery shop alone. Two people sew clothes together. A group of farmers sells its maize together. Before you read on, say how these businesses are different in who owns them.

## Sub lesson 1 — One owner — the sole trader, and partners together

In Zambia's Grade 4 teaching module, Sub-topic 4.8.2 is Types of Businesses. The first form is the **sole trader**: one person owns the business alone, makes the decisions alone, and takes the profit — and the risk — alone. The module tells it through **Mr Banda's grocery shop**: Mr Banda's shop is his own.

The second form is a **partnership**: two or more people own a business together, share the work, share the capital they put in, and share the profit. The module's partnership is **Mary and John's tailoring partnership** — two tailors working as one business.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cooperative — members owning together

The third form is a **cooperative**: a business owned together by its members, who join it, work through it and share what it earns. The module's cooperative is a **farmers' maize cooperative** — maize farmers who group together, so their maize is collected, stored and sold together, instead of each farmer selling a small amount alone.

In a cooperative the members are the owners together: that is what makes it different from one sole trader or a small partnership.

## Sub lesson 3 — The limited company — owned by shareholders

The fourth form is a **limited company**: a bigger business owned by **shareholders** — people who each own a share, a part, of the company. The module's example is a **transport company owned by shareholders**: many people own parts of it together, and the company runs the buses or trucks as one business.

So the four forms, in order of how ownership widens: one owner alone (sole trader), a few owners together (partnership), the members owning together (cooperative), and many shareholders each owning a part (limited company).
