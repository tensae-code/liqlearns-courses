# What to produce?

## Do it first

A country's leaders list what could be made this year: bread, tractors, uniforms, jewellery. They cannot maximise all four. Which pairs fight each other? Say your answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The problem of allocation

What to produce is also called the problem of allocation of resources: every economy must decide the type and quantity of goods it will make, because its resources cannot make everything.

[Picture 24014 — a national shopping list being chosen between bread, machines, and luxuries]

## Sub lesson 2 — The great either-ors

The decision runs along named pairs: consumption goods against capital goods, civil goods against military goods, necessities against luxuries. More of one side always means less of the other.

## Sub lesson 3 — No economy escapes it

Rich and poor, planned and free — every economy answers this question somehow, by a plan, by markets, or by custom. Refusing to choose is itself a choice, and scarcity collects its cost anyway.
