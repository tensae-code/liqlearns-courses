# Free resources and scarce resources

## Do it first

Air, sunshine, fertile land, a skilled nurse's hour. Which could you take as much of as you wanted, free, without running out for others? Which have prices? Say the test you are using.

## Sub lesson 1 — Resources are inputs

Resources are inputs used to produce goods and services. Every product you have ever used began as resources — nature's gifts and people's effort — fed into production.

[Picture 24021 — sunshine and stream water on the free side; fertile land, clean water and skilled labour on the priced side]

## Sub lesson 2 — Free resources

A resource is free when more is available than people want at zero price — a free gift of nature with no price: air, sunshine, a mountain stream. The guide's answer on air pollution (teacher guide — evidence): air stays a free resource because no price is paid to breathe it, even though pollution's harm is paid for — the course's first use of the word externality.

## Sub lesson 3 — Economic resources — and the test

A resource is economic, or scarce, when less is available than people want at zero price, so it carries a non-zero price. Price is the test of which kind a resource is: priced means scarce; priceless and unlimited means free.
