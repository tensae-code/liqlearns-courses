# Fair prices, costs and the basic basket

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: a fair price the family budget can reach. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — A fair price the family budget can reach

Inside its commerce chapter, the senior book states a standard for prices: products must have a **fair price accessible to the family budget**.

A price is not fair in this book because a seller asks it or a market bears it. It is fair when a family's budget can reach it — the family, not the market, is the measuring stick.

## Sub lesson 2 — The basic basket — what fair prices must cover

The standard has a name and a contents list: the **basic basket (canasta básica)**. Fair prices matter because the basic basket must be **coverable** — a family must be able to buy the basket its life requires.

The Grade 7 book priced the food basket as an indicator; the senior book turns the basket into law's business. That is the next part.

## Sub lesson 3 — The State regulates costs — the Sundde and the fair-gains law

The book names the machinery: the Venezuelan State **regulates costs through laws**, and the **Sundde** watches over the application of the **Law of Fair Gains, Costs and Prices**.

The same Sundde the Grade 7 book taught consumers to denounce to is here the watcher of a whole prices law. Fair price, in Venezuela's senior book, is a legal standard with an institution behind it.
