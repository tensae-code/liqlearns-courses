# Special cases of the demand curve and elasticity

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.2 §11 Special cases of the demand curve and elasticity** — teaches: vertical, horizontal and unit-elastic demand curves. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Vertical demand — quantity does not respond

Greece's Grade 12 textbook closes its elasticity survey with the borderline shapes. A **vertical** demand curve means quantity demanded is the **same at every price** — perfectly inelastic demand, elasticity zero.

Buyers take the same amount whether the price rises or falls, as with a life-needing good in the short run where no substitute and no delay is possible.

## Sub lesson 2 — Horizontal demand — quantity responds without limit

A **horizontal** demand curve is the opposite borderline: at the going price buyers will take **any quantity**, but above it they take **none** — perfectly elastic demand, elasticity without limit (infinite).

It is the demand a single small seller faces in perfect competition: charge a cent more than the market price and every buyer walks away.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit-elastic demand

Between the borderlines sits **unit elasticity**: quantity responds in the **same proportion** as price — a 10% price rise, a 10% quantity fall — so elasticity is exactly **one**.

The three special cases fix the ends and the middle of the spectrum: zero (vertical), one (unit), infinite (horizontal). Every real demand sits somewhere on that scale.
