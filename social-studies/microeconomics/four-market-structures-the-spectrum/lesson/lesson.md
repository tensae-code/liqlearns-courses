# Four market structures — the spectrum

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — What a market structure is

A market is the mechanism that brings buyers and sellers into contact. Market structures are classified by three things: the number and size of firms and consumers, the type of product sold, and how freely information flows among the participants. Change those three and the firm's power over price changes with them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four structures in order

The unit studies four structures, placed on a single spectrum from most to least competitive: perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, and pure monopoly. The rest of the unit walks the spectrum in exactly that order, one structure at a time.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the spectrum matters

Where a firm sits on the spectrum decides how it must behave: whether it takes price as given or sets it, whether its demand curve is flat or sloping, and whether profits can survive in the long run. Every diagram in this unit is the spectrum, drawn one stop at a time.
