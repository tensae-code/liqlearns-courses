---

Microeconomics is concerned with single decision-making units: a household deciding what to buy, a firm deciding what to make, an industry of firms making the same kind of thing. Its questions are sized to one unit at a time.

The micro questions are particular ones: one product's price, one person's income or saving. Its fundamental problem is resource allocation — which is price determination — and its main tools are the demand and supply of particular goods and factors.

Around the two main branches stands a wider family of newer branches the book names — development economics, environmental economics, labour economics, monetary economics, health economics and others — each applying the same thinking to one field of life.
