# Movement along vs shift of the demand curve

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.2 §7 Change in quantity demanded vs change in demand** — teaches: a price change moves along the curve, other determinants shift it. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — A price change moves along the curve

Greece's Grade 12 textbook separates two things students constantly mix. First: when the good's **own price** changes and nothing else does, quantity demanded changes **along the same demand curve** — up the curve when price rises, down it when price falls.

The curve itself has not moved. The same buyers, with the same incomes and tastes, are simply naming a different quantity at the new price.

## Sub lesson 2 — Other determinants shift the curve

Second: when a **determinant other than price** changes — income, tastes, the price of a related good — the whole curve **shifts**. At every price, buyers now want more (shift right) or less (shift left) than before.

A shift means the demand itself has changed; a movement means only the chosen quantity has changed, because the price changed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading a diagram without mixing the two

The test in any diagram: ask what changed. Price of the good itself → movement along. Anything else on the demand side → shift of the curve.

Mixing them reverses cause and effect: a price rise caused by stronger demand is a shift followed by a new equilibrium, not a movement that 'caused' itself.
