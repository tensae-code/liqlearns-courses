# Video script — Cardinal utility and the consumer's optimum

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Utility is the satisfaction a consumer gets from consuming goods and services. The cardinal approach measures that satisfaction in absolute numbers called utils. Marginal utility is the extra satisfaction from consuming one more unit of a good — the change in total utility divided by the change in quantity. In the book's worked activity, total utility rises from 40 utils at four cups of tea to 50 utils at five cups, so the fifth cup's marginal utility is 50 − 40 = 10 utils.

As a consumer consumes more and more of a good, the extra satisfaction from each further unit gets smaller: marginal utility diminishes. The first cup of tea on a thirsty morning adds a lot; the fifth adds little. This falling pattern is the engine of the whole unit — it is why buyers eventually stop, and why the demand curve slopes down.

The consumer's optimum under the cardinal approach is where the ratio of marginal utility to price is equal for every good — marginal utility per birr spent is the same everywhere — and the whole income is spent. If one good gave more MU per birr than another, moving a birr toward it would raise total satisfaction, so equality is the resting point. From diminishing marginal utility the book also derives the demand curve: it is the positive segment of the marginal-utility curve when utility is measured in money.
