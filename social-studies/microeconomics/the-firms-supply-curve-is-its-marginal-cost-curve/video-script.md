# Video script — The firm's supply curve is its marginal cost curve

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Below the shutdown price the firm supplies nothing at all: it shuts down, as the previous lesson showed. The supply curve therefore does not start at zero price and climb smoothly — it is absent entirely until price reaches the minimum of AVC.

Above the shutdown price, the firm supplies the quantity at which price equals marginal cost. Raise the price and the P = MC point slides up the rising MC curve to a larger quantity; lower it and the quantity slides down. Price by price, the MC curve itself answers 'how much will you supply?'

So the firm's short-run supply curve simply is the part of its MC curve lying above AVC — one curve doing two jobs. Summing those segments sideways across all firms — adding every firm's quantity at each price — gives the upward-sloping industry supply curve that meets demand to set the market price.
