# Video script — What shifts supply

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

When a good's own price changes, quantity supplied extends or contracts along the same supply curve — a movement, not a new curve. Extension when price rises, contraction when it falls. Only a change in a supply condition — not the good's own price — draws a new curve.

Supply shifts when its conditions change: factor costs (dearer inputs shift supply left), technology (better methods shift it right), external shocks such as floods, taxes (left) and subsidies (right), transport conditions, and the prices of other goods a producer could make instead — if that other price rises, producers switch and this good's supply shifts left. Each shifter changes the quantity offered at every price.

The Tables 2.7–2.8 grid reads supply shifts the same way as demand shifts. A rightward supply shift lowers equilibrium price and raises quantity; a leftward shift — a tax, a flood — raises price and lowers quantity. Combined demand-and-supply shifts can leave price or quantity uncertain, which the grid marks honestly.

## Grade 11 deepening from the Grade 10 teacher guide extension

The Grade 10 list of supply determinants gains one more member from the teacher guide: the objective of the firm. What a firm is trying to do — maximise profit, grow its market share, or simply survive — changes how much it offers at each price, just as surely as its costs do. Two firms with identical costs but different objectives will not supply the same quantities.

Work the guide's own equilibrium exercise with care. Demand is Q = 250 − 3P and supply is Q = 2P − 50. They meet where 250 − 3P = 2P − 50, giving P = 60 and Q = 70. At a price of 75, demand is 250 − 225 = 25 and supply is 2(75) − 50 = 100 — a surplus of 75 units. The guide's key reaches the same surplus conclusion but prints the supply step with a flipped sign; the numbers taught here are recomputed, not copied.
