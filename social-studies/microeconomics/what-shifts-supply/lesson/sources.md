Grade 10 teacher-guide note (placements 5–6): the objective of the firm joins the supply determinants from *Economics, Teacher Guide Book, Grade 10*, printed pp. 18–19 (teacher guide — evidence). The guide's review key for the equilibrium exercise (demand Q = 250 − 3P, supply Q = 2P − 50) computes supply at P = 75 as 2(75) + 50 = 200 — a sign error in the key as printed. The build teaches the corrected arithmetic: 2(75) − 50 = 100, against demand of 250 − 3(75) = 25, so the surplus at P = 75 is 75 units and the equilibrium (P = 60, Q = 70) stands as the key gives it. The guide's activity keys (teacher guide — evidence, printed pp. 24–28) also give: price elasticity for Activity 2.3, income elasticity 0.6 for Activity 2.4, and supply elasticity 0.017 for Activity 2.5.

**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Bangladesh, Yemen, Armenia, Iraq, United States, Pakistan, Nigeria, Greece, Samoa).**

Samoa's own way (Year 9 Business Studies Book 1): Economics opens with producers, not definitions — a Samoan is first a fisher, planter or weaver in one of three industries, and money itself is introduced through Samoa's own history (fine mats and tapa, German marks, pounds, the tala of 1967).

Samoa's own way (Year 11 Business Studies Book 1): The market strand is taught with Samoan schedules — Ioane's barbershop supply curve, Kuki's juice, taro supply before White Sunday, Lesina's sticky-bun demand at the school canteen.

Samoa's own way (Year 11 Social Studies Book 2): A whole super-unit runs on one crop — the coconut carries opportunity cost, commodity prices, export earnings, government support and even OPEC's cartel, because copra is where a Samoan family meets the world market.

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Economics): A full NZ-style supply/demand course rebuilt on Samoan ground — Apia milk and taro-fertiliser markets, VAGST, the tala/yen foreign-exchange market, Samoan public/merit goods, and money-supply definitions for Samoa's banks.

Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 11 New Economics SS2): SS2 pairs every theory block with its Nigerian application in the same theme — wage policy and trade unions, agricultural policy and marketing, revenue allocation in the federation, the Nigerian Stock Exchange and inflation in Nigeria.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 11 Economics Class 11): a full Urdu-medium microeconomics course whose every Urdu section heading carries the English term in brackets, so the learner meets both languages' vocabulary at once; theory is built from schedules, tables and hand-drawn curves throughout, and distribution (rent, wages, interest, profit) closes the book as one factor-pricing chapter.


United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Economics): a full Western-style microeconomics course rooted in Iraq — economics itself is traced to Mesopotamia (the first banks at Nasiriyah, loan-interest rules, Hammurabi's laws), worked examples run in dinars on chickens, notebooks, dates and children's toys, and the monopoly and oligopoly examples are Iraq's white-oil distribution and its three mobile-phone companies.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Social Science student): Grade 10 is a full Economics course inside Social Science — demand and supply are taught with a school-canteen juice schedule priced in dram, the production-possibilities curve with duduks and shvis (Armenian instruments), and personal finance through Armenian institutions: Central-Bank-licensed lenders, the ACRA credit bureau, the Financial System Mediator and the Rate.am / Appa.am comparison sites, including a fake "Gazprom Armenia" investment-scam case.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

Worked-answer error, taught corrected: the §2.2.3 supply-factor bullet on the price of other goods reads backwards as printed. Taught corrected here: if the price of another good a producer could make instead RISES, supply of this good falls (shifts left), because producers switch to the better-paying good — and the reverse when that other price falls.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 10 Aganina): Economics is taught inside an entrepreneurship course — market theory arrives as the theory an entrepreneur needs, with tenge-denominated supply examples (a potato supply curve at 50-70 tenge), world oil prices deciding whether an Almaty-region furniture workshop profits, and Kazakhstan's own post-Soviet transition as the opening history.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 10 Duisenkhanov Part 1): the same national curriculum taught in a three-lesson rhythm — one theory lesson, one game, one Kazakhstani or world business case (Rocket Study, Yuframe, Shymbulak, Elon Musk) — so every Economics concept is immediately played and then applied.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 10 Ekonomi Tingkatan 4): a full KSSM economics course in Malay with bilingual BM–BI glossaries, ringgit-denominated tables throughout, and Malaysian institutions (Bank Negara, KWSP/EPF, PERKESO) as the living examples.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.
