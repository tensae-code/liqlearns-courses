# Household budget — income, spending and saving

## Do it first

Think of your own family's money for one month. Before you read on, list where the money comes from — every source you can name — and one thing your family saves for.

## Sub lesson 1 — The household budget — a plan of income against spending

Mongolia's Grade 7 book teaches family economics through the **household budget**: a **plan of income against spending**. In 3.2 Family economics, the household budget, the family sets what it expects to receive on one side and what it expects to spend on the other — and the book works it as a **family table in thousand tögrög**, so the plan is real numbers a family can check, not an idea.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where the income comes from — wages, interest, dividends, business income, pensions

The income side of Mongolia's budget is named source by source: **wages**, **savings interest**, **dividends**, **business income** and **pensions**. A household's income is not one lump — each part comes from a different place, and the budget plan starts by listing them all before a single tögrög is spent.

## Sub lesson 3 — Saving goals — and loans and debt

The plan does not end with spending. The book puts **saving goals** inside the budget — amounts the family means to set aside on purpose — and it faces **loans and debt** in the same plan: money borrowed is part of the family's economics, and repaying it is spending the budget must carry. A budget that plans its saving and its debts is the family's whole money picture in one table.
