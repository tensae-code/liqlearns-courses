**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Mongolia, Samoa).**

Samoa's own way (Year 10 Business Studies Book 2): Personal finance is taught through one Letogo family — the Taitos — whose fortnightly budget mixes a teacher's salary, hotel wages with overtime, subsistence taro and fishing, church donations and SNPF deductions.

Samoa's own way (Year 11 Business Studies Book 2): Economic management is tested against Samoan values — a wedding budget where six family branches each raise $800, church donations count as essential spending, and an emergency fund is kept for sua and tofa obligations.

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 7 Social Studies 7): Economics enters through the child's own life — needs, the family budget in tögrög, household chores and herding work — before any market abstraction; property is taught through the school itself.
