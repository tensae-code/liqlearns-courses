# Video script — Why opportunity cost rises — the law of increasing opportunity cost

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The law of increasing opportunity cost: as more and more of one good is produced, the opportunity cost of each extra unit rises. The worked X–Y table showed it in numbers — 5, then 15, then 20 units of Y per unit of X.

The cost rises because resources are specialised and not equally adaptable to both uses. The resources best suited to the new good move first and cost little; later moves drag away resources that were much better at the old good.

Rising costs bend the frontier: the PPF is concave — bowed out — to the origin. A straight-line frontier would mean constant cost; the bow is the picture of costs that grow with every step.
