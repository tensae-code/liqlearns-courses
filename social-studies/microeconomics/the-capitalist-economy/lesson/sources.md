**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Laos, Dominican Republic, Venezuela, Dominica, Cambodia, Bangladesh, Honduras, Fiji, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Samoa, Bhutan, Sri Lanka). Papua New Guinea is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class IX Economics IX): Economics is framed throughout by Gross National Happiness (GNH) — material vs spiritual well-being — and every concept is anchored in Bhutanese examples (ngultrum-era money history, hydropower exports to India, Kheng bamboo products, Royal Monetary Authority).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class XI Economics XI): Class XI re-teaches the Class IX foundations at full analytical depth (elasticity calculation, equilibrium shifts, BoP correction) and adds Bhutan-specific system, Five-Year Plan and trade-policy chapters.

Samoa's own way (Year 10 Business Studies Book 1): Economics is taught through Samoan life — fa'asamoa borrowing, remittances, fa'alavelave and church spending in family budgets, local producers such as Vailima and copra drying at Lefaga, and a stated absence of consumer-protection law ("the buyer must beware").

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 10 Social Science TG): Grade 10 Economics is the labour and capitalism year — two full units on labour (unions, division of labour, labour law, labour costs), then the market system judged by its costs and benefits, trade through television and the internet, and a closing unit interrogating the capitalist ideology itself. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales 11, Porras y Gamboa): a commercial textbook built on the same national programme as the CONED anthology but with far more economic detail — full PAE I/II/III measure lists, the CAT export-certificate fraud, state banking's six effects, and Costa Rica's services/call-centre economy measured against the region.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 10 Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 economics is economic geography of Fiji itself — village, semi-subsistence and commercial farming, the sugar industry's markets and problems, then industry as primary, secondary and tertiary activity with location factors and trade.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 6 Venezuela G6 Social Sciences SB Spanish): G6 names the system outright — capitalism, dependent capitalism and the informal economy are defined in the pupil's own book, and development is redefined as gains in people's standard of living with the least environmental damage. This lesson teaches the placements' content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 8 Venezuela G8 Historia de la Humanidad SB Spanish): economic systems are taught as history's engine — modes of production (feudal, capitalist, socialist) are defined and compared, and the 1929 crisis is taught with its mechanism: overproduction meeting wages that did not rise. This lesson teaches the placements' content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 6 Ciencias Sociales 6° Primaria): economic systems arrive inside world history — capitalism is introduced through the Scientific and Industrial Revolutions and Adam Smith, then set against socialism and Marx in a compare-the-systems table the pupils build themselves, opening with the question of whether their own country is capitalist or socialist.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 7 Ciencias Sociales 1° Secundaria): at secondary level the tax lesson becomes concrete Dominican fiscal practice — the ITBIS rate a pupil sees added at the shop till, its reduced rate and its exemptions for the family food basket, water, electricity, health and education.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 4° Secundaria): at this level the analysis turns Marxian in vocabulary — capitalism is explained through the separation of the worker from the means of production and the owner's appropriation of surplus labour (plusvalía), and imperialism through cartels, monopoly centralisation and the export of capital.

The teacher guide's capitalism–socialism comparison answer asserts flatly that "there is no similarity between capitalism and socialism" — recorded in the placements as the guide's stated position; this lesson does not teach that assertion as fact, since the mixed-economy lesson in this same course is built on the two systems combining.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Laos' own way (Laos Grade 10 Business student): Economics is taught as the theory inside a "become a businessperson" course, drawn from village life — weaving, market stalls and repair shops sorted into producing, trading and services; the circular flow is drawn between households and the village business, and "produce for whom?" is answered with a Rolls-Royce.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 4 G4 Student CCSS): Grade 4 widens the lens to Central America — regional production and diversification, tourism as an earner, and a first named look at capitalism and globalisation.
