# The capitalist economy

## Do it first

On one street, five privately owned shops compete for the same customers, and the town office only keeps order. Who decides what the shops stock — the office, or the customers? Say your answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Private ownership, profit motive

In a capitalist economy all means of production are privately owned, and production is undertaken by private entrepreneurs for profit, with negligible government intervention — hence its other names, free-market or laissez-faire economy. The guide adds Loucks's definition (teacher guide — evidence): private ownership and use for private profit of man-made and nature-made capital.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its working features

Its features: the right to private property; consumer sovereignty — consumers' choices direct production; competition among sellers, buyers, workers and employers; and self-interest as the motive force. The guide's list adds freedom of enterprise, the profit motive and the price mechanism (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 3 — Limited government — and the cost

Government is limited, confined largely to defence and law and order. The system's named cost stands beside its freedoms: large income inequality, because rewards follow ownership and market success, not need.
