# Simón Rodríguez — trades, work and social economy

## Sub lesson 1 — A trade for one's economy

Venezuela's Grade 3 book teaches economics through a person: **Simón Rodríguez**, Bolívar's teacher, who knew how to educate for freedom.

His thought, as the book gives it to 8-year-olds: people must be **trained in a trade (un oficio) for their economy**, and in politics to organise and build the Republic. A trade is not a school subject here — it is a person's economic foundation.

## Sub lesson 2 — School that combines study and productive work

Rodríguez's school is a working school: its teaching must **combine study and productive work**.

The two are not rivals in his design. Study without work leaves a person unable to live; work without study leaves the Republic unbuilt. The Grade 3 book puts that union at the centre of its one economics chapter.

## Sub lesson 3 — Three maxims and work as a right and a duty

The book gives Rodríguez's **three educational maxims**: popular education, assignment to useful exercises, and a founded aspiration to property.

The third maxim has a ground: that aspiration is given by **work**, which is a **human right and a social duty** and part of the art of living. And the book sends its pupils out with a question — to find out what Rodríguez's **"Economía Social" (Social Economy)** is.
