# Short-run equilibrium — MR equals MC

## Do it first

A firm's marginal cost line crosses its price line twice: first while MC is still falling, later while MC rises. At which crossing should the firm stop expanding? Say which and why, in one sentence each.

## Sub lesson 1 — The two conditions

By the marginal approach, equilibrium needs two conditions together: marginal revenue equals marginal cost, and MC must be rising, cutting MR from below. The first crossing of the two curves, where MC is still falling, is rejected: stopping there would leave profitable expansion undone, because just beyond it MR pulls above MC again.

## Sub lesson 2 — For the competitive firm — price equals MC

For a competitive firm marginal revenue is simply the price, since every unit sells at the same price. The equilibrium rule therefore collapses to price equals marginal cost: expand while price covers the cost of one more unit, stop exactly where the rising MC reaches the price.

## Sub lesson 3 — Profit, break-even or loss

Whether the outcome is profit, zero profit or loss is read off against average cost at the equilibrium output. Price above AC gives supernormal profit — the (P − AC) × Q rectangle. Price at the minimum of AC gives break-even: normal profit only. Price below AC gives a loss. The book's worked activity applies the whole apparatus to a cost function with an equilibrium price of 100 birr.
