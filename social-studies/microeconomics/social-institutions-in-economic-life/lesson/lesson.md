# Social institutions in economic life

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.1 §6 Social institutions** — teaches: how social institutions (property, family, state) frame economic behaviour. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Property, family and the state

Economic life does not happen in empty space. Around every act of buying, selling and producing stand **social institutions** — the settled rules and bodies a society lives by. Greece's Grade 12 textbook names the frame plainly: **property**, the **family** and the **state**.

**Property** says who owns what, and therefore who may use a thing, lend it or sell it. Without settled property, no one would plant, build or save, because the fruit could be taken by anyone.

## Sub lesson 2 — The family as an economic institution

The **family** is an economic institution before it is anything else in this lesson: it is where needs are first met, where income is shared, and where the next generation of workers is raised and taught.

Decisions inside a family — who works, who studies, what is bought first — are economic decisions, taken under the family's own customs and duties, not only under market prices.

## Sub lesson 3 — The state as the frame's third pillar

The **state** completes the frame: it writes and enforces the rules — property law, contracts, money — and it taxes, spends and provides services no market supplies on its own.

So the lesson's chain is: institutions first, behaviour inside them second. Change the institutions — who may own land, what the state guarantees — and the same people will produce, trade and save differently.
