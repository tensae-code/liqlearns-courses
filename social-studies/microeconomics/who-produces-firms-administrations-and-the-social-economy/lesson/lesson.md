# Who produces — firms, administrations and the social economy

## Do it first

Think of three things you used this morning — bread, a bus ride, a lesson at school. Before you read on, say: who produced each one? Were they all produced by the same kind of producer?

## Sub lesson 1 — Enterprises — a common structure

France's Seconde SES manual, in its chapter on how wealth is created, asks "Qui produit ?" — who produces? Its first answer is the **enterprise**.

Enterprises look very different — a bakery, a sewing workshop, a car firm — but the manual shows they share **a common structure**: people organised together to produce, arranged in a **hierarchy** (some direct, others carry out), and joined by **the contract** — the agreement that binds each worker to the enterprise and says what each side owes the other. An enterprise produces goods and services **to sell them**: production is its business, and the sale is how it lives.

## Sub lesson 2 — Public administrations — producing for everyone

The second kind of producer is the **public administration**. Administrations produce too — schooling, hospital care, roads, public order — but they do not produce for a buyer. Their production is **non-market**: it is offered to everyone, paid for collectively, and given because people need it, not because each user pays its price at the door.

So the same activity can sit on different sides of this lesson: a lesson taught in a public school is an administration's production; a lesson sold by a private company is an enterprise's production. The producer's kind, not the product, is what this lesson sorts by.

## Sub lesson 3 — The social and solidarity economy (ESS)

The third kind is the **social and solidarity economy** — in French, the *économie sociale et solidaire*, the **ESS**. Its organisations — cooperatives, mutual societies and associations — really produce and really sell, like enterprises, but they are built on a different rule: **people come before profit**. The surplus is shared or put back into the shared purpose, and the members decide together.

France's manual teaches the three kinds as one map of who produces: enterprises, public administrations, and the ESS. Every producer you will ever meet fits one of the three — and asking which one tells you how it is organised, why it produces, and who its production is for.
