Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 7 Year 7 Social Science Pupil's Book): Economics widens from Fiji to the Pacific — resources are owned by tribes and clans as well as individuals, Fiji's story is told through sugar, tourism, fish, gold and bottled water exports, and work is taught through migration and remittances.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 8 Exploring the World Year 8 Social Science): The primary money teaching becomes a practical spend-wisely manual (budgets, impulse buying, marketing, cards and research), and work is reframed as globalisation and aid in the Pacific.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 5 ESD Reader 3 People and the Natural Environment in the Pacific): A regional Pacific reader used in Fiji — farming is taught as a spectrum from subsistence through semi-subsistence to cash crops for export, set against the cost of chasing overseas demand.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 9 Ciencias Sociales 3° Secundaria): economic geography is taught continent-first and then brought home — North America's industrial rise, Latin America's import-substitution strategy, and finally the Dominican Republic's own industrialisation, told through Trujillo's monopoly factories and the wartime switch from rice importer to rice exporter.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 4 Term 1): The economy is met as the country's working sectors — farming first (with Jordan's own crops and its export surplus), then industry split into extractive and manufacturing, then the energy Jordan must import or harvest from its sun and wind.
