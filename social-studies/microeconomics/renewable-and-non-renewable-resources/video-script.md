# Video script — Renewable and non-renewable resources

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Renewable resources can be replaced over time by natural processes — quickly, like sunshine, or very slowly, like soil forming over hundreds of years. The book's list: solar and wind energy, soil, trees, geothermal pressure and groundwater.

Non-renewable resources are a fixed stock found in the ground: natural gas, coal, oil and metals. What is used is gone; the overall supply stays roughly constant no matter how carefully it is spent.

Recycling can stretch non-renewable resources — a metal used twice delays the mine — but it cannot refill the stock. That difference is why the two kinds demand different care, as the next lesson shows.

Grade 12 divides renewable resources into biological ones (forests, fish, biomass) and physical ones (soil fertility, the ozone layer, the earth's waste-processing capacity) — and warns that renewables are potentially exhaustible if harvested past regeneration. Some yield monetised flows (timber, oil); others give unpriced benefits (fresh air, rivers). Together they sit in an ecosystem: the interdependent biological-physical system holding them, whose resilience is how much disturbance it absorbs before its parameters change. Key terms pinned here: sustainable development, ecology, environmental degradation, depletion of natural capital.
