# Goods, products and services

## Do it first

A farmer grows rice. Some of it his family eats; some of it he takes to the market to sell. Is the rice he eats the same kind of thing, in the book's words, as the rice he sells? Say what you think before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Goods and products — the farmer's rice

Things stored in a market for sale are **products**; things produced for one's own use are **goods**. The book's example is a farmer's rice: the rice the farmer grows to eat at home is a good, and the same rice taken to market to sell is a product. The thing is the same — what makes it a good or a product is what it is produced for.

## Sub lesson 2 — Perishable and non-perishable products

Products split by how long they keep. **Perishable products** — fruit and vegetables — must be consumed or specially preserved at once. **Non-perishable products** — clothes, cosmetics and furniture — keep until an expiry date, and need their own preservation.

## Sub lesson 3 — Services — work done for other people

Work done for other people is a **service**. A service is not a thing that is stored or sold on a shelf; it is the work itself, done for someone else — and a service can be paid.
