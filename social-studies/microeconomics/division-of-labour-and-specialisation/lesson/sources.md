**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Costa Rica, Nigeria, Greece, Samoa, Bhutan). Papua New Guinea is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class XI Economics XI): Class XI re-teaches the Class IX foundations at full analytical depth (elasticity calculation, equilibrium shifts, BoP correction) and adds Bhutan-specific system, Five-Year Plan and trade-policy chapters.

Samoa's own way (Year 9 Business Studies Book 1): Economics opens with producers, not definitions — a Samoan is first a fisher, planter or weaver in one of three industries, and money itself is introduced through Samoa's own history (fine mats and tapa, German marks, pounds, the tala of 1967).

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Economics): A full NZ-style supply/demand course rebuilt on Samoan ground — Apia milk and taro-fertiliser markets, VAGST, the tala/yen foreign-exchange market, Samoan public/merit goods, and money-supply definitions for Samoa's banks.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 10 Social Science TG): Grade 10 Economics is the labour and capitalism year — two full units on labour (unions, division of labour, labour law, labour costs), then the market system judged by its costs and benefits, trade through television and the internet, and a closing unit interrogating the capitalist ideology itself. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 12 Economics TG): Grade 12 Economics turns analytical — demand and supply with graphs, elasticity coefficients, the incidence of a sales tax drawn between producer and consumer, market structures illustrated with PNG firms, and a full unit on trade relations and foreign aid, the economy PNG actually lives in. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 10 New Economics SS1): SS1 builds the whole subject from Nigerian daily life — naira prices, markets, banks and sectors — before any formal theory, and gives Nigeria's own economy, agriculture in West Africa and Nigerian banking a full chapter each.

Costa Rica's own way (Estudios Sociales CONED 7): Economics is taught inside history and geography — the Neolithic surplus as the first economy, pre-Columbian markets and tribute, then Costa Rica's own ecological footprint/ecological debt (Estado de la Nación 2016 data) and its world-famous payment-for-environmental-services (PSA/Fonafifo) forest scheme as the national case studies.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.
