# The plantation economy of colonial Saint-Domingue — short answer

Answer in one or two sentences, from the lesson.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What was a habitation, and what did the book's estates produce?
**Answer:** A habitation was a production unit — an estate organised to grow and process one crop for export; the book's estates produced sugar, coffee and indigo.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** How does the book reconstruct the plantation economy?
**Answer:** From its traces on the ground — pupils inventory the vestiges, maps and plans of actual colonial habitations near their school, estate by estate, before the system is generalised.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What three things are recorded for each estate, and what do they show?
**Answer:** The planter, the size of the exploitation and the enslaved workforce — together they read the estate as a production unit: who held it, how big it was, and whose forced labour made it produce.
