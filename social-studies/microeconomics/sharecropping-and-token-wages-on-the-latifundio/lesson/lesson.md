# Sharecropping and token wages on the latifundio

## Sub lesson 1 — Two productions side by side — latifundio and conuco

In the same opening chapter, the republican history book sets two productions side by side: **export production in great latifundios** and **subsistence production in conucos and minifundios**.

Both worked with **backward techniques**. The difference between them is not method but destination — one produces for the world market, the other to feed the family that works it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Aparceros — paying with part of the harvest

The **sharecroppers (aparceros)** worked land that was not theirs and **paid the landowners with part of the harvest**.

No money changes hands in this rent: the crop itself is divided. Sharecropping ties the worker to the owner's land and to the owner's share, season after season — the Grade 5 book's latifundio charge, now seen from inside the republic's first decades.

## Sub lesson 3 — Fichas — wages spendable only at the owner's store

Beside the sharecroppers worked the **peons**, for a **miserable wage** generally paid in **tokens (fichas)**.

The token is the trap: fichas were **accepted only in the landowner's own store**, so the wage came back to the owner in the end. Around both groups stood the **commercial bourgeoisie**, dedicated to imports, exports and granting loans — the chapter's third economic actor.
