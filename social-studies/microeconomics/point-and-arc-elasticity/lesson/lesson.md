# Point and arc elasticity

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.2 §9 Point elasticity and arc elasticity** — teaches: elasticity at a point versus between two points of the curve. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Elasticity at a point

**Point elasticity** measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded at **one point** of the demand curve — at one exact price. Greece's Grade 12 textbook sets it beside its twin: elasticity measured at a point uses the curve's slope at that price together with the price and quantity there.

Point elasticity is the precise local measure: how strongly quantity would respond to a tiny price change right here.

## Sub lesson 2 — Elasticity over an arc

**Arc elasticity** measures responsiveness **between two points** of the curve — over the arc between an old price and a new one. It is the measure used when the price change is not tiny but a real step from one price to another.

Because it spans a stretch of the curve, arc elasticity describes the whole step, not the behaviour at either end alone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing the measure

The two measures answer two different questions. Tiny change at a known price → point elasticity. A stated move from price A to price B → arc elasticity.

On a straight-line demand curve the point elasticity differs from place to place — high near the price axis, low near the quantity axis — which is exactly why the point measure exists: one number cannot describe the whole line.
