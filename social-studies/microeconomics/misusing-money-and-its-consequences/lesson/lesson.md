# Misusing money and its consequences

## Do it first

Before you read on, write what you already think this lesson's title means — misusing money and its consequences — in one sentence of your own. Keep it; you will check it against the lesson at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Misusing money in the home

**Misusing money** is using the family's money badly — not on what the home needs. The book teaches this as a home lesson, in the family, where money is first handled.

## Sub lesson 2 — Poverty, loss and hunger

The first consequences the book names are **poverty**, **loss** and **hunger** — the home that misuses money ends with less, and with not enough to eat.

## Sub lesson 3 — Theft, quarrels and suffering

Misusing money also leads to **theft**, **quarrels** and **suffering** in the home — the harm spreads from money to how people live together.

## Say it to your companion

Explain misusing money and its consequences to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, say in your own words what misusing money and its consequences teaches, part by part.

2. Write the key words from memory — misusing money in the home, poverty, loss and hunger, theft, quarrels and suffering — and write one sentence of your own for each.

3. Check your Do it first sentence against the lesson: fix anything the lesson teaches differently.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
