# Consumer optimum — where the curve touches the line

## Do it first

Lay the budget line over an indifference map in your mind. Three curves are near the line: one below it, one touching it, one beyond it. Which curve's touching point would you choose, and why not the other two?

## Sub lesson 1 — The highest reachable curve

The consumer reaches the highest indifference curve the budget allows where a curve is tangent to — just touches — the budget line. Points on lower curves are reachable but worse: money or satisfaction is left unused. Curves beyond the line are unattainable at this income and these prices. The book walks the consumer point by point down the budget line until the tangency point E is reached.

## Sub lesson 2 — The tangency condition — MRS equals the price ratio

At the tangency point the two slopes are equal: the curve's slope, the MRS — the consumer's own rate of swap — equals the line's slope, the price ratio — the market's rate of swap. When the consumer's rate and the market's rate agree, no trade along the line can improve things. All income is spent at the optimum, as the budget-line lessons assumed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why other points fail

Sit below the tangency on the line and the MRS exceeds the price ratio: the consumer values X more highly than the market does, so trading toward X along the line raises satisfaction. Sit beyond it and the reverse holds. Only at E do the two valuations agree — which is why the consumer settles there and nowhere else.
