# Economics and the other sciences

## Do it first

In Yemen's Grade 11 economics book (Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad), **Unit 1, Lesson 3 — Economics' relation to the other sciences** — maps the sciences economics lives among.

## Sub lesson 1 — Economics with sociology and politics — people and power

Economics studies human activity, so it borrows from **sociology**, the science of society: customs, classes and institutions shape what people need, produce and exchange.

It also works with **politics**, the science of power and the state. Governments tax, spend, coin money and set the laws inside which every market operates — no economy can be understood apart from its politics. In return, economics feeds both: it explains the material life that societies and states must organise.

## Sub lesson 2 — Economics with history — the record of the past

**History** gives economics its memory. Economic life today — its farms, industries, money and laws — is the outcome of a past that history records.

The historian's record lets the economist test ideas against what actually happened: how markets grew, how crises unfolded, how other societies organised production. Economics feeds history back by explaining the material side of past events.

## Sub lesson 3 — Economics with statistics and mathematics — counting and measuring

Economics must count. **Statistics** supplies the method: collecting, ordering and comparing the numbers of economic life — prices, quantities, incomes, populations.

**Mathematics** supplies the language of exact relations: schedules, curves and formulas, like the demand function, state in symbols what words state loosely. The economist borrows both toolkits — and the problems of economics, in return, have pushed both sciences to develop new tools.
