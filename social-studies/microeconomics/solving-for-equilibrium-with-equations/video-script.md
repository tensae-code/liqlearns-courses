# Video script — Solving for equilibrium with equations

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Alongside the table and graph methods, equilibrium can be solved algebraically: write demand and supply as equations and set the quantities equal, Qd = Qs. The price that makes the two quantities the same number is the equilibrium price — everything else is arithmetic.

Wheat demand is Qd = 80 − 3P and supply is Qs = 9P − 40. Setting them equal: 80 − 3P = 9P − 40, so 120 = 12P and P = Birr 10 per kg. Putting P = 10 back into demand: Q = 80 − 3 × 10 = 50 kg. The equilibrium is Birr 10 and 50 kg.

Always check with the other function: supply at P = 10 gives Qs = 9 × 10 − 40 = 50 kg — the same quantity. If the two sides disagree, the algebra has slipped. Table, graph and equations are three views of one meeting point, and all three must name the same price and quantity.

Watch the steps. Buyers: Qd equals 80 minus 3P. Sellers: Qs equals 9P minus 40. Set them equal: 80 minus 3P equals 9P minus 40. Move the terms: 120 equals 12P. So P equals 10 birr. Put P back: Q equals 80 minus 30, which is 50. Check the other side: 9 times 10 minus 40 is also 50. The market balances at Birr 10 and 50 kilos.
