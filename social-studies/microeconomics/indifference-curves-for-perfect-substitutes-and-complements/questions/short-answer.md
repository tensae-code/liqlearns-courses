# Indifference curves for perfect substitutes and complements — short answer

Answer in one or two sentences, from the lesson.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Explain "Perfect substitutes — straight lines" in your own words.
**Answer:** Perfect substitutes have straight-line indifference curves and a constant MRS.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Explain "Perfect complements — L shapes" in your own words.
**Answer:** Perfect complements have L-shaped indifference curves, and extra units of one good alone add nothing.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Explain "Higher curves still mean more" in your own words.
**Answer:** For both perfect substitutes and perfect complements, higher indifference curves mean higher satisfaction.
