# Indifference curves for perfect substitutes and complements — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Which statement best matches "Perfect substitutes — straight lines"?
a) straight-line curves and a constant MRS for goods that replace each other at a fixed rate  b) L-shaped curves; only the corner counts, extra of one good alone adds nothing  c) in both cases, farther-out curves still mean higher satisfaction
**Answer: a) straight-line curves and a constant MRS for goods that replace each other at a fixed rate**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Which statement best matches "Perfect complements — L shapes"?
a) straight-line curves and a constant MRS for goods that replace each other at a fixed rate  b) in both cases, farther-out curves still mean higher satisfaction  c) L-shaped curves; only the corner counts, extra of one good alone adds nothing
**Answer: c) L-shaped curves; only the corner counts, extra of one good alone adds nothing**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Which statement best matches "Higher curves still mean more"?
a) L-shaped curves; only the corner counts, extra of one good alone adds nothing  b) in both cases, farther-out curves still mean higher satisfaction  c) straight-line curves and a constant MRS for goods that replace each other at a fixed rate
**Answer: b) in both cases, farther-out curves still mean higher satisfaction**
