# Non-collusive oligopoly — Cournot and Bertrand

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Competing without agreement

Where firms compete independently, each must guess rivals' reactions before every move, since any action will be answered. That uncertainty makes prices rigid — firms fear the wars a price cut would start — and advertising aggressive, as the safer battlefield. Two named models close the unit, differing in what the rivals choose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cournot — competing on quantities

In the Cournot model, firms compete on quantities of a homogeneous product. Each firm sets its output independently, at the same time as its rivals, taking the others' quantities as given and choosing the output that maximises its own profit against them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bertrand — competing on price

In the Bertrand model, firms compete on price instead, at constant marginal cost. Each undercuts the other to win the market, and the undercutting only stops at the competitive level — the model ends with the firms producing the competitive level of output, as if the market were perfectly competitive, despite there being only a few of them.
