# Positive statements and normative statements

## Do it first

Sort these two aloud: 'Secondary schools in Ethiopia are increasing' and 'Every school should give a free lunch.' Which could you settle by counting? Which only by arguing or voting?

## Sub lesson 1 — Positive — what is

Positive economics describes what is, was or will be, and its statements can be settled by facts. The book's examples: secondary schools in Ethiopia increasing, and an inflation rate figure — both can be counted and checked.

[Picture 24004 — two speech bubbles over the same market — one reporting a fact, one giving an opinion]

## Sub lesson 2 — Normative — what ought to be

Normative economics asks what ought to be. It carries value judgements — the book's examples are school feeding in all schools and government intervening when necessary — and disagreement over it can only be settled by opinion or voting.

## Sub lesson 3 — Telling them apart

The test is the settlement: if counting or measuring could end the argument, the statement is positive; if only values, opinions or a vote can end it, it is normative. Both kinds are used in economics, but they must never be confused.
