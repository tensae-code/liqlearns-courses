# Externalities — private cost, social cost

## Do it first

A factory sells soap cheaply — and the laundry next door pays to rewash everything the smoke touches. Whose cost is missing from the soap's price?

## Sub lesson 1 — Costs and benefits that leak

An externality is a cost or benefit falling on third parties outside the price. The laundry owner never agreed to the factory's smoke, and is never paid for bearing it — the harm travels free of the market.

[Picture 25213 — a factory, its smoke over a laundry line, and two cost curves drawn apart]

## Sub lesson 2 — MSC = MPC + MEC

With a negative externality, marginal social cost exceeds the firm's marginal private cost: MSC = MPC + MEC, where MEC is the marginal external cost. The market, seeing only private cost, over-produces; the gap between the market output and the social optimum is deadweight loss — value destroyed by producing too much.

## Sub lesson 3 — The mirror — positive externalities

Positive externalities run in reverse: education and vaccination benefit far more people than the buyer, so the market under-produces them. Same logic, opposite direction — too little instead of too much.
