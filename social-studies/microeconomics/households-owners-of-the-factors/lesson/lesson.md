# Households — owners of the factors

## Do it first

List what your household gives to the working world — work, a room or land used by others, money lent, a small business run — and what comes back for each. Say the pairs aloud.

## Sub lesson 1 — Households own the factors

Households own the factors of production — land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship. Because they own them, households are the economy's original sellers: every production starts with something a household supplies.

[Picture 24005 — a household handing over work and land-use and receiving four labelled income streams]

## Sub lesson 2 — Selling services, receiving incomes

Households sell their factor services to producers and receive the factor incomes in return: rent for land, wages for labour, interest for capital and profit for entrepreneurship. Four factors, four named payments.

## Sub lesson 3 — Spending, saving, taxing

Households spend most of that income on goods and services, save part of it, and pay taxes from it. The teacher guide adds the household's aim in one line: households aim to maximise income (teacher guide — evidence).
