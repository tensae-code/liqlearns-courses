# The three stages of production

## Do it first

Workers keep joining one fixed workshop: at first each new worker lifts the average; later the average falls but output still grows; at last extra workers get in each other's way and output drops. Three stages — say where each begins.

## Sub lesson 1 — The production table

The worked schedule holds capital fixed at 10 units and adds labour from 0 to 9, with output in quintals rising 0 → 108, levelling, then falling to 80. TP, AP and MP are all read off this one table.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stages I and II

Stage I runs while AP rises to its peak. Stage II runs where AP and MP fall but MP stays positive and TP still rises — the sensible operating range the curves show, the range a rational producer chooses.

## Sub lesson 3 — Stage III — and the rules beneath

Stage III begins where MP turns negative and TP itself falls, because too much variable input crowds the fixed input. Beneath the stages sit the guide's TP–MP rules (teacher guide — evidence): TP rising at an increasing rate means MP rises; TP rising at a diminishing rate means MP declines; TP at its maximum means MP is zero; TP falling means MP is negative.

## Sub lesson 4 — Stage I and Stage III — the two bad ends

Stage I runs while marginal product is rising — the fixed land is still under-used, and average product climbs with it. Stage III runs where marginal product is negative — extra workers reduce total output. No sensible producer operates in Stage III, and none stops early in Stage I while each worker adds more than the last.

[Picture 24355 — the total product curve coloured in three stage bands, with AP and MP curves stacked below]

## Sub lesson 5 — Stage II — the rational stage

Stage II is where marginal product is positive but falling: each worker still adds output, just less than the one before. This is the rational stage — the only stretch where a producer balancing extra output against extra cost can sensibly choose a workforce. The wheat farmer's real decision lives entirely inside Stage II.

## Sub lesson 6 — The product-curve rules

The book states the curve rules outright. TP and MP move together: TP rises while MP is positive, fastest where MP peaks, and falls when MP turns negative. And MP cuts AP at AP's maximum: while MP sits above AP it pulls the average up; once it falls below, it drags the average down — the crossing is exactly the average's peak.
