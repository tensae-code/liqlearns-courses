# Market equilibrium — shortage and surplus

## Do it first

A stall prices shirts at Birr 10 and a crowd pushes to buy more than the stall holds. Then at Birr 30 the shirts sit unsold. Which price is too low, which too high — and what does each do next? Say your answers first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where the schedules meet

Market equilibrium is the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. In the shirts schedule the two quantities meet at Birr 20, with 3 shirts changing hands. At that price there is no pressure to move: buyers find shirts, sellers find buyers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shortage bids the price up

Below equilibrium, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied — a shortage, also called excess demand. Buyers compete for too few goods, bidding against each other, and the price is pushed up toward equilibrium. The gap between the quantities at that wrong price is the measured size of the shortage.

## Sub lesson 3 — Surplus bids the price down

Above equilibrium, quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded — a surplus, also called excess supply. Sellers undercut each other to clear unsold goods, and the price is pushed down toward equilibrium. Shortage and surplus are thus named and measured the same way, in opposite directions.
