# Video script — Economic cost and accounting cost

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Accountants count only purchased inputs — the explicit costs: wages, materials, depreciation, interest and utilities. Their cost is the money that actually left the firm for inputs bought from others.

Economists count opportunity cost, so economic cost = implicit costs + explicit costs. Every resource the firm uses is charged at what it costs the economy — including the owner's own resources that no invoice ever names.

The difference in treatment changes the profit figure a business reports, which is why the two professions' profits differ: the economist's profit is smaller by exactly the implicit costs the accountant left out.
