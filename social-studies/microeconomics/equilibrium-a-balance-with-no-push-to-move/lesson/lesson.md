# Equilibrium — a balance with no push to move

## Do it first

A scale holds perfectly level, neither pan sinking. Touch nothing — will it move on its own? Now say what 'no tendency to change' would mean for a buyer and for a firm.

## Sub lesson 1 — The borrowed picture

Equilibrium is a state of balance with no tendency to change — borrowed from the physical image of opposing forces holding an object still. Nothing inside the situation pushes it to be different.

[Picture 24032 — a balance scale with demand weights on one pan and supply weights on the other, level]

## Sub lesson 2 — Consumers and firms at rest

A consumer in equilibrium has no wish to re-allocate income — the spending pattern already gives the best it can. A firm in equilibrium has no wish to change output. Rest, here, means satisfied with the plan, not asleep.

## Sub lesson 3 — The market mechanism

The tendency of a market to move toward the price where the opposing forces balance is the market mechanism. The book cautions honestly: real markets may never actually rest at equilibrium, but they tend toward it when no outside force acts.
