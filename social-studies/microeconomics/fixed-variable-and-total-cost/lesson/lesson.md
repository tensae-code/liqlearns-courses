# Fixed, variable and total cost

## Do it first

A workshop pays rent whether it makes one chair or none, and buys wood only for the chairs it makes. Which bill is fixed and which varies? Say your answers first — then meet their exact names.

## Sub lesson 1 — Costs are production in birr

Costs are derived from production: the physical input story of the unit's first half is now retold in money. In the short run the split follows the inputs — costs of the fixed inputs cannot change with output, costs of the variable input do. That one distinction builds the whole cost family.

[Picture 24320 — a rent bill that stays fixed beside a flour bill that grows with output]

## Sub lesson 2 — Fixed and variable totals

Total fixed cost (TFC) is the cost of the fixed inputs — rent is the picture — and in the book's birr table it stays at Birr 100 at every output, including zero. Total variable cost (TVC) is the cost of the variable input: zero when nothing is made, climbing as output climbs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Total cost — the sum

Total cost is the sum: TC = TFC + TVC. In the table TC climbs with output — because only TVC climbs — reaching Birr 230 at 7 units, of which 100 is the unchanging fixed part and 130 the variable part. Every per-unit cost in the next lesson is one of these three totals divided, or differenced, per unit.

## Teacher-guide evidence — the table extended to output 8

The teacher guide's key completes the student book's TFC/TVC exercise table and extends it one row beyond the student text, to output 8: at output 8, TVC is 31 and TC is 131 (so the fixed part is TC − TVC = 100, the table's TFC). The same keys restate the product–cost mirror-image rules in their cleanest form — AP/MP rising → AC/MC falling; AP/MP falling → AC/MC rising; AP/MP at its maximum → AC/MC at its minimum — confirm Stage II as the rational firm's stage, and describe technical progress as an upward shift of the production function or a downward movement of the isoquant.
