# Total, average and marginal product

## Do it first

Five workers harvest 100 quintals. A sixth worker joins and the harvest becomes 115. What is the whole output, the output per worker, and what the sixth worker added? Compute all three before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Total product

Total product (TP) is the whole output from the factors employed — the full harvest, the full week's production, counted entire. It is the first reading of any production table: how much, in total, came out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Average product — and productivity

Average product is AP = TP/L: output per worker, and the book's measure of labour productivity — productivity itself being output per unit of input. AP tells you how much each worker carries on average.

## Sub lesson 3 — Marginal product — and the AP–MP rules

Marginal product is MP = ΔTP/ΔL: the extra output from one additional unit of the variable input, other inputs held constant — a change in units, not a percentage. The guide adds the relationship rules (teacher guide — evidence): while MP lies above AP, AP rises; MP intersects AP exactly where AP is at its maximum; while MP lies below AP, AP falls.

## Sub lesson 4 — The three product measures

Total product (TP) is the whole output. Average product, APL = TP ÷ L, is output per worker. Marginal product, MPL = change in TP ÷ change in L, is the extra output from one more worker — and it is read as the slope of the total product curve: steep where MPL is large, flat where it fades.

## Sub lesson 5 — The wheat hectare worked

The law is worked on one hectare of wheat with labour running from 0 to 15 workers. Total product climbs, peaks at 60 quintals with 10–11 workers, then falls as extra workers crowd the fixed land. Each worker's marginal product rises briefly, then shrinks, then — past the peak — turns negative.

## Sub lesson 6 — The law of diminishing marginal productivity

The pattern is the law of diminishing marginal productivity: as more of the variable input is added to fixed inputs, the extra output from each further unit eventually falls. It is diminishing marginal utility's twin on the production side — crowding, in the wheat field, is the cause you can see.
