# Video script — Companies and cooperatives as business forms

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iran's Grade 10 Economics book, in Lesson 2 "Choosing the type of business", teaches how a **joint-stock company** is formed. The company is owned by its **shareholders** — the people who hold its shares. The shareholders are led through a **board**, a **chair** and a **chief executive (CEO)** who runs the company day by day. Ownership and running are separated: many people own, a named few direct, one chief executive manages. The same lesson gives the company form its two great strengths. A shareholder's liability is **limited**: the shareholder risks the share they put in, not everything they own. And because shares can be sold to many people, a company can **raise capital** — and bank finance beside it — far beyond one owner's purse. Against these stand the costs: setting a company up costs more, and the state lays down **rules protecting investors**, which the company must follow. Iran's Grade 8 Social Studies book teaches the **cooperative company (شرکت‌های تعاونی)**: a group pools its investment and works together under the cooperatives law to meet its livelihood and job needs, **sharing profit and loss**. Every member is equal — whatever the size of a member's capital share, **each member has one vote**, unlike non-cooperative companies, where votes follow the size of the holding. The Grade 10 book sets Iran's Commercial Code company types as research beside its own cooperative section.
