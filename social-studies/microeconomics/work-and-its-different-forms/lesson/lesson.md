# Work and its different forms

## Do it first

Look at the people working around you today — at home, on the street, at school. Name three different kinds of work you can see. Why does each one matter? Say it before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why work matters

In the United Arab Emirates' first social-studies book, economics begins with a lesson called "I love to work". Its first teaching is the importance of work: work is how people meet their needs and how a country is served and kept going.

Work is not all the same. It takes **different forms** — different jobs, done by different people, in different places. This lesson names those forms the book's way.

## Sub lesson 2 — A profession — a job built on knowledge and experience

The book gives its own word for one form of work: a **profession** (مهنة). A profession is defined in the book as **a job built on knowledge and experience**.

That definition is the test: a profession is not picked up in a moment — it rests on what the worker knows, learned and practised, and on the experience they have built by doing it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The most important jobs in my country

The book then turns to the learner's own country: **the most important jobs in my country**. Among the jobs it names is the **policeman** — the worker who keeps people safe — and it asks the learner to know the other important jobs of their country alongside it.

Do it the book's way: name the most important jobs in your own country, and for each one say what it gives the country — and whether it is a profession, a job built on knowledge and experience.

## Say it to your companion

Explain work and its different forms to your companion in your own words: why work matters, what a profession is, and which jobs your country counts as most important. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
