# Entrepreneurship and the entrepreneur

## Do it first

Think of someone you know who runs their own small business. Before you read on, write what they do on their own, what they risk, and one need their business meets.

## Sub lesson 1 — Entrepreneurship — independent, initiative, risk-bearing activity

Kazakhstan's Grade 10 books open their entrepreneurship course by defining the thing itself: **entrepreneurship is independent, initiative, risk-bearing activity**. Aganina's 1.1 Introduction to Entrepreneurship teaches it that way — the entrepreneur acts on their own, takes the initiative, and bears the risk. Duisenkhanov's Lesson 1 Introduction to Entrepreneurship opens the same course with the same starting point: what entrepreneurship is, before anything is built on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The individual entrepreneur and the businessman — and needs and goods

Both books name the people and the starting point. The **individual entrepreneur** and the **businessman** are the course's first concepts — the person who carries on the activity. And the activity starts from people: a **need** is what a person lacks and wants met, and a **good** is what meets it. Aganina's 1.1 teaches needs and goods alongside the entrepreneur; Duisenkhanov's Lesson 1 teaches the concepts individual entrepreneur, businessman, need and good together.

## Sub lesson 3 — The subject, aims and tasks of entrepreneurship

Duisenkhanov's Lesson 1 also sets out the **subject, aims and tasks of entrepreneurship** — what the subject studies, what it aims at, and what tasks it sets the learner. That frame is the course's own map: first say what entrepreneurship is and who the entrepreneur is, then study how it works.

## Say it to your companion

Explain entrepreneurship and the entrepreneur to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, say in one sentence what entrepreneurship is, naming all three marks of it.

2. A person works for a wage in someone else's shop and risks none of their own money. Say, from the lesson, why that is not entrepreneurship.

3. Write the key words from memory — entrepreneurship, individual entrepreneur, businessman, need, good — and write one sentence of your own for each.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
