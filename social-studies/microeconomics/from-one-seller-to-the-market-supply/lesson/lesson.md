# From one seller to the market supply

## Do it first

At Birr 4, one seller offers 75 tons of potatoes. There are 120 sellers like him. What does the market offer at Birr 4? Multiply it out and say the total first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Adding sellers at each price

Market supply is built exactly like market demand: horizontal summation. In the shirts table, the quantities of all sellers are added at each price — sideways across the row. The market schedule is those totals, and its curve slopes upward like every seller's own curve, only farther out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Scaling one seller to the market

One seller's supply is Qs = 20p − 5. At p = Birr 4, that seller offers 20 × 4 − 5 = 75. A market of 120 such sellers offers 75 × 120 = 9,000 — the book's 9,000 tons of potatoes at Birr 4. Scaling and adding are the same operation done two ways, and they must agree.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the scaling must check

The agreement between adding and scaling is the lesson's safety check. If the market figure cannot be rebuilt from the one-seller figure, an arithmetic slip has crept in. Market curves feed every equilibrium that follows, so this check — one seller, times the number of sellers, at the same price — is run every time.
