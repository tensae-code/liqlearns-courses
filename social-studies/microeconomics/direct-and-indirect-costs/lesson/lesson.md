# Direct and indirect costs

## Do it first

Think of a small workshop or stall. Before you read on, list everything it pays for in a month — then mark each payment: is it for the thing being made itself, or for keeping the place running?

## Sub lesson 1 — Knowing the production costs of your business

Both Laos Grade 11 books teach the same starting rule: **every business must know its costs**. The Combined book's Ch. 2 §1, "Knowing the production costs of your business", and the Business book's Ch. 2 §6.1 sort those costs into two kinds — **direct costs** and **indirect costs** — with classification exercises and worked tables, so the learner practises the sorting, not only the names. The Business book drills it with the **SMOC sorting exercise (Salary, Material, Other, Capital)**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Direct costs — material costs and direct labour costs

**Direct costs** split into two: **material costs** — what the business pays for the materials that go into what it makes — and **direct labour costs** — what it pays for the labour that makes it. The Business book shows the split in a bilingual diagram: direct costs are the costs of the product itself, in materials and in the hands that make it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Indirect costs — the rest of running the business

**Indirect costs are the rest** — all the other expenses of running the business. Both books list them: **rent, salaries** (the Business book names the owner's and managers' salaries), **loan interest, electricity, telephone and insurance**. They are real costs — the business must pay them to keep running — but they are not the material or the making labour of any one product.

## Say it to your companion

Explain direct and indirect costs to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, sort these into direct and indirect costs: wood for a table, the carpenter's pay, rent, insurance, telephone, loan interest. Say which kind each one is.

2. A tailor says her only costs are cloth and thread. Name two kinds of cost from the lesson she has left out.

3. Write the key words from memory — direct costs, material costs, direct labour costs, indirect costs — and write one sentence of your own for each.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
