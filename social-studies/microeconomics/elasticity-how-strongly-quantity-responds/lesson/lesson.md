# Elasticity — how strongly quantity responds

## Do it first

A rubber band stretches a lot under a small pull; a rope barely moves. If price is the pull and quantity the stretch, which good is the rubber band — salt, or restaurant meals? Say your answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Responsiveness, measured

Elasticity is the measure of responsiveness: how strongly quantity reacts when one of its determinants changes. It is always a ratio of percentage changes — the percentage change in quantity divided by the percentage change in the determinant — so it can be compared across goods, prices and currencies.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why percentages, not units

Percentages strip out the units. A rise of Birr 1 is huge for a matchbox and nothing for a car; a rise of 10% means the same thing to both. That is why elasticity — not the slope of a curve — is the course's measure of how touchy buyers and sellers are.

## Sub lesson 3 — The elasticity block ahead

This opening idea branches into the measures that follow: price elasticity of demand, cross elasticity between related goods, income elasticity, and elasticity of supply. Each keeps the same skeleton — quantity's percentage response to one determinant's percentage change — and only swaps the determinant.
