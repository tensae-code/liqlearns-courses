# The law of demand

## Do it first

Oranges at Birr 5 a kilo: a family buys 5 kilos a week. At Birr 1, they buy 13. Price fell — what did quantity do? And what stayed the same in the story for the rule to hold?

## Sub lesson 1 — The law stated

Ceteris paribus — other things remaining the same — price and quantity demanded move inversely: higher price, lower quantity demanded; lower price, higher quantity demanded. A demand schedule tabulates it: the book's oranges run from 5 kg per week at Birr 5 per kg to 13 kg at Birr 1 per kg.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the curve slopes down

The guide supplies the two reasons the student text asks for but does not answer (teacher guide — evidence): when price falls, the same money buys more — real income has risen; and the good becomes relatively cheaper than its substitutes, so buyers switch toward it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The salt exception that proves the shape

The guide's review answers add the limiting case (teacher guide — evidence): the quantity of salt demanded tends to be unresponsive to changes in its price, because people buy some amount of salt irrespective of price — a necessity whose demand barely moves.
