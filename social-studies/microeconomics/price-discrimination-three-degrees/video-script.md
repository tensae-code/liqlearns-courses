# Video script — Price discrimination — three degrees

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

First-degree discrimination charges every buyer their exact reservation price — the most that buyer would pay. The book's example is a doctor who gauges each patient's ability to pay. It captures the whole consumer surplus for the seller and, unusually, eliminates deadweight loss — but it demands knowing every buyer's maximum, so it is rare in practice.

Second-degree discrimination varies the price by the quantity bought, under one schedule offered to all: bulk discounts, airline seat classes on the same flight. Larger purchases pay a lower price per unit, so buyers sort themselves into the seller's price steps by how much they take.

Third degree, the commonest, segments the market into groups and charges each group a different price: railway concessions for seniors, student discounts at cinemas and museums. Profit maximisation requires MC = MR in each segment separately. Discrimination can be based on income, location, age, quantity, the seller's relationship with the buyer, or how often the buyer visits.
