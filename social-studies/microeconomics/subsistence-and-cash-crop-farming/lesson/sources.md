| Bulgaria part — Economics expansion — "Business in the village" (Бизнесът на село): in villages and small towns the farmyard and the fruit and vegetable garden are an important source of additional income: rural producers grow plants and animals and produce a large part of the food the family needs (meat, milk, eggs, fruit and vegetables), and the yields often exceed the household's needs, giving a surplus of products; but to offer the production on the market it must meet food-safety requirements — the health, hygiene and ecological norms in its production, storage and processing; the organisational form of agricultural business is the farm, which must be registered and meet set requirements to work legally (for a livestock farm: enough space, warmth, light, hygiene, quality food and veterinary control; farms with over 10 animals are set up outside the settlement; milk is bought up only at specialised points and stored at up to 6°C); bio products — healthy foods obtained ecologically, without mineral fertilisers, pesticides, biostimulants and preservatives — are harder to produce but sell dearer, so the biological farm is a good opportunity for small business: the lesson's table sets the mass farm (chemical protection, artificial fertilisers, higher yields, lower prices) against the bio farm (ecological protection, compost, free-range animals, lower yields, higher prices) (filed as people-and-resources/farming-for-sale-vs-farming-for-home; stocked here as the closest existing lesson covering that thing, the original slug recorded in this row) | Bulgaria, *Bulgaria Grade 6 Technology and Entrepreneurship*, "Business in the village" (Бизнесът на село) — printed p. 70 |

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 5 ESD Reader 3 People and the Natural Environment in the Pacific): A regional Pacific reader used in Fiji — farming is taught as a spectrum from subsistence through semi-subsistence to cash crops for export, set against the cost of chasing overseas demand.

Fiji's own way — this lesson: This lesson is built from that book's placement: Case study 1, Taro in Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu / How people farmed sustainably in the past — teaches: subsistence farming grows only what the family and village need, extra crops can be sold or traded, Niue taro is now a cash crop exported to New Zealand, and Tonga practises semi-subsistence farming with food sold at market or exported — printed p. 7. The lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
