# How prices are set

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one Cambodian example of this lesson's subject from your own life — a family business, a market price, a job, or a government programme you have heard named. Say in one sentence what you already think about it. The lesson will test that sentence.

## Sub lesson 1 — A price is determined, not guessed

A product's price does not fall from the sky. It is determined — set by a seller weighing real things, and tested in the market where buyers accept it or walk away. Cambodia's Grade 12 pricing lesson takes the seller's side of the counter and works through the weighing.

## Sub lesson 2 — What a seller weighs

The seller weighs the cost of making or buying the product — a price below cost eats the business. They weigh what buyers can and will pay, and what rival sellers charge for the same or similar goods. The weighed price must cover cost, tempt buyers and stand beside rivals' prices at once.

## Sub lesson 3 — Pricing as a decision

Setting a price is therefore a decision with consequences: too high, and goods sit unsold; too low, and the business sells itself poor. The skilled seller re-weighs when costs, buyers or rivals move — pricing is not done once, but kept under review as the market moves.
