# Video script — Returns to scale

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Returns to scale ask the long-run question: scale ALL inputs by a factor m — what happens to output? If output grows by more than m, returns to scale are increasing. Exactly m: constant. Less than m: decreasing. The comparison is always output's multiple against the inputs' multiple.

The bakery panels make the cases concrete. Double everything and get more than double the bread: increasing returns — size itself helps. Exactly double: constant returns. Less than double: decreasing returns — the bigger operation gets in its own way. Unlike the short-run stages, nothing here is fixed; the whole recipe grows.

Technological change is different again: an upward shift of the whole production function — more output from the SAME inputs. It is sorted by its bias: capital-saving (labour-using), labour-saving (capital-using), or neutral, judged by what happens to the marginal products and the MRTS. A better seed variety is the farm's version: the curve itself rises.
