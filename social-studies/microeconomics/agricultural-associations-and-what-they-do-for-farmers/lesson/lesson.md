# Agricultural associations — and what they do for farmers

## Do it first

A farmer alone must buy seed dear, borrow dear, carry his crop to market himself and find his own buyers. Before you read on, say what would change if all the farmers of a valley acted together through one association. Hold your answer — the lesson lists the association's tasks.

In Yemen's Grade 4 social studies book (Al-Tarbiya al-Ijtima'iya), **Unit 2, Lesson 1 — My homeland's agricultural resources** — after the crops themselves, the book names the **agricultural associations** and their tasks.

## Sub lesson 1 — Inputs and loans at low prices

The association's first tasks serve the season's start: it provides **farm inputs** — what the farmer needs to sow and tend the crop — **and loans, at low prices**.

Cheap inputs and cheap credit through the association free the farmer from buying and borrowing dear on his own, which is the lone farmer's heaviest burden.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marketing the crops — and transport

At harvest, the association **markets the farmers' crops**: it takes on the selling — finding the buyers and the prices — that a lone farmer would struggle to do well.

With marketing goes **transport**: the association arranges the carrying of the crops to where they are sold, so the harvest actually reaches its market.

## Sub lesson 3 — Selling the surplus abroad

What the homeland produces beyond its own need — the **surplus** — the association **sells abroad**.

That last task joins the association to the cash crops' story: the farmers' combined surplus, marketed together, becomes an export, earning for the farmers and for the homeland together.
