# Basic needs and other needs

## Do it first

Your family is at the supermarket. On the shelf in front of you there is food, and there is a new game. Which one must your family have, and which one could wait? Say why, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Basic needs — what we cannot do without

In the United Arab Emirates' first social-studies book, this idea is stated in the book's own "economic fact" box. Some needs are **basic needs** (الحاجات الأساسية): the things a person cannot do without.

The basic needs are **food, water, housing and clothing**. Without food and water we cannot live. Without housing we have nowhere to shelter. Without clothing we are not protected. These needs come first, before everything else.

## Sub lesson 2 — Other needs — things beyond the basics

Other needs are not basic. The book names **games, computers and tourism** as needs that are not basic: a person can live without them, even though they may want them and enjoy them.

That is the difference this lesson teaches: a basic need is one you cannot do without; another need is one you can do without, however much you would like it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sorting needs at the supermarket shelf

The book teaches this sorting through a family at the supermarket. In the family's dialogue at the shelf, each thing is asked about in turn: is this a basic need, or is it another need?

Do the same sorting yourself. Food for the family — basic. Water — basic. A game — not basic. A computer — not basic. A trip away (tourism) — not basic. The basics are bought and secured first; the other needs come after.

## Say it to your companion

Explain basic needs and other needs to your companion in your own words: the four basic needs, the three other needs the book names, and how the family at the supermarket shelf sorted them. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw two shelves from memory — one labelled "basic needs" with food, water, housing and clothing, and one labelled "other needs" with games, computers and tourism — and label every part.

2. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.

3. Write a list of things your own family needs this week, and sort it the book's way: basic needs first, other needs after.
