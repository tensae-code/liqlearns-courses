# The slave economy

## Do it first

In the communal society, everything was shared and there were no rich and no poor. Before you read on, say: what would have to change for one group of people to end up owning another group? Keep your answer — Eritrea's Grade 7 book gives its own.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first class society — owners and slaves

Eritrea's Grade 7 Social Studies teaches economics as a ladder of systems. After the primitive communal society comes the **slave society** — and the book names it plainly: **the first class society**.

As an economic system, its mark is a split that the communal society did not have. On one side stand the **slave-owners**, who own the land, the tools — and the workers themselves. On the other side stand the **slaves**, people who are owned, who work for their owners and keep nothing of what they produce as their own. Production and property, which the communal society held in common, are now divided between classes: those who own, and those who are owned.

## Sub lesson 2 — How people became slaves — debt and war

How did a person become a slave? The book gives two roads.

The first is **debt**: a person who owed what he could not pay could be made a slave for it — the debt was collected in the debtor's own person and labour. The second is **war**: people taken captive in war were made slaves by their captors.

Hold the two roads apart: debt made slaves from inside the society, war captivity brought them in from outside it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Free farmers and craftsmen — and the caste form

Owners and slaves were not the whole society. Alongside them stood **free farmers and craftsmen** — people who were not owned, who worked their own land and their own trades and kept what they made.

And the book names one more form of the system: the **caste form**, where a person's place in the order of work and rank is fixed by birth, not chosen or earned.

So the slave economy, whole: the first class society — owners and slaves at its centre, slaves made by debt and by war, free farmers and craftsmen beside them, and the caste form fixing places by birth.
