# Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — Arab economic thought

## Do it first

A loaf costs more this year than last year. Before you read on, say what you think decides a thing's price — the work in it, how much of it there is, or how much money is chasing it. Hold your answer — two Arab thinkers reasoned about exactly this, centuries ago.

In Yemen's Grade 11 economics book (Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad), **Unit 1, Lesson 1 — Historical development of economic thought** — gives the **Arab-Islamic contribution** its own place, through two thinkers: **Ibn Khaldun** and **Al-Maqrizi**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ibn Khaldun — division of labour and value by labour

**Ibn Khaldun** explained the **division of labour**: a society produces far more when work is split among people, each doing a part, than when each person tries to do everything alone.

He also taught that **value is determined by labour** — the worth of a thing comes from the human work embodied in it. The labour theory stands at the centre of his economics.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ibn Khaldun — prices set by supply and demand

Ibn Khaldun also saw how **prices are set by supply and demand**. When a good is scarce and many want it, its price rises; when it is plentiful, its price falls.

This is the market's own mechanism, described plainly: price is not fixed by decree or custom alone — it answers to how much of a good there is against how much it is wanted.

## Sub lesson 3 — Al-Maqrizi — money quantity and rising prices

**Al-Maqrizi** studied money and prices together. His key insight: the **quantity of money** is connected to **price rises** — when money becomes more plentiful, prices go up.

That link — more money, higher prices — is one of the oldest observations in monetary economics, and Al-Maqrizi made it from watching the markets and currencies of his own time.
