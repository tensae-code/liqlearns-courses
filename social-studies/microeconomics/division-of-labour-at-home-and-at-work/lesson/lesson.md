# Division of labour — at home and at work

## Do it first

Look at the work done in your home today. Before you read on, name who does each task — and say whether one person does everything, or the work is shared out.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sharing tasks at home — the household division of labour

Mongolia's Grade 7 book starts the division of labour where the child lives: **sharing tasks at home**. In 3.3, the household's work — the chores and herding work of the family — is divided among its members, each person taking a part. The division of labour is first met as the family's own way of getting all its work done.

## Sub lesson 2 — Specialisation in production — doing one kind of work well

In 3.4, Division of labour and its significance, the book widens the same idea to production: **specialisation** — instead of everyone doing a little of everything, each worker does **one kind of work well**. Work divided this way is how production is organised, and it is the grown-up form of the task-sharing the child knows from home.

## Sub lesson 3 — Profession and skill shape income — the actor's career

The book then follows what divided work means for pay: **profession and skill shape income**. The case study is a **Mongolian actor's career** — a profession built on a practised skill, whose earnings follow from the work the actor has specialised in. What a person does, and how skilled they are at it, decides what their work can earn.
