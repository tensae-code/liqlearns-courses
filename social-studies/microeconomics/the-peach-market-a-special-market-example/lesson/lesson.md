# The peach market — a special market example

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.5 §6 Example of a special market: the peach market** — teaches: a seasonal harvest market with near-vertical supply, where price falls as the crop arrives. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — A harvest that arrives all at once

Greece's Grade 12 textbook closes its equilibrium chapter with a market of its own countryside: **peaches**. The crop ripens in a short season and arrives at market in a rush — and ripe peaches cannot wait.

Once picked, the quantity on offer is what the trees gave; it cannot be held back for a better week without rotting. The market must clear today's pile at whatever price buyers will pay for it today.

## Sub lesson 2 — Near-vertical supply

With the harvest fixed once picked, the peach supply curve in season is **near-vertical**: quantity barely answers price. A high price cannot call more peaches into existence this week; a low price cannot send picked peaches back to the tree.

Demand therefore sets the price almost alone. In a glut week price collapses; in a scarce week it soars — the demand curve slides along a supply that stands still.

## Sub lesson 3 — The lesson of the special market

The peach market is the textbook's special example because it isolates one force: what an equilibrium looks like when supply cannot adjust in the time available.

The same shape appears wherever quantity is fixed for the moment — fish at the morning quay, seats for tonight's match, rooms in a festival week — and in each, price does all the adjusting that quantity cannot.
