# Production of goods and services

## Do it first

Hold a shirt in one hand — or picture one. Then picture your last lesson at school. Before you read on, say: which of the two was produced as a thing, and which was produced as a service? Hold your answer — the lesson gives both their names.

## Sub lesson 1 — Production — turning raw materials into products and services people need

In Saudi Arabia's Grade 4 social studies book, Unit 4 — Economics — **Lesson 14, The concept of economics** — defines **production**: turning raw materials into products and services people need.

Raw materials on their own do not meet people's needs. Wood is not yet a table; cloth is not yet clothing. Production is the work that changes what is available into what people can use — a product they can hold, or a service they can receive. Both count as production, and the book divides production into two kinds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Material production — cars, phones and clothing

The first kind is **material production**: making material products — things that can be seen and touched.

The book's examples are **cars, phones and clothing**. Each one begins as raw materials and ends as a finished product a person can hold, wear or drive. When you ask "what came out of this production?" the answer is a thing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Immaterial production — services: education, treatment and tourism

The second kind is **immaterial production**: providing **services** — work people need that does not leave a material thing behind.

The book's examples are **school education, hospital treatment and tourism**. A lesson taught, a patient treated, a visitor guided: nothing material is handed over, yet something people need has truly been produced. Services are production just as much as cars and phones are.

That is the whole division: production turns raw materials into products and services people need — material production (cars, phones, clothing) and immaterial production, the services (school education, hospital treatment, tourism).
