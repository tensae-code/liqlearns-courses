# Too much wanted, too much offered — excess demand and excess supply

## Do it first

At a very low price, a queue forms and the stall empties in minutes. At a very high price, goods sit unsold all day. Name what is 'in excess' in each scene before you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Excess demand — too much wanted

Excess demand is the state where consumers want to buy more than producers will supply at that price (teacher guide — evidence). It lives below the equilibrium price, where the low price tempts buyers beyond what sellers bring.

[Picture 24034 — the five-row birr table with the middle row highlighted as equilibrium and the rows above and below shaded as shortage and surplus sides]

## Sub lesson 2 — Excess supply — too much offered

Excess supply is the mirror state: consumers want less than producers offer at that price (teacher guide — evidence). It lives above the equilibrium price, where the high price calls out goods buyers will not take.

## Sub lesson 3 — The worked birr table

The guide's worked market (teacher guide — evidence, arithmetic verified for this lesson): at Birr 1–5 per kg, demand falls 500 → 100 kg while supply rises 100 → 500 kg. Below Birr 3 there is excess demand, above it excess supply, and equilibrium sits at Birr 3 with 300 kg traded.
