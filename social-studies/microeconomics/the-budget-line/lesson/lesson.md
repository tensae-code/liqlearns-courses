# The budget line

## Do it first

A household has 100 birr. Bread costs 4 birr each, sugar 20 birr a kilo. How much bread could they buy if they bought no sugar? How much sugar if no bread? Work both out before reading on.

## Sub lesson 1 — A wish backed by purchasing power

Demand is a wish backed by purchasing power. The budget line shows every combination of two goods a consumer can buy with a fixed money income at given market prices. It is summarised in the budget equation: spending on X plus spending on Y equals income — price of X times quantity of X, plus price of Y times quantity of Y, equals the money available.

## Sub lesson 2 — The worked example — 100 birr, bread and sugar

The book's household has 100 birr for bread at 4 birr and sugar at 20 birr. Spend everything on bread: 100 divided by 4 gives 25 breads — one end of the line. Spend everything on sugar: 100 divided by 20 gives 5 sugars — the other end. These two intercepts anchor the line; every mix between them that spends exactly 100 birr sits on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — On, inside and outside the line

Points on the line spend the whole income. Points inside the line are feasible — affordable, with money left over. Points outside the line are unattainable at this income and these prices, however much the consumer wants them. The consumer of this theory is assumed to spend fully, so the chosen point sits on the line itself.
