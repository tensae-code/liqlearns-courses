**Acceptance count: taught in 18 countries (Ethiopia, France, Dominican Republic, Jordan, Cambodia, Egypt, Yemen, Fiji, Armenia, Nicaragua, Iraq, United States, Pakistan, Bolivia, Nigeria, Greece, Samoa, Bhutan). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class IX Economics IX): Economics is framed throughout by Gross National Happiness (GNH) — material vs spiritual well-being — and every concept is anchored in Bhutanese examples (ngultrum-era money history, hydropower exports to India, Kheng bamboo products, Royal Monetary Authority).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class XI Economics XI): Class XI re-teaches the Class IX foundations at full analytical depth (elasticity calculation, equilibrium shifts, BoP correction) and adds Bhutan-specific system, Five-Year Plan and trade-policy chapters.

Samoa's own way (Year 9 Business Studies Book 1): Economics opens with producers, not definitions — a Samoan is first a fisher, planter or weaver in one of three industries, and money itself is introduced through Samoa's own history (fine mats and tapa, German marks, pounds, the tala of 1967).

Samoa's own way (Year 9 Business Studies Book 2): Economics is introduced only to mark it off from accounting — accounting information helps economic decision-making about the best use of resources.

Samoa's own way (Year 10 Business Studies Book 1): Economics is taught through Samoan life — fa'asamoa borrowing, remittances, fa'alavelave and church spending in family budgets, local producers such as Vailima and copra drying at Lefaga, and a stated absence of consumer-protection law ("the buyer must beware").

Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 10 New Economics SS1): SS1 builds the whole subject from Nigerian daily life — naira prices, markets, banks and sectors — before any formal theory, and gives Nigeria's own economy, agriculture in West Africa and Nigerian banking a full chapter each.

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Compendio Ciencias Sociales; Bolivia Grade 7 Texto de Aprendizaje Ciencias Sociales Secundaria 1): economics is taught almost entirely through Bolivia's own history — the colonial extraction institutions (encomienda, mita, tribute), the silver-to-tin mining cycles and the barons who captured them, and the recurring national answers: nationalisation (1937, 1952, 2006), agrarian reform, stabilisation plans and the plurinational rejection of water privatisation. the student counterpart of the compendium's Year 1 — economics enters through the agricultural revolution's surplus and through the Andean and Mesoamerican worlds the students inherit (ayllu, calpulli, Tiahuanaco's trade).

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 Social Studies Class 4 SNC): Economics enters at Class 4 as one named chapter of Social Studies, taught in Urdu with English concept labels, through Pakistani examples — the State Bank of Pakistan, a National Bank of Pakistan cheque, and Pakistan's own import/export goods.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 11 Economics Class 11): a full Urdu-medium microeconomics course whose every Urdu section heading carries the English term in brackets, so the learner meets both languages' vocabulary at once; theory is built from schedules, tables and hand-drawn curves throughout, and distribution (rent, wages, interest, profit) closes the book as one factor-pricing chapter.


United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Economics): a full Western-style microeconomics course rooted in Iraq — economics itself is traced to Mesopotamia (the first banks at Nasiriyah, loan-interest rules, Hammurabi's laws), worked examples run in dinars on chickens, notebooks, dates and children's toys, and the monopoly and oligopoly examples are Iraq's white-oil distribution and its three mobile-phone companies.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 Social Science student): Economics enters in Grade 7 as one chapter of Social Science, built around the learner's own future profession, household money in dram, and a worked personal budget from Abovyan; scarcity is also presented critically, as partly created by market arrangements.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 1): A full direct-approach course built on the circular flow, with Fiji examples throughout — ginger supply curves, Fiji import tables, quarry and land-rent income, the Attu family budget, Fiji credit facilities, sugar-industry production stages and Reserve Bank of Fiji policy tools.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Dominican Republic's own way (Dominican Republic Grade 2 Ciencias Sociales 2° Primaria): Economics enters through the family and the community — goods are introduced from what a child receives and consumes at home, money from family purchases, and budgeting from a story about neighbours who spend on parties and brand clothes and then cannot buy food.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

The attributed definitions (Smith, Ricardo, Say, Walker, Mill) and the guide's verdict that the scarcity definition suits economics best come from the teacher guide only and are labelled (teacher guide — evidence) in the table above; the student book traces the same growth of definitions without naming those authors.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 Term 1): Trade is taught from Aqaba port and the Silk Road into Jordan's real 2022 export/import charts, and the economy lesson ends in entrepreneurship — Jobs and Apple beside Jordanian start-up projects the class must hunt down itself.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika): a workbook-style first economics course adapted from Cyprus, built around fill-in worksheets and Greek data — the labour-force shift table is Greece's own movement from farms to services.
