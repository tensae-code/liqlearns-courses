# What economics is

## Do it first

Before you read, write down three things you want that you cannot have today, and one thing you used — time, money, land, work — to get something else instead. Keep the list open: the whole lesson is about that gap between wanting and having.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where the word comes from

The word economics comes from the Greek oikonomia — household management: running a home with limited stores so that everyone's needs are met as fully as possible. The whole subject grows out of that household problem, scaled up to a town, a country and the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — How the definition grew

Thinkers first defined economics through wealth — making and holding valuable things. Then the lens widened to welfare — people's wellbeing, not just their riches. Finally it settled on scarcity: how people use limited resources to meet wants that never end. Each wider lens kept the ones before it and saw more.

## Sub lesson 3 — The definition this course uses

Economics is the social science that studies the efficient allocation of scarce resources to meet unlimited human needs as fully as possible. Four ideas carry the whole definition: resources are scarce, they must be allocated — shared out among uses, the allocation should be efficient, and needs are unlimited.
