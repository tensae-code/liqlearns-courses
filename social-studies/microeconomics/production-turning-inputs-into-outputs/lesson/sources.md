**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Syria, France, Bahamas, Jordan, Cambodia). Zimbabwe is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): the Heritage-Based Curriculum infant syllabus carries a named "Career guidance and financial literacy" topic from age 4 — Economics begins as goods vs services told through the cook and the baker, a bread production chain (farmer → miller → baker), and a play shop corner where children practise buying with play money and swapping by barter.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science ECD B Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): ECD B repeats the infant Economics ladder one year up with local goods — maize, clay pots (pfuko), baskets (tswanda) — and a market scene that names vendor (seller), customer (buyer) and money before the money-versus-barter role play.


Bahamas' own way: manufacturing is introduced at Grade 6 in one plain sentence — making things from different raw materials put together to make something else.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies): one six-page lesson, Made in Syria, carries the whole Grade 3 Economics load — bread made the old way against bread made by machine, and every product traced back to its raw material.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 6 Term 2): Economics arrives as one late unit built entirely on Jordanian ground — Jordan's solar and wind projects, water scarcity and water harvesting, and a closing project on Jordan's water problem; production is taught through a biscuit's journey from wheat field to home.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 2 Term 2): A whole early-years Economics unit built on a family shopping trip — a real receipt in Jordanian dinars, Jordanian coins and banknotes to order by value, the wheat-to-bread chain for producer and consumer, a child's own money box for saving, and a closing lesson that tours Jordan's own industries.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 3 Term 2): Trade is taught as a Jordanian family story that starts at the hundred-year-old Hejaz railway, runs from barter and camel peddlers to e-commerce, and lands on the child's own choices — what a family can and cannot do without, where income comes from, and what 2, 3, 5 or 12 dinars in hand can buy.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.
