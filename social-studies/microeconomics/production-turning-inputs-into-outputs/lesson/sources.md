**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Syria, France, Bahamas, Jordan, Cambodia, Bangladesh, Fiji, Vietnam, Armenia, Nicaragua, Bulgaria, Iran, Colombia). Zimbabwe is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Colombia's own way (Colombia Grade 2 Escuela Nueva G2 Cartilla 1): rural Escuela Nueva children learn the economy from their own vereda — farm, livestock and fishing work first, then how town and country exchange products.

Colombia's own way (Colombia Grade 5 Escuela Nueva G5 Cartilla 2): the fullest primary Economics unit in the set — work, goods, sectors, money, prices and the market are all taught from the child's own plaza de mercado, ending in a real market-day fieldwork and market survey.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 Social Science student): Economics enters in Grade 7 as one chapter of Social Science, built around the learner's own future profession, household money in dram, and a worked personal budget from Abovyan; scarcity is also presented critically, as partly created by market arrangements.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 1): A full direct-approach course built on the circular flow, with Fiji examples throughout — ginger supply curves, Fiji import tables, quarry and land-rent income, the Attu family budget, Fiji credit facilities, sugar-industry production stages and Reserve Bank of Fiji policy tools.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): the Heritage-Based Curriculum infant syllabus carries a named "Career guidance and financial literacy" topic from age 4 — Economics begins as goods vs services told through the cook and the baker, a bread production chain (farmer → miller → baker), and a play shop corner where children practise buying with play money and swapping by barter.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science ECD B Teacher's Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): ECD B repeats the infant Economics ladder one year up with local goods — maize, clay pots (pfuko), baskets (tswanda) — and a market scene that names vendor (seller), customer (buyer) and money before the money-versus-barter role play.


Bahamas' own way: manufacturing is introduced at Grade 6 in one plain sentence — making things from different raw materials put together to make something else.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies): one six-page lesson, Made in Syria, carries the whole Grade 3 Economics load — bread made the old way against bread made by machine, and every product traced back to its raw material.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 6 Term 2): Economics arrives as one late unit built entirely on Jordanian ground — Jordan's solar and wind projects, water scarcity and water harvesting, and a closing project on Jordan's water problem; production is taught through a biscuit's journey from wheat field to home.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 2 Term 2): A whole early-years Economics unit built on a family shopping trip — a real receipt in Jordanian dinars, Jordanian coins and banknotes to order by value, the wheat-to-bread chain for producer and consumer, a child's own money box for saving, and a closing lesson that tours Jordan's own industries.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 3 Term 2): Trade is taught as a Jordanian family story that starts at the hundred-year-old Hejaz railway, runs from barter and camel peddlers to e-commerce, and lands on the child's own choices — what a family can and cannot do without, where income comes from, and what 2, 3, 5 or 12 dinars in hand can buy.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Economics): a full academic course built around Cambodia's own transition — the market system is introduced, judged, and then located in Cambodia itself (its market-economy foundations, its national economy, its reform programme), and the book closes with the history of economic thought from the first schools to Keynes.


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 3 Technology and Entrepreneurship): Grade 3 turns the concepts into team projects with real selling — a charity Martenitsa bazaar and a farmers' market where each group must research its product, set its price and present it to clients.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 7 Geography and Economics): the economy of a country is taught as three families of activities — extractive, processing and serving — and Bulgaria's own story is told in four stages, from the craft towns and the first Sliven textile factory of 1834 through nationalisation and the cooperatives to the post-1989 market transition and EU membership in 2007.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 10 Geography and Economics): at Grade 10 the pupil meets the whole national economy at once — its three sectors with the 2021 employment shares (services 74.2%, processing 21.9%, extractive 3.9%), GDP and GNI defined in a box, and the market economy explained through Bulgaria's own post-1989 reform: privatisation, commodity and labour exchanges, and prices set on the market.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 5 Social Studies): industry is taught through Iran's own map — mother industries (iron smelting, petrochemicals, machine-building) located from Iranian mines such as Sarcheshmeh copper and Bafq iron ore.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 7 Social Studies): production and consumption are taught with Iranian anchors throughout — the historic bazaar as market and social centre, compulsory third-party car insurance, and the Silk Road crossing Iran as a bridge between East and West.
