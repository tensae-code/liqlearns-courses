# Collusive oligopoly — cartels and price leadership

## Do it first

Three rival sellers are tired of undercutting each other. What agreement would make all three richer at once — and what would each be tempted to do in secret afterwards?

## Sub lesson 1 — Why rivals cooperate

Oligopolists may instead cooperate to minimise competition and maximise their joint profit. Working together, the group can hold the market at something like the monopoly price — the outcome competition among themselves would destroy. Collusion comes in a formal and an informal form.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cartel — formal collusion

Formal collusion through a cartel fixes the price and the output for all members, sometimes selling everything through a common agency. The cartel is a shared monopolist built out of separate firms — which is why its agreements always carry the temptation for each member to cheat quietly and sell more.

## Sub lesson 3 — Price leadership — informal collusion

Informal collusion most often takes the form of price leadership: one dominant firm initiates price changes and the rest follow its lead. No agreement is signed; the followers simply watch the leader's price board and copy it, and the market moves as one without a word being exchanged.
