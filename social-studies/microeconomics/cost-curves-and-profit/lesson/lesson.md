# Cost curves and profit

## Do it first

A firm sells 100 units at 50 birr each and its average cost is 42 birr. Work out its total revenue, its profit per unit, and its total profit before reading on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The shapes of the cost curves

The average total cost curve is U-shaped: average cost falls as output spreads fixed costs and uses inputs better, reaches a minimum, then rises. The marginal cost curve dips then rises under the law of variable proportions — first each extra unit gets cheaper to make, then dearer, as the fixed input is crowded.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three marginal-average rules

Three rules govern the pair of curves. Average cost falls while marginal cost is below it — cheap extra units pull the average down. Average cost rises while marginal cost is above it. And average cost is at its minimum exactly where marginal cost crosses it. These three rules read every cost diagram in the unit.

## Sub lesson 3 — The two profit formulas

Profit is total revenue minus total cost. The same profit can be computed per unit: price minus average cost, times quantity — the per-unit profit strip times the number of units. If price is below average cost the same formula gives a loss, which is how the next lessons read profit, break-even and loss off one diagram.
