# Human capital

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika), **3.2 Labour** — teaches: human capital as the skills and education embodied in workers. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Skills embodied in workers

Inside its labour section, Greece's Grade 9 workbook names the thing plainly: **human capital** — the **skills, knowledge and education embodied in workers**, which make their labour worth more.

Like other capital, it is a stock built by investment: years of schooling, training and practice are accumulated in the person, and they yield their return over the whole working life.

## Sub lesson 2 — Investing in human capital

Building human capital costs — school fees, training time, earnings given up while studying — and the costs are paid because the return is expected: higher productivity and higher pay over a working life.

Firms invest in it through training; states invest through public education; families invest through the years they support a student. All three are buying the same stock: capability inside people.

## Sub lesson 3 — Human capital and the economy

An economy's human capital shows in what its workers can do: the same machines produce more in skilled hands, and new methods are adopted faster where workers can learn them.

That is why the workbook files human capital inside the labour factor: labour is not just a count of hands — it is hands multiplied by what their owners know.
