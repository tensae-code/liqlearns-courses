# Pigouvian taxes, subsidies and the Coase theorem

## Do it first

The factory could stop its smoke for 100 birr; the laundry loses 150 birr living with it. Name every way this could end well — before the economists are introduced.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pigou's tax

Pigou, in 1920, proposed taxing the polluter by exactly the external cost, so the private cost curve rises to meet the social one. Faced with the true cost, the firm chooses the social output itself. The tax is named a Pigouvian tax.

## Sub lesson 2 — The subsidy mirror

Positive externalities are fixed symmetrically: a subsidy lowers the buyer's effective price toward the social benefit, and production rises toward the optimum. Tax the harmful spillover, subsidise the helpful one — one mirror, two faces.

## Sub lesson 3 — Coase — let them bargain

Coase, in 1960, showed that with clear property rights and low bargaining costs the parties can negotiate to the efficient outcome themselves — whichever of them holds the right. The factory buys the right to smoke, or the laundry pays it to stop; efficiency arrives either way, and only the distribution differs.
