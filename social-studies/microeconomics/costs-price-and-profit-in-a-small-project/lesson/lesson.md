# Costs, price and profit in a small project

## Do it first

Your class is going to bake and sell bread. Before you read on, say: what three money numbers must the class fix before it sells a single loaf — and at the end, how will the class know whether it made a profit or a loss? Hold your answers — the lesson works them the Guatemalan books' way, on the project's own paperwork.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cost, price and profit margin — the project's marketing phase

In Guatemala's Grade 7 entrepreneurship guide, the class runs a real school productive project (the PPE — *proyecto productivo escolar*) end to end: build the oven, quote suppliers, bake, sell.

Its **marketing and selling phase** is defined by its money work: **determining the cost of the product, the price and the profit margin** (*margen de ganancia*), **identifying buyers and selling**. Cost, price and margin are not fixed after the selling — they are fixed as part of the selling phase itself, before and while the product reaches its buyers.

## Sub lesson 2 — The liquidation — income, expenses, balance, profit or loss

The project's closing phase is the **liquidation of costs**: raising the capital, paying wages, liquidating and recording the costs.

It is done on a set format. On one side goes the **income or investment**; on the other side go the **expenses** (*egresos*). The **balance** is found from the two totals, and **profit or loss** (*ganancia o pérdida*) is found by **subtracting total expenses from total income**: a **positive** result is a **profit**, a **negative** result is a **loss**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Costing a product whole — and fixing its selling price

Guatemala's Grade 8 guide costs a product whole, on a juice stall's worked example. **Investment** and **costing** (*costeo*) are defined, and the two kinds of cost are distinguished:

- **Variable costs**, which change with production — raw materials, packaging, labour, sales commissions.
- **Fixed costs**, paid whether the firm produces or not — rent, fixed salaries, depreciation, interest, electricity, promotion.

The **total cost** is the sum of the two. The **cost of one juice** is found by dividing each kind of cost by the **1,000 juices projected** and adding the two unit costs. Then the **selling price** is fixed by looking at **competitors' prices, quality and the profit margin wanted**, and the **net profit margin** (*margen de ganancia neta*) is computed as **projected sales minus total cost** — with capital recovery and depreciation explained for a new business.
