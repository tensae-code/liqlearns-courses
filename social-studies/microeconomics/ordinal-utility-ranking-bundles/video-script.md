# Video script — Ordinal utility — ranking bundles

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The ordinal approach drops the idea that satisfaction can be measured in utils. A consumer facing two bundles can only do one of three things: prefer the first, prefer the second, or be indifferent between them — equally satisfied with either. Total utility is written simply as a function of the quantities consumed, with no numbers attached to the satisfaction itself.

The approach assumes consumers are rational and aim at the most satisfaction; that measurement is ordinal only — rankings, not numbers; that the marginal rate of substitution diminishes; that the consumer can order all bundles completely; that preferences are transitive and consistent; that money income is limited, which forces choice; and non-satiation — more of a good is always preferred to less.

Rankings alone turn out to carry everything the theory needs. From consistent preferences the next lessons build indifference curves — all the bundles a consumer ranks as equal — and from those, the consumer's optimum and the demand curve, without a single util ever being counted.
