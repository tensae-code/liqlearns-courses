# Video script — Short-run equilibrium under monopolistic competition

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The firm's demand curve slopes downward — its variety is distinct enough for that — but it is more elastic than a monopolist's, because close substitutes exist on every side. A price rise costs this firm many more customers than it would cost a monopolist, whose buyers have nowhere else to go. Marginal revenue slopes beneath the demand curve, as it does for any price-setting firm.

Short-run equilibrium again needs the two familiar conditions: MR = MC, with MR cutting MC from below. The machinery of the whole unit is one machinery — only the shape of the demand curve and what entry does later change from structure to structure.

The book's worked diagram shows the firm fixing its price above average cost at the equilibrium output and earning supernormal profit in the short run. That profit is labelled POSR on the diagram — the per-unit gap between price and average cost, multiplied across the output sold.
