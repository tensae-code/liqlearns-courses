# Cooperatives — people working together

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 6 Koinoniki kai Politiki Agogi), **Unit D Ch.4 The economic organisation of municipalities** — teaches: cooperatives as groups of people who work together to advance common interests, e.g. an agricultural cooperative producing and selling its members' produce together. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a cooperative is

Greece's Grade 6 book, inside its one economics chapter, defines the form in a sentence a child can carry: a **cooperative** is a group of people who **work together to advance their common interests**.

The members own the undertaking together and decide together; the cooperative exists for the members' shared need, not for an outside owner's profit.

## Sub lesson 2 — The agricultural cooperative

The book's example is the **agricultural cooperative**: farmers **produce and sell their members' produce together** — pooling the harvest to reach buyers, prices and transport no single small farm could command alone.

Together the members gain the scale of a big seller while remaining the owners of their own farms; the cooperative is their shared instrument, not their master.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cooperatives in the municipality chapter

The Grade 6 chapter meets cooperatives inside the town's economic organisation, and the companion filing's closing activity weighs a weaving and embroidery cooperative's effects — including **more jobs** where the members live.

That placement is the lesson: a cooperative is not an abstraction but a way a community organises work — neighbours pooling what each has too little of alone.
