# Demand — schedules, curves and the function

## Do it first

Amina sells coffee. At Birr 0 she could give away 9 kg; at Birr 45 nobody buys. Guess the quantities at Birr 10, 20 and 30 before you read her schedule.

## Sub lesson 1 — Demand — willingness plus ability

Demand is defined strictly: willingness to buy plus ability to pay. Wanting a good you cannot pay for is not demand; being able to pay for a good you do not want is not demand either. Only the two together count, at a stated price and time.

[Picture 24305 — Amina's coffee stall with a price board, quantities falling as price rises]

## Sub lesson 2 — The demand function and schedule

Demand is written as a function, Qd = a − bP: quantity demanded falls as price rises, at the rate b. Amina's coffee schedule shows it in numbers — quantity demanded falls from 9 kg at Birr 0 all the way to zero at Birr 45. Each row of the schedule is one price and the quantity buyers take at that price.

## Sub lesson 3 — The demand curve and the law

Plotted, the schedule becomes the downward-sloping demand curve. The law of demand states the pattern: as price falls, quantity demanded rises; as price rises, quantity demanded falls — other things equal. The slope comes straight from Unit 1: diminishing marginal utility means buyers take more only at a lower price.
