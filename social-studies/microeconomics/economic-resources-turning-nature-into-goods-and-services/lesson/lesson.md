# Economic resources — turning nature into goods and services

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — What an economic resource is

In Palestine's Grade 5 Social Studies book, the lesson on the components of life on Earth first sorts **natural resources** — water, sun, air and soil. It then takes one more step: an **economic resource** is a **natural resource people convert into a good or service they benefit from**. Nature alone is not yet an economic resource; the converting is what makes it one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Turning nature into goods and services

The book gives the conversions one by one. **Soil** becomes an economic resource **when it is farmed**. **Minerals** become economic resources **when they are extracted, traded or manufactured**. **Sunlight** becomes one when it is used **for power**. In each case a thing found in nature is turned, by people's work, into a **good** people use or a **service** people benefit from.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why economic resources matter — income, jobs and Hebron stone

**Industries rest on economic resources** — and because they do, economic resources **raise income and create jobs**. The book's local case is **Hebron stone and marble**: the stone in the ground is a natural resource; quarried, cut and sold — as a job, as building material and as an export — it is an economic resource at work. The lesson is taught through a family dialogue at that Hebron quarry.
