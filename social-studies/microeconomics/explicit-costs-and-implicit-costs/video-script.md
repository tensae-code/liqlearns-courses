# Video script — Explicit costs and implicit costs

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Explicit costs are actual cash outlays to outsiders who supply inputs — payments for labour, land, capital, raw materials, fuel, transport and power. These are the out-of-pocket costs, the ones an account book records by itself.

Implicit costs are the values of resources the firm owns and uses itself — an owner-manager's salary not taken, the rent the owner's own building could have earned. No cash moves, yet the cost is real.

An implicit cost is valued at what the resource could earn in its best alternative use — the owner's building at the rent another tenant would pay, the owner's time at the wage another job would pay. It is opportunity cost wearing a cost accountant's clothes.
