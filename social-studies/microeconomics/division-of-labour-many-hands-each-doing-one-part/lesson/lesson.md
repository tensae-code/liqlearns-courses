## Sub lesson 1 — Tiwanaku stops being self-sufficient

When Tiwanaku became a city, its economy stopped being self-sufficient and became interdependent: no household, and no community, made everything it needed any more — each part depended on the work of others.

## Sub lesson 2 — Specialised occupations and the surplus

Specialised occupations appeared in manufactures, crafts and trade. The division of labour raised farm productivity so much that a third of production fed the population, and the surplus freed community labour to build Kalasasaya and Puma Punku — without slavery.

## Sub lesson 3 — The agricultural revolution — trades, surplus and first trade

Bolivia's Grade 7 student book teaches the same turning point from the beginning: agriculture and herding made society organise around the division and specialisation of labour, generating the first trades; the surplus produced was commercialised, beginning trade — first on exchange, later on buying and selling.
