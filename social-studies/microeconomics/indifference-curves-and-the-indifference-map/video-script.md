# Video script — Indifference curves and the indifference map

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An indifference schedule — also called an indifference set — is a table of combinations of two goods that give a consumer the same satisfaction. The book's table trades oranges against bananas through four combinations: as bananas increase step by step, oranges decrease, yet the consumer is exactly as satisfied at every row, because each row swaps one good for the other at just the balancing rate.

Plot the schedule's combinations on a graph and join them: the result is an indifference curve, also called an iso-utility curve. Every point on the curve is a different mix of the two goods, but all of them sit at one and the same level of satisfaction — that is what 'iso', equal, means here.

One consumer has not one curve but a whole family of them — an indifference map. Curves farther from the origin carry higher satisfaction, because they hold more of both goods. The consumer would always rather sit on a higher curve; which curve is actually reachable is decided by income and prices, the subject of the budget-line lessons.
