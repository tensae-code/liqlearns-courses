# Video script — What shifts demand

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Beyond its own price, demand is set by the prices of related goods — substitutes that replace it, like tea for coffee, and complements used with it, like sugar with tea or a SIM card with a phone — and by income, tastes, population, season and expectations. Change any of these and buyers want a different quantity at every price.

The vocabulary is sharp. When own price changes, quantity demanded extends or contracts — a movement ALONG the same curve: extension when price falls, contraction when it rises. When any other determinant changes, demand itself increases or decreases — the WHOLE curve shifts, right for an increase, left for a decrease. A cheaper substitute, for example, shifts demand leftward: less is bought at every price.

Tables 2.7 and 2.8 summarise the effects: each demand or supply shift, alone and combined, mapped to its effect on equilibrium price and quantity. Read the grid as a revision map for the whole unit — a rightward demand shift raises both price and quantity; a leftward shift lowers both; combined shifts can leave one of the two uncertain.
