# Responsible consumers and consumer rights

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: buy what is needed and can be afforded. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Buy what is needed and can be afforded

The Grade 7 book's consumer is **responsible, never a consumista** (a consumption addict). A responsible consumer buys **what is needed** and **what can be afforded**.

Need and means are the two tests every purchase must pass. Buying beyond either one is the behaviour the book teaches its pupils to refuse — in themselves first.

## Sub lesson 2 — The abuses — speculation, hoarding, false offers

Consumption has enemies, and the book lists them: **price speculation**, **hoarding of products**, **false offers**, contract and guarantee breaches, and **wrong weights or prices**.

These are not bad luck; they are abuses against consumers' socioeconomic rights. Naming them is the first step — the book's next step is to say where they are fought.

## Sub lesson 3 — Denouncing to the Sundde — defending socioeconomic rights

The responsible consumer does not only shop carefully; they **defend socioeconomic rights** by **denouncing** the abuses.

The address for the denunciation is named: the **Sundde** — the Superintendencia Nacional para la Defensa de los Derechos Socioeconómicos, the national superintendency for the defence of socioeconomic rights. Consumer rights in this book are rights you exercise, not rights you merely have.
