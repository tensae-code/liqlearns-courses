# Video script — Market structures compared

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Market structures are classified by three tests: the degree of competition, the type of product — identical or differentiated — and the numbers of sellers and buyers. Run the tests on any market and its structure is named: the tests, not the product's importance, decide.

Perfect competition stands at one pole: very many sellers of an identical product, none able to affect price. Beyond it lie the imperfect structures, where sellers are fewer, products may differ, and firms gain some power over price — rising until monopoly, a single seller, at the far pole.

Structure decides behaviour. A firm with rivals selling the same thing must take the market price; a firm alone can set it; firms in between compete by brands or watch each other. The next four lessons walk the spectrum one structure at a time, in the book's order.
