Samoa's own way (Year 9 Business Studies Book 1): Economics opens with producers, not definitions — a Samoan is first a fisher, planter or weaver in one of three industries, and money itself is introduced through Samoa's own history (fine mats and tapa, German marks, pounds, the tala of 1967).

Samoa's own way (Year 10 Business Studies Book 1): Economics is taught through Samoan life — fa'asamoa borrowing, remittances, fa'alavelave and church spending in family budgets, local producers such as Vailima and copra drying at Lefaga, and a stated absence of consumer-protection law ("the buyer must beware").

Samoa's own way (Year 11 Business Studies Book 1): The market strand is taught with Samoan schedules — Ioane's barbershop supply curve, Kuki's juice, taro supply before White Sunday, Lesina's sticky-bun demand at the school canteen.

Samoa's own way (Year 11 Social Studies Book 2): A whole super-unit runs on one crop — the coconut carries opportunity cost, commodity prices, export earnings, government support and even OPEC's cartel, because copra is where a Samoan family meets the world market.

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Economics): A full NZ-style supply/demand course rebuilt on Samoan ground — Apia milk and taro-fertiliser markets, VAGST, the tala/yen foreign-exchange market, Samoan public/merit goods, and money-supply definitions for Samoa's banks.

Samoa's own way (Year 13 Economics Guide): Year 13 economics is anchored on Samoa itself — the Central Bank of Samoa (not a generic central bank), one oil supplier as a Samoan monopoly, Samoan government revenue/expenditure tables, and a closing national-economy session built from the Central Bank Governor's 2002 address.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 12 Business Studies TG): the Grade 12 Business Studies guide carries a hidden Economics course inside Strand 1 — its Content Standard 1.1 is expressly about "economic principles" and its content background is drawn from Economics textbooks (Bandara's Economics for Grade 12 and Mickleburg's Economics Principles and Policies in the South Pacific) — and it returns to Economics in pricing (elasticity, supply and demand), in consumer protection enforced by the Independent Consumer and Competition Commission (ICCC), and in how goods are priced and paid for on the internet. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 10 New Economics SS1): SS1 builds the whole subject from Nigerian daily life — naira prices, markets, banks and sectors — before any formal theory, and gives Nigeria's own economy, agriculture in West Africa and Nigerian banking a full chapter each.

United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Social Science student): Grade 10 is a full Economics course inside Social Science — demand and supply are taught with a school-canteen juice schedule priced in dram, the production-possibilities curve with duduks and shvis (Armenian instruments), and personal finance through Armenian institutions: Central-Bank-licensed lenders, the ACRA credit bureau, the Financial System Mediator and the Rate.am / Appa.am comparison sites, including a fake "Gazprom Armenia" investment-scam case.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 8 Exploring the World Year 8 Social Science): The primary money teaching becomes a practical spend-wisely manual (budgets, impulse buying, marketing, cards and research), and work is reframed as globalisation and aid in the Pacific.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 1): A full direct-approach course built on the circular flow, with Fiji examples throughout — ginger supply curves, Fiji import tables, quarry and land-rent income, the Attu family budget, Fiji credit facilities, sugar-industry production stages and Reserve Bank of Fiji policy tools.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Worked-answer errors corrected here, named only in this file: (1) The teacher guide's cloth-and-sugar worked answer mislabels its column ("opportunity cost per kg of sugar in metres of cloth") while computing successive changes in sugar, and its last step breaks the increasing-cost trend it is meant to show — this lesson therefore teaches the method and the verified X–Y solution (5, 15, 20 units of Y per unit of X, recomputed step by step: Y falls 100→90→60→20 as X rises by 2 each step, so each step costs 10, 30, 40 units of Y for 2 units of X) and does NOT copy the guide's cloth-and-sugar figures. (2) Opportunity cost is read as a magnitude (absolute value), as the student book notes.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 10 Ekonomi Tingkatan 4): a full KSSM economics course in Malay with bilingual BM–BI glossaries, ringgit-denominated tables throughout, and Malaysian institutions (Bank Negara, KWSP/EPF, PERKESO) as the living examples.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 6 Technology and Entrepreneurship): Grade 6 prices a product the Bulgarian way — cost price (себестойност) built from direct and indirect costs, a calculation (калкулация) that adds the planned profit, and an "alternative price" for the road not taken.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika): a workbook-style first economics course adapted from Cyprus, built around fill-in worksheets and Greek data — the labour-force shift table is Greece's own movement from farms to services.
