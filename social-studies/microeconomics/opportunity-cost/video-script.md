# Video script — Opportunity cost — the next best thing you gave up

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Choosing more of one thing means less of another. The opportunity cost of a decision is the value of the next best alternative given up — not everything you gave up, only the single best thing you passed over.

Opportunity cost is measured in goods and services, not money, and it rests on substitution — the same resources switched between alternative uses. The book's illustration trades a metre of cloth against the computers the same resources could have made.

From a production-possibility table, read each step: how much of good Y is lost when X rises. The guide's worked X–Y solution (teacher guide — evidence, recomputed and verified for this lesson): as X rises by 2 each step and Y falls 100 → 90 → 60 → 20, the cost per unit of X is 5, then 15, then 20 units of Y — rising at every step.

Worked example, slowly: X rises 0 to 2 and Y falls 100 to 90 — the step costs 10 of Y, so 5 of Y per unit of X. Next step: Y falls 90 to 60 — 30 of Y, 15 per unit of X. Last step: Y falls 60 to 20 — 40 of Y, 20 per unit of X. The cost rises at every step.
