# Average and marginal product

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.3 §5 Average and marginal product** — teaches: output per worker and the extra output of one more worker. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Average product — output per worker

Greece's Grade 12 textbook, having defined total product, divides it. **Average product** is the total product divided by the number of units of the variable input — output **per worker** when labour is the variable input: AP = TP ÷ L.

Average product answers the question 'how much does each worker produce, on average?' — the firm's simplest productivity measure.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marginal product — the extra output of one more worker

**Marginal product** is the **extra** total product from adding **one more unit** of the variable input, everything else held fixed: the output of the last worker hired.

Where average product looks backward over all workers, marginal product looks at the margin — the change the newest input brings. Hiring decisions are made on the margin.

## Sub lesson 3 — How the two move together

As workers are added to fixed premises and machines, marginal product first rises (workers specialise and help each other), then falls as crowding sets in — the subject of the next lesson's law.

Average product follows marginal product: while the marginal worker adds more than the average, the average rises; once the marginal adds less than the average, the average falls. The marginal curve pulls the average curve behind it.
