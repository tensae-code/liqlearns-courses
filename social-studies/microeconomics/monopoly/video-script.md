# Video script — Monopoly

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Monopoly — from Greek roots meaning one seller — is the structure where a single firm is the whole industry and acts as price maker. The home example is Ethiopian Electric Power (ELPA) supplying electricity. Its features: a single seller, no close substitutes, price-making power, the possibility of price discrimination, and restricted entry keeping rivals out.

Monopoly power comes from: no close substitutes; economies of scale that crush small rivals; ownership of key inputs — Ambo mineral water's spring is the book's case; patents, copyrights, licences and trade restrictions granted by law; and unique products or secret ingredients, like those of MOHA soft drinks and Coca-Cola. Each source is a wall keeping the market to one seller.

The monopolist faces a downward-sloping demand, P = a − bQ, so TR = aQ − bQ², AR is the price, and MR falls below AR — to sell one more unit, the price must drop on every unit. In the birr table for outputs 0–8, TR peaks at 30 around 5–6 units, and MR turns negative beyond the peak: past it, selling more actually lowers revenue.

## Grade 11 deepening from the Grade 10 teacher guide extension

The teacher guide adds one source of monopoly to the student book's list: exclusive — special — knowledge of low-cost production or technology. A firm that alone knows how to produce at a cost rivals cannot match holds a monopoly position even without a patent or a law protecting it, because no rival can profitably meet its price.

The guide's review key answers what sets the strength of a firm's monopoly power: the number of sellers — fewer means stronger; the closeness of substitutes — fewer close substitutes means stronger; product differentiation — a more distinct product means stronger; and how possible entry is — the harder entry is, the longer the power lasts.
