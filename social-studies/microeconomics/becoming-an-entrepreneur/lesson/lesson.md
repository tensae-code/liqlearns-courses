# Becoming an entrepreneur

## Do it first

Think of a business you would like to start one day. Before you read on, answer three questions about it: what exactly would you sell, who exactly would buy it, and why would they buy it from you and not from someone else?

## Sub lesson 1 — Judging an idea — product, customer and competitive advantage

The final chapter of Albania's Grade 12 teacher guide is Bëhu sipërmarrës — becoming an entrepreneur. It teaches that an idea is judged before it is funded, on three tests.

**Product:** what exactly is offered, and does it meet a real need well? **Customer:** who exactly will buy it, how many of them are there, and can they pay? **Competitive advantage:** why will they buy from this business rather than the rivals — better quality, lower price, a better place, a service nobody else gives?

An idea that fails one test is not yet a business, however exciting it feels. The guide's entrepreneur improves the idea until it passes, or walks away before the money is spent.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing the business plan — marketing, finance, management

An idea that passes the tests is written down as a **business plan**. The guide names its working parts.

**Marketing:** the product, its price, where it is sold and how customers hear of it — the plan for winning the customers the idea was judged on. **Finance:** the money needed to start, where it comes from, the expected costs and income, and how the business survives its first thin months. **Management:** who does what — the people, their tasks, and how decisions are made and checked.

The plan is not paperwork for its own sake: it is the idea, the money and the people made to agree on paper before they must agree in real life.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ethical decisions in the plan

The guide closes the course by putting **ethical decisions** inside the business plan itself, not after it. The founder decides in advance how the business will treat its workers, its customers, its suppliers and its neighbours — honest weights and claims, fair pay and safety, promises kept.

Writing those decisions into the plan matters because later, under pressure, a business does what was decided when heads were cool.

So the course that began with scarcity ends with a person: judging an idea honestly, planning it fully, and choosing from the start to run it rightly.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the whole lesson to your companion in your own words: judging an idea — product, customer and competitive advantage, writing the business plan — marketing, finance, management, and ethical decisions in the plan. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
