# Oligopoly

## Do it first

Two phone networks serve a whole country. One cuts its price. Can the other ignore it? Say what happens next — that watching-each-other is the heart of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — A few firms, each watching the others

Oligopoly is a market of a few interdependent firms — world crude oil is the book's global case. The firms sell homogeneous or differentiated products, and each firm's demand shifts as rivals react: no firm can plan without guessing the others' response. Interdependence, not just fewness, is the defining trait.

## Sub lesson 2 — Barriers and the Ethiopian cases

Entry is blocked by barriers: the scale of efficient production, the capital needed, patents, and control of raw materials. Ethiopia's cement and sugar factories are the book's home cases — industries where a handful of large plants supply the country and new entrants face exactly those walls.

## Sub lesson 3 — Duopoly — the two-firm case

Duopoly is the two-firm case of oligopoly, and Ethiopia lives it: Ethio Telecom and Safaricom Ethiopia face each other as the country's two telecom networks. With only two, every price move, offer and network promise by one is a direct challenge to the other — interdependence at its purest.
