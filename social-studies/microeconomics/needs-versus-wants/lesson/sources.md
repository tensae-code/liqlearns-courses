**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Rwanda, Jordan, Armenia, Bulgaria, Iraq).**

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Economics): a full Western-style microeconomics course rooted in Iraq — economics itself is traced to Mesopotamia (the first banks at Nasiriyah, loan-interest rules, Hammurabi's laws), worked examples run in dinars on chickens, notebooks, dates and children's toys, and the monopoly and oligopoly examples are Iraq's white-oil distribution and its three mobile-phone companies.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 Social Science student): Economics enters in Grade 7 as one chapter of Social Science, built around the learner's own future profession, household money in dram, and a worked personal budget from Abovyan; scarcity is also presented critically, as partly created by market arrangements.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 4): P4 separates needs from wants for the first time and ties money to the district — Lake Kivu fishing, Kigali markets and tourist crafts — with saving taught as planning before spending and comparing what a bank pays.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 6 Term 2): Economics arrives as one late unit built entirely on Jordanian ground — Jordan's solar and wind projects, water scarcity and water harvesting, and a closing project on Jordan's water problem; production is taught through a biscuit's journey from wheat field to home.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 3 Term 2): Trade is taught as a Jordanian family story that starts at the hundred-year-old Hejaz railway, runs from barter and camel peddlers to e-commerce, and lands on the child's own choices — what a family can and cannot do without, where income comes from, and what 2, 3, 5 or 12 dinars in hand can buy.


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 1 Technology and Entrepreneurship): Economics enters in Grade 1 inside a craft workbook — each concept arrives with a thing to make: a wish tree for needs and wants, a magic wallet for the personal budget, a shopping-list notepad for the supermarket.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 2 Technology and Entrepreneurship): the Grade 1 concepts return one level up — professions are sorted into makers of goods and performers of services who are paid for their labour, and the family budget is a folder the child makes and fills with the family's own income-and-expense plan.

Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 5 Technology and Entrepreneurship): from Grade 5 the subject becomes a real textbook and Economics gets its own section — scarcity is named as the science's central problem, and price is taught through two market diagrams: big demand lifts prices, big supply pushes them down until some firms go bankrupt.
