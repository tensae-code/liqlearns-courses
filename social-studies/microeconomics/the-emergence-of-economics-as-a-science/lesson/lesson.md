# The emergence of economics as a science

## Do it first

People have bought, sold and farmed for thousands of years — but economics as a science is much younger. Before you read on, guess what had to change in the world before a science of economics could be born. Hold your answer.

In Yemen's Grade 11 economics book (Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad), **Unit 1, Lesson 2 — Emergence and definition of economics** — tells when economics became a **science** in its own right.

## Sub lesson 1 — Born with the industrial revolution

Economic ideas are ancient, but economics as a **science** was born with the **industrial revolution**. When factories, wage labour and great markets remade economic life, the old scattered reflections were no longer enough — the new economy needed systematic study.

The birth is dated by a book: the science begins when its first great systematic work appears.

## Sub lesson 2 — Smith's Wealth of Nations — 1776

In **1776**, **Adam Smith** published **The Wealth of Nations**. The book gathered the economy into one system — production, division of labour, markets and prices treated together, with reasons.

That is why 1776 marks the science's birth: from Smith onward, economics is a discipline with its own questions and its own method, not a chapter inside other subjects.

## Sub lesson 3 — Keynes's General Theory — 1936, after the 1929 crisis

The science was **reshaped** after the **crisis of 1929**, when mass unemployment and collapsed demand defied the old explanations.

In **1936**, **Keynes** published the **General Theory**. Economics turned to the whole economy at once — total spending, employment and slumps — and the modern science, with its macroeconomics, dates from that reshaping.
