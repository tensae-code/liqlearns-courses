# Self-employment versus wage employment

## Do it first

Think of two people you know: one who works for herself, and one who is paid a monthly salary by an employer. Before you read on, say one good thing and one hard thing about each person's way of working.

## Sub lesson 1 — Working for yourself versus being a monthly-salaried employee — the side-by-side tables

Laos' Grade 10 Business Combined book puts the choice in a table. In Ch. 4 §§1.2–1.3 it sets **working for yourself** beside **being a monthly-salaried employee**, with the **advantages and disadvantages of each side written side by side**.

The tables weigh the two ways of working on the same points — **independence, income and security**. Independence asks who decides how you work. Income asks what you earn and how it comes. Security asks how safe your living is when work is slow. The book's way is not to praise one side: it lays both sides out, point by point, so the learner chooses with the whole picture in front of them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Profit and income — the self-employed person's return

Ch. 4 §2 names what the self-employed person works for: **profit and income**. The person who works for herself does not receive a salary from an employer — her return is the profit and income her own business brings in. That is the other side of the independence in the tables: the return is hers, and so is the risk that it changes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Saving part of each year's income

The same section closes with a rule for that return: **part of each year's income is to be saved**. Because the self-employed person's income is not a fixed monthly salary, the book teaches the habit in the same breath as the return itself — earn the profit and income, and put part of each year's income aside.

## Say it to your companion

Explain self-employment versus wage employment to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
