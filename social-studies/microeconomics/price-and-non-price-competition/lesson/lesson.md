# Price and non-price competition

## Do it first

Two shops on your street sell the same trainers. Before you read on, write two different ways one shop could win the other's customers — one that changes the price, and one that does not.

## Sub lesson 1 — Price competition — competing on lower price

Kazakhstan's Grade 10 books split competition by the weapon used. **Price competition** is **competing on lower price**: the seller wins customers by charging less. Aganina's 2.5 teaches price competition by that name; Duisenkhanov's Lesson 27 sets learners to describe and judge price competition against its opposite.

## Sub lesson 2 — Non-price competition — competing on quality and difference

**Non-price competition** is **competing on quality and difference**: the seller wins customers not by charging less but by offering something better or different. Both books teach the pair together — price and non-price competition are the two ways rivals fight, and a learner must be able to tell them apart and judge each.

## Sub lesson 3 — Competitive strategies — flank attack, encirclement and blue ocean

Aganina's 2.5 goes on to **competitive strategies**, naming among them **flank attack**, **encirclement** and uncontested **"blue ocean" markets** — markets a firm creates for itself where no rival yet fights it. Duisenkhanov's Lesson 27 adds **types of competitors** to the same study: before choosing a strategy, know who the competitors are and what kind each is.

## Say it to your companion

Explain price and non-price competition to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, sort these into price competition and non-price competition: cutting the price by a tenth; making the product last twice as long; charging less than the shop next door; offering a difference no rival offers.

2. A firm stops fighting its rivals head-on and creates an uncontested market of its own. Say, from the lesson, what that strategy is called.

3. Write the key words from memory — price competition, non-price competition, flank attack, encirclement, blue ocean — and write one sentence of your own for each.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
