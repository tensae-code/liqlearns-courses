# Business ethics and social responsibility

## Do it first

A shopkeeper notices the scales in the shop give a little less than the weight shown. Fixing them costs money; saying nothing earns more. Before you read on, say what the shopkeeper should do — and why.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ethical theories and dilemmas

Chapter 13 of Albania's Grade 12 teacher guide is Etika e biznesit — business ethics. It opens with the theories people use to judge a choice: by its **results** (the choice that does the most good overall), by **duty and rules** (some things must not be done, whatever they earn), and by **character** (what a good person, and a good business, habitually does).

A **dilemma** is a choice where the theories pull apart — where profit points one way and duty another, or two duties clash. The guide teaches pupils to work a dilemma openly: name the choice, name who is affected, test it against each theory, and only then decide.

The point is not a trick answer; it is that a business's hard choices can be reasoned about, not just felt.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethics at work — workplace and organisational ethics

The guide then brings ethics inside the firm. **Workplace ethics** is conduct between people at work: honesty in records and claims, fairness in hiring, promotion and pay, respect and safety for workers, and refusing bribes and favouritism.

**Organisational ethics** is the same demand at the level of the whole business: truthful advertising and honest weights and measures toward customers, fair dealing with suppliers, keeping promises in contracts, and leaders who set the standard they ask of others.

An organisation gets the ethics it practises at the top and rewards day by day — written codes matter only when the daily incentives point the same way.

## Sub lesson 3 — Social responsibility — the business and society

The chapter closes with **social responsibility**: a business uses society's people, roads, law and environment, so it owes society more than its tax bill.

A responsible business limits the harm it causes — pollution, unsafe products, exhausted workers — and contributes where it can: fair jobs, training, support for its community. The guide sets this beside the narrower view that a business's only duty is profit within the law, and has pupils argue where the line should fall.

Either way, the lesson's claim stands: ethics is not decoration on a business. It is part of how the business is run, judged by everyone it touches.
