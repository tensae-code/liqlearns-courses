# Consumerism and responsible consumption

## Do it first

Look at the things you own. Pick one thing you bought even though the one you already had still worked. Before you read on, say in one sentence why you bought it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The consumer's responsibility — correct consumption

Iran's Grade 7 Social Studies book, in Lesson 8 "Consumption", sets the consumer a responsibility beside the consumer's rights: **correct consumption** — consuming in the right way, in the right measure, for real needs. Rights protect the consumer against the seller; responsibility governs the consumer's own hand.

## Sub lesson 2 — Consumerism — buying what is not really needed

Against correct consumption the book sets **consumerism (مصرف‌گرایی, defined in the book)**: **buying what is not really needed**. Its own example is small and exact — buying **a new sharpener while a sound one sits at home**. The old thing works; the purchase adds nothing but the buying itself. That is the pattern consumerism names, at every size of purchase.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why consumerism is pushed — bulk production and advertising

The book then asks why the push is so strong. **Producers today produce in bulk for profit rather than for real needs**, and they **push sales with advertising**. Consumerism is not only a weakness in the buyer; it is a pressure built by the seller's side of the market — which is why the consumer's responsibility is to test every purchase against the real need.
