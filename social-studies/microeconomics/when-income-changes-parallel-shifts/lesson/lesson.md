# When income changes — parallel shifts

## Do it first

The household's income doubles from 100 to 200 birr, and neither price moves. What happens to the 25-bread and 5-sugar intercepts? Guess both new numbers before reading on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Higher income — both intercepts rise

With prices fixed, higher income raises both intercepts in the same proportion: the consumer can buy more of both goods. At 200 birr instead of 100, the bread intercept doubles from 25 to 50 and the sugar intercept doubles from 5 to 10. The line moves outward, parallel to its old self.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lower income — the line shifts inward

Lower income does the mirror image: both intercepts fall in the same proportion and the line shifts inward, parallel to itself. The affordable triangle shrinks from both sides at once, because the shortfall hits spending on both goods together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the shift is parallel

The shift is parallel because the slope does not change: the slope is a price ratio, and no price has moved. Shift versus pivot is the distinction to hold: income changes slide the line; only price changes can rotate it.
