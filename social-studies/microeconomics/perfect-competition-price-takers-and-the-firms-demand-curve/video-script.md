# Video script — Perfect competition — price takers and the firm's demand curve

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Because each competitive firm is insignificant next to the whole market and its product is identical to rivals', no single firm can move the price. The market's demand and supply intersect and set the price; the industry is the price maker. Each firm simply takes that price as given — the firm is a price taker.

At the market price the firm can sell any quantity it produces; above it, nothing — buyers go to identical rivals; below it, there is no reason to charge less. So the firm's demand curve is a horizontal line at the market price. For the firm, price, average revenue, demand and marginal revenue are all that same line: every extra unit sold adds exactly the price to revenue.

Because the price never changes, total revenue grows by the same amount for every unit sold, so the total revenue curve is a straight line through the origin, rising at the constant rate of the price. This straight TR line is what the total approach to equilibrium, two lessons on, compares against the curving total cost line.
