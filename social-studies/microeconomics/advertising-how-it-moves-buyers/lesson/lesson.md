# Advertising — how it moves buyers

## Do it first

Think of one advertisement you saw recently. Before you read on, write what it wanted you to do — and whether you believed it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Advertising is everywhere — its forms

Bulgaria's Grade 3 book teaches the **ways of advertising goods and services**: **leaflet, brochure, internet advertisement, catalogue, television advertisement, billboard**. In people's everyday life, as in nature — bright flowers win pollinators as customers — **advertising attracts the buyers' attention and helps the spread of goods**. The Grade 3 team project makes an advertising souvenir and films an advertising video clip for a healthy cereal snack.

## Sub lesson 2 — How advertising moves buyers — inform, interest, motivate

The Grade 7 book defines it: **advertising is a form of marketing communication whose aim is to inform clients about the offered products, awaken their interest and motivate them to buy**. Its messages reach consumers through special advertising materials (business cards, posters, catalogues), print media (newspapers, magazines, letters) and electronic media (radio, television, internet, telephone). The **firm sign (trademark)** is the graphic image that distinguishes one entrepreneur's goods and services from the others'. Advertising's benefit is that it **informs consumers and spurs competition** between producers, which leads to improving quality and lowering prices. Grade 3 asks the sharp question alongside: how does advertising influence consumers to prefer particular products over the other offers — and **should we always believe advertising?**

## Sub lesson 3 — When advertising misleads — the rules

The Grade 7 book warns that **advertising messages sometimes exaggerate and mislead**, so in many countries strict requirements are set on advertising: it **must not mislead consumers, promise easy profits, imitate established marks and products, claim unverifiable advantages, attract attention in socially unacceptable or offensive ways, or slander and offend competitors**.
