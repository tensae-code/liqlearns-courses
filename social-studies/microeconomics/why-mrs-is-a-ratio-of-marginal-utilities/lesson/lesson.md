# Why MRS is a ratio of marginal utilities

## Do it first

You gain a little satisfaction from one more banana and lose a little by giving up some oranges, yet you end exactly as satisfied as before. Write the balance in words: what must equal what?

## Sub lesson 1 — Moving along the curve — gains cancel losses

Slide a little way along one indifference curve: the consumer gains some X and gives up some Y. The extra X brings extra utility; the lost Y takes utility away. But the consumer ends on the same curve — exactly as satisfied as before — so the utility gained and the utility lost must cancel: the total change in utility is zero.

[Picture 24610 — a see-saw of utility gained and utility lost balancing at zero]

## Sub lesson 2 — Solving the balance

Write the balance with marginal utilities: the gain is the marginal utility of X times the extra units of X; the loss is the marginal utility of Y times the units of Y given up. Setting gain equal to loss and rearranging gives the units of Y surrendered per unit of X — the MRS itself — as a ratio: MRS equals the marginal utility of X divided by the marginal utility of Y.

## Sub lesson 3 — What the ratio explains

The ratio explains why the MRS falls. As the consumer gains X, the marginal utility of X falls; as Y is given up, the marginal utility of Y rises. The ratio of a shrinking number to a growing one must fall — so diminishing MRS and diminishing marginal utility are the same story told in the two approaches' two languages.
