# Consumer equilibrium and the demand curve

## Do it first

You have Birr 22. Bread costs Birr 2, injera costs Birr 4. How would you split the money to get the most satisfaction? Say your split first — the lesson checks it with arithmetic.

## Sub lesson 1 — One good — where MU equals price

For one good, the consumer maximises satisfaction where marginal utility equals price: MUx = Px. Buy one more and its MU is above the price — worth it. Buy past that point and MU falls below the price — not worth it. The budget equation frames what the consumer can afford in the first place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Many goods — equal MU per birr

With many goods, satisfaction is maximised when the marginal utility per birr is equal across all goods, and equal to the marginal utility of money. The worked basket: bread at Birr 2 and injera at Birr 4 on an income of Birr 22 settle at 3 bread and 4 injera, costing 6 + 16 = 22 birr, for 40 utils of total utility. If one good gave more MU per birr, moving a birr to it would raise the total — so equality is the resting point.

## Sub lesson 3 — The demand curve derived

The demand curve itself is derived from this equilibrium. As a good's price falls, the quantity at which MU equals the new, lower price is larger — because MU diminishes, you must consume more before MU falls to the new price. Join those price–quantity points and the downward-sloping demand curve appears: it is the MU curve read as a choosing rule.
