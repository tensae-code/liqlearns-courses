# Video script — Average and marginal cost curves

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Dividing the totals by output gives the per-unit family: AFC = TFC ÷ Q, AVC = TVC ÷ Q, ATC = TC ÷ Q, with ATC = AFC + AVC read as a vertical distance between the curves. Marginal cost, MC = change in TC ÷ change in Q, is the cost of one more unit. The full birr table runs outputs 0–7.

In the table MC falls to 5 at 5 units, then rises. MC cuts both AVC and ATC at their minimum points — while MC is below a curve it pulls the average down; above, it pushes it up. The lowest point of ATC is named optimum capacity: the output where a unit costs least to make.

Long-run LTC, LAC and LMC are flatter U-shapes than their short-run partners: economies of scale pull LAC down on its falling stretch, diseconomies push it up on the rising stretch. The unit closes with mirror-image rules linking product and cost curves: when AP and MP rise, AC and MC fall; when AP and MP peak, AC and MC bottom out.
