# Uses of price elasticity of demand

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.2 §14 Usefulness of elasticity of demand** — teaches: what firms and the state use elasticity estimates for. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — What firms use elasticity for

Greece's Grade 12 textbook ends the elasticity chapters with their **usefulness**. A firm that knows its demand's elasticity knows what a price change does to its revenue — the previous lesson's rule — and can price deliberately instead of by habit.

Elasticity also tells the firm where price competition is dangerous (elastic demand, where a rival's cut steals quantity fast) and where product differences that make demand less elastic are worth building.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the state uses elasticity for

The **state** uses elasticity too. A tax on a good with inelastic demand raises steady revenue because quantity barely falls; a tax on an elastic good shrinks the taxed activity itself.

The same knowledge guides subsidies and price interventions: support changes behaviour strongly where elasticity is high, and mostly transfers money where it is low.

## Sub lesson 3 — Elasticity as a decision tool, not a label

The lesson's closing point: elasticity is not a number to memorise but a tool that firms and the state actually decide with — pricing, taxing, subsidising.

Every use runs through the same mechanism: elasticity measures how strongly quantity will answer, and every price, tax or subsidy decision is a bet on exactly that answer.
