# Feudalism — land, serfdom and the self-sufficient manor

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your own words what you already think this lesson's Venezuelan case shows: land — the base of wealth. Keep your first answer — you will test it against the book's own account as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Land — the base of wealth

Venezuela's Grade 8 history book teaches feudalism as a **mode of production**, and its base is stated first: **land ownership was the base of wealth**.

The chapter's title gives the society's three jobs — work, combat and prayer. Who owned the land stood at the top of all three; who worked it stood at the bottom, and the next part gives their exact bargain.

## Sub lesson 2 — The serf's bargain — a plot for life, labour for the lord

The **lord**, owner of the land, received **part of the harvest** for its use, and **three days of labour a week** from his serfs on his reserve, his walls and his defence.

In return the serfs **held plots for life** for their family's subsistence. The bargain is unequal and exact at once — the book gives its pupils both sides, in days and in harvest shares.

## Sub lesson 3 — The self-sufficient fief and the ruin of town trade

The **fief produced everything its inhabitants needed** — great fiefs became **self-sufficient production centres**.

The other side of self-sufficiency is in the same sentence of the book: while the fiefs supplied themselves, **town merchant economies fell into ruin**. An economy that needs nothing from outside also trades with nobody — feudal self-sufficiency and weak trade are one fact, not two.
