# Perfect competition

## Do it first

A wheat seller at a big market tries charging Birr 5 above every other stall for identical wheat. What happens to her sales by noon? Say your answer first — that failure is this lesson's core.

## Sub lesson 1 — The features

Perfect competition's features: many buyers and sellers, a homogeneous product — salt and wheat are the book's examples — free entry into and exit from the market, perfect information for all, and profit maximisation as the firm's goal. Together they leave no firm any power over price.

[Picture 24323 — a grain market with many stalls selling identical wheat, one small stall facing a price board it cannot change]

## Sub lesson 2 — The firm as price taker

The perfectly competitive firm is a price taker: its demand curve is horizontal at the market price. Charge above it and buyers go elsewhere; the market price is the only price at which it can sell. The firm's whole decision is how much to sell at the given price.

## Sub lesson 3 — Revenue — AR equals MR equals P

Its revenue arithmetic is the simplest in the unit: TR = P × Q, and every extra unit sold adds exactly the price, so AR = MR = P = D. Average revenue, marginal revenue, price and demand are one and the same horizontal line — the benchmark against which monopoly's falling MR will stand out.

## Sub lesson 4 — Two more features — mobility and no government hand (teacher guide)

The teacher guide's answer key adds two features to the perfect-competition list the student book gives: perfect mobility of factors of production — labour and capital move freely to wherever they earn most — and absence of government intervention — no taxes, subsidies or controls bending the market's outcome. Together with the listed features (many buyers and sellers, homogeneous product, free entry and exit, perfect information, profit maximisation) they complete the ideal the model assumes.

## Sub lesson 5 — Revenue under perfect competition — the corrected key (teacher guide, corrected)

Under perfect competition a firm's price, average revenue and marginal revenue are one and the same number, because every unit sells at the market price. The guide's worked activity prices output at Birr 200 per kg for 100 kg: total revenue is 200 × 100 = Birr 20,000, and marginal revenue — the revenue from one more kg — is Birr 200, equal to the price, exactly as AR = MR = P requires.
