# Video script — Slope of the budget line — the price ratio

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The slope of the budget line is the negative of the price ratio — the price of the X-axis good divided by the price of the Y-axis good, with a minus sign because the line falls. It is the market's own exchange rate between the two goods: how much of Y one more unit of X costs, at market prices.

The book verifies the slope of the bread-and-sugar line two ways. From the price ratio: −4 birr over 20 birr equals −0.2. From the intercepts: moving along the line, gaining 25 breads means giving up 5 sugars, and −5 over 25 is again −0.2. Two different roads, one answer — the slope is a property of the prices alone.

With fixed prices the price ratio never changes, so the budget line is straight with a constant slope from end to end. Only a price change can tilt it — which is why the pivots of the next lessons change the slope, while income changes, which leave prices alone, cannot.
