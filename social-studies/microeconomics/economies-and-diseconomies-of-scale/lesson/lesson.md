# Economies and diseconomies of scale

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.3 Cost §8 Economies and diseconomies of scale** — teaches: why unit cost first falls then rises as the firm grows. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Economies of scale — unit cost falls as the firm grows

Greece's Grade 12 textbook pairs the two forces on the long-run curve. **Economies of scale**: as the firm grows, **unit cost falls** — bigger machines are cheaper per unit of output, bulk buying wins discounts, specialists replace jacks-of-all-trades, and fixed costs spread over more units.

This is the falling stretch of the long-run average cost curve: growth itself is the saving.

## Sub lesson 2 — Diseconomies of scale — unit cost rises past a size

Growth does not pay forever. **Diseconomies of scale**: past some size, **unit cost rises** — management layers multiply, decisions slow, communication frays, and coordinating thousands of workers costs more than the scale saves.

This is the rising stretch of the long-run curve. The same growth that first cut costs now adds them, through the sheer difficulty of running something very large.

## Sub lesson 3 — The U of the long run

Together the two forces bend long-run average cost into its wide **U**: falling while economies rule, flat near the best size, rising when diseconomies take over.

The bottom of the U marks the scale the firm's technology rewards — smaller firms survive where diseconomies bite early, giant firms where economies run long, which is why industries differ in their typical firm size.
