# Price elasticity of demand

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Point and arc measures

Price elasticity of demand is measured two ways. Point elasticity measures responsiveness at a single point of the curve. Arc elasticity measures it between two points, averaging over the stretch between them — the book's worked arc comes out at 0.6, inelastic. The negative sign demand always carries is conventionally ignored, so elasticities are compared as sizes.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five cases

The cases run the whole range. Elastic: above 1 — quantity responds more than price. Inelastic: below 1, like the worked 0.2 case — quantity responds less. Unitary: exactly 1. Perfectly inelastic: 0 — quantity never moves, a vertical curve. Perfectly elastic: infinity — quantity moves without limit at one price, a horizontal curve. Each case has its own curve steepness, steep for stiff, flat for stretchy.

## Sub lesson 3 — What decides the size

The determinants of price elasticity: whether the good is a necessity or a luxury; how many substitutes exist; the buyer's income level; the share of income the good takes; urgency — medicines cannot wait; durability; how often it is bought; and the time allowed to adjust. More substitutes, more time and a bigger budget share all make demand stretchier.
