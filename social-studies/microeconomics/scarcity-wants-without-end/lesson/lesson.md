# Scarcity — wants without end, resources with limits

## Do it first

Two scenes: today the shop has no sugar at today's price; and your family always wants more than its income can buy, every year. Which one will still be true in ten years? Say why.

## Sub lesson 1 — What scarcity is

Scarcity is the imbalance between unlimited wants and the finite resources available to meet them. A good is scarce when less is available than people would take at zero price — if it were free, they would want more of it than exists.

[Picture 24008 — a balance tipped between a short stack of goods and an endless queue of wants, with a small shortage-today sign beside it]

## Sub lesson 2 — Scarcity is not shortage

A shortage is different: people cannot get what they want at the prevailing price — a specific, short-term problem. Scarcity is universal and endless; a shortage is one good, one price, one moment.

## Sub lesson 3 — No scarcity, no problem

If resources were abundant — enough of everything for every want — there would be no economic problem at all. Scarcity is therefore the root the whole subject grows from.
