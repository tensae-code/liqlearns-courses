# Video script — Enterprises and market concentration

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An enterprise is a business organised to produce and sell. Enterprises differ in structure — in size, in what they do, and in how they are organised and owned. Cambodia's lesson first maps this variety, because concentration can only be seen against the variety it narrows.

Concentration is the drift of business into fewer, larger hands: big enterprises grow, buy or squeeze out smaller ones, until a few firms hold much of a market. The lesson names the drift plainly, so pupils can spot it in a real market and not only in a book.

When a market concentrates, the few large firms gain power over prices and over the small firms and workers around them, while buyers lose the protection of many competing sellers. Structure is therefore not a dry map: how enterprises are organised decides how a market treats everyone in it — the question the book's closing state-and-private-sector lesson takes up.
