# Video script — Product groups — industries of close substitutes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

'Industry' properly means all the firms making one homogeneous product — one identical good, as under perfect competition. The word only fits where the product does not differ from firm to firm.

Under differentiation there is no industry in that strict sense, only a product group: firms making close substitutes. Each firm, producing its own distinct product, is in a sense its own industry — yet it cannot be studied alone, because its rivals' products press on it from every side.

What binds a product group together is high cross-elasticity of demand: a price change by one firm visibly shifts the demand of the others. Cut your price and your group-mates lose customers to you; raise it and they gain yours. That visible shifting is the test of who belongs in the group.
