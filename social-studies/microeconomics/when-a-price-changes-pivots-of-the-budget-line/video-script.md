# Video script — When a price changes — pivots of the budget line

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A change in one good's price leaves the other intercept untouched and rotates the line around it. If the price of X falls, the X-intercept grows and the line pivots outward on the X-axis; if the price of X rises, the X-intercept shrinks and the line pivots inward. The Y-intercept cannot move, because neither income nor the price of Y has changed.

The argument runs identically for the price of Y: the line pivots around the fixed X-intercept. If both prices rise in the same proportion, both intercepts fall in that proportion — the one case where a price change looks like an income fall, sliding the line inward parallel, because the price ratio, and so the slope, is unchanged.

The unit's closing activity gives a student 60 birr of pocket money to split between tea at 3 birr and coffee at 6 birr: intercepts of 20 teas and 10 coffees. Then the activity varies his income — a parallel shift — and each price in turn — two pivots — so all three movements of the line are practised on one small budget.
