# Video script — From one buyer to the market demand

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Market demand is built by horizontal summation: at each price, add the quantities of every individual buyer. In the wheat table, buyers A, B and C are added quantity-by-quantity at each price — sideways across the row, never down the column. The market schedule is those row totals, price by price.

The same addition works with functions. One buyer demands Q = 50 − 5P. A market of 100 identical buyers demands 100 times that at every price: 5000 − 500P. At P = 4, one buyer takes 30 and the market takes 3,000 — the multiplication checks against the addition.

Plotted, the market demand curve slopes down like every individual curve, only farther out — more quantity at each price. Everything Unit 2 does with equilibrium from here on uses this market curve against the market supply curve, so the adding step is worth proving until it is automatic.
