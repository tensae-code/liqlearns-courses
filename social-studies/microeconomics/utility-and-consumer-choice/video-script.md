# Video script — Utility and consumer choice

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Utility is the satisfaction a consumer gets from consuming a good. It is relative: it varies by person, by place and by time — the same coffee delights one person and leaves another cold. Utility is also different from usefulness: a thing can give utility without being useful, and be useful without giving this consumer any utility.

The cardinal approach measures utility in numbers called utils. It assumes the consumer is rational, utility is measurable in utils, the marginal utility of money stays constant, marginal utility diminishes, and total utility depends on the quantities consumed. These assumptions let the course add, compare and maximise satisfaction with arithmetic.

Each assumption does a job. Rationality means the consumer aims at the most satisfaction their income allows. Constant marginal utility of money lets prices be compared in utils. Diminishing marginal utility is the engine of the next lessons: it is why each extra unit matters less, and why buyers stop.
