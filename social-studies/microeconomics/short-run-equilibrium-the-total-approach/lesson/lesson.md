# Short-run equilibrium — the total approach

## Do it first

A firm's TR and TC both rise with output, but at different rates. At very low output TC is above TR; later TR pulls ahead; later still TC catches up. Where between those regions would you choose to produce?

## Sub lesson 1 — The short run — at least one input fixed

In the short run at least one input is fixed: the firm can change its output, but not the size of its plant. The equilibrium question is therefore narrow and practical: given the cost curves this fixed plant produces, which output makes the most profit — or the least loss?

## Sub lesson 2 — The total approach — the widest gap

By the total revenue–total cost approach, the firm is in equilibrium at the output where the vertical gap of total revenue over total cost is greatest. To the left of that output, expanding adds more revenue than cost; to the right, extra units add more cost than revenue. The widest gap is the largest attainable profit, read straight off the two curves.

## Sub lesson 3 — What the approach leaves open

The total approach finds the best output but says nothing directly about price or per-unit profit, and reading a gap off two curves is imprecise. The marginal approach of the next lesson answers the same question with a sharper rule — and adds the diagram that reads profit, break-even or loss at a glance.
