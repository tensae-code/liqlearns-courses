# Unfair competition and competition law

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold the lesson's one thing in mind and say what you already think it means: Unfair competition is conduct contrary to law, to the principles of good faith and honesty, to business customs and other standards in business — misleading indications, disparaging or disrupting another enterprise — that harms or may harm the lawful rights and interests of other enterprises or consumers (Competition Law 2018). Hold your answer — the lesson takes it in parts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Conduct against law, good faith and honesty

**Unfair competition** is conduct **contrary to law, to the principles of good faith and honesty, to business customs and other standards in business**. Competition itself is lawful rivalry; unfair competition breaks the rules that make rivalry honest.

## Sub lesson 2 — Misleading, disparaging, disrupting

Its forms include **misleading indications**, and **disparaging or disrupting another enterprise**. The book's case: a **mattress maker whose baseless advertising denigrated rivals' products** — winning customers by lying about competitors, not by competing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Harm to enterprises and consumers — the Competition Law 2018

Such conduct **harms or may harm the lawful rights and interests of other enterprises or consumers** — which is why Vietnam's **Competition Law 2018** names and forbids it. The line the lesson draws: compete on price and quality; never on deception.
