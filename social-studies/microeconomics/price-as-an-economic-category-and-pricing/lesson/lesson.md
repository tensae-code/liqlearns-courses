# Price as an economic category — and pricing

## Do it first

Pick one thing sold near your home. Before you read on, write its price — then write everything you think is packed inside that one number.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a price is — and its functions

Kazakhstan's Grade 10 books give price a lesson of its own. Aganina's 2.6 Pricing teaches **the essence and functions of price as an economic category** — **what a price is, and its functions**. Duisenkhanov's Lesson 30 carries the same title and the same starting point: before pricing anything, say what a price is and what work it does.

## Sub lesson 2 — The structure of a price — cost, profit, excise and VAT

A price is built, not guessed. Aganina's 2.6 teaches **the structure of a price built from production cost, profit, excise and VAT, into wholesale and retail prices** — the number on the tag is those parts added together, and the wholesale price and the retail price are different stages of the same build. Duisenkhanov's Lesson 30 teaches the same build from its first rule: **price must cover production costs**, and the **structure of price formation** follows from it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Pricing policy and pricing strategies — and the state's pricing policy

On top of the structure sit choices. Both books teach **pricing policy and pricing strategies** — the rules and plans by which a seller sets prices over time, not once. And Aganina's 2.6 adds the public side: **state pricing policy** — the state too has a policy toward prices, standing behind the prices sellers set.

## Say it to your companion

Explain price as an economic category — and pricing to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the 3 sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, list the parts a price is built from, in the order the book builds them.

2. A seller sets a price below the cost of production. Say, from the lesson, which first rule of pricing that breaks.

3. Write the key words from memory — price, functions of price, production cost, profit, excise, VAT, wholesale price, retail price, pricing policy — and write one sentence of your own for each.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
