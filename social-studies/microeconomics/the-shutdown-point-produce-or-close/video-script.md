# Video script — The shutdown point — produce or close?

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A loss-making firm should not automatically close. Shutting down saves the variable costs — the ones that stop when production stops — but the fixed costs are already committed: the closed firm still loses them in full. So the real question is whether producing loses less than closing would.

If the price covers average variable cost, every unit sold pays for its own variable inputs and contributes something toward the fixed costs, so the loss from producing is smaller than the whole fixed cost lost by closing. The firm keeps producing in the short run despite the loss, hoping the price recovers.

The point where the MC curve crosses the AVC curve — the minimum of AVC — is the shutdown point. Below that price, revenue cannot even cover variable costs: each unit produced adds to the loss, and the firm shuts down at once. Between the shutdown price and the break-even price at minimum AC, the firm produces at a loss but stays open.
