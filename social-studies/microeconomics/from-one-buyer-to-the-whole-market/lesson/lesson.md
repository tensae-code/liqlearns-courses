# From one buyer to the whole market — market demand

## Do it first

At Birr 3, Alem buys 2 kilos and Bekele buys 5. What is the market demand at Birr 3 — and what would be wrong with answering 'Birr 6'? Say it before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Adding at the same price

Market demand is found by horizontally summing what every buyer demands at each price: at one price, add all the quantities. Quantities are added at the same price — prices are never added together.

[Picture 24028 — two household curves side by side adding, quantity by quantity, into one wider market curve]

## Sub lesson 2 — The market curve

The market demand curve is likewise the horizontal summation of all individual demand curves: at every price, the market curve sits as far right as all the individual quantities piled together. It is wider, and slopes down like each of its parts.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why 'horizontal' matters

Horizontal summation is the direction of the addition: sideways along the quantity axis. Adding vertically — prices on top of prices — would describe one buyer paying twice, which no market ever does.
