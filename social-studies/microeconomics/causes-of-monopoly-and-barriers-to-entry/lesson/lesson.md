# Causes of monopoly and barriers to entry

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.6 §8 Causes of monopoly creation** — teaches: legal, resource and scale barriers that create monopolies. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Monopoly is made by barriers

A monopoly lasts only if other firms **cannot enter**. Greece's Grade 12 textbook therefore teaches monopoly through its **causes** — the barriers that keep rivals out: legal, resource and scale barriers.

Remove the barrier and the monopoly leaks: entry begins, and the single seller becomes one seller among several. Keep the barrier and the single seller stays.

## Sub lesson 2 — Legal and resource barriers

**Legal barriers**: the state grants an exclusive right — a licence, a franchise, a patent — or itself reserves the activity, so entry is forbidden by law however profitable it looks.

**Resource barriers**: one firm owns or controls the essential resource — the only spring, the key mineral deposit, the indispensable site — so rivals cannot produce even if the law allows them to try.

## Sub lesson 3 — Scale barriers

**Scale barriers**: in some industries the efficient size is so large relative to the market that one firm supplying the whole market produces more cheaply than two splitting it — the natural monopoly case, like a water network.

A would-be entrant must enter huge or enter uncompetitive; knowing this, most never enter at all, and the single supplier remains.
