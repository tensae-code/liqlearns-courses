**Acceptance count: taught in 17 countries (Ethiopia, France, Mongolia, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Bangladesh, Yemen, Fiji, Vietnam, Armenia, Nicaragua, Iran, Iraq, United States, Pakistan, Bolivia, Nigeria). Albania is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 10 New Economics SS1): SS1 builds the whole subject from Nigerian daily life — naira prices, markets, banks and sectors — before any formal theory, and gives Nigeria's own economy, agriculture in West Africa and Nigerian banking a full chapter each.

Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 11 New Economics SS2): SS2 pairs every theory block with its Nigerian application in the same theme — wage policy and trade unions, agricultural policy and marketing, revenue allocation in the federation, the Nigerian Stock Exchange and inflation in Nigeria.

Bolivia's own way (Bolivia Grade 10 Texto de Aprendizaje Ciencias Sociales Secundaria 4): alongside the national story (Sucre's tax design, the silver cycle, the rubber boom), this year carries the wave's most textbook-like basic economics in the Bolivia set — supply and demand, the production–distribution–consumption cycle and the three sectors, all inside a unit on natural resources and Mother Earth.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 11 Economics Class 11): a full Urdu-medium microeconomics course whose every Urdu section heading carries the English term in brackets, so the learner meets both languages' vocabulary at once; theory is built from schedules, tables and hand-drawn curves throughout, and distribution (rent, wages, interest, profit) closes the book as one factor-pricing chapter.


United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales 10): Economics is taught as a national project — definitions are given in both the objective (Engels) and subjective (Robbins) currents, the five basic problems are posed as Nicaragua's own choices (stay a dependent raw-material supplier or seek economic independence), and every institution is Nicaraguan: ENABAS fair-price shops, the MEFCCA family-economy ministry, Banco Produzcamos, the Central Bank's pre-announced 5% crawl, ALBA and PETROCARIBE.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Social Science student): Grade 10 is a full Economics course inside Social Science — demand and supply are taught with a school-canteen juice schedule priced in dram, the production-possibilities curve with duduks and shvis (Armenian instruments), and personal finance through Armenian institutions: Central-Bank-licensed lenders, the ACRA credit bureau, the Financial System Mediator and the Rate.am / Appa.am comparison sites, including a fake "Gazprom Armenia" investment-scam case.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Economic & Legal Education): a full school Economics course built on Vietnamese cases and current law — the Si Ma Cai periodic market for exchange, pork-price swings for market functions, the 2025 State Budget Law's own revenue/expenditure tables, corporate income-tax tiers and the progressive personal income-tax schedule, and a student's 80/13/7 spending rule for personal finance.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 11 Economic & Legal Education): the macro topics are taught from Vietnam's own statistics and history — the CPI chart 2016–2021, the 775% inflation of 1986, COVID-era unemployment of 3.2%, the Q3 2022 labour-market bulletin — and the State's controlling role is quoted from the Law on the State Bank and the Labour Code.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 11 Economics Year 11 Part 1): A full direct-approach course built on the circular flow, with Fiji examples throughout — ginger supply curves, Fiji import tables, quarry and land-rent income, the Attu family budget, Fiji credit facilities, sugar-industry production stages and Reserve Bank of Fiji policy tools.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

France's own way (SES 2de, Manuels Libres): SES teaches economics, sociology and political science as one subject from Seconde; the economics is built from data and cases (INSEE, the France Relance plan read as a Keynesian model, housing and oil prices, the gender pay gap in spreadsheets) with a synthesis and exercise for nearly every notion.

Pairing note carried from the placements: the student textbook is the finished 2023 edition, while the teacher guide is ©2022 with an unfinished placeholder printing block — edition-mismatched companions; the guide's answer keys are partly incomplete/misaligned with this book's review questions, and no answer-key figure is taught here unchecked.

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 8 Social Studies 8): the economy is taught from the home district outward — pupils map the enterprises around their own school, work a flour price schedule, then write a real small-business plan.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 10 Aganina): Economics is taught inside an entrepreneurship course — market theory arrives as the theory an entrepreneur needs, with tenge-denominated supply examples (a potato supply curve at 50-70 tenge), world oil prices deciding whether an Almaty-region furniture workshop profits, and Kazakhstan's own post-Soviet transition as the opening history.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kazakhstan Grade 10 Duisenkhanov Part 1): the same national curriculum taught in a three-lesson rhythm — one theory lesson, one game, one Kazakhstani or world business case (Rocket Study, Yuframe, Shymbulak, Elon Musk) — so every Economics concept is immediately played and then applied.

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 10 Ekonomi Tingkatan 4): a full KSSM economics course in Malay with bilingual BM–BI glossaries, ringgit-denominated tables throughout, and Malaysian institutions (Bank Negara, KWSP/EPF, PERKESO) as the living examples.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.
