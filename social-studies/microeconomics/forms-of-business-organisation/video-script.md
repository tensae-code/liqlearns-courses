# Video script — Forms of business organisation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The simplest form is one person's own business: a single owner puts in the money, makes the decisions and keeps the profit. It is quick to start and the owner answers to nobody. But the same owner also carries every loss alone, and the business can raise only as much money as that one person has or can borrow.

Other forms share the capital: two or more people put money in together and own the business together. Sharing the capital means a bigger start than one person could manage, and the owners share the work and the decisions as their agreement sets out. It also means the profit is shared, and the owners must settle disagreements between themselves.

Choosing a form is choosing who owns, who decides, who shares the profit and who carries the loss. A family in Cambodia weighs exactly these questions when it organises its business: stay one person's own, or bring others in with shared capital. The form is not a label — it changes the owners' rights and risks every day the business runs.
