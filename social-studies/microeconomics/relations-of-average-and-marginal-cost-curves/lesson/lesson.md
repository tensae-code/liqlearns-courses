# Relations of the average and marginal cost curves

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.3 Cost §5 Relations of the average and marginal cost curves** — teaches: marginal cost crossing average cost at its minimum. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Marginal cost crosses average cost at its minimum

Greece's Grade 12 textbook states the relation as a rule of the curves: the **marginal cost curve crosses the average cost curve at the average curve's minimum point** — and the same holds for average variable cost.

Below that crossing, marginal cost lies under average cost; above it, marginal cost lies over it. The crossing is not a coincidence of drawing — the next sub lessons show why it must happen.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the marginal pulls the average

Think of the average as a running mean and the marginal as the newest entry. If the newest unit costs **less than the average**, adding it drags the average **down**. If it costs **more**, it pulls the average **up**.

So average cost falls while marginal cost is below it and rises while marginal cost is above it — and can only stop falling exactly where the two meet: its minimum.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading the crossing

The crossing point is the firm's **efficient scale** marker on the cost diagram: the output at which unit cost is lowest, where the falling pull of spreading costs and the rising pull of diminishing returns exactly balance.

Every cost diagram in the course is read through this relation — find MC, find where it cuts AC, and you have found minimum average cost without computing a single figure.
