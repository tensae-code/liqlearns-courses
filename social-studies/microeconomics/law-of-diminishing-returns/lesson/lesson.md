# The law of diminishing returns

## Do it first

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.3 §6 The law of diminishing or non-proportional returns** — teaches: marginal product eventually falls as more of the variable input is added. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Adding more of one input to fixed others

The **law of diminishing (non-proportional) returns**, in Greece's Grade 12 textbook: as more and more units of a **variable input** are added to **fixed inputs**, the extra output from each added unit **eventually falls**.

The word 'eventually' carries the law. At first the extra workers may add more than the ones before — the law does not deny the early rise; it states that the rise cannot continue forever against a fixed factor.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why returns diminish

The cause is proportion. Workers multiply while the machines, rooms and land do not: each added worker has less of the fixed input to work with, waits more, crowds more, and so adds less.

Nothing is wrong with the workers — the **mix** of inputs has become unbalanced. The fixed factor is spread ever thinner over the variable one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where the law bites

The law is a short-run law: it rules wherever at least one input is fixed. It explains why output cannot be raised without limit by piling one input onto a fixed base — in the farm, the workshop and the kitchen alike.

Escaping it requires changing the fixed input itself — more land, bigger premises, better machines — which is a long-run decision, the horizon where all inputs can vary.
