Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 10 Social Science TG): Grade 10 Economics is the labour and capitalism year — two full units on labour (unions, division of labour, labour law, labour costs), then the market system judged by its costs and benefits, trade through television and the internet, and a closing unit interrogating the capitalist ideology itself. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 Social Studies Class 4 SNC): Economics enters at Class 4 as one named chapter of Social Studies, taught in Urdu with English concept labels, through Pakistani examples — the State Bank of Pakistan, a National Bank of Pakistan cheque, and Pakistan's own import/export goods.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 Social Studies Class 5 SNC): The Class 5 chapter deepens the Class 4 set with Pakistan's own institutions as the teaching examples — PIA, Pakistan Railways, Pakistan Television, Pakistan Post and WAPDA for public services, the State Bank of Pakistan (established 1948), and Dr Akhtar Hameed Khan's microfinance work for social entrepreneurship.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 11 Economics Class 11): a full Urdu-medium microeconomics course whose every Urdu section heading carries the English term in brackets, so the learner meets both languages' vocabulary at once; theory is built from schedules, tables and hand-drawn curves throughout, and distribution (rent, wages, interest, profit) closes the book as one factor-pricing chapter.


Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 Social Science student): Economics enters in Grade 7 as one chapter of Social Science, built around the learner's own future profession, household money in dram, and a worked personal budget from Abovyan; scarcity is also presented critically, as partly created by market arrangements.
Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Social Science student): Grade 10 is a full Economics course inside Social Science — demand and supply are taught with a school-canteen juice schedule priced in dram, the production-possibilities curve with duduks and shvis (Armenian instruments), and personal finance through Armenian institutions: Central-Bank-licensed lenders, the ACRA credit bureau, the Financial System Mediator and the Rate.am / Appa.am comparison sites, including a fake "Gazprom Armenia" investment-scam case.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 5 Year 5 Social Studies): A full primary money curriculum in one unit — work and wages first, then the Fiji dollar against other currencies, then a family budget, a personal saving plan and what banks do.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Grade 6 Year 6 Social Science): The Year 5 money unit is deepened with employer-employee relations, a worked fortnightly family budget, and income tax and FNPF deductions explained through what the government pays for.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11 Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad): a compact principles course that starts from the history of economic thought — including Hammurabi, Confucius, Kautilya, Ibn Khaldun and Al-Maqrizi — and works every schedule and elasticity example in Yemeni rials on one good, oranges, from demand through equilibrium.

Albania's own way (Albania Grade 12 Ekonomia 12 Teacher Guide): a full Grade 12 course that runs micro to macro and then turns practical — business ethics, success skills and personal finance close the course; Albanian anchors throughout (Bank of Albania videos for the personal-budget lesson, the Albanian government budget in the government chapter, the types of money in circulation in Albania). This Albania material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies): the choice is the child's own — purchases split into necessary ones that cannot be postponed and ones that can, closing on two needed things and money for only one, after the money box is opened on 900 saved Syrian pounds.

Mongolia's own way (Mongolia Grade 7 Social Studies 7): Economics enters through the child's own life — needs, the family budget in tögrög, household chores and herding work — before any market abstraction; property is taught through the school itself.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).


Bulgaria's own way (Bulgaria Grade 5 Technology and Entrepreneurship): from Grade 5 the subject becomes a real textbook and Economics gets its own section — scarcity is named as the science's central problem, and price is taught through two market diagrams: big demand lifts prices, big supply pushes them down until some firms go bankrupt.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Economics): the whole book is taught through one running family story (the Mohammadi family and their bio-input workshop); Iran-specific content is woven in throughout — export orders from Oman and Iraq blocked by US banking sanctions, the "resistance economy" (اقتصاد مقاومتی) as the answer to sanctions, and warnings against parking savings in gold coins, dollars, cars and housing instead of production.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika): a workbook-style first economics course adapted from Cyprus, built around fill-in worksheets and Greek data — the labour-force shift table is Greece's own movement from farms to services.
