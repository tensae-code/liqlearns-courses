# Scarcity forces choice

## Do it first

Your household has money for one of: school books, a phone repair, or a celebration meal. Which do you choose, and what goes unchosen? Say the chain out loud: limited money, limited buying, a choice, a cost.

## Sub lesson 1 — The chain begins

Because resources are scarce, output is limited: only so much can be produced. And because output is limited, not every want can be met. The book's chain runs scarcity → limited resources → limited output → choice → cost.

[Picture 24009 — the chain as five linked boxes from scarce to cost]

## Sub lesson 2 — Everyone must choose

So households, firms and governments must all choose: what to produce, in what quantity, and what to leave unproduced. Choosing is not a weakness of poor economies — it is forced on every economy by scarcity itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choice ends in cost

The chain's last link is cost: every choice leaves something unchosen, and that unchosen thing is the choice's price. The next lesson gives that price its proper name — opportunity cost.

## Say it to your companion
