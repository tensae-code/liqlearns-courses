# Productivity — output per input

## Do it first

Think of any work you do repeatedly. Before you read on, write how you would measure how much of it gets done in one hour — and what it costs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Indicators for studying an economy — natural, value and labour

Bulgaria's Grade 10 book groups the indicators used to analyse an economy into three kinds: **natural** indicators (the quantity of raw materials used, the volume of arable land, the number of animals, the length of the railway lines, the quantities of goods), **value** indicators (the quantity of the produced production and the performed work in value — money — units) and **labour** indicators (the number of the employed, classified by sex, age, education and qualification).

## Sub lesson 2 — Labour productivity — output per unit of time

Among the other indicators the book names **labour productivity (производителност на труда) — the quantity of invested labour for the production of a definite product per unit of time**. Productivity ties the two sides of production together: what is produced, and the labour it took, inside a set time. Raise the product from the same labour in the same time, and productivity has risen.

## Sub lesson 3 — The cost price as an indicator

Beside productivity the book names **the cost price (себестойност на продукцията), which expresses the money expenses for the production and the sale of the production**. Read together, the two indicators judge an economy's work from both sides: how much comes out per unit of labour, and what the output costs in money to produce and sell.
