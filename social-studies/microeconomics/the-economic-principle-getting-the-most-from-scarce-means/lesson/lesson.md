# The economic principle — getting the most from scarce means

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 9 Oikonomika), **1.5 The economic principle** — teaches: achieving the greatest result with given means, or a given result with the least means. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — The principle stated both ways

Greece's Grade 9 workbook gives the rule its own section: the **economic principle** — achieve the **greatest result with given means**, or a **given result with the least means**.

The two forms are one principle seen from two sides: fix the means and maximise the result, or fix the result and minimise the means. Either way, scarce means must not be wasted.

## Sub lesson 2 — The principle at work

A student with two study hours before a test applies the principle by spending them where they gain the most marks. A household with a fixed food budget applies it by feeding the family best within the budget — or feeding them the same for less.

Waste is the principle's enemy in both directions: means idle that could have produced, or means spent beyond what the result required.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the principle comes first

The workbook places the principle right after scarcity and choice for a reason: it is the **working rule** scarcity forces. Because means are scarce, every user of means — person, firm, state — is judged by how much result the means yield.

Later lessons give the principle its instruments — budgets, costs, productivity — but the rule itself is already complete here: most result, least means.
