# Consumer rights and protection

## Do it first

You buy a thing in a shop. At home you find it is faulty — it does not work as it should. What do you think you have the right to ask for? Say it, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The consumer's rights — quality goods and full information

Belarus's Grade 9 book meets this inside the chapter on the person as a consumer. A consumer is not left unprotected: under **Belarus's law on consumer protection**, the consumer holds **legal, economic and social rights**.

Two of those rights come first. The consumer has the right to **quality goods** — goods that are as they should be. And the consumer has the right to **full information** about what they are buying, so the choice is made knowingly, not blindly.

## Sub lesson 2 — When goods are faulty — repair, replacement and compensation

Rights must answer the faulty-goods case. When goods turn out to be faulty, the consumer has the right to **free repair or replacement** of the faulty goods — the seller puts the fault right, or the goods are exchanged, at no cost to the consumer.

And where the fault has caused loss, the consumer has the right to **compensation** — the loss made good.

## Sub lesson 3 — Who defends the consumer — state bodies, courts and consumer societies

The consumer does not defend these rights alone. They are defended by **state bodies**, by the **courts**, and by **consumer societies** — the organisations of consumers themselves.

So the protection stands on three legs: the law gives the rights, the faulty-goods rights put a fault right, and named defenders stand behind the consumer when a seller will not.
