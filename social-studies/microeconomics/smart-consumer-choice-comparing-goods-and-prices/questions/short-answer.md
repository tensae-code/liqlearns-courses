# Smart consumer choice — comparing goods and prices — short answer

Answer in one or two sentences, from the lesson.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Name five things the label of a good tells you.
**Answer:** Any five of: its name, producer, trademark, quantity, contents, date of production, use-by date, way of use and storage, quality and purpose.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Give four questions from the reasonable-shopping checklist.
**Answer:** Any four of: do you have an exact list of the goods you seek; do you know where to find them; why do some goods attract attention first; must advertising always be believed; what do you want to learn when buying packaged goods; what would you ask the seller about unpackaged goods; how much should you buy of perishable products; must you be careful with goods at low or reduced prices and with street sellers.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** How does the pupil decide between two goods in the comparison exercise?
**Answer:** The pupil researches each good's price, quality, delivery term, trial and return possibilities, guarantee and service, marks the advantages "+" and the drawbacks "−", and chooses the good with the better balance.
