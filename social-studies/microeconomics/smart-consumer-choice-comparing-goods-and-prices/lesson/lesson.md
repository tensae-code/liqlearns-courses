# Smart consumer choice — comparing goods and prices

## Do it first

Think of the last packaged good you or your family bought. Before you read on, write what its label told you — and what it did not tell you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Read the label before you buy

Bulgaria's Grade 3 book, in "In the shop — read the label", teaches that you can learn more about the good you are buying from its **label**. The label of a good carries its **name, producer, trademark, quantity, contents, date of production, use-by date, way of use and storage, quality and purpose**. The pupil ticks what each label tells and designs a label for a favourite cereal snack. The **barcode** is the computer code for the price and other information about the good.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shop reasonably — only the most necessary and the best you can afford

The Grade 5 book's "Advice for reasonable shopping" teaches: **to shop reasonably means to buy only the most necessary and the best of what you can afford**. Its checklist asks: do you have an exact list of the goods you seek, do you know where to find them, why do some goods attract attention first, must advertising always be believed, what do you want to learn when buying packaged goods, what would you ask the seller about unpackaged goods, how much should you buy of perishable products, and must you be careful with goods at low or reduced prices and with street sellers?

## Sub lesson 3 — Compare before you choose — price, quality, guarantee

The same Grade 5 advice has the pupil **research a chosen good** before buying: its **price, quality, delivery term, trial and return possibilities, guarantee and service** — with the advantages marked "+" and the drawbacks "−". The good with the better balance of advantages is the smart choice, not simply the cheapest or the first one that catches the eye.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the lesson to your companion in your own words, part by part, from the sub lessons. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Using the lesson, list everything a good's label carries, from memory.

2. Write a reasonable-shopping list for a week and mark the most necessary item on it. Say why it comes first.

3. Compare two similar goods on paper: price, quality, delivery, return, guarantee — mark "+" and "−", then name your choice and why.

4. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.
