# Total and marginal utility

## Do it first

Imagine eating bananas one by one. The first tastes wonderful. What about the fifth? The eighth? Say when eating one more stops helping and starts hurting.

## Sub lesson 1 — Total utility and marginal utility

Total utility (TU) is the whole satisfaction from all the units consumed. Marginal utility (MU) is the extra satisfaction from one more unit: MU = change in TU divided by change in quantity. In the banana table, total utility climbs unit by unit to a saturation point of 37 utils at 7 units.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saturation — where more stops helping

As consumption rises, marginal utility falls to zero exactly at the saturation point — 7 bananas and 37 utils in the table. Beyond saturation, marginal utility turns negative: eating more makes you worse off, and total utility itself falls. The peak of the TU curve and the zero of the MU curve are the same moment seen two ways.

## Sub lesson 3 — The law of diminishing marginal utility

The pattern is a general rule of consumption: the law of diminishing marginal utility. Each extra unit of a good adds less satisfaction than the one before it, other things equal. It holds for the oranges example, the banana table, and ordinary eating and drinking everywhere.
