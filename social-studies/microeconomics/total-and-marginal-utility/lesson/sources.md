**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Iraq, United States, Pakistan, Nigeria, Greece, Bhutan, Sri Lanka).**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class IX Economics IX): Economics is framed throughout by Gross National Happiness (GNH) — material vs spiritual well-being — and every concept is anchored in Bhutanese examples (ngultrum-era money history, hydropower exports to India, Kheng bamboo products, Royal Monetary Authority).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class XII Economics XII): Class XII is the analytical capstone — utility/indifference analysis, production, cost and revenue, distribution theory, statistics and index numbers, then Bhutan-flavoured public finance (deficit financing's effects on the Bhutanese economy, Bhutan budgetary procedure) and national income as compiled by Bhutan's National Statistics Bureau.

Nigeria's own way (Nigeria Grade 11 New Economics SS2): SS2 pairs every theory block with its Nigerian application in the same theme — wage policy and trade unions, agricultural policy and marketing, revenue allocation in the federation, the Nigerian Stock Exchange and inflation in Nigeria.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 11 Economics Class 11): a full Urdu-medium microeconomics course whose every Urdu section heading carries the English term in brackets, so the learner meets both languages' vocabulary at once; theory is built from schedules, tables and hand-drawn curves throughout, and distribution (rent, wages, interest, profit) closes the book as one factor-pricing chapter.


United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Economics): the senior course runs from Mesopotamia's first city-states to BRICS, and keeps landing in Iraq — the choice example is electricity versus optical internet, the sustainable-development example is cutting Iraq's dependence on oil, foreign borrowing is illustrated by Iraq's infrastructure projects, and students are sent to the Central Bank of Iraq's own website.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias): the national Lykeio textbook teaches micro first then macro in one volume, opening economics through Robinson Crusoe's one-man economy, and closes with the EU and the Greek economy itself; worked examples use euros, olive oil and peaches.
