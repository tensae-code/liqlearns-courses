**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Rwanda, Malaysia, Dominica, Uzbekistan, Egypt, India, United States, Pakistan, Samoa, Bhutan). Papua New Guinea is teacher-guide evidence only — named here, counted nowhere.**

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Class X Economics X): Class X turns from concepts to Bhutan itself — the Bhutanese economy's features, public finance, GNH as the official development measure alongside GDP, Bhutan's balance of payments and rupee-crisis case material.

Samoa's own way (Year 12 Economics): A full NZ-style supply/demand course rebuilt on Samoan ground — Apia milk and taro-fertiliser markets, VAGST, the tala/yen foreign-exchange market, Samoan public/merit goods, and money-supply definitions for Samoa's banks.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 10 Social Science TG): Grade 10 Economics is the labour and capitalism year — two full units on labour (unions, division of labour, labour law, labour costs), then the market system judged by its costs and benefits, trade through television and the internet, and a closing unit interrogating the capitalist ideology itself. This Papua New Guinea material stands on the teacher guide as evidence only.


Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 Social Studies Class 5 SNC): The Class 5 chapter deepens the Class 4 set with Pakistan's own institutions as the teaching examples — PIA, Pakistan Railways, Pakistan Television, Pakistan Post and WAPDA for public services, the State Bank of Pakistan (established 1948), and Dr Akhtar Hameed Khan's microfinance work for social entrepreneurship.


United States' own way (United States Grade 12 Principles of Microeconomics for AP® Courses 2e): an AP®-framed American university text — every model is run on US data and institutions (Bureau of Labor Statistics spending data, FRED series, US antitrust and environmental law, the US safety net, a US–Mexico trade worked example), dollars throughout, graph-first with a named four-step method for equilibrium changes.

United States' own way (United States Grade 13 American Government 3e): economics appears inside civics as the machinery of public policy — goods classified by who can be excluded, and the federal budget as the arena where Keynesian ideas meet mandatory spending.

India's own way (India Grade 8 Social and Political Life-III): The government's economic role is taught through Chennai's water — who gets how much, who pays, and what happens when a basic need is left to profit — and through Bhopal, where cheap labour and weak safety enforcement are priced in workers' lives.

Dominica's own way: Economics lives inside the CSEC Principles of Business course — money, markets, banks, taxes, GDP and Caribbean problems (unemployment, migration, debt) are taught as the economics core of a business textbook, always through Caribbean cases (ECCB, CARICOM/CSME, sou-sou savings, the Barbados and Dominica sugar taxes). This lesson teaches the placement's content in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.

No matching teacher guide exists in this band: the book recorded as this text's teacher guide proved to be the 2011 GEQIP Grade 12 teacher's guide for the old syllabus, not this book's companion, and supplies no answer keys for it (proof in the placements file's verification block).

Malaysia's own way (Malaysia Grade 11 Ekonomi Tingkatan 5): Form 5 turns to the state and the world — Bank Negara's policy tools, the national budget (Belanjawan), and Malaysia's own trade composition and direction as the data pupils analyse.

Egypt's own way (Secondary 12 Statistics and Economics): a full school Economics course in six chapters, ending at home — the last chapter brings globalisation back to Egypt and Africa, and the money chapter tells Egypt's own story (the National Bank's 1898 notes, the Central Bank of Egypt from 1961).
