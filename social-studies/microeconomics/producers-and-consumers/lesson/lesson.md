# Producers and consumers

## Do it first

Think of the bread you ate most recently. Before you read on, say: how many people touched it before you — and what did each one do?

## Sub lesson 1 — The producer — the one who grows and makes

In Syria's Grade 3 Social Studies book, the lesson صنع في سورية (Made in Syria) follows bread from the field to the table. On the way it names the first of this lesson's two people:

The person who **grows and produces** the wheat is a **producer**. The person who **makes** the bread is also a producer. A producer is the one who brings a good into being — growing it, making it, producing it.

The book's own examples run wider than bread: the worker who makes pottery is a producer too. Wherever a good comes from, the person who produces it stands at its start.

## Sub lesson 2 — The consumer — the one who buys and uses

At the other end of the chain stands the second person. The person who **buys and eats** the bread is a **consumer**. A consumer is the one who uses a good up — eating it, drinking it, wearing it, using it.

The book's own sentence work fixes the pair: *the child consumes water*. The child did not make the water ready; the child uses it. Buying and using — that is the consumer's side of every good.

## Sub lesson 3 — Produces or consumes? — telling the two apart

The Syrian lesson makes pupils prove the difference in sentences, completing each with **"produces"** or **"consumes"**: the child *consumes* water; the worker *produces* pottery. The test is always the same question — with this good, does the person make it, or use it?

The lesson closes by turning the test on the pupil's own morning: name three products you consume in the morning. Bread, milk, water — for each one there is a consumer (you) and, behind it, producers who grew and made it. One person can stand on both sides in a single day: the baker consumes water and produces bread.

## Say it to your companion

Explain producers and consumers to your companion in your own words: who the producer is, who the consumer is, and how the book's sentence test tells them apart. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name three products you consumed this morning, and for one of them name its producers, step by step back to the raw material.

2. Answer this lesson's questions from questions/ without the lesson open; re-read only the parts you missed.

3. Write the key words from memory — producer, consumer, produces, consumes — and write one sentence of your own for each, in the book's pattern.
