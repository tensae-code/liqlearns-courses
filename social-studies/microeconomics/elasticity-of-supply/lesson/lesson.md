# Elasticity of supply

## Do it first

Coffee sits in sacks in a store when its price jumps. Can the seller respond today — this week — or must the response wait for a harvest? Say which, and what makes the difference.

## Sub lesson 1 — The measure — always positive

Elasticity of supply measures how quantity supplied responds to price, at a point (point measure) or between two points (arc measure). Unlike demand's, its sign is always positive: higher price, larger quantity supplied — the law of supply itself guarantees the two move together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Coffee in stock — the quick response

The book illustrates supply response with coffee held in stock. A seller holding sacks can answer a price rise at once by releasing stock — expectations of future prices decide how much is held back and how much is let go. Stored goods respond fast; goods still to be grown cannot.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its determinants

Four things set supply elasticity: expectations of future prices — holding stock back for a better price; the production period and the time allowed to respond — longer means stretchier; how easily factors can be substituted for one another; and the number of sellers in the market. Time is the master determinant, here as with demand.
