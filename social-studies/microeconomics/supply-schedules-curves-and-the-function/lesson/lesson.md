# Supply — schedules, curves and the function

## Do it first

A shirt seller offers nothing at Birr 5, a few at Birr 15, many at Birr 30. Why does the quantity rise with the price — and why is there a price below which she offers nothing? Say your reasons first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The supply function

Supply is written Qs = a + bP: quantity supplied rises as price rises, at the rate b. The plus sign is the whole difference from demand's minus — sellers are encouraged by higher prices, buyers are discouraged by them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The minimum price

The function carries a minimum price below which nothing is offered. Where the formula would give zero or a negative quantity, the real offer is zero: under that price, selling does not cover the trouble and cost of bringing the good to market.

## Sub lesson 3 — The supply schedule and curve

A price–quantity schedule lists the offer at each price, rising row by row, and plotted it becomes the upward-sloping supply curve. The pattern is the law of supply: higher price, larger quantity supplied, other things equal — the mirror image of the demand side.
