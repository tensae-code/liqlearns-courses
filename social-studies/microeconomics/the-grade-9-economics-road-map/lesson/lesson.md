# The Grade 9 economics road map

## Do it first

Look at the unit titles in order. Which do you think are about one buyer or one firm, and which are about the whole country? Say your answers first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Units 1–3 — foundations, problems and markets

The course opens with foundations. Unit 1 Introducing Economics asks what economics is. Unit 2 takes the basic economic problems and the economic systems that answer them. Unit 3 turns to economic resources and markets. Prices are in birr throughout — oranges, jeans and a room heater — and the micro/macro table works with teff prices and Ethiopia's GDP.

## Sub lesson 2 — Units 4–6 — demand and supply, production, money

Unit 4 introduces demand and supply. Unit 5 introduces production and cost. Unit 6 introduces money — Axumite coins, amole salt bars and the Maria Theresa thaler in Ethiopia's own money history, and the birr in the circular-flow definition.

## Sub lesson 3 — Units 7–8 — the whole economy, then enterprise

Unit 7 introduces macroeconomics — disguised unemployment and Ethiopia's trade deficit among its examples. Unit 8 closes with basic entrepreneurship, with Ethiopian founders Bethlehem Tilahun Alemu of SoleRebels and Haile Gebrselassie as exemplars. Every unit follows the same rhythm: an introduction and printed Unit Objectives, Start-up Activities that begin from what pupils already think, numbered sections each opening with its own objectives and a Start-up Activity and closing with a numbered Activity, Key Concepts at the unit opening, a Unit Summary, and a multi-part Review Questions set — true/false, multiple choice, short answer, and in Unit 2 worked problems.

## Say it to your companion
