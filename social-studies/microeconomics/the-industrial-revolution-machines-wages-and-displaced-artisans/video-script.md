The industrial revolution machines wages and displaced artisans — as Colombia's book teaches it, says the narrator: The two sides of mechanisation in source documents — Leeds textile employers argue carding/spinning machines and the flying shuttle cut manual work by a third yet improved wages and commerce; The artisans' 1794 petition answers that machines took artisans' livelihoods, left many without work or bread, and would let a few rich employers monopolise all business; The 1806 commission adds industry and commerce growth alongside rising tax weight and merchandise prices pressing on wages;
