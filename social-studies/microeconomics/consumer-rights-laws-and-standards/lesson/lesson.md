# Consumer rights — laws and standards

## Do it first

You buy a packet of food in a shop. It looks fine, but it was made badly and makes you ill. Before you read on, say who you think should have stopped that packet from reaching you — and how.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rights protected by laws and regulations — sound, quality goods

Iran's Grade 7 Social Studies book, in Lesson 8 "Consumption", teaches that **consumers have rights protected by laws and regulations**. The first of them is the right to buy **sound, quality goods** — goods that are what they claim to be and safe to use. The right is not a favour from the seller; it stands in law before any purchase is made.

## Sub lesson 2 — Permits, fines and punishment — the law with teeth

The same section names how the protection is enforced. Producers work under **production permits** — permission to make and sell what they make. A producer who breaks the rules faces **fines and punishment** for the violation. Rights, permits and penalties are one system: the law says what the consumer may claim, and backs the claim against the producer who ignores it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The standard — the specification every producer must meet

Beside the laws stands the **standard (استاندارد, defined in the book)**: the **acceptable specification** every producer must meet. The standard matters above all for the goods that touch people's **health and safety** — **food, hygiene goods, and electrical and heating appliances**. A thing that meets the standard has been measured against a written specification, not against the seller's promise.
