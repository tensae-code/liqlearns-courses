# Hands-on — Substitute and complementary goods

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with pairs
Name two pairs from your own life: one pair of substitutes and one pair of complements. For each pair, say aloud what happens to the second good's demand when the first good's price rises.

## Draw it and check it
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — two drinks that can replace each other on one side, and a lamp with its bulb — and label its parts with the lesson's key words. Then open lesson/lesson.md, compare, and fix every label you placed wrong.

## Teach it on the spot
Close the lesson. Teach the whole of sub lesson 1 aloud, as if to a younger learner, with no notes. Where you stop or guess, that is the part to re-read.
