# Movement along vs shift of the supply curve

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of one case of this lesson's subject from your own life or town and say what you already know about it. Hold your answer — the lesson takes its subject in three parts, in order.

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.4 §6 Change in quantity supplied vs change in supply** — teaches: own-price moves along the curve, other determinants shift it. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Own price moves along the supply curve

The supply twin of the demand rule, from Greece's Grade 12 textbook: when the good's **own price** changes and nothing else does, quantity supplied changes **along the same supply curve** — more supplied at a higher price, less at a lower one.

The curve stays put: the same firms, with the same costs and technology, simply offer a different quantity at the new price.

## Sub lesson 2 — Other determinants shift the curve

When a **supply determinant other than price** changes — input prices, technology, taxes on the producer — the whole curve **shifts**: at every price, firms now offer more (shift right) or less (shift left).

Costlier inputs shift supply left, because each output now costs more to bring; better technology shifts it right, because each price now covers more output.

## Sub lesson 3 — The same test as demand

The diagram test is the one already learned for demand: what changed? The good's own price → movement along. Anything else on the supply side → shift.

Keeping the two apart matters for reading causes: a price rise that follows a supply shift (say, a failed harvest) is an effect of the shift, not a movement that explains itself.
