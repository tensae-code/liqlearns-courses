# The production possibilities frontier

## Do it first

An economy can make food or computers, not unlimited amounts of both. Sketch any line you think joins its best possible combinations. Say what a point beyond your line would mean.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the frontier shows

The production possibilities frontier (PPF, or PPC) shows every combination of two goods an economy can produce with its given resources and technology. The book's worked table pairs food, in tonnes falling 500 → 0, against computers rising 0 → 2,000.

[Picture 24011 — the food–computers curve with one point on it, one inside and one outside, each labelled]

## Sub lesson 2 — Its assumptions

The curve is drawn under stated assumptions: resources fixed in quantity and quality; two classes of output; full employment and full production; technology unchanged; and some inputs better suited to one good than the other.

## Sub lesson 3 — On, inside, outside

Points on the curve are attainable and efficient; points inside are attainable but inefficient — resources wasted or idle; points outside are unattainable with present resources. One picture thus displays scarcity, choice and opportunity cost together, and defines production efficiency and resource allocation beside it.
