# Kinds of goods — consumer, producer, durable and perishable

## Do it first

Take one thing — a loaf of bread. Before you read on, sort it as many ways as you can: can you touch it? Who eats it? Does it get used up at once, or last? Hold your sortings — the lesson sorts every good five ways.

In Yemen's Grade 11 economics book (Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad), **Unit 2, Lesson 3 — Economic goods** — classifies goods: the same stock of goods sorted several different ways.

## Sub lesson 1 — Material goods and services — individual and collective consumption

The first sorting is by form: **material goods** — things that can be touched and held — against **services**, work done for others that satisfies a need without a material thing changing hands.

The second sorting is by who consumes: goods of **individual consumption**, used by one person or family, against goods of **collective consumption**, provided for the community together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Consumer goods and production goods — durable and perishable

The third sorting is by purpose: **consumer goods** satisfy needs directly, while **production goods** are used to make other goods — the tool and the material stand behind the finished thing.

The fourth sorting is by lasting: **durable goods** serve over a long period, used again and again, while **perishable goods** are consumed quickly and must be replaced.

## Sub lesson 3 — Necessities and luxuries

The fifth sorting is by urgency: **necessities** are the goods life requires — they come first in any family's or country's spending.

**Luxuries** are goods beyond necessity: wanted, enjoyed, but done without when means are short. Every good a society handles can be placed in this sorting as well as the other four.
