# Characteristics of economic needs

## Do it first

Name three things you needed today. Before you read on, ask of each one: could I ever have enough of it? Could something else take its place? Did I need it last year too? Hold your answers — the lesson lists what all needs are like.

In Yemen's Grade 11 economics book (Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad), **Unit 2, Lesson 2 — Economic needs** — after defining needs, the book lists their **characteristics**: what needs are like, as a kind.

## Sub lesson 1 — Needs are multiple, relative and satiable

Needs are **multiple**: nobody has one need — food, clothing, housing, learning, rest and more arrive together, and meeting one does not end the others.

They are **relative**: what counts as a need differs by person, society and circumstance — a need is judged against a life, not in the abstract. And each single need is **satiable**: satisfy it and it quiets — enough food ends this hunger, even though hunger will return.

## Sub lesson 2 — Needs are substitutable and complementary

Needs are **substitutable**: one good can take another's place in meeting a need — a need can often be met in more than one way, and people switch between the ways.

And needs are **complementary**: some needs come linked, so meeting one calls for meeting another alongside it — goods that are used together satisfy their needs together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Needs grow — with civilisation, income, time, place, culture and age

Needs are not fixed. They **grow with civilisation** — as societies develop, new needs appear that earlier generations never felt. They grow **with income** — more means bring more needs within reach and into habit.

They also change **with time, place, culture and age**: the needs of one era, one land, one culture and one stage of life are not another's. That growth is why the economic problem never finally closes.
