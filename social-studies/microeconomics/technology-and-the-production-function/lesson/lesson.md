# Technology and the production function

In Greece's book (Greece Grade 12 Arches Oikonomikis Theorias), **Ch.3 §7 The effect of technological change on production** — teaches: better technology shifting what the same inputs can produce. This lesson teaches that placement in the course's own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Technology shifts what inputs can produce

Greece's Grade 12 textbook gives technological change its own section in the production chapter. The **production function** states the output a given set of inputs can produce with the technology in use. **Better technology** — a better method, machine or organisation — lets the **same inputs produce more**.

On the diagram the whole production function shifts: at every input level, output is higher than before. Nothing about the inputs changed; what changed is what they can do.

## Sub lesson 2 — New method, not just more input

Moving along a production function (more input, same method) must not be confused with shifting it (same input, better method). The first is growth of quantity; the second is growth of capability.

A firm that buys a second identical machine has more input. A firm that finds a method producing the same goods with half the labour has better technology — and its costs fall at every scale.

## Sub lesson 3 — Technology and costs

Because better technology yields more from the same inputs, it lowers the cost of each unit produced — the same wage and rent bill is now spread over more output.

That is why firms chase technology even when it is expensive to adopt: the one-time cost of the new method is weighed against a permanent fall in unit cost.
