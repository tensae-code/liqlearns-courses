# Impulse buying and needs vs wants

## Do it first

You have K10. You were sent to buy bread. At the shop you see sweets you did not plan to buy. Before you read on, say what you could do — and what could go wrong if you buy the sweets first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Needs and wants

In Zambia's Grade 4 teaching module, Sub-topic 4.9.1 is Impulse Buying, inside Topic 4.9, Learning About Money. It starts from the difference between **needs** and **wants**. A **need** is a thing we must have — like the bread the family will eat. A **want** is a thing we would like to have but can do without — like the sweets.

Money is limited: the same K10 cannot be spent twice. So needs and wants cannot be treated the same — the needs must be provided for first.

## Sub lesson 2 — Impulse buying — buying without thinking

**Impulse buying** is buying something at once, on the spot, without thinking and without planning — because you saw it and wanted it in that moment. The module teaches it through the **K10 bread-and-sweets story**: money meant for a need, bread, is spent on a want, sweets, the moment the sweets are seen.

The trouble with impulse buying is what comes after: the money is gone, and the need it was meant for is still there, unmet.

## Sub lesson 3 — Managing impulse buying — needs first

The module's way to manage impulse buying is to put needs first and think before spending. In the **"Mwansa and the K20" scenario**, a learner with K20 must decide what the money is really for before any of it is spent: needs first, then wants only with what is left — and a want can wait.

The class practises this in a **classroom market with play money**: with play kwacha in hand, learners buy from the classroom stalls, and learn to check — is this a need or a want? Did I plan this? — before the play money leaves their hand.

## Say it to your companion

Explain needs, wants and impulse buying to your companion in your own words: the K10 bread-and-sweets story, and how the "Mwansa and the K20" scenario manages it. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
