# The organiser — entrepreneur, risk and profit

## Do it first

Land, workers and tools are all in one yard — but nothing is being made. Before you read on, say what is missing, and what that missing person stands to gain or lose. Hold your answer — the lesson names the organiser.

In Yemen's Grade 11 economics book (Mabadi' Ilm al-Iqtisad), **Unit 3, Lesson 4 — Organisation** — takes the fourth element of production: the **organiser**.

## Sub lesson 1 — The organiser combines the factors

The **organiser** is the person who **combines the factors of production** — land, labour and capital — into one working unit aimed at a product.

Left separate, the factors produce nothing together. Organisation is the act that joins them, sets the plan, and carries the venture as one enterprise.

## Sub lesson 2 — The organiser bears the risk of loss

Combining the factors means committing resources before anything is sold — and the outcome is uncertain. The organiser **bears the risk of loss**: if the venture fails, the loss falls on him.

That risk-bearing is not an accident of the role; it is the role's price. The organiser stakes first and learns last whether the stake was well placed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Profit — revenue minus costs

The organiser's reward is **profit**: **revenue minus costs** — what the venture takes in, less everything it spent producing and selling.

If revenue exceeds costs, the remainder is profit; if costs exceed revenue, the difference is loss, and it is the organiser who absorbs it. Profit and risk are the two sides of the same office.
