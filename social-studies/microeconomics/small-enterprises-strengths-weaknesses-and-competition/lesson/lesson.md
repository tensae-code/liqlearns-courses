# Small enterprises — strengths, weaknesses and competition

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold the lesson's one thing in mind and say what you already think it means: Small enterprises are flexible — quick to change product designs, close in managing labour, easy to renew technology — but they work with little capital that is hard to borrow, lack market information and skilled workers and managers, and compete weakly against firms in the same industry and larger firms at home and abroad, leaving them at risk of bankruptcy. Hold your answer — the lesson takes it in parts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Flexibility — the strengths of being small

Vietnam's Grade 10 study-topics book teaches the advantages first: small enterprises are **flexible**. They can **quickly change product designs** to meet market demand, **closely manage labour**, and **easily renew technology** — a small firm can turn faster than a large one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Little capital and thin information — the difficulties

Against those strengths stand the difficulties: **little capital that is hard to borrow**, a **lack of market information**, and **low worker and management skill**. The small enterprise starts its competition with less money, less knowledge of its market and less trained people.

## Sub lesson 3 — Competing against bigger firms — the bankruptcy risk

So their **competitiveness is limited** — against firms in the same industry and against **larger firms at home and abroad**. A firm that cannot match its rivals on capital, information or skill is left **at risk of bankruptcy** — the book's honest closing word on the small enterprise's position.
