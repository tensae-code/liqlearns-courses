# What economics asks — what, how and for whom to produce

## Do it first

Think of a box of dates on your family's table. Before you read on, say: where did it come from, how did it reach you, and who decided dates would be grown at all? Hold your answers — the lesson names each of those steps.

## Sub lesson 1 — Economic activity — producing, distributing, exchanging, consuming

In Saudi Arabia's Grade 4 social studies book, Unit 4 — Economics — opens with **Lesson 14, The concept of economics**. The book teaches economics through **economic activity**: everything people do with goods and services as they move through life.

Economic activity has four movements. **Producing** — making goods and providing services. **Distributing** — carrying and sharing out what is produced so it reaches people. **Exchanging** — trading what is produced, buying and selling. **Consuming** — using the goods and services at the end of the chain.

Follow the book's own example. Dates are produced on the farm, distributed to the markets, exchanged when a family buys them, and consumed at the table. Milk makes the same journey: produced at the dairy, distributed, exchanged, consumed. Economics is the study of that whole journey, not of one step alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — What do we produce, and how do we produce it

Because no place can produce everything at once, the economist asks questions before production begins. The book names three questions, and the first two are about the producing itself.

**What do we produce?** A farm, a factory and a whole country must each choose: dates or milk, this good or that service. The choice comes first, because resources used for one product cannot also make another.

**How do we produce it?** The same product can be made in different ways — with more hands or more machines, on this land or that. Choosing the way of producing is the second question.

## Sub lesson 3 — For whom do we produce

The third question looks at the end of the chain: **for whom do we produce it?** Goods and services are not made for nobody. They are made for the people who will receive them, exchange for them and consume them.

The three questions belong together — what, how, and for whom. Saudi Arabia's book teaches them through the Kingdom's own production: dates and milk are what is produced, the farms and dairies are how, and the families who buy and consume them are for whom. Every economy, large or small, answers the same three questions about everything it makes.
