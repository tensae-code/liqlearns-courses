| more preservation methods: drying removes water so spoilage microorganisms cannot develop — natural drying by sun and wind (weather-dependent, wide drying yards needed) and artificial drying in dryers (convection, contact, freeze-drying (sấy thăng hoa), cold drying, solar drying — shorter time, less factory space, quality assured); curing with salt or sugar works by osmosis — salt or sugar mixed into the food draws water out of the microbial cells and kills or prevents the spoilage organisms (salted fish; fruit in syrup needs a sugar concentration of 60–65% or more, and syrup-preserved food must be kept in sealed bottles and jars away from air); adjusting the food's pH (vinegar soaking, lactic fermentation — pickled vegetables (muối chua)) — easy and long-keeping but usually combined with canning or bottling, and it changes the food's sensory character and nutrient value; antimicrobial substances — permitted food additives with antiseptic effect (sorbic acid, sodium benzoate, propionic acid…), effective and cheap but dangerous to food safety if misused, and natural ones (phytoncides in onion and garlic; lysozyme in tears, tissues and egg white); canning (đóng hộp) — widely applied, keeps food long with its nutrient value, usually combined with other methods; and physical agents — ultraviolet (UV) rays against mould and bacteria on tools and food surfaces (fast, effective, little effect on food quality; widely used in industrial production, e.g. eggs), ultrasound, which destroys microorganisms and is used in milk and fruit-juice production without changing the food's nutrients or taste, and ionising radiation, which disrupts bacterial cells so they cannot reproduce (original string `more preservation methods`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 9 — Mô đun Chế biến thực phẩm*, printed p. 19 |
