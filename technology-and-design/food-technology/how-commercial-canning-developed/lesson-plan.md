# Lesson plan — How commercial canning developed

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Food Technology · **Lesson:** How commercial canning developed
**Place in the curriculum:** Food Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** commercial canning began with Appert's prize-winning experiments: food sealed in glass jars and heated in boiling water kept — then, in 1809, food in sealed metal containers — the line that leads to the conventional canning method used today.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The prize and the problem** — Armies and navies needed food that travelled and kept; a prize was offered for a method — and Appert won it.
2. **Appert's experiments — seal and heat** — Appert sealed food in glass jars and heated them in boiling water: the food kept. In 1809 the method moved into sealed metal containers.
3. **To the conventional method** — Today's conventional canning is Appert's line, industrialised: fill, seal, heat by schedule, cool — controlled at every step.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what problem the prize addressed.
2. Describe Appert's method and the 1809 step.
3. State what modern canning kept and what it changed.
## What you need
A food can and a glass jar to compare.
