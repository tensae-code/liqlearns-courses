# Food additives

## Do it first
Read the ingredients on any packaged snack. Among the flour and sugar hide names you do not cook with. Each was added on purpose, to do a job. This lesson is learning to ask: which job?

## Sub lesson 1 — What an additive is
Colour, flavour, texture, keeping quality — where nature or process falls short, an additive is dosed in. The definition matters: an additive is intentional and functional, not a contaminant and not an ingredient like flour or water.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kinds and purposes — the chart
Read the chart as a toolbox: each row answers 'what job, which agent'. Preservatives extend safe life; pH adjusters hold the acidity a product's safety and flavour need. A kind without a purpose is marketing; a purpose names its kind.

## Sub lesson 3 — Checking real packaged foods
Take three packages and read their ingredient lists against the chart. Which additives appear? What job does each do in that food? The lesson ends in literacy: a shopper who can read a label can judge a product.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: processed food carries additives — substances added on purpose, each for a job: the book's chart sets kinds against purposes, preservatives and pH-adjusting agents among them, and the teaching is checked against real packaged foods. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
