# Commercial freezing

## Do it first
Two frozen fish: one frozen slowly in still air, one frozen fast between cold plates. Thawed, one is firm, one weeps and falls apart. The cold was the same. The speed was not.

## Sub lesson 1 — Birdseye and the cold plates
Contact is the trick: plates touch the package on both sides, drawing heat out quickly with no insulating air gap. Fast, even freezing was the commercial breakthrough — food that thawed close to fresh.

## Sub lesson 2 — Snap, blast and immersion
Three routes to speed: plates (contact), air (blast), liquid (immersion). Snap freezing names the goal — frozen before quality can drain away. Each suits different products: packs, loose pieces, delicate items.

## Sub lesson 3 — Speed decides quality; not everything freezes
The crystal is the quality: big crystals rupture cells, and the thawed food weeps and mushes. Fast freezing is quality preservation, not just cold storage. Yet some foods — those whose structure water ruins — do not freeze well by any method, and the book names which foods freeze well and which do not.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: commercial freezing was transformed by Birdseye's contact freezing between cold plates; alongside snap freezing, blast freezing and immersion freezing, the lesson is that freezing speed decides quality — and that some foods freeze well and others do not. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
