# Lesson plan — Commercial freezing

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Food Technology · **Lesson:** Commercial freezing
**Place in the curriculum:** Food Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** commercial freezing was transformed by Birdseye's contact freezing between cold plates; alongside snap freezing, blast freezing and immersion freezing, the lesson is that freezing speed decides quality — and that some foods freeze well and others do not.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Birdseye and the cold plates** — Birdseye's contact freezing pressed packaged food between cold plates, freezing it fast and evenly.
2. **Snap, blast and immersion** — Snap freezing freezes very fast; blast freezing drives very cold air over the food; immersion freezing plunges food into a freezing liquid medium.
3. **Speed decides quality; not everything freezes** — Slow freezing grows large ice crystals that tear food's structure; fast freezing keeps crystals small — and some foods still freeze badly.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Food Technology. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain contact freezing's advantage.
2. Tell snap, blast and immersion freezing apart.
3. Explain why speed decides quality and why some foods fail.
## What you need
No materials; ice from a freezer to observe crystal texture if available.
