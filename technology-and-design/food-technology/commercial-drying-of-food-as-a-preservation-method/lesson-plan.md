# Lesson plan — Commercial drying of food as a preservation method

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Food Technology · **Lesson:** Commercial drying of food as a preservation method
**Place in the curriculum:** Food Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** commercial drying preserves food by removing its moisture under controlled conditions — the industrial form of one of the oldest preservation methods — so micro-organisms cannot grow and the food keeps, light and stable, until water returns at cooking.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The principle — water out** — Drying preserves by taking moisture below the level micro-organisms need to grow.
2. **From sun to controlled drying** — Traditional drying trusts sun and wind; commercial drying controls heat, airflow and time for an even, safe result.
3. **Quality and re-use** — Drying changes food — flavour concentrates, texture changes — and the dried product must be stored dry and restored with water at use.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Food Technology. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State drying's preservation principle.
2. Give three things commercial control adds to sun drying.
3. Describe dried food's storage and return to use.
## What you need
A dried food sample (raisin, dried fruit) and its fresh counterpart if available.
