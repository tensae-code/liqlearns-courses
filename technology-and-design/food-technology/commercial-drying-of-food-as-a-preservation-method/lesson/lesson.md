# Commercial drying of food as a preservation method

## Do it first
A fresh mango rots in days and weighs heavy with water. Dried, it keeps for months in a small packet. What left the fruit — and what, exactly, did that departure prevent?

## Sub lesson 1 — The principle — water out
No water, no growth: the spoilage lesson's condition, removed wholesale. Dried food keeps without cold, weighs little, and travels well — which is why drying serves both the village rack and the factory.

## Sub lesson 2 — From sun to controlled drying
Control buys three things: evenness (no wet pockets left to mould), safety (temperatures and handling that keep the product clean), and speed (weather no longer decides). The method is ancient; the management is modern.

## Sub lesson 3 — Quality and re-use
A dried food is a stored ingredient, not a failed fresh one. Stored where moisture cannot return, it keeps; soaked or cooked in water, it comes back to the pot. Judging drying means judging the whole round trip.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: commercial drying preserves food by removing its moisture under controlled conditions — the industrial form of one of the oldest preservation methods — so micro-organisms cannot grow and the food keeps, light and stable, until water returns at cooking. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
