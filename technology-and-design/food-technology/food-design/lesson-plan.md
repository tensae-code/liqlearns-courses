# Lesson plan — Food design

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Food Technology · **Lesson:** Food design
**Place in the curriculum:** Food Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malaysia expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** food design gives food pattern and form — and matters because it follows trends, offers choice, raises value and introduces cultures.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Malaysia's *Reka Bentuk dan Teknologi* (RBT — Design and Technology) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Malaysia counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The kinds of food design** — Food is designed like any product: Malaysia shows fruit and vegetable carving, bakery products, and moulded cakes and jellies — food given pattern and form on purpose, to be eaten with the eyes first.
2. **Why food design matters** — Food design matters commercially and culturally: it follows trends, gives consumers choice, meets new tastes, raises commercial value, and introduces cultures to one another at the table.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Food Technology. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the kinds of food design around us.
2. Explain why food design matters, commercially and culturally.
3. Explain how Malaysia's book teaches it.
## What you need
Pictures of carved produce, bakery and moulded desserts; simple foods to shape.
