# Setting and serving the table

## Do it first
Look at a table where people are about to eat. Point to each piece on it and guess its job. Then learn the pieces properly — and how the whole table is laid.

## Sub lesson 1 — The tableware and what each piece does
Every meal is eaten with tableware — plates, a cup, a knife, a fork, a spoon and a napkin. Each piece has one job: the plate holds the food, the knife cuts, the fork lifts, the spoon takes soup and drinks, the cup holds the drink, and the napkin keeps hands and mouth clean. Naming each piece and its job comes before laying any table.

## Sub lesson 2 — Laying the table for breakfast
For breakfast the table is laid simply: a plate in front of each seat, the knife and fork beside it, the spoon where the hand reaches it, a cup for the drink, and a napkin. The learner lays the breakfast table piece by piece, in the pictured order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Laying the table for lunch
Lunch adds more: a soup plate and the pieces lunch needs join the breakfast setting. The learner compares the two laid tables and names what lunch adds and why — a fuller meal needs fuller tableware.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: each piece of tableware has its place and its job, and the table is laid one way for breakfast and a fuller way for lunch. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
