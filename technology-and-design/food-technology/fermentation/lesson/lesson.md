# Fermentation

## Do it first
A breadfruit tree drops a hundred ripe fruit in a fortnight — far more than a village can eat fresh. Burying them sounds like waste. In Samoa it was banking: the pit turns a glut into a reserve.

## Sub lesson 1 — The preserving acid
Fermentation is a managed population: the fermenting micro-organisms multiply first, fill the food with their acid, and the acid bars the door against the spoilers. The food changes — flavour, texture, keeping quality — and the change is the preservation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Masi ulu — breadfruit in a pit
Breadfruit ripens all at once and cannot wait. Samoa's answer: the fruit goes into a leaf-lined pit, is covered, and ferments there — a store that keeps the harvest edible long after the season. The book gives the historical account of masi: a famine reserve built into the landscape.

## Sub lesson 3 — Building and covering the pit
The leaf lining keeps the food clean from the earth and holds moisture; the covering seals the ferment from air and pests and keeps its warmth. A fermenting pit is equipment: built to dimensions of practice, it does the same work a factory vessel does, in earth and leaves.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: fermentation preserves food with special micro-organisms — yeasts and bacteria — whose acid stops spoiling: Samoa's worked case is masi ulu, breadfruit fermented in a leaf-lined pit, and how that pit is built and covered. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
