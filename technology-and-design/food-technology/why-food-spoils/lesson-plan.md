# Lesson plan — Why food spoils

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Food Technology · **Lesson:** Why food spoils
**Place in the curriculum:** Food Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** food spoils because bacteria, moulds, yeasts and enzymes grow and work in it — they need warmth, moisture and food — and preservation works by removing one of those conditions: drying, changing the temperature, or adding lemon juice, vinegar, salt or sugar, while warmth, moisture, light, oxygen and poor handling are the factors that make food go bad.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

## Sub lessons
1. **The spoilers and what they need** — Bacteria, moulds and yeasts are living micro-organisms; enzymes are the food's own working chemicals — all of them active when conditions suit: warmth, moisture and food to grow on.
2. **Preservation removes a condition** — Dry the food and moisture goes; chill, freeze or cook and temperature turns against growth; lemon juice, vinegar, salt or sugar make the food itself hostile.
3. **The factors that make food go bad** — Warmth, moisture, light, oxygen and poor handling invite spoilage — storage fights all five.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Food Technology. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the spoilers and the three conditions they need.
2. Match each preservation move to the condition it removes.
3. List the five spoilage factors and the storage rule they imply.
## What you need
Two bread samples for observation over days (teacher-managed), or pictures.
