# Lesson plan — The principles of preservation

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Food Technology · **Lesson:** The principles of preservation
**Place in the curriculum:** Food Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** preservation rests on one principle: raw food is a breeding ground for bacteria, so preservation destroys or slows them — by freezing, by heating and bottling or canning, by pickling in vinegar, by preserving with salt or sugar, or by smoking or drying — and blanching at 95-100°C before preserving inactivates the enzymes that cause discolouring and bad flavours.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The principle — destroy or slow** — Raw food breeds bacteria. Every preservation method either destroys them or slows them to a standstill.
2. **The methods, family by family** — Freezing; heating and bottling or canning; pickling in vinegar; salt or sugar; smoking or drying — each with its mechanism.
3. **Blanching — the enzyme step** — Before preserving, fruit and vegetables are blanched at 95-100°C to inactivate the enzymes that cause discolouring and bad flavours.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the destroy-or-slow principle.
2. Give each method family's mechanism.
3. Explain blanching: temperature, purpose, consequence of skipping.
## What you need
No materials; a preserved product's label or picture for discussion.
