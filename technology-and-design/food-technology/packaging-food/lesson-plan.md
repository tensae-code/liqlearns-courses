# Lesson plan — Packaging food

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Food Technology · **Lesson:** Packaging food
**Place in the curriculum:** Food Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** food packaging grew from a protective container to active packaging: flushing oxygen out with nitrogen, polymer films whose gas permeability controls the atmosphere inside the pack, and microwave packaging — with environmentally friendly packaging in glass, aluminium, plastics and paper judged beside them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The protective container — packaging's first job** — Packaging began as protection: hold the food, keep dirt and damage out, carry information.
2. **Active packaging — managing the air inside** — Active packs control the atmosphere: oxygen is flushed out with nitrogen, and polymer films are chosen whose gas permeability lets the pack breathe at a designed rate.
3. **Environmentally friendly packaging — glass, aluminium, plastics, paper** — Materials are judged for protection and for their environmental cost: glass, aluminium, plastics and paper each carry both.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Food Technology. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State packaging's first job.
2. Explain nitrogen flushing and permeability-matched films.
3. Compare glass, aluminium, plastic and paper environmentally.
## What you need
Several real food packages to examine.
