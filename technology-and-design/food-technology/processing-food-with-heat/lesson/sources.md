the boiling water stays clear; animal foods cooked soft, not tough or mushy; leafy vegetables just cooked, still green; roots with starch cooked through and mealy — practice: boiled cauliflower and carrot), cooking/simmering (nấu: food cooked in a large amount of seasoned liquid, animal ingredients first then plant, seasoning adjusted at the end; food soft, not tough or mushy, fragrant, attractive colour — practice: chicken soup with sweetcorn and mushroom), braising (kho: food cooked in a small amount of strongly flavoured liquid, often over low heat until the liquid is nearly or fully absorbed; soft and soaked with flavour, little or no liquid left, slightly sticky, golden brown — practice: pork belly braised with eggs, with caramelised sugar (nước hàng) for colour); by steam — steaming (hấp: food placed on a rack, tray or plate in the steamer, the heat kept so steam rises plentifully and continuously until cooked; cooked soft and drained, little or no water, characteristic fragrant taste — practice: fish steamed with soy sauce, about 25 minutes); by hot air — grilling/baking (nướng: food placed on a grill or skewered, or foil-wrapped, over the heat source, turned for even cooking, or baked in an oven (the mung-bean cake baked at 150 °C for 50–60 minutes); cooked evenly, not tough, fragrant and rich, golden); by hot oil or fat — frying (rán/chiên: ingredients put into hot fat, drained so the fat does not splash, turned until evenly golden and cooked through; crisp and spongy, drained of fat, cooked through, not burnt or under-golden — practice: fried spring rolls (nem rán)), dry-roasting (rang: food put into a pan or pot with very little fat and stirred continuously until dry, firm and as required; dry, firm, fragrant, attractive colour — practice: fried rice with vegetables), stir-frying (xào: food put into a pan with a moderate amount of fat and stirred continuously until just cooked; cooked through, keeping its softness and moisture, fragrant, attractive colour — practice: beef stir-fried with bell peppers and onion);
