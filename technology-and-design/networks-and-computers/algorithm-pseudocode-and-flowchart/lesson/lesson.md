# Algorithm, pseudocode and flowchart

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Malaysia's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three ways to plan a program
An algorithm is the step-by-step plan; pseudocode writes the plan in plain ordered words; a flowchart draws it with symbols — start/end, process, input/output, and the flow arrows joining them. Malaysia teaches all three together, on real situations: making tea, and Arif’s morning routine.

## Sub lesson 2 — Checking, improving, applying
Plans are checked and improved before they are coded, and the same tools break down practical problems — the book uses pseudocode and a flowchart to plan measuring and marking a board. Thinking tools serve the workshop too.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: an algorithm is the plan, pseudocode writes it in words, and a flowchart draws it in symbols — all checked and improved before use. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
