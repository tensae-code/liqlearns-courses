# Representation of information

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Representation of information
Greece's book teaches it this way: the binary code and data representation, computer codes, number systems and converting between decimal, binary and hexadecimal (*Computers & Peripherals*, printed p. 34). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Representation of information — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: the binary code and data representation, computer codes, number systems and converting between decimal, binary and hexadecimal. Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: The binary code and data representation, computer codes, number systems and converting between decimal, binary and hexadecimal. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
