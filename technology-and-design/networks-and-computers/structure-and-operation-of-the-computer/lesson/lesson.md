# Structure and operation of the computer

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Structure and operation of the computer
Greece's book teaches it this way: computer architecture, the CPU (registers, architecture), memory, the bus, peripheral units, decoding circuits, data-transfer techniques (interrupts, DMA), handshaking, and serial communication (the serial port, the UART, modem communication, the RS-232C standard, its signals/pins and connecting terminal devices) (*Computers & Peripherals*, printed p. 50). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Structure and operation of the computer — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: computer architecture, the CPU (registers, architecture), memory, the bus, peripheral units, decoding circuits, data-transfer techniques (interrupts, DMA), handshaking, and serial communication (the serial port, the UART, modem communication, the RS-232C standard, its signals/pins and connecting terminal devices). Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.
