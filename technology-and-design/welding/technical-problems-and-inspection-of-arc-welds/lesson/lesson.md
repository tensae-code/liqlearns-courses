# Technical problems and inspection of arc welds

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Technical problems and inspection of arc welds
Greece's book teaches it this way: edge preparation, arc striking and holding, welding parameters, arc blow, thermal distortion, cracking (and hydrogen's role), porosity and other defects, preheating, quality control and acceptance, visual inspection, non-destructive and destructive testing, the quality-control sheet (*Welding*, printed p. 212). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Technical problems and inspection of arc welds — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: edge preparation, arc striking and holding, welding parameters, arc blow, thermal distortion, cracking (and hydrogen's role), porosity and other defects, preheating, quality control and acceptance, visual inspection, non-destructive and destructive testing, the quality-control sheet. Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Edge preparation, arc striking and holding, welding parameters, arc blow, thermal distortion, cracking (and hydrogen's role), porosity and other defects, preheating, quality control and acceptance, visual inspection, non-destructive and destructive testing, the quality-control sheet. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
