# Arc welding methods

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Arc welding methods
Greece's book teaches it this way: principles of arc welding, filler addition, shielding from the atmosphere, MMA (coated electrodes), MIG/MAG, FCAW, TIG, submerged-arc (SAW) and plasma (PAW); current types and when each is used; welding machines and connections; the welder's hazards and eye/face protection with the mask (*Welding*, printed p. 166). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Arc welding methods — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: principles of arc welding, filler addition, shielding from the atmosphere, MMA (coated electrodes), MIG/MAG, FCAW, TIG, submerged-arc (SAW) and plasma (PAW); current types and when each is used; welding machines and connections; the welder's hazards and eye/face protection with the mask. Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Principles of arc welding, filler addition, shielding from the atmosphere, MMA (coated electrodes), MIG/MAG, FCAW, TIG, submerged-arc (SAW) and plasma (PAW); current types and when each is used; welding machines and connections; the welder's hazards and eye/face protection with the mask. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
