# Lesson plan — Computers as community technology

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Computers as community technology
**Place in the curriculum:** First Steps (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Canada expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a computer is an electronic device that takes in, processes, stores and displays information — and a whole community of jobs, from personal computers to supercomputers, keeps that work going.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student reader — Canada's *Technology in the Community* (Tulliniliara Reading Series, Level 14), Government of Nunavut / Inhabit Education (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **What a computer does** — A computer is an electronic device that works with information in four steps: it takes information in, it processes it, it stores it, and it displays it.
2. **Kinds of computers** — Computers come in different sizes for different jobs.
3. **The jobs that keep computers working** — Computers stay useful because people look after them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of First Steps. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four things a computer does with information.
2. Tell a personal computer, a mainframe and a supercomputer apart.
3. Name three jobs that keep computer systems working.
## What you need
A computer or tablet to look at if one is nearby; otherwise, the pictures in this lesson and a partner to talk with.

1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of three computers side by side growing in size — a small personal computer, a room-sized mainframe and a hall of supercomputer cabinets — the student orders them smallest to biggest and says who each one serves.
