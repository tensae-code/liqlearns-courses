# Computers as community technology

## Do it first
Think of the last time you used a computer, tablet or phone. Tell a partner: what information went in, what the machine did with it, and what it showed you at the end. You have just described a computer at work.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a computer does
A computer is an electronic device that works with information in four steps: it takes information in, it processes it, it stores it, and it displays it. Typing, calculating, saving a photo and showing a page on the screen are those four steps at work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kinds of computers
Computers come in different sizes for different jobs. A personal computer serves one person at a desk. A mainframe serves many people and a whole organisation at once. A supercomputer is the biggest and fastest kind, built for very large problems such as weather forecasting.

## Sub lesson 3 — The jobs that keep computers working
Computers stay useful because people look after them. Software engineers design the programs computers run. Technicians fix and maintain the machines. IT managers organise the whole computer system so everyone can keep working.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a computer is an electronic device that takes in, processes, stores and displays information — and a whole community of jobs, from personal computers to supercomputers, keeps that work going. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
