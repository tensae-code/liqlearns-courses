# Evolution of technical systems

## Do it first
Think of how people have travelled by road: horse cart, steam car, petrol car, electric car. The need never changed — carry people along roads. Say one thing each new generation did better than the one before.

## Sub lesson 1 — Families of objects sharing a function of use
A family is defined by its function of use, not by its shape. All lamps that light a room are one family; all vehicles that carry people by road are another. Reading a family in time order shows the evolution: what stayed the same (the function) and what changed (the technical principles used to fulfil it).

## Sub lesson 2 — The electric vehicle as the running case
The electric vehicle keeps the family function — carry people and goods by road — but changes the energy chain: stored electrical energy, an electric motor, electronic control. Comparing it with the petrol vehicle generation by generation shows evolution clearly: same need, same function of use, new principles. Evolution is driven by needs, by available technology and by constraints such as pollution and cost.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: technical systems evolve in families: objects that share the same function of use form a family, and each generation answers the same need better — the electric vehicle is the running case. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
