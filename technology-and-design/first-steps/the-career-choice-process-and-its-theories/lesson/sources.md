| the career-choice process and its theories: Holland's code theory (people whose chosen occupation matches their personality adapt easily and succeed; personalities and work environments fall into six groups — Technical/Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising/Managerial, Conventional/Operational — most people combining two or three) — finding which personality groups you belong to, then matching the corresponding work environments, grounds the choice; the career-tree theory (the "roots" are personality, interests, abilities and occupational values; the "fruit" is what everyone wants from an occupation — job opportunities, a good work environment, high salary, stable work, being respected; only a choice based on the roots yields the sweet fruit); the Ikigai idea (the ideal occupation where what you love, what you are good at, what you can be paid for and what the world needs meet); and the three steps of choosing — step 1 evaluate yourself (interests, capacity, personality, health, family circumstances, wishes for occupational values; using tests, others' opinions, trying different jobs); step 2 research the labour market (list the occupation groups you care about; find each occupation's job positions, tasks, requirements of qualities and capacity, work environment, development opportunities, income); step 3 decide (compare the results of steps 1 and 2 and choose the most suitable field); the influencing factors — subjective (capacity, interests, personality) and objective (school, family, society, peer group) (original string `the career-choice process and its theories`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 9 — Định hướng nghề nghiệp*, printed p. 24 |
