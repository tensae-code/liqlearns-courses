# Parts and materials of a tool

## Do it first
Pick up a hammer or a similar tool. Point to the part you hold and the part that does the work. Say what each part is made of. This lesson names those parts and explains the choices behind them.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 The parts of a tool
Look closely at a tool and it separates into parts. The handle is the part you grip: it must sit well in the hand. The working edge is the part that meets the material \u2014 the hammer's face, the knife's blade, the saw's teeth. Naming a tool's parts is the first step to understanding how it works and how to care for it.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Why materials are chosen
Materials are not chosen by chance. A hammer blade must be metal, because the working part needs strength and resistance \u2014 wood or plastic would fail at the first blow. Handles are made in wood, plastic or metal, each giving a different grip and weight. When you choose or judge a tool, ask of each part: what material, and why that one?

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a tool has parts \u2014 a handle for grip and a working edge that does the job \u2014 and each part's material is chosen for a reason, like metal for strength. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks \u2014 fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
