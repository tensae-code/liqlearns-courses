# Parts of an object and the function of each part (ballpoint pen)

## Do it first
Take a ballpoint pen you may open. Twist or pull it apart carefully over a table. Lay the pieces in a row and count them. Putting it back together is the second half of the task \u2014 and this lesson's method.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 The pen taken apart
The way to learn an object's parts is to dismantle it and put it back together. The guides do this with ballpoint pens \u2014 ordinary and retractable. Spread out, a pen's elements are: the body, the ink cartridge, the tip support, the ball at the point, the cap, and the end plug. Reassembling the pen proves you found them all: a missing part shows at once.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Each part's function
Each part earns its place by its function. The body is the holder you grip and the case for the rest. The ink cartridge stores the ink. The tip support holds the point in place. The ball rolls as you write, carrying ink onto the paper \u2014 it is the part that gives the ballpoint its name. The cap protects the point and stops ink drying or marking; the end plug closes the body. Part by part, function by function: that is how any object is analysed.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: to know an object, take it apart: the ballpoint pen separates into body, ink cartridge, tip support, ball, cap and end plug \u2014 and each part has its own function. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks \u2014 fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
