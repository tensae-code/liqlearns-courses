# Tools and what they are for

## Do it first
Find three tools where you are. For each one, finish this sentence: \u201cThis tool is for \u2026\u201d If you can, add the trade that uses it most.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Tools across the trades
A trade's tools fit its work. The stonemason shapes and lays stone; the tiler cuts and sets tiles; the leatherworker cuts, pierces and stitches leather. For each tool, the guide asks the same question: what is it used for? A tool's use is the quickest way to know it \u2014 shape follows the job.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Tools from daily life
Tools are not only in workshops. Kitchens, gardens, classrooms and repair corners all hold tools: each one made for a job. Practise naming tools from daily life together with their use \u2014 a tool named with its job is a tool you can choose on purpose, not just pick up.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: every tool is for something: learn a tool by the job it does in its trade, and you can name and choose tools across daily life. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
