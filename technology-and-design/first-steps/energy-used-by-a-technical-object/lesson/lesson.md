# Energy used by a technical object

## Do it first
Look at three working things \u2014 a lamp, a bicycle, a fan or sail if you can see one. For each, say what energy makes it work. If you cannot name it yet, that is the question this lesson answers.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Energy sources \u2014 renewable and non-renewable
Every technical object that works uses energy, and energy comes from sources. Some sources are non-renewable: oil, gas, coal and wood are used up and do not come back quickly. Others are renewable: wind, sunlight and moving water return again and again. Energy is also needed to make objects, not only to run them \u2014 a factory uses energy before the object ever reaches your hands.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Energy changes form
Energy changes from one form into secondary energies: electricity, heat, light and movement. A vehicle burns chemical fuel and moves. A bicycle turns muscular energy into movement. A sailboat turns wind into movement. Naming the change \u2014 what energy goes in, what comes out \u2014 explains how the object works.

## Sub lesson 3 \u2014 The forms of energy objects use
In daily life, objects use different forms of energy: electrical energy runs lamps and machines; light energy from the sun can be caught by solar panels and made into electricity; muscular energy moves bicycles and tools; wind energy moves sailboats and windsocks. For any working object, ask: which form of energy makes it work?

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: technical objects run on energy: energy comes from sources, renewable or not, and changes form \u2014 into electricity, heat, light or movement \u2014 to do the object's work. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
