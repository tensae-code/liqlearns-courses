# Joining and assembly in making

## Do it first
Find a joined corner near you \u2014 a frame, a box, a chair leg. Push it gently sideways. Does it hold square, or does it wobble? What holds it is this lesson's subject.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Assembly brackets hold the angle
Making often means joining: separate parts must become one object that holds its shape. The assembly bracket (\u00e9querre) is a small L-shaped piece fixed across a corner. It keeps the two parts at a right angle \u2014 90 degrees, square and true \u2014 and it strengthens the whole object, because a braced corner cannot easily fold or twist.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Screws and nails fix the parts
Screws and nails are the fixings that hold parts together. In the sieve make, screws and nails fix the mesh to the frame so it cannot pull away in use. A fixing must suit its job: long enough to grip both parts, placed where the strain falls. An object is only as strong as its joinings.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: parts become one object by assembly: brackets (\u00e9querres) hold right angles and strengthen the whole, and screws and nails fix part to part. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
