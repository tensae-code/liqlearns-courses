# The need

## Do it first
Think of the last object you used today — a bag, a lamp, a phone. Say out loud: who uses it, and what did they need it to do? That need, said plainly, is where the product began.

## Sub lesson 1 — The need behind a product
A need is not the object. 'I need light to read after dark' is a need; 'a lamp' is one possible answer. Morocco's course opens here on purpose: the designer first expresses the need and checks it is real — validates it — and only then starts thinking about products. A need that is badly expressed produces a product nobody wanted.

## Sub lesson 2 — Who the product serves — client and user
A client asks for or buys the product; a user lives with it. In a school workshop the teacher may be the client and the pupils the users. A product can also serve an internal client — another service of the same enterprise that needs a part or a tool. For each product, ask: who asked for it, who uses it, and are they the same person? The need is written from their point of view, not the maker's.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: every product exists because of a need: someone — a client or user — needs something done, and the product is the answer; a need must be expressed and validated before anything is designed. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
