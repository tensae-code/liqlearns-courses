# Technology as a system

## Do it first
Pick a system you know — a bicycle, a school gate, a kettle. Say what it is for in one sentence. Now say what goes in and what comes out. You have just begun a functional description of a system.

## Sub lesson 1 — Describing a system — functional and behavioural analysis
To describe a system, start from outside: its function of use — the service it renders to its user. Then follow its behaviour: inputs (energy, material, information), the transformation inside, and outputs. The same system can be described at several levels, and each description must stay true to the need the system answers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Points of view and the life cycle — from need to the Cdcf
The user's point of view names the service expected; the maker's names what can be built; the maintainer's names what must stay repairable. The life cycle runs from the expression of the need through design, making, use and maintenance to the end of life. The Cdcf is the document that turns the need into functions and criteria the finished system will be judged against — it is written before making, and checked after.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a technical system is described, not just looked at: functional and behavioural analysis says what it does and how it behaves, from several points of view, across its life cycle — from the expression of need to the functional specification (Cdcf). Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
