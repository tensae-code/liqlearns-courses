# Eco-design

## Do it first
Look at a made object near you. Ask three questions: What need does it answer? What is it made of? What will happen when it is thrown away? Those three questions, asked at the start, are eco-design.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Designing with impacts in mind
Ordinary design asks: does it work, does it please? Eco-design asks more, from the start: what need does this object truly answer? What materials will it take, and what will become of them? What happens at the end of its life? An object designed with its impacts in mind does less harm at every stage \u2014 because the choices were made before anything was cut.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 The first object \u2014 a prototype
Eco-design still begins with a first object: the prototype. The prototype is the design made real at small cost \u2014 you hold it, test it against the need, and let it teach you what to change. In eco-design the prototype also tests the impact choices: can it be repaired, can its materials be recovered? A prototype that answers those questions saves both material and mistakes.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: eco-design thinks about an object's impacts from the very start: the need it answers, the materials chosen, and a first object \u2014 a prototype \u2014 to learn from. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
