# Lesson plan — GPS technology

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** GPS technology
**Place in the curriculum:** First Steps (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Canada expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** GPS finds where you are by joining three parts — satellites in orbit sending signals, ground stations tracking them, and a portable device listening to several satellites at once.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student reader — Canada's *Technology in the Community* (Tulliniliara Reading Series, Level 14), Government of Nunavut / Inhabit Education (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The three parts of GPS** — The Global Positioning System, GPS for short, has three parts that work together.
2. **How the device finds your place** — One satellite signal is not enough.
3. **Who uses GPS** — Many northern and outdoor workers rely on GPS: hunters, boaters, pilots, fishers and prospectors use it to travel and work safely, and the coast guard uses it to find and help people on the water.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of First Steps. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three parts of the GPS system.
2. Explain why a GPS device needs signals from several satellites.
3. Name people who use GPS in their work and travel.
## What you need
Nothing special — a partner to talk with; a GPS device or phone map to look at if one is nearby.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of the three GPS parts in one scene — satellites above the Earth sending signal lines down, a ground station tracking them, and a traveller holding a small GPS device — the student points to each part and says its job.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of signal lines arriving at a handheld device from one satellite, then two, then four, with the position dot becoming sure only when several signals arrive — the student counts the satellites.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the three parts aloud — satellites, ground stations, portable devices — the student repeats them.
   - ADD VIDEO: a short video of the lesson demonstration — see video-script.md.

4. **Questions** — the questions/ files, spread over the sub lessons.
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block in lesson/lesson.md.
