# GPS technology

## Do it first
Imagine you are out on the land or the water, far from any road. How would you know exactly where you are, and how to get back? Tell a partner your ideas. Then learn how GPS answers that question from space.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three parts of GPS
The Global Positioning System, GPS for short, has three parts that work together. Satellites orbit the Earth and send out signals. Ground stations on Earth track those satellites and keep the system accurate. A portable GPS device in your hand receives the signals and works out where you are.

## Sub lesson 2 — How the device finds your place
One satellite signal is not enough. The device listens to signals from several satellites at the same time and compares them; where the signals agree, that is your position. That is why GPS can guide a traveller across open land or water where there are no roads or signs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Who uses GPS
Many northern and outdoor workers rely on GPS: hunters, boaters, pilots, fishers and prospectors use it to travel and work safely, and the coast guard uses it to find and help people on the water.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: GPS finds where you are by joining three parts — satellites in orbit sending signals, ground stations tracking them, and a portable device listening to several satellites at once. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
