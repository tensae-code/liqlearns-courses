# Making a technical object

## Do it first
Name a made object in the room. Now retell its birth in four beats: what was gathered, how the workplace was prepared, how it was made, how it was finished. That four-beat run is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Gather, prepare, make, finish
A technical object is made in a run of stages: gather the materials and tools, prepare the workplace, make the object step by step, and finish it \u2014 edges smoothed, joints checked, the object tested at its job. The run is the same whatever the object; what changes is the object itself. Two makes in the guides carry the whole run.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 The sieve (tamis)
The sieve (tamis) is made of a mesh held in a frame. Sieves work in the kitchen, sifting flour; in masonry, grading sand; and as filters wherever fine and coarse must part. Making one teaches the run plainly: the frame must be true, the mesh fixed firmly to it, and the finished sieve tested by sifting.

## Sub lesson 3 \u2014 The checkerboard (damier)
The checkerboard (damier) begins with its study phase \u2014 need, function, dimensions, materials, steps \u2014 then gathering, preparing the workplace, and making through to finishing. The board is plywood or cardboard, worked with ruler and set square so the squares are true; the pieces are cut from cork. A game you can play is the proof the make succeeded.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: making a technical object runs gather, prepare, make, finish \u2014 taught whole on two makes: a sieve (tamis) and a checkerboard (damier). Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks \u2014 fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
