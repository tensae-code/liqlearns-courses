# Representing a technical object in its environment, with a written description

## Do it first
Sketch a bicycle alone on the page. Now sketch it again with a rider, a road and a hill. Write two things the second drawing tells you that the first could not.

## Sub lesson 1 — The object in its environment
A tool drawn alone on a blank page shows its shape and hides its work. Haiti's sequence asks each team to represent a mechanised tool in its context of use — the plough in the field, with soil, worker and draft power around it. The environment is information: it shows scale, the ground it works, and what the tool acts on. Representation means the object plus its world.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing the descriptif
Beside the representation, each team writes the tool's descriptif: what it is, its parts, its function of use, how it is worked. Words carry what a picture cannot — names, purposes, order of operations. The descriptif is not a caption; it is the second half of the representation, written to be read with the drawing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Checking the pair — drawing and words together
The finished work is judged as a pair: could a reader who has never seen the tool understand, from the representation and the descriptif together, what the tool is, where it works and what it does? Anything the drawing shows that the words contradict — or the words claim that the drawing hides — is a fault to fix. This is technical communication in its simplest complete form.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a technical object is fully represented when it is shown in its environment of use together with a written description (descriptif) — the drawing shows where and how it works, the words say what it is. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
