# From writing to printing

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — From writing to printing
Greece's book teaches it this way: early scripts (cuneiform, hieroglyphic, Cretan-Minoan, the alphabet), reproduction of texts in the East, copying in Europe, and Gutenberg — movable type, the press and inks (*Printing Tech*, printed p. 7). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — From writing to printing — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: early scripts (cuneiform, hieroglyphic, Cretan-Minoan, the alphabet), reproduction of texts in the East, copying in Europe, and Gutenberg — movable type, the press and inks. Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Early scripts (cuneiform, hieroglyphic, Cretan-Minoan, the alphabet), reproduction of texts in the East, copying in Europe, and Gutenberg — movable type, the press and inks. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
