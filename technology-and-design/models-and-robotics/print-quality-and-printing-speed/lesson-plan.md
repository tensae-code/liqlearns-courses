# Lesson plan — Print quality and printing speed

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Models and Robotics · **Lesson:** Print quality and printing speed
**Place in the curriculum:** Models and Robotics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Kuwait expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** layer height and print speed pull in opposite directions: thin layers and slower printing give a finer finish and take longer, while thick layers and faster printing finish sooner with a rougher surface — the maker chooses the trade for each job.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student book — Kuwait's *الذكاء الرقمي 8* (*Digital Intelligence, Grade 8, Term 2*), Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Layer height and surface quality** — Layer height is the thickness of each printed layer.
2. **Print speed** — Print speed is how fast the printer head moves as it lays material down.
3. **Trading quality against speed** — Every print chooses a balance.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Models and Robotics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how layer height changes surface quality.
2. Say what print speed changes, and what too much speed can spoil.
3. Choose quality or speed settings for a named job and give the reason.
## What you need
No materials are needed; if available, two printed samples of the same shape at different layer heights to compare.

1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of two printed blocks of the same shape side by side — one with fine, smooth layers and one with thick, ridged layers, with a clock beside each — the student says which block used thin layers and which took longer.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a GIF of a printer head moving slowly and laying a fine line, then moving fast and laying a thick line — the student names the setting that changed each time.
