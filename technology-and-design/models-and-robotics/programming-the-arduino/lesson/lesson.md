# Programming the Arduino

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Mongolia's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Programming the Arduino
Mongolia's book teaches it this way (*Design Drawing & Technology 12 (Mongolia)*, printed p. 113): the board's anatomy (14 digital pins, 6 PWM, 6 analogue inputs, 7–12 V supply, 40 mA per pin), installing the IDE, and the Blink program — pinMode(13, OUTPUT), digitalWrite, delay(1000). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Programming the Arduino — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: the board's anatomy (14 digital pins, 6 PWM, 6 analogue inputs, 7–12 V supply, 40 mA per pin), installing the IDE, and the Blink program — pinMode(13, OUTPUT), digitalWrite, delay(1000). Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Mongolia's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Programming the Arduino — the board's anatomy (14 digital pins, 6 PWM, 6 analogue inputs, 7–12 V supply, 40 mA per pin), installing the IDE, and the Blink program — pinMode(13, OUTPUT), digitalWrite, delay(1000). Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
