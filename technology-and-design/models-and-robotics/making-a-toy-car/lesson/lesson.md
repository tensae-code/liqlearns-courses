# Making a toy car

## Do it first
Plan a paper toy car on one sheet: draw its body, mark where the two axle holes go, and write the order of making — cut, bore, assemble, adjust, finish — beside it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cutting and boring to dimension
Making starts from the working drawing: lengths and widths are measured and marked, then cut to dimension — no bigger, no smaller. Holes are bored to the right diameter: an axle hole too tight will not turn; too loose, the wheel wobbles. Jamaica's Grade 4 project 'Keep the Wheels Turning' teaches cutting and boring as measured operations, not guesses.

## Sub lesson 2 — Assembling to specification, and adjusting
Assembly follows the plan's order and its specification: this part first, fixed this way, to this size. Then the maker tests and adjusts — Jamaica's own example is trimming the axle length so the wheels sit right and turn freely. Adjusting is normal making, not failure: the specification is the judge, and small corrections are how real work meets it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Finishing, safely
A finish protects the material and completes the design: paint or a wrapping, chosen for the material and applied evenly. Every stage of the project — cutting, boring, assembling, finishing — is done observing the safety considerations for its tools and materials. The finished car is then tested for accuracy and workability: does it match the plan, and does it run?

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a toy car is made in order: cut the material to dimension, bore the holes to the right diameter, assemble the components to specification, adjust until it runs true, and finish it safely. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
