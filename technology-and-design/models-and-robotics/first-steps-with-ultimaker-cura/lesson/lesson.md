# First steps with UltiMaker Cura

## Do it first
Look at a finished 3D model on screen (or in a picture). Ask: could a printer build this in one step? It cannot — it builds in thin layers. The program that cuts the model into those layers is the slicer, and this lesson opens it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a slicer does
A slicer is the bridge between the model and the printer. It cuts the 3D model into hundreds of thin horizontal layers, works out the path the printer head must travel for each layer, and writes the whole job as instructions the printer can follow. No slicer, no print: the printer cannot read the model on its own.

## Sub lesson 2 — Importing a 3D model into Cura
The first step in UltiMaker Cura is to import the model file: open it, and the model appears standing on Cura's virtual printer bed. Check it there — is it the right size, standing the right way up, and sitting flat on the bed? A model floating above the bed, or lying at the wrong size, is fixed now, on screen, not after printing has started.

## Sub lesson 3 — Preparing the model for printing
With the model placed, choose the print settings for the job and let Cura slice it. Then preview the result layer by layer: watch how the printer will build it, check the estimated time and material, and only then save or send the sliced file to the printer. Preparing well is what makes the first real layer match the model you saw.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: before a printer can build anything, a slicer program — UltiMaker Cura — must turn the 3D model into thin layers and printer instructions; this lesson covers importing a model into Cura and preparing it for printing. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
