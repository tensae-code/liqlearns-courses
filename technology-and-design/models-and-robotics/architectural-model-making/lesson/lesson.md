# Architectural model making

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Materials, tools and exact dimensions
The model's materials are light and cuttable: foam sheet (یونولیت), woods and card, cut with tools including the hot-wire cutter that slices foam cleanly. Before cutting, exact dimensions are taken from the architectural drawings and scaled — the model is only as true as its measurements. Iran's Grade 8 architecture module surveys the materials and tools and teaches the measuring first.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building to a scale drawing: the mosque at 1:50
The worked model is a mosque at 1:50. Plan and elevations are transferred to foam, walls are cut and joined, and a foam dome is formed; the stages continue with a flat roof, coverings for the openings, and painting — done before final assembly, while the parts are still reachable. Six stages carry the model from drawing to finished building.

## Sub lesson 3 — A city and traffic model
The second project widens the scale: a city/traffic model begins from a street-network plan, its parts — streets, blocks, buildings — are made separately, and the final assembly (اتصال) joins them in their places. The city model teaches what the single building cannot: how buildings sit together in a planned whole.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: an architectural model is a building at scale: its materials are chosen and cut by exact dimensions taken from the drawings — Iran's module builds a mosque at 1:50 and a whole city quarter in miniature. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
