# Building a walking robot

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Mongolia's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Building a walking robot
Mongolia's book teaches it this way (*Design Technology 8 (Mongolia)*, printed p. 90): parts from 5×18×225 mm slats with Ø3 holes, assembling the frame in axonometric order, fitting wheels and the crank mechanism, and wiring its electric motor circuit. The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one. Mongolia's *Design Drawing & Technology 11 (Mongolia)* adds (printed p. 136): a walking robot — setting requirements, drawing its kinematic scheme with the standard symbols (motor, pulley, bearing, crank), building the trajectory and evaluating against a 5-point rubric Mongolia's *Design Drawing & Technology 11 (Mongolia)* adds (printed p. 163): its working drawing at true size (67 mm frame, Ø3 and Ø1.8 holes), choosing gear or belt drive, mounting the battery motor, and testing the step length

## Sub lesson 2 — Building a walking robot — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: parts from 5×18×225 mm slats with Ø3 holes, assembling the frame in axonometric order, fitting wheels and the crank mechanism, and wiring its electric motor circuit. Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Mongolia's book shows.
