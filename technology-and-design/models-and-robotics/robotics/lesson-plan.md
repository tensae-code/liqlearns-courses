# Lesson plan — Robotics — how robots are built and move

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Models and Robotics · **Lesson:** Robotics — how robots are built and move
**Place in the curriculum:** Models and Robotics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Moldova expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a robot stands on history and laws — automata, Jaquet-Droz, the first programmable industrial robot, Asimov's three laws — and is built as chassis and modules, gear or worm transmissions, and a locomotion system — wheels, tracks, legs or belts — fitted to its surfaces.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Moldova's *Educație tehnologică* (Technological Education) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Moldova counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **What a robot is — history and laws** — Grade 9 robotics opens historically: the automata of the past — Jaquet-Droz's mechanical figures among them — the first programmable industrial robot, and the first computer, the line that leads to the modern robot.
2. **How robots are built and move** — Construction follows: robot models are built on a chassis or housing that holds the components and modules; movement is produced by transmissions with gear wheels or worm drives; and locomotion is chosen for the ground — wheels, tracks, legs, or conveyor belts — each system fitted to the surfaces it must cross..

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Outline the history of automata and robots and state Asimov's laws' place.
2. Describe how robots are built and how their locomotion fits the surface.
3. Explain how Moldova's book teaches it.
