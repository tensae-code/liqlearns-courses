# Robotics — how robots are built and move

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Moldova's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a robot is — history and laws
Grade 9 robotics opens historically: the automata of the past — Jaquet-Droz's mechanical figures among them — the first programmable industrial robot, and the first computer, the line that leads to the modern robot. Over the history stand Asimov's three laws of robotics: the famous rules imagined for how robots must behave toward humans.

## Sub lesson 2 — How robots are built and move
Construction follows: robot models are built on a chassis or housing that holds the components and modules; movement is produced by transmissions with gear wheels or worm drives; and locomotion is chosen for the ground — wheels, tracks, legs, or conveyor belts — each system fitted to the surfaces it must cross.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a robot stands on history and laws — automata, Jaquet-Droz, the first programmable industrial robot, Asimov's three laws — and is built as chassis and modules, gear or worm transmissions, and a locomotion system — wheels, tracks, legs or belts — fitted to its surfaces. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
