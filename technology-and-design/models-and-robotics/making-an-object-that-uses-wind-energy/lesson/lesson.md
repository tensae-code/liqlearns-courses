# Making an object that uses wind energy

## Do it first
Wet a finger and hold it up, or watch washing on a line: which way does the air move, and how strongly? A windsock answers both questions at a glance \u2014 this lesson builds one.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 What a windsock shows
Some objects work by using the wind's energy instead of fighting it. The windsock (manche \u00e0 air) is a long open cone of light material on a pole. Wind enters its wide mouth and streams it out: the sock points downwind, showing the wind's direction, and the more fully it flies and lifts, the stronger the wind \u2014 its strength made visible. Airfields use windsocks so pilots can read the wind at a glance.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Building the windsock
The classroom windsock is built from plastic bags for the light flying body, wooden sticks for its frame and support, and bottles in its construction, following the making steps: form the sock, fix it so it can swing freely to face the wind, and mount it where air moves. The test is outdoors: does it point with the wind, and does it fly fuller as the wind strengthens?

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a windsock (manche \u00e0 air) uses wind energy to show the wind's direction and strength \u2014 built from plastic bags, wooden sticks and bottles, as at airfields. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
