# Making a balloon-powered car

## Do it first
Blow up a balloon and release it without tying. Watch its wild flight. Now imagine that same push tamed by wheels and a body, aimed along the floor \u2014 that is the car you will build.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 A car pushed by air
Blow up a balloon and let it go: it darts away as its air rushes out. The rushing air pushes one way, and the balloon is pushed the other \u2014 that push-back is reaction. A balloon-powered car fixes the balloon to a body so the push-back drives the car forward along the ground instead of darting through the air.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Building the car
The car is built from cardboard for the body, straws as tubes and axle guides, bottle caps for wheels, wooden sticks for axles, and tape to fix it all, with the balloon as the engine. Blow through the straw to fill the balloon, pinch, set the car down and let go: the air escapes through the straw and the car rolls. Straight wheels and a centred balloon make a straight run \u2014 the run tests the build.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a car can be pushed by reaction: air rushing out of a balloon drives a cardboard car on bottle-cap wheels \u2014 propulsion you build and watch. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks \u2014 fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
