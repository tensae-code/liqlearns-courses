# Lesson plan — Making the model stick to the printer bed

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Models and Robotics · **Lesson:** Making the model stick to the printer bed
**Place in the curriculum:** Models and Robotics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Kuwait expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a print only succeeds if its first layers stick firmly to the printer bed — bed adhesion — because a model that lifts or detaches early ruins the whole print, however good its later settings are.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student book — Kuwait's *الذكاء الرقمي 8* (*Digital Intelligence, Grade 8, Term 2*), Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why first layers detach** — The first layer is the model's foundation.
2. **How adhesion is secured** — Adhesion is secured before and during the first layer.
3. **Checking the first layer** — Watch the first layer go down and judge it: lines joined evenly, no gaps, no lifting edges.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Models and Robotics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why the first layer decides whether a print succeeds.
2. Name ways adhesion is secured.
3. Say what a good first layer looks like, and what to do about a bad one.
## What you need
No materials are needed; if available, a printer bed (or a picture of one) and a failed print that lifted at a corner.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of a printer bed with a model's first layer laid evenly across it, one corner of a second, failed model curling up beside it — the student points to the flat layer and the curled corner and says which print will succeed.
