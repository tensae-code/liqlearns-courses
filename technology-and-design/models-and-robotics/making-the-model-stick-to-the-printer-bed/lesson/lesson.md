# Making the model stick to the printer bed

## Do it first
Look at a failed print with one corner curled up (or a picture of one). The top layers may be neat — but the print still failed, at the very bottom. This lesson is about that first grip on the bed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why first layers detach
The first layer is the model's foundation. If it does not grip the bed, the model shifts, lifts at the corners as it cools, or comes loose completely while the printer keeps printing into empty air. Heat changes, a dirty or uneven bed, and a first layer laid too far from the bed all weaken that grip.

## Sub lesson 2 — How adhesion is secured
Adhesion is secured before and during the first layer. The bed is clean and level, so the first line is pressed evenly onto it everywhere. The first layer is printed slowly and close, giving it time and contact to grip. Where a model still needs help, adhesion helpers — a wider first-layer skirt of extra lines around the base (a brim), or a flat raft under the whole model — give it more holding area.

## Sub lesson 3 — Checking the first layer
Watch the first layer go down and judge it: lines joined evenly, no gaps, no lifting edges. A bad first layer will not heal itself in later layers, so the honest move is to stop, fix the cause, and start again. Two minutes of checking saves a whole print.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a print only succeeds if its first layers stick firmly to the printer bed — bed adhesion — because a model that lifts or detaches early ruins the whole print, however good its later settings are. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
