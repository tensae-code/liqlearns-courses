| technique for rearing pet birds: the cage (sized so the bird can fly and move short distances — too small stresses it and sheds feathers, too large makes it wild and hard to tame; wooden and bamboo cages are hard to clean and harbour disease, metal cages are better — stainless steel or electrostatic-painted iron; new birds are gradually accustomed to the cage), the accessories (perches of bamboo, wood or natural branches — two perches per cage at different heights; feed and water cups, preferably automatic ones to save daily changing and reduce spillage; a tray lined with thin sand, newspaper or absorbent paper, cleaned and disinfected periodically), feeding (natural food — insects and fruit, varying by age and season; or ready-made feed fitted to the species and age with protein, minerals, vitamins and calcium; feed only the day's amount; extras such as ant eggs, termites, crickets, beef or bananas by species), choosing birds (healthy, quick, bright-eyed, clean nostrils and vent), care (quarantine a new bird separately for about 2 weeks; hang the cage in an airy place away from draughts, rain and direct sun, no higher than head height; bathe the bird regularly and sun the cage 15–30 minutes to raise metabolism; cover the cage during moulting), and disease prevention (hygienic feed and water, an airy but not draughty cage position, timely treatment with the right medicine and dose) (original string `technique for rearing pet birds`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 11 — Chuyên đề học tập: Công nghệ chăn nuôi*, printed p. 37 |
