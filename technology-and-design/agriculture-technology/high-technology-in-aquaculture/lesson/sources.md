| high technology in aquaculture — the recirculating aquaculture system (RAS) and Biofloc: RAS is the technology of farming by reusing the water source, most of the waste water after farming being treated and returned to the farming system in a closed cycle; its advantages (high yield, water saving, biosecurity, limiting environmental pollution and pathogen entry into the system, and food-safety control) and disadvantages (only suited to high-value species; high initial investment; energy cost for operation — electricity — and skilled operators needed); its five component tanks and working principle (1: the farming tank; 2: the mechanical filter tank, where the water from the farming tank is filtered by a drum filter and most solid waste is retained and removed; the water then goes to tank 3: the dissolved-waste holding tank; from there it is pumped to tank 4: the biological filter tank, rich in bacteria, where the toxic substances dissolved in the water (such as H₂S, NO₂⁻, NH₃, …) are converted by microorganisms into non-toxic substances, and dissolved oxygen is added by an oxygen-generating device and the pH adjusted so the water meets the farmed animals' requirements; the water then flows down to tank 5: the treated clean-water holding tank before being returned to tank 1) — RAS is usually applied to high-economic-value species (sturgeon, salmon, sturgeon-like cá tầm, lobster shrimp, …) in places short of clean water or with limited farming area, and combined with hydroponic vegetable growing it forms the Aquaponic system, drawn with the bacteria converting waste to NO₃⁻ feeding the vegetable beds above the fish tanks; and Biofloc technology (using communities of bacteria, algae, protozoa, … organised together in suspended floc particles in the water to improve water quality, treat waste and prevent disease in intensive farming systems; carbon is supplied continuously at a high level so the C/N ratio in the system fluctuates from 10/1 to 20/1, creating conditions for heterotrophic bacteria to develop and convert the organic substances in the farming water (leftover feed, faeces, microorganisms, …) into their biomass — the biofloc particles — which also serve as supplementary feed for the farmed animals while maintaining water quality; its advantages (blocking pathogen entry, improving biosecurity, raising water-use efficiency, improving feed conversion, raising yield and economic efficiency — for example whiteleg shrimp farmed by Biofloc reaches 95% survival, feed cost down 30% against traditional farming, shrimp yield about 34–40 tonnes/ha) and disadvantages (high initial investment; the aeration system must run continuously so a stable power source is needed; high energy consumption; the farmer needs knowledge and training); Biofloc suits species able to tolerate high suspended solids and digest protein from Biofloc, such as shrimp and tilapia, and is applied successfully in Vietnam to whiteleg shrimp and tilapia (original string `high technology in aquaculture`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 105 |
