| the technique of nursing shrimp seed in tanks: prepare the nursing tank (cement, composite or tarpaulin-lined tanks of 4–8 m³; clean and disinfect with chlorine or iodine at suitable dose and rinse with clean water; the water used for larval nursing is treated to the correct process — for marine shrimp, salinity from 28‰ to 30‰), select and stock the larvae (healthy larvae with phototaxis, produced at seed farms meeting the regulated conditions; nursing seasons — the North from March to November, the South all year; stocking density 150–250 larvae per litre for black tiger shrimp and 200–250 per litre for whiteleg shrimp; before stocking, bathe the larvae in formalin solution at 200 ppm concentration for 30 seconds; release them gradually to acclimatise to the tank water, the transport water and tank water differing by no more than 0.5 °C and 1‰ salinity), care and manage (the feed type and amount differ by larval metamorphosis stage — in the first 1.5–2 days, at the Nauplius stage, no feeding; in the following four days, the Zoea stage, feed live algae or dried algae at 4 times/day with artificial feed at 0.5–1 g/m³ per time alternating; in the next three days, the Mysis stage, suitable artificial feed at 4 times/day at 1–1.5 g/m³ per time, combined with live feed; in the Postlarvae stage, suitable artificial feed 4 times/day at 1.5–2 g/m³ per time combined with newly hatched Artemia at 3–4 g/m³ per time, 4 times/day; monitor the environment and larval health regularly — during nursing, at the Nauplius and Zoea stages the water is not changed, and from the Mysis stage part or all of the water may be changed depending on the nursing system), and harvest (harvest when the postlarvae are about 12 days old for whiteleg shrimp or about 15 days for black tiger shrimp; when harvesting, drain part of the tank water and use suitable tools to transfer the shrimp seed so as to limit damage) — the larval development after hatching runs through the Nauplius (3 sub-stages), Zoea (3 sub-stages) and Mysis (3 sub-stages) stages over about 9–10 days before becoming Postlarvae (original string `the technique of nursing shrimp seed in tanks`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 74 |
