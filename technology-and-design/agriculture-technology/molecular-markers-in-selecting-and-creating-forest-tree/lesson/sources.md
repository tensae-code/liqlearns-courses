| molecular markers in selecting and creating forest-tree varieties: most forest trees are long-lived, flowering and fruiting late and taking many years to harvest, so traditional variety selection and creation takes many years and much labour and cost; molecular markers let breeders identify the individuals carrying the gene for the desired trait right at the seedling stage, greatly shortening selection time and reducing cost and labour — the comparison diagram shows traditional creation (father tree × mother tree → offspring → grown tree → select by phenotype → tree carrying the desired trait) against marker-assisted creation (the same cross, but the offspring are selected by molecular marker → the selected carry the desired trait); the markers commonly applied in forest-tree selection are SSR (simple sequence repeat) markers; achievements in Vietnam: using SSR markers, 7 hybrid acacia lines (BB055, BV350, BV376, BV434, BV523, BV584 and BV586) reaching yields of 26–35 m³/ha/year and 10 fast-growing eucalyptus lines (UC16, UC51, UC113, CU123, UC52, CU182, UE72, UC55, CU98, CU82) reaching 30.7–45 m³/ha/year were selected, of which 7 acacia hybrid lines and 2 eucalyptus lines (CU98 and CU82) have been recognised as new forest-tree varieties for widespread production (original string `molecular markers in selecting and creating forest-tree varieties`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 9 |
