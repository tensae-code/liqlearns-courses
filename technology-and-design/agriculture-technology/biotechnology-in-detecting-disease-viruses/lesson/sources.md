| Lesson part | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| biotechnology in early detection of disease viruses: detecting a virus within 2–3 days of infection, before it multiplies enough to cause disease (traditional methods only detect it after the incubation period) — the process for the H5N1 avian-influenza virus in five steps (take the disease sample, extract the total RNA, synthesise cDNA from the RNA by reverse transcription, amplify the cDNA by PCR, and check the PCR product by agarose gel electrophoresis), raising the effectiveness of prevention and limiting outbreaks and losses (original string `biotechnology in early detection of disease viruses`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 11 — Công nghệ chăn nuôi*, printed p. 75 |
| biotechnology in detecting disease viruses, for DNA viruses: the detection process in four steps (take the disease sample, extract the total DNA, amplify the virus's characteristic gene segment by PCR, check the PCR product by electrophoresis) — fast and highly accurate, though it needs specialised machines, skilled technicians and fairly high cost (original string `biotechnology in detecting disease viruses, for DNA viruses`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 11 — Chuyên đề học tập: Công nghệ chăn nuôi*, printed p. 21 |
| biotechnology in diagnosing aquatic diseases early: the PCR technique (widely applied to detect disease agents early and accurately, especially viruses — yellow-head virus and white-spot virus in shrimp, herpesvirus in koi, haemorrhagic-disease virus in grass carp, …; the process of detecting a DNA virus causing aquatic disease in four steps: step 1: collect the aquatic sample; step 2: extract the total DNA; step 3: amplify the pathogen's specific gene segment by the PCR reaction; step 4: run electrophoresis and examine the PCR product — with fluorescence technology now improving PCR into real-time PCR, which both determines qualitatively whether the sample is infected and quantifies the target gene segment in the test sample) and rapid diagnostic kits (diagnostic tools integrating the components needed to test a sample qualitatively, giving fast results and easy to perform in the field; some kits are applied in diagnosing aquatic diseases such as the nervous-necrosis kit for marine fish, the white-spot kit for shrimp and the yellow-head kit for the giant river prawn; the kit diagnosis process in six pictured steps: 1 collect the aquatic sample, 2 add buffer solution, 3 grind the sample, 4 draw the sample solution, 5 put the sample into the diagnostic kit, 6 read the result after 15 minutes — positive and negative results shown on the test strip) (original string `biotechnology in diagnosing aquatic diseases early`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 128 |
