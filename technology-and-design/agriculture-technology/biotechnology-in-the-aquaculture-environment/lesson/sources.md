| biotechnology in treating the aquaculture environment: in treating organic waste (selecting bacterial strains safe for the farmed animals that decompose the organic waste in the water, multiplying them and making microbial preparations to add to the farming environment — the strains commonly added are Bacillus subtilis, Bacillus licheniformis, Lactobacillus acidophilus, Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Rhodopseudomonas palustris; when the waste source is decomposed the microorganisms' own feed source is limited and their growth falls; and extracting enzymes able to decompose organic matter — amylase, protease, cellulase — and adding them to the environment to treat organic substances), in treating toxic gases (selecting microorganism strains able to decompose the toxic gases in the farming water (NH₃, NO₂, H₂S, …), multiplying them in large quantity and adding them to the environment; the two bacterial groups commonly joining the nitrogen conversion in water are Nitrosomonas spp. and Nitrobacter spp., and the nitrogen conversion process is drawn as a cycle: fish feed → fish waste → ammonia (NH₃), toxic to fish → converted by Nitrosomonas bacteria to nitrite (NO₂⁻), also toxic → converted by Nitrobacter bacteria to nitrate (NO₃⁻), not toxic at under 50 mg/L → absorbed by aquatic plants, and removed by water changes), and in treating harmful microorganisms (isolating and selecting microorganisms antagonistic to the pathogens in the farming water and adding them to destroy or inhibit the pathogens; the beneficial microorganisms used include probiotic bacteria such as Bacillus spp., Enterococcus spp. and Lactobacillus spp., and Streptomyces-group bacteria producing antibiotics are the most widely used) (original string `biotechnology in treating the aquaculture environment`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 63 |
| biotechnology in managing the aquaculture water environment: producing microbial preparations that clean the farming water (thanks to biotechnology, scientists created microbial preparations able to clean the farming water and pond bottom, with strains of clear origin and stable properties, … helping clean the water and pond bottom, balance the pond microflora, reduce harmful microorganisms, create favourable conditions for the farmed animals' growth and development, and besides, some microbial preparations can convert the toxic gases (NH₃, H₂S, NO₂) in the pond into non-toxic substances (NH₄⁺, NO₃⁻); the microorganism strains commonly used to produce water-cleaning preparations include Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Lactobacillus acidophilus, Bacillus subtilis, Bacillus licheniformis, Nitrosomonas sp. and Rhodopseudomonas palustris; the preparations can be used directly by dissolving them in water and splashing evenly over the pond surface, or multiplied by adding molasses and rice bran and incubating before use; the production process in four steps: isolate and select beneficial microorganism strains able to decompose organic matter quickly in the farming water; multiply and collect the biomass; mix the microorganism strains with carrier in suitable proportions to create the preparation; check, package, preserve and use), and managing farming-water quality with Biofloc technology (Biofloc uses communities of bacteria, algae, protozoa, … organised together in suspended floc particles in the water to improve water quality, treat waste and prevent disease in intensive farming systems; the microorganisms in the biofloc particles, especially heterotrophic bacteria, absorb nitrogen compounds and convert the organic matter in the farming water into protein biomass as feed for the farmed animals, and some microorganisms in the biofloc can inhibit some pathogens of aquatic animals, so the water source in the system is always quality-assured; the Biofloc system suits species able to tolerate high suspended-solids concentration in the water such as pangasius, carp, tilapia, cá măng biển, cá nâu, grouper, whiteleg shrimp, black tiger shrimp, …; the working principle drawn: leftover feed, faeces and the animals' urine raise ammonia (NH₃) in the water → Nitrosomonas and Nitrosomus bacteria convert ammonia to nitrite (NO₂⁻) → Nitrobacter and Nitrospira bacteria convert nitrite to nitrate (NO₃⁻) → denitrifying bacteria (Pseudomonas, Achromobacter, …) convert nitrate to nitrogen gas (N₂); meanwhile bacteria use carbon, nitrogen and oxygen to develop into large populations (protein-rich communities) that clump into suspended flocs (biofloc particles) forming feed for shrimp and fish, with extra carbon supplied continuously and the carbon/nitrogen ratio kept at 10 to 20/1; Biofloc is widely applied in the shrimp industry, helping shrimp farmers raise survival and growth and raise yield, and is expanding to tilapia models in Vietnam; to apply Biofloc the ponds must be tarpaulin-lined or cement, with aeration systems; it is not applied to earthen ponds, so the initial investment cost is large) (original string `biotechnology in managing the aquaculture water environment`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 34 |
