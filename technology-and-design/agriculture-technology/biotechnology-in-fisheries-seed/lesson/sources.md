| biotechnology in selecting aquatic seed — molecular markers: marker-assisted selection identifies the individuals carrying desired genes (genes deciding fast growth, disease resistance, …) right at an early development stage, shortening the selection time and raising labour efficiency — applied in Vietnam through molecular markers to select pangasius broodstock able to tolerate salinity and grow fast, and whiteleg shrimp lines with fast growth — with the comparison diagram of selecting shrimp broodstock by the traditional method (observe morphology → select individuals with the desired traits as broodstock) against the molecular-marker method (select by molecular marker → the selected individuals carry the desired trait's gene as broodstock) (original string `biotechnology in selecting aquatic seed`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 76 |
| biotechnology in multiplying aquatic seed: using reproductive stimulants (hormones of animal origin or synthetic substances, used in artificial fish reproduction; injecting stimulant into sexually mature fish stimulates the development of eggs and sperm and induces spawning — the common stimulants in seed production are LRHa, HCG, PG and GnRHa, used singly or in combination; the worked scheme for pangasius: the female receives priming injections of HCG one to three times, each dose 10–24 hours apart, then the decisive injection after 10–24 hours, and eggs are stripped 10–24 hours later; the male is injected with HCG, and sperm is stripped for artificial fertilisation), controlling the sex of aquatic animals (in some species males and females differ in appearance or growth rate — male tilapia grow faster than females, female giant river prawns are smaller — so sex control aims production at the more profitable sex; the most common method feeds the animals a sex-reversing hormone such as oestrogen (to females) or testosterone (to males); in masculinising tilapia, the male-determining hormone 17α-methyltestosterone is mixed evenly into the feed and fed right after the fry have used up the yolk sac, for 21 consecutive days, giving 85–95% males in the stock), and cold preservation of sperm (cryopreservation gives control of the seed-production process, especially where there is sex-phase mismatch in artificial reproduction, eases transport compared with moving live males, and limits genetic degradation from inbreeding; short-term preservation in four steps — collect sperm, dilute it with a preservative, add antibiotic, store in a refrigerator at 0–4 °C, lasting from hours to a month; long-term preservation in liquid nitrogen in five steps — collect sperm by gently stroking the lower abdomen towards the genital opening (the sperm is milky white and free of impurities); check sperm motility by diluting with distilled water or isotonic solution and examining under the microscope (a sample with motility above 50% meets the standard); mix the sperm with preservative and cryoprotectant and put the mixture into storage straws; cool the straws in liquid-nitrogen vapour at about −76 °C (nitrogen surface 1–5 cm) for 3–25 minutes by species; put the straws into liquid nitrogen at −196 °C for storage, which can exceed three months) (original string `biotechnology in multiplying aquatic seed`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 77 |
| biotechnology in producing aquatic seed: using hormones to control broodstock maturation and spawning (some hormones stimulate the development of the animals' gonads (also called spawning stimulants); using them helps males and females mature sexually at a defined time, making seed production proactive about timing; hormones also stimulate spawning synchronously, raising the spawning rate, fertilisation rate and hatching rate against natural spawning without hormones; the commonly used hormones are HCG and LRHa — with the worked diagram for carp seed production: the female receives stimulant injection 1, then stimulant injection 2 after 6–8 hours, and the eggs are stripped; the male receives the stimulant injection and the sperm is stripped; then artificial fertilisation), cold preservation (cryopreservation) of sperm, eggs and embryos (the technique of preserving sperm, eggs and embryos at −196 °C in liquid nitrogen, helping preserve them for a long time; it is applied to conserve rare aquatic varieties and makes transporting sperm, eggs and embryos more convenient — the basic steps of cold-preserving fish sperm pictured: collect the sperm from the fish, examine it under the microscope, load it into straws, and store the straws in the liquid-nitrogen tank), and controlling the sex of aquatic animals (in some species the male has economic advantages over the female or the reverse — male giant river prawns grow faster and reach a maximum size of 32 cm while females reach only 25 cm; monosex male tilapia grow faster and more uniformly, with higher yield and economic efficiency than female tilapia; monosex farming also reduces reproduction and natural spawning in the pond, helping the animals grow fast and uniformly with high survival, yield and economic efficiency over traditional farming — with the steps of creating monosex male tilapia by the hormone method drawn: select paired broodstock and let them spawn → fertilised eggs → hatch the eggs → fry (both male and female) → treat with 17α-methyl testosterone → monosex all-male tilapia) (original string `biotechnology in producing aquatic seed`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 28 |
