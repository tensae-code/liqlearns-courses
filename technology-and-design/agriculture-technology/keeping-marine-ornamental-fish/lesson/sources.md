| keeping marine ornamental fish: prepare the tank (tank size plays an important role — marine ornamental fish are strongly affected by changes in living conditions, so marine ornamental tanks usually have large size, minimum volume about 150 litres, with a wide tank surface; choose the tank material and accompanying equipment (pump, lighting lamps, filter, wave maker, …) suitably to avoid rust from seawater, prioritising glass material and limiting metal materials), select and stock the fish (marine ornamental fish are very diverse; choose easy-to-keep species such as cá khoang cổ (Amphiprion), Nemo clownfish (cá hề Nemo), black seahorse (cá ngựa đen), cá ngựa vằn, …; marine ornamental fish are usually transported sealed in nylon bags containing 1/3 clean water and 2/3 oxygen, water temperature from 22 °C to 25 °C, transported at a cool time of day, and when transporting in hot summer the vehicle needs air conditioning or extra ice to cool the water; most marine ornamental fish are of uneven size, so when stocking choose fish of similar size, from 6 to 8 cm/fish, stocked at a density of 5 to 10 fish per tank of 100–200 litres volume; before stocking into the tank, bathe the fish in freshwater for 5–10 minutes to remove external parasites and microorganisms attached to the fish and avoid bringing pathogens into the tank, and stock gradually to avoid temperature and salinity shock), care (environment management — the marine ornamental water must be natural seawater, salinity fluctuating from 28‰ to 30‰, temperature from 27 °C to 28 °C, pH from 8 to 8.5; feed and feeding — marine ornamental feed is diverse by species: Artemia (brine shrimp eggs), bobo (Moina, freshwater) live feed or industrial feed can be used; feed once to twice a day, observe the fish catching prey to adjust the feed amount and avoid leftovers polluting the water source; water change and tank hygiene — change the water weekly, noting that the temperature, salinity and pH of the new water must match the tank water to avoid shocking the fish, changing only 10% to 20% of the tank water volume each time, and periodically use microbial preparations (men vi sinh) to clean the ornamental tank), and common diseases and handling (white-spot disease is common in marine ornamental fish, caused by a skin parasite creating white spots on the body, gills and fins making the fish breathless, reducing or stopping eating, weak; if severe it can kill the whole tank; the disease occurs year-round but causes more harm in the spawning season and cold season; it spreads fast because the larvae disperse through the water current and care tools, and contact between diseased and healthy fish; prevention — buy healthy seed from reputable, quality facilities with inspection and no pathogens, do not stock too densely since disease easily arises, the farming water source must be checked regularly and free of toxic substances (poison gas, heavy metals, …), the water quality ensured at suitable pH, temperature, dissolved oxygen and salinity and kept stable, limit adding new fish to the tank before they are assured, feed fully and avoid leftovers; handling — when signs of disease are detected, separate heavily diseased fish from the tank, disinfect the tank with disinfectant medicine; if the fish still eat, mix parasite-killing medicine into the feed for 2–3 consecutive days; if the fish stop eating, dissolve the parasite-killing medicine in the tank water and soak the fish for 1–2 days to kill the parasites, then change to clean water, noting the clean water must match the tank water in temperature, pH and salinity so the fish are not shocked; besides white spot, marine ornamental fish often suffer fish lice and flukes (sán lá) parasitising at the fin bases and gills, larger parasites visible to the naked eye, prevented and handled like white-spot disease) (original string `keeping marine ornamental fish`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 46 |
