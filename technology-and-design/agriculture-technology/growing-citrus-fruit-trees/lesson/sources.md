| growing citrus fruit trees (cam, bưởi, quýt, chanh): planting technique — best in the spring crop (February–April) and autumn crop (August–October); spacing by species (pomelo 5 × 5 m or 5 × 6 m; orange 4 × 4 m or 4 × 5 m; lemon and kumquat 3 × 3 m or 3 × 4 m, rows 5–6 m apart); prepare the hole 60 × 60 × 60 cm (dig wider in poor soil) and dress it basally with 20–30 kg organic fertiliser + 0.5–1.0 kg potassium + 1.0–1.5 kg superphosphate + 0.5–1.0 kg lime powder, all mixed with the topsoil and refilled; plant in a small hole at the centre, slit the bag, set the plant, fill 2–3 cm above the bag surface, firm by hand around the base, mound the soil into a shallow bowl around the base, stake and tie against wind, mulch the base with dry plant material and water thoroughly; care — weeding and earthing up around the base 2–3 times a year; top-dressing by annual tables for the basic-architecture and business periods (orange example: organic 25–30 then 50–70 kg/tree/year, nitrogen 0.3–0.8 then 1.2–2.0, phosphorus 0.5–1.0 then 1.5–2.0, potassium 0.3–0.8 then 1.2–2.0 kg/tree/year), split into four applications a year (in the business period: after harvest 100% organic + 40% P + 30% K; at flowering 40% N + 40% K; at fruit set 30% N + 30% K; one month after the third application 30% N + 30% K), applied in a trench dug around the canopy projection 20–30 cm wide and 15–20 cm deep or broadcast under the canopy, then covered and watered; watering to keep soil moisture 65–70% in the architecture period, and in the business period by growth stage (restrict water at flower-bud differentiation; every 2–3 days at flowering/fruit set and fruit growth, 55–65 litres a time; every 15–20 days when the fruit is formed, 1–1.5 months before harvest; every 10–15 days after harvest, 70–80 litres), preferring water-saving techniques (drip, rain spray); pruning and canopy shaping — architecture period: year 1 pinch the tip at 80 cm above ground to make level-1 branches; year 2 keep 3–5 strong level-1 branches spread evenly on the trunk and pinch level-1 tips at 50–60 cm to make level-2 branches; year 3 remove some level-2 branches, keeping two per level-1 branch to make levels 3–4; business period: remove dead, damaged, pest-infested, crossing and weak inner branches and thin small or malformed fruit; controlling flowering and fruiting — promote flowering with Paclobutrazol at 0.02–0.04% watered around the base at 2.5–5 g per metre of canopy diameter, or sprayed at 1000–2000 ppm, possibly combined with restricted watering; increase fruit set with GA₃ at 20–40 ppm sprayed at flowering and young-fruit stages (for orange; Brassinolide 5 mg/100 L for citrus at flowering and young fruit); pest and disease control by the four principles for using plant-protection chemicals (right chemical, right timing, right concentration and dose, right method) with biological products preferred, against leaf miner, stem borers, mealybugs and the greening (HLB), canker and scab diseases (original string `growing citrus fruit trees (cam, bưởi, quýt, chanh)`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 9 — Mô đun Trồng cây ăn quả*, printed p. 20 |
