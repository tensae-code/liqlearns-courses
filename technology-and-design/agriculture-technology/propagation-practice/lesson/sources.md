| propagation practice: taking cuttings step by step — choose a healthy semi-woody stem; cut sections 10–15 cm with a knife, trimming the leaves; dip the base 1–2 cm in rooting-stimulant solution for 5–10 seconds; insert the cuttings slightly slanting into a tray of moist soil 3–5 cm deep at 5 × 5 cm or 10 × 10 cm spacing; water to keep moist; after 10–15 days check rooting, and move well-rooted cuttings (roots turned yellow-brown) to the nursery (original string `propagation practice`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 7*, printed p. 22 |
| propagation practice: taking cuttings in five steps — (1) choose the cutting: a semi-woody branch (cành bánh tẻ), neither too young nor too old, healthy and pest-free, with enough buds; (2) cut the cuttings into sections about 5–10 cm long with 2–4 leaves, removing the shoot tip and the part next to the mother stem and trimming the leaves; (3) treat the cuttings: dip the base 1–2 cm into rooting-stimulant solution for 5–10 seconds (or as directed); (4) plant the cuttings slightly slanting, 3–5 cm deep, at 5 × 5 cm or 10 × 10 cm spacing — or one per nursery bag, bags set close together for care; (5) care: water to keep moist; after 10–15 days inspect, and move rooted cuttings (roots turned from white to yellow) to the nursery; with the preparation of a loose, disease-free, well-drained rooting medium (clean sand, soil mixed with sand, light soil, or soil mixed with coconut fibre and rice husk) and tools (knife, shears, watering can with rose, glass jar, nursery bags) (original string `propagation practice`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 9 — Mô đun Trồng cây ăn quả*, printed p. 14 |
| propagation practice: air layering in four steps — (1) choose the branch: 1–2 cm in diameter, mid-canopy and reaching the light, with short internodes and plump buds; (2) ring the bark: with a knife ring the bark 10–15 cm from the branch fork, the ringed section 3–5 cm long, peel off all the bark and scrape away the cambium layer on the wood; leave 2–3 days for the cut to dry; (3) apply rooting stimulant to the upper part of the ringed cut or mix it into the balling medium, then ball: mix the medium (garden soil or dried pond mud crumbled small, possibly mixed with hoarded manure, tree moss, coconut fibre… in a 2 parts soil + 1 part organic matter ratio) and add water to 70–80% moisture, wrap it around the ringed section, cover with a nylon bag and tie both ends tightly with nylon string; (4) cut and plant the layer: when roots show through the nylon yellowish (about 30–60 days after balling), cut the layered branch from the tree, remove the nylon and plant it in a shaded nursery; preparation: balling medium mainly moist garden soil, nylon bags, nylon string, rooting stimulant, knife and layering shears (original string `propagation practice`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 9 — Mô đun Trồng cây ăn quả*, printed p. 16 |
| propagation practice: bud grafting with a wood chip (ghép mắt nhỏ có gỗ) in four steps — (1) take the bud: on the budwood branch, use the grafting knife to cut a bark piece about 1.5–2.0 cm long containing a dormant bud and a little wood; (2) choose the grafting position and open the mouth: choose a straight part of the rootstock stem 15–20 cm above the nursery-bag surface and cut a bark piece (as for taking the bud) to make the graft mouth, this piece slightly larger than the bud piece; (3) insert the bud: press the bud piece into the opened mouth on the rootstock and bind it tightly and closely with the binding strip; (4) care for the grafted plant: after about 15–20 days, cut off the rootstock top above the graft with shears, remove the binding; regularly remove shoots that are not the graft bud, water with supplementary NPK fertiliser to push the bud's growth; the grafting methods named (T-budding, window budding, patch budding, side grafting, cleft/splice grafting, shoot grafting…) and the tools (grafting knife, pruning shears; rootstock plant and budwood branch from a đầu dòng (elite mother) tree of a common local fruit species; self-dissolving or nylon binding strips) (original string `propagation practice`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 9 — Mô đun Trồng cây ăn quả*, printed p. 17 |
