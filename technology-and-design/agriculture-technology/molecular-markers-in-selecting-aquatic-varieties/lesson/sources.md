| molecular markers in selecting aquatic varieties: molecular markers (also called DNA markers) are known nucleotide sequences (DNA segments) located at defined positions on the chromosome, the DNA segments closely linked to the gene for the trait of interest; markers can distinguish between individuals of this variety and other varieties — the common molecular markers in aquatic selection are restriction-fragment-length markers (RFLP), microsatellite markers and single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) markers; molecular markers are widely applied in aquatic selection work such as identifying species (cá chim vây vàng, lobster, seaweed, …), determining the genetic diversity of pangasius, red tilapia, cá diêu hồng, … populations, tracing origin and building pedigree trees, and thanks to markers breeders have selected aquatic varieties of high economic value (fast-growing Norwegian salmon, fast-growing pangasius with salt tolerance, fast-growing tasty V1 carp with good resistance, …) — with the comparison diagram of selecting fast-growing, salt-tolerant, disease-resistant tilapia by the traditional method (cross → offspring → grown fish → evaluate growth, salt tolerance and disease resistance → select the individuals) against the molecular-marker method (the offspring's growth, salt tolerance and disease resistance are evaluated by molecular marker → the individuals with fast growth, salt tolerance and good resistance are selected right away) (original string `molecular markers in selecting aquatic varieties`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 24 |
