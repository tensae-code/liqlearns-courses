# Caring for agricultural tools — maintenance, storage and dangers

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Maintenance and storage of agricultural tools
The Primary 4 unit continues: maintenance keeps the tools in good condition, and after use the tools are stored in a safe place. The two habits are one discipline — a tool cleaned, kept and put away safely is ready for the next work and dangerous to no one in between.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dangers of misusing agricultural tools, and precautions
The book names the dangers plainly: cuts from sharp tools, and agrochemical mist among them. Against each danger stand the precautions while using the tools — the safety teaching is specific to the farm, its sharp edges and its chemicals, not copied from a workshop list.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: farm tools are kept in good condition, stored in a safe place after use, and handled knowing their dangers — sharp edges cut, and agrochemical mist harms — with precautions to match. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
