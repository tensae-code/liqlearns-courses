| farming tilapia commercially in cages: preparing the cage (choose the site — cages only in planned areas on rivers, reservoirs and hydroelectric lakes with clean, flowing water meeting the quality limits table (pH 6.5–8.5, dissolved oxygen ≥ 4 mg/L, ammonia < 1 mg/L, NO₂⁻ 0.02 mg/L, H₂S 0, transparency ≥ 30 cm, alkalinity 60–180 mg CaCO₃/L, per QCVN 02-22:2015/BNNPTNT); river cages in airy places with wide water surface and steady flow of 0.2–0.3 m/s, the total cage area not over 0.2% of the water surface, cages 50–100 m apart; reservoir cages airy, at least 15 m from shore and clear of shading trees, each cluster under 30 cages spaced 150–200 m apart; cage sizes and materials — the common cage is a net cage with a stainless-steel frame, usually steel tube of Ø = 44 with two layers of net, 3 m deep (50 cm above the water and 30 cm of net sunk below the bottom to stop fish escaping), common sizes 6 × 6 × 3 m (108 m³) or 9 × 6 × 3 m (162 m³), each cage raised by a system of 8–12 floats of 200 L plastic drums, HDPE cage systems also used; each cluster needs a house for workers, a store for feed and medicines, and life jackets), selecting and stocking seed (healthy, uniform fish of bright colour and quick reaction, with no deformity or disease; stock in the early morning or cool afternoon, first bathing the fish in diluted salt solution at 2% for about 5–10 minutes and releasing them gradually to acclimatise; the stocking density by size in the table — red tilapia 6–8 cm at 5–10 g: 40–50 fish/m³, 8–10 cm at 15–20 g: 30–40/m³; tilapia likewise), care (feeding — when newly stocked use floating pellets of about 30–35% protein, pellet size 1–2 mm; when the fish are larger, pellets of 28–30% protein, size 3–4 mm; feed twice daily at about 8–9 am and 3–4 pm with floating pellets, the daily amount about 5–7% of the fish weight in the cage in the first 1–2 months and about 3–5% in the following months, reducing on bad-weather days; feeding can be by hand or by automatic feeder programmed for the regime and hours; cage management — observe daily and promptly fix incidents such as rubbish clogging the river flow into the cage, clean the cage the same day and remove dead fish to regulation; periodically clean the net to create flow for the fish, once a week in summer and once per two weeks in winter, by spraying and washing the net gently so the fish do not jump out; health management — comprehensive prevention: disinfect tools and the cage water source, periodically feed vitamin C and immune boosters mixed into the feed; when disease occurs, isolate the dead fish, do not throw carcasses into the surrounding water, check clinically, send samples of diseased fish to the nearest laboratory and consult specialists; disinfect nets and tools, sprinkle TCCA tablets or hang BKC bags in the cage; treat with permitted medicines to regulation, at the right dose and method), and harvest (when the cage tilapia reach commercial size, over 1.0 kg/fish, after 6–8 months; stop feeding 1–2 days before harvest; harvest in the large cage by gathering the fish to one corner to make catching easier; prepare the transport means carefully — the live fish must be kept, transported for consumption in clean, cool water with enough oxygen supplied, and should be consumed the same day; the transport equipment of tanks on a truck pictured) (original string `farming tilapia commercially in cages`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 94 |
