| keeping freshwater ornamental fish in tanks, and koi in ponds: tank keeping (prepare the tank — ornamental tanks have many shapes and sizes, but the tank must be large enough for oxygen to diffuse in and toxic gases out, and covered tanks need an aeration and filtration system; the tank frame must be strong so the tank does not deform, of iron, aluminium, stainless steel or hard plastic, and the walls of glass, tarpaulin or cement, …, glass being the most common material in the home; place the tank where it is convenient for light and care, give the tank about an hour of sunlight a day between 8 and 10 am, and also use suitable lighting lamps; the support equipment pictured: filter sponge for the tank bottom, air pump (máy sục khí), heater (temperature-regulating rod), decorations, aquatic plants, gravel and stones, fish net, siphon tube, and an environment test kit (pH test kit); select and stock the fish — choose fish of bright characteristic colour, quick and healthy, with no deformity; before stocking, soak the bag holding the fish in the tank to balance the temperature between the bag water and the tank water, then open the bag and let the fish swim out by themselves; to add new fish to a stocked tank, keep the new fish in a separate tank to monitor disease before putting them into the common tank; the stocking density depends on the species, the fish size and the equipment; care and management — environment management: the freshwater ornamental-fish water must be clean, the temperature maintained from 25 °C to 28 °C, pH stable from 7 to 8.5, dissolved oxygen from 5 to 7 mg/L; feed and feeding: the feed is fresh feed or industrial pellets by each species' habit, fed once to twice a day at an amount equivalent to 1–2% of the fish's body weight; water change and tank hygiene: change the water 2–3 times a month depending on tank size and stocking density, avoiding heat shock for the fish when changing water in winter, especially in the northern region, and clean the tank with dedicated friendly tools, gently, avoiding stressing the fish; other issues: for tanks, fit a cover or barrier so fish do not jump out and get injured, the light must be suitable or use LED lamps, when using lamps avoid switching them on and off suddenly, causing stress, and note electrical safety; common diseases and prevention/treatment — white-spot disease caused by a protozoan parasite (white spots like grains of salt on the body, the fish reduce or stop eating; the disease usually harms in the cold season, so note raising the temperature and keeping the tank water temperature from 28 °C to 30 °C; when disease is detected, promptly disinfect the tank water with permitted disinfectant medicines, then use parasite-killing medicine soaked or mixed into the feed for 2–3 consecutive days) and water-mould fungal disease (caused by water mould, usually appearing on cold days of the year; the fish are lethargic, reduce or stop eating, and grey mould patches appear on the body; prevention — avoid injuring the fish when catching and moving them to a cold-season tank, keep the tank clean and sanitised, keep the water clean to avoid fungal infection; when fish are infected, reduce feeding, disinfect the tank water, change to new water and maintain the tank temperature)), and koi in ponds (prepare the pond — koi ponds have diverse sizes and shapes depending on area; a household garden pond is usually from 4 m², with depth from 0.6 to 1.5 m; koi can be kept in a recirculating system besides a filter tank and the equipment — pump, particle filter system, biological filter system and water reuse; the koi water source needs pH from 7 to 7.5, temperature from 20 °C to 28 °C, dissolved oxygen from 5 to 7 mg/L, free of heavy metals and toxic gases; for each new pond, soak water in the pond for 2–3 weeks and drain and change the water 2–3 times to remove toxic substances, impurities and pathogens before stocking; select and stock — healthy koi of bright colour and clear origin with quarantine certification to regulation; koi have many colours such as black, white, red, spotted and mixed (the three main types on the market are Japanese koi, Chinese koi and Vietnamese koi); transport gently avoiding abrasion, in the early morning or cool afternoon; when stocking, gradually balance the temperature between the transport water and the pond water to avoid heat shock; the stocking density is from 1 to 2 fish/m³, possibly higher when the fish are small; the common stocking size is 10–12 cm (about 30–50 g/fish); care — environment management: the koi water source must be clean, the temperature in the tank/pond maintained from 20 °C to 29 °C with stable pH from 7 to 7.5, dissolved oxygen from 5 to 15 mg/L, salt concentration fluctuating from 0.3% to 0.7%; feed and feeding: koi feed is industrial feed of protein content from 26% to 40%, small fish needing more protein than large fish, supplemented with vitamins, immune stimulants, colour enhancers and digestive enzymes, … for more beautiful-coloured fish; the daily feed amount is from 2% to 3% of the total fish body weight, fed until full once to twice a day, and on cold days or when changing water reduce the amount or stop feeding; water change and pond hygiene: change the water regularly, every 2 days change 1/3 of the pond water volume, use water soaked in a holding tank for 2–3 days before putting it into the pond, when algae grow thick use a brush to sweep the settled waste to the drain sump area to suck the waste out of the pond; other issues: apple snails or other aquatic plants such as lotus and súng can be stocked in the pond at a balanced ratio to avoid oxygen deficiency, and ornamental plants or a flowing waterfall can be added around the pond for aesthetics; common diseases and handling — parasitic diseases of koi kept in recirculating tanks (lice (rận cá), leeches (đỉa), melon-seed worms (trùng quả dưa), anchor worms (trùng mỏ neo), … making the fish behave abnormally with much mucus on the body, white-spot lesions or bleeding spots at the fin bases and scales; prevention and treatment require periodic disinfection of the pond water to kill parasite larvae, and periodic use of parasite-killing medicine with the active ingredient Praziquantel mixed into the feed to the maker's instructions) and KHV disease of koi (usually occurring in northern Vietnam in winter and early spring when the water temperature falls to 18–29 °C, caused by a virus of the Herpesviridae family with DNA genetic material; the fish darken, eyes sink, mouth wide open gasping, secrete much mucus creating white foamy patches on the body, gills necrotic with ulcers on the body; prevention — absolutely keep separate tools for each tank, disinfect if tools are shared, seed of clear origin, avoid bringing pathogens into farming ponds, quarantine newly bought koi separately, disinfect the water source to kill pathogens in the water, avoid cross-contamination, and good care and nutrition to raise the fish's natural resistance; disease handling — when disease occurs, scoop out dead or heavily diseased carcasses or fish and destroy the carcasses without spreading them to surrounding areas, disinfect the water source, supply dissolved oxygen by aerator to ventilate the gills, mix synthetic vitamin C and Beta-Glucan into the feed to raise resistance, raise the temperature of infected ponds on cold days to limit disease damage, and for ponds infected with gill-rot bacteria antibiotics may be used in the feed for 5 consecutive days to the specialist's instructions; KHV has an incubation period of up to 14 days, so newly arrived fish must be isolated long enough to ensure safety before import)) (original string `keeping freshwater ornamental fish in tanks, and koi in ponds`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 40 |
