| farming whiteleg shrimp in ponds in three stages, and farming Ben Tre clams on tidal flats: the shrimp technique divides the period from seed to commercial shrimp into three stages to raise growth rate, reduce disease risk and production cost and raise survival and yield — the pond system (choose and prepare three different ponds of 1,000–2,000 m² each; the ponds may be earthen or raised on the surface (stages 1 and 2) and lined with HDPE tarpaulin, round or rectangular with a central drain sump (hố xi phông) in the middle; install aeration systems (stage 1) or both paddle wheels and aeration (stages 2 and 3); stage 1 and 2 ponds should have a roof against hot sun; pond hygiene — earthen ponds renovated to the correct process, tarpaulin ponds washed and the tarpaulin disinfected before farming; the farming water filtered and disinfected to process before entering the pond), selecting and stocking (healthy seed of body length 9–11 mm at the Postlarvae stage, of certified quality from regulated seed farms; before transport in bags the shrimp must be acclimatised for salinity and pH to the farming pond's conditions; stock in the early morning or cool afternoon, balancing temperature between the transport bag water and the pond water to avoid heat shock; stocking density 500–1,000 shrimp/m² in stage 1, 250–500/m² in stage 2 and 100–150/m² in stage 3), care (feed industrial feed of high protein content and suitable pellet size by age to the maker's recommendation, 4–6 times/day by growth stage, regularly using feeding trays (sàng ăn) to check leftover feed and adjust appropriately; environment management — siphon daily to collect waste from the pond, check water quality for timely treatment, periodically every 4–5 days replace 20–30% of the pond water, use microbial preparations to the maker's recommendation), and harvest (stage 1: after 25–30 days, when the shrimp reach 800–1,000 per kg, harvest and transfer them to the stage-2 pond; stage 2: after 25–30 more days at 200 per kg, harvest and transfer to stage 3; stage 3: after 30 more days, at commercial size of about 30–50 per kg, harvest by draining about 50% of the pond water then pulling a net to catch the shrimp for sale); and the clam technique (choose and prepare the flat — Ben Tre clams farmed on tidal flats in estuaries and bays with small waves and gentle tides, an airy position not stagnant, some freshwater inflow; the flat's bottom is sand-mud (sand 60–80%), salinity from 15‰ to 25‰, the exposure time (when the tide is out) not over 8 hours/day, the flat not polluted by industrial, agricultural or domestic waste water; before stocking, clean rubbish, loosen the soil, level the flat and make small furrows for the water to drain when the tide falls, and fence the flat with netting; select and stock seed — healthy seed of glossy shell, not chipped or broken, uniform in size; two main seed crops a year, April–June and September–October; spread the seed evenly over the flat surface at the densities in the table by seed size (20,000 per kg: 5,000/m²; 10,000: 3,000/m²; 1,000: 400/m²; 800: 350/m²; 500: 300/m²); care — clams are filter-feeding plankton eaters needing no feeding; periodically thin out the density, inspect the fences and beds, remove harmful organisms and dead clams; harvest after 12–18 months at 30–50 clams/kg, by partial harvest (thinning) or harvesting the whole flat, when the tide is out) (original string `farming whiteleg shrimp in ponds in three stages, and farming Ben Tre clams on tidal flats`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 97 |
