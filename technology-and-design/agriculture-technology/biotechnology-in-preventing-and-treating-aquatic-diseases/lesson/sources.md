| biotechnology in preventing and treating aquatic diseases: diagnosing disease by PCR (thanks to biotechnology, dangerous diseases have been screened from broodstock, ensuring no pathogens are carried before spawning to create clean seed stocks; during farming, biotechnology helps detect early and accurately some viruses on aquatic animals, so prevention and treatment are highly effective, reducing the risk of spread and losses — the drawback is that it requires modern equipment and highly qualified laboratory technicians; the steps of diagnosing a disease caused by an RNA virus (Reovirus) on grass carp by PCR drawn: collect the aquatic sample → extract the total RNA → synthesise the complementary strand (cDNA) by the RT-PCR reaction → amplify the DNA by the PCR reaction → electrophoresis and examine the PCR product), producing inactivated vaccines for fish (vaccines in aquaculture are usually inactivated vaccines used mainly on fish; to produce a vaccine, first isolate the disease-causing microorganism, then apply biotechnology to select the strain of high virulence and strong antigenicity to make the vaccine seed strain; once the vaccine strain is selected, multiply its biomass, then inactivate the microorganism biomass to produce the aquatic disease-prevention vaccine — the flow drawn: pathogenic bacteria → seed strain → multiply the biomass → inactivate the bacteria → inactivated vaccine → vaccinate the fish; some vaccines researched and produced for fish diseases: the vaccine against popeye disease of tilapia caused by Streptococcus agalactiae, the vaccine against ulcer and haemorrhage of marine fish caused by Streptococcus iniae, Vibrio anguillarum and Vibrio harveyi, the vaccine against haemorrhage caused by Lactococcus garvieae on salmon, …; these vaccines have an average protection ability of 60% to 70%), and producing microbial preparations that raise immunity for aquatic animals (biotechnology creates preparations from beneficial microorganisms (probiotics); the preparations raise digestion ability, balance the gut microflora and stimulate the immune system, thereby raising the animals' disease prevention and treatment ability; note that when a microbial preparation is mixed into aquatic feed, part of it will dissolve and escape into the environment, reducing the beneficial microorganism density; the production process in four steps: isolate and select beneficial microorganism strains; culture and multiply the biomass in a suitable medium; mix the microorganism biomass with a suitable carrier to make the preparation; check, package, preserve and use — worked for Lactobacillus plantarum: the L. plantarum strain → propagation grade 1 → propagation grade 2 → bulk fermentation system producing biomass → concentrate the biomass → freeze-dry the biomass → mix and check the product → check, package, preserve and use; the microorganisms used to make preparations are usually isolated from the digestive tract of healthy aquatic animals, so they are almost harmless to the animals; using microbial preparations can raise natural immunity, support digestion and stimulate growth in aquatic animals; some strains commonly used to produce probiotic preparations supplemented into aquatic feed are Lactobacillus sp., Bacillus sp. and Saccharomyces sp.)
