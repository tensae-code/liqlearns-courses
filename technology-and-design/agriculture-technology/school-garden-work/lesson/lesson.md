# School garden work

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first garden work
The youngest learners sow and transplant, water (riego), weed (escarde) and harvest (cosecha) in the school garden, plot or nursery (huerto, parcela, vivero), with the dibber (plantador) and watering can (regadera) as their tools. Cuba's programmes return to the garden in grade after grade.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planning and caring like a grower
Older learners prepare the ground and the beds (canteros), make organic fertilisers, choose sowing methods and take up the rake. In Grade 6 the garden is planned and designed — a map or croquis first — then cared for after sowing with weeding, cultivator passes, hilling and watering, under agroecological soil management with polycultures and crop rotation.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the school garden is worked as a whole craft: sow and transplant, water, weed and harvest — and, as the learner grows, prepare beds, feed the soil organically, plan the garden on a map, and rotate the crops. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
