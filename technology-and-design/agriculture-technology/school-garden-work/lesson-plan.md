# Lesson plan — School garden work

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Agriculture Technology · **Lesson:** School garden work
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the school garden is worked as a whole craft: sow and transplant, water, weed and harvest — and, as the learner grows, prepare beds, feed the soil organically, plan the garden on a map, and rotate the crops.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's Educación Laboral programmes and methodological orientations (teacher programme documents) — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba is evidence only for this lesson, counted nowhere in the acceptance count.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The first garden work** — The youngest learners sow and transplant, water (riego), weed (escarde) and harvest (cosecha) in the school garden, plot or nursery (huerto, parcela, vivero), with the dibber (plantador) and watering can (regadera) as their tools. Cuba's programmes return to the garden in grade after grade.
2. **Planning and caring like a grower** — Older learners prepare the ground and the beds (canteros), make organic fertilisers, choose sowing methods and take up the rake. In Grade 6 the garden is planned and designed — a map or croquis first — then cared for after sowing with weeding, cultivator passes, hilling and watering, under agroecological soil management with polycultures and crop rotation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
