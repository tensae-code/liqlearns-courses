| biotechnology in fisheries — the roles and achievements survey: the concept (applying biotechnology in the fisheries field to raise the efficiency of seed work, farming, effective management of aquatic resources, sustainable fisheries development and ecological-environment protection), with achievements by field: in seed work, gene technology and assisted-reproduction technology raise the efficiency of selection and variety creation, creating varieties with outstanding advantages in yield, quality and disease resistance that traditional methods cannot reach, while varieties can also be created by purely farming methods without commercial farming under strict process, with no residual antibiotics or banned chemicals, passing the technical barriers when exporting (the GIFT tilapia line growing fast; the Moana black tiger shrimp — clean seed, fast growth, good adaptation; the Coho salmon with growth rate raised 50% over the original line; pangasius lines selected for fast growth and salinity tolerance; fast-growing V1 carp with tasty flesh and good resistance; whiteleg shrimp growing fast; monosex tilapia and giant river prawn with male ratio over 90%); in disease prevention and treatment, PCR supports diagnosis and early, accurate detection of pathogens on aquatic animals, and effective vaccines and microbial preparations have been developed for aquatic animals while raising their resistance and inhibiting harmful microorganisms (RT-PCR kit detecting infectious myonecrosis virus (IMV) on shrimp from virus strains isolated in Vietnam; PCR kits for Iridovirus on marine fish, KHV on koi and RT-PCR for spring viraemia of carp (SVC); Multiplex PCR detecting infectious hypodermal and hematopoietic necrosis virus in blood-forming and epithelial organs of black tiger shrimp; inactivated vaccines researched successfully — the vaccine against liver-kidney pus disease caused by Edwardsiella ictaluri bacteria on pangasius, the vaccine against Streptococcosis disease on commercial tilapia, vaccines against Vibrio spp. on marine farmed fish, and the vaccine against viral nervous necrosis (VNN) on fish); in controlling the farming-water environment, biotechnology creates microbial and enzyme preparations that inhibit harmful microorganisms in the environment, improve the farming environment (water and pond bottom), raise the animals' feed-absorption ability and so raise yield and product quality (microbial preparations of the genus Bacillus, a preparation containing 4 strains of the genus Bacillus, preparations containing the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae, … which stabilise water quality and balance pH, reduce some toxic gases (H₂S, NH₃, NO₂) in the pond, stimulate the development of plankton as natural feed for shrimp and fish); in feed production, biotechnology raises feed quality and the efficiency of using feed raw materials such as fermenting soybean meal, breaking down agricultural by-products to produce protein-rich raw materials for aquatic feed, and the emulsification technique to enrich HUFA (highly unsaturated fatty acids) — a main nutritional source in the larval stage of aquatic animals — in live feeds such as Artemia, Moina and some algae (the composition and content of HUFA in live seed feeds is usually unbalanced and unstable, reducing nursing efficiency with slow growth, low resistance, high deformity rate and low survival); and in processing, biotechnology is applied mainly to maintain and raise product quality, with potential in pharmaceuticals and cosmetics (microbial and enzyme technology extracting and purifying collagen from pangasius skin and fish-processing by-products for the pharmaceutical and cosmetics industries; extracting glucosamine from shrimp and crab shells; extracting chondroitin sulfate from shark cartilage; separating essential amino acids and calcium from fish bones — high-value raw materials for research and functional-food development) (original string `biotechnology in fisheries`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Lâm nghiệp – Thuỷ sản*, printed p. 19 |
