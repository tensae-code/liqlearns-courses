# Dyeing and finishing textiles

## Do it first
Look at three textiles near you: one strongly coloured, one white, and one that feels specially smooth, stiff or soft. Guess which processes each one has been through — dyeing, bleaching, or finishing. Keep your guesses to check against the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dyeing with synthetic dyes, additives and mordants
Dyeing colours a textile through and through, not just on its surface. This lesson's dyeing uses synthetic dyes. Dyes do not work alone: dye additives help the dyeing process, and mordants (puretus, in the Finnish material) are substances that help the dye bind to the fibre so the colour holds. The right additive and mordant depend on the fibre being dyed, which is why fibre knowledge comes before dyeing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bleaching — oxidising and reducing agents
Bleaching removes colour instead of adding it. The Finnish material names two families of bleaching agents: oxidising bleaching agents, which work by oxidation, and reducing bleaching agents, which work by reduction. They are different chemistry with the same job — lightening or whitening the textile — and choosing between them depends on the fibre and the result wanted. Bleaching agents are chemicals: handle them by their safety instructions, every time.

## Sub lesson 3 — Finishing — improving fabric properties
Finishing improves a fabric's properties after it is made. Finishing can be mechanical — done by physical treatment of the fabric — or chemical — done with substances applied to it. The point is always a named improvement: a fabric finished to behave better in use than the unfinished cloth. Dyeing, bleaching and finishing together are how a plain textile becomes the material a product actually needs.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: dyeing gives a textile its colour and finishing improves what the fabric can do — dyeing uses dyes with additives and mordants, bleaching uses oxidising or reducing agents, and finishing improves fabric properties by mechanical or chemical means. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
