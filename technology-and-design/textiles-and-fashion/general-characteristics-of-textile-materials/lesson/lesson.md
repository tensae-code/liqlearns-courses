# General characteristics of textile materials

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — General characteristics of textile materials
Greece's book teaches it this way: classification of textile materials (fibres, yarns, fabrics), the general characteristics of fibres and fabrics, and the general methods of identifying fibres (preliminary examination, microscopic examination, burning test, solubility test, staining test, melting point, specific gravity) (*Textile Raw Materials*, printed p. 17). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — General characteristics of textile materials — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: classification of textile materials (fibres, yarns, fabrics), the general characteristics of fibres and fabrics, and the general methods of identifying fibres (preliminary examination, microscopic examination, burning test, solubility test, staining test, melting point, specific gravity). Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Classification of textile materials (fibres, yarns, fabrics), the general characteristics of fibres and fabrics, and the general methods of identifying fibres (preliminary examination, microscopic examination, burning test, solubility test, staining test, melting point, specific gravity). Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
