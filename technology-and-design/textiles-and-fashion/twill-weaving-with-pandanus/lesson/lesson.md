# Twill weaving with pandanus

## Do it first
Before a builder cuts timber, they measure the house. Before this weaving starts, the weaver measures the finished piece — on paper. Why must the size be known before the cords are cut?

## Sub lesson 1 — Pandanus as a weaving material
The leaf is stripped, cleaned and prepared so it lies flat and even. Material preparation is half the weaving: uneven strips make uneven cloth, in pandanus as in any fibre.

## Sub lesson 2 — The two anchor cords — lengths from the finished size
Nothing is guessed: the finished size decides how long the two anchor cords must be, with allowance for the working. Measuring from the product backwards — size first, materials second — is the planner's habit this lesson teaches.

## Sub lesson 3 — The twill process, step by step
Work the pattern in order, keep the stepping regular, and check each row against the last. The diagonal of twill appears only if every row steps the same way — rhythm is accuracy.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: twill weaving with pandanus is planned before it is woven: the two anchor cords are prepared with their lengths worked out from the finished size, then the twill process proceeds step by step. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
