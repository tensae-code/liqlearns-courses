# Lesson plan — Twill weaving with pandanus

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Twill weaving with pandanus
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** twill weaving with pandanus is planned before it is woven: the two anchor cords are prepared with their lengths worked out from the finished size, then the twill process proceeds step by step.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Pandanus as a weaving material** — Pandanus leaves are prepared into strips — a Pacific weaving material with its own handling.
2. **The two anchor cords — lengths from the finished size** — Before weaving, work out the anchor cords' lengths from the size the finished piece must be.
3. **The twill process, step by step** — Twill crosses the strips in a stepped over-and-under pattern, building the diagonal texture row by row.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how pandanus is prepared for weaving.
2. Explain working out anchor-cord lengths from the finished size.
3. Describe the twill stepping pattern.
## What you need
Pandanus or substitute strips (paper) for practice weaving; a ruler or tape.
