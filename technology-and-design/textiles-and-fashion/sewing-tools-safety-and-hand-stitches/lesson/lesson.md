# Sewing tools, safety and hand stitches

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tools and safety
The sewing kit holds sharp and pointed tools — needles, pins, scissors, the buttonhole opener — beside the pincushion that keeps pins safe and findable. Safety rules govern them all: pins in the cushion, never in clothes or mouth; sharp tools passed handle-first and stored closed. Iran's Grade 7 clothing module teaches the tools and their safety rules first, through software demonstrations and class work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hand stitches: buttons, hooks and buttonholes
The first hand-sewing jobs are fastenings: a button sewn with a thread shank so it sits above the fabric, hooks sewn firm at the edge, and the buttonhole stitch — the mouse-tooth stitch — worked round a cut slit to stop it fraying. Each is a small, complete sewing skill used in every later garment.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: hand sewing begins with its tools used safely — from buttonhole opener to pincushion — and its first stitches: sewing on buttons and hooks, and the buttonhole (mouse-tooth) stitch. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
