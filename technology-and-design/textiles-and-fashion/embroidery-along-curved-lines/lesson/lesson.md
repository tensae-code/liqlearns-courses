# Embroidery along curved lines

## Do it first
Trace a petal shape in the air with one finger — see how your finger keeps turning. Sewing a curve means turning like that, stitch by stitch. Then learn to embroider a flower.

## Sub lesson 1 — From straight stitching to curves
A curve cannot be sewn with long straight stitches: the line bends a little at every step. The embroiderer takes shorter stitches on a curve and turns the work gradually, so the stitching follows the bend smoothly instead of cutting across it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Embroidering a flower
The flower's curved lines — petals and stem — are embroidered stitch by stitch along their bends. The learner follows each petal's curve, keeping stitches short and even, until the whole flower stands stitched on the cloth.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: curved lines are embroidered with short, even stitches, turning the work gradually so the stitching follows the bend. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
