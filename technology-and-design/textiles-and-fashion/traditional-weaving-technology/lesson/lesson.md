# Traditional weaving technology

## Do it first
Picture a house with open sides in strong sun. What could you hang that shades the inside, lets air move, and can be rolled up when not needed? Samoa's answer is woven: the polasisi blind.

## Sub lesson 1 — Materials from the place
Traditional technology starts from what the islands provide. Coconut leaflets are prepared into strips of even width; sinnet is rolled from coconut husk fibre into a strong cord. The materials are renewable, local and repairable — chosen by generations of testing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Weaving the polasisi
A polasisi is a woven blind for the Samoan fale (house): the panel must be even, tight enough to screen sun and rain-driven air, and flexible enough to raise or lower. Weaving follows the same warp-and-weft logic as cloth, worked in leaf at house scale.

## Sub lesson 3 — A technology, not a decoration
Judge the polasisi as technology: it does a job, is made to a repeatable method, and can be repaired and remade from the same materials. Samoa's book files its making in an appendix of weaving instructions — a written record of the community's own technique.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Samoa's traditional weaving technology makes polasisi blinds for the fale from coconut leaves and sinnet — local materials, prepared and woven by a known sequence into a working part of the house. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
