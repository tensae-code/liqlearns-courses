# Lesson plan — Traditional weaving technology

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Traditional weaving technology
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Samoa's traditional weaving technology makes polasisi blinds for the fale from coconut leaves and sinnet — local materials, prepared and woven by a known sequence into a working part of the house.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Materials from the place** — Coconut leaves give the weaving strips; sinnet — sennit cord from coconut fibre — gives the lashing.
2. **Weaving the polasisi** — The leaf strips are woven into a flat blind panel, and sinnet binds and strengthens it.
3. **A technology, not a decoration** — The blind is a working building part: it shades, screens and ventilates the fale.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the materials of a polasisi and where they come from.
2. Describe what a polasisi does in a fale.
3. Explain why traditional weaving counts as technology.
## What you need
No special materials; a woven mat or blind to examine if available.
