# Traditional clothing materials and construction

## Do it first
Look at a modern lavalava — a simple wrap garment. Its ancestors were not bought cloth but made material: bark, leaves and fibre. What would you need to learn before you could dress from plants alone?

## Sub lesson 1 — Clothing from bark, leaf and fibre
Before woven cloth arrived, Samoan clothing was manufactured from plants: bark beaten and prepared, ti leaves gathered and fastened, fau fibre worked into wearable material. Each garment type — malo, titi, lavalava — is a construction: materials selected, prepared and assembled for the body.

## Sub lesson 2 — Siapo — bark cloth
The inner bark is stripped, soaked and beaten until the fibres spread into a continuous sheet. Siapo shows the whole technology chain in one material: harvest, preparation, forming, finishing — and it later becomes the ground for Samoa's famous painted designs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Blackening with mud and smoke
Colour and preservation came from the environment: burying or soaking in mud and holding in smoke darkened the material. The finishing step is deliberate technology — changing a material's colour and durability with controlled natural processes.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: early Samoan clothing was made from what the islands give: malo, titi, lavalava and siapo from bark, ti leaves and fau fibre, blackened with mud and smoke. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
