# Lesson plan — Textile printing

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Textile printing
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** textile printing puts a design on cloth by a prepared surface: stencil printing step by step — design, transfer to plastic or card, cut with a craft knife, print — beside screen printing and roller printing, with a design brief completed for one method.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Stencil printing, step by step** — Design first, then transfer the design to plastic or card, cut the openings with a craft knife, and print through them.
2. **Screen and roller printing** — A screen prints through a mesh; rollers print continuously — both repeat a design exactly, at speed.
3. **A design brief for one method** — The brief names the product, the design, and the printing method chosen — with reasons.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List stencil printing's steps in order.
2. Tell stencil, screen and roller printing apart.
3. Say what a printing design brief must decide.
## What you need
Card or plastic sheet and a craft knife only under supervision; paper for design work.
