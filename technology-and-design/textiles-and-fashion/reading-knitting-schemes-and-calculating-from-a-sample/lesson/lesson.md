# Reading knitting schemes and calculating from a sample

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Moldova's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — The gauge sample and the tape
Grade 6 knitting becomes exact: a gauge sample is knitted first — a small test piece in the article's stitch and yarn — and its width is measured with the tape. The sample is the article in miniature: its measure carries the truth about your hands, yarn and needles.

## Sub lesson 2 — Calculating stitches, reading schemes
From the sample's measure, the stitches for the article's size are calculated — so many stitches in the sample's width, therefore so many for the article. The work is read from technological schemes (scheme tehnologice), and the module's techniques include circular knitted objects.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: knitting is calculated, not guessed: knit a gauge sample, measure its width with the tape, and calculate the stitches for the article's size — reading the technological schemes (scheme tehnologice) as you go. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
