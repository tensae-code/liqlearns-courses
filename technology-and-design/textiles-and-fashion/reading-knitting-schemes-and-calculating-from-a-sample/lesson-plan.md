# Lesson plan — Reading knitting schemes and calculating from a sample

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Reading knitting schemes and calculating from a sample
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Moldova expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** knitting is calculated, not guessed: knit a gauge sample, measure its width with the tape, and calculate the stitches for the article's size — reading the technological schemes (scheme tehnologice) as you go.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Moldova's *Educație tehnologică* (Technological Education) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Moldova counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The gauge sample and the tape** — Grade 6 knitting becomes exact: a gauge sample is knitted first — a small test piece in the article's stitch and yarn — and its width is measured with the tape.
2. **Calculating stitches, reading schemes** — From the sample's measure, the stitches for the article's size are calculated — so many stitches in the sample's width, therefore so many for the article.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Knit a gauge sample and measure it.
2. Calculate the stitch count for an article's size from the sample.
3. Explain how Moldova's book teaches it.
## What you need
Needles, yarn, a measuring tape, and paper for the calculation.
