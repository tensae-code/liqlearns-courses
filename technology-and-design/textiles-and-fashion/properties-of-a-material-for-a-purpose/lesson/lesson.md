# Properties of a material for a purpose

## Do it first
On a hot day, compare two shirts if you can — one cotton, one synthetic. Which would you rather wear, and sleep in? Name the properties behind your choice.

## Sub lesson 1 — Properties decide the purpose
Choosing a material is matching. List what the job demands, list what the material offers, and see whether the lists agree. Cotton absorbs moisture, feels soft, washes well and stays comfortable in heat — a strong match for things worn on the skin and slept in.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cotton in a hot climate
Cotton takes up sweat instead of leaving it on the skin, lets air move, and survives frequent washing. That is why it suits clothing and bed linen where the weather is hot and humid: its properties answer the climate's demands.

## Sub lesson 3 — Testing a choice
Handle the material, wet it, wash it, wear it: does it do what the purpose needs? Samoa's book sets learners investigating cotton's properties and answering questions from what they find — the investigation, not the label, justifies the choice.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a material is chosen for a purpose by its properties: cotton suits clothing and bed linen in Samoa's climate because of how it behaves in heat, moisture and washing. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
