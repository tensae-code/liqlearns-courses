# Lesson plan — Adding colour to fibres

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Adding colour to fibres
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Samoa adds colour to bark cloth in siapo making by three processes — immersion (fui), rubbing (elei) and freehand painting (tutusi/mamanu) on a pattern carved on the upeti board — and dyes, natural and synthetic, are chosen and evaluated for the fibre.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Siapo — colour on bark cloth** — Siapo begins as plain bark cloth; colour and pattern turn it into Samoa's decorated cloth.
2. **The three processes and the upeti** — Fui immerses, elei rubs, tutusi/mamanu paints freehand — and the upeti board carries the carved pattern.
3. **Natural and synthetic dyes, evaluated** — Natural dyes come from plants and earth; synthetics from manufacture — each is judged by colour, fastness and suitability.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three siapo colouring processes and what each does.
2. Explain the upeti board's role.
3. Evaluate a dye for a fibre.
## What you need
A decorated bark cloth or picture of siapo if available; sample dyed cloths to compare.
