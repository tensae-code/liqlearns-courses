# Weaving

## Do it first
Look closely at any woven cloth — a shirt, a sheet, a mat. Find threads running one way and threads crossing them. Follow one crossing thread with a fingertip: over, under, over, under.

## Sub lesson 1 — Warp and weft
Set up the warp first: parallel threads stretched in order. Then the weft does the travelling, crossing the warp again and again. Every woven cloth, from a shirt to a mat, is this one idea — two thread sets interlacing at right angles.

## Sub lesson 2 — The weave patterns
In plain weave the weft goes over one, under one — firm and simple. Twill crosses in steps, making diagonal lines and a strong cloth. Satin floats the thread over several warps, giving a smooth, shiny face. Hopsack groups threads for a loose, open texture. Terry leaves loops standing, which drink water — the towel weave.

## Sub lesson 3 — What each weave produces
Choose the weave for the job. A towel needs terry loops; a work shirt needs the firmness of plain or twill; a dress may want satin's smooth face. Weaving is engineering in thread: structure first, surface second.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: weaving interlaces two sets of threads — warp lengthwise, weft across — and the pattern of interlacing makes plain, satin, hopsack, twill and terry weaves, each producing a different cloth. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
