# Lesson plan — Textile fibres

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Textile fibres
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Finland expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every textile starts as a fibre, and fibres fall into families — synthetic, cellulose-based regenerated, protein-based, and animal fibres such as sheep's wool — each with its own properties, life cycle and care.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student learning material — Finland's *Katse tulevaan* (TuleVa project learning material, published via aoe.fi) (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (learning material for craft teaching, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Synthetic and regenerated fibres** — Synthetic fibres are made by people from chemical building blocks; they are a large family of modern textile fibres.
2. **Protein-based and animal fibres** — Protein-based fibres come from protein material.
3. **Properties, life cycle and care — read the fibre, not just the fabric** — The Finnish material cards treat every fibre the same three ways: its properties (how it feels, holds warmth, takes moisture and wears), its life cycle (where it comes from, how it is made into textile, and what happens at the end), and its handling and care (washing, drying and storing it so it lasts).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain synthetic and regenerated fibres in your own words.
2. Explain protein-based and animal fibres in your own words.
3. Explain properties, life cycle and care — read the fibre, not just the fabric in your own words.
## What you need
If available, two or three clothing labels listing fibre content, to practise with; otherwise paper and pencil.
