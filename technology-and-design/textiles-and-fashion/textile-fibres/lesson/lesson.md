# Textile fibres

## Do it first
Find the label in a piece of clothing and read its fibre content. Write down each fibre named. Is any of them wool or another animal fibre? Keep the label notes — this lesson sorts those names into families.

## Sub lesson 1 — Synthetic and regenerated fibres
Synthetic fibres are made by people from chemical building blocks; they are a large family of modern textile fibres. Regenerated fibres are different: they start from cellulose — plant material — which is dissolved and formed again into a fibre. Both families are manufactured, but their starting material differs, and that difference runs through their whole life cycle, from production to disposal.

## Sub lesson 2 — Protein-based and animal fibres
Protein-based fibres come from protein material. The animal fibres in the Finnish cards are led by sheep's wool, with other animal fibres beside it: angora, mohair and cashmere. Each animal fibre has its own feel and strength — cashmere and angora are prized for softness, mohair for its lustre and resilience, wool for warmth and elasticity — and each needs care that respects that it came from an animal.

## Sub lesson 3 — Properties, life cycle and care — read the fibre, not just the fabric
The Finnish material cards treat every fibre the same three ways: its properties (how it feels, holds warmth, takes moisture and wears), its life cycle (where it comes from, how it is made into textile, and what happens at the end), and its handling and care (washing, drying and storing it so it lasts). Learn to ask those three questions of any fibre and you can predict how a fabric will behave before you work with it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: every textile starts as a fibre, and fibres fall into families — synthetic, cellulose-based regenerated, protein-based, and animal fibres such as sheep's wool — each with its own properties, life cycle and care. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
