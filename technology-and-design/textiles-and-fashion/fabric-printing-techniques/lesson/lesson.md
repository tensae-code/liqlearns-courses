# Fabric printing techniques

## Do it first
Look at a printed T-shirt, bag or curtain near you. Find where the pattern sits: is the colour all through the cloth, or does the pattern sit on the surface in definite shapes? Trace one repeated motif with your finger and keep it in mind.

## Sub lesson 1 — Printing a pattern with pigments
Fabric printing is not dyeing. In printing, a pattern (kuviointi) is applied onto the surface of a textile using pigments — colour carried in a printing paste or paint — so the design appears where the printer puts it and nowhere else. The rest of the fabric keeps its own colour. That is why printing is the technique for motifs, pictures and repeating patterns on cloth.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stencil and screen-style techniques
Among the techniques in the Finnish material's printing cards are stencil and screen-style printing. In stencil printing the pattern is cut into a stencil and colour is applied through its openings. In screen-style printing the pattern is held in a screen and colour is pushed through the open parts onto the fabric below. Both techniques let the same pattern be printed again and again, evenly, on piece after piece.

## Sub lesson 3 — Getting a clean print
A clean print is planned, not lucky. The fabric is laid flat and still, the stencil or screen is placed exactly where the pattern belongs, the pigment is applied evenly, and the stencil or screen is lifted away carefully so the wet pattern is not smudged. Test on a scrap first, let each print dry before stacking, and clean the stencil or screen so the next print stays sharp.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: fabric printing (kuviointi) puts a pattern onto a textile with pigments — the pattern sits in a design that is applied to the fabric surface, using techniques such as stencil and screen-style printing. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
