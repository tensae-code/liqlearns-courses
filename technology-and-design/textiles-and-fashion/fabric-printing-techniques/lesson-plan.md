# Lesson plan — Fabric printing techniques

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Fabric printing techniques
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Finland expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fabric printing (kuviointi) puts a pattern onto a textile with pigments — the pattern sits in a design that is applied to the fabric surface, using techniques such as stencil and screen-style printing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student learning material — Finland's *Katse tulevaan* (TuleVa project learning material, published via aoe.fi) (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (learning material for craft teaching, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Printing a pattern with pigments** — Fabric printing is not dyeing.
2. **Stencil and screen-style techniques** — Among the techniques in the Finnish material's printing cards are stencil and screen-style printing.
3. **Getting a clean print** — A clean print is planned, not lucky.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain printing a pattern with pigments in your own words.
2. Explain stencil and screen-style techniques in your own words.
3. Explain getting a clean print in your own words.
## What you need
Paper and pencil for the design work in this lesson; any real printing needs fabric, pigment and an adult's supervision with clean-up planned.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of a stencil laid on flat fabric with pigment being dabbed through its cut openings, the finished printed motif showing beside it — the student points to the stencil openings and to where the pattern appears on the fabric.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a screen being lifted away from fabric to reveal a sharp printed motif underneath — the student watches the moment the pattern appears and checks its edges.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the clean-print steps aloud — fabric flat and still, stencil or screen placed exactly, pigment applied evenly, lift carefully, let it dry — the student repeats the steps in order.
   - ADD VIDEO: a short video of the lesson demonstration — see video-script.md.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's key rule aloud.
4. **Questions** — the questions/ files, spread over the sub lessons.
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block in lesson/lesson.md.
