# Sewing tools and equipment

## Do it first
Lay out any sewing tools you can find at home. Sort them: cutting, measuring, joining, marking. Which pile is missing its tool? That gap is what a basic kit fills.

## Sub lesson 1 — Scissors — the cutting tool
Paper blunts the blades meant for fabric. Keep one pair for cloth only, wipe them clean, store them closed and dry, and carry them point-down or in a case. A sharp, true pair cuts cleanly without chewing the edge.

## Sub lesson 2 — The tape measure
Measure flat, keep the tape straight and not stretched, and read at eye level. Bodies, fabric widths and pattern pieces are all sized with the tape — an early measuring error is sewn into everything that follows.

## Sub lesson 3 — The basic kit and its care
A basic kit is small: hand needles, pins, threads, a tape measure, scissors and a way to mark cloth. Care is part of the skill — rusty pins stain, bent needles wander, lost sharps endanger feet. Put every tool back in its place after use.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: sewing begins with a small kit used well: scissors chosen for cloth and cared for, a tape measure for true sizes, and basic equipment kept clean, sharp and safe. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
