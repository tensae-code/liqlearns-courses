# Lesson plan — From material to product

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** From material to product
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Finland expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a product starts long before sewing: yarns differ by their material and by how they were spun, and careful pattern drafting (kaavoitus) plans the pieces so the garment or product is cut economically, with little waste.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student learning material — Finland's *Katse tulevaan* (TuleVa project learning material, published via aoe.fi) (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (learning material for craft teaching, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Yarns differ by material** — A yarn is fibres spun together into a thread, and its material decides much of its behaviour.
2. **Yarns differ by spinning method** — The same fibre can become different yarns.
3. **Pattern drafting (kaavoitus) — cut economically** — Pattern drafting, kaavoitus in the Finnish material, is drawing the product's pieces carefully before any cutting.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain yarns differ by material in your own words.
2. Explain yarns differ by spinning method in your own words.
3. Explain pattern drafting (kaavoitus) — cut economically in your own words.
## What you need
Paper, pencil and a ruler for drafting practice; a ball or skein of yarn to examine if available.

1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of a paper pattern draft laid over fabric, its pieces fitted closely together with small gaps, a pencil and ruler beside it — the student points to how the pieces are arranged to waste little material.
