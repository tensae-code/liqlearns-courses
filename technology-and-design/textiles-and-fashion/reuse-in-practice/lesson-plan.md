# Lesson plan — Reuse in practice

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Reuse in practice
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Finland expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** old material is new material: a worn pair of jeans (kierrätysfarkut, recycled jeans) can become a new product, and a careful maker plans the work to produce as little waste as possible.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student learning material — Finland's *Katse tulevaan* (TuleVa project learning material, published via aoe.fi) (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (learning material for craft teaching, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Recycled jeans as a material** — Denim from old jeans is a strong, proven material: it has already survived years of wear, and a pair of jeans carries ready-made riches — pockets, strong seams, belt loops and large flat pieces of cloth in the legs.
2. **A new product from old jeans — the teaching pilot** — The Finnish material teaches this as a pilot for 7–9-year-olds: children take recycled jeans and make a new product from them.
3. **Minimise the waste your own work produces** — The pilot's second theme is the maker's own waste: plan so that your work produces as little waste as possible.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain recycled jeans as a material in your own words.
2. Explain a new product from old jeans — the teaching pilot in your own words.
3. Explain minimise the waste your own work produces in your own words.
## What you need
No cutting is required for the reading; for the hands-on planning, paper and pencil. Real making from jeans needs an adult's help with cutting tools.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of a worn pair of jeans laid flat with its useful parts labelled — leg cloth, pockets, strong seams, belt loops — and a sketch of a new bag beside it — the student points to each labelled part and says what it could become.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a pair of jeans being turned page-like through three sketches — jeans, jeans with cut lines marked, and the finished new product — the student follows which part of the jeans becomes which part of the product.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the pilot steps aloud — choose the product, plan the parts, cut economically, make it, finish it — the student repeats the steps in order.
   - ADD VIDEO: a short video of the lesson demonstration — see video-script.md.

4. **Questions** — the questions/ files, spread over the sub lessons.
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block in lesson/lesson.md.
