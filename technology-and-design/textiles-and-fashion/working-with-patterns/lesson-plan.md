# Lesson plan — Working with patterns

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Working with patterns
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a pattern becomes a garment only if it is laid out right: know the fabric width, lay pieces on the grain, cut on the fold where the pattern says, and place the largest pieces first.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Fabric width and the layout** — Fabric comes in widths — the book lays out a puletasi top on 90 cm and on 115 cm fabric — and the width decides the layout.
2. **On the grain and on the fold** — Pattern pieces carry a grain line that must lie along the fabric's lengthwise threads, and symmetrical pieces are cut on the fold.
3. **Largest pieces first** — Place the biggest pattern pieces first, then fit the smaller ones into the gaps.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Plan a layout for a given fabric width.
2. Lay a piece on the grain and on the fold correctly.
3. Explain largest-pieces-first.
## What you need
Paper pattern pieces and a length of cloth or large paper for layout practice.
