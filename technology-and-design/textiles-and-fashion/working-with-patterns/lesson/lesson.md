# Working with patterns

## Do it first
Fold a piece of paper in half and draw half a shirt shape against the fold. Cut it out and open it: a whole, symmetrical piece. That is cutting on the fold — and if your drawn edge had slipped off the fold, the opened piece would be lopsided.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fabric width and the layout
The same pattern pieces fit differently on narrow and wide cloth. Plan the layout on the actual width before cutting: pieces may turn or nest together on wide fabric that must run lengthwise on narrow. Lay everything out first; cut only when the whole garment fits.

## Sub lesson 2 — On the grain and on the fold
Cloth cut off-grain hangs twisted. Keep the grain line parallel to the selvedge, measuring at both ends. Where the pattern marks a fold, place that edge exactly on the folded fabric so the cut piece opens out whole and symmetrical.

## Sub lesson 3 — Largest pieces first
Big pieces have the fewest possible homes; small pieces can fill leftovers. Largest-first laying wastes the least fabric and guarantees the main pieces fit before the cloth is committed.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a pattern becomes a garment only if it is laid out right: know the fabric width, lay pieces on the grain, cut on the fold where the pattern says, and place the largest pieces first. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
