# Lesson plan — Traditional sewing and embroidery techniques

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Traditional sewing and embroidery techniques
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Moldova expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** traditional embroidery decorates the folk world: garments and household and ritual objects carry traditional ornament and colour, worked with the tradition's sewing and embroidery techniques and ornamental stitches.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Moldova's *Educație tehnologică* (Technological Education) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Moldova counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Where the embroidery lives** — Grades 5–6 open embroidery from the tradition: folk garments decorated with embroidery, and household and ritual objects decorated the same way.
2. **Traditional ornament, colour and stitches** — The tradition has its language: traditional ornament and colour, worked with sewing and embroidery techniques and ornamental stitches.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe where traditional embroidery lives — garments, household and ritual objects.
2. Work ornamental stitches in traditional ornament and colour.
3. Explain how Moldova's book teaches it.
## What you need
Canvas, threads in traditional colours, and pictures of folk garments and ritual objects.
