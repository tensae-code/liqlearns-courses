# Lesson plan — Properties of fibres, yarns and fabrics

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Properties of fibres, yarns and fabrics
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fibres, yarns and fabrics each have properties — tensile strength above all — and manufacturers choose materials by the qualities a product needs.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Three levels of material** — A fibre is spun into a yarn; yarns are woven or knitted into a fabric — and each level has its own properties.
2. **Tensile strength** — Tensile strength is how much pulling a material stands before it breaks.
3. **The qualities manufacturers look for** — Strength is one quality among several: evenness, length, fineness and behaviour in making all count.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell fibre, yarn and fabric apart.
2. Define tensile strength and say how it is tested.
3. Name qualities besides strength that a manufacturer checks.
## What you need
Two different yarns or threads of clearly different strength.

2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a single fibre, the yarn it is twisted into, and the fabric woven from that yarn, side by side with a pulling test on the yarn — the student points to fibre, yarn and fabric and says where the pulling test acts.
