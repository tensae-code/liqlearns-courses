# Colour theory for textile design

## Do it first
Hold a coloured cloth against two different backgrounds — one dark, one light. Does the colour itself seem to change? It does not change; your eye's reading does. That is colour at work.

## Sub lesson 1 — The colour wheel and schemes
Neighbours on the wheel blend calmly; opposites strike against each other. A scheme is a deliberate pick — harmonious neighbours for a quiet cloth, opposites for a bold one. The wheel turns taste into a system a designer can plan and repeat.

## Sub lesson 2 — What colours do to people
A colour can feel warm or cool, festive or mourning, formal or playful — and the reading differs by culture and occasion. The textile designer works with these effects on purpose: the same pattern in different colours becomes a different garment.

## Sub lesson 3 — Optical illusions of colour
A colour looks lighter on a dark ground and darker on a light one; stripes can widen or slim a figure. Designers use these illusions deliberately — to flatter, to warn, to make a small pattern read at a distance.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: colour choice in textiles is designed, not guessed: the colour wheel organises hues into schemes, and colours carry psychological and cultural effects and optical illusions that drive clothing and textile decisions. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
