# Hand sewing stitches

## Do it first
Turn up the hem of a garment and look at the stitching that holds it. Can you see it from the right side? Good hemming hides. Now find a plain seam: its stitches march in and out in a straight, even row.

## Sub lesson 1 — The running stitch
Thread the needle, knot the end, and take small even stitches along the line: down through the cloth, up again, the same length each time. Evenness is the whole skill — stitches of equal length and spacing hold evenly and look right.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hemming — close hemming
Fold the edge, press or pin it, then take small slanting stitches that catch a thread or two of the garment and a bite of the fold. Close hemming keeps the stitches small and close, so the hem sits flat and the stitching nearly disappears.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hemming a curve
On a curve the folded edge is longer than the line it must meet, so the extra fullness is eased in — small gathers or tiny tucks spread evenly — before hemming, or the hem will pucker and twist. Work slowly, a little at a time, checking the lie of the cloth as you go.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: hand sewing joins and finishes cloth with named stitches: the running stitch for seams and gathering, and hemming stitches — close hemming and hems on curves — that hold edges neatly and nearly invisibly. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
