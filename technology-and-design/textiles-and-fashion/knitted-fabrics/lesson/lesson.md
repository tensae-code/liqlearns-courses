# Knitted fabrics

## Do it first
Take a knitted garment (a T-shirt or jersey) and a woven one (a shirt). Stretch each gently widthwise. Which gives? Look very closely at the T-shirt: can you see loops instead of over-and-under crossings?

## Sub lesson 1 — Loops, not crossings
One yarn can make a whole knitted fabric: each new loop is drawn through the loop before it, row on row. Because the cloth is loops, it stretches — pull it and the loops change shape; let go and they spring back.

## Sub lesson 2 — Weft and warp knitting
In weft knitting a single yarn travels across, making single jersey (one face smooth, one rougher) and double jersey (two interlocked layers, firmer and smoother both sides). In warp knitting many yarns loop lengthwise at once, giving a more stable cloth that does not ladder as easily.

## Sub lesson 3 — Woven against knit
Lay the two side by side, as Samoa's book does: the woven holds its shape and frays at a cut edge; the knit gives in every direction, resists creasing, and can ladder if a loop breaks. Neither is better — each serves different garments.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: knitted fabrics are made of loops, not interlaced threads: weft knitting loops one yarn across, warp knitting loops yarns lengthwise — and looped cloth behaves differently from woven cloth. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
