# Matching decoration to the fibre

## Do it first
Put one drop of water on a cotton cloth and one on a raincoat or synthetic cloth. Watch: one sinks in, one sits on top. That single difference decides half of decorating.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fibres drink differently
Drop water on cotton and on a synthetic cloth: one drinks, the other beads. A dye or print paste is a liquid carried into or onto the fibre, so absorbency decides whether the colour takes evenly, faintly, or not at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Which decoration suits which fibre
A thirsty fibre accepts immersion dyeing and printing. A fibre that resists water may need a different dye class, a surface method, or a texture worked mechanically instead. The fibre's behaviour, not the decorator's wish, sets the menu.

## Sub lesson 3 — Test before you decorate
Decorate a scrap first: check the colour's strength, evenness and fastness on this fibre and fabric. The trial costs a scrap; skipping it can cost the whole piece.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: decoration must match the fibre: fibres differ in absorbency, so a dye, print or texture that sings on one fibre fails on another — the decorator tests absorbency first. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
