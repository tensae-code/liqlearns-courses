# Lesson plan — Adding texture to textiles

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Textiles and Fashion · **Lesson:** Adding texture to textiles
**Place in the curriculum:** Textiles and Fashion (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** texture is added to textiles by working material into the surface — feathers threaded and woven in, the textures of fine mats, and sinnet (sennit) work — so the cloth is felt as well as seen.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Texture is surface you can feel** — A textile's texture is how its surface feels and catches light: smooth, rough, raised, looped.
2. **Feathers threaded and woven in** — Feathers can be threaded and woven into a textile, adding softness, colour and value.
3. **Fine mats and sinnet work** — Fine-mat weaving gives fine, close texture; sinnet (sennit) cord work adds strong, patterned relief.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Textiles and Fashion. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define texture in textiles.
2. Describe how feathers are added to a textile.
3. Tell fine-mat texture from sinnet texture.
## What you need
Two textiles of clearly different texture to handle.
