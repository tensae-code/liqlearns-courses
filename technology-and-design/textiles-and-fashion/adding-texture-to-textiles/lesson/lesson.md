# Adding texture to textiles

## Do it first
Close your eyes and run your fingers over two cloths — one smooth printed cotton, one woven mat or textured garment. Describe each surface in three words. You have just read texture.

## Sub lesson 1 — Texture is surface you can feel
Decoration is not only colour. Raising part of the surface, laying material on it, or working a cord into it changes the hand-feel and the look together. Texture is chosen like colour: for a purpose and an effect.

## Sub lesson 2 — Feathers threaded and woven in
Each feather is prepared and secured into the weaving so it stays: threaded through, woven in, lying in the planned direction. The result is a surface no dye can imitate — real depth and movement.

## Sub lesson 3 — Fine mats and sinnet work
The fine mat's texture comes from narrow strips woven close — fineness itself is the achievement. Sinnet work lays a rolled coconut-fibre cord in patterns, giving raised lines that are both decoration and structure.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: texture is added to textiles by working material into the surface — feathers threaded and woven in, the textures of fine mats, and sinnet (sennit) work — so the cloth is felt as well as seen. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
