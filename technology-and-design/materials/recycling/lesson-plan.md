# Lesson plan — Recycling

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Recycling
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** recycling is part of the work process, not an afterthought: know why it matters, what the state policy asks, and how materials — even the mercury in fluorescent lamps — are recovered and returned to use, paper included.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's *Educación Laboral y Dibujo Básico — 7mo grado (2023 Biblioteca edition)* and the Cuba books in lesson/sources.md — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba counts in the acceptance count for its student-book placements; its programme and methodological-orientations placements are evidence only, counted nowhere.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why recycling matters** — Cuba's 2023 Grade 7 book gives recycling its own section (El reciclaje): recovering materials saves resources and protects the environment, and state environmental policy makes it a duty of the workplace, not a private hobby.
2. **Recovering, in practice** — The book's examples are concrete: recovering mercury from fluorescent lamps — a hazardous recovery done properly — and recycling paper. Recycling sits inside the work process itself: the maker plans recovery as one of the stages of working.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
