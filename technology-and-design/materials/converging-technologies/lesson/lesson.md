# Converging technologies

## Sub lesson 1 — Four families, one meeting point
Modern technology grows in four great families: nanotechnology works at the scale of atoms and molecules; biotechnology works with living systems; information technology works with data and communication; cognitive technology works with how minds and machines think. For a long time each family grew on its own. Now they increasingly meet and merge — a medical sensor, for example, may be built at nano scale, read living tissue, send its data through information networks and use cognitive software to judge what it finds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why convergence matters
When technologies converge, the result is more than the sum of its parts: each family lends the others its strength. Convergence is why single devices now sense, compute, communicate and act at once. Iran's book opens its new-materials module with convergence because the materials that follow — nanomaterials, composites, smart materials and biotechnology products — are exactly where the families meet.
