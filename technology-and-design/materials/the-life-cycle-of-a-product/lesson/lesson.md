# The life cycle of a product

## Do it first
Pick an object near you and tell its life story in five beats: designed, made, sold, used, and \u2014 one day \u2014 finished. What will become of it then? That last beat is where this lesson does its work.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 The stages of an object's life
A technical object's life runs in stages: it is designed, made, carried to the point of sale, bought and used, and one day it reaches end of life. Each stage raises questions. At design: what need, what materials? At the point of sale: how did it arrive, and in what state? At end of life: what becomes of it? Following the whole life, not only the using, is what life-cycle thinking means.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Sorting and recycling
At end of life, sorting (tri) is the act that decides what happens next: waste and materials separated into kinds, so that what can be recycled is recycled instead of buried or burned. The 3R rule orders the choices: reduce, reuse, recycle. Local practice makes it real \u2014 in Libreville, bottles are collected for recycling, and sorted material has a route back into making.

## Sub lesson 3 \u2014 What sorting saves
Sorting and recycling are worth the effort because of what they save. Recycling saves raw materials \u2014 oil, ores and wood stay in the ground or the forest \u2014 saves energy, because making from recovered material usually takes less than making from new, and reduces pollution from waste and from extraction. An object's life ends best when its materials begin another.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: every technical object has a life: designed, made, sold, used, and at the end of life sorted, recycled or thrown \u2014 and each stage raises its own questions. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
