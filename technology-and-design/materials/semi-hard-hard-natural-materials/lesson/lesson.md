# Semi-hard/hard natural materials

## Do it first
Look at semi-hard/hard natural materials around you — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood. What is it made of, what does it do, and who uses it? Tell a partner what you notice. Then learn how Indonesia's Prakarya books teach it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Semi-hard/hard natural materials
Semi-hard/hard natural materials: plant fibres, leather/hide, sand, bamboo, rattan, wood, stone, metal — properties and craft products. The book works it through: plant fibres, leather/hide, sand, bamboo, rattan, wood, stone, metal — properties and craft products. In Indonesia this topic sits in the Kerajinan (craft) strand of Prakarya / Prakarya dan Kewirausahaan, the school subject that joins craft, engineering, cultivation and processing in one course. The teacher guide works it at Unit II Kegiatan 1 material exploration (*Prakarya Kerajinan G7 TG*, p. 94).

## Sub lesson 2 — How it is done — the book's own sequence
The Indonesian books never jump straight to making. The sequence is: observe and explore first, then plan or design (merancang), then produce, then present the finished work — with packaging and a label where the product needs one — and reflect on what was made. Worked that way, semi-hard/hard natural materials is learned as a complete process, not a single trick: each step is checked before the next one begins.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Indonesian way — local potential, safety and care
Indonesia's own way is content here. Prakarya builds from local potential (potensi lokal): the materials, plants, animals and traditions of the learner's own region are the starting stock, and tools and materials are modified (modifikasi) to fit local conditions instead of copied unchanged. Safety procedures (Prosedur Keselamatan Kerja) come before any making begins, and waste is treated as material to use, not rubbish to throw away.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: semi-hard/hard natural materials: plant fibres, leather/hide, sand, bamboo, rattan, wood, stone, metal — properties and craft products. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
