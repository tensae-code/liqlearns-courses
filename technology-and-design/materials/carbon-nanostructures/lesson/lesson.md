## Sub lesson 1 — Fullerenes
A fullerene is a hollow cage of carbon atoms. The best known, C60, joins 60 carbon atoms into a ball of hexagons and pentagons, like a football; C70 makes a slightly longer cage. Because the cage is hollow and stable, fullerenes can carry other atoms and molecules and are studied for medicine, lubricants and electronics.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carbon nanotubes
A carbon nanotube is a sheet of carbon one atom thick, rolled into a seamless tube. Tubes may be single-walled or multi-walled, and their properties change with the roll's angle. Nanotubes are far stronger than steel at a fraction of the weight and conduct electricity and heat well, so they are used to strengthen composites and in electronic devices.
