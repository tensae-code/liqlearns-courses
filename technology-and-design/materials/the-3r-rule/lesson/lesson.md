# The 3R rule \u2014 repair, reuse, recycle

## Do it first
Hold an object you might throw away. Ask the three questions in order: Could I have reduced this waste? Can this be reused? Can it be repaired or recycled? The order you asked in is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Reduce first, reuse before recycling
The 3R rule is an order, not a list. Reduce comes first: make less waste to begin with. Reuse comes second: use the thing, or its materials, again as they are. Recycle comes last \u2014 not because it is bad, but because recycling costs energy: sorting, collecting and re-making all take work. Whoever recycles before reducing and reusing has skipped the cheaper steps.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Repair, reuse, recycling \u2014 the end-of-life choices
At an object's end of life, the 3R questions become practical: can it be repaired and live on? Can it be reused, by someone, for something? Only when both answers are no does recycling take it. Repairing, reusing and recycling objects instead of throwing them away is the habit the rule builds \u2014 each kept object is raw material and energy not spent.

## Sub lesson 3 \u2014 Judging by life-cycle criteria
Designers use the same thinking before the object exists. A technical object can be designed and judged by life-cycle criteria: the materials used, the making cost, the energy needed, the possibility of repair, and the possibility of recycling. An object that scores well on those criteria is easier on the world at every stage of its life.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: at end of life, order matters: reduce first, reuse before recycling (recycling costs energy), repair what can be repaired \u2014 and judge objects by life-cycle criteria before they are made. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks \u2014 fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
