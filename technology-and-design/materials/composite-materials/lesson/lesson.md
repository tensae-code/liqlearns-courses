# Composite materials

## Sub lesson 1 — Matrix and reinforcement
Every composite has two parts. The matrix is the continuous material that surrounds and binds; the reinforcement is the stronger part embedded in it — fibres, particles or layers. Straw-and-mud is the oldest example: mud is the matrix, straw the reinforcement, and the brick holds together far better than mud alone. Modern composites repeat the same idea with resins, glass fibre, carbon fibre and metals.

## Sub lesson 2 — How composites are made
Iran's book surveys the making routes: powder metallurgy, casting, hot pressing, sintering, extrusion and injection, and hand lay-up of fibre sheets. The route follows the matrix — metal, polymer or ceramic — and the shape wanted. Composites earn their cost where lightness with strength matters: electronics, aerospace and cars are the book's named fields of use.

## Say it to your companion
