# How nanomaterials are made

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Top-down and bottom-up
Top-down making starts from a bulk piece and breaks, cuts or grinds it down to nano size — like carving a statue from a block. Bottom-up making starts from atoms and molecules and assembles them into nano structures — like building a wall brick by brick. Iran's book surveys both families of methods; the choice between them decides the product's purity, shape and cost.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nanoparticles at work
The book's worked examples are nanoparticles: titanium dioxide (TiO2) nanoparticles act as photocatalysts, speeding chemical reactions in light — used in self-cleaning and air-cleaning surfaces; magnetic iron-oxide nanoparticles can be guided and detected by magnets, which makes them useful in medicine and imaging. A nanoparticle earns its place by doing a job its bulk form cannot do.
