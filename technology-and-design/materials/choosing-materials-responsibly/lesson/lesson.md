# Choosing materials responsibly

## Do it first
Look at one material or product near you — its packaging, label or tag. Write down everything the label tells you: what it is made of, any safety marks, any eco-labels or certificates. Keep the list for the checks in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Safe first — hazard and chemical checks
The first check is safety. A responsible material is a safe, non-hazardous product: it should not endanger the maker, the user, or the people who later repair or recycle it. Chemical criteria are part of that check — read the safety data sheet for a product when one exists, and treat unknown chemicals as a reason to stop and find out more, not as a detail to ignore.

## Sub lesson 2 — Labels and certificates you can check
Trusted marks help you check a claim instead of just believing it. The EU eco-label marks products that meet European environmental criteria. Certificates and standards — ISO criteria and the Oeko-Tex mark for textiles — show that a material has been tested against a named rule. A label is only useful if you know what it stands for, so learn the few marks used in craft work and look for them when you choose.

## Sub lesson 3 — Circular use, no waste, reliable suppliers
Responsible choosing also looks past the purchase. Circular-economy thinking asks: can this material be reused, repaired into something, or recycled at the end? Resource-efficient use asks: am I using only what the product needs? And the supplier matters too — choose suppliers whose reliability and responsibility you can trust, because a cheap material from an unknown source can carry hidden costs in safety and waste.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a responsible material choice is checked before you buy or use it — it is safe, it carries trusted labels and certificates, it fits circular-economy thinking, it is used without waste, and it comes from a reliable supplier. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
