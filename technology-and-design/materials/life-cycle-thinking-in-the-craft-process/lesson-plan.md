# Lesson plan — Life-cycle thinking in the craft process

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Life-cycle thinking in the craft process
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Finland expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a craft product has a whole life — material, technique, design, making, care and repair, and recycling — and a good maker thinks about every stage before starting, not only the making.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student learning material — Finland's *Katse tulevaan* (TuleVa project learning material, published via aoe.fi) (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (learning material for craft teaching, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The start of the map — material, technique, design** — The Finnish craft process map begins before anything is made.
2. **Making, and care and repair** — MAKING is the middle of the life, not the whole of it: work safety, practising the technique first, and material trials come before the finished product.
3. **Recycling — the map closes the circle** — The last stage is RECYCLING: sorting waste properly, reusing material, and using leftover material for something new instead of binning it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Materials. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain the start of the map — material, technique, design in your own words.
2. Explain making, and care and repair in your own words.
3. Explain recycling — the map closes the circle in your own words.
## What you need
Paper and pencil for drawing your own life-cycle map; no tools or materials are made with in this lesson.
