# Life-cycle thinking in the craft process

## Do it first
Think of one thing you own that was made — a bag, a toy or a piece of clothing. Write down everything that happened to it before you got it, while you use it, and what will happen when it is worn out. Keep the list: the lesson gives that list a shape.

## Sub lesson 1 — The start of the map — material, technique, design
The Finnish craft process map begins before anything is made. MATERIAL comes first, judged on responsibility, economy and durability: where it comes from, what it costs the maker and the world, and how long it will last. TECHNIQUE follows: which tools, which workspace, and how the work stays safe. DESIGN comes next: sketches, experiments and prototypes test the idea, and one hard question is asked — is this product really necessary at all?

## Sub lesson 2 — Making, and care and repair
MAKING is the middle of the life, not the whole of it: work safety, practising the technique first, and material trials come before the finished product. Then the product goes out to be used, and its life continues with CARE AND REPAIR: cleaning it properly, replacing parts that wear out, and repairing damage instead of throwing the product away. A product that can be cleaned and repaired lives far longer than one that cannot.

## Sub lesson 3 — Recycling — the map closes the circle
The last stage is RECYCLING: sorting waste properly, reusing material, and using leftover material for something new instead of binning it. Life-cycle thinking (elinkaariajattelu, in the Finnish material) means holding this whole map in mind at every stage — a choice at the material stage decides how hard repair will be, and a choice in design decides how much waste the making leaves behind.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a craft product has a whole life — material, technique, design, making, care and repair, and recycling — and a good maker thinks about every stage before starting, not only the making. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
