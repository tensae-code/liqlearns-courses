# Lesson plan — Marquetry/inlay work

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Wood · **Lesson:** Marquetry/inlay work
**Place in the curriculum:** Wood (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** marquetry (tarso) makes pictures and patterns in wood itself: pieces of wood are cut to a design and set together into the surface, so the decoration is the material, not a coating on it.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's Educación Laboral programmes and methodological orientations (teacher programme documents) — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba is evidence only for this lesson, counted nowhere in the acceptance count.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The tarso technique** — Cuba's Grade 5 guidance teaches marquetry as the tarso technique (§2.4 of the wood unit): a design is realised by cutting wood pieces to shape and fitting them together into one surface.
2. **Realising a tarso article** — The realisation runs: design first, pieces cut to the design, fitted edge to edge, the whole set and finished as one article. The grain and tone of each piece are part of the palette — the maker chooses wood the way a painter chooses colour.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
