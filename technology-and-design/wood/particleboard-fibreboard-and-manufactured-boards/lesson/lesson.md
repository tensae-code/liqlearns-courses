# Particleboard, fibreboard and manufactured boards

## Do it first
A tree gives planks a few hand-widths wide. A wardrobe door is wider than any plank. Where does a sheet that wide come from? It is manufactured — built from pieces of wood too small to use alone.

## Sub lesson 1 — Plywood — crossed veneers
Crossing the grains is the invention: the board resists splitting in every direction and stays flat. The bond type grades the glue's weather resistance — from interior-only to marine — and an overlay can give the face a finished surface. Choose plywood by layers, bond and face.

## Sub lesson 2 — MDF and particle board — fibres and chips
MDF's fineness is its virtue: it cuts and paints to a smooth finish with no grain to tear — but screws hold in its face, not its edge (the face-only fixing rule). Particle board is cheaper and coarser, fine where it is covered and lightly loaded.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hardboard — dense and tempered
Hardboard serves as panelling, backing and lining — thin but tough. Tempering upgrades it for harder service. All four boards share one idea: small wood, re-formed with glue and pressure, becomes a large flat sheet no tree could give.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: manufactured boards are engineered from wood pieces: plywood is veneers glued in crossed layers, MDF is fibres pressed to a smooth board fixed through the face, particle board is chips formed and pressed, and hardboard is a dense fibre board, tempered for hardness — each graded and bonded for its use. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
