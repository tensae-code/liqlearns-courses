# Protecting and preserving timber

## Do it first
Two fence posts: one treated, one plain. In a few wet seasons one stands and one rots at the ground line. The difference is invisible — it is inside the wood. What put it there?

## Sub lesson 1 — Why timber needs preserving
Outdoor and ground-contact timber fails in years untreated. Preservation is not paint: it changes the wood itself, deep inside, so the organisms that would eat it cannot.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pressure treatment
Pressure does what brushing cannot: it drives the preservative through the whole sapwood, not just the surface. The treated timber keeps its strength and shape — only its hospitality to rot and insects is gone.

## Sub lesson 3 — CCA and the preservative classes
In CCA (copper chrome arsenate), each component has a job — the copper fights fungi, the arsenic fights insects, and the chrome fixes the preservatives into the wood so they stay. Preservative classes grade treatment from low hazard (interior, dry) to high (ground contact, marine): the class on the stamp must match the place the timber will serve.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: timber is preserved by pressure treatment: preservative is forced deep into the wood — Samoa teaches the CCA water-borne preservative and what each of its components does — and preservative classes match the treatment to the hazard the timber will face. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
