# Lesson plan — Protecting and preserving timber

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Wood · **Lesson:** Protecting and preserving timber
**Place in the curriculum:** Wood (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** timber is preserved by pressure treatment: preservative is forced deep into the wood — Samoa teaches the CCA water-borne preservative and what each of its components does — and preservative classes match the treatment to the hazard the timber will face.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why timber needs preserving** — Untreated timber is food: fungi rot it, insects eat it, weather opens it — preservation poisons the food and seals the fibres.
2. **Pressure treatment** — In pressure treatment the timber is sealed in a vessel and preservative is forced in under pressure until the wood is impregnated.
3. **CCA and the preservative classes** — CCA is a water-borne preservative whose components share the work; preservatives are classed by the hazard they protect against.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Wood. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why timber is preserved.
2. Describe pressure treatment.
3. Give each CCA component's job and the idea of hazard classes.
## What you need
No chemicals handled; a piece of treated timber's stamp to read if available.
