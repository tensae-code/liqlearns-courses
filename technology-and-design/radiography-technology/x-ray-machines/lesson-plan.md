# Lesson plan — X-ray machines

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Radiography Technology · **Lesson:** X-ray machines
**Place in the curriculum:** Radiography Technology (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Canada expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an X-ray machine sends invisible X-ray energy through the body: it passes through soft parts but is stopped by dense bone, so the special picture shows bones white and soft parts dark — a view inside the body without opening it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student reader — Canada's *Technology in the Community* (Tulliniliara Reading Series, Level 14), Government of Nunavut / Inhabit Education (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **What X-rays are** — X-rays are a form of invisible energy, like light — but unlike light, they can pass through many things that light cannot.
2. **Why bones show white and soft parts show dark** — Not everything lets X-rays through equally.
3. **What X-rays are used for** — Doctors and health workers use X-ray pictures to find broken bones, to look for signs of illness inside the body, and dentists use them to check teeth, including parts hidden below the gum.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Radiography Technology. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what X-rays are and how they differ from light.
2. Explain why bone shows white and skin and muscle show black on an X-ray picture.
3. Name three uses of X-ray machines.
## What you need
Nothing special — a partner to talk with. Learners never operate X-ray equipment; this lesson is learned from the explanation and the activities.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of an X-ray image of a hand — white bones clear against dark soft parts — beside a normal photo of the same hand — the student points to one bone in both pictures and says why it shows white.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of X-ray rays passing through a drawn arm: rays crossing the soft parts reach the screen, rays meeting the bone are stopped, and a white bone shape forms — the student explains the white shape as it appears.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the rule aloud — through soft parts, shown dark; stopped by bone, shown white — the student repeats the rule.
   - ADD VIDEO: a short video of the lesson demonstration — see video-script.md.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's key rule aloud.
4. **Questions** — the questions/ files, spread over the sub lessons.
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block in lesson/lesson.md.
