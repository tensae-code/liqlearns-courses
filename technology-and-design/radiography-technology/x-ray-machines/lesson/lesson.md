# X-ray machines

## Do it first
Hold your hand up to a bright light: the light stops at your skin and you see only the outside. Doctors sometimes need to see the bones inside, without any cut. How could invisible energy do that job? Tell a partner your ideas, then learn how.

## Sub lesson 1 — What X-rays are
X-rays are a form of invisible energy, like light — but unlike light, they can pass through many things that light cannot. An X-ray machine aims this energy at the part of the body being examined, and a special paper or screen on the other side catches the picture the energy makes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why bones show white and soft parts show dark
Not everything lets X-rays through equally. Skin and muscle let them pass, so those parts show black on the special paper. Dense bone stops them, so bone shows white. The result is a picture of the inside of the body: white bones standing out against dark soft parts, each bone's shape clear to read.

## Sub lesson 3 — What X-rays are used for
Doctors and health workers use X-ray pictures to find broken bones, to look for signs of illness inside the body, and dentists use them to check teeth, including parts hidden below the gum. The picture guides the care that follows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: an X-ray machine sends invisible X-ray energy through the body: it passes through soft parts but is stopped by dense bone, so the special picture shows bones white and soft parts dark — a view inside the body without opening it. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
