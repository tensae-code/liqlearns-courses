# Reading a real wiring diagram

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the diagram shows
A real wiring diagram shows the whole installation as connected: the supply, every switch, socket and lamp, and the wire runs joining them. Unlike a picture of the finished board, the diagram is read by tracing: follow each wire from its start to its end and name every part it touches. Iran's Grade 9 Electricity module teaches diagram-reading on the real diagram of the circuits the class is about to wire.

## Sub lesson 2 — From diagram to board
The diagram decides the work before the work begins: which parts are needed, where each sits, and which wire joins what. A wiring error found on the diagram costs a pencil line; the same error on the board costs rewiring — or worse. Reading the diagram is therefore a practical skill with a safety weight, not a drawing exercise.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a wiring diagram is the circuit's map: it shows how every wire and part connects, and reading it truly — tracing each run — comes before touching a wire. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
