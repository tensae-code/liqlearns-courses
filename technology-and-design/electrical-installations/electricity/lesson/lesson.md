# Electricity

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sources and effects of the current
Cuba's 1989 Grade 7 book opens electricity with generation — chemical and magnetic — and then the current's three effects: thermal, luminous and magnetic. Every device the learner will meet later is one of these effects put to work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Conductors and insulators
Materials divide by what they do to the current: conductors carry it, insulators block it. The division is practical, not abstract — it decides what wires are made of, what they are covered with, and what may be touched safely.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: electricity begins with where the current comes from and what it does: generated chemically and magnetically, the current shows itself in heat, light and magnetism — and matter either lets it pass (conductors) or stops it (insulators). Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
