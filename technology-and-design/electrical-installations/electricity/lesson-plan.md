# Lesson plan — Electricity

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electrical Installations and the Home · **Lesson:** Electricity
**Place in the curriculum:** Electrical Installations and the Home (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** electricity begins with where the current comes from and what it does: generated chemically and magnetically, the current shows itself in heat, light and magnetism — and matter either lets it pass (conductors) or stops it (insulators).
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's *Educación Laboral — Trabajos de Taller y Dibujo Básico — 7mo grado (1989 edition)* and the Cuba books in lesson/sources.md — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba counts in the acceptance count for its student-book placements; its programme and methodological-orientations placements are evidence only, counted nowhere.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sources and effects of the current** — Cuba's 1989 Grade 7 book opens electricity with generation — chemical and magnetic — and then the current's three effects: thermal, luminous and magnetic. Every device the learner will meet later is one of these effects put to work.
2. **Conductors and insulators** — Materials divide by what they do to the current: conductors carry it, insulators block it. The division is practical, not abstract — it decides what wires are made of, what they are covered with, and what may be touched safely.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
