# Joining electrical conductors — splices

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three splices
Cuba's Grade 8 course names its splices: the rabo de cochino (pigtail) twists conductors together for a simple join; the Western Union splice joins in line for strength under pull; the T-derivation takes a branch off a running conductor without cutting its run.

## Sub lesson 2 — Insulating the splice
A bare splice is unfinished work and a danger: the join is insulated so it cannot touch other conductors or hands. The rule closes every splicing lesson — join, check the join carries and holds, then insulate.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a splice is a joint that must act like unbroken wire: the pigtail (rabo de cochino), the Western Union and the T-derivation each join conductors for a different situation — and every splice is finished by insulating it. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
