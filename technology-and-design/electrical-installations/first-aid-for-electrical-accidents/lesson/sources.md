| electrical accidents: what happens when current passes through the body (muscle spasm at first, then paralysis of blood circulation and breathing, then death; severity depends on current magnitude, duration and path through the body) and the three main cause groups — (1) direct contact with live things (bare wires, sockets, leaking appliances with damaged insulation), when you must cut the breaker and use safe tools to repair; (2) violating the safety protection distances to the high-voltage grid and transformer stations (air discharge can shock without contact) — the official clearance table by voltage (to 22 kV: 1–2 m; 35 kV: 1.5–3 m; 110 kV: 4 m; 220 kV: 6 m; 500 kV: 7 m wide; vertical clearances 2–6 m) and the electrical warning signs (no climbing — high voltage, danger of death; do not switch on — people working; warning — electricity above); (3) approaching a place where a live wire has fallen to the ground, whose surroundings can shock without direct contact (original string `electrical accidents`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 8*, printed p. 60 |
| first aid for a victim of an electrical accident: first separate the victim from the power source — switch off the nearest switching device (switch, breaker), wear protective equipment (rubber sandals, insulating gloves…) and use insulating objects (stand on a wooden board or insulating mat, use a dry wooden or bamboo stick to push the wire or the victim away), and never touch the victim directly while the source is not cut; then first aid on the spot — step 1 check the victim's state (if conscious, loosen clothing and move them to a convenient place and call for help; if unconscious, not breathing or breathing unevenly, with convulsions, start chest compressions and rescue breathing at once); step 2 artificial respiration — chest compressions (kneel beside the victim, stacked hands on the chest, press fast and strong 5–6 cm deep, release fully; about 100–120 per minute) and rescue breaths by nose-to-nose or mouth-to-mouth (16–20 per minute until the victim breathes again; ratios — alone, 2 breaths after every 15 compressions; two rescuers, 1 breath after every 5 compressions); step 3 take the victim to the nearest medical facility (original string `first aid for a victim of an electrical accident`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 8*, printed p. 67 |
