# Repairing electrical installations

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Finding the fault: continuity
Repair begins with diagnosis: a continuity check shows whether a cable, a resistance or a fuse still carries the current or is broken inside. Cuba's books teach the check first — on cables, resistances and fuses — before any part is replaced.

## Sub lesson 2 — Repairing plugs, receptacles and switches
The repairs themselves are the small ones of home and school: plugs, receptacles (tomas) and switches, opened, checked, reconnected or replaced. The safety norms govern every step, and the 1989 book extends the craft to appliances — the electric iron, lighter and cooker, and fluorescent-lamp faults.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: small electrical repairs are a taught craft: check continuity to find the fault, then repair plugs, receptacles and switches — always inside the safety norms, and never on a live circuit. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
