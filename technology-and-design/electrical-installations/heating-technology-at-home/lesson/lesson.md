# Heating technology at home

## Do it first
On a cold day, a home stays warm in every room, even rooms far from any fire. Where does the warmth start, and how does it travel to the far rooms? Tell a partner your ideas, then trace the real path.

## Sub lesson 1 — The boiler furnace and the hot water
The heart of the system is a boiler furnace: it burns fuel and uses the heat to make water hot. A pump then pushes the hot water out through pipes to radiators in the rooms around the house. Each radiator gives the water's heat to the room's air, the cooled water returns to the boiler, and the circle starts again.

## Sub lesson 2 — The thermostat controls the heat
A thermostat is the system's controller. It senses how warm the room is and controls how much heated water is pumped: when the room is cold it calls for more hot water, and when the room is warm enough it eases the flow back. That is how a home holds a steady, comfortable temperature instead of swinging between too hot and too cold.

## Sub lesson 3 — Other burning-fuel heaters, and the people who keep heating running
Simpler heaters work on the same burning-fuel idea: a Coleman stove burns fuel to heat the space around it, and a qulliq — the traditional Inuit lamp-stove — heats by burning fuel in the same way. Whatever the heater, someone must keep it running: plumbers look after the water and pipes, and furnace technicians look after the furnace itself.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a boiler furnace burns fuel to heat water, the hot water is pumped to radiators around the rooms, and a thermostat decides how much heated water flows — so the whole home stays warm. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
