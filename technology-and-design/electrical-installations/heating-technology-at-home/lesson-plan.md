# Lesson plan — Heating technology at home

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electrical Installations and the Home · **Lesson:** Heating technology at home
**Place in the curriculum:** Electrical Installations and the Home (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Canada expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a boiler furnace burns fuel to heat water, the hot water is pumped to radiators around the rooms, and a thermostat decides how much heated water flows — so the whole home stays warm.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student reader — Canada's *Technology in the Community* (Tulliniliara Reading Series, Level 14), Government of Nunavut / Inhabit Education (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The boiler furnace and the hot water** — The heart of the system is a boiler furnace: it burns fuel and uses the heat to make water hot.
2. **The thermostat controls the heat** — A thermostat is the system's controller.
3. **Other burning-fuel heaters, and the people who keep heating running** — Simpler heaters work on the same burning-fuel idea: a Coleman stove burns fuel to heat the space around it, and a qulliq — the traditional Inuit lamp-stove — heats by burning fuel in the same way.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Electrical Installations and the Home. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the path heat takes from the boiler furnace to a room.
2. Explain what a thermostat does.
3. Name other burning-fuel heaters and the workers who keep heating systems running.
## What you need
Nothing special — a partner to talk with. Real furnaces and stoves are hot and are adults' work; this lesson is learned from the explanation and the activities, never by opening a heater.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of a house cut open showing a boiler furnace, pipes carrying hot water to radiators in three rooms, and a thermostat on a wall — the student traces the hot water's circle with a finger.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of hot water flowing from a boiler through pipes to a radiator, the radiator glowing as the room warms, then the thermostat dial easing the flow back — the student says what the thermostat did.
