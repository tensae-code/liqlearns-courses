| sizing the home system's devices by calculation: the total consumed power P = ΣPi (allowing for future loads, loads not running simultaneously, and loads not always at full rating); the current through a conductor from P = U × I × cos φ, so I = P/(U × cos φ), with power factor cos φ = 0.8 for motor loads and 1.0 for others such as lamps and fridges; the conductor cross-section S = I/J, where J is the permitted current density (for copper conductors about 6 A/mm² in the worked example: a 2,000 W load at cos φ = 0.8 draws 11.4 A, needing about 1.9 mm², so a standard 2.0 mm² conductor is chosen from the standard sizes 0.5–6.0 mm²); and the breaker's rated current Idm = I × hat, with the safety factor hat = 1.2 for non-motor loads and 2.0–2.5 for motor loads (worked: a 2,200 W water heater draws 10 A, so a 12 A breaker is needed; an 850 W water pump at cos φ = 0.8 draws 4.83 A, takes a 1 mm² conductor, and with hat = 2 needs a 10 A breaker — MCB1P/10A or MCCB1P/10A from the market table of common ratings 6–32 A) (original string `sizing the home system's devices by calculation`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 12 — Công nghệ điện – điện tử*, printed p. 47 |
