# The hydraulic system

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — The hydraulic system
Greece's book teaches it this way: hydraulic terms and Pascal's law, mechanical advantage, hydraulic fluids and contamination, the basic hydraulic circuit (reservoirs, pumps, valves, accumulators, actuators, motors), open- and closed-centre systems, emergency hydraulic systems (*Aircraft Tech I*, printed p. 291). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — The hydraulic system — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: hydraulic terms and Pascal's law, mechanical advantage, hydraulic fluids and contamination, the basic hydraulic circuit (reservoirs, pumps, valves, accumulators, actuators, motors), open- and closed-centre systems, emergency hydraulic systems. Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Hydraulic terms and Pascal's law, mechanical advantage, hydraulic fluids and contamination, the basic hydraulic circuit (reservoirs, pumps, valves, accumulators, actuators, motors), open- and closed-centre systems, emergency hydraulic systems. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
