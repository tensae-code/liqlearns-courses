**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Belarus, Cuba, Fiji, Iran, Jamaica, Samoa, Sri Lanka, Kazakhstan) — plus teacher-guide evidence only (Ethiopia), named here, counted nowhere.**

Kazakhstan's own way: Kazakhstan teaches this inside Художественный труд / Көркем еңбек (Artistic Labour) and Трудовое обучение / Еңбекке баулу (Labour Training) — Topic 9 "Our little friends" — the birdhouse (құс ұясы) is built from a dimensioned drawing: wall, roof and base parts drawn with millimetre dimensions (90 mm, 110 mm, 160 mm) and transferred to cardboard before cutting. The Kazakh classroom's own examples, materials and national crafts named in the Kazakhstan provenance rows above are part of the teaching, not decoration.

Cuba's own way: Cuba teaches acotado (dimensioning) in both Grade 7 editions — the 1989 book as its own chapter (Cap. 5) and the 2023 book inside Dibujo Básico I (§1.8).

Fiji's own way: Fiji teaches dimensioning inside Year 11 orthographic drawing, as the steps in dimensioning that follow the principal-views work.

Jamaica's own way: Jamaica teaches working-drawing reading on the toy car the class is making — dimensions and annotations are interpreted on the car's own simple working drawing, and the finished product is evaluated against that drawing/plan, so the drawing is both the making instructions and the judge.

Era note: this guide is an older GEQIP-generation book (first edition, 2003 E.C., about 2010/11) — an earlier curriculum generation than Ethiopia's newer books. This lesson presents it as exactly that: the guide's testimony about that era's Grade 12 Technical Drawing course. It is never presented as the current syllabus, and nothing here has been checked against a current syllabus. No pupil (student) book for Technical Drawing has been read — no student book exists in hand — so this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

No book error flagged by the placements touches this lesson.

Belarus's own way: Belarus teaches applying dimensions inside its standard execution rules for the whole drawing — dimension lines, extension lines, arrowheads and the figure are placed and read to the rules, as one part of the single Grade 9 *Чарчэнне* drafting course.

Samoa's own way: Samoa teaches dimensioning as architectural convention for building drawings — show only necessary dimensions, never repeat a dimension, and place figures to be read clearly — rules stated for the shelter drawings the learner is about to make.

Iran's own way: Iran teaches dimensioning twice over: in Grade 7 through Module 8's table of line types and thicknesses — main line, centre/symmetry line and the rest, with what each is used for — and in Grade 9 by dimensioning a drawing in CAD software, following the hand-dimensioning rules.

Sri Lanka's own way: Sri Lanka adds its Tamil-medium Technology stream teaching (Educational Publications Department; visual-read method, as filed in the book entries above) — adding dimensions to orthographic views (section 1.5.3 of the projection chapter).
