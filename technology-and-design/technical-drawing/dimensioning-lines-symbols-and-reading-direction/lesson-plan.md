# Lesson plan — Dimensioning — Lines, Symbols and Reading Direction

**Course:** Technical Drawing · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Dimensioning — Lines, Symbols and Reading Direction
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Dimensioning (guide Lesson 4.1) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a dimension is built from named parts — dimension line, extension lines, arrowheads and figure — and its figure is read in one of two systems, aligned or unidirectional.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the drawing lessons of Units 1–3 (sketching, auxiliary views and sections, teacher evidence) — the learner can already draw an object; this lesson teaches how its sizes are written on it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The parts of a dimension** — dimension line, extension lines, arrowheads, the figure, and the leader line — what each part is for.
2. **Two ways to read the figure — aligned and unidirectional** — the aligned system, where the figure follows its line, and the unidirectional system, where every figure stays horizontal.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Unit 1–3 drawing lessons — sketching, auxiliary views and sectional views. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the parts of a dimension: dimension line, extension lines, arrowheads, figure and leader line, and say what each one does.
2. Draw a neat dimension with correct arrowheads and a clear figure.
3. Read and draw figures in the aligned system — the figure sits along the dimension line and is read from the bottom or the right.
4. Read and draw figures in the unidirectional system — every figure stays horizontal and is read from the bottom of the sheet.

## What you need
Paper, a sharp pencil, a ruler and an eraser. A drawn block or plate to dimension.
