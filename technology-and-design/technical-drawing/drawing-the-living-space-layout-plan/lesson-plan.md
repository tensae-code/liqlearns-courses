# Lesson plan — Drawing the living-space layout plan

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Drawing the living-space layout plan
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Moldova expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a living space is designed on paper first: the arrangement plan starts from the beneficiary's own wishes and needs, and values the space both functionally and aesthetically.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Moldova's *Educație tehnologică* (Technological Education) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Moldova counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Starting from the beneficiary** — Moldova's Grade 9 interior part puts the person before the plan: the arrangement starts from the beneficiary's individual wishes and needs — who lives there, how they live, what the space must do for them.
2. **The plan on paper — function and aesthetics** — The arrangement plan is then put on paper first, before anything is moved or built.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw an arrangement plan for a living space.
2. Show how the plan answers the beneficiary's wishes, functionally and aesthetically.
3. Explain how Moldova's book teaches it.
## What you need
Squared paper, ruler, pencil, and a real room to plan.
