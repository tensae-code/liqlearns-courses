# Drawing the living-space layout plan

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Moldova's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Starting from the beneficiary
Moldova's Grade 9 interior part puts the person before the plan: the arrangement starts from the beneficiary's individual wishes and needs — who lives there, how they live, what the space must do for them. The drawing serves a named person's life, not an abstract room.

## Sub lesson 2 — The plan on paper — function and aesthetics
The arrangement plan is then put on paper first, before anything is moved or built. The plan values the space two ways at once: functionally — does it work? — and aesthetically — is it good to be in? A plan that passes only one test is sent back.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a living space is designed on paper first: the arrangement plan starts from the beneficiary's own wishes and needs, and values the space both functionally and aesthetically. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
