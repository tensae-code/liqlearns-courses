# Lesson plan — Geometrical constructions

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Geometrical constructions
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malta expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** regular figures — triangle, pentagon, hexagon and octagon — are constructed exactly with ruler, compasses and protractor, each side and angle coming from the construction, not from guessing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student workbook — Malta's *Design and Technology — Graphical Communication Component — Workbook 1 ("Communicate Graphically")*, Directorate for Quality and Standards in Education, Malta, 2015 (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student workbook, not evidence-only).

## Sub lessons
1. **The construction tools and how they work together** — A geometrical construction builds a figure exactly, using instruments: the ruler rules straight lines, the compasses draw circles and arcs and carry lengths from place to place, and the protractor measures and sets out angles.
2. **Constructing a triangle** — A triangle is constructed from its given sides or angles: rule a base line, set the compasses to each given length and swing arcs from the base's ends, and where the arcs cross is the third corner.
3. **Constructing regular polygons: pentagon, hexagon, octagon** — A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name what the ruler, compasses and protractor each do in a construction.
2. Construct a triangle from given lengths with compasses and ruler.
3. Construct a regular pentagon, hexagon and octagon, and say how the centre angle is found.
## What you need
A ruler, compasses, a protractor, a sharp pencil and plain paper.
