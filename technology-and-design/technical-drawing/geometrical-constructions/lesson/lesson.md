# Geometrical constructions

## Do it first
Rule a base. With compasses set to two given lengths, swing arcs from each end and join the crossing point. Measure the sides to prove they match the given lengths.

## Sub lesson 1 — The construction tools and how they work together
A geometrical construction builds a figure exactly, using instruments: the ruler rules straight lines, the compasses draw circles and arcs and carry lengths from place to place, and the protractor measures and sets out angles. The rule of construction is that sizes come from the instruments — a length is stepped off with compasses, an angle is set with the protractor — never measured by eye and hoped for.

## Sub lesson 2 — Constructing a triangle
A triangle is constructed from its given sides or angles: rule a base line, set the compasses to each given length and swing arcs from the base's ends, and where the arcs cross is the third corner. Join it to the base ends and the triangle stands exact — every side the length it was given, because the compasses carried those lengths, not your eye.

## Sub lesson 3 — Constructing regular polygons: pentagon, hexagon, octagon
A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal. The pentagon has five, the hexagon six and the octagon eight. They are constructed by setting out the centre angle with the protractor (360° divided by the number of sides), or by stepping the side length around a circle with compasses, then joining the points in order. Malta's workbook constructs all three, so the method is practised until any regular figure can be built the same way.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: regular figures — triangle, pentagon, hexagon and octagon — are constructed exactly with ruler, compasses and protractor, each side and angle coming from the construction, not from guessing. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
