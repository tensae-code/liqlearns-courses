# Satellite positioning for surveyors

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Greece's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Satellite positioning for surveyors
Greece's book teaches it this way: what GPS is (the Global Positioning System, position in X, Y, Z), the older ways of fixing position (stars, compass, sextant, radio-wave trilateration from transmitters of known coordinates), the satellite constellations (Navstar GPS, GLONASS, BeiDou, Galileo/GNSS), how a GPS fix works (triangulation between spheres, satellite timing/synchronisation, distance measurement), receiver types and accuracy (simple receivers 3–5 m, dual-frequency GPS for higher accuracy) (*Geoinformatics in Technical Works*, printed p. 3). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Satellite positioning for surveyors — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: what GPS is (the Global Positioning System, position in X, Y, Z), the older ways of fixing position (stars, compass, sextant, radio-wave trilateration from transmitters of known coordinates), the satellite constellations (Navstar GPS, GLONASS, BeiDou, Galileo/GNSS), how a GPS fix works (triangulation between spheres, satellite timing/synchronisation, distance measurement), receiver types and accuracy (simple receivers 3–5 m, dual-frequency GPS for higher accuracy). Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Greece's book shows.
