# Lesson plan — Tessellation

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Tessellation
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malta expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a tessellation is a pattern of shapes that fit perfectly together with no gaps and no overlaps — squares, rectangles, triangles and hexagons do it alone, and pairs of regular polygons do it together.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student workbook — Malta's *Design and Technology — Graphical Communication Component — Workbook 2 ("Communicate Graphically")*, Directorate for Quality and Standards in Education, Malta, 2015 (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student workbook, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **What a tessellation is** — Tessellation means covering a flat surface with shapes that fit perfectly together: no gaps between them and no overlaps, the pattern able to repeat forever.
2. **Shapes that tessellate alone** — Malta's workbook works with the shapes that fit alone: squares, rectangles, triangles and hexagons.
3. **Tessellating two polygons together** — Two regular polygons can succeed together where one alone fails: octagons with squares filling the gaps between them, triangles with hexagons, triangles with squares, and a 60° rhombus in the pattern.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a tessellation is and test a shape against the no-gaps rule.
2. Name four shapes that tessellate alone.
3. Repeat a two-polygon tessellation (such as octagons and squares) across a page.
## What you need
A pencil, a ruler, grid paper and plain paper.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of three patterns side by side: hexagons fitting perfectly, pentagons leaving gaps, and octagons with small squares in the gaps — the student says which tessellate and why.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a GIF of one octagon-and-square unit repeating across a page, tile by tile, until the surface is covered — the student points to the repeating unit.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the rule aloud — no gaps, no overlaps, repeat forever — the student repeats it.
   - ADD VIDEO: a short video of the lesson demonstration — see video-script.md.
