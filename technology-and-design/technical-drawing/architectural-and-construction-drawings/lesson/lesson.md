# Architectural and construction drawings

## Do it first
Draw a one-room building three ways: a simple plan with walls, a door and a window shown by convention, one facade, and a section line marked on the plan. Label each drawing.

## Sub lesson 1 — How building drawings differ from machine drawings
Architectural-construction drawings show buildings, not machine parts: they are drawn at building scales, show whole structures and their parts in place, and follow construction's own conventions. The reader who expects machine-drawing habits will misread them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plans, facades and sections
A building is shown chiefly in plan (looked down on, cut at a set level), in facades/elevations (its outside faces), and in sections (cut through the building). Together they give the building's layout, look and construction, as the views give a part's shape.

## Sub lesson 3 — Conventional images and symbols on construction drawings
Walls, openings, windows, doors, stairs and materials are shown by conventional images and symbols agreed for construction drawings. Learning the symbol set is learning to read a building drawing at all.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: building drawings speak their own dialect of technical drawing — plans, facades and sections of buildings, drawn and read with the conventional images and symbols of construction, differing from machine drawings in scale, content and convention. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
