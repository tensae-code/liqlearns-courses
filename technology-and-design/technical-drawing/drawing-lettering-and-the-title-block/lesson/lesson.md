# Drawing lettering and the title block

## Do it first
Rule a title block on a practice sheet and fill it in for an imaginary part: name, scale and sheet. Then letter the part's name above it in drawing lettering, all capitals the same height.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sheet formats and layout
Drawing sheets come in standard formats, and a drawing is laid out on the sheet to the rules: the views placed with room for dimensions, the sheet used evenly, and the frame and margins as the standard requires. Standard layout is what lets any reader find things in the same place on any drawing.

## Sub lesson 2 — The title block — асноўны надпіс
Every drawing carries its title block (асноўны надпіс), the ruled box that names the drawing: what the object is, who drew and checked it, the scale, the sheet and the material where it applies. It is filled in as part of executing the drawing, not as an afterthought.

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing lettering
Letters and figures on a drawing are not handwriting. They are drawn in the standard drawing font — upright or sloping as the standard sets, of set heights for headings, notes and figures — so a drawing reads to standard whoever lettered it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a drawing is executed to standard rules — the sheet has a standard format and layout, a title block (асноўны надпіс) that names the drawing, and a standard drawing font so every letter and figure reads the same whoever draws it. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
