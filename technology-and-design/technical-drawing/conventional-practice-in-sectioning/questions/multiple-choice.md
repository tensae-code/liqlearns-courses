# Conventional practice in sectioning — the agreed exceptions — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Conventional representations exist so that…
a) drawings can be finished faster, right or wrong b) thin parts do not read as thick solid chunks, and every reader can rely on the same rules c) hatching can be skipped everywhere d) each drafter can invent a private style
**Answer: b) thin parts do not read as thick solid chunks, and every reader can rely on the same rules**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** A spoke cut along its length by the cutting plane is…
a) hatched heavily b) drawn unhatched, in outline c) left out of the drawing d) drawn dashed
**Answer: b) drawn unhatched, in outline**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A bolt cut along its axis is conventionally…
a) drawn uncut and unhatched b) hatched like a block c) shown only in a note d) omitted
**Answer: a) drawn uncut and unhatched**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The same rib cut ACROSS by the plane is…
a) still never hatched b) hatched like any other solid c) drawn solid black always d) removed
**Answer: b) hatched like any other solid**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The switch for the rule is…
a) the size of the part b) whether the plane cut along the length/axis or across it c) the part's colour d) who drew it
**Answer: b) whether the plane cut along the length/axis or across it**

**6. (Sub lesson 1)** If every drafter invented their own way of drawing sections, the result would be…
a) faster making b) drawings that different readers understand differently — and makers misled c) prettier pages d) no change
**Answer: b) drawings that different readers understand differently — and makers misled**
