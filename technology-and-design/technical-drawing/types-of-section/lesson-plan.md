# Lesson plan — Types of section — choosing and drawing the right cut

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Types of section — choosing and drawing the right cut
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Sectional View — Lesson 3.2, 8 periods (2 theory, 6 practice) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the types of sectional view, compared by what each does best, so you can select the appropriate type for a given object and draw the section in that type — including the true shape of the cut surface.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the Grade 12 teacher guide is the sole book in hand for this subject (see lesson/sources.md). It needs pencil, paper and the earlier lessons of its unit as recommended background, nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
1. **The main types, compared** — The standard family of sections: a full section cuts the whole object along one plane and removes the nearer half.
2. **Choosing the right type** — Choose by the object, not by habit.
3. **Drawing a section in the chosen type** — Draw it in order.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Unit 3 — Sectional View. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the main types of section and what each does best.
2. Select the appropriate type for a given object and defend the choice.
3. Draw a sectional view of an object in the preferable type.
4. Leave out hidden-detail lines where the section shows the feature plainly.
## What you need
A soft pencil, plain paper and a ruler.
