# Types of section — choosing and drawing the right cut

## Do it first
Draw the same hollow block four quick ways: cut straight through the whole thing; cut and keep one half outside, one half in section; cut along a bent path that passes through two holes not in one line; cut only a small broken patch where a detail hides. Four different cuts, four different jobs — this lesson is learning which cut fits which object.

## Sub lesson 1 — The main types, compared
The standard family of sections: a full section cuts the whole object along one plane and removes the nearer half. A half section shows one half in section and the other half as an outside view — good for symmetrical objects. An offset section bends the cutting plane so it passes through features that do not lie in one straight line. A broken-out section cuts away only a small local patch to reveal one hidden detail. A revolved section turns the cut profile of a bar or spoke in place, inside the view; a removed section draws that profile outside the view, where there is room. Each type buys clarity at some cost: the more of the object a section removes, the more outside information is lost.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the right type
Choose by the object, not by habit. Interiors everywhere and nothing important outside? Full section. Symmetrical, with the outside still worth showing? Half section. Features scattered off one line? Offset — the bends in the cutting plane are shown by thickened ends, never by lines in the section view itself. One small hidden detail? Broken-out, leaving the rest of the view intact. The profile of a long part — a bar, an arm, a spoke? Revolved or removed. The test of a choice: does this type reveal the interior features the drawing exists to describe, with the least loss?

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing a section in the chosen type
Draw it in order. Lay the cutting-plane line through the features (bent, for an offset section). Project the section view from the arrows' side. Draw the cut faces and hatch them; where the cut surface must be made or measured, remember its outline in the section is found by projection like any other shape, and an inclined cut face may need the true-shape methods of Unit 2. Draw visible edges beyond the plane in outline. Check: no hatching in voids, no hidden-detail lines kept where the section itself now shows the feature plainly.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the types of sectional view, compared by what each does best, so you can select the appropriate type for a given object and draw the section in that type — including the true shape of the cut surface. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
