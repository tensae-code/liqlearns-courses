| Lesson part | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| the house drawing (bản vẽ nhà): the technical drawing used to design and build a house, containing representations (floor plan, elevation, section…) and the data fixing the house's shape, size and layout — the elevation (mặt đứng) is the orthogonal projection of the house's outside onto the frontal or profile plane, showing its outer shape; the floor plan (mặt bằng) is the orthogonal view of the remaining part after imagining the house cut by a horizontal plane, describing the positions and sizes of walls, doors, windows, equipment and furniture; the section (mặt cắt) has its cutting plane parallel to the frontal or profile plane and shows the parts and heights of the house (original string `the house drawing (bản vẽ nhà)`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 8*, printed p. 28 |
| the representations of a house, reading a house drawing, and making a floor plan: the house's main representations are the floor plan (mặt bằng), the elevation (mặt đứng) and the section (mặt cắt); the floor plan of each storey is the sectional view obtained with imaginary horizontal cutting planes passing through the windows (about 1.5 m above the floor), expressing the positions and sizes of walls, partitions, doors, windows and staircases and the arrangement of rooms, equipment and furniture; the elevation is the house's orthogonal projection onto an upright plane, expressing its external architecture (the main elevation is the projection seen from the front); the section is obtained with an imaginary upright cutting plane through the house's empty spaces — a longitudinal section if the plane runs along the house's length, a transverse section if across its width — expressing the structure of the house's parts and the heights of its storeys, doors, windows and staircases; reading a house drawing in order — first read the elevations to picture the house's external architecture; then read the floor plans of each storey in turn to understand the arrangement of the functional areas inside (corridors, rooms, door kinds, staircases, auxiliary areas…); read the sections by the cutting-plane positions marked on the ground-floor plan, combined with reading each storey's floor plan to understand its internal space better; making a floor plan in steps — step 1 draw the axis system of the walls or columns in thin dash-dot lines, the axes numbered with capital letters A, B, C… and the numbers 1, 2, 3… (Hình 15.5a); step 2 draw the outlines of the columns, walls and partitions (15.5b); step 3 draw the staircase symbols and the parts lying below the cutting plane such as windows, interior equipment and sanitary equipment in thin continuous lines (15.5c); step 4 write the dimensions (15.5d) (original string `the representations of a house, reading a house drawing, and making a floor plan`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 10 — Thiết kế và công nghệ*, printed p. 89 |
