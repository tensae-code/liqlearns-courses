# A short history of graphic representation

## Do it first
Draw the same simple object three ways in a row: as a free pictorial depiction, as a ruled plan with one measure, and as a small technical drawing with two views and a dimension. Label each with what it can and cannot do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Early depictions
People have represented things in images since the earliest times: depictions of animals, people, tools and dwellings recorded what mattered and passed it on. These early images show and tell, but they do not yet give exact size or construction.

## Sub lesson 2 — Images made to rules
Over time, makers and builders needed more than a likeness: plans and drawings made to agreed rules, with views and measures, so that a thing could be made by someone who had never seen it. Graphic representation became a language with conventions.

## Sub lesson 3 — The modern technical drawing
The modern technical drawing brings the development together: standard views, dimensions, line types and lettering, executed to standards, readable in any workshop. Knowing its history shows why its rules exist — each one answers a problem earlier images could not solve.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: graphic images have a history — from early depictions of things people saw and made, through drawings made to rules, to the modern technical drawing a maker can build from. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself

2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
