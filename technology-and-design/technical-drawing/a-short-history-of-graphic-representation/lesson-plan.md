# Lesson plan — A short history of graphic representation

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** A short history of graphic representation
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Belarus expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** graphic images have a history — from early depictions of things people saw and made, through drawings made to rules, to the modern technical drawing a maker can build from.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student textbook — Belarus's *Чарчэнне (Technical Drawing / Drafting), Grade 9*, by В. Н. Вінаградаў (V. N. Vinogradov), National Institute of Education, Minsk, 2008 (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student textbook, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Early depictions** — People have represented things in images since the earliest times: depictions of animals, people, tools and dwellings recorded what mattered and passed it on.
2. **Images made to rules** — Over time, makers and builders needed more than a likeness: plans and drawings made to agreed rules, with views and measures, so that a thing could be made by someone who had never seen it.
3. **The modern technical drawing** — The modern technical drawing brings the development together: standard views, dimensions, line types and lettering, executed to standards, readable in any workshop.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
