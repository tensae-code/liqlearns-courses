# Lesson plan — Drawing standards

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Drawing standards
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a norm (Norma Cubana, NC-ISO) is the agreed rule that makes drawings readable by everyone: what a norm is, why drawings follow one, and how the Cuban norm adapts the ISO standard.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's *Educación Laboral y Dibujo Básico — 7mo grado (2023 Biblioteca edition)* and the Cuba books in lesson/sources.md — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba counts in the acceptance count for its student-book placements; its programme and methodological-orientations placements are evidence only, counted nowhere.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **What a norm is** — Cuba's 2023 Grade 7 book opens its standards teaching (§1.2) with the idea itself: a norm is an agreed, written rule. Drawings follow one so that any reader, anywhere, reads the same drawing the same way.
2. **The Cuban norm, from ISO** — The Norma Cubana for drawing adapts the international ISO norms (NC-ISO) to Cuban practice. The Grade 8 programme builds on it throughout its drawing unit — formats, lettering, dimensioning and scales as the NC-ISO norms prescribe.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
