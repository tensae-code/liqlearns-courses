# Scales in drawing

## Do it first
Before reading on, try the topic's core task once on your own: full-size, reduction and enlargement scales. Keep what you did — you will check it against the lesson at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — What scales in drawing is
Full-size, reduction and enlargement scales. That is the heart of this lesson: everything else in it is the parts, the order and the checks that make this work in practice. Sri Lanka's textbook teaches this as its own topic, in full, and this lesson follows that teaching: learn what the thing is first, in plain terms, before you handle its details.

Sri Lanka teaches this topic from more than one book in this set: The scale rule, and the three ways to draw — full size (1:1), reduced scale and enlarged scale. Each book's own part is recorded, book by book and page by page, in the sources for this lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — The parts and how they fit together
Work through the topic part by part, in the order the book presents it. For each part, learn three things: its name, what it does, and how it connects to the part before and after it. A part you can name and explain is a part you can use; a part you can only point at is not yet learned. Where the topic is a process, the order of the steps is itself part of the teaching — doing the steps out of order changes the result.

## Sub lesson 3 — Using it well — and checking your work
Now apply the whole topic to a fresh task, unaided, and check the result against the lesson's own rules: every part present, every step in order, nothing skipped. Checking is not an extra — it is the last step of the work itself. Fix one thing you find, and say what you changed and why.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: full-size, reduction and enlargement scales. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
