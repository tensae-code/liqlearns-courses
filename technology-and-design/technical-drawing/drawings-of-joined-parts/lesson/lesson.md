# Drawings of joined parts

## Do it first
List six joined things around you. Sort them into detachable and permanent joints, name the kind of each, and sketch how you would show one of each on a drawing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Detachable joints
Detachable joints hold parts together yet let them be separated without damage: threaded and other fastened joints are the typical case. A drawing shows the fastening and the parts it joins so the joint can be made — and later taken apart — from the drawing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Permanent joints
Permanent joints cannot be separated without destroying the joint or the parts: welded, soldered, riveted and similar joints. Their drawings use the representations and designations agreed for each permanent kind.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading a joint on a drawing
To read a joint, name its family first — detachable or permanent — then its kind, then how the representation shows the parts and the fastening. That order tells you how the thing was assembled and whether it can ever come apart.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: parts are joined in two great families — detachable joints, which can be taken apart, and permanent joints, which cannot — and a drawing shows each joint by the representation agreed for its kind. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
