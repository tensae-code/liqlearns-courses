# Line types on drawings

## Do it first
Rule four lines across a page: a faint construction line, a firm black outline, a centre line of long and short dashes, and a fold line as the workbook draws it. Label each one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Construction lines and outlines
A drawing is built on construction lines: faint, light lines you draw first to set out shapes, centres and sizes. They are guides, not the thing itself, so they stay faint. The outline is the opposite: a firm black line drawn over the finished edge of the object, the line that says 'the thing ends here'. Learning to keep the two apart — faint while you build, black when you finish — is the first discipline of the drawing board.

## Sub lesson 2 — Centre lines
A centre line marks the middle of a round or symmetrical part. It is drawn as a chain of long and short dashes running through the centre, sticking out a little beyond the shape on both sides. Where two centre lines cross, they mark the exact centre point — of a circle, a hole or a whole symmetrical object.

## Sub lesson 3 — Fold lines
A fold line shows where a flat sheet will be folded — on a net or development, it is the edge where one face bends up against the next. It is drawn differently from an outline so the maker never confuses 'cut here' or 'the edge' with 'bend here'. In Malta's workbook each line type is first copied, then used in real drawing tasks, so the hand learns the line and its meaning together.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: each kind of line on a technical drawing has one job — a faint construction line to build with, a black outline for the finished edge, a centre line through the middle, and a fold line where sheet material bends. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
