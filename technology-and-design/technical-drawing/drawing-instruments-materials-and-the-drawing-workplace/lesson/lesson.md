# Drawing instruments, materials and the drawing workplace

## Do it first
Lay out a drawing workplace: fix a sheet square on the board, place pencil, ruler, set squares, compasses and eraser in a row, and name the job of each one aloud.

## Sub lesson 1 — Instruments, and how drawing was executed through history
Drawings have been executed in different ways through history, but the hand drafter's instruments stay recognisable: pencils, compasses, rulers, set squares and erasers, each made for one kind of line or curve. Knowing an instrument means knowing the job it alone does well.

## Sub lesson 2 — Materials and media
The drawing is made on a material with a material: sheet paper of the right kind, pencils of the right grade, and the media the task needs. The appendices of a drafting course list the additional materials used for independent work, so a learner working alone can set up completely.

## Sub lesson 3 — The drawing workplace
A drawing workplace is set before work begins: the sheet fixed square, instruments within reach in their order, light on the work, and finished sheets kept flat and clean. A tidy workplace is not decoration — it is how accurate work stays accurate.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a drafter works with named instruments and materials at a properly set workplace — and how drawings are executed has changed through history, from hand instruments to the additional materials used for independent work. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
