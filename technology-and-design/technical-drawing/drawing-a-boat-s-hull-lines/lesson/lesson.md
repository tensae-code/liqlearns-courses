# Drawing a boat's hull lines

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Mongolia's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Drawing a boat's hull lines
Mongolia's book teaches it this way (*Design Technology 9 (Mongolia)*, printed p. 92): the three orthogonal projections of the hull (side, half-breadth and body plan) on a numbered grid, choosing the widest section, and building the hull from sheet or carved from a block in clamps (2×5 cm battens). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Drawing a boat's hull lines — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: the three orthogonal projections of the hull (side, half-breadth and body plan) on a numbered grid, choosing the widest section, and building the hull from sheet or carved from a block in clamps (2×5 cm battens). Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Mongolia's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Drawing a boat's hull lines — the three orthogonal projections of the hull (side, half-breadth and body plan) on a numbered grid, choosing the widest section, and building the hull from sheet or carved from a block in clamps (2×5 cm battens). Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
