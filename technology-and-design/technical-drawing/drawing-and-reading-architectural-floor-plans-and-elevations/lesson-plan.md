# Lesson plan — Drawing and reading architectural floor plans and elevations

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Drawing and reading architectural floor plans and elevations
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a building is drawn as a floor plan (the view from above, cut through the walls) and as elevations (the flat views of its faces) — Samoa teaches drawing a plan to given data, a wall elevation with framing, window and door, and a roof truss of given span and pitch.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The floor plan — drawing to given data** — A floor plan is drawn to scale from given wall and window data: wall lines, openings, door swings.
2. **The wall elevation — framing, window and door** — An elevation draws one face flat: the framing members, the window and the door in their true positions and sizes.
3. **The roof truss — span and pitch** — A truss is drawn from its span (the width it crosses) and pitch (its slope): two data points decide the whole triangle.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw a simple floor plan from given wall/window data.
2. Draw a wall elevation with framing, window and door.
3. Say how span and pitch decide a truss drawing.
## What you need
Ruler, paper, pencil; squared paper helps.
