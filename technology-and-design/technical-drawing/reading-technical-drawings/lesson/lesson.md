# Reading technical drawings

## Do it first
Take any part drawing. Cover the views. Read only the title block and write what it tells you. Then uncover the views, then the dimensions, writing after each step what new thing you now know about the part.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a finished drawing tells you
A finished drawing tells you what the object is, its shape in every view, its true sizes, and the requirements it must meet. Reading is the general skill of taking all of that off the sheet in a sensible approach, instead of guessing from the biggest view.

## Sub lesson 2 — The order of reading — title block first
Belarus teaches a set order for reading a part's drawing: begin with the title block, which names the part and gives the scale and material; only then turn to the views. The title block frames everything that follows.

## Sub lesson 3 — Views, dimensions and requirements
After the title block, read the views to build the shape in your mind, then the dimensions for true sizes, then the technical requirements. Keep going between them until the part's shape is fully understood — reading ends when you could describe or make the part, not when you have seen every line.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: reading a drawing is an order, not a glance — title block first, then the views, the dimensions and the technical requirements, until the part's shape is fully understood. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
