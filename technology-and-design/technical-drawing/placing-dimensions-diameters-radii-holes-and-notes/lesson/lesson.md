# Placing Dimensions — Diameters, Radii, Holes and Notes

## Do it first

A maker reads this note: 'hole 10 deep 12'. Two questions they cannot answer: is 10 the diameter — and is 12 the full-depth part or does it include the drill's point? By the end of this lesson you will write that note so neither question can be asked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diameters, radii and centres

**Diameters** are dimensioned with a numerical value **preceded by the diameter symbol** — written here as Ø, so Ø40 means a round feature 40 across. The symbol tells the maker the feature is circular before any view is studied; never leave it off a true diameter.

**Radii** are dimensioned the same way with the **radius symbol** R before the value: R15 is an arc struck from a centre 15 away. A radius dimension line runs from the centre to the arc, with its arrowhead touching the arc.

**Cylinders should be dimensioned in the rectangular view where possible** — the view where the cylinder appears as a rectangle. There the diameter is given across the rectangle's width and the length along it, both in one view, with no hidden lines and no need to dimension inside a circle.

Where a dimension is given **to the centre of an arc or radius, show a small cross at the centre** — two short centre lines crossing exactly at the point the radius swings from. The cross is the maker's guarantee of where that centre lies.

## Sub lesson 2 — Holes specified in notes — counter-bore, spot-face, countersink, blind depth

Some hole features cannot be dimensioned cleanly in place, so **the diameter and depth of counter-bored, spot-faced or countersunk holes are specified in a note** beside a leader pointing to the hole.

A **counter-bore** is a flat-bottomed enlargement at the top of a hole, seating a bolt head or nut: the note gives the through-hole diameter, then the counter-bore diameter and its depth — 'Ø10 through; counter-bore Ø18, 5 deep'. A **spot-face** is the same idea made shallow — just enough flat for a bolt head or nut to sit squarely. A **countersink** is a conical enlargement that lets a flat-head screw sit flush: the note gives the hole diameter and the countersink diameter (and its angle where the standard requires it).

A **blind hole** — one that does not pass through — has its depth specified in a note too, and the rule is exact: **the depth is the depth of the full diameter, measured from the surface of the object.** The drill's conical point digs a little beyond, but that pointed part holds nothing and is not counted in the depth. So 'Ø10, 12 deep' now answers both opening questions: diameter 10, and 12 of full-diameter depth from the surface.

## Say it to your companion

Read your companion a hole note you have written. They must say back exactly what the maker will drill: through or blind, which diameters, which depths. Any hesitation means the note is not finished.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the rectangular view of a cylinder 50 in diameter and 90 long, and dimension it in that view: Ø50 across, 90 along.
2. Draw a circle with an arc of R30 and mark its centre with a small cross; dimension the radius from the cross.
3. Draw a block with a counter-bored hole and write its full note; then a blind hole beside it with its depth note, measured by the full-diameter rule.
