# Lesson plan — Placing Dimensions — Diameters, Radii, Holes and Notes

**Course:** Technical Drawing · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Placing Dimensions — Diameters, Radii, Holes and Notes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Dimensioning (guide Lesson 4.4) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the placement rules for round features: diameters carry the diameter symbol, radii the radius symbol, cylinders are dimensioned in their rectangular view, an arc's centre is marked with a small cross, and hole depths and finishes are specified in notes.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole of Unit 4 before it — parts, kinds and arrangements of dimensions (teacher evidence). This is a doing lesson: the rules are learned by applying them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Diameters, radii and centres** — the diameter symbol before the value, the radius symbol before the value, cylinders in the rectangular view, and the small cross at an arc's centre.
2. **Holes specified in notes — counter-bore, spot-face, countersink, blind depth** — hole finishes whose diameter and depth are specified in a note, and the blind-hole depth rule.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Arranging dimensions — the two methods. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Dimension a diameter with its numerical value preceded by the diameter symbol, and a radius with the radius symbol.
2. Dimension a cylinder in the rectangular view where possible.
3. Mark the centre of an arc or radius with a small cross, and dimension to that centre.
4. Specify a counter-bored, spot-faced or countersunk hole fully in a note: diameter and depth.
5. Specify the depth of a blind hole correctly in a note — the depth of the full diameter from the surface.

## What you need
Paper, a sharp pencil, a ruler, a compass and an eraser.
