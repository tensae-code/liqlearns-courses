**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Belarus, Cuba, Fiji, Moldova, Mongolia, Vietnam).**

Cuba's own way: Cuba introduces the assembly drawing (dibujo de ensamble) in Grade 9 as first notions (§8.2), after sections — the drawing of the whole, read from the drawings of the parts.

Fiji's own way: Fiji teaches the assembly drawing in Year 12 Strand 3 — drawing an assembly with bolts and screw threads, and the conventions for bars and tubes, compression springs, and nut, bolt and washer proportions.

Belarus's own way: Belarus teaches this inside *Чарчэнне*, its single Grade 9 technical drawing (drafting) course in Belarusian — a complete course in textbook order, from graphic information and standard execution rules through projection, sections, threads, joints and assembly to architectural-construction drawings, closing with a short terminological dictionary (inventory-only, not placed).

Moldova's own way: Moldova teaches the assembly drawing (desen de asamblare) inside the Grade 5 wood module of *Educația tehnologică* — the drawing that carries the form, dimensions and number of pieces needed to assemble an object — and teaches reading one on a stool: how many pieces, what material, what basic dimensions, which line types.

Mongolia's own way: Mongolia teaches this inside Visual Art & Technology (Дүрслэх урлаг, технологи) — what an assembly drawing contains — views, the parts list (number, name, quantity, material), position numbers on leader lines, and the three kinds of assembly dimension (overall, joining, fitting). The Mongolian classroom's own examples named in the provenance rows above are part of the teaching, not decoration. Mongolia's books are image-only scans read by a VISUAL-READ method, as filed in the book entries above.

Vietnam's own way: Vietnam teaches this inside its Vietnamese-language Công nghệ (Technology) student books — the Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống (KNTT) series, NXB Giáo dục Việt Nam (Vietnam Education Publishing House), for the Ministry of Education and Training (Bộ Giáo dục và Đào tạo) — Assembly drawings. The Vietnamese classroom's own examples named in the provenance rows above are part of the teaching, not decoration.
