# Assembly drawings

## Do it first
Sketch a simple assembly of three parts — for example a bolt, a plate and a nut — in place. Label each part, add the overall size, and list what a reader would still need a part drawing for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Purpose and content of an assembly drawing
An assembly drawing's purpose is to show a unit as a whole: every part in its place, how the parts fit and work together, and the overall sizes. Its content is the assembled unit, not any single part's full detail.

## Sub lesson 2 — What an assembly drawing must show
It must show the parts assembled in the views needed, the parts identified, and the dimensions that govern the assembly — overall, joining and checking sizes. A part's own making details belong on its part drawing, not here.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading assembly drawings and detailing
Reading an assembly drawing means recovering each part's shape from the whole. Detailing (дэталіраванне) is the work that follows: from the assembly drawing, making the separate working drawing of each part, complete enough to make that part.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: an assembly drawing shows a whole unit assembled — its purpose and content are the parts in place and how they fit — and from it a reader works back, by detailing (дэталіраванне), to the separate part drawings. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
