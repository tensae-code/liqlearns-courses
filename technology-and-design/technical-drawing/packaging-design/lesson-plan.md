# Lesson plan — Packaging design

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Packaging design
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malaysia expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** good packaging is eco-friendly, easy to store, fully labelled, user-friendly, and keeps the product’s quality.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Malaysia's *Reka Bentuk dan Teknologi* (RBT — Design and Technology) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Malaysia counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Packaging around us** — Packaging wraps food and non-food products alike, and Malaysia begins with the traditional craft: packaging from plant materials — coconut and banana leaves — still living beside factory packaging.
2. **The characteristics of good packaging** — Good packaging is judged by named characteristics: eco-friendly material, easy to store, complete label information, user-friendly handling, and keeping the product’s quality.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Recognise packaging kinds, including traditional plant packaging.
2. Judge a package against the five characteristics.
3. Explain how Malaysia's book teaches it.
## What you need
Real packages, food and non-food; leaves or paper for wrapping practice.
