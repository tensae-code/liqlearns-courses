# Drawing an auxiliary view — the true shape of an inclined surface

## Do it first
Take the tilted book from before. Its cover never shows true shape in front or top view. Now hold a sheet of paper parallel to the cover and imagine looking straight through it at the cover: the shape you would trace is the cover's true shape. Drawing that view, properly projected, is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Set up: the edge view and the parallel plane
Start from the view where the inclined surface shows as a line — its edge view. That line tells you the surface's tilt. Place your auxiliary plane parallel to that line: draw a folding (reference) line parallel to the edge view, at a convenient distance from it. Everything in the auxiliary view is measured against this line.

## Sub lesson 2 — Project the features across
From every corner and feature point of the inclined surface, draw a projector at right angles to the edge view, running across the folding line into the auxiliary space. Then carry each point's distance over: in the view next to the edge view, measure how far each point sits from the corresponding reference line, and lay that same distance off from your new folding line along its projector. Join the transferred points in the same order as on the object. Curves and holes on the surface are transferred point by point the same way.

## Sub lesson 3 — Check the true shape
Check your work three ways. Every projector must be at right angles to the edge view. Every transferred distance must equal the distance measured in the neighbouring view — a point never changes its distance from the reference line when it moves into the auxiliary view. And the finished face must look plausible as the real surface: same number of corners, same holes, in the same arrangement. That true-shape drawing is what a maker needs to cut the surface.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: producing a complete auxiliary view of a whole object: project its features onto a plane parallel to the inclined surface, and the drawing describes that surface's true shape. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
