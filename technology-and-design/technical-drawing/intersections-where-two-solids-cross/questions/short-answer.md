# Intersections — Where Two Solids Cross — short answer

Answer in a short sentence.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Why must the line of intersection be found before either solid is made?
**Answer: because the opening each solid needs is exactly that curve — cut without it, the solids cannot fit each other.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** When is the cutting-plane method needed rather than direct work from generators?
**Answer: where no face of the solids shows edge-on in a view to read piercing points from directly.**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** In the development of an intersected cylinder, what curve appears when the carried piercing points are joined in order?
**Answer: the true curved edge of the opening — the flat pattern's cutting line where the second solid will pass.**

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** How do you check a finished intersection drawing against the solids themselves?
**Answer: every plotted point must lie on both surfaces at once, in both views — a point that fails that test in either view is plotted wrong.**
