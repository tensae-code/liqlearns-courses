# Lesson plan — Intersections — Where Two Solids Cross

**Course:** Technical Drawing · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Intersections — Where Two Solids Cross
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Development and Intersection (guide Lesson '6.3' — misnumbered in the guide, see sources) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** where two solids meet, their surfaces cross in a line of intersection, found point by point from piercing points — and that curve must be carried into the developments before either solid can be made.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the development lessons before it in this unit (teacher evidence): a learner who can develop a single solid now develops two that pass through each other.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Piercing points and the line of intersection** — the curve where two surfaces cross, built from the points where each solid pierces the other.
2. **The two methods of finding the points** — working from edges and generators where a view already shows a surface edge-on, and auxiliary cutting planes where it does not.
3. **Visible, hidden — and the curve in the development** — sorting the found curve into visible and hidden parts, then carrying it into the flat patterns of both solids.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Developing solids — parallel line, radial line, triangulation. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a line of intersection is and why it must be found before intersected solids can be made.
2. Find piercing points: the points where an edge or generator of one solid passes through the surface of the other.
3. Use the two finding methods in turn: working from edges and generators directly, and working through auxiliary cutting planes.
4. Join piercing points in their true order and separate the visible parts of the curve from the hidden parts.
5. Carry the intersection curve into the development of an intersected prism or cylinder, point by point on its generators or faces.

## What you need
Paper and card, a ruler, a compass, dividers, scissors and a sharp pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Push a pencil (a cylinder) through a hole in a card box (a prism) at a slant. Look at the line where the pencil's surface meets the box's face: it is not a circle, and it is not straight. That curved line — where the two surfaces cross — is what this lesson teaches you to find on paper, before anything is cut. (lesson/lesson.md).
