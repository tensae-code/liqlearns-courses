# Graphic information and the kinds of drawings

## Do it first
Take one familiar object. Describe it three ways on one page: in words, as a quick pictorial sketch, and as a simple diagram with labels. Say what each version tells well and what it leaves out.

## Sub lesson 1 — What graphic information is
People describe objects in more than words. A description can be a word description, a photograph, a drawing, a sign or a symbol. When the description is carried by an image, it is graphic information: the shape, size and arrangement of a thing are shown, not only told.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pictorial images and graphic images proper
Pictorial (visual) images show a thing as the eye sees it — a photograph or a realistic picture. Graphic images proper are made to rules: drawings, diagrams, charts, schemes and technical drawings. They may look less like the thing, but they carry exact information a maker can work from.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing the right kind of image
Each kind of image does a different job. A photograph records how a thing looked; a scheme shows how parts connect; a technical drawing gives the exact shape and size to make. The skilled reader names the kind first, then reads only what that kind can honestly tell.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: graphic information is how a person describes objects with images — and the images divide into pictorial (visual) images and graphic images proper: drawings, diagrams, charts, schemes and technical drawings. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
