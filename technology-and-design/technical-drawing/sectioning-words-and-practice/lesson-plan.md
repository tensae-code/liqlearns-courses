# Lesson plan — Sectioning — words and common practice

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Sectioning — words and common practice
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Sectional View — Lesson 3.1, 1 period (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the language of sectioning: the imagined cut, the cutting-plane line, the section view, and section lining (hatching) — the conventions that make a section readable.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the Grade 12 teacher guide is the sole book in hand for this subject (see lesson/sources.md). It needs pencil, paper and the earlier lessons of its unit as recommended background, nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
1. **The imagined cut and the cutting-plane line** — A section view shows interior details clearly by imagining the object cut and the nearer part removed.
2. **Section lining — hatching** — Where the cutting plane passes through solid material, the cut face is marked with section lining, also called hatching: thin, evenly spaced lines, usually drawn at 45 degrees, all running the same way across one part.
3. **Reading and drawing a simple section** — To read a section: find the cutting-plane line in the other view, follow its arrows, then in the section view separate hatched material (solid, cut) from unhatched spaces (voids, and parts lying beyond the cut drawn in outline).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Unit 3 — Sectional View. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a section view, a cutting-plane line and section lining.
2. Hatch a cut face correctly and leave voids unhatched.
3. Read a simple section using the cutting-plane line and its arrows.
4. Draw a simple section of a hollow object.
## What you need
A soft pencil, plain paper, and a hollow container to imagine cut open.
