# Sketching techniques — lines, circles and proportion

## Do it first
Draw a long straight line across your page, free-hand, in one steady movement of your arm. Mark its middle by eye, then check with a folded paper strip: how close were you? Now draw a big circle inside a lightly drawn square. These two tries are exactly the skills this lesson makes fluent.

## Sub lesson 1 — Straight lines, long lines and midpoints
Sketch a straight line by moving your whole arm, not just your fingers, and by looking at the end point you are heading for, not at the pencil tip. For a long line, keep the same steady arm movement across the whole page; a line drawn in short finger strokes wobbles. To find the midpoint of a line by eye, judge the halfway point, mark it lightly, then check by comparing the two halves — fold the paper or step a scrap of paper along it — and correct the mark until both halves match.

## Sub lesson 2 — Circles, angles and arcs
The guide teaches four methods of drawing a circle free-hand; the most useful begins with a light enclosing square: draw the square the size of the circle, add its centre lines and diagonals, mark where the circle crosses them, then join the marks with a smooth curve. Sketch angles by first sketching the two arms from one vertex in the directions you judge, then checking the opening against a known angle, such as the corner of a book for a right angle. An arc is part of a circle: find its centre first, then swing the arc through its end points.

## Sub lesson 3 — Proportion and multi-view sketching
Proportion means the parts of a sketch keep their true relative sizes: if the real object is twice as wide as it is tall, the sketch is too — measure with your pencil held at arm's length, thumb marking the length. Lay out the whole sketch lightly in proportion before any detail. A multi-view sketch shows a 3D object in flat views — front, top and side — each view showing only what is seen looking straight at that face, the views kept in line with each other so widths and heights agree across views.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the hand skills of sketching: straight and long lines, midpoints, circles, angles and arcs, proportion, and multi-view sketches of 3D objects. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
