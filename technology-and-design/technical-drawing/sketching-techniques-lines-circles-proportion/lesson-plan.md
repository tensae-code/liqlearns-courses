# Lesson plan — Sketching techniques — lines, circles and proportion

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Sketching techniques — lines, circles and proportion
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Sketching and Visualization — Lesson 1.2, 3 periods (1 theory, 2 practice) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the hand skills of sketching: straight and long lines, midpoints, circles, angles and arcs, proportion, and multi-view sketches of 3D objects.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the Grade 12 teacher guide is the sole book in hand for this subject (see lesson/sources.md). It needs pencil, paper and the earlier lessons of its unit as recommended background, nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
1. **Straight lines, long lines and midpoints** — Sketch a straight line by moving your whole arm, not just your fingers, and by looking at the end point you are heading for, not at the pencil tip.
2. **Circles, angles and arcs** — The guide teaches four methods of drawing a circle free-hand; the most useful begins with a light enclosing square: draw the square the size of the circle, add its centre lines and diagonals, mark where the circle crosses them, then join the marks with a smooth curve.
3. **Proportion and multi-view sketching** — Proportion means the parts of a sketch keep their true relative sizes: if the real object is twice as wide as it is tall, the sketch is too — measure with your pencil held at arm's length, thumb marking the length.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Unit 1 — Sketching and Visualization. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sketch straight and long lines with a steady arm movement.
2. Find the midpoint of a line by eye and check it.
3. Sketch a circle using the enclosing-square method.
4. Lay out a sketch in true proportion and sketch front, top and side views of an object.
## What you need
A soft pencil, plain paper, and a small block or box to sketch in three views.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first task and do it again slowly, one step at a time, saying each step aloud. Drawing skills grow by doing; a shaky first attempt is the normal start, not a failure.
