# Lesson plan — Surface Development — Flat Sheets That Become Things

**Course:** Technical Drawing · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Surface Development — Flat Sheets That Become Things
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Development and Intersection (guide Lesson '6.1' — misnumbered in the guide, see sources) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a development is the flat pattern of a solid's whole surface, cut from sheet and folded, formed or rolled into the thing itself — the sheet-metal worker's drawing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the drawing course before it (teacher evidence) — the learner can draw and dimension objects; this unit teaches how their surfaces unroll flat.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a development is — and who works from one** — the flat pattern of a whole surface; the sheet objects of everyday life; the sheet-metal worker's starting drawing.
2. **Solids and their surfaces — what the pattern must contain** — prisms with flat faces, cylinders with a curved surface that unrolls, pyramids and cones — identifying the types before developing them.
3. **Hems and joints — finishing the edges** — hems fold an edge back for strength and safety; joints join the meeting edges of the folded pattern.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Unit 4 dimensioning lessons — a development must be dimensioned truly before it can be cut. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a surface development is and what it is for.
2. Name things made from flat sheet by development: boxes, cans, funnels, pans, pipes, elbows, ducts and gutters.
3. Tell the common solids and their surfaces apart — prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone — and say which faces each development must contain.
4. Say what a hem is and what a joint is in sheet-metal drawing, and why edges are hemmed.

## What you need
Paper and card, scissors, a ruler, a pencil and something to fold against (a ruler's edge). A real tin can or box to study.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Take a small cardboard box and unfold it flat — carefully, along its seams. Lay it out and look: the whole outside of the box is there on one flat sheet, joined at fold lines. Sketch what you see. That flat layout is the box's development, and this lesson is about drawing such layouts on purpose. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Go back to the Do it first task and do it again with real things in the hand — this course is learned by drawing and making, not by reading alone.
- Work the lesson's check questions sub lesson by sub lesson; a missed check names the exact sub lesson to re-read.
- Make the thing in card before drawing it finally: a model that will not fold or fit is telling you which length is not yet true.
