# Threads on drawings

## Do it first
Draw a short length of rod. Show an external thread on it by the convention — crest and root lines, and where the thread ends — then add a thread designation beside it. Do the same for a hole with an internal thread.

## Sub lesson 1 — The conventional representation
A real thread is a helix, and drawing every turn would bury the drawing. So threads are represented conventionally: the crest and root are shown by set line types, in views and in sections, the same way on every drawing.

## Sub lesson 2 — External and internal threads
An external thread (on a bolt or rod) and an internal thread (in a nut or tapped hole) are each shown by the convention for their case, including how far the thread runs and where it ends. The reader tells them apart by the convention, not by counting turns.

## Sub lesson 3 — Designating a thread
Beside the representation goes the designation: the agreed code that states the thread — its kind, nominal size and, where needed, pitch and length. Representation shows where the thread is; designation says exactly which thread it is.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: screw threads are not drawn turn by turn — they are shown and designated by convention: set lines for the crests and roots of internal and external threads, and a designation that states the thread's kind and size. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
