**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Belarus, Fiji, Malaysia, Malta, Sri Lanka) — plus teacher-guide evidence only (Ethiopia), named here, counted nowhere.**

Fiji's own way: Fiji teaches pictorial drawing across the years — Year 9 takes isometric views and converting orthographic to isometric with grids, with oblique and perspective views; Year 10 takes 3D/isometric, oblique, and one- and two-point perspective in steps; Year 11 takes oblique projection, oblique circles and arcs and inclined surfaces; Year 12 takes perspective drawing; Year 13 takes isometric projection including the four-centre method for circles, spheres and hemispheres.

Era note: this guide is an older GEQIP-generation book (first edition 2003 E.C., funded through the General Education Quality Improvement Project, GEQIP) — a different curriculum generation from the current syllabus. These lessons present it as such, as the guide's testimony about the course of its era, never as the current syllabus unchecked. No student book for Technical Drawing exists in hand, so nothing here could be checked against a pupil text.

Honesty: No book error is flagged in the placements for this lesson. The placements give the rubric points exactly as taught here. No narrower pages than printed pp. 7–18 are given, so none are claimed.

Malta's own way: Malta teaches this inside the Graphical Communication component of Design and Technology, through graded workbooks — a worked example to copy, then graded practice in the spaces provided, closed by an "I can" self-check page (assessment-only, not placed). That copy-then-practise workbook way is carried here exactly as the books teach it.

Belarus's own way: Belarus teaches the pictorial view by rule first — axonometric projections of plane figures constructed step by step — and then as the technical pictorial drawing (тэхнічны рысунак), with shading, so a solid's form reads at a glance.

Malaysia's own way: Malaysia teaches all three pictorial families in one Tingkatan 1 sweep — oblique (cavalier and cabinet on a 45° receding axis), isometric, and perspective in two-point and three-point form, above and below eye level — each built step by step from box, horizon line and vanishing points.

Sri Lanka's own way: Sri Lanka adds its Tamil-medium Technology stream teaching (Educational Publications Department; visual-read method, as filed in the book entries above) — the pictorial-view chapter's four methods survey — perspective, parallel, dimetric and isometric — as the ways to show length, width and height in one view; the isometric method taught inside the pictorial-view chapter, after perspective, parallel and dimetric methods; perspective view used to present a product design in the metalwork chapter.
