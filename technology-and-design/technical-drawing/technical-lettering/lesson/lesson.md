# Technical lettering

## Do it first
On grid paper, print the full alphabet and the numbers 0–9, one character per square. Check: are all letters the same height? Is the spacing even? Redo the three worst letters.

## Sub lesson 1 — Printing letters and numbers evenly
Lettering is printing, not joined-up writing: each capital letter and each number is drawn as its own clear shape, all the same height, evenly spaced, so the words on a drawing can never be misread. Malta's workbook practises the whole alphabet and the numbers — including the diameter sign Ø — by copying printed examples letter by letter until the hand keeps every character the same size and weight.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guidelines, grids and vertical lettering
Guidelines are the faint ruled lines that keep lettering level and one height: you print between them, never above or below. A grid does the same job in squares, holding each letter upright in its own space. In vertical lettering the letters stand straight up; keeping them vertical, evenly spaced and one height is the skill — a sloping or wandering line of letters makes a drawing look careless and can be misread.

## Sub lesson 3 — Lettering your name and class on the drawing
A drawing is finished when it says who made it and what it is. The workbook's final lettering task is printing your own name and class on a grid — the same careful printing, now used for real: level, even, vertical letters that another person can read without guessing. Practise until your name on a grid looks printed, not written.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: every letter and number on a drawing is printed by hand, evenly and clearly, between guidelines — so anyone can read the drawing's words and figures at a glance. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
