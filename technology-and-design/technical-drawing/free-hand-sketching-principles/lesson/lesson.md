# Free-hand sketching — principles and place in design

## Do it first
Take a pencil and plain paper. In two minutes, sketch a familiar object — a chair, a cup or a tool — with no ruler and no erasing. Show it to someone and ask: can you tell what it is, and roughly how big it is? That quick drawing, done only with your hand and eye, is a free-hand sketch.

## Sub lesson 1 — What sketching is and what it does
Sketching is a fast form of graphical communication. Words and numbers alone are slow and easy to misunderstand; a sketch shows shape at a glance. A free-hand sketch does four jobs: it organises your thoughts, it generates new ideas as you draw, it records dimensions next to the shape, and it transfers information to another person. An engineer with no instruments at all — even alone on an island — can still think and communicate with a pencil sketch, because sketching needs no artistic talent: once the basic techniques are known, the limit on a sketch is not the ability to draw, it is the ability to think through the details of the design.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sketching in the design process, and how it differs from instrument drawing
Sketching belongs to the beginning phase of design, where the final geometric parameters and constraints of a thing are first fixed. Ideas are tried, changed and sharpened on paper before any careful instrument drawing is made. Instrument drawing uses rulers, compasses and set squares to make an exact, scaled final drawing; free-hand sketching uses only hand and eye to get the idea down fast, in good proportion. The two are partners: sketch first to think, draw with instruments later to make exactly.

## Sub lesson 3 — Materials and the sketch notebook
You need very little: a soft pencil that makes a clear line, plain paper, and an eraser used sparingly — sketch over a faint wrong line rather than stopping to rub out. Keep your sketch ideas in a notebook, so an idea is never lost and you can watch your own thinking improve. Hold the pencil lightly, draw with your whole arm for long lines, and let early lines stay light until the shape is sure.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: sketching is a fast, free-hand way of communicating ideas graphically — it organises thoughts, generates ideas, records dimensions and transfers information, and it opens the design process. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
