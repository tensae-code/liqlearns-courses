# Lesson plan — Free-hand sketching — principles and place in design

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Free-hand sketching — principles and place in design
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Sketching and Visualization — Lesson 1.1, 1 period (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sketching is a fast, free-hand way of communicating ideas graphically — it organises thoughts, generates ideas, records dimensions and transfers information, and it opens the design process.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the Grade 12 teacher guide is the sole book in hand for this subject (see lesson/sources.md). It needs pencil, paper and the earlier lessons of its unit as recommended background, nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
1. **What sketching is and what it does** — Sketching is a fast form of graphical communication.
2. **Sketching in the design process, and how it differs from instrument drawing** — Sketching belongs to the beginning phase of design, where the final geometric parameters and constraints of a thing are first fixed.
3. **Materials and the sketch notebook** — You need very little: a soft pencil that makes a clear line, plain paper, and an eraser used sparingly — sketch over a faint wrong line rather than stopping to rub out.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Unit 1 — Sketching and Visualization. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what free-hand sketching is and name its four jobs.
2. Explain where sketching sits in the design process.
3. Tell free-hand sketching apart from instrument drawing.
4. Set up a sketch notebook and use sketching materials properly.
## What you need
A soft pencil, plain paper and a notebook. No instruments.
