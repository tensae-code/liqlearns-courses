# Drawing a labelled schematic of a made device

## Do it first
Sketch the schematic of a torch circuit from memory, then label every component. Swap with a partner: can they name each part from your labels alone, without asking you? Fix every label they stumble on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reproducing the schematic
Jamaica's motor project ends on paper: reproducing a simple schematic diagram of the electromagnetic motor. Reproducing is not copying carelessly — it is drawing the circuit as it truly is in the finished device, every connection present, so the drawing and the device agree. A schematic that omits a connection is a wrong instruction to the next maker.

## Sub lesson 2 — Labelling every component, correctly and neatly
The guide's standard is double: all components correctly and neatly labelled. Correctly means the label matches the symbol and the real part — battery labelled as battery, coil as coil. Neatly means lettering placed clear of the lines, readable without turning the page, nothing crowded or crossed out. Neatness in a drawing is not decoration; it is what makes the drawing usable by someone else.

## Sub lesson 3 — Diagrams that illustrate the layout
Jamaica also asks for simple diagrams to illustrate the motor's layout: the arrangement drawing beside the connection drawing. Together they are the maker's full record — how it connects, and how it is arranged — the same pair of views the flashlight project taught (schematic and cross-section), now produced by the learner for their own device.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a made device is recorded by reproducing its schematic diagram with every component correctly and neatly labelled — a drawing another maker could build and check from. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
