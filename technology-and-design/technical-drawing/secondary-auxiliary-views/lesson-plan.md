# Lesson plan — Secondary auxiliary views — projecting a projection

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Secondary auxiliary views — projecting a projection
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Auxiliary Views — Lesson 2.3, 5 periods (1 theory, 4 practice) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the secondary auxiliary view: when a surface is oblique — inclined to every principal plane — project it twice, first to a primary auxiliary view, then again, until its true shape appears.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the Grade 12 teacher guide is the sole book in hand for this subject (see lesson/sources.md). It needs pencil, paper and the earlier lessons of its unit as recommended background, nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why one auxiliary view is not enough** — An oblique surface gives no edge view in any principal view, and a first auxiliary plane can only be set parallel to one direction at a time.
2. **Building the primary auxiliary view** — Choose a principal view and a direction of projection.
3. **Projecting again: the secondary view** — Now treat the primary auxiliary view as your new starting view.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Unit 2 — Auxiliary Views. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain why an oblique surface needs two projections.
2. Build a primary auxiliary view that gives an edge view of the surface.
3. Project again to a secondary auxiliary view.
4. Keep point labels and transferred distances consistent through both steps.
## What you need
A soft pencil, plain paper, a ruler, and patience — this lesson is mostly drawing practice.
