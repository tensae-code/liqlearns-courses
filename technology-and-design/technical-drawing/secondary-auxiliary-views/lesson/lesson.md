# Secondary auxiliary views — projecting a projection

## Do it first
Lean a card so it tilts both sideways and forwards at once — oblique to the floor, the wall and the side wall all at once. No ordinary view and no single special view can show its true shape. It can still be found — by projecting twice. That double projection is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why one auxiliary view is not enough
An oblique surface gives no edge view in any principal view, and a first auxiliary plane can only be set parallel to one direction at a time. So the first (primary) auxiliary view is chosen to win something halfway: set its plane so that in the primary auxiliary view the surface shows as a line — an edge view at last — or so that a key line of the surface shows in true length. True shape is still one step away.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the primary auxiliary view
Choose a principal view and a direction of projection. Draw the first folding line, project every point of the surface across at right angles, and transfer distances from the neighbouring principal view exactly as in an ordinary auxiliary view. Work carefully: every error here is carried into the second projection and doubled. Label your points and keep the same labels through both projections.

## Sub lesson 3 — Projecting again: the secondary view
Now treat the primary auxiliary view as your new starting view. In it the surface shows edge-on (or a key direction shows truly). Draw a second folding line parallel to that edge view, project the points again at right angles to it, and transfer distances — this time measured in the primary auxiliary view from its reference line. Join the points. In this secondary auxiliary view the oblique surface finally appears in its true shape and true size.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the secondary auxiliary view: when a surface is oblique — inclined to every principal plane — project it twice, first to a primary auxiliary view, then again, until its true shape appears. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
