# Lesson plan — Orthographic projection — points, lines, planes and surfaces

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Orthographic projection — points, lines, planes and surfaces
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Auxiliary Views — Lesson 2.1, 6 periods (2 theory, 4 practice) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** orthographic projection: how points, lines and planes project onto planes, the six principal views, the three classes of surface — and why inclined and oblique surfaces defeat the principal views.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the Grade 12 teacher guide is the sole book in hand for this subject (see lesson/sources.md). It needs pencil, paper and the earlier lessons of its unit as recommended background, nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
1. **Orthographic projection and the principal views** — In orthographic projection, the lines of sight are parallel and at right angles to the picture plane, so the view shows the true shape of any face parallel to that plane, undistorted.
2. **Why the principal views fail: inclined and oblique surfaces** — Surfaces come in three classes.
3. **Folding lines, reference lines and the auxiliary idea** — Imagine each projection plane hinged to its neighbour: unfold the planes flat onto the paper and the hinge lines become folding lines on the drawing.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Unit 2 — Auxiliary Views. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain orthographic projection and name the six principal views.
2. Project a point, a line and a plane onto a plane.
3. Classify a surface as normal, inclined or oblique.
4. Explain why an auxiliary view is needed and what folding and reference lines do.
## What you need
A soft pencil, plain paper, a ruler for checking your free-hand layout, and a book or block to tilt.
