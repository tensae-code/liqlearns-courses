# Lesson plan — Recognising geometric solids

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Recognising geometric solids
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malta expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the common geometric solids can be recognised and named on sight — cube, cuboid, square pyramid, cone, sphere, cylinder and hemisphere — and told apart even when two are combined into one object.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student workbook — Malta's *Design and Technology — Graphical Communication Component — Workbook 2 ("Communicate Graphically")*, Directorate for Quality and Standards in Education, Malta, 2015 (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student workbook, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Solids with flat faces: cube, cuboid, square pyramid** — A cube has six equal square faces.
2. **Round solids: cone, sphere, cylinder, hemisphere** — The cone has a circular base and one curved surface rising to a point.
3. **Combined solids** — Real objects are often two solids joined: the workbook's combined cylinders are cylinders of different sizes stacked or joined into one object.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the cube, cuboid and square pyramid and say how each is told apart.
2. Name the cone, sphere, cylinder and hemisphere on sight.
3. Break a combined solid into the simple solids it is made of.
## What you need
A pencil and plain paper; coloured pencils help for the shading work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of seven solids in a row — cube, cuboid, square pyramid, cone, sphere, cylinder, hemisphere — unlabelled, with one combined cylinder beside them — the student names each one and the two parts of the combined solid.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a GIF of a combined cylinder splitting apart into its two cylinders and joining again — the student names each part as it separates.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the solids aloud — cube, cuboid, square pyramid, cone, sphere, cylinder, hemisphere — the student repeats the names.
   - ADD VIDEO: a short video of the lesson demonstration — see video-script.md.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's key rule aloud.
4. **Questions** — the questions/ files, spread over the sub lessons.
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block in lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first task and do it again slowly, one step at a time, saying each step aloud. Drawing skills grow by doing; a shaky first attempt is the normal start, not a failure.
