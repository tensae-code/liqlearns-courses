# Computer-aided drawing (CAD)

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — What CAD is for
In CAD, the drawing lives in the computer: lines, circles and dimensions are exact, copies are perfect, and a change is made once instead of redrawn everywhere. Iran's Grade 9 book opens its Computer Drawing module by surveying where CAD drawing is used — in jobs and projects across engineering, building and manufacturing — because the software is learned as a working tool, not an exercise.

## Sub lesson 2 — The drawing-software environment
The CAD environment presents a drawing area, toolbars and menus of drawing and editing commands, and aids for precision such as snap. The learner's first skill is orientation: where the tools live, how a command is started and ended, and how the drawing is saved and reopened. From that environment the module proceeds to drawing shapes and editing them — the subjects of the next lessons.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: computer-aided drawing does the drawing board's work in software — used across jobs and projects wherever exact, changeable, shareable drawings are needed. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
