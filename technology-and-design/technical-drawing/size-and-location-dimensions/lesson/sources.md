**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Belarus, Iran, Malta, Moldova, Sri Lanka, Greece, Vietnam) — plus teacher-guide evidence only (Ethiopia), named here, counted nowhere.**

Era note: this guide is an older GEQIP-generation book (first edition, 2003 E.C., about 2010/11) — an earlier curriculum generation than Ethiopia's newer books. This lesson presents it as exactly that: the guide's testimony about that era's Grade 12 Technical Drawing course. It is never presented as the current syllabus, and nothing here has been checked against a current syllabus. No pupil (student) book for Technical Drawing has been read — no student book exists in hand — so this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

No book error flagged by the placements touches this lesson. Unit 4's own period total reconciles in the guide (7 = 1+2+2+2), as the placements verify.

Malta's own way: Malta teaches this inside the Graphical Communication component of Design and Technology, through graded workbooks — a worked example to copy, then graded practice in the spaces provided, closed by an "I can" self-check page (assessment-only, not placed). That copy-then-practise workbook way is carried here exactly as the books teach it.

Belarus's own way: Belarus teaches scale as a drawing rule in its own right — the object is drawn larger or smaller than life, while the dimension figure always states the object's true size.

Moldova's own way: Moldova teaches scale inside the Grade 5 drawing pages — enlargement 2:1 and 4:1, reduction 1:2 and 1:4, natural size 1:1 — with the rule that every dimension on a technical drawing is in millimetres and “mm” is not written beside the figure.

Iran's own way: Iran teaches dimensioning (اندازه‌گذاری) of a drawn part together with drawing to scale in Grade 7 — enlarging scales 2:1, 5:1, 10:1 included — and applies it in the woodwork project drawn at 1:2 and dimensioned.

Sri Lanka's own way: Sri Lanka adds its Tamil-medium Technology stream teaching (Educational Publications Department; visual-read method, as filed in the book entries above) — dimensioning the orthographic views of objects so size and position are fully stated.

Greece's own way: Greece teaches this inside its Greek-language Gymnasio and EPAL technology books (Ministry of Education / ITYE Diophantos) — drawing scales for the model: what 1:25, 1:50, 1:100 mean, the scale must be written on the drawing, and the figure written is always the TRUE dimension (e.g. 30 m) whatever the scale. The Greek classroom's own examples named in the provenance rows above are part of the teaching, not decoration. As filed for *Τεχνολογία Β΄ Γυμνασίου (Technologia B' Gymnasiou — Technology, Grade 8)*: Greece's own way note: Grade 8 Technology uses the "group work" method themed on Industry — the class simulates a production company, takes its roles, and builds a scale model (ομοίωμα/μακέτα) of the production unit.

Vietnam's own way: Vietnam teaches this inside its Vietnamese-language Công nghệ (Technology) student books — the Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống (KNTT) series, NXB Giáo dục Việt Nam (Vietnam Education Publishing House), for the Ministry of Education and Training (Bộ Giáo dục và Đào tạo) — Size and Location Dimensions — the Theory. The Vietnamese classroom's own examples named in the provenance rows above are part of the teaching, not decoration. Vietnam's books are image-only PDFs read by a VISUAL-READ method (rendered pages read as images), as filed in the book entries above — no visual read is presented as a text read.
