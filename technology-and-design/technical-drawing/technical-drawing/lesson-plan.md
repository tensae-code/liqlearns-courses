# Lesson plan — Technical drawing

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Technical drawing
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Morocco expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** technical drawing speaks in set drawing types — cavalier perspective shows a part in one view, the assembly drawing (dessin d'ensemble) shows how parts fit together — and every drawing is made to a scale, read off against the real object.

## Sub lessons
1. **Cavalier perspective and the assembly drawing** — Cavalier perspective is a pictorial drawing: one view shows the object's three dimensions, with the front face true shape and depth edges drawn at an angle, shortened by a set ratio. The assembly drawing (dessin d'ensemble) shows a whole mechanism assembled — every part in place — so the reader sees how the parts fit and work together, each part numbered for the parts list.
2. **Scales in drawing** — A scale is the ratio between the drawing and the real object. Scale 1:1 is full size; 1:2 reduces; 2:1 enlarges — Morocco's exercise photographs a Moroccan coin and works it at scale 2:1, so every length on the drawing is twice the coin's real length, and real lengths are recovered by dividing by the scale.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what cavalier perspective shows and what it cannot be used for.
2. Say what an assembly drawing shows and how its parts are identified.
3. Convert lengths between drawing and object at a given scale, including 2:1.
## What you need
A coin, a ruler, drawing paper and a pencil.
