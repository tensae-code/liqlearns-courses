# Technical drawing

## Do it first
Hold a coin up and look at it: your drawing of it could be the same size, bigger, or smaller. Draw its circle twice as big. You have just used scale 2:1 — the scale Morocco's exercise works on a photographed Moroccan coin.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cavalier perspective and the assembly drawing
Each drawing type answers a different question. Cavalier perspective answers 'what does it look like, in one view?' — fast to read, not for measuring depth truly. The assembly drawing answers 'how does it go together?' — parts in their working positions, numbered balloons leading to the nomenclature (parts list). A detail drawing of one part answers 'how is this part made?' Morocco's Module 4 teaches what each type shows before any of them is drawn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Scales in drawing
The scale is written on the drawing and governs every measurement taken from it: real length = drawn length ÷ scale ratio. Enlargement scales (2:1, 5:1) serve small parts like the coin; reduction scales (1:2, 1:50) serve large objects. Reading a drawing without its scale is guessing — the scale is part of the drawing's language.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: technical drawing speaks in set drawing types — cavalier perspective shows a part in one view, the assembly drawing (dessin d'ensemble) shows how parts fit together — and every drawing is made to a scale, read off against the real object. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
