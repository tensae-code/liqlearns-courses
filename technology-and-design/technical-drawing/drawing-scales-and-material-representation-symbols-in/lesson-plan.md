# Lesson plan — Drawing scales and material representation symbols in architectural drawing

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Drawing scales and material representation symbols in architectural drawing
**Place in the curriculum:** Technical Drawing (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** architectural drawing speaks in scale and symbols: line types carry meaning, scale ratios like 1:10 and 1:100 shrink buildings onto paper truly, and standard symbols stand for materials in plans.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Line types carry meaning** — On a drawing, the kind of line is part of the message: visible edges, hidden edges and centre lines are drawn differently.
2. **Scale ratios, worked** — Scale is a ratio: 1:10 means 1 unit on paper for 10 in reality; 1:100 shrinks a hundredfold.
3. **Symbols for materials in plans** — In plan and section, materials are shown by agreed symbols and hatchings, not drawn realistically.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Technical Drawing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read line types on a drawing.
2. Convert measurements at 1:10 and 1:100 both ways.
3. Say why materials use standard symbols in plans.
## What you need
A ruler, paper, and a tape measure for the Do it first conversion.
