# Drawing scales and material representation symbols in architectural drawing

## Do it first
Measure a door in your home, then draw it 100 times smaller: at 1:100 its 90 cm width becomes 9 mm on paper. You have just used an architectural scale.

## Sub lesson 1 — Line types carry meaning
A reader trusts the line code. Solid lines show what is seen; dashed lines hide what is behind; chain lines mark centres. Draw a line in the wrong type and the drawing lies politely — the reader builds the lie.

## Sub lesson 2 — Scale ratios, worked
Work it both ways. A 6 m wall at 1:100 is 6 cm on paper; a 45 mm paper line at 1:10 is 450 mm in reality. Big objects take small scales (a site at 1:100 or smaller); details take large scales (a joint at 1:10 or 1:1). Choosing the scale is choosing what fits and what can be read.

## Sub lesson 3 — Symbols for materials in plans
A wall cut in section is filled with the symbol for its material — one pattern for concrete, another for timber, another for earth — so the builder reads the construction at a glance. The symbol set is a shared language: use the standard mark or the plan cannot be trusted.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: architectural drawing speaks in scale and symbols: line types carry meaning, scale ratios like 1:10 and 1:100 shrink buildings onto paper truly, and standard symbols stand for materials in plans. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
