# Lesson plan — Worked Developments — the Answer Plates

**Course:** Technical Drawing · **Title:** Technical Drawing · **Lesson:** Worked Developments — the Answer Plates
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Development and Intersection, closing the unit (the guide's printed solution plates, pp. 100–104) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the guide's lettered answer plates A–J are the worked solutions to Unit 5's development exercises (Checkpoint 5.11 / Exercise 1) — reading them plate by plate shows exactly what a correct development looks like, and checking your own drawing against them is how the standard is learned.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the two lessons before it in this unit — developing solids and intersections (teacher evidence). It closes the course: these plates are the accuracy bar those lessons are drawn to.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How to use an answer plate** — what the plates are: full-page worked answers, lettered A–J, to Checkpoint 5.11 / Exercise 1; draw first, then check against the plate.
2. **The prism, transition and pointed-solid plates** — solution B (hexagonal-prism developments, fold lines, numbered stations), solution C (gridded transition development with divided circle), solution D (pointed solid, numbered plan divisions).
3. **The cone-with-intersection and duct plates** — solution G (cone development carrying an intersecting solid, curve by concentric arcs) and solutions H and I (duct developments with diamond-shaped cutouts); the remaining letters stand in the same family.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Developing Solids — Parallel Line, Radial Line, Triangulation, and Intersections — Where Two Solids Cross. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the plates are: the worked answers, lettered A–J, to Checkpoint 5.11 / Exercise 1, and use them the right way round — attempt first, check after.
2. Read solution B: follow a hexagonal prism's development panel by panel along its numbered stations, and name what the fold lines and the two end faces do.
3. Read solution C: follow a transition development built by projecting a grid from the elevation, and say what the divided circle below it supplies.
4. Read solution D: follow a pointed solid's development from the numbered divisions around its plan circle.
5. Read solution G: follow a cone development carrying an intersecting solid, its intersection curve built by concentric arcs; and solutions H and I: duct developments whose diamond-shaped cutouts must land in the flat pattern exactly where the mating piece needs them.

## What you need
Paper and card, a ruler, a compass, dividers, scissors and a sharp pencil — and your own finished developments from the two lessons before this one, to check.

## If the learner is stuck
- Go back to the Do it first task and do it again with real things in the hand — this course is learned by drawing and making, not by reading alone.
- Work the lesson's check questions sub lesson by sub lesson; a missed check names the exact sub lesson to re-read.
- If your drawing and a plate disagree, do not rub out either one: measure both, station by station, and find the first station where they part — the error lives there, not everywhere.
