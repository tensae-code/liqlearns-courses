# Drilling in wood and metal

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — The brace, bits and drills
Cuba's 1989 book teaches drilling inside its tools chapter: the berbiquí (brace) with its chuck, the barrenas and brocas (bits and drills) it turns, and the drilling machines that do the same work by power. Each bit belongs to its material.

## Sub lesson 2 — Drilling technique
A good hole is made before the bit turns: mark the centre, secure the work, start true, and keep the drill square to the surface. Wood and metal differ in bit, speed and feel — the driller reads the material while drilling.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: drilling makes a true hole in wood or metal: the brace (berbiquí) turns the bit by hand, the drilling machine by power — and the skill is choosing the bit, holding the work, and drilling straight, centred and to depth. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
