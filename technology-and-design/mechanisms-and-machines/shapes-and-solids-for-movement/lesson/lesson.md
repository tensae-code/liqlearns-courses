# Shapes and solids for movement

## Do it first
Look at a toy car or a picture of one. List every shape you can find on it, and beside each shape write the job it does for the car's movement.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shapes on a working thing
Look at any car and read its shapes. The wheels are circles because only a circle rolls smoothly. The windows and panels are flat rectangles and curves that cover and protect. Triangular shapes appear where strength without weight is needed, because a triangle holds its shape. Jamaica's project starts by identifying the shapes and solids found on a car and asking how each shape influences how the car works.

## Sub lesson 2 — Solids and composite objects
A wheel is a short cylinder; an axle is a long thin cylinder; a chassis is a flat cuboid. Joined together they make a composite object: the whole car. Makers think in simple solids even when the finished thing looks complicated, because each simple solid can be measured, drawn and made on its own before the parts are joined.

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing shapes in 2D and 3D
A 2D drawing shows one face with true lengths — the drawing you measure and cut from. A 3D drawing shows the solid as the eye sees it, so its whole form can be checked. Jamaica's learners produce shapes, solids and composite objects in both 2D and 3D drawing orientations for the car's components, so the drawing set answers both questions: how big is each part, and how will the whole look and move?

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the shapes and solids a machine is built from decide how it moves — a car's round wheels, flat body panels and solid chassis each do a movement job — and makers draw those shapes in 2D and build them in 3D. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
