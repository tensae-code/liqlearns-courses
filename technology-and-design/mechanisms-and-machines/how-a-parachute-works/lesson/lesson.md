# How a parachute works

## Do it first
Drop a sheet of paper flat, then drop the same sheet crumpled into a ball. The flat sheet falls slower. You have just seen a parachute's whole secret in miniature.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Slowing a fall
A falling person or load gathers speed until landing becomes dangerous. The parachute's function is to slow that fall so the landing is safe. It does not stop the fall \u2014 gravity still pulls \u2014 it makes the descent slow enough to survive and to land on feet, or to set a load down whole.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Canopy, air resistance and lines
The canopy is the wide cloth dome that opens above the faller. As it descends, the canopy pushes against a large body of air, and the air pushes back: that push-back is air resistance, and it grows with the canopy's area. The suspension lines run from the canopy's edge down to the harness, spreading the load so the canopy holds its shape and the faller hangs evenly beneath it.

## Sub lesson 3 \u2014 Other uses of the parachute idea
The same idea works elsewhere. A braking parachute behind an aircraft opens on the runway and drags against the air to slow the landing run. Athletes train with small parachutes that resist their running, building strength against the drag. Wherever moving air can be caught, resistance can be put to work.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a parachute slows a fall for a safe landing: its canopy catches the air, air resistance holds the fall back, and suspension lines carry the load. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
