# Simple machines

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a simple machine is
Rwanda's Primary 6 book defines the simple machine first: a device that makes work easier. The idea is small and complete — no engine, no power, just shape and arrangement turning effort into advantage.

## Sub lesson 2 — The types of simple machines
The types follow with Rwanda's own examples: levers (the wheelbarrow, the crowbar), wheel and axle (the windlass), pulleys (the elevator, the cargo lift), inclined planes (the ladder), wedges, and screws. Each type is one idea repeated everywhere: once you can see the type, you can find simple machines all around you.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a simple machine makes work easier, and there are a handful of kinds — lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge and screw — each hiding inside the tools and lifts of everyday life. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
