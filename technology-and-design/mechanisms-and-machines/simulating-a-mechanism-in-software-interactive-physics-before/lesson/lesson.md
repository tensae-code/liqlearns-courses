# Simulating a mechanism in software (Interactive Physics) before building it

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Build it in the software first
A simulation assembles the mechanism's parts on screen — bodies, joints and drives — and lets it move under the software's physics. The learner watches the motion the real build would produce: does the part travel where it should, at the right speed, without colliding or jamming? Iran's Grade 9 module places simulation in the build sequence deliberately: before building, not after.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fail cheaply, then build truly
A failed simulation costs minutes; a failed build costs materials and days. Simulation lets the design be changed — a length, a pivot, a gear — and run again at once, until the motion is right. Only then does the class build in material, already knowing the mechanism works. The software does not replace the build; it protects it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: before cutting material, a mechanism can be built in software and run: simulation in Interactive Physics shows whether the motion works — and fails cheaply when it does not. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
