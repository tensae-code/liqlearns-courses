# Mechanical energy in devices and transport

## Do it first
Hold a ball at shoulder height, then let it fall. While it is held, its energy is stored; while it falls, the stored energy becomes movement. Say the two energy names out loud at the right moments.

## Sub lesson 1 — Work, energy and movement words
Mechanical energy cannot be talked about without its words. Energy is the ability to do work; work is done when a force moves something. Velocity is how fast something moves in a direction; acceleration is how that velocity changes; mass is how much matter a thing has; impact is what happens when moving things meet. Jamaica's toy-car project opens with exactly these key terms, because the car the learner will make obeys all of them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kinetic and potential energy
Energy of movement is kinetic energy: the faster and heavier a moving thing is, the more it carries. Stored energy of position is potential energy: a car held at the top of a ramp, water held behind a dam, a stretched band. Let go, and potential becomes kinetic; slow a moving thing to a stop on a height, and kinetic becomes potential. Devices are built to control that swap.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mechanical energy in devices and transport
Everyday devices use mechanical energy: a falling hammer drives a nail, a wound spring drives a clock, pedals drive a bicycle. Transport uses all three states of matter: solid wheels, gears and pistons; liquids in hydraulic lifts and brakes; gases in steam and in the expanding gases that push an engine's pistons. Jamaica teaches the states of matter inside the making itself — how solids, liquids and gases are used in the make and operation of transport mechanisms.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: mechanical energy is the energy of movement and position — stored as potential energy, moving as kinetic energy — and devices and transport machines work by changing one into the other. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
