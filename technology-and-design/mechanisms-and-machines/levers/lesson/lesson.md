# Levers

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Parts of a lever
Rwanda's Primary 6 book takes the lever apart into three named parts: the effort (where you push or pull), the fulcrum or pivot (the point it turns on), and the load (the thing moved). Every lever ever used — crowbar, wheelbarrow, seesaw — is those three parts arranged somehow.

## Sub lesson 2 — The classes of levers
Levers are classed by which part sits in the middle: fulcrum in the middle, load in the middle, or effort in the middle. The class is not a label to memorise but a reading skill — look at any lever, find the middle part, and you have its class and a hint of how it trades force for movement.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a lever is a bar turning on a pivot: effort at one place, the fulcrum in between, the load at another — and the order of those three parts decides the lever's class. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
