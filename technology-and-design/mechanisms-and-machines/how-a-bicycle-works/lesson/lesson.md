# How a bicycle works

## Do it first
Picture a bicycle. Ask three questions: What makes it go? What makes it turn? What makes it stop? Answer with parts, not magic \u2014 this lesson names them.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Propulsion \u2014 moving the bicycle
To explain a bicycle, link each function to its working principle and its elements. Propulsion \u2014 making it move \u2014 is solved by the pedals, the chain and the rear wheel: the rider's legs turn the pedals, the chain carries the turning to the wheel, and the wheel pushes the bicycle forward. Name the elements and the chain of action, and the function is explained, not just pointed at.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Steering and braking
Steering is solved by the handlebar joined to the front wheel: turn the bar and the wheel, and the bicycle, follow. Braking is solved by the brakes \u2014 levers at the hands, pads that grip the wheel or its disc \u2014 turning the rider's squeeze into friction that slows and stops the wheel. Three functions, three technical solutions: that pattern explains any machine.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a bicycle works by chosen technical solutions: one for propulsion, one for steering, one for braking \u2014 link each function to the elements that perform it. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
