# Lesson plan — Forces and friction in mechanisms

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Mechanisms and Machines · **Lesson:** Forces and friction in mechanisms
**Place in the curriculum:** Mechanisms and Machines (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a force is a push, a pull or a turn — forces start things moving, speed them up, slow them down, stop them, float them and change their shape, size or direction — and friction is the force that resists sliding.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Jamaica's *National Standards Curriculum Guide — Grades 4–6 — Resource & Technology* (Ministry of Education, Youth and Information, Kingston, Jamaica, © 2018), a curriculum guide with full unit teaching content, read COMPLETE (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (curriculum guide with full teaching content, not evidence-only).

## Sub lessons
1. **Push, pull, turn** — Every force is a push, a pull or a turn, and each one changes how a thing moves or rests.
2. **What forces do** — One list covers it: start, speed up, slow down, stop, float, change shape, size or direction.
3. **Friction, the resisting force** — Friction grips where surfaces rub: it stops the slide you do not want and steals the movement you do.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Mechanisms and Machines. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three kinds of force action: push, pull and turn.
2. List what forces can do to a thing, using Jamaica's list.
3. Explain friction and demonstrate one of its effects.
## What you need
A book, a smooth table, and a rough cloth or mat.
