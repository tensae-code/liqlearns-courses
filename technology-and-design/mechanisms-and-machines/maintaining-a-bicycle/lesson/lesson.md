# Maintaining a bicycle

## Do it first
Look at a bicycle's chain, brakes and tyres \u2014 or picture them. Which would you oil, which would you check, which might you replace? Sort them, and you have begun the maintenance round.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 The maintenance operations
Maintaining a bicycle means doing its operations in turn. Lubricate the chain so it runs smoothly and wears slowly. Check the brakes: pads gripping well, levers firm. Check the tyres for wear and damage, and the cables for fraying or slack. Each check is small; together they are the difference between a bicycle that is ready and one that fails on the road.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Spare parts and oil
The maintainer's kit is spare parts and oil. Brake pads wear down and are replaced. A tyre can be replaced when worn or damaged. Cables stretch and fray and are renewed. Oil serves the chain and moving parts. Keeping the small kit ready turns most failures into ten-minute jobs instead of a dead bicycle.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: bicycle maintenance is a set of operations: lubricate the chain, check brakes, tyres and cables \u2014 with spare parts (brake pads, tyre, cables) and oil as the kit. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks \u2014 fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
