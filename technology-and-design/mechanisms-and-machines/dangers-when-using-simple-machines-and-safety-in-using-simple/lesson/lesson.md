# Dangers when using simple machines, and safety in using simple machines

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dangers when using simple machines
The same arrangement that makes work easier can hurt: loads slip, bars spring back, wedges and screws bite, ropes and pulleys drop what they lift. The book names the dangers of its own machines — the dangers belong to the very examples the class has just learned.

## Sub lesson 2 — Safety in using simple machines
Safety is the matching discipline: use each machine for its work, keep body parts out of the line of force, check the machine before loading it, and never trust a load you have not secured. Taught as the unit's closing lesson, safety is the price of the advantage.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: simple machines are strong helpers with real dangers — a lever multiplies force on fingers and loads alike — so Rwanda closes the unit by naming the dangers and teaching safety in using them. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
