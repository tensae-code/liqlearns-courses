# How a piston engine moves a snowmobile

## Do it first
Push a toy car or slide your hand along a table: something had to push to make it move. A snowmobile has no one pushing it — so where does its push come from? Tell a partner your guess, then find out.

## Sub lesson 1 — Burning fuel makes energy
Inside the engine is a closed tube called a cylinder. Gasoline burns inside it, and the burning releases energy as hot, expanding gas. That burst of energy is the engine's power — everything else in the engine exists to catch it and put it to work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pistons move, and the movement drives the tracks
A piston is a plug that fits tightly inside the cylinder and can slide. Each burst of burning gas pushes the piston down; the piston rises again ready for the next burst, moving up and down again and again, very fast. The engine turns that up-and-down movement into turning movement, and the turning drives the snowmobile's tracks, which grip the snow and pull the machine forward.

## Sub lesson 3 — The same engine in cars and trucks — and the mechanic's job
Snowmobile, car and truck engines work the same way: fuel burns in cylinders and pistons carry the movement to the wheels or tracks. Engines wear out and break, so mechanics maintain them, fix what fails and replace worn engine parts to keep the machines running.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: burning gasoline inside a cylinder makes energy, the energy pushes pistons up and down, and that up-and-down movement is turned into the turning that drives a snowmobile's tracks. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
