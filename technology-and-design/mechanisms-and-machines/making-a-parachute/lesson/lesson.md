# Making a parachute

## Do it first
Hold a small plastic bag open by its handles and let it drop. It catches a little air. This lesson turns that hint into a real, tested parachute.

## Sub lesson 1 \u2014 Materials and making steps
The make uses simple materials: plastic sheet or paper for the canopy, tape to strengthen its edge and line points, string for the suspension lines, and a lump of modelling clay as the load. The steps follow the making run: cut the canopy, fix the lines at even points around its edge, measure the lines equal \u2014 unequal lines tip the canopy and spill its air \u2014 and attach the clay weight where the lines meet.

## Sub lesson 2 \u2014 Testing the fall
A parachute is tested by dropping it from a height and watching the fall. A good one blooms open, hangs straight and lands its weight gently. A tipped or spinning fall is information: check line lengths, check the canopy's centre, and adjust. Making, testing and fixing is the whole method \u2014 the fall grades the work.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a working parachute can be made from plastic sheet or paper, tape, string and a modelling-clay weight \u2014 following the making steps and testing the fall. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks \u2014 fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
