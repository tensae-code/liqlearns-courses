# About the Technology & Design course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks and teacher guides. The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'.

## Ethiopia, Technical Drawing — the guide's way (teacher evidence)

The Grade 12 Technical Drawing **teacher guide** (2003 E.C.)
opens with the course's own shape: five units climbing from
free-hand sketching through projection and sectioning to
dimensioning, surface development and intersection. That shape
is the frame the Technical Drawing lessons hang on. The guide is
an older GEQIP-generation book; it is presented as its era's
course, with no pupil book read, so every lesson stands on
labelled guide evidence at 0 countries' acceptance. Its
course-intro picture and five-unit intro video ideas are
course-level material held here, not lessons.

**The course-intro picture** (teacher guide, printed pp. 1–105 —
the five-unit ladder the guide's contents promise):

**The period budget** (teacher guide, printed pp. 1–105): the
course runs **58 periods in total, and Unit 5 — Development and
Intersection — alone takes 21 of them**; the rest climb the same
ladder (Units 2, 3 and 4 take 13, 12 and 7, and Unit 1's lessons
are allocated 1 + 3 + 3 = 7 against the "Total Periods: 5" its
head prints — a book error the lessons' sources flag). In every
lesson, practice takes the larger share of the periods.

**The uniform lesson script** (teacher guide, printed
pp. 1–105): every lesson in the guide is scripted the same way —
outcomes → competencies → "Planning ahead" → "Hints for
Teaching" → Teaching Notes → checkpoints → assessments →
printed solutions. That demonstrate-then-practise,
solution-carrying pattern is the guide's way, and the lessons
in this course are built in its shape.

**The five-unit intro video ideas** (teacher guide, printed
pp. 1–105) — described slots, one short course-intro walk
through the five units in order; filming is a later pass:

1. Unit 1, Sketching and Visualization: a hand sketching an
   idea free-hand — the designer's first language, needing no
   artistic talent, only thinking through the design.
2. Unit 2, Auxiliary Views: an inclined face that no principal
   view shows truly, answered by its auxiliary view unfolding
   along a folding line.
3. Unit 3, Sectional View: a cutting plane passing through an
   object, the front half lifting away to show the inside.
4. Unit 4, Dimensioning: a drawing becoming a complete set of
   instructions — dimension lines, arrows and figures placed so
   no further word is needed to make the thing.
5. Unit 5, Development and Intersection: a flat sheet folding,
   forming and rolling into the finished object — the unit the
   course spends its most time on, ending in the worked
   solution plates.

## Ethiopia, Technical Drawing — the guide's solution plates and references (teacher evidence)

**The solution plates** (teacher guide, pp. 100–104): the
guide's worked plates, lettered A–J — the answer key to Unit
5's development exercises (Checkpoint 5.11 / Exercise 1). They
are stocked as the lesson "Worked developments — the answer
plates" in the Technical Drawing course, which teaches
solutions B (hexagonal-prism developments, fold lines,
numbered stations), C (gridded transition development with
divided circle), D (pointed solid, numbered plan divisions),
G (cone development carrying an intersecting solid, curve by
concentric arcs) and H and I (duct developments with
diamond-shaped cutouts), with the remaining lettered solutions
in the same family.

**The Reference bibliography** (teacher guide, p. 105): the
guide's own sourcing shelf for the whole course, filed here in
full:

- Jensen & Helsel, *Engineering Drawing* (with AutoCAD, 4th
  edition, Macmillan/McGraw-Hill, New York).
- T. Jeyapoovan, drawing text (Vikas Publishing House, 2004).
- Giesecke, Mitchell & Spencer, *Technical Drawing* (6th
  edition, Macmillan, 1974).
- Herbert W. Yankee, *Machine Drafting and Related
  Technology* (Webster Division, McGraw-Hill, 1966).

The Reference page's typesetting interleaves the four entries
(authors, editions and publishers jumbled together); the
identifications above are as read from the page.
