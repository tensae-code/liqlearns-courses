# Lesson plan — Basic product sketches in 2D and 3D; choosing the best product idea using the design elements; sketching the chosen product; improving the chosen sketch

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** Basic product sketches in 2D and 3D; choosing the best product idea using the design elements; sketching the chosen product; improving the chosen sketch
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malaysia expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sketch product ideas in 2D and 3D, choose the best by the design elements, then sketch and improve the chosen product.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Malaysia's *Reka Bentuk dan Teknologi* (RBT — Design and Technology) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Malaysia counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sketching products in 2D and 3D** — Product ideas are sketched both flat and solid: basic product sketches in 2D show the faces, and in 3D show the whole form, so ideas can be compared fairly before one is chosen.
2. **Choosing and improving by the elements** — The best idea is chosen using the design elements as the tests; the chosen product is then sketched properly and improved — the sketch is a decision tool, not a drawing exercise.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sketch a product idea in 2D and in 3D.
2. Choose between ideas using the elements, and improve the chosen sketch.
3. Explain how Malaysia's book teaches it.
## What you need
Sketching paper and pencils.
