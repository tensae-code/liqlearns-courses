# Lesson plan — The construction process stages and the technological route card

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** The construction process stages and the technological route card
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the school technological card (carta tecnológica escolar) turns the construction process into a school habit: stages in order, operations named, means listed — the same discipline the route card teaches, written for the learner's own build.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's *Educación Laboral y Dibujo Básico — 7mo grado (2023 Biblioteca edition)* and the Cuba books in lesson/sources.md — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba counts in the acceptance count for its student-book placements; its programme and methodological-orientations placements are evidence only, counted nowhere.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Stages, written as a school card** — Cuba's 2023 Grade 7 book teaches the construction process stages through the carta tecnológica escolar: the learner writes the stages of their own article's making, in order, before making starts.
2. **From card to article** — The card is then the build's companion: work follows it stage by stage, and the finished article is compared against it. The habit transfers — any new article can be planned the same way, because the stages do not change, only the article does.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.
