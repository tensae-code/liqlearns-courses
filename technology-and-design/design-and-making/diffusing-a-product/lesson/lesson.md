# Diffusing a product

## Do it first
Think of a good product you only heard about late. Write how you finally heard of it, and what the message told you. That message was somebody's diffusion work.

## Sub lesson 1 — A made product must be made known
Haiti's water sequence does not end at the production line. Diffusion (diffusion) is its closing technology: the device exists, priced and produced in series — and is worth nothing to the village that never hears of it. Making the product known is part of the technology project, taught with the same seriousness as the making.

## Sub lesson 2 — Designing the communication support
The teams design the communication support for their device: an advertising brochure for the water filter. A brochure is itself a designed product — it must say what the device is, what need it answers, what it costs and how to get it, in words and images its readers own. The design skills of the whole course return here, aimed at a reader who must be persuaded, not taught.

## Sub lesson 3 — Entrepreneurship — answering a societal need
The sequence closes with its synthesis: entrepreneurship is answering a societal need with a good or service. The class has lived the definition — a surveyed drinking-water need, a specified, prototyped, produced, priced and diffused answer. Diffusion completes the arc: the need was found in society, and the answer returns to society, announced.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a made product must be diffused — made known: the communication support (here, an advertising brochure for the water-filter device) carries the product to its users, and the whole project closes on entrepreneurship as answering a societal need with a good or service. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
