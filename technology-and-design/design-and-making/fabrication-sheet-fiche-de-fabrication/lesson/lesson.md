# Fabrication sheet / fiche de fabrication

## Do it first
Think of something you have made or cooked. Write, from memory, the materials and the steps in order. Notice what you cannot remember exactly — that gap is why makers write the sheet during the work.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a fabrication sheet records
A fiche de fabrication is the written record of a make. It records with what the thing was made — the materials and tools used — and how it was made — the operations in their order. In Haiti's fishing sequence, each group produces a fabrication sheet for its boat maquette: the sheet is part of the deliverable, not an extra. A make without its sheet cannot be checked against its specification or repeated by another group.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing the sheet for your make
The sheet follows the fabrication as it happened: each operation is named, with the material shaped and the tool used, in the order the work was done. Guessed or copied steps corrupt the record — if a step was changed during making, the sheet records the change. Written this way, the sheet is evidence: a reader can rebuild the same object from the sheet alone and get the same result.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the sheet matters
The fabrication sheet is what lets a make be judged and repeated. Against the specification, it shows whether the planned process was followed. For another maker, it is the recipe. For the group itself, it is where improvements are planned: the step that wasted material or time is visible on the sheet, and the next make changes that step. Recording is a technology skill in its own right.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a fabrication sheet records with what and how a thing was made — materials, tools and the steps in order — so the make can be checked, repeated and improved. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
