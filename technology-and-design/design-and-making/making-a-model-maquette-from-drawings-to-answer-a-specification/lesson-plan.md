# Lesson plan — Making a model / maquette from drawings to answer a specification

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** Making a model / maquette from drawings to answer a specification
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Haiti expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a maquette is a model built from drawings to answer a specification: the drawings come first, the model is built to the cahier des charges, and the finished model is checked against it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — Haiti's ITAP Projet Nectar pedagogical sequences (MENFP) are the books in hand for this topic (see lesson/sources.md). Teacher guides are evidence, counted nowhere in the acceptance count.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Drawings first, then the model** — The specification is turned into drawings before anything is cut.
2. **Building the maquette to the specification** — Materials are traced, cut and shaped so the model matches its drawings.
3. **What the model proves — and what it cannot** — A maquette tests shape, proportion and assembly at small cost.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Turn a specification into drawings for a model.
2. Build a maquette that matches its drawings.
3. Say what a model proves and what it cannot prove.
## What you need
Paper and pencil for drawings, card or light material, cutting tools used under the safety rules, and the specification (cahier des charges) for the model.
