# The design brief

## Do it first
You are asked to make 'a top'. A top for what — a social, for work, for sport? Write three features your top must have for one chosen occasion. You have just begun a design brief.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a brief is
Before making, the maker writes what success looks like. For a garment: who wears it, for what occasion, and which features are non-negotiable. Every later decision — fibre, style, decoration — is judged against this page.

## Sub lesson 2 — Working a brief — the garment project
The brief does not end at planning. The garment is constructed step by step — pattern notches matched so pieces meet truly, layers pinned and tacked before machining, seams machined, the hem finished by hand — and at the end the product is evaluated against the brief that ordered it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Briefing a new garment — the school-social top
The order matters: purpose first (what must this top do at a social?), materials knowledge second (which fibres give those features?), design decisions last (style and print chosen to deliver the features). Decide style first and the purpose is served by luck.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a design brief states what is to be made and what it must do: Samoa works it twice — constructing a garment to a brief, and identifying the key features a garment needs for its purpose before deciding style and design. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
