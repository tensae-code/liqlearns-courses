# Lesson plan — Evaluating a made product

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** Evaluating a made product
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a made product is evaluated with a prescribed checklist and rating scale — neatness, accuracy and functionality — and judged against the original need it was made to meet.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Jamaica's *National Standards Curriculum Guide — Grades 4–6 — Resource & Technology* (Ministry of Education, Youth and Information, Kingston, Jamaica, © 2018), a curriculum guide with full unit teaching content, read COMPLETE (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (curriculum guide with full teaching content, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The checklist and the rating scale** — Evaluation is not an impression: a prescribed checklist names what to look at, and a rating scale records how well each item scores.
2. **Neatness, accuracy, functionality** — Three words carry the critique: is it neat, is it accurate to its design, and does it function?
3. **Judged against the original need** — The last question is the first one: does it meet the original need? — and the project's careers and next steps grow from the verdict.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Use a checklist and rating scale to evaluate a made product.
2. Critique a product for neatness, accuracy and functionality.
3. Evaluate a product against its original need and state a verdict.
## What you need
A made product (flashlight, motor or any project piece), a checklist and a pencil.
