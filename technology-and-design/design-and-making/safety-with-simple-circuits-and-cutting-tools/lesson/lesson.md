# Safety with simple circuits and cutting tools

## Do it first
Before touching any tool, walk the flashlight project stage by stage — housing, wiring, shade, assembly — and write one safety precaution for each stage. Only then begin.

## Sub lesson 1 — Precautions with simple circuits
Battery circuits are gentle, but the habits learned on them are the habits of all electrical work. Work with dry hands; use wires with sound insulation except at the stripped contact; never join the battery's two sides directly together (a short circuit heats the wire and kills the battery); and check the layout against the schematic before the final connection is made. Jamaica teaches safety precautions to observe when making simple circuits, as part of the circuit work itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cutting tools, used safely
Cutting tools appear at several stages of the flashlight build, and Jamaica names using cutting tools safely as its own teaching point. The rules travel with the tool: blade directed away from the body and the holding hand; material held or supported so it cannot slip into the cut; full attention while the edge is moving; the tool closed, covered or laid safely the moment the cut ends — never carried open, never left where a hand will find it blindly.

## Sub lesson 3 — Safety at each stage
Jamaica's guide places safety precautions at each stage of the project, not in one opening talk. That placement is the lesson: before each new stage, the maker asks what this stage can hurt and what its precaution is. A maker who asks that question by habit is safe in workshops far beyond this one.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: electrical making has its own safety rules: precautions are observed when making simple circuits and at each stage of the project, and cutting tools are used safely throughout. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
