# Workshop safety and material-shaping processes

## Do it first
Look at a cutting tool your class uses. Write where your holding hand goes, where your free hand must stay, and where the cut travels. That is a safety instruction in your own words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Safety before the tool moves
In Haiti's fishing sequence, the making of the boat maquettes is governed by safety instructions for handling tools — consignes de sécurité given before the work, not after an accident. The rule is general: know how the tool is held, where the free hand stays, where the cut travels, and what protection the task needs, before the first move. Safety is part of the process, not a pause in it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tracing, cutting, shaping — the three shaping moves
Material-shaping processes turn flat stock into parts. Tracing (traçage) transfers the drawing's lines onto the material. Cutting separates the part along the traced line. Shaping (façonnage) gives the part its final form — bending, filing, smoothing, curving. Each move has its own tools and its own dangers, which is why the safety instructions name the moves separately.

## Sub lesson 3 — Assembling the model safely
The maquettes are assembled under the same discipline: parts are held securely, sharp edges and points are controlled, and tools are put down safely between operations. A workshop that is safe for the first cut and careless at assembly is not safe. The lesson closes where it began: the instructions cover the whole fabrication, from tracing to the finished assembly.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: safe making rests on three shaping moves — tracing, cutting and shaping — each done under safety instructions for handling the tools that do it. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
