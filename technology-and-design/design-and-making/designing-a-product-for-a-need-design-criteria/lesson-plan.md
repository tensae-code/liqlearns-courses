# Lesson plan — Designing a product for a need (design criteria)

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** Designing a product for a need (design criteria)
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a product is designed for a stated need and judged by criteria — for Jamaica's flashlight: ease of handling and care, brightness, size and shape, an easy switch, a durable housing, and a sound circuit.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Jamaica's *National Standards Curriculum Guide — Grades 4–6 — Resource & Technology* (Ministry of Education, Youth and Information, Kingston, Jamaica, © 2018), a curriculum guide with full unit teaching content, read COMPLETE (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (curriculum guide with full teaching content, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Start from a stated need** — Design begins with who needs what: Jamaica's teams design a simple light for children, for hurricane season — a real user, a real situation.
2. **The criteria of a good flashlight** — Six qualities judge the design: handling and care, brightness of beam, size and shape, ease of the switch, durability of the housing, and the circuit design.
3. **Designing against the criteria** — Every design choice is a trade among the criteria — bigger batteries brighten the beam but grow the size — and the criteria settle the argument.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State a user need plainly enough to design against it.
2. Name Jamaica's six flashlight criteria and explain each one.
3. Use criteria to settle a design trade-off (e.g. brightness against size).
## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and a real flashlight to judge.
