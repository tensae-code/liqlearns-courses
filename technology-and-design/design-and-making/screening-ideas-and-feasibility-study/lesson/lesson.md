# Screening ideas and feasibility study

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Screening ideas
Generation produces many ideas; screening chooses among them. Ideas are compared against the need, the customer, the cost and the maker's ability, and the weak ones are set aside with reasons recorded. Screening is judgement applied after generation — the stage brainstorming deliberately postpones.

## Sub lesson 2 — The feasibility study
A feasibility study asks whether the chosen idea can actually succeed. Market feasibility studies the buyers: who they are, what they will pay, what competes, and how the product will be marketed. Technical feasibility studies the making: equipment, facilities, materials and skills — can this be produced, here, at the needed quality and cost? An idea that fails either test is changed or dropped before real money is spent.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: before building, ideas are screened and the survivor is tested by a feasibility study — can it sell (market feasibility) and can it be made (technical feasibility)? Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
