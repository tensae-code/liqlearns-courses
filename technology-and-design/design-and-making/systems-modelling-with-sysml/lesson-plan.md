# Lesson plan — Systems modelling with SysML

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** Systems modelling with SysML
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Morocco expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** SysML models a system in diagrams — requirements, behaviour and structure — so a design can be read, checked against its functional specification (Cdcf), and discussed before anything is built.

## Sub lessons
1. **Modelling a system with SysML — requirements, behaviour, structure** — SysML is a modelling language for systems. Its requirements diagrams record what the system must satisfy; its behaviour diagrams (use-case, activity, sequence, state) record what the system does; its structure diagrams (block definition, internal block) record what the system is made of. Each diagram answers one question and stays readable on one page.
2. **Reading the diagrams against a functional specification (Cdcf)** — Morocco works SysML on a real specification: an automated gate's Cdcf. Each requirement in the Cdcf is traced into the requirements diagram, each behaviour is checked against a use case, and each block against the structure the behaviour needs — a requirement with no block, or a block serving no requirement, is a design fault found early.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three SysML diagram families and the question each answers.
2. Read a use-case diagram: actors, system boundary, services.
3. Trace one requirement from a Cdcf into the diagrams and say what would count as a fault.
## What you need
Paper and a ruler for drawing diagram boxes and links; a simple specification to model (a gate, a lamp, a water tank).
