# Systems modelling with SysML

## Do it first
Draw three boxes for a school gate: what it must do, what happens when a car arrives, what parts it has. You have sketched the three SysML questions: requirements, behaviour, structure.

## Sub lesson 1 — Modelling a system with SysML — requirements, behaviour, structure
A model is not the system — it is a chosen view of it. The requirements diagram links each requirement of the specification to the parts that satisfy it. The use-case diagram shows the actors around the system and the services it renders them. The block-definition diagram shows the system's blocks and how they compose. Morocco teaches SysML inside systems engineering: model first, so errors are found on paper.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the diagrams against a functional specification (Cdcf)
Reading a model against its Cdcf is a checking discipline: pick a requirement, find it in the requirements diagram, follow it to the behaviour that fulfils it, then to the blocks that carry that behaviour. The Grade 9 guide details the same three diagram families — requirements diagram, use-case diagram, block-definition diagram — as the teacher resource for the whole collège course.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: SysML models a system in diagrams — requirements, behaviour and structure — so a design can be read, checked against its functional specification (Cdcf), and discussed before anything is built. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
