# The design project — carrying a project through

## Do it first
Your class must light a small lamp without mains electricity, using only wind. List what you would need to know before building anything: how strong the wind is, how fast a rotor must turn, what the lamp needs. That list is a project beginning properly.

## Sub lesson 1 — From context and specification to a buildable model
Carrying a project through means no stage is skipped. The context analysis asks who the product serves and what constraints bind it — including rules: Morocco notes the building-permit rule for masts of 12 m or more. Measurement comes before making: wind speed is measured, not assumed, because the alternator choice and the rotor size depend on it. The proposed model must answer the specification on paper before any part is cut.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making, assembling and testing
Making follows the model's drawings and the functional chains: rotor, transmission, alternator, support. Assembly joins the chains in order — energy chain from wind to electricity, information chain if the turbine regulates itself. The final test is a verdict on the whole project: measured output against the specification's promise. A project is finished when the made thing passes its own specification, and the report says how.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a design project is carried through in full: analyse the context, write the specification, model the system, make and assemble the parts, then test that it works — Morocco's worked project designs and builds an autonomous wind turbine. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
