# Setting up an enterprise

## Do it first
Imagine you will sell one product at your school — juice, notebooks, phone charging. Answer three questions: who would buy it, what would it cost you to make one, what price would you charge? You have just walked the enterprise path.

## Sub lesson 1 — Finding the idea and studying the market
The idea alone is not enough; the market study turns an idea into a decision. A survey asks future clients directly — the Moroccan course surveys buyers for a solar-energy product before committing. A product study examines the product itself: what it does, what it costs, who it serves. If the study shows no clients or no margin, the honest answer is to change the idea.

## Sub lesson 2 — Name and logo, price from costs, and the commercial plan
Price is calculated, not wished: add the costs of one unit — materials, energy, work, distribution — then add the margin that keeps the enterprise alive; Morocco works the calculation for a fan manufacturer. The commercial plan says how clients will learn of the product (advertising poster), how it is presented (packaging: the product's own pack, grouping pack, transport pack), and through which channels it is sold, including modern channels.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: setting up an enterprise runs in order: find the idea, study the market, choose a name and logo, fix the selling price from costs and margin, and write the commercial plan that will sell the product. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
