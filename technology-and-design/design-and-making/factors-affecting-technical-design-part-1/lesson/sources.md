besides form, the product's beauty is also expressed through graphic-design principles including proportion, balance, harmony and space); ergonomics (nhân trắc — the factor expressing the relation between the structure, shape and movement capacity of the human body and the designed product: a correctly designed product makes the user feel safe, convenient, comfortable and does not affect health in use; in designing products for people, three main elements need care — the user's body size, the movements performed when using the designed product, and the body's reactions when using the product through the senses; a design ensuring good ergonomics includes use and work in a neutral posture, suitable interface design and reasonable manipulation, use of familiar operations, limiting strange and difficult operations, little movement and repeated action, creating movements that change posture, creating an operating space large enough, avoiding prolonged static postures, avoiding excessive forces, avoiding sharp points, focusing, maintaining a comfortable working environment); safety (an toàn — for the product, the user and the environment, one of the factors most affecting the design process, needing consideration at many different times from design and production to product use; design must attend to factors related to electricity, heat, and potential fire and explosion incidents of the product in use, and use warnings or express messages to the user about the safety level — for example red means danger, amber means caution, green means safe); energy (năng lượng — the energy needed to design, make and use the product is currently taken mainly from depleting fossil sources, so a designed product needs to consume as little energy as possible, and energy saving must be set right in the design process; design activity affects the exploitation and use of renewable energy sources to replace fossil energy such as wind, solar, water and biomass energy); the product life cycle (vòng đời sản phẩm — analysis covers the detailed measures at all stages of production, use and disposal of the product; the energy and materials used are determined and the waste created is measured at each stage of the product's production process; life-cycle analysis lets the engineer calculate and quantify fuel and material consumption in use, transport and recycling and the waste-disposal cost when the product is discarded); sustainable development (phát triển bền vững — development that meets present needs without harming future generations' ability to meet their needs; a designed product on one side positively affects production and life, on the other can exhaust natural resources and negatively affect the environment such as air and water pollution and toxic chemical contamination; these effects run from the design process through use to disposal of the product; this factor must be cared about in every step of the design process, ensuring minimum negative impact on the environment, and using recyclable materials is one solution toward sustainable development) (original string `factors affecting technical design, part 1`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 10 — Thiết kế và công nghệ*, printed p. 109 |
