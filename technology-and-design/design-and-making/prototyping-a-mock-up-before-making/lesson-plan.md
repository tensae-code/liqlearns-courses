# Lesson plan — Prototyping — a mock-up before making

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** Prototyping — a mock-up before making
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before making the real thing, make a cheap mock-up of it — Jamaica's makers build the toy car's body in cardboard first and check it fits the chassis — so faults are found in card, not in the final material.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Jamaica's *National Standards Curriculum Guide — Grades 4–6 — Resource & Technology* (Ministry of Education, Youth and Information, Kingston, Jamaica, © 2018), a curriculum guide with full unit teaching content, read COMPLETE (see lesson/sources.md). ## Sub lessons
1. **Why mock up first** — A mock-up is a full-size, cheap-material trial of the design: every fault it reveals costs card, not the real material.
2. **Checking the mock-up against what exists** — The mock-up is tested against the fixed parts it must fit — Jamaica's car body mock-up is checked for compatibility with the chassis before the real body is made.
3. **Testing the finished project** — Prototyping thinking continues to the end: the finished project is tested for accuracy and workability.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain why a mock-up is made before the real thing.
2. Make a full-size cardboard mock-up and check its compatibility with the part it must fit.
3. Test a finished project for accuracy and workability.
## What you need
Cardboard, scissors, tape, a ruler, and the object or chassis the mock-up must fit.
