| the means supporting technical design, and applying methods and means through the process (worked on a posture-warning device for school pupils): the main means for performing the design steps are documents, diagrams, books and newspapers, the internet, paper and drawing pens, sticky notes, coloured paper; electronic devices and software (computers, smartphones (photography, recording, video, notes…), drafting and office software (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Mindmap…), technical-design support software such as AutoCAD, SolidWorks, Tinkercad, SketchUp… letting objects be expressed in many views for observing 2D and 3D images, survey software and information-processing software, 3D-printing support software…); measuring tools (length-measuring tools, angle rulers, diameter-measuring tools (Panme/micrometer), set squares, compasses, multimeters, thermometers…); materials (mica sheets, foam sheets, plastic sheets, wood, metal sheets, 3D-printing filament coils…); and material-machining tools and equipment (hand saws, files, chisels, drills, cutting machines, welding machines, glue guns, 3D printers, 3D pens…); applying them stage by stage — defining the problem (observe everything around, pass places regularly, use the 5W1H questioning technique to know users' needs and difficulties clearly; record answers to understand the problem; use survey tables on software to analyse information faster and more exactly; photography and video help analyse information — the example discovers pupils' wrong sitting posture at the desk: eyes closer than the safe 25–30 cm to the book, back bent, chest against the table, risking spinal curvature and short-sightedness); overview research (in some cases the idea appears immediately in the mind, but for a feasible, best solution, research more information about the problem — what knowledge helps solve it, who has solved it before, what their solution's features are; apply the survey method, design questionnaires to survey the problem or interview product users and service-experience providers for detailed, multi-dimensional information; use search tools to find needed information, documents and related scientific research and existing products; present with a mind map for an overview of the problem; study existing market solutions — hand-held posture reminders, adjustable-angle reading stands, adjustable desk-and-chair sets — analysing them to find inconveniences (uncomfortable, cramped, easy to forget when sitting too close, no reminder sound when the eyes are too close); the new product must be compact, easy to use, able to detect and warn when a pupil sits at the desk with eyes closer than the safe distance); determining the product's requirements (consider the product from many viewpoints — feasibility, working effectiveness, technical indices, safety, economics, aesthetics, environmental friendliness…; use checklists or Rubric evaluation tables to list the important criteria so none is missed; the requirements table for the distance-warning machine: (1) detect wrong sitting posture — warn when the eyes are within 30 cm; (2) warn the sitter correctly — switch off the warning when the eyes are over 30 cm; (3) usable by many ages — customisable to many users' heights; (4) aesthetic, compact, easy to make — within 15 cm × 18 cm, the smaller the better; (5) comfortable and safe — placed in front of the cover, not obstructing writing, causing no danger to children in use; (6) reasonable price — cheaper than existing products of the same purpose); proposing, evaluating and choosing a solution (find ideas by brainstorming, creating as many ideas as possible, with a chance of a highly creative solution appearing; use mind maps in this stage to systematise and arrange thinking visually; the candidate solutions for the warning device — stretching a spring wire to ring a bell, touching a switch placed in front, using an infrared sensor to receive signals and warn by light or buzzer — evaluated in a table of advantages, disadvantages and interest (the spring wire: simple, low cost, but uncomfortable when reading or writing, cannot be observed; the front switch: compact, better than keeping the wire, has a signal, comfortable, but still takes up the small desk space so it can be avoided; the infrared sensor: does not obstruct the view, wide sensing range, no direct effect on the body, but a more complex electronic circuit than usual and many shapes with bells and lights; the sensor solution chosen); building the prototype (the solution must be presented concretely in shape, size, material, technical requirements and the relative positions of the parts on the detail drawing, assembly drawing and other representations; technical-design support software such as AutoCAD, SolidWorks, Tinkercad, SketchUp… represents objects in 2D and 3D images, and programming-support software creates complex parts exactly and quickly from the design on the computer, simulating the product's operation; use hand tools and material-machining equipment such as files, hand saws, drills, cutting machines, welding machines… to machine the materials; use the parts list (bảng kê) to note clearly the parts making the prototype — for the warning machine: ultrasonic sensor HY-SRF05 43 × 20 × 17 mm, red and green LEDs, 5 V buzzer, resistors 1 kΩ and 10 Ω, test circuit board 165 × 55 × 10 mm, Arduino Uno R3 board, Arduino cable, male-male and male-female jumper wires, cardboard, 5–9 V battery); testing and verifying the solution (the prototype is experimented on in a real environment (or recreated like the real one) to check the parameters and corresponding results under the experiment conditions to process the data, adjust and improve the prototype if needed; compare the results with the product requirements table to evaluate the level of success of the designed product; use surveys to collect users' opinions to evaluate the product's usefulness and convenience; then complete the product and make its technical file); making the technical file (for pupils' design exercises, the learning result is reported through the product's technical file and idea-publication activities organised at educational institutions; reports can be presented as writing, exhibitions, posters, books, films, documents…; the popular activity is organising technical festivals and exhibitions of products together with their technical files) (original string `the means supporting technical design, and applying methods and means through the process (worked on a posture-warning device for school pupils)`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 10 — Thiết kế và công nghệ*, printed p. 121 |
