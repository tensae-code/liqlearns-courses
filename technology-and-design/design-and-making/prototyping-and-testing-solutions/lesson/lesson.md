# Prototyping and testing solutions

## Do it first
Pour muddy water through a cloth. Look at what comes through. Write what the cloth caught and what it did not. You have just tested a one-layer prototype — this lesson builds the full device.

## Sub lesson 1 — Prototype — the first working try
In the water-treatment sequence, each team produces a prototype drinking-water device. A prototype's job is evidence: does this solution work at all, and where does it fail? It is built from the chosen solution, cheaply and honestly, because its failures are the information the next version needs. Judging a prototype as a finished product misses its purpose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the bottle filter
The prototype device is a bottle filter with named layers: gravel and clay balls, fine sand, activated charcoal and cotton. Each layer has work to do as water passes — the coarse layers stop the coarse dirt, the sand filters finer, the charcoal takes up what water carries dissolved and coloured, the cotton holds the bed together at the outlet. The build is the specification made testable.

## Sub lesson 3 — Trying it — and working safely
The prototype is tried: water goes through, and the result is examined against the need the specification stated. The building itself is governed by safety instructions for cutting and sharp tools — the bottle is cut, and sharp edges are part of the work. Prototype, test, and safety arrive together in the sequence, because a solution that cannot be built and tried safely is not yet a solution.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a prototype is a first working version built to be tried: Haiti's teams build a bottle water-filter — gravel and clay balls, fine sand, activated charcoal, cotton — and test whether their solution actually cleans water. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
