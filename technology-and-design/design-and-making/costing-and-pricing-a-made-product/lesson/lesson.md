# Costing and pricing a made product

## Do it first
List everything that goes into one portion of a food you could sell — ingredients, fuel, packaging, your time. Add it up. That sum is why a price is built, not guessed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cost price and production cost
Haiti's water sequence gives pricing its own session, and it starts below the price: the cost price and production cost of the device — materials, and the costs of producing it in the small series the class organised. A price set without the costs is a wish; the costs are counted first, per unit, from the real production the learners ran.

## Sub lesson 2 — Margin and taxes
On top of the costs stand two additions: the margin — what the makers add for the enterprise to live and grow — and taxes, what must be handed over by rule. Selling price = costs + margin + taxes, in that construction. Leaving out taxes invents profit that is not there; leaving out margin prices the enterprise to die politely.

## Sub lesson 3 — Setting the price against competition
The session's final move is outward: the price is positioned against competition — what do comparable solutions cost the buyer? A price built from costs but blind to competition may sit where no buyer will meet it; a price copied from competition but blind to costs may sell at a loss. The sequence teaches both eyes open: build from costs, then position against the market.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a selling price is built, not guessed: from the cost price and production cost of the device, plus margin and taxes — and the final price is set with the competition in view. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
