# Work schedules for a design project

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Malaysia's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two ways to draw time
A work schedule draws time two ways: network activity blocks with milestones — activities joined in their true order — and Gantt or bar charts, which lay the work across weeks; the book works a basket-making project over 9 weeks.

## Sub lesson 2 — Project control
A schedule is also controlled: resources, cost, quality and spending are checked against it as the project runs, so drift is caught while it can still be fixed.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a work schedule is drawn as network blocks with milestones and as a Gantt chart — and controlled on resources, cost, quality and spending. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
