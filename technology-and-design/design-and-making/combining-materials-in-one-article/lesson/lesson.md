# Combining materials in one article

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing each material's part
A combined article starts by dividing the job: the strong frame, the light cover, the flexible join. Cuba's Grade 6 programme names the combination directly — metal with recovered plastic, wood and wire — in animal silhouettes, key rings and mobile toys.

## Sub lesson 2 — Joining unlike materials
Unlike materials meet unlike: the maker must join metal to plastic, wood to wire, without splitting, melting or tearing any of them. The joining method is chosen per pair, tested on offcuts, and only then used on the article.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: one article can be built from several materials together — metal, recovered plastic, wood and wire — when each material is given the part of the work it does best. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
