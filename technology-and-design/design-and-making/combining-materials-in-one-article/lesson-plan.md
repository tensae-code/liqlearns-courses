# Lesson plan — Combining materials in one article

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** Combining materials in one article
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one article can be built from several materials together — metal, recovered plastic, wood and wire — when each material is given the part of the work it does best.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's Educación Laboral programmes and methodological orientations (teacher programme documents) — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba is evidence only for this lesson, counted nowhere in the acceptance count.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Choosing each material's part** — A combined article starts by dividing the job: the strong frame, the light cover, the flexible join. Cuba's Grade 6 programme names the combination directly — metal with recovered plastic, wood and wire — in animal silhouettes, key rings and mobile toys.
2. **Joining unlike materials** — Unlike materials meet unlike: the maker must join metal to plastic, wood to wire, without splitting, melting or tearing any of them. The joining method is chosen per pair, tested on offcuts, and only then used on the article.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Offcuts of metal, plastic, wood and wire; joining means (wire, glue, small screws); sketch paper.
