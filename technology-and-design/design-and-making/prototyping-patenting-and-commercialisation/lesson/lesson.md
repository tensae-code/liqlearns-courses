# Prototyping, patenting and commercialisation

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Prototype, then improve
A prototype (pilot) is the first working form of the product, built to be tested. Its weaknesses are found in use, the design is improved, and only the improved version goes to market launch. Skipping the prototype means the first customers do the testing — at the maker's expense.

## Sub lesson 2 — Patent and the routes to market
A patent protects an invention that meets three conditions: it is novel (new), useful, and non-obvious. In return for publishing how it works, the inventor gains the exclusive right to it for a time. Commercialisation then takes one of several routes: produce and market it yourself, license it to a producer, or sell the rights — each route trading control against risk and reach.
