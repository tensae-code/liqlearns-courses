# Choosing technical solutions

## Do it first
You have a fixed sum to furnish one room. List three things you want in it and what each costs. Now choose. Write which want you traded away and why — you have chosen a technical solution.

## Sub lesson 1 — Solutions are chosen, not found
The closing resource synthesis of Haiti's greenhouse sequence states the general rule behind the whole project: a technical solution is a choice. For the greenhouse, several workable solutions existed; the group chose among them. Choosing well means naming what is being traded — a bigger structure against its cost, a stronger material against its price — instead of pretending one solution was simply 'the answer'.

## Sub lesson 2 — Size, materials and budget trade-offs
Size, materials and budget form the trade-off triangle of the synthesis. Enlarge the size and the budget strains; upgrade the materials and the budget strains the other way; cut the budget and size or materials must yield. A chosen solution is a stated balance among the three — and the statement can be defended, changed and learned from, which a silent choice cannot.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dimensions follow space, contents and budget
The synthesis gives the dimensions rule outright: a greenhouse's dimensions follow the available space, its contents (what will grow and be done inside), and the budget. Measure the space first, list the contents, count the budget — the dimensions are the result of the three, not a number picked before them. The rule transfers: any made thing is sized by where it goes, what it holds and what can be spent.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: technical solutions are chosen by trading off size, materials and budget together — and a structure's dimensions follow the available space, what it must hold, and the budget, never one factor alone. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
