| methods and techniques supporting technical design: the common methods — brainstorming (động não — used to mobilise many new, creative ideas or analyse and evaluate solutions to a problem in the design process, working through discussion, ideas focusing on the problem, not limiting any idea, and from there drawing out the most prominent idea; usable in all steps of the design process, and used much in the steps of defining the problem, proposing, evaluating and choosing a solution, experimenting and verifying the solution, and adjusting the design to complete the product); the mind map (sơ đồ tư duy — using key words combined with connecting lines, arrows, pictures, symbols, colours… by simple, easy-to-understand rules of one's own, drawing a map of one's thinking in a visual and unified way, helping to systematise and arrange thinking, analyse information to solve a problem or record it effectively; used in defining the problem, overview research and determining requirements, expressing the specific context of the problem, evaluating resources to solve the problem, clarifying the design requirements and the criteria the product must achieve); the survey method (điều tra — collecting information by questionnaire with many people at the same time using a printed questionnaire; the questioned person marks their own opinion in the corresponding box; used in the defining-the-problem stage of the design process to collect information defining the research purpose, the requirements and the wishes of users for the product to be designed; the survey is conducted by direct interview, telephone interview, letter or internet); the questioning technique (kĩ thuật đặt câu hỏi — a thinking technique using a system of purposeful, logically ordered questions to learn, collect information, discover and study a problem in depth; from there, plan and build the design process to the user's actual requirements and needs; the most common question technique is 5W1H — What, Where, When, Why, Who, How; it ensures all aspects of the problem of concern are covered; used in almost all stages of technical design, and especially effectively in defining the problem, overview research and determining the product's requirements); the SCAMPER method (a creative-thinking method improving an existing product, process or service by posing and answering questions in seven different aspects — Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, Rearrange/Reverse — ensuring diversity of viewpoint, the technique forming new creative ideas in technical design activity; used in the stages of proposing, evaluating and choosing a solution, building the prototype for the solution, and adjusting the design — thanks to it solutions and products become more creative and effective); and other methods and techniques — Checklist, PMI (analysing strengths and weaknesses), scientific research, Rubric evaluation tables… (original string `methods and techniques supporting technical design`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 10 — Thiết kế và công nghệ*, printed p. 119 |
