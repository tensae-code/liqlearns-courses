| Lesson part | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| the basic steps of technical design, as a flow that can repeat until the product meets the requirements: step 1 define the problem and build the criteria (describe the real situation clearly — what the problem or main need is, why it must be solved, whom solving it benefits, what product must be designed and what criteria it must meet: function, durability, aesthetics, price, size, style, efficiency of fuel/energy use, environmental protection…); step 2 research the overview, propose and choose a solution (study similar products on the market and the science and technology for solving the problem, inheriting existing solutions' strengths and avoiding their errors; propose several solutions, weigh each one's pros and cons, and choose the most optimal for the economic conditions, resources and time — the chosen solution must stick closely to the criteria); step 3 build the prototype (the chosen solution is expressed as a detail drawing with full information to make the prototype — the first version of the product, possibly in different materials from the final product; prepare materials, plan the making and assembly, and build it, checking and evaluating against the criteria during and after making); step 4 test and evaluate (test the prototype, measure the technical parameters, compare with the criteria; adjust as needed until it meets them; experts and customers may join the evaluation; computers and simulation software now support design and testing so well that some cases need no physical prototype); step 5 make the technical documentation (the final step: the technical file of the designed product — technical drawings for manufacture, plus documents on use, installation, operation, maintenance and repair; afterwards the designer may publish the results or register the invention's copyright if the solution is new and creative) (original string `the basic steps of technical design, as a flow that can repeat until the product meets the requirements`) | Vietnam, *Công nghệ 8*, printed p. 96 |
