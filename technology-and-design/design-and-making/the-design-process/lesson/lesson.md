# The design process

## Do it first
Think of one small problem near you — a door that will not stay open, a book that keeps falling. Write the problem in one sentence, sketch two different answers, and say which one you would make and why. You have just run the first three steps of the design process.

## Sub lesson 1 — Define, generate, select
Jamaica's guide gives every project the same backbone, called the 'E' Design Process. First, define the problem in its context: who needs what, where, and why. Next, generate ideas — more than one, on purpose, because the first idea is rarely the best. Then select a solution: compare the ideas against the resources you actually have, how efficiently each one works, and what each one costs. A solution you cannot make with your materials is not a solution yet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Test, produce, evaluate
Before full making, test the solution — a trial, a mock-up or a small try-out shows faults while they are still cheap to fix. Then produce it: work safely, accurately and efficiently. When it is finished, evaluate it against the original problem and the design, not against your hopes: does it do the job it was defined to do? Evaluation is a step of the process, not an afterthought.

## Sub lesson 3 — Present, and the five aims
The last step is to present the results: show the product and explain how it answers the need. Jamaica's appendix names five aims for the whole process: identify needs and problems; plan work to criteria and deadlines; use resources, including the natural environment, to make alternative proposals; produce a product or system that solves the need; and evaluate both the solution and the process. The same seven steps run in every Jamaica project in this course — the toy car, the flashlight and the electromagnetic motor.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: designing follows Jamaica's 'E' Design Process: define the problem in its context, generate ideas, select a solution, test it, produce it, evaluate it against the original problem, and present the results. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
