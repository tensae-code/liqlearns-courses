# Lesson plan — The design process

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Design and Making · **Lesson:** The design process
**Place in the curriculum:** Design and Making (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** designing follows Jamaica's 'E' Design Process: define the problem in its context, generate ideas, select a solution, test it, produce it, evaluate it against the original problem, and present the results.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Jamaica's *National Standards Curriculum Guide — Grades 4–6 — Resource & Technology* (Ministry of Education, Youth and Information, Kingston, Jamaica, © 2018), a curriculum guide with full unit teaching content, read COMPLETE (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (curriculum guide with full teaching content, not evidence-only).

Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Define, generate, select** — Every project starts by naming the real problem, then making more than one idea, then choosing one against resources, efficiency and cost.
2. **Test, produce, evaluate** — The chosen idea is tested, then made with safety, accuracy and efficiency, then judged honestly against the original problem.
3. **Present, and the five aims** — Results are presented so others can see the problem, the process and the answer — and the whole process serves five aims.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Design and Making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the seven steps of Jamaica's 'E' Design Process in order.
2. Define a problem in its context and generate more than one idea for it.
3. Select a solution using resources, efficiency and cost, and say how it would be tested and evaluated.
## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and one real small problem to work on.
