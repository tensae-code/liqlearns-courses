# The energy chain

## Do it first
Follow a hair-dryer's energy: wall socket, switch and settings, heating element and motor, fan blades, moving hot air on your hair. Five jobs in a row — feed, distribute, convert, transmit, act. That row is the energy chain of every powered system.

## Sub lesson 1 — Feed and distribute
Feed answers 'energy, in the right form, enough of it?'; distribute answers 'to which parts, when, how much?'. A distribution fault starves one part while another is fed — the chain reading separates the two questions so faults are placed correctly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Convert and transmit
The motor's two parts name the conversion: the stator is the fixed part, the rotor the turning part; driven as a dynamo the machine is reversible — the same device converts in both directions. LED public lighting appears in this lesson as enrichment: conversion efficiency is why an LED lights a street on a fraction of a filament lamp's energy.

## Sub lesson 3 — Act — actuators at the end of the chain
Reading a whole chain end to end — feed, distribute, convert, transmit, act — is Morocco's Grade 9 energy discipline: any powered system, from a hair-dryer to a wind farm, is described by naming the carrier of each function. A weak link anywhere caps the action everywhere: the actuator can only spend what the chain delivers.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the energy chain (chaîne d'énergie) runs in five functions — feed, distribute, convert, transmit, act — carrying energy from its source to the action the user wants, each function with its own requirements and devices. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
