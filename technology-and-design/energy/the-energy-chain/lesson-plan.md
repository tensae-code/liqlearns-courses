# Lesson plan — The energy chain

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Energy · **Lesson:** The energy chain
**Place in the curriculum:** Energy (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Morocco expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the energy chain (chaîne d'énergie) runs in five functions — feed, distribute, convert, transmit, act — carrying energy from its source to the action the user wants, each function with its own requirements and devices.

## Sub lessons
1. **Feed and distribute** — Feed (alimenter) delivers energy to the system under requirements: the nature of the current, the voltage, the power — Morocco works a wind-farm power/energy calculation to size the feeding. Distribute (distribuer) then sends the fed energy to the parts that need it, under control — switches, valves and distributors — as the hair-dryer's supply choices show: which elements receive energy, and how much, is a distribution decision.
2. **Convert and transmit** — Convert (convertir) changes energy's form: the DC motor turns electrical energy into rotation (stator standing, rotor turning), the dynamo runs the same machine backwards — rotation into electricity — and pneumatic cylinders turn air pressure into straight movement. Transmit (transmettre) then carries the converted energy to the action point, keeping or changing the movement: rotation's characteristics (speed, torque) and the difference between transmitting a movement and transforming it (rotation into translation, for example).
3. **Act — actuators at the end of the chain** — Act (agir) is the chain's end: actuators spend the transmitted energy on the system's task — motors turning, cylinders pushing, heaters heating, lamps lighting. The actuator is chosen by the action: its power, its movement, its force must match what the task demands and what the chain upstream can deliver.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five energy-chain functions in order, in French and English.
2. Describe the DC motor's conversion (stator/rotor) and the dynamo's reversibility.
3. Read a familiar powered system as a full energy chain.
## What you need
A hair-dryer or fan to observe (unplugged for handling), or system diagrams.
