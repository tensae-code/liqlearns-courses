# Renewable energy technology

## Do it first
List every source of energy your home uses. Mark each one: does nature replace it, or is it used up? Keep the marked list — it is this lesson's starting map.

## Sub lesson 1 — Renewable sources around the house
Haiti's photovoltaic sequence opens by identifying renewable sources: sunlight, wind, flowing water, and plant and animal matter (biomass) — sources that nature replaces, unlike a fuel that is used up. The identification is local: which of these sources does the learner's own place actually have? A technology lesson that starts from the sources at hand can later size a real installation for a real house.

## Sub lesson 2 — How a photovoltaic installation works
The sequence explains a domestic photovoltaic installation's functioning as a chain: the panel captures sunlight and produces direct current; a regulator protects the battery that stores it; the inverter changes stored direct current into the alternating current the house's appliances use. Each link has one job, and the chain fails if a link is missing — a panel without storage gives power only while the sun shines.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Kay peyi house as the example
The installation is not abstract: it is designed for a maison « Kay peyi » — a Haitian country house — and its real loads: lamps, a television, a fan, a radio. Teaching the system on one named house keeps every later step (schema, measurement, sizing) attached to a place the learners can picture, with appliances they know.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: renewable energy technology harvests sources that renew themselves — sun, wind, water, biomass — and a photovoltaic system turns sunlight into electricity a house can use. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
