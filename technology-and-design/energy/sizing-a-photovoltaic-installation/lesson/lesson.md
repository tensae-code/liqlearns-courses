# Sizing a photovoltaic installation

## Do it first
List the electrical appliances in one room of your home and find each one's watts (on its label or plug). Add them. That total is the beginning of a sizing calculation.

## Sub lesson 1 — List the loads first
Haiti's sequence sizes the Kay peyi installation from its loads: the two-room house's lamps, television, fan and radio, each with its power given in watts. The load list is the foundation — an installation sized without it is a guess. Listing first also forces the honest question: what will this house actually run, and for how long each day?

## Sub lesson 2 — The inverter — sum the powers
The inverter is sized by summing the appliance powers: add the watts of everything that may run at once, and the inverter must be able to supply that total as alternating current. Undersize it and the house's own appliances defeat the installation; the sum, not a hope, sets the choice.

## Sub lesson 3 — The battery — amp-hours from energy ÷ voltage
Battery capacity is calculated in amp-hours (Ah): the energy the house needs from storage, divided by the regulator voltage, gives the capacity the battery must hold. The sequence teaches the calculation itself — energy ÷ voltage = Ah — so learners can size storage for any house, not only for the Kay peyi example they worked.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: sizing (dimensionnement) starts from the load list in watts: the inverter is chosen by summing the appliances' powers, and the battery's capacity in amp-hours comes from the energy needed divided by the regulator voltage. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
