# Components of a photovoltaic system

## Do it first
Name every part of a bicycle in the order energy travels from your legs to the road. Now do the same for sunlight reaching a lamp in a solar house. The second chain is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four main components
The resource annex of Haiti's sequence names the system's main components: the panel, which captures sunlight as direct current; the regulator, which controls the charging and protects the battery; the battery, which stores the electricity; and the inverter, which converts it for the house. Four components, four distinct jobs — a system description that cannot name all four is incomplete.

## Sub lesson 2 — The energy chain
The components form a chain (chaîne d'énergie): sunlight → panel (DC produced) → regulator (charging controlled) → battery (energy stored) → inverter (DC changed to AC) → the house's appliances. Reading the chain in order explains every connection in the installation's schema: each wire exists because energy passes that way.

## Sub lesson 3 — The inverter's job — DC to AC
Panels and batteries work in direct current (DC); the house's appliances — television, fan, lamps — run on alternating current (AC). The inverter (onduleur) is the component that changes DC into AC, and that single role is why it stands last in the chain before the loads. Remove it, and stored solar electricity cannot run the house.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a photovoltaic system has four main components in one energy chain — panel, regulator, battery, inverter — and the inverter's role in the chain is to change direct current into alternating current. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
