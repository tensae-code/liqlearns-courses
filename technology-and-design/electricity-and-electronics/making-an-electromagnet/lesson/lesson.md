# Making an electromagnet

## Do it first
Write the component list for an electromagnet from memory of the lesson's rule — source, core, coil — then check it against the lesson. If your list names all three with their sizes, you are ready to build.

## Sub lesson 1 — The components
Jamaica's Grade 6 build specifies its components exactly: an AA 1.5 V battery as the source, a 4-inch wire nail as the iron core, and 5 m of copper (magnet) wire for the coil. Listing the components needed is the lesson's first step — the maker who cannot list the build cannot start it, and the list is checked before any winding begins.

## Sub lesson 2 — Winding the coil on the core
The coil is the magnet-maker. Each turn adds its effect to the turns beside it, so the winding must be tight, even and in one direction along the nail, with the two wire ends left free and their insulation stripped where they will meet the battery. The nail, being iron, concentrates the field the coil creates — the same coil in air is far weaker.

## Sub lesson 3 — Creating the field from the battery
The moment both ends touch the battery's terminals, current flows in the coil and the nail picks up paper clips and pins: a magnetic field from an electric source. Disconnect one end and the field dies at once. Jamaica's learners create the field, test it, and feel the electromagnet's defining truth in their hands — this magnet obeys the switch. (Work briefly and disconnect between tests: a connected coil drains and warms the battery.)

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: an electromagnet is made from three things — an AA 1.5 V battery, a 4-inch wire nail and 5 m of copper wire wound around it — and connecting the battery creates a magnetic field from an electric source. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
