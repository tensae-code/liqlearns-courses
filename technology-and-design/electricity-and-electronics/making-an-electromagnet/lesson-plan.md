# Lesson plan — Making an electromagnet

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** Making an electromagnet
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an electromagnet is made from three things — an AA 1.5 V battery, a 4-inch wire nail and 5 m of copper wire wound around it — and connecting the battery creates a magnetic field from an electric source.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Jamaica's *National Standards Curriculum Guide — Grades 4–6 — Resource & Technology* (Ministry of Education, Youth and Information, Kingston, Jamaica, © 2018), a curriculum guide with full unit teaching content, read COMPLETE (see lesson/sources.md). ## Sub lessons
1. **The components** — List the build before touching it: battery for the current, nail for the core, copper wire for the coil — nothing else is needed.
2. **Winding the coil on the core** — The wire is wound tightly and evenly around the nail, turn against turn, leaving ends free to reach the battery.
3. **Creating the field from the battery** — Connect the ends to the battery and the nail becomes a magnet — a magnetic field created from a battery source, present only while the circuit is complete.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Electricity and Electronics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the components of Jamaica's electromagnet with their specifications.
2. Wind a tight, even coil on a nail core with free, stripped ends.
3. Connect the battery to create the field, test it, and explain why disconnecting kills it.
## What you need
An AA 1.5 V battery, a 4-inch wire nail, 5 m of copper (magnet) wire, paper clips for testing (adult supervision).
