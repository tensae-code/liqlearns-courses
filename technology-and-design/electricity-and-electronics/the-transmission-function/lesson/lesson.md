# The transmission function

## Do it first
Picture a bicycle: your legs turn the pedals, the back wheel turns the bike forward — but your feet never touch the back wheel. The chain transmits the movement. A pulley and belt do the same job in a machine room.

## Sub lesson 1 — Transmitting movement — pulley-belt and other mechanisms
Morocco's lead mechanism is pulley-belt: a driving pulley, a driven pulley, one belt. Equal pulleys pass the speed unchanged; a small driver turning a large driven pulley slows the output and strengthens its turning effect (torque); crossed belts reverse the direction, open belts keep it. The belt can also slip under overload — a weakness that doubles as protection.

## Sub lesson 2 — With and without an intermediate organ
Reading a transmission means naming: the driving organ, the driven organ, the intermediate organ if any, and the transformation (same speed? slower and stronger? reversed?). That four-part reading is the same function reading used across Morocco's Module 2 — transmission is the energy chain's 'transmit' link in mechanical form.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the transmission function carries movement from where it is made to where it is used: pulley-and-belt and the other mechanisms transmit rotation — directly, or through an intermediate organ that can change its speed and direction. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
