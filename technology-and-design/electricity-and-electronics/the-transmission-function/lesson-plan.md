# Lesson plan — The transmission function

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** The transmission function
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Morocco expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the transmission function carries movement from where it is made to where it is used: pulley-and-belt and the other mechanisms transmit rotation — directly, or through an intermediate organ that can change its speed and direction.

## Sub lessons
1. **Transmitting movement — pulley-belt and other mechanisms** — Transmission answers a distance problem: the motor is here, the work is there. Pulley-and-belt carries rotation between two shafts with a flexible belt; gears mesh directly; chains run on sprockets. Each mechanism transmits the movement and, by the sizes it uses, can change its speed and sometimes its direction.
2. **With and without an intermediate organ** — Transmission may be direct — the two organs touch, like meshing gears — or through an intermediate organ, like the belt or chain that links pulleys and sprockets from a distance. The intermediate organ is chosen when shafts are far apart, when slip-protection is wanted, or when the layout demands it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name driving, driven and intermediate organs in a pulley-belt drive.
2. Say how pulley sizes change output speed and turning effect.
3. Tell direct transmission from transmission through an intermediate organ.
## What you need
A bicycle to observe, or pictures of pulley-belt, gear and chain drives.
