# Making a working flashlight

## Do it first
Lay out the parts of a flashlight in build order on the table (or on paper): housing material, batteries, wire, bulb, shade material, switch. Number them in the order you would fit them, and say why that order works.

## Sub lesson 1 — The housing and the connections
Jamaica's flashlight is a real build. The housing is constructed from cardboard, shaped to hold the batteries and bulb. Components are connected with adhesive strip where the guide specifies it. Making is planned from the schematic and cross-section the learners have read: the housing is not a box first and a circuit later — the two are designed together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wires to the battery and the bulb
The connections carry the lesson's precision. Wires are cut to the length the layout needs — too short will not reach, too long tangles and shorts. Each end is stripped of its insulation for 2.5 cm so the bare copper can make a true contact. One connection goes to the battery base; the bulb is contacted at two points — its screw-thread side and its foot contact at the base — because the current must enter the bulb one way and leave the other to complete the loop.

## Sub lesson 3 — The shade, and accurate assembly
A bare bulb lights in all directions; the flashlight's conical shade gathers the light and aims it. Making and attaching the shade true — centred on the bulb, firmly fixed — is the build's last shaping job. Jamaica stresses assembling accurately throughout: parts seated where the cross-section shows them, contacts firm, the switch working — because in a circuit, a small inaccuracy is the difference between light and no light.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a working flashlight is built in order: cardboard housing first, then batteries and wires fitted and connected to the bulb's two contacts, then the conical shade — assembled accurately so the circuit is complete. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
