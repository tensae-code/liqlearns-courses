# Lesson plan — Making a working flashlight

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** Making a working flashlight
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a working flashlight is built in order: cardboard housing first, then batteries and wires fitted and connected to the bulb's two contacts, then the conical shade — assembled accurately so the circuit is complete.

## Sub lessons
1. **The housing and the connections** — The cardboard housing holds everything; components are connected with adhesive strip, and the order of fitting decides whether the circuit can be completed.
2. **Wires to the battery and the bulb** — Copper wire is cut to length, stripped 2.5 cm at the end, and connected to the battery base and to the bulb's screw-thread and foot contacts.
3. **The shade, and accurate assembly** — The conical shade is made and attached to throw the light forward, and the whole device is assembled accurately.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Electricity and Electronics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Construct a cardboard housing and fit the components in build order.
2. Cut copper wire to length, strip 2.5 cm, and connect it to the battery base and the bulb's two contacts.
3. Make and attach a conical shade and assemble the flashlight accurately.
## What you need
Cardboard, batteries, copper wire, a small bulb, adhesive strip/tape, scissors and a ruler (adult supervision for stripping and cutting).
