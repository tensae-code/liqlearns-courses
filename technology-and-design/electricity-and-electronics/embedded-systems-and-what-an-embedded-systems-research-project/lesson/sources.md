| embedded systems and what an embedded-systems research project involves: the concept (systems integrating hardware and software, designed for a dedicated computing and control purpose; they are embedded inside a larger system to perform one defined function with little or no human intervention — for example the controller board at the centre of a self-propelled robot, controlling obstacle avoidance from the ultrasonic and infrared sensor data at its front), the three main directions for implementing embedded systems today (microcontroller-based — the most popular direction, easy to complete and highly flexible with low initial investment, but usually higher power consumption and slower response than the other research directions; ASIC-based (Application Specific Integrated Circuit) — every function designed and realised entirely in hardware, giving very small size, extremely fast response and very low energy consumption, mass-producible, but with high initial investment and functions that cannot be changed once fabricated; FPGA-based (Field Programmable Gate Array) — the product's functions configured entirely on hardware like an ASIC, but after completion new features can still be updated by programming in a hardware description language (HDL)), the characteristics of embedded-systems research projects (high complexity and specialisation, meeting strict constraints on real-time processing, high reliability and low price; a wide, interdisciplinary research scope — beyond electronics and programming the team needs knowledge and methods in the product's application field; and tight hardware–software integration — the embedded system is one unified whole, so projects often require hardware–software integration right from the design stage through implementation and testing), and the related contents of a specific project (hardware: the push button's structure and working mechanism and signal form, the debouncing circuit for the button, and the hardware development platform — microcontroller, FPGA or ASIC; software: the programming technique — bare-metal embedded programming or application programming — and the toolchain of low-level libraries, debugging/compiling tools and the integrated development environment (IDE) suited to the chosen hardware platform; integration and testing: hardware–software partitioning — deciding whether bounce-noise processing is done in hardware or software — and the test scenarios and procedures after integration: simulation, prototyping, measurement) (original string `embedded systems and what an embedded-systems research project involves`) | Vietnam, *Chuyên đề học tập Công nghệ 12 — Công nghệ điện – điện tử*, printed p. 20 |
