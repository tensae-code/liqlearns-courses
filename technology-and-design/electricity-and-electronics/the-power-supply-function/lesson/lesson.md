# The power-supply function

## Do it first
Look at three objects: a torch, a bicycle, a kettle. What energy goes into each — chemical, muscular, electrical? And what comes out — light, movement, heat? You have just filled a power-supply correspondence table.

## Sub lesson 1 — Forms of energy at input and output
Reading the supply function means naming both ends. Input: what form enters (a torch: chemical energy stored in cells). Output: what form the object's action takes (light, plus a little heat). Morocco teaches this with an energy-form correspondence table: object by object, input form matched to output form, so the supply is chosen to fit the conversion the object performs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Matching supply to object
This is the first function of the energy chain the Grade 9 course names 'feed' (alimenter): requirements of an electrical supply — nature of current, voltage, power — are the detailed form of the same matching rule. Supply first, because every later function depends on the energy this one delivers.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the power-supply function (fonction alimenter) feeds a technical object with energy: name the form of energy at the input, the form at the output, and match them — an object can only use energy in the form its supply delivers. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
