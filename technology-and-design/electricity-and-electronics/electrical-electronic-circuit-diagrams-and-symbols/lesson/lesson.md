# Electrical/electronic circuit diagrams and symbols

## Do it first
Draw and label all eight symbols from memory: cell, battery, lamp, switch, resistor, LED, buzzer, solar cell. Check against the workbook and fix any that are wrong.

## Sub lesson 1 — The standard symbols
Every component has one agreed symbol, so any circuit drawing can be read by anyone: the cell (one short and one long line), the battery (cells together), the lamp, the switch, the resistor, the LED, the buzzer and the solar cell. Malta's workbook has you draw and name each symbol until the set is known by heart — a symbol wrongly drawn is a part wrongly read.

## Sub lesson 2 — Schematic diagram and layout diagram
The same circuit can be drawn two ways. A schematic (circuit) diagram uses the symbols and shows how the circuit is connected — what joins what, in what order — arranged neatly for reading how it works. A layout diagram shows the circuit as it physically lies: the real positions of the parts and wires on the board or in the product. One tells you the logic; the other tells you the place.

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing a schematic from a layout
The workbook's key skill is converting: given a layout, draw the schematic. Work part by part: find each real component, replace it with its symbol, and keep the connections in the same order, straightened into the schematic's neat lines. Check at the end that every part in the layout appears once in the schematic and every connection matches.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: circuits are drawn with standard symbols — cell, battery, lamp, switch, resistor, LED, buzzer, solar cell — and a schematic diagram shows how the circuit works, while a layout diagram shows where the parts physically sit. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
