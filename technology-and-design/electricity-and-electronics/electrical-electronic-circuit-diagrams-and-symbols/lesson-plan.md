# Lesson plan — Electrical/electronic circuit diagrams and symbols

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** Electrical/electronic circuit diagrams and symbols
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malta expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** circuits are drawn with standard symbols — cell, battery, lamp, switch, resistor, LED, buzzer, solar cell — and a schematic diagram shows how the circuit works, while a layout diagram shows where the parts physically sit.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student workbook — Malta's *Design and Technology — Graphical Communication Component — Workbook 2 ("Communicate Graphically")*, Directorate for Quality and Standards in Education, Malta, 2015 (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student workbook, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The standard symbols** — Every component has one agreed symbol, so any circuit drawing can be read by anyone: the cell (one short and one long line), the battery (cells together), the lamp, the switch, the resistor, the LED, the buzzer and the solar cell.
2. **Schematic diagram and layout diagram** — The same circuit can be drawn two ways.
3. **Drawing a schematic from a layout** — The workbook's key skill is converting: given a layout, draw the schematic.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Electricity and Electronics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw and name the symbols for cell, battery, lamp, switch, resistor, LED, buzzer and solar cell.
2. Say how a schematic diagram differs from a layout diagram.
3. Draw a schematic from a given layout, with the correct symbols.
## What you need
A pencil, a ruler and plain paper.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of the same simple circuit drawn twice — once as a layout with parts in their real places, once as a neat schematic with symbols — the student matches each real part to its symbol.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a GIF of a layout diagram's parts lifting off one by one and landing as symbols in a schematic — the student names each symbol as it lands.

5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first task and do it again slowly, one step at a time, saying each step aloud. Drawing skills grow by doing; a shaky first attempt is the normal start, not a failure.
