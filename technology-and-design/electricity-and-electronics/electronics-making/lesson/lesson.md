# Electronics making

## Do it first
Look at any broken remote or old phone board: green board, copper tracks, small striped cylinders. Those stripes are a code you can read, and the flat side of an LED's rim is a sign you must obey. Making starts with reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Producing a printed circuit
Morocco's Module 3 teaches making as a chain: diagram → layout → board production → component placing → soldering → test. Each step is checked against the diagram, because a track error copied onto a board is copied onto every board made from it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Resistor colour codes and LED polarity
Reading components before placing them is the maker's discipline: decode each resistor's bands and check against the parts list; identify each LED's legs before soldering, because desoldering a reversed LED risks the board. Morocco pairs the two readings in one lesson because both are the same habit — the component tells you what it is, if you read it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: electronics making produces a printed circuit that works: tracks carry the connections on a board, components are read before they are placed — resistor values by their colour codes, LEDs by their polarity. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
