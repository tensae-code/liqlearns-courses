# The simple electric circuit

## Do it first
Take a battery, a small bulb and two wires (or draw them). Arrange them so the bulb would light, then remove one wire and say what changes. The difference between the two arrangements is the whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where electrical energy works
Jamaica's Grade 5 project opens by surveying energy sources and the electrical energy in familiar devices: the fan that cools, the flashlight that lights, the calculator and the radio. Different devices, one secret: each contains a circuit, a path built for electric current. Discovering current flow — that electricity must travel in a complete loop — is the project's first discovery.

## Sub lesson 2 — The components of a circuit
Name the parts and their jobs. The battery (the source) pushes the current. Conductors — wires — carry it from part to part. The bulb is the load: it turns electrical energy into light (and a little heat). The switch is the gate: closed, it completes the loop; open, it breaks it. Jamaica teaches the components together with how the circuit operates, explained with a diagram — the learner draws the loop they are describing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Types of circuits, and the complete loop
A circuit with one path is the simple series loop of the flashlight. Other arrangements give current more than one path. Jamaica introduces the types of circuits at this level through the flashlight's own question — what happens when one part fails or the switch opens? The answer teaches the rule: current flows only around a complete circuit; any break — an open switch, a loose wire, a dead battery — stops everything on that path.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a simple circuit is a complete loop — battery, conductors, bulb and switch — around which current flows; break the loop anywhere and the flow stops. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
