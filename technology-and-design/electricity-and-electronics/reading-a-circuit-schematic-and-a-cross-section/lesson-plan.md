# Lesson plan — Reading a circuit schematic and a cross-section

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** Reading a circuit schematic and a cross-section
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Jamaica expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a schematic diagram shows how a circuit's components are connected, using symbols; a cross-sectional view shows the inside of a thing as if cut through — together they tell the maker how the flashlight works before it is built.

## Sub lessons
1. **Reading a schematic** — A schematic is the circuit drawn in symbols: follow the lines to follow the current, from the source, through each component, and back.
2. **Reading a cross-section** — A cross-section is a view of the inside: imagine the object cut through, and draw what the cut reveals.
3. **Using the two views together** — Schematic for the connections, cross-section for the arrangement — a maker who can read both can build, check and fault-find.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read a simple circuit schematic, tracing the current path through its symbols.
2. Read a cross-sectional view and describe the arrangement inside an object.
3. Say which view answers a connections question and which answers an arrangement question.
## What you need
The flashlight schematic and cross-section (or a learner-drawn pair), paper and a pencil.
