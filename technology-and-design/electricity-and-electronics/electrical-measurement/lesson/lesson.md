# Electrical measurement

## Do it first
Look at any battery or charger label at home. Find a number with V and, if you can, one with A or W. Write what each number is attached to. You are reading measurements before taking them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Voltage and current — tension and intensity
Haiti's sequence measures the circuit model it builds, and the measurement starts with the distinction: tension (voltage) is the electrical push between two points, measured in volts (V); intensity (current) is the flow through a point, measured in amperes (A). Power — what a receiver consumes — is counted in watts (W). Confusing the two quantities makes every later reading meaningless, so the names, units and symbols are learned together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Voltmeter and ammeter on the model circuit
The voltmeter measures the tension at the generator and at the receivers of the low-voltage circuit maquette; the ammeter measures the intensity flowing in the circuit. Learners take real readings from their own built model — measurement is done on a working circuit, not described from a picture — and record each reading with its unit.

## Sub lesson 3 — Adding receivers — watching consumption change
The sequence's experiment adds receivers to the circuit and measures again: with more lamps or loads connected, the circuit's consumption rises, and the instruments show it. This is the measured foundation for sizing: an installation must supply what its receivers together draw, which is why the sizing lesson that follows begins with a list of loads.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: voltage (tension) is measured in volts with a voltmeter and current (intensity) in amperes with an ammeter — and adding receivers to a circuit raises what the measurements show it consumes, counted in watts. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
