# The detection function

## Do it first
Stand at a doorway and call out 'someone there' or 'no one there' each time the door area changes. You are a detector giving TOR information — two states only. Morocco's detectors do this for robot arms, fire alarms and filling tanks.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sensors and what they detect
Morocco's examples span three worlds: a robot arm whose position is detected so its movement can be controlled; a gas/smoke sensor standing guard in a fire-alarm system; level detection watching a tank in an automatic filling installation. In each case the sensor's job is the same: watch one fact, report it faithfully, all the time.

## Sub lesson 2 — All-or-nothing (TOR) logic information
A TOR detector does not say how much — it says whether. The fire alarm's sensor says 'smoke' or 'no smoke'; the level detector says 'reached' or 'not reached'; the robot arm's detector says 'in position' or 'not in position'. Systems combine several TOR signals with logic to decide their next action.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the detection function senses the world for a system: a sensor watches a position, a gas, a level — and delivers all-or-nothing (TOR) logic information: the thing is there, or it is not. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
