# Lesson plan — The detection function

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** The detection function
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Morocco expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the detection function senses the world for a system: a sensor watches a position, a gas, a level — and delivers all-or-nothing (TOR) logic information: the thing is there, or it is not.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sensors and what they detect** — A sensor converts a physical fact — position, presence, gas, smoke, liquid level — into an electrical signal the system can use. Detection is the first link of the information chain: nothing can be processed or signalled until something has been detected.
2. **All-or-nothing (TOR) logic information** — The information a detector delivers here is TOR — tout ou rien, all-or-nothing: two states only, 1 or 0, present or absent, level reached or not reached. TOR information is simple and robust, and control built on it switches cleanly: fill while 'not full', stop at 'full'.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a sensor does and name the three Moroccan detection examples.
2. Explain TOR information and give two TOR statements.
3. Say where detection sits in the information chain.
## What you need
Pictures of a robot arm, a fire alarm and a filling tank; any simple switch or float to handle.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the opening doing task in lesson/lesson.md.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of three detectors at work — a robot arm's position sensor, a smoke sensor over a room, a level float in a tank — each with a 0/1 lamp beside it — the student names the fact each sensor watches and its two states.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a GIF of a tank filling, its level rising to a float, the float flipping the signal from 0 to 1 and the inflow stopping — the student calls out the state change as it happens.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the TOR rule aloud — two states only: present or absent, reached or not reached — the student repeats it.
