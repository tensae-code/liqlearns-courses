# Electrical circuits — parts and types

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Parts of a circuit; series and parallel
Every circuit has its parts — energy source, conductors, a control such as a switch, and the load that does the work. Joined in series, the parts share one path; in parallel, each load has its own. Cuba's books teach both arrangements with the norm symbols that draw them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Circuits at work
The Grade 8 course lands the circuit in real devices: how the induction cooker, the microwave oven, energy-saving bulbs and LED lamps work, and the installing of an electric bell. The 1989 book adds the quantities — intensity, resistance, voltage, power — and the transformer and fluorescent lamp.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a circuit is a closed path with parts that have jobs: source, conductors, control and load — and circuits join in series or in parallel, drawn with norm symbols, so that bells ring, lamps light and appliances work as drawn. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
