## Sub lessons
1. **Timers and delayed contacts** — A timer measures a set duration and acts when it expires: a contact that closes late (delayed make) or opens late (delayed break). Timing turns sequences into timed sequences — a barrier that stays open long enough for the car to pass, a lamp that stays on while you cross the yard.
2. **Static (NE 555) vs electromechanical timing** — Electromechanical timers count with a small motor or a thermal/mechanical delay — simple, visible, limited. Static timing counts electronically: the NE 555 integrated circuit charges a capacitor through a resistor, and the delay is set by those two values — no moving parts, precise, adjustable.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a delayed contact does.
2. Explain how the NE 555 sets its delay (R and C).
3. Compare static and electromechanical timing and place each.
## What you need
A stopwatch or phone timer; a diagram of a barrier cycle.
