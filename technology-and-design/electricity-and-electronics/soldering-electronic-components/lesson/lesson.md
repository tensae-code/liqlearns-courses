# Soldering electronic components

## Do it first
Draw a stripboard assembly from the workbook: component side and soldering side, copper strips, one component, one solder joint. Label every part.

## Sub lesson 1 — The stripboard: two sides, two jobs
Stripboard is a board drilled with holes in a grid, with copper strips running along one side. Components stand on the plain component side, their legs poking through the holes; the soldering happens on the other side, where the legs meet the copper strips. The strips carry the connections along the board, so the layout of parts on top and the strips below together make the circuit. Malta's workbook has you label the whole assembly until both sides and their jobs are clear.

## Sub lesson 2 — Solder, the joint and the iron
Solder is a metal that melts at a heat the soldering iron can give, then sets hard as it cools, joining a component's leg to the copper strip — that join is the solder joint, both a mechanical hold and an electrical connection. The iron has three parts to know: the tip (the hot end that melts the solder), the safe-handling part (the handle you hold), and the heating-element part (which makes the heat), with a mains power cord feeding it. The workbook's labelled 2-D sketch of the iron fixes those parts in memory.

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing the stripboard layout
Before soldering, the circuit is drawn as a stripboard layout: the board's strips and holes, each component in its place, each joint marked. Drawing it first means the hot work is only copying a checked plan. A good joint is quick, neat and shiny; a slow, messy joint can damage the board or fail to connect — which is why the drawing and the labelling come before the iron is ever switched on.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: stripboard circuits are built on the component side and soldered on the copper-strip side: the iron melts solder to make each joint, and the iron itself is known by its parts — tip, safe-handling part and heating-element part. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
