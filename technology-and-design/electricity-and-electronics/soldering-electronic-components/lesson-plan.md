# Lesson plan — Soldering electronic components

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** Soldering electronic components
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Malta expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** stripboard circuits are built on the component side and soldered on the copper-strip side: the iron melts solder to make each joint, and the iron itself is known by its parts — tip, safe-handling part and heating-element part.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a student workbook — Malta's *Design and Technology — Graphical Communication Component — Workbook 2 ("Communicate Graphically")*, Directorate for Quality and Standards in Education, Malta, 2015 (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student workbook, not evidence-only).

Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The stripboard: two sides, two jobs** — Stripboard is a board drilled with holes in a grid, with copper strips running along one side.
2. **Solder, the joint and the iron** — Solder is a metal that melts at a heat the soldering iron can give, then sets hard as it cools, joining a component's leg to the copper strip — that join is the solder joint, both a mechanical hold and an electrical connection.
3. **Drawing the stripboard layout** — Before soldering, the circuit is drawn as a stripboard layout: the board's strips and holes, each component in its place, each joint marked.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Electricity and Electronics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Label the component side and soldering side of stripboard and say what the copper strips do.
2. Name the parts of a soldering iron: tip, safe-handling part, heating-element part, mains cord.
3. Draw a stripboard circuit layout showing parts and joints.
## What you need
For drawing work: a pencil and paper. (Real soldering only with a teacher, an iron stand and eye protection.)
