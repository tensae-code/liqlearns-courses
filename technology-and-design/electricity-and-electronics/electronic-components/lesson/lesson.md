# Electronic components

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — LED circuits and the multimeter
A light-emitting diode (LED) lights when current passes in the right direction — and burns out if the current is too strong, so a resistor is placed in series to limit it. Iran's Grade 8 electronics module builds LED lamps, then measures the working circuit with a multimeter: voltage and resistance are measured in parallel — the meter connected across the part — a connection rule the book fixes by practice.

## Sub lesson 2 — The transistor
The transistor is a three-legged component: base, emitter and collector, drawn in circuits by its own symbol. A small current at the base controls a larger current between collector and emitter, so the transistor works as a switch and an amplifier — the component that makes electronic control possible. Knowing its three legs and its symbol is the entry to every later circuit in the module.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: electronic components each do one job in a circuit — the LED turns current into light under a resistor's protection, the meter proves what the circuit is doing, and the transistor switches and amplifies. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
