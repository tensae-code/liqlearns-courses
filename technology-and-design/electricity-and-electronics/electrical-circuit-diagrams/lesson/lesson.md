# Electrical circuit diagrams

## Do it first
Draw your home's lighting as you imagine it: source, wires, switches, lamps — in any symbols you invent. Then look at what a stranger could not understand in your drawing. That gap is why standard schemas exist.

## Sub lesson 1 — From the real installation to its schema
An electrical circuit diagram (schema) shows an installation's components and connections in standard symbols, so the whole installation can be read, checked and built from the drawing. Haiti's photovoltaic sequence makes learners draw the schema of their installation: panel, regulator, battery, inverter and loads, connected as they will really be connected.

## Sub lesson 2 — Comparing installations before drawing
The schema is not the first step. The sequence first compares existing photovoltaic installations — how others arranged the same components — and the group chooses its solution against its specification. Only then is the schema drawn. A diagram drawn before the comparison freezes the first idea; drawn after, it records the best idea the group could justify.

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing the schema of the chosen solution
Drawing the schema means placing each component's symbol and showing every connection between them, in the order energy will flow. The finished schema is checked two ways: against the chosen solution (does the drawing match the decision?) and against the components list (is everything the installation needs present and connected?). The schema then guides the building of the circuit model.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: an installation's schema is its circuit drawn in symbols — drawn only after existing installations are compared and a solution is chosen, so the diagram records a decision, not a guess. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
