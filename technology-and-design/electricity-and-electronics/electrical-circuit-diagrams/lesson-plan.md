# Lesson plan — Electrical circuit diagrams

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Electricity and Electronics · **Lesson:** Electrical circuit diagrams
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Electronics (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Haiti expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an installation's schema is its circuit drawn in symbols — drawn only after existing installations are compared and a solution is chosen, so the diagram records a decision, not a guess.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — Haiti's ITAP Projet Nectar pedagogical sequences (MENFP) are the books in hand for this topic (see lesson/sources.md). Teacher guides are evidence, counted nowhere in the acceptance count.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **From the real installation to its schema** — The diagram is the installation, drawn in the language of symbols.
2. **Comparing installations before drawing** — Existing solutions are compared so the schema draws a chosen design.
3. **Drawing the schema of the chosen solution** — Symbols placed, connections shown, the drawing checked against the plan.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Electricity and Electronics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an installation schema shows.
2. Explain why comparison comes before drawing.
3. Draw and check a simple installation schema.
## What you need
Component symbols for panel, regulator, battery, inverter and loads; drawing materials; pictures of two existing PV installations to compare.
