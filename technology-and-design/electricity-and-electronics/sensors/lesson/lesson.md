# Sensors

## Do it first
Close your eyes and put a hand near (not on) a warm cup: you sense heat without touching. A thermistor does the same job for a weather station — its resistance changes with temperature, and the circuit reads the change. Every sensor is one measured thing plus one principle.

## Sub lesson 1 — The acquire function — sensor types and what each measures
The garage-door case in the same lesson follows information from the sensors: door position, obstacle presence and the user's command are acquired first, and every later decision in the system reads from those acquisitions. A system knows only what its sensors tell it — a missing or mis-placed sensor is a blind spot no processing can repair.

## Sub lesson 2 — What each sensor measures and its principle
Reading a sensor by its principle predicts its behaviour: a thermistor must touch or approach what it measures; a pressostat answers at a set pressure, not continuously; a position contact answers only at its point. Choose the sensor by the quantity, then check its principle fits the place and the kind of information (analogue or logic) the chain needs.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a sensor is the acquire function made hardware: each sensor type measures one thing — temperature, pressure, light, presence — by a physical principle that turns the measured fact into an electrical signal. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
