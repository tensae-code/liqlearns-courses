# Making a rectangle and a square out of paper

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Making the rectangle
Straight after the toy bird, the book makes shapes: first the rectangle, cut and formed out of paper. A shape you have made is a shape you know — its sides and corners are in your fingers, not only on the page.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making the square
Then the square: the rectangle's equal-sided brother. Making both in one lesson teaches the difference by hand — the square is the rectangle whose sides agree.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a rectangle and a square are not just drawn — they are made: Rwanda's Primary 2 learners produce the two shapes out of paper with their own hands. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
