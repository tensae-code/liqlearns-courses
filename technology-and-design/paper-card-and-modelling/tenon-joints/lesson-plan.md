# Lesson plan — Tenon joints

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Paper, Card and Modelling · **Lesson:** Tenon joints
**Place in the curriculum:** Paper, Card and Modelling (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Moldova expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a tenon joint (îmbinarea prin cepuri) joins wood with wood: a tenon cut on one piece fits a matching mortise in the other — single or multiple — marked out with set square and marking gauge, cut with fine-tooth saw, chisels and mallet.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Moldova's *Educație tehnologică* (Technological Education) textbooks — see lesson/sources.md. Moldova counts in the acceptance count (student books, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Tenon and mortise — single and multiple** — Grade 6 joining rises to the tenon joint (îmbinarea prin cepuri): one piece ends in a tenon — a shaped tongue — and the other is cut with a matching mortise, the slot that receives it.
2. **Marking out and cutting** — The joint is won at the marking: tenon and mortise dimensions are marked out with set square and marking gauge, so the two halves agree before cutting.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Paper, Card and Modelling. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell single from multiple tenon joints.
2. Mark out and cut a tenon and its matching mortise.
3. Explain how Moldova's book teaches it.
## What you need
Practice wood, set square, marking gauge, fine-tooth saw, flat chisels and a wooden mallet.
