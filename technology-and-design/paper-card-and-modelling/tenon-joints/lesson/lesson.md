# Tenon joints

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Moldova's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tenon and mortise — single and multiple
Grade 6 joining rises to the tenon joint (îmbinarea prin cepuri): one piece ends in a tenon — a shaped tongue — and the other is cut with a matching mortise, the slot that receives it. The joint comes in two forms: single (simple), one tenon; and multiple (compound), several tenons side by side.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marking out and cutting
The joint is won at the marking: tenon and mortise dimensions are marked out with set square and marking gauge, so the two halves agree before cutting. The cutting tools are the fine-tooth saw, the planes, the flat chisels and the wooden mallet.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a tenon joint (îmbinarea prin cepuri) joins wood with wood: a tenon cut on one piece fits a matching mortise in the other — single or multiple — marked out with set square and marking gauge, cut with fine-tooth saw, chisels and mallet. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
