# Clay modelling

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Moldova's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Materials, tools and the norms for clay work
Grade 4 clay work opens with its kit and its rules: the modelling materials and tools, and the personal-hygiene and work-protection norms for clay work. Clay is a hand material — the norms keep hands, tools and workplace clean and safe through every modelling lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hand-modelling — whistle, plate on a mould, decor
The techniques are two: free modelling by hand, carried out on a clay whistle toy; and modelling on a mould (calapod), carried out on a decorative plate. The plate closes with traditional ceramic decor and chromatics — the inherited patterns and colours of Moldovan ceramics.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: clay modelling works by hand under its own norms: know the materials and tools, keep the hygiene and protection rules — then model freely, or on a mould (calapod), with traditional decor and chromatics. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
