# Modelling in plasticine

## Do it first
Take a lump of plasticine (or soft clay or dough). Try to make a small bowl just by pressing it. Then learn the four named ways this book models: shaping, decorating, pulling and joining.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shaping a bowl — the first modelling make
The first make is a bowl: a ball of plasticine is pressed and hollowed with the thumbs until its walls stand even all round. The bowl teaches the basic feel of the material — warm it in the hands, press evenly, and check the shape from every side.

## Sub lesson 2 — Applied decoration — a plasticine rose
Applied decoration (налепные украшения) means ornament stuck onto a surface: thin plasticine pieces are shaped separately — here a rose — and pressed onto the work to decorate it. The decoration is modelled first, then applied, so the base stays clean.

## Sub lesson 3 — The pulling method — a turtle
In the pulling method the figure is drawn out of one piece: parts of the turtle — head, legs, tail — are pulled and stretched from the main lump instead of being stuck on. Nothing is added; the whole animal comes out of the single piece.

## Sub lesson 4 — The constructive method — a cat
In the constructive method the figure is built by joining separately modelled parts: body, head, legs, ears and tail are each shaped on their own, then joined firmly into one cat. Joins are pressed and smoothed so the figure holds together.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: plasticine can be shaped into a form, decorated with applied ornament, pulled out of one piece, or built by joining separate parts — four methods, four different makes. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
