# Marionette mechanism

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Kazakhstan's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Marionette mechanism
Kazakhstan's books teach it this way (the student book, printed pp. 80–97): Puppets moved by strings or a rod; a flat camel marionette made; a volumetric horse marionette from waste (cups for head/body, paper-spring legs, strings to a control stick); the orteke dancing-goat puppet worked by the dombra player's fingers (Lessons 19–20). The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marionette mechanism — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: puppets moved by strings or a rod; a flat camel marionette made; a volumetric horse marionette from waste (cups for head/body, paper-spring legs, strings to a control stick); the orteke dancing-goat puppet worked by the dombra player's fingers (Lessons 19–20). Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what the Kazakh book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Marionette mechanism — puppets moved by strings or a rod; a flat camel marionette made; a volumetric horse marionette from waste (cups for head/body, paper-spring legs, strings to a control stick); the orteke dancing-goat puppet worked by the dombra player's fingers (Lessons 19–20). Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
