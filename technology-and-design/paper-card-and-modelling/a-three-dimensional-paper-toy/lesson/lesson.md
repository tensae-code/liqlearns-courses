# A three-dimensional paper toy

## Do it first
Take a sheet of paper and try to make it stand up by itself. Folding and joining edges is the trick. Then learn how this book builds a whole standing hare from paper.

## Sub lesson 1 — Flat paper becomes a solid toy
A three-dimensional (volumetric) toy does not lie flat like a picture: it stands, with a front, sides and a back. The paper is folded and joined along its edges so the flat sheet takes a solid form that keeps its shape.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making a hare
The hare is built step by step: the body takes its solid form first, then head, ears and details are added and fixed. The finished hare stands on its own — the test that the construction is right.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a three-dimensional paper toy is folded and joined until flat paper holds a solid form that stands on its own. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
