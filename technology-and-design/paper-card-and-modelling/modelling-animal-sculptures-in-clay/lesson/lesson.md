# Modelling animal sculptures in clay

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Preparing the clay
The modelling material is a mixture: clay, fine sand and water. Preparing it is the first skill — the proportions decide whether the sculpture stands or slumps, so the mix is part of the make, not a detail before it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Modelling the goat and the bird
Two sculptures are modelled: a goat, then a bird. Modelling means building the animal's form by hand — body, legs, head — until the clay reads as the animal. Two different animals teach that the method is one, the forms are many.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: clay, fine sand and water, worked by hand, become animals: Rwanda's Primary 2 learners model a goat and a bird as sculptures in clay. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
