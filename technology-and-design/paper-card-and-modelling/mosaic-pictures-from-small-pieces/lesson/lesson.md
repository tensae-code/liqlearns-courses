# Mosaic — pictures from small pieces

## Do it first
Draw a simple shape — a leaf or a berry. Imagine covering it completely with tiny pieces, no gaps. Then learn which small materials this book uses, and why each one fits its picture.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mosaic from paper pieces and natural materials
The first mosaics cover the picture with small pieces of coloured paper — a poplar tree — and then with natural materials: seeds and rice laid as a raised mosaic make a swallow. The rule is the same: small pieces, placed close, following the drawing underneath.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mosaic from pencil shavings, eggshell and crumpled napkin
Workshop leftovers become mosaic materials: pencil shavings make a hedgehog's prickles, crushed eggshell makes a raised cockerel, and balls of crumpled paper napkin make a lamb's wool. Each material is chosen because its texture matches the thing pictured.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mosaic from sponge
Pieces of sponge, cut small, cover the picture with a soft, dotted texture — here raspberries. Sponge pieces are pressed into place one by one until the berries stand full and round.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a mosaic covers a drawing with many small pieces placed close together, and the material is chosen for its texture — shavings for prickles, napkin balls for wool, sponge for soft berries. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
