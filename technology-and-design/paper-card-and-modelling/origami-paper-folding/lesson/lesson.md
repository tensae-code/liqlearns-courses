# Origami — paper folding

## Do it first
Take a square of paper. Fold one corner to the opposite corner and press the fold flat with a fingernail. That sharp fold is the whole secret of origami — now learn to build figures from folds alone.

## Sub lesson 1 — Folding figures from a square sheet — an owl
Origami starts from a square of paper. Fold follows fold in a fixed order — corners to the centre, edges to the middle line — and the flat square becomes an owl. Each fold must be sharp and exact, or the later folds will not meet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Folding a figure of a person
The same folding craft makes a figure of a person: head, body and limbs all come from the folded sheet. The learner follows the fold order and checks the figure against the pictured steps as it grows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: origami makes a figure from one square sheet by folding in a set order — no cutting and no glue — and every fold must be sharp and exact. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
