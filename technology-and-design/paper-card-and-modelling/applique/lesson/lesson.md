# Appliqué

## Do it first
Tear a scrap of paper into small pieces with your fingers — no scissors. Try to make a simple shape from the torn pieces. Then learn the three kinds of appliqué in this book.

## Sub lesson 1 — Torn-paper appliqué — a mushroom on a meadow
Torn-paper appliqué uses pieces torn by hand, not cut: the torn edge is soft and uneven, which suits natural shapes. Small torn pieces are glued onto the background to build a mushroom standing on a meadow.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fabric appliqué — a duck
Fabric appliqué cuts the picture's shapes from cloth instead of paper. The duck's body, wing and beak are cut from fabric pieces and stuck onto the background, so the picture gains the cloth's colour and texture.

## Sub lesson 3 — Three-dimensional appliqué — a cat and a fish
Three-dimensional appliqué is built up in relief: pieces are layered or raised so the picture stands out from the background instead of lying flat. The cat and the fish rise off the page, and shadows show their height.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: appliqué sticks pieces onto a background to make a picture — torn paper for soft edges, fabric for cloth texture, and raised pieces when the picture must stand out. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
