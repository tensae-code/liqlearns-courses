# Constructing from cardboard boxes

## Do it first
Look at an empty cardboard box. Turn it round. What could its shape become — a house, an animal, a vehicle? Then learn how this book constructs a wolf's head from boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Boxes as building material
A cardboard box already has a solid shape — walls, corners and a hollow inside. The constructor looks at a box and asks what it could become: its shape suggests the part it can play in the build, and boxes of different sizes combine into bigger constructions.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building a wolf's head
Boxes and cardboard are joined and shaped into a wolf's head: the main box gives the head's form, added cardboard shapes the muzzle, ears and teeth. The learner builds, checks the figure from the front and sides, and fixes what does not read as a wolf.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: constructing from boxes starts from the shape the box already has, then joins and adds cardboard until the figure reads clearly. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
