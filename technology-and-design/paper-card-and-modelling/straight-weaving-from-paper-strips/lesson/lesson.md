# Straight weaving from paper strips

## Do it first
Take two strips of paper. Lay one across the other — over, then imagine a third going under. That crossing, repeated, is weaving. Now learn to weave a whole panel.

## Sub lesson 1 — Plain weaving — over and under
Straight (plain) weaving lays one set of paper strips side by side, then works a strip across them: over one, under the next, over, under, to the end of the row. The next row starts the opposite way, so the strips lock each other in a chequered pattern.

## Sub lesson 2 — Weaving a fence
The woven pattern becomes a fence: rows of interlaced strips stand as the fence's woven panel. The learner weaves steadily, keeping strips straight and close, until the panel holds together on its own.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: weaving interlaces strips over-and-under, each row starting the opposite way to the row before, so the strips lock into one panel. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
