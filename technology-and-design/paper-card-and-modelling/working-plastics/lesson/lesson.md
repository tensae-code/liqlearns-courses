# Working plastics

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Mongolia's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Working plastics
Mongolia's book teaches it this way (*Design Technology 8 (Mongolia)*, printed p. 76): bending polystyrene by heating, cutting PVC foam with a hot wire, welding polyethylene film, and cutting phenolic sheet — with the safety signs for each machine. The learner works through the named materials and steps in order, saying what each step does before doing the next one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Working plastics — doing it yourself
Now the learner does the whole task unaided: bending polystyrene by heating, cutting PVC foam with a hot wire, welding polyethylene film, and cutting phenolic sheet — with the safety signs for each machine. Working from the first sub lesson's sequence, the learner plans the make, works through it step by step, and checks the finished piece against what Mongolia's book shows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: Working plastics — bending polystyrene by heating, cutting PVC foam with a hot wire, welding polyethylene film, and cutting phenolic sheet — with the safety signs for each machine. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
