# Making toys in clay and wire

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Toys in clay
Primary 4 models in clay: a clay doll, a cat from clay — and Primary 6 returns to clay for a doll and animals. Clay takes any form the hand gives it; its toys are solid, shaped, and still.

## Sub lesson 2 — Toys in wire
Wire does the opposite work: it bends, springs and holds a line in the air. The toy bicycle is bent from wire in Primary 4; the motorcycle is made from wires in Primary 6 — and Primary 4 already pairs them, modelling a wire doll beside the clay one, and a motorcycle from clay: the same toy tried in both materials.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: clay and wire are the maker's pair across Rwanda's making years: a toy bicycle bent from wire, dolls and animals modelled in clay, a motorcycle in both — soft material and springy material, each doing what the other cannot. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
