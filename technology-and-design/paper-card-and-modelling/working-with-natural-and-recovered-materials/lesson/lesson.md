# Working with natural and recovered materials

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Collecting and classifying
Natural materials are classed by origin — vegetal, animal, mineral. Recovered materials are what use has left: containers, lids, boxes, production offcuts. Cuba's primary programmes teach collecting and classifying as the first operation, before any article is designed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making with them
The techniques follow the materials: punching seeds and shells with awl (punzón) on the sufridera and threading them (ensarte) on thread or cord, gluing (engomado), cutting with round-point scissors. In Grade 7 the lesson widens: classifying paper, card, glass and plastics, and recycling and its economic importance.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: natural and recovered materials are a real stock for making: know what they are, collect and classify them — natural by origin, recovered by source — and make articles, with punching and threading (ensarte) and gluing among the techniques. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
