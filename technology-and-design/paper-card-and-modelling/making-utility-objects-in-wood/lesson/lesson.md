# Making utility objects in wood

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — A spoon in wood
The first wood make is a spoon: small, daily, and unforgiving — a spoon must be smooth, shaped to the mouth and the pot. Carving it teaches wood's grain and patience in one small object.

## Sub lesson 2 — A hoe handle
The second make serves the farm: a hoe handle. A handle is judged by grip, length and strength — utility measured in the hand and in the field, tying the woodwork back to the agricultural tools of Primary 4.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: wood enters Rwanda's making as utility: a spoon in wood and a hoe handle — objects the home uses every day, carved and shaped by the Primary 5 learner. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
