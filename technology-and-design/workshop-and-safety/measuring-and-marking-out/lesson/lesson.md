# Measuring and marking out

## Do it first
Measure a pencil, a key, a screwdriver and a phone in millimetres, as the workbook does. Write each length. Measure each one again — the two readings must agree.

## Sub lesson 1 — Measuring in millimetres
Lengths in the workshop are measured in millimetres, because making needs finer steps than centimetres. Put the ruler's zero exactly at one end of the thing — not the ruler's edge, the zero mark — keep your eye directly above the far mark, and read the millimetres. Malta's workbook practises on everyday things first: a pencil, a key, a screwdriver, a mobile phone. Measure twice before you trust a number.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marking out a design
Marking out is drawing the design's true lines on the material (or the drawing sheet) before cutting or making: the sizes you measured become lines in the right places. The ruler gives straight edges and lengths, set squares give true right angles and parallels, and compasses give circles and arcs. In the workbook you mark out a given design — a jeep — so every tool is used on one real job.

## Sub lesson 3 — Checking before you cut
Marking out is the last cheap moment to be wrong: a line can be rubbed out, a cut cannot be uncut. Check each marked line against the design's sizes, check corners are square with the set square, and only then make. Accurate marking out is what makes the finished thing match the design.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: measure in millimetres with a ruler, reading from the zero mark — then mark out a design accurately with ruler, set squares and compasses before any making begins. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep the sheet: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
