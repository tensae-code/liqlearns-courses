# Measuring, marking and checking in wood and metal work

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — The measuring means
Cuba's 1989 book gathers the means of measurement for wood and metal in one section: folding rule and tape measure for lengths, the pie de rey (caliper) for thicknesses and diameters, each read exactly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marking and checking
Marking puts the measurement on the work: the gramil (marking gauge) scores a line parallel to an edge, compasses carry distances and strike arcs, the false square carries angles. Checking closes the triangle — the work is verified against the measure before and after cutting.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: before cutting comes measuring: the folding rule, tape measure, caliper (pie de rey), marking gauge (gramil), compasses and false square measure, mark and check the work in wood and metal. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
