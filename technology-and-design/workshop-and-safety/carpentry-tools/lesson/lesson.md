# Carpentry tools — use, care and dangers

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Rwanda's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Identifying carpentry tools and using them
Rwanda's Primary 5 book first identifies the carpentry tools, then teaches their usage: the saw family — bow/frame saw, table saw, wood/hand saw — each for its cut, and the T-square, which draws straight lines on wood. A tool is learned as a name, a shape and a job together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Maintenance and dangers
Maintenance is specific: check the saw blade's sharpness, sharpen it, clean the tools, and oil the T-square's metal parts against rust. The book closes with the dangers of carpentry tools and the health and safety measures that answer them — sharp tools are kept well precisely because they are dangerous.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: the carpentry tools are known by name, used the right way, kept sharp, clean and oiled against rust — and respected, because every one of them can cut the careless hand. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
