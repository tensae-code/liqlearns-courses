# Hand tools and materials

## Do it first
Before reading on, try the topic's core task once on your own: hand tools and materials. Keep what you did — you will check it against the lesson at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — What hand tools and materials is
Hand tools and materials: in Fiji's teaching this covers sliding bevel, rip saw; hardware (handles, hooks); working with non-metals; pipe connectors and adapters; manufactured board and its uses; metal properties (ductility); finishes incl. wax; sharpening. That is the heart of this lesson: everything else in it is the parts, the order and the checks that make this work in practice. Fiji's textbook teaches this as its own topic, in full, and this lesson follows that teaching: learn what the thing is first, in plain terms, before you handle its details.

## Sub lesson 2 — The parts and how they fit together
Work through the topic part by part, in the order the book presents it: sliding bevel, rip saw; hardware (handles, hooks); working with non-metals; pipe connectors and adapters; manufactured board and its uses; metal properties (ductility); finishes incl. wax; sharpening. For each part, learn three things: its name, what it does, and how it connects to the part before and after it. A part you can name and explain is a part you can use; a part you can only point at is not yet learned. Where the topic is a process, the order of the steps is itself part of the teaching — doing the steps out of order changes the result.

## Sub lesson 3 — Using it well — and checking your work
Now apply the whole topic to a fresh task, unaided, and check the result against the lesson's own rules: every part present, every step in order, nothing skipped. Checking is not an extra — it is the last step of the work itself. Fix one thing you find, and say what you changed and why.

## Say it to your companion
