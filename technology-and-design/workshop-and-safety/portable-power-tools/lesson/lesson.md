# Portable power tools

## Do it first
A hand saw takes a minute to cut what a circular saw cuts in seconds. That speed is the gift and the danger in one fact: everything a power tool does, it does before you can react. What must therefore be done before it starts?

## Sub lesson 1 — The four tools and their work
Each tool replaces hand effort with a motor, and each is specified — blade or bit size, power, speed — for a range of work. Choosing the tool is choosing the cut: straight, flat, round, or curved.

## Sub lesson 2 — Safe use, step by step
Never reach over a blade, never work on loose material, never set a tool down still moving. The steps are the same order every time, so habit itself becomes the guard.

## Sub lesson 3 — Size and speed — the drill rule
A big bit at high speed overheats, grabs and can tear the work or the wrist. Match the speed to the bit as the tool's specification directs — the relation between size and speed is a safety rule, not a preference.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: portable power tools — circular saw, planer, drill and jigsaw — cut, shape and bore at machine speed: each has its specification, its step-by-step safe use, and its protections: eye and ear protection, clamped work, and drill size matched to speed. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
