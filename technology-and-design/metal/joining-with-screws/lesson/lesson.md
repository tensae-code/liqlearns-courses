# Joining with screws

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Threads and heads
A screw's thread is a spiral ridge wrapped round its shaft; turning the screw winds the thread into the material, pulling the parts together with great force from little effort. Screw heads come in shapes matched to their drivers — the head must fit the tool, or it chews and fails. Iran's Grade 8 module teaches threads and heads together, as one system.

## Sub lesson 2 — Driving screws safely
Safe driving is technique: choose the screw for the materials, prepare the hole where the material demands it, keep the driver seated and straight, and stop at firm — overdriving strips the thread and ruins the joint. The module's screw-joining finishes its project: the metal photo frame is fixed on its wooden base, metal joined to wood by screws.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a screw joins by its thread: the spiral ridge grips the material as the head drives it home — and driving screws safely is a technique, not a force. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
