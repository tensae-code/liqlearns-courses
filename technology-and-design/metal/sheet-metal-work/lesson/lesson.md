# Sheet metal work — marking out, cutting, sawing, filing and drilling

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Marking out and cutting
Work begins on the flat sheet: the pattern is marked out with a scriber and needle, whose fine lines survive handling as pencil would not. Sheet thickness decides the cutting tool — snips shear thin sheet along the marked lines, while wire and thicker stock go to the saw. Iran's Grade 8 metalwork module opens here, around its photo-frame project.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sawing and filing
Sawing starts with a small groove so the blade does not skate, and proceeds with steady strokes; filing follows its own safety rules — handle secure, work clamped, strokes in one direction — until the surface is filed clean and bright, rust removed. A clean filed surface is the sign the technique is right.

## Sub lesson 3 — Centre-punching and drilling
Before drilling, the hole's centre is marked with a centre punch (سنبه نشان): the punched dent stops the drill point wandering across the sheet. Drilling then follows its safety rules — work held, no loose clothing or hair, the drill stopped before adjustments. Punch first, drill second: the order is the lesson.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: sheet metal work follows one order: mark out truly with scriber and needle, cut with the tool that fits the material, then saw, file and drill to the line — each step with its safety rule. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
