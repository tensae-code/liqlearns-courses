# Lesson plan — Metals and alloys

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Metal · **Lesson:** Metals and alloys
**Place in the curriculum:** Metal (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Cuba expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** metals are known by their properties — physical, chemical, mechanical and technological — and sheet metal and wire by their own: plasticity, hardness and conductivity decide what each metal can become.
**This lesson's own level:** stocked from Cuba's *Orientaciones metodológicas: Educación Laboral — 5to grado* and the Cuba books in lesson/sources.md — see lesson/sources.md. Cuba counts in the acceptance count for its student-book placements; its programme and methodological-orientations placements are evidence only, counted nowhere.

Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Metals, alloys and their properties** — An alloy is a metal mixed for better properties. Cuba's Grade 8 course classifies the properties four ways — physical, chemical, mechanical, technological — with malleability among them, and joins the lesson to recycling metals and its environmental impact.
2. **Sheet metal and wire; where metals come from** — The primary courses meet metals as laminado (sheet) and alambre (wire), with plasticity, hardness and electrical conductivity as the properties that matter at the bench. The fuller picture adds origin: mines, the production outline, the profiles metals arrive in — and protecting sheet and wire against oxidation and corrosion.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
