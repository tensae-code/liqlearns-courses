# Metals and alloys

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Cuba's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Metals, alloys and their properties
An alloy is a metal mixed for better properties. Cuba's Grade 8 course classifies the properties four ways — physical, chemical, mechanical, technological — with malleability among them, and joins the lesson to recycling metals and its environmental impact.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sheet metal and wire; where metals come from
The primary courses meet metals as laminado (sheet) and alambre (wire), with plasticity, hardness and electrical conductivity as the properties that matter at the bench. The fuller picture adds origin: mines, the production outline, the profiles metals arrive in — and protecting sheet and wire against oxidation and corrosion.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: metals are known by their properties — physical, chemical, mechanical and technological — and sheet metal and wire by their own: plasticity, hardness and conductivity decide what each metal can become. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
