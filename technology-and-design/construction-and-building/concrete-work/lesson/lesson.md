# Concrete work

## Do it first
A square corner without a square: measure 3 metres one way, 4 the other — if the diagonal is exactly 5, your corner is a true right angle. Builders have trusted Pythagoras on site for centuries.

## Sub lesson 1 — Setting out 3-4-5, formwork and reinforcement
Measure 3 along one side, 4 along the other: the diagonal must read 5, or the corner is not square. The boxing is built level and strong — wet concrete pushes hard. Reinforcement is placed where the slab will stretch, held off the ground so concrete covers it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Calculating, ordering and mixing
Work out each bay's volume (length × width × thickness) and total them, with a margin for waste. In mixing, cement, aggregate and water follow the mix design: extra water makes placing easier and the floor weaker, permanently. The ratio is the strength.

## Sub lesson 3 — Placing, screeding and finishing
Screeding strikes the surface level with a straightedge riding the formwork. Edging rounds the borders against chipping; grooving cuts control lines where shrinkage cracks may come, neatly. Timing is the finisher's skill: work the surface as it stiffens, neither soupy nor set.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a concrete floor is laid in order: set out with the 3-4-5 method, box formwork in bays with reinforcement, calculate and order the material volumes, then mix, place, screed and finish — with the water-cement ratio deciding the strength. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
