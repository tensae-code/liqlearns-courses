# Building walls in other bonds

## Do it first
Before reading on: take the materials this lesson names and try the task in your own way first. Then read how Iran's book teaches it, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — Header bond
In header bond every brick is laid with its end face showing, so the wall's thickness is the brick's length and every brick ties across the wall. The bond suits thick, strong and curved work, at the cost of more bricks and a plainer face.

## Sub lesson 2 — Header-and-stretcher bond
The combined bond alternates: a course of headers ties the wall across, a course of stretchers lengthens and faces it — in the one-brick wall of Iran's Grade 9 module, the two bonds alternate to give both tie and face. Choosing a bond is choosing how the wall holds together: the pattern is the engineering.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a one-brick wall needs stronger patterns: header bond, all ends showing, and header-and-stretcher bond, courses alternating — each bond tying the thicker wall together. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
