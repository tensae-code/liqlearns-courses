# Lesson plan — Building construction

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Construction and Building · **Lesson:** Building construction
**Place in the curriculum:** Construction and Building (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a timber building stands on its foundations and footings, and rises in named framing members — bearers, joists, ledger, decking, fascia, battens and plates — joined with timber connectors, the roof set by pitch and checked plumb.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Foundations and footings** — The foundation is the whole supporting system; the footings are the parts that spread the load into the ground — post footings carry posts.
2. **The framing members and their order** — Bearers carry joists; joists carry decking or flooring; plates cap walls; battens carry roofing; the ledger fixes a new structure to an old one; fascia finishes the roof's edge.
3. **Connectors, pitch and plumb** — Timber connectors — Trip-L-Grip and joist hangers — tie members together against lifting and slipping; roof pitch sets the slope; plumb checks the vertical.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell foundation from footings.
2. Trace the load path through the framing members.
3. Say what connectors, pitch and plumb each do.
## What you need
A timber structure to observe (a deck, a veranda) if available.
