# Building construction

## Do it first
Stand on a deck or wooden floor and bounce gently. Under your feet, unseen: boards on joists, joists on bearers, bearers on posts, posts on footings. Trace the path your weight takes to the ground.

## Sub lesson 1 — Foundations and footings
Every load in the building travels down to the ground. A post's footing gives it a footing wide enough not to sink. Confuse the two words and drawings get misread: footings are parts; the foundation is the system they belong to.

## Sub lesson 2 — The framing members and their order
Learn the members as a load path: roof to battens to rafters to plates; floor to joists to bearers to posts to footings. A member's name is its place in the path — name it and you know what it carries.

## Sub lesson 3 — Connectors, pitch and plumb
Nails alone can withdraw; formed metal connectors grip the joint. Pitch is measured and set at the drawing stage, not guessed on the roof. Plumb — true vertical — is checked with level or line at every stage, because a frame out of plumb fights every sheet and door that follows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: a timber building stands on its foundations and footings, and rises in named framing members — bearers, joists, ledger, decking, fascia, battens and plates — joined with timber connectors, the roof set by pitch and checked plumb. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
