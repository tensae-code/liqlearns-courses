# Lesson plan — Boatbuilding construction sequence

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Construction and Building · **Lesson:** Boatbuilding construction sequence
**Place in the curriculum:** Construction and Building (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Haiti expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a Haitian canot is built in a fixed order — keel laid first on supports, then stem, transom and frames held by shores — and the stem's angle sets the boat's speed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — Haiti's ITAP Projet Nectar pedagogical sequences (MENFP) are the books in hand for this topic (see lesson/sources.md). Teacher guides are evidence, counted nowhere in the acceptance count.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **The keel first** — The backbone is laid first, on supports, and everything hangs from it.
2. **Stem, transom and frames** — The ends are set, then the ribs that give the hull its shape.
3. **Boat timbers — oak and cedar prepared** — The sequence stands on timbers chosen and prepared for the work.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Construction and Building. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the build order: keel, stem, transom, frames.
2. Say what the stem angle controls.
3. Name oak and cedar as prepared boat timbers.
## What you need
A model or diagram of a canot hull, timber samples or pictures of oak and cedar, and the part names in French and English.
