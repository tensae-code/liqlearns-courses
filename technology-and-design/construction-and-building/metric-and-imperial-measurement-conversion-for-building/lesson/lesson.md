# Metric and imperial measurement conversion for building

## Do it first
A plan says 900 mm. The tape in the store is marked in inches. How wide is the opening in inches — and would 'about three feet' be close enough to order a door? (900 ÷ 25.4 ≈ 35.4 inches; a door is ordered exact.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Two systems on one site
One inch is exactly 25.4 mm. Every conversion on site descends from that fact. A misread conversion is not a small error — a frame member 25 mm out is a door that never fits.

## Sub lesson 2 — The conversion table
Tables exist to kill arithmetic slips at the moment of measuring. Find the size, read across: the equivalent stands there. For sizes off the table, multiply or divide by 25.4 — and check by converting back.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rules of thumb and checking
Estimate with the rule, verify with the table or the exact factor, then cut. The habit is: rough by thumb, exact by table, checked by converting back. Speed is welcome; unverified speed is waste.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: builders in Samoa meet both systems: millimetres and inches convert by fixed relations — the book's mm/inch conversion table and its rules of thumb let a builder move between a metric drawing and imperial materials without error. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.
