# Lesson plan — Metric and imperial measurement conversion for building

**Course:** Technology and Design · **Title:** Construction and Building · **Lesson:** Metric and imperial measurement conversion for building
**Place in the curriculum:** Construction and Building (CURRICULUM.md) — stocked in the Samoa expansion, 2026-11-17.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** builders in Samoa meet both systems: millimetres and inches convert by fixed relations — the book's mm/inch conversion table and its rules of thumb let a builder move between a metric drawing and imperial materials without error.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on student book evidence — Samoa's MESC textbooks (see lesson/sources.md). It counts in the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Picture and GIF numbers are assigned in a later media pass; the slots below are described in words.

## Sub lessons
1. **Two systems on one site** — Drawings may be metric while timber, pipes and tools arrive in imperial sizes: the builder must convert, exactly.
2. **The conversion table** — The book's table pairs millimetres with inches across the working range, so common sizes convert by reading, not calculating.
3. **Rules of thumb and checking** — Rules of thumb give quick estimates — about 25 mm to the inch, about 300 mm to the foot — but final cuts use exact conversion.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Construction and Building. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the exact inch-millimetre relation.
2. Convert common sizes by table and by factor, both ways.
3. Say when a rule of thumb suffices and when it does not.
## What you need
A calculator and, if available, a tape marked in both systems.
