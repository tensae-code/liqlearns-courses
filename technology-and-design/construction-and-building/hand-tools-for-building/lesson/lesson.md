# Hand tools for building

## Do it first
Stretch a string tightly between two pegs and sight along it: a perfectly straight line, longer than any ruler you own. Builders have set out houses with that line for thousands of years.

## Sub lesson 1 — Setting out and measuring — string lines, tape rules, spirit levels
A string line stretched between pegs is a straight edge metres long. The tape rule measures against it; the spirit level's bubble, centred, proves a surface level or a post plumb. These three tools decide where everything else goes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cutting and shaping — saws, chisels, the marking gauge, the sliding bevel
Match the saw to the cut: crosscut across the grain, panel saw for sheet and finer work. A chisel is driven by hand or mallet, always away from the body, its edge guarded when stored. The marking gauge turns an edge into a true parallel line; the sliding bevel carries an angle from one place to another unchanged.

## Sub lesson 3 — Care and safe use
Clean tools after use, keep edges sharp and protected, store dry, and check handles before swinging or striking. Safety is mostly sequence: clamp the work, keep hands behind the edge, carry tools point-down, and never force a dull tool — force is where injuries live.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: building hand tools mark out, measure, cut and shape: string lines set out the work, chisels shape, spirit levels true it, tape rules size it, the marking gauge lines it, saws cut it — each cared for and used safely. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
