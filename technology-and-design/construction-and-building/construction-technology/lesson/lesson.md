# Construction technology

## Do it first
Before your next group task of any kind, write who does what and what each person needs. Compare that minute of organising with how the task goes. Construction begins exactly this way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Preparing the site
Haiti's greenhouse sequence gives site preparation its own stage: the ground is prepared, the work is organised, and everything the build needs is made ready before fabrication begins. A structure built on an unprepared site inherits every problem the preparation would have removed. Preparation is construction work, not a delay before it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Team responsibilities and tooling
The build is organised as team work with assigned responsibilities — who prepares, who measures, who assembles, who checks — and the tooling is assigned with the tasks. Construction technology is social as much as material: a structure rises correctly when every responsibility is named before the work starts, so nothing is done twice and nothing is left undone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Building to the specification
Fabrication follows the specification written earlier in the project: the greenhouse is built to its stated main function, materials and dimensions. The cahier des charges stands beside the build as the check — each stage of the structure is compared with it. Building to specification is what separates construction from improvisation.

## Say it to your companion
Explain in your own words: construction technology turns a specification into a standing structure: the site is prepared with assigned team responsibilities and the right tooling, then the structure is built following the cahier des charges. Say it once plainly, then answer one question your companion asks about it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the Do it first task again, carefully this time, using everything the lesson taught.
2. Check your work against the lesson's own checks — fix one thing you find.
3. Keep your work: it is practice evidence, and the next lesson builds on it.
