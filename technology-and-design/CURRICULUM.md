# Technology & Design course — CURRICULUM (the path)

Technology & Design is one world course, organised by **levels,
not grades**: a learner climbs when they are ready, not when a
school calendar says so. The school grades the books come from
appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the
course's organisation.

## Ethiopia, Technical Drawing (Wave 4, built 2026-11-12; pictures 26800–26999)

The first stocked part is **Technical Drawing**, built from
Ethiopia's Grade 12 Technical Drawing **teacher guide only** —
no pupil book has been read, so every lesson stands on labelled
teacher-guide evidence and carries the 0-countries acceptance
line, and every sources file carries the era note: the guide is
an older GEQIP-generation book (2003 E.C.), taught as its era's
course, never presented as the current syllabus unchecked.

- **Band A — Units 1–3** (9 lessons, 123 questions): free-hand
  sketching, sketching techniques, isometric pictorial sketching,
  orthographic projection, auxiliary views (primary and
  secondary), sectioning words and practice, types of section,
  conventional practice in sectioning. Pictures 26800–26808,
  GIFs 26810–26815 (drawn, frame-checked).
- **Band B — Units 4–5** (7 lessons, 93 questions):
  dimensioning (lines and symbols, size and location, the two
  arranging methods, placing dimensions), surface development,
  developing solids (parallel line, radial line, triangulation)
  and intersections of solids. Pictures 26900–26904, 26906,
  26908; GIFs 26905, 26907 (drawn, frame-checked; two renders
  remade when their geometry was wrong). Honesty carried: Unit
  5's misnumbering (its lessons are numbered 6.1–6.3, there is
  no Unit 6) taught corrected and flagged in sources.

and wave4-td-B-units4-5.md.

## Canada, Technology in the Community (T&D expansion, stocked 2026-11-17)

Stocked from Canada's *Technology in the Community*
(Tulliniliara Reading Series, Level 14) — Government of Nunavut
(Department of Education / Department of Family Services),
developed and published in Canada by Inhabit Education, written
by Jordan Hoffman, illustrated by Ali Hinch, © 2019,
ISBN 978-0-2287-0447-8 (English edition; the Inuktitut edition
is a parallel edition of the same book, read once, placed

COMPLETE to the last teaching page (printed p. 32). All 7
placements were NEW LESSON NEEDED; each was resolved through
the T&D slug registry to its canonical lesson and built new.
Canada counts in the acceptance count (student book, not
evidence-only): every lesson below stands at taught in
1 country (Canada).

- **First Steps** (3 lessons, 13 questions each): What
  technology is (printed p. 2); Computers as community
  technology (printed pp. 5–9); GPS technology
  (printed pp. 14–17).
- **Mechanisms and Machines** (1 lesson, 13 questions): How a
  piston engine moves a snowmobile (printed pp. 10–13).
- **Electrical Installations and the Home** (1 lesson,
  13 questions): Heating technology at home
  (printed pp. 18–21).
- **Textiles and Fashion** (1 lesson, 13 questions): The
  sewing machine — stocked from the placement "How a sewing
  machine makes a stitch" (printed pp. 22–25).
- **Radiography Technology** (1 lesson, 13 questions):
  X-ray machines (printed pp. 26–30).

Canada's own way is content and is carried in each lesson's

and sinew beside the sewing machine, and GPS through the
hunters, boaters, pilots, fishers, prospectors and coast

## Finland, Käsityö — life-cycle and textile materials (T&D expansion, stocked 2026-11-17)

Stocked from Finland's *Katse tulevaan* ("Look to the
future") — *KATSE TULEVAAN — Tulevaisuuden taidot,
vastuulliset materiaalivalinnat ja elinkaariajattelu
käsityöprosessissa*, Future skills, responsible material
choices and life-cycle thinking in the craft process —
TuleVa project learning material, a joint project of four
craft schools (basic art education), published via aoe.fi
(Avointen oppimateriaalien kirjasto — Library of Open
Educational Resources, run by the Finnish Ministry of
Education and Culture and OPH with CSC), in Finnish,
47 pp., read COMPLETE (all 47 pages). All 7 placements
were NEW LESSON NEEDED; each was resolved through the
T&D slug registry to its canonical lesson and built new.
Finland counts in the acceptance count (learning material
for craft teaching, not evidence-only): every lesson below
stands at taught in 1 country (Finland).

- **Materials** (2 lessons, 13 questions each): Choosing
  materials responsibly (printed p. 9); Life-cycle thinking
  in the craft process (printed p. 12).
- **Textiles and Fashion** (5 lessons, 13 questions each):
  Dyeing and finishing textiles (printed p. 23); Fabric
  printing techniques (printed p. 28); From material to
  product (printed p. 29); Reuse in practice
  (printed p. 14); Textile fibres (printed p. 32).

Finland's own way is content and is carried in each
lesson's sources: Käsityö (the Finnish craft subject)
teaches the craft process as a whole life cycle —
elinkaariajattelu — with responsible material choices,
the textile-material cards (each fibre's properties, life
cycle and care), kaavoitus (careful pattern drafting for
economical cutting), kuviointi (fabric printing), and a
reuse pilot for 7–9-year-olds making a new product from
kierrätysfarkut (recycled jeans) while minimising the
waste their own work produces.

## Kuwait, Digital Intelligence — 3D printing (T&D expansion, stocked 2026-11-17)

Stocked from Kuwait's الذكاء الرقمي 8 (*Digital Intelligence,
Grade 8, Term 2*) — Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait,
first edition 2025/2026 series (2025/2026 (1447H),
intermediate stage, Term 2), in Arabic, 306 PDF pp.
(printed book to p. 302 + references), read COMPLETE in
full from the text layer. The book as a whole is Kuwait's
Digital Intelligence (ICT) subject; only its first journey
— digital fabrication: 3D printing from idea to finished
model — and the design side of its closing integrated
project are Technology & Design teaching, and only those
are stocked here. All 7 placements were NEW LESSON
NEEDED; each was resolved through the T&D slug registry
to its canonical lesson and built new. Kuwait counts in
the acceptance count (student book, not evidence-only):
every lesson below stands at taught in 1 country
(Kuwait).

- **Models and Robotics** (6 lessons, 13 questions each):
  3D printing, from idea to model (printed pp. 23–32);
  First steps with UltiMaker Cura (printed pp. 33–48);
  Print quality and printing speed (printed pp. 49–64);
  The model's strength from the inside (printed
  pp. 65–76); Printing overhanging parts (printed
  pp. 77–90); Making the model stick to the printer bed
  (printed pp. 91–103).
- **Design and Making** (1 lesson, 13 questions): The
  integrated smart-building project (design side)
  (printed pp. 285–297) — the design side only; the
  project's network plan is ICT work and is not stocked.

Kuwait's own way is content and is carried in each
lesson's sources: 3D printing is taught as a complete
journey inside the Digital Intelligence subject — idea to
model, the slicer (UltiMaker Cura) as its own step, then
each print setting as its own lesson (quality against
speed, infill strength, supports, bed adhesion) — closing
with an integrated smart-building project where the
building's parts and IoT devices are modelled and
prepared virtually with geometric precision before
execution, alongside the network plan.

## Jamaica, Resource & Technology — Engineering & Mechanisms projects (T&D expansion, stocked 2026-11-17)

Stocked from Jamaica's *National Standards Curriculum
Guide — Grades 4–6 — Resource & Technology* —
Ministry of Education, Youth and Information, Kingston,
Jamaica, © 2018, 188 PDF pp. (printed pagination to
p. 167; printed p. = PDF p. − 20), read COMPLETE. The
guide's Engineering & Mechanisms units — one
design-and-make project per grade — and its Design
Process material are stocked here; its other strands
(Agriculture, Family & Consumer Management, Business
Basics) belong to other courses and are not stocked.
Of the 23 placements, 21 were NEW LESSON NEEDED (each
resolved through the T&D slug registry to its canonical
lesson; 19 built new, 1 stocked into the existing
Measuring and marking out lesson, and Evaluating a made
product carries 2 of the 21) and 2 stocked into existing
Technical Drawing lessons. Jamaica counts in the
acceptance count (curriculum guide with full unit
teaching content, not evidence-only).

- **Grade 4 — 'Keep the Wheels Turning' (toy car):**
  The design process (printed p. 31); Mechanical energy
  in devices and transport (printed p. 30); Forces and
  friction in mechanisms (printed p. 31); Shapes and
  solids for movement (printed p. 34); Hand tools
  (printed p. 33); Making a toy car (printed p. 33);
  Prototyping — a mock-up before making (printed
  p. 35); Working safely with tools and materials
  (printed p. 33); Careers in automotive and mechanical
  engineering (printed p. 35); plus Measuring and
  marking out stocked into the existing lesson (printed
  p. 33) and the 2 existing-lesson placements below.
- **Grade 5 — 'This Little Light of Mine' (flashlight):**
  The simple electric circuit (printed p. 72); Reading a
  circuit schematic and a cross-section (printed p. 75);
  Designing a product for a need (design criteria)
  (printed p. 75); Making a working flashlight (printed
  p. 76); Safety with simple circuits and cutting tools
  (printed p. 75); Evaluating a made product (printed
  p. 75).
- **Grade 6 — 'Let's Magnetize Together' (electromagnet
  and motor):** Magnets and electromagnetism for
  technology (printed p. 115); Making an electromagnet
  (printed p. 115); Building a simple electromagnetic
  motor (printed p. 116); Drawing a labelled schematic
  of a made device (printed p. 116); Evaluating a made

- **Existing Technical Drawing lessons stocked from
  Jamaica** (2 placements): Free-hand sketching —
  principles (printed p. 35); Dimensioning — lines,
  symbols and reading direction (printed p. 33). Both
  also keep their Ethiopia teacher-guide evidence,
  counted nowhere.

Jamaica's own way is content and is carried in each
lesson's sources: one named method — the 'E' Design
Process — runs through every project; each grade's
teaching rides on one design-and-make project (toy car,
flashlight, electromagnet and motor); safety is attached
to every practical task; products are evaluated with a
prescribed checklist and rating scale for neatness,
accuracy and functionality against the original need;
and the flashlight is designed for a stated Jamaican
need — a simple light for children, for hurricane
season.
