# Lesson plan — Photosynthesis

**Course:** Biology · **Title:** How plants make food · **Lesson:**
Photosynthesis
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology's first built lesson — the
template example that shows the lesson shape. Biology has no written
curriculum yet; this plan's placement line gets its term/unit when
the biology curriculum is written.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a plant makes its own food —
the leaf takes in sunlight, air, and water, and turns them into food
for the plant and oxygen for us.

**Honest state of this lesson:** lesson/lesson.md is the EXAMPLE text
that shows the shape (it says so at its own top). The real text gets
built from every FINISHED country's biology books that teach this,
merged under the merge rule — nothing cut, every different
explanation kept as an example. This plan is written against the
example text so the shape is complete; when the real merged text
lands, the plan is re-checked against it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule — and for a science
lesson they bite hard: a real photograph of a real leaf, never a
drawing made just to explain.

## Before this lesson
- Nothing in Biology yet — this is the course's first lesson. (In
  the wider platform, Pencil and Lines is the standing recommended
  start for writing work.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why a plant must make its own food (it cannot walk to find
   food).
2. Name the three things the leaf takes in: sunlight, air, water.
3. Name the two things that come out: food (sugar) for the plant,
   oxygen for us.
4. Say the recipe line from memory: Leaf + sunlight + water + air →
   food for the plant + oxygen for us.

## What you need
A green leaf and sunlight — the lesson starts outside.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — go outside, find a green leaf, hold it up to the
   sun, feel the sun's warmth on your hand; the leaf is catching it
   too (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of a real green
     leaf held up against real sunlight (a real photograph, with
     its source) — the student goes and does the same with a real
     leaf before reading on.
2. **The explanation** — the plant cannot walk, so it makes food;
   what the leaf takes in; what the sunlight's energy does inside
   the leaf; the oxygen let out (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under the recipe: a picture of the recipe as a row —
     a leaf, a sun, drops of water, moving air going in on the left;
     a growing plant and the air we breathe coming out on the
     right — the student points along the row and says each part.
   - ADD AUDIO: the explanation read aloud — the student listens
     once with the leaf in hand, then listens again and says the
     recipe line at its place.
3. **Remember it like this** — the recipe line itself is the
   lesson's memory hook (lesson/lesson.md, end).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the recipe acted with a real leaf in real sun, in the
     super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes what goes in with what comes out → the row picture: left side
  is the shopping, right side is the cooking. Oxygen is cooked, not
  shopped.
- Says the plant "eats sunlight" → close, and the lesson's own words
  fix it: the leaf takes in sunlight, air AND water; the sunlight's
  energy does the turning.
