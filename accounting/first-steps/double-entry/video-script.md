# Video script — Double Entry

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Every transaction has two sides: for every debit entry there is a corresponding credit entry, so the books always balance.

Sorting transactions before recording them: cash transactions, where both sides of the exchange happen at once (payment in cash, in other forms of money such as cheques and money orders, or in kind), versus credit transactions, where payment is deferred to a later date. The dual effect: every transaction increases or decreases balance-sheet items in a way that keeps the balance sheet balancing (an asset and a liability can move together; some transactions touch more than two items, and selling stock above its cost creates profit) — taught Kenya's way, by redrawing the whole balance sheet after each transaction before the ledger is ever introduced. The rules for recording in ledger accounts: an increase in an asset is debited and a decrease credited; an increase in a liability or in capital is credited and a decrease debited (capital treated as a special liability); expenses behave like assets (increase debited) and revenues the opposite (increase credited). The double-entry concept itself: for every debit entry a corresponding credit entry, the particulars column naming the other account affected and the folio column cross-referencing it — plus the book's five-step method for analysing any transaction before recording it (identify the accounts, classify them, find increase or decrease, choose the side).

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
