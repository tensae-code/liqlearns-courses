# Hands-on — The Accounting Equation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List everything a small shop near you owns — its stock, its counter, its cash. Then ask of each thing: whose money bought it — the owner's, or someone the shop still owes? Sort your list into those two piles. That sorting is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: everything a business has (its assets) comes from somewhere: from the owner (capital) or from outsiders (liabilities) — so assets always equal capital plus liabilities, and every transaction keeps that balance. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
