# Video script — The Accounting Equation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Everything a business has (its assets) comes from somewhere: from the owner (capital) or from outsiders (liabilities) — so assets always equal capital plus liabilities, and every transaction keeps that balance.

The basic terms the equation is built from: debtor, creditor and goods, then assets — property with a monetary value, split into fixed assets (kept to run the business, expected to last over a year) and current assets (cash or near-cash, under a year), with the characteristics of an asset. Liabilities as what the business owes outsiders: long-term liabilities (not settled within a year) and short-term/current liabilities (payable within a year, such as bank overdrafts and creditors), with the characteristics of a liability. Capital as the owner's claim on the business (money and items the owner contributes), and the book-keeping equation built from where resources come from: assets = capital (internal source) + liabilities (external source), rearranged to find any one unknown. Net worth as another name for capital: the owner's contributions plus profits less drawings, found by taking total assets minus liabilities, and negative when liabilities exceed assets.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
