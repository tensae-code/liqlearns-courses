# Hands-on — What Is Accounting

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of the last money decision you made — to spend, to save, to borrow. Now ask: what did you need to know before you decided? Write down the money facts you used. That need — facts about money, in order, before a decision — is where accounting begins.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: accounting is the system that records a business's money transactions, sorts and summarises them into statements, and analyses them so the people who use the information can decide — and book-keeping, the systematic recording, is its first part. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
