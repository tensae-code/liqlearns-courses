# The Accounting Cycle

## Do it first

Follow one sale in a shop from start to finish: the receipt written, the day's sales listed, the totals moved into the accounts, and at the year's end the profit statement drawn. Write the four steps in order. You have just walked the accounting cycle.

## The accounting cycle as a whole

The accounting cycle closed out: preparing the financial statements and the reports developed from them is the final stage — input (transactions recorded on source documents), process (the information classified and summarised in the journals and ledgers), output (financial statements prepared).

**The accounting cycle.** The service-company cycle as a whole, in the book's three stages: recording (analyse transaction evidence, journalise, post to the ledger), summarising (trial balance, adjusting journal, adjusted trial balance, worksheet, closing journal, post-closing trial balance) and reporting (income statement, statement of changes in equity, statement of financial position, cash-flow statement) (Indonesia's book.)

**The accounting cycle.** The worksheet (kertas kerja): the ten-column worksheet that carries trial balance, adjustments, adjusted trial balance, income-statement columns and balance-sheet columns in one working paper before the statements are drawn (Indonesia's book.)

**The accounting cycle.** Closing entries and the post-closing trial balance: closing the nominal (income and expense) accounts and drawings to equity, then proving the real accounts that carry forward (Indonesia's book.)

**The accounting cycle.** Closing entries of a trading company: closing through the COGS/income-summary route so the merchandise accounts close with the nominal accounts (Indonesia's book.)

**The accounting cycle.** The guide's own compression of the service cycle's summarising stage: closing entries move the balances of the nominal accounts into the real accounts (assets, liabilities, equity) at period end, their source data is the worksheet's income-statement and balance-sheet columns for the drawings or dividend account, and the post-closing trial balance is the last summarising step (Indonesia's teacher guide.)

**The accounting cycle.** Final accounts as the completion of the cycle: this book takes up where the first-year book stopped (journal, posting to the ledger, trial balance by totals and by balances) and completes the accounting cycle by preparing the final accounts — operating account, trading account, profit-and-loss account — and then the balance sheet, so the period's result can serve owners, financiers, debtors,… (Sudan's book.)

**The accounting cycle.** The accounting process (cycle): a series of steps regularly repeated in the same order — (1) identify transactions through their source documents, (2) record transactions in a journal, (3) post to ledger accounts, (4) prepare the trial balance, (5) make year-end adjustments, (6) prepare financial statements (Tanzania's book.)

**Stocktake.** The inventory (απογραφή): the detailed verification, counting, recording and valuation in one currency of all asset items at a given moment, done in two stages (identify/count/record, then value); its kinds — general vs partial, real (from physical checking) vs theoretical (from the books), regular vs extraordinary — and its recording in the Book of Inventories and Balance Sheets; a partial inventory is also a control tool against theft, embezzlement and error (Greece's book.)

**Year-end-sequence.** The order of the year-end accounting work, as the book lists it: (1) summary (first provisional) trial balance from the accounts; (2) general inventory; (3) comparison of the accounts with the inventory and their adjustment to it; (4) second provisional trial balance; (5) determination of the operating results and the year's result and distribution of the result; (6) final trial balance; (7) the financial statements (year-end balance sheet, Results of the Year statement, appropriation table, General Operating account statement); (8) closing the year's books; (9) opening the new year's books (Greece's book.)

**Opening-the-books.** The opening inventory and opening balance sheet: a trader keeping third-category books for the first time draws up an opening inventory (Κ.Β.Σ. art. 27 — where there are no fixed assets or stocks, the remaining asset and liability items are simply listed in the opening entry instead); because the partners' contributions include fixed assets, the model firm draws up the opening inventory and then the opening balance sheet, both entered in the certified Book of Inventories and Balance Sheets (Greece's book.)

**Closing-the-month.** The month-end routine: once all the month's documents are recorded, the analytical journals are closed — each first-degree account's movement is totalled down its column and the totals are agreed across, so that in the summary entry the total debits equal the total credits — and the month's total movement passes through the Centralised Journal into the General Ledger (Greece's book.)

**Year-end-sequence.** The start of the year-end work: after December's summary entries the Centralised Journal is finally totalled and closed (it will serve the new year), and the General Ledger trial balance drawn at that point is called the first provisional trial balance (1ο προσωρινό ισοζύγιο), to distinguish it from the later 31-December trial balances; then the year-end inventory is drawn, because during the year events such as accounting errors, devaluations, overvaluations and thefts change the financial position without appearing in the accounts, so balances alone cannot give the result or the balance sheet (Greece's book.)

**Cycle part.** The complete accounting cycle in its eight stages, including the adjustment stage (adjustments made in the Jurnal Am and posted to the ledger — revenue and expense adjustments, depreciation, allowance for doubtful debts, omitted entries), the adjusted trial balance (testing the accuracy of double entry after adjustment) and the closing entries (closing all revenue and expense accounts through the Jurnal Am and ledger) (Malaysia's book.)

**The accounting cycle as a whole.** The process of accounting drawn as the full cycle: identification of transactions → recording in journal → posting to ledger → trial balance → adjusted trial balance → preparation of financial statements → analysis, interpretation and reporting, with rectification of errors inside the cycle (Bhutan's book.)

**The cycle as a whole.** Unit 12.2 Final Reports: the whole accounting cycle, from source documents through to the final reports (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**The cycle.** Strand 2 Unit 2: the accounting cycle as a steps diagram — transaction, extract data from source documents, transfer to journals, post to ledgers, balance the ledgers and extract a trial balance, financial statements (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Cycle.** The accounting process as a whole: its stages from transaction to statements (Fiji's book.)
