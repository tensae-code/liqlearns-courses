# Correcting Errors

## Do it first

A bookkeeper enters a payment of 90 as 9 on one side only. The two sides of the books no longer agree. How would you hunt the mistake? And could a mistake hide even when the sides agree? Both questions are this lesson.

## Errors part of the lesson

The limits of the trial balance: the errors that make it disagree (partial omission, unequal amounts, wrong balances posted, wrong side) versus the errors it cannot reveal even when it balances — total omission, error of original entry, error of commission, compensating errors, complete reversal of entries and error of principle.

**Correcting errors.** The suspense account and cash differences at stocktaking: when the physical cash count differs from the cash book, the difference is carried to a suspense account (حساب معلق) — a cash surplus credited, a shortage debited — until it is explained, so the book cash is made to agree with the cash actually in the safe, and the suspense balance shows among the balance-sheet balances (Sudan's book.)

**Correcting errors.** Errors against frauds, and why errors are corrected: an error is an undeliberate, accidental mistake from which no one is intended to benefit, unlike a fraud; errors must be corrected so the financial statements show a true and fair view of performance and financial position (Tanzania's book.)

**Correcting errors.** The classification of errors and the procedures for correcting them: the classes of error (those that do and do not affect the agreement of the trial balance) and the journal procedure for correcting each class (Tanzania's book.)

**Correcting errors.** Correction of errors using the suspense account: where the trial balance disagrees, the difference is parked in a suspense account and cleared as each error is found and corrected by journal entry (Tanzania's book.)

**Correcting errors.** The revised income statement and statement of financial position: after the corrections, the profit and the financial-position figures are reworked to show the effect of the corrected errors on the statements (Tanzania's book.)

**Correcting-errors.** Accounting errors and their correction: erasing or scratching out is forbidden; a wrong journal entry is corrected by accounting means — a total reversal (ολικός αντιλογισμός) cancelling the wrong entry, followed by the correct entry; a partial reversal (μερικός αντιλογισμός) when only one side of the entry is wrong; and a supplementary entry when the amount recorded was too small (with a partial reversal for the difference when it was too large) (Greece's book.)

**Error kinds part.** Correction of errors (Pembetulan Kesilapan): errors divide into two kinds — kesilapan ketara (apparent errors: clear errors that can be seen from an unbalanced trial balance, the debit total not equal to the credit total; five types, including the casting errors made while preparing the books of prime entry, the ledger or the trial balance) and kesilapan tidak ketara (non-apparent errors: errors that occur but cannot be seen from the trial balance because its totals still balance) — and the methods of correcting each, in the Jurnal Am and the ledger, with the adjusted trial balance prepared after the non-apparent errors are corrected (Malaysia's book.)

**Error types part.** The non-apparent error types defined and corrected: ketinggalan (a transaction not recorded at all in the books of prime entry and the ledger), komisen (recorded in the wrong account under the same account class), prinsip (recorded in the wrong account under a different class — computer repairs recorded in the Computer account, corrected by journal: debit Belanja Membaiki Komputer, credit Komputer), amaun (the wrong amount recorded on both the debit and credit sides, the account over- or under-recorded; the correction identifies the over- or under-recorded amount and records the difference on the opposite side if over-recorded, the same side if under-recorded — cash sales of RM930 recorded as RM903 corrected by RM27), terbalik or songsang (the double entry made on reversed sides) and saling mengimbangi (the same error on the debit side and the credit side of an account, over- or under-recorded equally) (Malaysia's book.)

**Statements after correction part.** Financial statements after the correction of errors (Penyata Kewangan Selepas Pembetulan Kesilapan): the statements are prepared after the adjusted trial balance; the correction of errors gives an effect inside the financial statements; and if a correction is not taken into account in the previous financial year, it gives an effect on the profit or loss of the current financial year (Malaysia's book.)
