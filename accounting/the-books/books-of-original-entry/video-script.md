# Video script — Books of Original Entry

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Before anything reaches the ledger, each transaction is first written, in time order, in a book of original entry — a journal fed by a source document that proves the transaction happened.

Source documents as the documentary evidence every record starts from: what a source document is, that it proves the transaction took place and is filed for reference, and the first of them — the cash receipt (in duplicate, original to the payer; cash register machines and the cash sale slip for spot sales; a cheque and its counterfoil as documents in their own right). The two correcting documents: the debit note, sent by a supplier when the buyer has been undercharged (in effect an additional invoice), and the credit note (traditionally printed in red), sent when the customer's account must be reduced — overcharges, goods returned for wrong quality or damage, and charged packing cases returned. Payment vouchers: where no receipt can be obtained (casual workers, small petty-cash purchases), a voucher signed by the person receiving the money becomes the source document — "vouching" as proving the validity of a transaction, and the petty cash voucher. Why books of original entry exist: a small business can post straight to the ledger, but a growing one must first summarise transactions chronologically in journals (day books, subsidiary books, books of prime entry) fed by the source documents — the ten books listed: sales, sales returns, purchases, purchases returns, cash receipts, cash payments journals, the three-column cash book, petty cash book, analysis cash book and general journal.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
