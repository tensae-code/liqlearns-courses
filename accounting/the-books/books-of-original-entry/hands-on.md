# Hands-on — Books of Original Entry

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A shopkeeper sells on credit and writes the sale in a notebook the same day, before anything else. Why not wait and put it straight into the customer's account at month's end? Write your reason. That notebook — the first record — is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: before anything reaches the ledger, each transaction is first written, in time order, in a book of original entry — a journal fed by a source document that proves the transaction happened. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
