# The Trial Balance

## Do it first

Take six small accounts with their balances — three that owe the business, three the business owes. Add each side. If your two totals differ, something was recorded once instead of twice. Checking the two sides agree is this lesson.

## Trial balance part of the lesson

The trial balance as a statement at a date listing every account's debit balance in one column and credit balance in the other to check the arithmetical accuracy of the ledger, and its purposes (checking double entry was observed, summarising the ledger, facilitating the final accounts).

## Preparing a trial balance; what it proves and does not

The trial balance lists the balanced ledger accounts to check that total debit balances equal total credit balances; agreement does not prove there are no errors, but disagreement locates one.

**The trial balance.** Extracting the trial balance (teacher guide): from the ledger accounts prepared from the Sales Day Book, learners extract a trial balance as at the period end, listing the Sales account balance on the credit side and each receivable's balance (Miyoba, Z. Pimpa, M. Gondwe in the worked example) on the debit side, the two sides agreeing (Zambia's book.)

**The trial balance.** Trial balance after returns (teacher guide): from the returns postings, learners extract a trial balance as at 30 April 2025 in the worked example, with the Sales Returns balance on the debit side and the reduced receivables balances on the credit side, the two sides agreeing (Zambia's book.)
**The trial balance.** The trial balance (neraca saldo): preparing it from the ledger balances and what its agreement does and does not prove (Indonesia's book.)

**The trial balance.** The trial balance and its use: a report generated at the end of the accounting period listing every account and its debit or credit balance; if the two columns agree the books are taken as balanced, if they differ the errors must be found and rectified, and after adjustments the resulting adjusted trial balance is the one used to prepare the financial statements (Tanzania's book.)

**Trial-balance.** The trial balance of the General Ledger accounts (ισοζύγιο): drawn up to control the postings from journal to ledger by totalling every account's debits and credits and comparing the two grand totals (Greece's book.)

**Checking-analytical-ledgers.** Agreement statements (καταστάσεις συμφωνίας) of the analytical ledgers: because general and analytical accounts run in parallel, the totals of the analytical accounts' debits, of their credits, and of their balances must each agree with the corresponding figure of their general account (Greece's book.)

**Month-end-checks.** The month-end checks: a trial balance of the General Ledger is drawn (its debit and credit totals must agree with the Centralised Journal's), the analytical ledgers' trial balances must agree with their general accounts — the check that every analytical account has been updated — and the cash analytical journal doubles as the Cash account itself, so the cash balance can be followed and controlled daily (Greece's book.)

**Final-trial-balance.** The final trial balance and closing the books: after the distribution entries, the Final Trial Balance (οριστικό ισοζύγιο) of the General Ledger shows the operating and results accounts balanced to zero and only balance-sheet accounts carrying balances — it is the year-end balance sheet in another arrangement of the accounts; the year-end balance sheet is drawn from it into the Book of Inventories and Balance Sheets, the balances are transferred out through the Closing Balance Sheet account (89.01), and the books are reopened for the new year (Greece's book.)

**Trial balance part.** The trial balance (Imbangan Duga): the list of debit balances and credit balances taken from the ledger accounts at a particular date, prepared in 'T' form and in columnar form; its functions (checking the accuracy of the double entry and of the arithmetic); and its limits — a balanced trial balance does not show the books are free of error, because some errors do not affect its balance (with the ways of detecting errors in the trial balance, compensating errors among them) (Malaysia's book.)

**Correction part.** Correcting a trial balance (Pembetulan Imbangan Duga): working through a trial balance that contains several errors and putting each right (Malaysia's book.)

**Preparing a trial balance; what it proves.** The trial balance: its main objective is to check the arithmetical accuracy of what was recorded in the journal and posted to the ledger, prepared from the balanced ledger accounts (Bhutan's book.)
