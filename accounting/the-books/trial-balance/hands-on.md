# Hands-on — The Trial Balance

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take six small accounts with their balances — three that owe the business, three the business owes. Add each side. If your two totals differ, something was recorded once instead of twice. Checking the two sides agree is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a trial balance lists every ledger account's debit balance and credit balance at a date; if double entry was kept, the two columns agree — and agreement checks the arithmetic, not everything. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
