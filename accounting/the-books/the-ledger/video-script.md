This lesson is about one thing.
The ledger is the main book of accounts: one account for each item, where every transaction is classified and summarised so the balance of any item can be found at any date.

What the ledger is: the main book of accounts, one account for each item, an account as the T-shaped chronological record of everything affecting that item, with its title, debit (left) and credit (right) sides and the date, particulars, folio and amount columns on each side; ledger pages as folios, and how an account differs from a balance sheet. Recording stock movements: purchases of goods for resale go to the purchases account (not a stock account), sales and the two returns accounts (returns inwards from customers, returns outwards to suppliers) carry the movements, and closing stock comes from physical stock-taking, the stock account showing only that closing balance. Recording the remaining item types in their own accounts: expenses paid (debited to the expense account), revenues received such as commission, rent and discount received (credited to the income account), and drawings of cash or goods (debited to a drawings account; goods drawn are credited to purchases). Balancing an account: total both sides, put the difference on the smaller side as balance carried down so the totals agree, then bring the balance down on the opposite side for the new period — debit balance versus credit balance.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
