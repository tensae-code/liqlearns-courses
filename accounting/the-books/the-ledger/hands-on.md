# Hands-on — The Ledger

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A shop sells to ten customers on credit, buys from four suppliers, and pays rent and wages. At month's end the owner asks: how much does customer number three owe? Where would you look? One page per person and per thing — that place is the ledger.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the ledger is the main book of accounts: one account for each item, where every transaction is classified and summarised so the balance of any item can be found at any date. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
