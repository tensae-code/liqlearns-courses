# The Cash Book

## Do it first

Count the money a small business touches in one day: cash in from sales, cash out for stock, a cheque banked. Now write those movements in two columns — in and out. You have opened a cash book.

## Sub lesson 1 — The cash journals — receipts and payments

**Cash receipts journal.** The Cash Receipts Journal: cash is receipted and banked daily, entered from receipts, cash register tape or bank statement, with a separate GST analysis column; capital, loans and interest carry no GST.

**Cash payments journal.** The Cash Payments Journal: payments are made by cheque or automatic payment, evidenced by cheque butts and bank-statement entries, analysed into frequent columns plus sundry, with GST on most expenses except wages and drawings.

**Cash receipts journal.** The Cash Receipts Journal filled in column by column (date, particulars, receipt number, receipts, bank, sales, sundry), including bank-statement items marked B/S and daily banking totals.

**Cash payments journal.** The Cash Payments Journal: payments are made by cheque or automatic payment so a record exists, and the journal groups them by cheque number, details, bank and analysis columns (drawings, purchases, wages, sundry).

## Sub lesson 2 — The cash book and its forms — columns, contra entries, petty cash

**Cash book part of the lesson.** The cash book as the separate ledger that holds only the cash and bank accounts, kept apart from the general ledger because most transactions pass through cash or bank, because money needs special handling by a responsible person, and so both accounts can sit on one page; the five types named (single-column, two-column, three-column, petty cash book, analysis cash book).

**Cash book part of the lesson.** The single-column cash book: cash account and bank account kept as ordinary ledger accounts on separate pages, one money column on each side, for businesses operating on cash in hand only or mainly through the bank.

**Cash book part of the lesson.** Contra entries: a transaction between the cash and bank accounts themselves (cash banked, or cash withdrawn from the bank for office use) completes its double entry inside the cash book and is marked "C" in the folio column.

**Cash book part of the lesson.** The two-column cash book: the cash and bank accounts side by side on the same page, a cash column and a bank column on both the debit and credit sides.

**Cash book part of the lesson.** The three-column cash book: discount columns added beside the cash and bank columns — discounts allowed on the debit side, discounts received on the credit side; the discount columns are memorandum only (not part of the double entry), their totals are posted periodically to the discounts allowed and discounts received accounts, so the cash book serves as both a ledger and a book of original entry.

**Petty cash part of the lesson.** The petty cash book and the imprest system: small payments (cleaning, postage, staff tea) handled by a petty cashier answerable to the main cashier, payments supported by receipts or signed petty cash vouchers and analysed in columns, and the imprest (cash float) restored each period by reimbursing exactly what was spent so the float stays constant.

**Cash book part of the lesson.** The analysis (analytical) cash book: a cash book with extra columns analysing frequent receipts and payments, used by non-profit organisations and small businesses so the totals of each kind of receipt and expenditure are readily available.

**Single entry bookkeeping.** Single entry bookkeeping: a simple record of income and expenses in a manual cash book or spreadsheet, entering each receipt and payment and carrying a running balance after every entry; the system used by sole traders, charities and clubs (worked example: the cash book of Jameson's Cakes)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the cash book is the business's own record of its cash and bank money — receipts on one side, payments on the other — kept apart because most transactions pass through cash or bank.
