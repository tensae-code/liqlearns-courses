This lesson is about one thing.
The cash book is the business's own record of its cash and bank money — receipts on one side, payments on the other — kept apart because most transactions pass through cash or bank.

The cash book as the separate ledger that holds only the cash and bank accounts, kept apart from the general ledger because most transactions pass through cash or bank, because money needs special handling by a responsible person, and so both accounts can sit on one page; the five types named (single-column, two-column, three-column, petty cash book, analysis cash book). The single-column cash book: cash account and bank account kept as ordinary ledger accounts on separate pages, one money column on each side, for businesses operating on cash in hand only or mainly through the bank. Contra entries: a transaction between the cash and bank accounts themselves (cash banked, or cash withdrawn from the bank for office use) completes its double entry inside the cash book and is marked "C" in the folio column. The two-column cash book: the cash and bank accounts side by side on the same page, a cash column and a bank column on both the debit and credit sides.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
