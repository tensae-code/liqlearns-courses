# Hands-on — The Cash Book

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Count the money a small business touches in one day: cash in from sales, cash out for stock, a cheque banked. Now write those movements in two columns — in and out. You have opened a cash book.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the cash book is the business's own record of its cash and bank money — receipts on one side, payments on the other — kept apart because most transactions pass through cash or bank. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
