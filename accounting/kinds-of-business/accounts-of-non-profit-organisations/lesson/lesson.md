# Accounts of Non-Profit Organisations

## Do it first

Your sports club collects subscriptions, runs a canteen and ends the year with money in the bank. Nobody owns that money as profit. How should the club report its year? A club's own kind of accounts is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The club and its funds — features, the accumulated fund

**Opening part.** Clubs and societies as non-profit organisations: their features and sources of funds.

**Closing part.** The club's statement of financial position and the accumulated fund.

**The accumulated fund.** For a club or incorporated society the equity section is called the Accumulated Fund, members may not withdraw funds, and the year's result is named excess income over expenditure (or the reverse).

**Income and expenditure account.** A club's Statement of Income and Expenditure is its performance statement: income less expenditure gives the excess added to the Accumulated Fund.

**Non-profit organisations' finances.** Community organisations: members own them and take no drawings; incorporation under the Incorporated Societies Act gives limited liability and the power to issue debentures; their financial objectives (cut debt, save for assets, fund a project) are met by fundraising, grants and subscriptions; most keep only a cash book because cash is the asset to control.

**Receipts/payments, income/expenditure and activity statements.** The club's four statements: Statement of Receipts and Payments (= Statement of Changes in Cash Position), Supplementary Trading and Activities Statement for a fundraising activity's surplus, Statement of Income and Expenditure, and Statement of Financial Position with the Accumulated Fund.

## Sub lesson 2 — The club's accounts — receipts and payments, subscriptions, income and expenditure

**Receipts-and-payments part.** The receipts and payments account as the summary of the club's cash book.

**Subscriptions part.** The members' subscription (yuran) account, including subscriptions in arrears and paid in advance.

**Income-and-expenditure part.** The income and expenditure account for a club, adjusted like a profit and loss account.

**Trading-activity part.** The club trading account for a canteen/bar activity, worked inside the income and expenditure treatment (Malaysia's own way: the trading section is prepared before the income and expenditure account).

**Receipts and payments account.** A club's Statement of Change in Cash Position (Receipts and Payments) summarises the year's cash in and out to the closing bank balance.

**Subscriptions in arrears and in advance.** Club balance-day adjustments centre on subscriptions: subscriptions in advance are a current liability and subscriptions in arrears are worked out from membership × annual subscription against cash actually received.

**Accounts of non-profit organisations.** Non-profit organisations and their accounting system: bodies with legal personality whose activity is not trading and which exist for social, sporting, cultural or charitable aims (government bodies, clubs, associations, regional and international organisations); their accounting is mostly cash — all cash operations are recorded in the receipts-and-payments book (the same cash book as a profit… (Sudan's book.)

**Accounts of non-profit organisations.** The income-and-expenditure account: the non-profit's counterpart of the profit-and-loss account, prepared on the same principles — only the year's expenses (debit) and incomes (credit) enter it, amounts prepaid at year end are carried to the balance sheet instead, amounts accrued are added and shown in the balance sheet, and its balance is the surplus of income over expenditure (the accumulated… (Sudan's book.)

**Accounts of non-profit organisations.** The non-profit balance sheet: prepared as a trading organisation's is, its elements gathered from the previous year's balance sheet, the stock of assets, and the capital items inside the receipts-and-payments account (Sudan's book.)

**Accounts of non-profit organisations.** A profit-making activity inside a non-profit body: where the body runs a trading activity (a club buffet, a hotel restaurant), trading and profit-and-loss accounts are prepared for that activity first, then the income-and-expenditure account and the statement of financial position for the whole body (Sudan's book.)

**Non-profit.** Accounting for non-profit organisations: their records and statements in place of profit accounts (Fiji's book.)

**Non-profit part.** Accounts of not-for-profit organisations: why they keep accounts, subscriptions and the accumulated fund, the receipts and payments account and the income and expenditure account (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Introduction part.** Non-profit organisations introduced: what they are (Sri Lanka's book.)

**Non-profit-statements part.** The financial statements of non-profit organisations (receipts/payments and income/expenditure based) (Sri Lanka's book.)
