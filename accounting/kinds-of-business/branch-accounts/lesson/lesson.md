# Branch Accounts

## Do it first

A shopkeeper opens a second shop across town. The first shop — the head office — sends it goods, and the second shop sells them and sends money back. At the end of the year the owner asks: did the new shop make a profit? The head office's books must be able to answer that, even though the selling happened somewhere else. Keeping the accounts between a head office and its branches is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Branches and branch accounting

**Branches and branch accounting.** A branch is a business operating unit away from the head office, established as the business grows to increase visibility and accessibility. Branch accounting keeps the record between the head office and each branch so the branch's own result can be found. The lesson weighs the relevance and advantages of branch accounting, and its limitations — including the current question whether branches are still needed when the internet facilitates e-commerce (Tanzania's book).

## Sub lesson 2 — Non-autonomous (integrated) branches

**Non-autonomous (integrated) branches.** Some branches keep no full double-entry system of their own; their accounting is integrated into the head office's books. The head office records the transactions between itself and the branch and prepares the branch account in its own books to determine branch profit — under the debtors method, or under the stock-and-debtors method, which keeps fuller control accounts for the branch's stock and debtors (Tanzania's book).

## Sub lesson 3 — Autonomous branches and items in transit

**Autonomous (independent) branches.** Big branches keep their own books. The branch then treats the head office through a head-office current account, while the head office treats the branch through a branch current account, and every inter-branch transaction is recorded in both sets of books (Tanzania's book).

**Items in transit.** Goods or cash sent between the head office and a branch may still be on the way at the period end — recorded by the sender, not yet received by the other side. Items in transit are adjusted for at the period end so the two current accounts agree (Tanzania's book).
