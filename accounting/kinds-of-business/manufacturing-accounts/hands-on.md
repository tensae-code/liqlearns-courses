# Hands-on — Manufacturing Accounts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A furniture maker buys timber, pays carpenters and runs machines, then sells chairs. Before he can know his profit he must know what one chair cost to make. Building up the cost of production is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a maker must first find what production cost: direct materials, direct labour, direct expenses and factory overheads are gathered in a manufacturing account, and the cost of production passes to the trading account. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
