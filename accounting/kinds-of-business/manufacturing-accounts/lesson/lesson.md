# Manufacturing Accounts

## Do it first

A furniture maker buys timber, pays carpenters and runs machines, then sells chairs. Before he can know his profit he must know what one chair cost to make. Building up the cost of production is this lesson.

## Cost-classification part

Types of cost and work in progress: direct material, direct labour, direct expenses, factory overheads and prime cost.

## Main part

Preparing the manufacturing account in both T format and statement format and transferring cost of production to the trading account.

**How a manufacturer's transactions flow into the accounts.** The manufacturing company's accounting business process in its own order: buying raw and auxiliary materials and paying production labour, paying other production and operating costs, selling the finished goods, documenting every financial transaction and recording it (Indonesia's book.)

**Manufacturing accounts.** The operating account (حساب التشغيل): the final account of a manufacturing business that gathers every cost of making the goods — opening raw materials and work in progress, raw-material purchases, factory wages, motive power, factory rent, water, factory salaries and fire insurance — against closing raw materials and work in progress, to extract the cost of the goods manufactured, which passes… (Sudan's book.)

**Manufacturing.** Manufacturing statements: the statement that works out a manufacturer's cost of production (Fiji's book.)

**Job-costing.** Product costing by job order costing: collecting the costs of each job to price and control it (Fiji's book.)

**Cost-classification part.** Cost concepts for management accounting: cost unit and cost centre; classification by function (production/period), by element (direct/indirect), by behaviour (fixed/variable), and for decisions (relevant, sunk, opportunity, controllable) (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Overhead-absorption part.** Overhead cost: production vs non-production overheads, prime cost, and the distribution process — collection, allocation, apportionment (primary and secondary distribution) and absorption by an absorption rate to find cost per unit (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Manufacturing part.** Manufacturing accounts: calculating the cost of production (prime cost and the manufacturing account, incl. work in progress) and taking decisions from a manufacturer's financial statements (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Introduction part.** Production cost introduced: what production cost is (Sri Lanka's book.)

**Classification part.** Classifying production cost (direct/indirect, factory overheads) (Sri Lanka's book.)

**Cost-statement part.** Preparing the production cost statement for a period (worked through a furniture manufacturer's chair production) (Sri Lanka's book.)
