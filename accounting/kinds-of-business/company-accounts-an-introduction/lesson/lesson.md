# Company Accounts — an Introduction

## Do it first

A partnership grows and becomes a company. Instead of two owners' capital accounts there are now many shareholders, each holding shares. What is a share, and how is the owners' part of a company shown? That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shares — what they are, their kinds, and share capital

**Shares part of the lesson.** Share capital in its stages: authorised/registered, issued, called-up and paid-up capital, and how paid-up capital plus reserves make the networth.

**Shares part of the lesson.** Shares and stock: what a share is, and ordinary versus preference shares compared on dividends, assets on liquidation and voting rights.

**Shares part of the lesson.** The kinds of preference shares: convertible, cumulative, participating and redeemable.

## Sub lesson 2 — The company's equity, dividends and debt

**Opening part.** Introduction to the limited company: features and the private (Sendirian) versus public (Awam) company in outline.

**Share-issue part.** Types of capital and share issue: ordinary and preference shares, issue at par and at a premium, and the journal and ledger entries for an issue.

**Equity part.** The owners' equity of a limited company as presented in its statement of financial position.

**Dividends part.** Cash dividends: declaring and paying dividends on ordinary and preference shares and the entries involved.

**Debentures part of the lesson.** Debt instruments: bonds and debentures as securitised loans, how they differ from shares (interest versus dividends, maturity, priority on winding up), government bonds (FGN bonds, gilts), and types of corporate debt.

**Ownership forms compared (accounting view).** Samoa's way of comparing the four ownership forms side by side — sole trader, partnership, company, incorporated society — across owners, separate legal entity, source of finance, audit, who runs it, how owners receive profits and what document sets it up.

**Shares and the share register.** Forming a company from a partnership: shares issued at a set price for the partners' equity, a share register recording each shareholder's shares and amount paid up, including partly-paid shares with uncalled capital.

**Company equity section.** The company Statement of Financial Position: Shareholders' Equity shows issued and paid-up capital less uncalled capital; the business name gains "Ltd" and shareholders' names are not shown.

**Dividends and the solvency test.** Dividends are worked out per share on the paid-up portion only (a partly-paid shareholder gets a proportionally smaller dividend), and a company may pay dividends only if it passes the two-part solvency test in the Companies Act.

**Retained earnings.** The profit not paid out as dividends stays in the business as retained earnings inside Shareholders' Equity.

**Company accounts.** The share and its values: a share is a certificate representing a part of a capital company's capital, transferable by sale, gift, will or inheritance; its nominal value (printed on the share), issue value (never below nominal; above it only by the general assembly's decision), market value (the price it trades at on the securities market on a day), book value (its share of the company's net… (Sudan's book.)

**Company accounts.** The kinds of shares and the accounting treatment of a joint-stock company's capital: ordinary and preference shares and what each carries, then how the capital of joint-stock companies is treated in the accounts (Sudan's book.)

**Company accounts.** The financial statements of a company: unlike a sole proprietorship, a company (a public company above all) prepares a complete set — statement of financial position, statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, statement of cash flows and statement of changes in equity — with the notes to the financial statements (disclosure notes) presenting the basis of preparation and the… (Tanzania's book.)

**Types of financial statements.** The types of financial statements: the income statement (statement of profit or loss) reporting performance over a period, presented by function of expense with real Bhutanese company examples (NRDCL), beside the statement of financial position (Bhutan's book.)

**Financial instruments part.** Financial instruments: a contract that gives rise to a financial asset of one entity and a financial liability or equity instrument of another, with the entries for a bond held to maturity (interest on the financial asset, repayment at maturity) (Bhutan's book.)

**Debt finance part.** Raising debt finance in Bhutan's own market: the Dungsam Cement Corporation's third series bond of Nu. 1.5 billion at an 8% coupon rate, and the loan-account entries (drawdown, repayment split between finance cost and liability) (Bhutan's book.)

**Reserves part.** Reserves: created by transferring profits, governed by the company's financial-management policies and attitude to risk, and not available to pay dividends to shareholders (Bhutan's book.)

**Bonus shares and presentation part.** Bonus shares: issuing bonus shares to keep up the company's dividend culture (worked from a company that raised Nu. 200 million as 20 million shares at Nu. 10), and the presentation of equity and debt finance in the financial statements — paid-up share capital, reserves and retained earnings under the equity heading (Bhutan's book.)

**Adjusted trial balance part.** Preparing a limited company's statements: writing up the adjusted trial balance from the trial balance, with the adjusting entries recapitulated (accruals — accrued income and accrued expenses — under the accrual basis the framework requires) (Bhutan's book.)

**Share disclosures part.** What a company discloses about its shares: the number of shares authorised and the number issued, shown in the statement of financial position, the statement of changes in equity or the notes (Bhutan's book.)

**Changes in equity part.** The statement of changes in equity: accumulated profit brought forward, plus profit after tax, less dividend paid, gives the accumulated profit carried forward (Bhutan's book.)

**Liability.** Strand 1: shareholders' liability — limited by shares and limited by guarantee; when shareholders and directors become personally liable (trading while insolvent, disposing of assets below value, fraud, an overdrawn director's loan account, illegal dividends); the liquidator's role; liability for tax debts (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Dividends.** Strand 2 Unit 2: dividends — cash dividends against stock dividends against stock splits, and their effects on the balance sheet (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Opening.** The nature of company business: what a company is as a form of organisation (Fiji's book.)

**Formation.** Formation of companies: bringing a company into existence and issuing its capital (Fiji's book.)

**Annual-accounts.** The annual accounts of companies: the published statements a company prepares (Fiji's book.)

**Equity.** Sources of finance and the balance sheet of a limited company: shareholders' funds (Nigeria's book.)

**Balance-sheet.** Sources of finance and the balance sheet: liabilities and external borrowing, and the balance sheet itself (Nigeria's book.)

**Company-background part.** The legal background of limited liability companies: Companies Act No. 7 of 2007, classification of companies (limited by shares/guarantee, private, public, off-shore, foreign), types of shares (ordinary, preference, special) and stated capital (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Share-issue part.** Issuing shares for cash collected at once: IPO vs secondary offer, the share-issue and stated-capital journal entries, and pro-rata allotment with refunds when applications exceed the issue (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Rights-and-bonus-issues part.** Increasing share capital without new outside money and with it: rights issues, and reserve capitalisation (bonus issues) from revaluation reserve, general reserve and retained earnings (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Company-statements part.** Preparing a limited company's financial statements: statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, statement of financial position, statement of changes in equity, accounting policies and notes, under the Companies Act, the standards and the Inland Revenue Act (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**The 2018 guide expressly excludes instalment collection from the syllabus; this is the earlier edition's extra.** Issuing shares when the consideration is collected in instalments — on application, on allotment and on calls under s.55 Companies Act, with rejection/refund and pro-rata allocation of excess applications and setting excess application money against allotment/calls (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Absent from the 2018 guide.** Shares in arrears and their resale: the company's lien on shares whose call money is unpaid, the board's power to resell after due notice, deducting arrears, interest and resale expenses from the proceeds, paying the balance (capped at what he paid) to the original holder and transferring the remainder to stated capital (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)
