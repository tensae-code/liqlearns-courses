# Zakat Accounting

## Do it first

A trader you know keeps good books all year. At the end of the year he wants to pay his zakat — the share of his wealth that the sharia sets aside for its proper channels. Looking at his books, which figures does he need: everything he owns, or only some of it? What must come off first before he works out what is due? That working-out — done from the accounts — is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Zakat accounting as its own branch

**Zakat accounting as its own branch.** Zakat accounting is its own branch of accounting: it determines and values the wealth and the income on which zakat is due, measures the amount due, and distributes its proceeds to its channels, all under principles derived from the sharia. It is not tax accounting: zakat deducts all debts and costs before anything is computed, it falls on Muslims only, it excludes wealth from unlawful sources, it exempts the family's basic needs uniformly, it excludes gains that have not been held a full lunar year, and it works at fixed proportional rates. Two measures govern every computation in this lesson: the **nisab**, the minimum amount of wealth at which zakat becomes due, and the **hawl**, the full lunar year the wealth must be held (Sudan's book).

## Sub lesson 2 — Zakat on trade goods

**Zakat on trade goods.** Zakat on trade goods (urud al-tijara) covers buying and selling for gain, brokerage between merchants, hiring out trade goods and non-usurious money-changing. It is an annual zakat on the income realised through the lunar year, both in-kind and personal in character. The base is built from the trader's own books: current assets — stock, debtors, bills receivable and investments — less current liabilities, with inherited money, basic-needs costs and personal debts treated as the worked examples direct; the result is then tested against the nisab, and zakat is due only when the base reaches it (Sudan's book).

## Sub lesson 3 — Zakat on salaries, income-producing assets and crops

**Zakat on salaries and wages.** For a salary or wage earner the base is the gross salary over the year, less pension and social-insurance deductions, the taxpayer's debts and the cost of basic needs; what remains is compared with the year's nisab (Sudan's book).

**Zakat on income-producing assets, crops and fruits.** For income-producing assets (al-mustaghallat) — rented property, factories, farms, animal products and transport — the base is the gross yield less costs and expenses, the depreciation of the exploited assets, debts due and the cost of basic needs. For crops and fruits, the base and the zakat due are determined by their own rules (Sudan's book).
