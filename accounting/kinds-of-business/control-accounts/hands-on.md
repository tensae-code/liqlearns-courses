# Hands-on — Control Accounts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A business has two hundred customers who owe it money. The owner wants one number: how much is owed in total — and proof it is right. One account holding the total of all the personal accounts is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: control accounts hold the totals of the many personal accounts: the sales and purchases ledger control balances must agree with the schedules of debtors and creditors, and only the control balances enter the trial balance. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
