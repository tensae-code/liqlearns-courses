# Control Accounts

## Do it first

A business has two hundred customers who owe it money. The owner wants one number: how much is owed in total — and proof it is right. One account holding the total of all the personal accounts is this lesson.

## Control accounts and schedules

Posting a full month from every journal into the subsidiary ledgers and the general-ledger Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable Control Accounts; the schedules of receivables and payables must agree with the control balances, and only control balances enter the trial balance.

**Control part.** Control accounts (Akaun Kawalan): the summary of the receivables in the Sales Ledger and of the payables in the Purchases Ledger, kept so the company can check the ledger totals quickly; the formats of the Receivables Control Account and the Payables Control Account and their building from the books of prime entry; agreeing the control account with the individual personal accounts; and the set-off between the two ledgers when a business both buys from and sells to the same party (Malaysia's book.)

**Debtors and creditors.** Strand 2 Unit 2: accounts receivable and accounts payable and their management — credit policies, shortening transaction cycles, automation (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Credit-records.** Accounting for credit transactions: keeping the receivables and payables records that control credit buying and selling (Fiji's book.)

**Control-accounts part.** Control accounts and subsidiary ledgers: debtors and creditors subsidiary ledgers prepared, their relationship to the general ledger, and comparing control account balances with the lists of balances (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)
