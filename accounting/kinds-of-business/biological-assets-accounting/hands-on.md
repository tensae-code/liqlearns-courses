# Hands-on — Biological Assets

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take a real business you know — a shop, a farm, a workshop. Ask the question this lesson answers about it, and write down what you would need to look at first. That looking is where this lesson begins.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: biological assets are the living plants and animals a business holds — accounted for as their own topic under BAS 41, with bearer plants set apart from consumable assets, and shown as their own line in the statement of financial position, split current and non-current. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
