# Incomplete Records

## Do it first

A trader kept no proper books — only a notebook of cash and a pile of invoices — yet the tax office still asks: what was your profit? Starting from what he owns and owes at the start and end, could you find it? That rescue work is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What incomplete records are

**Opening part.** What incomplete records are, why traders keep them (single entry, missing books) and the two recovery methods the book teaches.

**Personal statement of affairs.** A personal Statement of Affairs (statement of net worth) lists an individual's assets and liabilities to find net worth, and sits beside a weekly personal budget of fixed and variable expenses; both are the statements a bank asks for with a loan application.

## Sub lesson 2 — Finding the profit — comparison and analysis methods

**Statement-of-affairs part.** The comparison method: opening and closing statements of affairs and working out profit from the movement in capital, adjusted for drawings and extra capital.

**Reconstruction part.** The analysis method: rebuilding sales, purchases and cash figures through control accounts and cash analysis so full final accounts can be prepared.

**Incomplete records.** Completing the picture from an opening balance sheet and total accounts: given a business's opening balance sheet and the year's total (control) accounts — total bank, total purchases, total sales, total creditors, total debtors, bills and the rest — the learner completes the trial balance and rebuilds the final accounts for a business whose records are incomplete (Sudan's book.)

**Personal statement of affairs.** A personal Statement of Affairs prepared for a bank loan application: listing personal assets and liabilities to find net worth, taken to the bank together with a budget (Samoa's book.)
