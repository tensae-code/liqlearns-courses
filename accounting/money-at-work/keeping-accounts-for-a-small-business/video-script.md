# Video script — Keeping Accounts for a Small Business

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A small business keeps its accounts by knowing what counts as a business transaction, keeping its documents as proof, classifying its spending, and budgeting — simple, accessible and up to date.

Personal financial documents must be kept as proof of payment and for tax, stored accessibly, simply and up to date (binder, box or cabinet). What counts as a transaction for a sole trader (money or goods moving in or out of the business, not the owner's private spending), opening the Sagone Car Repairs case that the rest of the book follows. Personal expenditure split into essential and non-essential, with the Samoan teaching point that the split depends on the person's values (church donations counted essential in the Taito family budget). Budgeting for a big one-off family event: list income, list essential and non-essential expenditure, make the budget balance with a surplus if possible — worked through an extended-family wedding budget where six family groups share the contribution.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
