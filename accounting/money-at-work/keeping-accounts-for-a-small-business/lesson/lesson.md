# Keeping Accounts for a Small Business

## Do it first

A sole trader buys stock for the shop and shoes for his child on the same day, from the same purse. Which of the two belongs in the business's books? Sorting the business's transactions from the owner's private ones is where this lesson starts.

## Keeping and storing documents

Personal financial documents must be kept as proof of payment and for tax, stored accessibly, simply and up to date (binder, box or cabinet).

## Transactions in a small business

What counts as a transaction for a sole trader (money or goods moving in or out of the business, not the owner's private spending), opening the Sagone Car Repairs case that the rest of the book follows.

## Personal expenditure classification

Personal expenditure split into essential and non-essential, with the Samoan teaching point that the split depends on the person's values (church donations counted essential in the Taito family budget).

## Personal/event budgeting (Samoa's family way)

Budgeting for a big one-off family event: list income, list essential and non-essential expenditure, make the budget balance with a surplus if possible — worked through an extended-family wedding budget where six family groups share the contribution.
