# Hands-on — Keeping Accounts for a Small Business

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A sole trader buys stock for the shop and shoes for his child on the same day, from the same purse. Which of the two belongs in the business's books? Sorting the business's transactions from the owner's private ones is where this lesson starts.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a small business keeps its accounts by knowing what counts as a business transaction, keeping its documents as proof, classifying its spending, and budgeting — simple, accessible and up to date. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
