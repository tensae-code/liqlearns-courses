# Budgeting and Variance Analysis

## Do it first

Take a real business you know — a shop, a farm, a workshop. Ask the question this lesson answers about it, and write down what you would need to look at first. That looking is where this lesson begins.

## Sub lesson 1 — The budgeting process and the operating budgets

**Opening part of the proposed lesson.** The budgeting process: a business requires proper budgeting, and managers must predict the factors affecting the business with reasonable precision, with problem-solving, organisational and monitoring skills (Bhutan's book.)

**Budgets part of the proposed lesson.** The operating budgets worked: the production budget built from the sales budget in units (sales + desired ending inventory = total required), and the selling and administration expense budget (Bhutan's book.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Standard costing and variance analysis

**Variance part of the proposed lesson.** Standard costing and variance analysis: the master budget as a static budget, unethical practices in budgeting named as a danger, and the variances calculated — labour efficiency variance = standard rate × (actual hours − standard hours) (Bhutan's book.)

**Material variances part of the proposed lesson.** The direct material variances: material price variance and material usage variance, favourable against unfavourable, worked from the month's figures (Bhutan's book.)
