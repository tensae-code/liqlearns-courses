# Hands-on — Capital Investment Appraisal

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take a real business you know — a shop, a farm, a workshop. Ask the question this lesson answers about it, and write down what you would need to look at first. That looking is where this lesson begins.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: capital investment appraisal is how a business judges a long-term project before committing the money: estimate the project's initial, operating and terminal cash flows, then test them with payback, the accounting rate of return and net present value. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
