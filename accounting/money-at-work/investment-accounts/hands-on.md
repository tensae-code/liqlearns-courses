# Hands-on — Investment Accounts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Rule a page into three columns — nominal value, capital, income. Buy an imaginary holding of shares on paper, receive a dividend, then sell half the holding, entering each item in its proper column. That separation of capital from income is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: an investment account records a holding of shares or fixed-interest securities in three columns — nominal value, capital and income — so the cost of the holding, the income it earns and the profit or loss on selling it are never mixed up. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
