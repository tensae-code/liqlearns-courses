# Investment Accounts

## Do it first

A business with spare cash buys shares in another company, and later sells some of them. The shares cost more than their face value, a dividend arrives part-way through the year, and the books must show what was capital and what was income. One special account keeps all of that straight — the investment account. How it works is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Investments and financial assets

**Investments and financial assets.** An investment is an asset acquired to generate more money than its current amount — the extra income compensating for time and inflation. Among the major categories of investments stand the financial assets: an asset whose value derives from a contractual claim, evidenced by a certificate, computer record, receipt or legal document rather than physical worth — treasury instruments, corporate bonds promising periodic interest, preference shares. The part closes with why financial-asset investment matters, to the investor and to the government (Tanzania's book).

## Sub lesson 2 — Buying and selling financial assets; the investment account for shares

**Buying and selling financial assets — the investment account for shares.** The procedures for purchasing and selling financial assets in Tanzania come first; then the accounting treatment of investment in shares. The investment account is a three-column account — nominal value, capital and income columns on each side — and the full cost of an investment is its price plus the additional expenses of acquiring it, recorded in the capital column (Tanzania's book).

## Sub lesson 3 — Quotations, fixed-interest securities and year-end entries

**Quotations, fixed-interest securities and year-end entries.** Shares are bought and sold under cum-div and ex-div quotations: under ex-div the buyer pays the seller the accrued dividend to the transaction date on top of the quoted price, recording the price and costs in the capital column and the accrued dividend in the income column. Fixed-interest securities are bought and sold the same way, under cum-interest and ex-interest quotations. At the year end, accrued interest and dividend income is established when the books close on a date that does not match the payment dates (Tanzania's book).
