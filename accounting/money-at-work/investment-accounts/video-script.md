This lesson is about one thing.
An investment account records a holding of shares or fixed-interest securities in three columns — nominal value, capital and income — so the cost of the holding, the income it earns and the profit or loss on selling it are never mixed up.

The investment account keeps three columns on each side: nominal value, capital and income. Price and buying costs go in the capital column; dividends and interest go in the income column.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
