# Payroll and Payslips

## Do it first

Look at any worker's pay slip — or imagine one. The pay at the top is not the pay at the bottom. List what could be added (overtime) and what could be taken off (tax) between the two. Reading that slip is this lesson.

## Payslips and net pay

How a pay advice slip works: wages from an hourly rate, salary divided from an annual amount, and deductions (PAYE tax, National Provident Fund) taken from gross pay to reach net pay.

## Payslips and overtime

The pay advice slip's required contents (employee and employer names, employee number, pay period, rate, gross pay, deductions, net pay) and how overtime at 1.5× and 2× the hourly rate is worked out from an annual salary.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a payslip shows how pay is built: hours at a rate (with overtime at one-and-a-half or double) make gross pay, deductions such as tax come off, and what is left is net pay. Give one real example from your own home, market or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: payslips and net pay, payslips and overtime. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a payslip shows how pay is built: hours at a rate (with overtime at one-and-a-half or double) make gross pay, deductions such as tax come off, and what is left is net pay.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
