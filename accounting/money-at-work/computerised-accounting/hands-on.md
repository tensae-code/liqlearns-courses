# Hands-on — Computerised Accounting

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A bookkeeper posts a month of sales by hand: a day of writing. A computer does it in minutes — but only if each document is coded to the right account first. What the computer does, and what the human must still do, is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: computers process accounting the same way hands do, only faster: source documents are coded by the chart of accounts, and accounting packages produce the ledgers, trial balance, reconciliations and statements. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
