# Computerised Accounting

## Do it first

A bookkeeper posts a month of sales by hand: a day of writing. A computer does it in minutes — but only if each document is coded to the right account first. What the computer does, and what the human must still do, is this lesson.

## Computers in accounting (Samoa's cost/resources way of framing it)

Manual vs computer processing: hardware (CPU, input, storage, output) and software (systems and application), electronic data processing coding source documents by the chart of accounts, and accounting packages producing ledgers, trial balance, GST returns, reconciliations and statements.

## Computerised accounting software

QuickBooks as a specialist accounting application: it automates bookkeeping (entering transactions and calculations), sales tax on invoices, bill tracking and online bill payment, financial reports (cash flows, profits, balance sheets, money owed), invoicing and invoice tracking, estimates and quotes, and receipt scanning from a phone; desktop vs cloud access; advantages (single data entry, speed, fewer errors, less labour, integration with online banking) against disadvantages (cost, set-up and training, technical failures, dependence on accurate input)

**Computerised accounting.** The development of computer accounting applications in business: accounting software on desktop, web and mobile platforms takes over the manual recording-to-reporting work, and the common Indonesian packages are named (Indonesia's book.)

**Computerised accounting.** Operating a spreadsheet package for accounting work: what spreadsheet software is and does for a company (tables, calculations, company reports, charts, payroll and fixed-asset lists, databases), the common packages, and the working parts of the worksheet — rows, columns, cells, formula bar (Indonesia's book.)

**Computerised accounting.** Processing data by character, formula and function: the four data types (text, numeric, date/time, formula), number formatting, arithmetic operators, and the mathematical, statistical, logic, lookup and absolute-reference functions used on accounting data (Indonesia's book.)

**Computerised accounting.** Tables and diagrams from accounting data: building the table first, then the chart types (column, line, pie, bar, area) and how a business reads its progress from them (Indonesia's book.)

**Computerised accounting.** Producing financial reports with the spreadsheet application: the chapter's project carries a company's data through the spreadsheet into finished financial reports (Indonesia's book.)

**Computerised accounting.** The guide's essential-material list for the spreadsheet chapter: the definition, functions and kinds of spreadsheet applications, the kinds of data character, formulas and functions, and the making of tables, diagrams and financial reports are the chapter's teachable core, with the accounting cycle already learned as its prerequisite (Indonesia's teacher guide.)

**Computerised accounting.** The accounting information system (AIS): a system has inputs, processing and outputs, and the AIS is the grouping of interrelated components and activities that takes accounting data in, processes it and issues accounting information — its contents and main functions (Tanzania's book.)

**Computerised-accounting.** Computerised accounting (μηχανογράφηση / μηχανογραφημένη λογιστική): what computerisation of a business's information handling is, what an automated accounting office is, the tools it needs, and its advantages — speed of recording, fast and reliable production of information, less working time, order in the records, an upgraded role for the accountant, and information the management can actually use — together with the basic principles of a general-ledger information system: the principles of accounting itself (double entry and the Ε.Γ.Λ.Σ.), the principle of keeping the laws, and the principle of functionality (Greece's book.)

**Using-accounting-software.** Setting up and running a general-ledger software package, step by step: first defining the parameters — creating the company, the journals, the document types and the accounts (each account characterised, e.g. expense/revenue accounts to be zeroed into the year's results, customer/supplier accounts feeding the tax office's ΚΕΠΥΟ invoice statements) and defining the accounting period; then entering the accounting articles, taking the printouts (journals, analytical ledgers, trial balances, ΚΕΠΥΟ, VAT returns) and doing the periodic work (opening/closing inventory, closing the period, opening the new year, producing the economic result) (Greece's book.)
