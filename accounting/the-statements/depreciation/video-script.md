This lesson is about one thing.
A long-life asset loses its power to serve year by year, so its cost less its residual value is spread as an expense over its useful life.

Depreciation spreads an asset's cost less residual value over its useful life because the asset loses future service potential; it is an expense based on historical cost. Straight-line method: the same amount each year from (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life, entered by General Journal, with accumulated depreciation as a contra-asset deducted from cost to give book value; a depreciation schedule tracks all three figures year by year. Diminishing (reducing) balance method: a fixed rate on the falling book value, suiting assets like computers that lose value fastest early.
