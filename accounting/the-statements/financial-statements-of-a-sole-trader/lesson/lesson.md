# Financial Statements of a Sole Trader

## Do it first

A trader asks three questions at year's end: Did I make a gross profit? After all expenses, a net profit? And what do I own and owe today? Three questions, three statements. Naming which statement answers which is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The trading account — gross profit

**Income statement part of the lesson.** The final accounts as a set (trading account, profit and loss account and balance sheet, also called financial statements), and the concept of a trading period/accounting period — the span at whose end accounts are balanced, a trial balance extracted, profit or loss determined and a balance sheet prepared; most businesses use one year, the government's being the financial/fiscal year.

**Trading account part of the lesson.** Determining profit or loss before any account is drawn: gross profit as sales minus cost of goods sold, cost of goods sold built from opening stock plus purchases less closing stock, and the adjustments — returns inwards deducted from sales, returns outwards from purchases, carriage inwards added to purchases as part of cost, carriage outwards treated as an expense.

**Trading account part of the lesson.** The trading account itself: the account form of the gross-profit calculation, its title carrying the business name and the period, gross profit or gross loss carried down.

**Income statement part of the lesson.** Telling revenue expenditure and receipts from capital expenditure and receipts, and which items belong in the trading account and which in the profit and loss account.

**Trading account part of the lesson.** Gross profit as sales minus cost of sales, cost of sales built from opening stock plus purchases minus closing stock, and the format of the trading account.

## Sub lesson 2 — The profit and loss account and the balance sheet

**Balance sheet part of the lesson.** The balance sheet as a statement of the financial position (assets, capital and liabilities) at a particular date, the idea of an accounting/trading period, and the format: heading with business name, statement name and date, assets on one side and capital plus liabilities on the other, the two totals equal.

**Balance sheet part of the lesson.** The classified balance sheet (fixed then current assets; capital, long-term then current liabilities), items arranged in order of permanency or in order of liquidity, a business solvent when assets exceed liabilities and insolvent (deficiency) when they do not, and who uses a balance sheet and why.

**Profit and loss part of the lesson.** The second step: net profit or net loss found by adding all revenues to the gross profit and deducting all expenses, and the profit and loss account as the account that does this.

**Balance sheet part of the lesson.** The balance sheet drawn after the final accounts (T format shown), with capital adjusted before it is entered: net profit added, a loss subtracted, drawings subtracted and additional capital added.

**Contents part.** The items that appear in the income statement (Penyata Pendapatan) and the statement of financial position.

**Profit and loss part of the lesson.** Net profit worked out from gross profit by adding other incomes and deducting the remaining expenses in the profit and loss account.

**Revenue, expenses and the two kinds of expenditure.** Profit is revenue less expenses for a set period; revenue and expense defined by their effect on owner's equity, and capital expenditure (buying an asset to keep) separated from revenue expenditure.

**The income statement.** The Statement of Financial Performance calculates profit for a period under the going-concern, accounting-period and matching concepts, classified differently for service and trading businesses.

**The worksheet route to final statements.** Samoa's way of finishing the statements: a worksheet carries the trial balance through the balance-day adjustments to an adjusted trial balance before the classified performance statement (selling, administrative expense groups) and position statement are drawn up.

**The income statement (trading and service).** The Statement of Financial Performance for a trading business: sales less returns, cost of goods sold built from opening inventory, purchases less returns out and carriage inwards, closing inventory, giving gross profit, then classified expenses to net profit; the service-business form shown beside it.

**The final statements and their users.** The statements a sole proprietor must complete each year and who uses them (owner, bank for lending/overdraft, Inland Revenue for tax, a prospective buyer): Statement of Accounting Policies, Statement of Financial Performance, Statement of Financial Position and Statement of Changes in Cash Position.
