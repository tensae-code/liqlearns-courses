# Financial Statements of a Sole Trader

## Do it first

A trader asks three questions at year's end: Did I make a gross profit? After all expenses, a net profit? And what do I own and owe today? Three questions, three statements. Naming which statement answers which is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The trading account — gross profit

**Income statement part of the lesson.** The final accounts as a set (trading account, profit and loss account and balance sheet, also called financial statements), and the concept of a trading period/accounting period — the span at whose end accounts are balanced, a trial balance extracted, profit or loss determined and a balance sheet prepared; most businesses use one year, the government's being the financial/fiscal year.

**Trading account part of the lesson.** Determining profit or loss before any account is drawn: gross profit as sales minus cost of goods sold, cost of goods sold built from opening stock plus purchases less closing stock, and the adjustments — returns inwards deducted from sales, returns outwards from purchases, carriage inwards added to purchases as part of cost, carriage outwards treated as an expense.

**Trading account part of the lesson.** The trading account itself: the account form of the gross-profit calculation, its title carrying the business name and the period, gross profit or gross loss carried down.

**Income statement part of the lesson.** Telling revenue expenditure and receipts from capital expenditure and receipts, and which items belong in the trading account and which in the profit and loss account.

**Trading account part of the lesson.** Gross profit as sales minus cost of sales, cost of sales built from opening stock plus purchases minus closing stock, and the format of the trading account.

## Sub lesson 2 — The profit and loss account and the balance sheet

**Balance sheet part of the lesson.** The balance sheet as a statement of the financial position (assets, capital and liabilities) at a particular date, the idea of an accounting/trading period, and the format: heading with business name, statement name and date, assets on one side and capital plus liabilities on the other, the two totals equal.

**Balance sheet part of the lesson.** The classified balance sheet (fixed then current assets; capital, long-term then current liabilities), items arranged in order of permanency or in order of liquidity, a business solvent when assets exceed liabilities and insolvent (deficiency) when they do not, and who uses a balance sheet and why.

**Profit and loss part of the lesson.** The second step: net profit or net loss found by adding all revenues to the gross profit and deducting all expenses, and the profit and loss account as the account that does this.

**Balance sheet part of the lesson.** The balance sheet drawn after the final accounts (T format shown), with capital adjusted before it is entered: net profit added, a loss subtracted, drawings subtracted and additional capital added.

**Contents part.** The items that appear in the income statement (Penyata Pendapatan) and the statement of financial position.

**Profit and loss part of the lesson.** Net profit worked out from gross profit by adding other incomes and deducting the remaining expenses in the profit and loss account.

**Revenue, expenses and the two kinds of expenditure.** Profit is revenue less expenses for a set period; revenue and expense defined by their effect on owner's equity, and capital expenditure (buying an asset to keep) separated from revenue expenditure.

**The income statement.** The Statement of Financial Performance calculates profit for a period under the going-concern, accounting-period and matching concepts, classified differently for service and trading businesses.

**The worksheet route to final statements.** Samoa's way of finishing the statements: a worksheet carries the trial balance through the balance-day adjustments to an adjusted trial balance before the classified performance statement (selling, administrative expense groups) and position statement are drawn up.

**The income statement (trading and service).** The Statement of Financial Performance for a trading business: sales less returns, cost of goods sold built from opening inventory, purchases less returns out and carriage inwards, closing inventory, giving gross profit, then classified expenses to net profit; the service-business form shown beside it.

**The final statements and their users.** The statements a sole proprietor must complete each year and who uses them (owner, bank for lending/overdraft, Inland Revenue for tax, a prospective buyer): Statement of Accounting Policies, Statement of Financial Performance, Statement of Financial Position and Statement of Changes in Cash Position.

**The income statement.** The income statement: prepared at the end of each 12-month trading period to calculate and monitor profit or loss (profit = total revenue − total costs), compared with previous periods to judge the business's health; it shows how well costs are controlled, and profitable statements attract investors and lenders; why holding a lot of cash does not by itself mean a profit

**The statements.** The income statement (estado de resultados): a financial report for a set period that shows in detail the income obtained, the expenses as they occur and the resulting profit or loss, so the information can be analysed and decisions taken; it identifies sales income (cash or credit) and the costs and expenses, to calculate the period's gain (Nicaragua's book.)

**The statements.** The structure of the income statement: (Nicaragua's book.)
**Financial statements of a sole trader.** The service company's financial statements: income statement, statement of changes in equity and statement of financial position prepared from the worksheet (Indonesia's book.)

**Financial statements of a sole trader.** Cost of goods sold (harga pokok penjualan): its components — opening inventory plus net purchases (purchases + freight-in − purchase returns − purchase discounts) minus closing inventory — and the practical shortcut layout for computing it (Indonesia's book.)

**Financial statements of a sole trader.** The trading company's financial statements from its worksheet: income statement built around sales, COGS and gross profit, statement of changes in equity and statement of financial position (Indonesia's book.)

**Financial statements of a sole trader.** The trading account: prepared only for the trading activity, it closes in the trading balances from the trial balance to give gross profit — net sales (sales less sales returns) less the net cost of purchases (opening stock + purchases − purchase returns + carriage, customs, insurance and packing on purchases) and the other trading expenses (Sudan's book.)

**Financial statements of a sole trader.** The profit-and-loss account: it gathers all revenues and expenses around the gross profit or gross loss from the trading account so the business can see why the result happened; if revenues exceed expenses the surplus is net profit, if expenses exceed revenues the deficit is net loss (in a non-profit body the same account is called the income-and-expenditure account) (Sudan's book.)

**Financial statements of a sole trader.** The balance sheet (statement of financial position): a statement, prepared outside the ledger, showing the business's assets and liabilities at the financial year end — fixed assets split into intangible (goodwill, patents, trademarks) and tangible (land, buildings, machinery, vehicles), current assets (stock, debtors, bills receivable, bank and cash), capital with reserves and retained profits,… (Sudan's book.)

**Financial statements of a sole trader.** Financial statements: their meaning, scope, purpose and objective, and their elements — written reports prepared by management for users who cannot demand tailored reports (Tanzania's book.)

**Financial statements of a sole trader.** Sole trader against company, and the sole proprietor's statements: the differences between the financial statements of a sole trader and of a company, then the preparation of the sole proprietor's statement of income and statement of financial position from a trial balance with year-end information (closing inventory, accruals, prepayments, irrecoverable debts, allowance for receivables) (Tanzania's book.)

**Balance-sheet.** The balance sheet (ισολογισμός): the summary statement drawn from the inventory, showing the business's asset items and their values at a given moment in one currency, resting on Ε = Π; it is the official, published picture of the business's property structure, entered in the same Book of Inventories and Balance Sheets with assets on the left page and liabilities on the right (Greece's book.)

**Reading-the-balance-sheet.** The three forms a balance sheet can take by the relation of Assets to real liabilities: positive (assets exceed obligations to third parties, so positive net worth exists), neutral (assets equal them — the business owns nothing of its own) and negative (assets fall short) (Greece's book.)

**Income-and-expense-accounts.** Why net worth is followed with more than the single Capital account: its changes during the year are tracked through expense accounts (decreases spent serving the business's activities), revenue accounts (increases from selling goods, products and services and from incidental and capital income) and extraordinary gains and losses accounts; revenues and gains are credited and expenses and losses debited because that is how they move net worth; these management accounts live only during the year and are closed into the operating and results accounts at its end — the book's worked reasons are a transport-cost total and an overtime total informing management decisions (Greece's book.)

**Revenue-accounts.** Revenue accounts: revenue is every value or receivable the business gains from its activities — from selling or exploiting goods, services and rights (sales of merchandise, of finished and semi-finished products, services income, incidental-occupation income, capital income) (Greece's book.)

**Finding-the-result.** Operating accounts and results-of-the-year accounts (λογαριασμοί εκμεταλλεύσεων και αποτελεσμάτων χρήσης): in an operating account the organic, regular, accrued revenues and expenses of one activity are matched to find that activity's organic result; the year's overall result is gathered in the Results of the Year account, whose balance — profit if credit, loss if debit — passes to Results for Appropriation for distribution under the articles and the law, or, in a sole-trader business, to the Capital account (Greece's book.)

**Finding-the-result.** Determining the firm's economic result: after the adjusting entries and agreement of the second provisional trial balance of all the ledgers, the net regular (organic) result is determined in the General Operating account (80.00), into which are transferred the inventory accounts (group 2), the organic expenses (group 6), the organic revenues (group 7) and the closing inventory of merchandise (Greece's book.)

**Introduction part.** Financial statements (Penyata Kewangan) introduced: the formal record containing a business's financial information, prepared in the structure and reporting format set by the reporting standards; the qualitative characteristics of the information (fundamental: relevance and faithful representation; enhancing: comparability, understandability, verifiability and timeliness); the components compared across three businesses (a grocery shop's sales, an eye clinic's treatment fees, a financial cooperative's interest income); and who uses them (the owner, employees judging the business's stability and ability to pay wages, shareholders and investors comparing businesses, and external chartered accountants engaged by businesses without their own accountant) (Malaysia's book.)

**Components part.** The components of the Trading and Profit & Loss Account and the Statement of Financial Position: an account as a summary of the transactions occurring in a period, and the components identified — hasil (revenue: income generated in the business, e.g. commission and interest received), belanja (expenses: expenditure paid in the business, e.g. rates, salaries, rent), aset, liabiliti and ekuiti pemilik (Malaysia's book.)

**Classification part.** The items of each component classified: operating against non-operating revenue; revenue and expenses that involve cash against those that do not (depreciation, bad debts and creating or increasing the allowance for doubtful debts involve no payment); assets defined (property owned by the business to generate revenue; physical — furniture, inventory, equipment — and non-physical — receivables and accrued interest) and classified by liquidity, useful life and durability into Aset Semasa and Aset Bukan Semasa, with the same item classified differently by the business's main activity (a furniture shop's tables are its inventory, another business's are its furniture); liabilities classified by maturity; and owner's equity as the business's 'debt' to the owner — the owner's claim on the business's assets (Malaysia's book.)

**Contra part.** Contra entries (Catatan Kontra): an entry that reduces the balance of the account related to it — sales returns against sales, purchases returns against purchases, accumulated depreciation, the allowance for doubtful debts — their position shown inside the Trading & P&L Account and the Statement of Financial Position, the contra balance sitting opposite its related account in the ledger, and the distortion if contra is omitted (without a Sales Returns account, sales and gross profit are overstated) (Malaysia's book.)

**Trading account part.** The Trading and Profit & Loss Account (Akaun Perdagangan dan Untung Rugi) without adjustments: its purpose (a business prepares it to know its profit or loss), prepared from the trial balance in 'T' form and in statement format, manually or with ICT applications, including how a gross loss is carried into the Profit & Loss Account (Malaysia's book.)

**Position statement part.** The Statement of Financial Position (Penyata Kedudukan Kewangan): prepared at the end of the accounting period after the Trading and Profit & Loss Account; its purpose and its statement format (non-current assets, then current assets, then owner's equity), with the Capital account worked (opening balance, net profit added, drawings deducted) (Malaysia's book.)

**Closing part.** Closing the revenue, expense and inventory accounts at the end of the financial period (Penutupan Akaun): why the closing is needed, and the transfers — net sales and cost of sales into the Trading Account, each revenue and expense account closed into the Profit & Loss Account, and the inventory account carried (Malaysia's book.)

**Classified statement of financial position.** Classifying assets and liabilities as current (turned into cash or used up within the next accounting period) and non-current, and presenting the classified Statement of Financial Position (Samoa's book.)

**Final accounts.** Unit 12.2: prepare a profit and loss statement and a balance sheet from a trial balance, with classification of items; the account format against the narrative format (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Statement formats.** Appendix: the balance sheet in narrative format and in account format (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Statement formats.** Appendix: the profit and loss statement in account format and in narrative format, with expenses classified as selling, administration and financial (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Statement formats.** Appendix: the profit and loss (revenue) statement format and the balance sheet format (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Reports.** Strand 1 Unit 2: business reports — the balance sheet and the income statement as the two forms of financial report, and the stakeholders who use financial reports (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Income and expenses.** Strand 2 Unit 2: actual income and expenses for a period budget — income defined; the net income formulas; expenses as operating against non-operating (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)
