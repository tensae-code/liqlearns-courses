# Video script — Financial Statements of a Sole Trader

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
At the end of a period a sole trader's records become statements: the trading account finds gross profit, the profit and loss account finds net profit, and the balance sheet shows the financial position at a date.

The balance sheet as a statement of the financial position (assets, capital and liabilities) at a particular date, the idea of an accounting/trading period, and the format: heading with business name, statement name and date, assets on one side and capital plus liabilities on the other, the two totals equal. The classified balance sheet (fixed then current assets; capital, long-term then current liabilities), items arranged in order of permanency or in order of liquidity, a business solvent when assets exceed liabilities and insolvent (deficiency) when they do not, and who uses a balance sheet and why. The final accounts as a set (trading account, profit and loss account and balance sheet, also called financial statements), and the concept of a trading period/accounting period — the span at whose end accounts are balanced, a trial balance extracted, profit or loss determined and a balance sheet prepared; most businesses use one year, the government's being the financial/fiscal year. Determining profit or loss before any account is drawn: gross profit as sales minus cost of goods sold, cost of goods sold built from opening stock plus purchases less closing stock, and the adjustments — returns inwards deducted from sales, returns outwards from purchases, carriage inwards added to purchases as part of cost, carriage outwards treated as an expense.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
