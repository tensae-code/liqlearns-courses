The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

This lesson is about one thing.
Ratios and percentages read the financial statements: one figure set against another judges profitability, liquidity and efficiency, and lets a business be compared across years and against other businesses.

What a ratio is (one item expressed in relation to another, for assessing performance and financial strength) and the first two: mark-up, gross profit over cost price, and margin, gross profit over selling price, each as a fraction, ratio or percentage. Converting between mark-up and margin: the same gross profit on different bases, so given the mark-up fraction the margin is found by adding the numerator to the denominator, and the reverse to return to mark-up. The liquidity and performance ratios: current (working capital) ratio, rate of stock turnover (cost of sales over average stock), acid test/quick ratio (current assets minus stock, over current liabilities) and return on capital (net profit over capital owned) — and what each is for: pricing and finding profit from mark-up/margin, judging whether short-term obligations can be met and whether resources lie idle, and comparing performance across periods and businesses. What financial statement analysis is, its purposes for management, investors and credit providers, and the three ratio families (profitability, liquidity, efficiency) as the book's own framework.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
