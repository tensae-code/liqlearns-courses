# Financial Statement Analysis and Ratios

## Do it first

Two shops each made a profit of 10,000 — but one used capital of 20,000 and the other 100,000. Which did better? A bare figure cannot say; a ratio can. Judging a business by figures set against each other is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What ratios are, and the profitability ratios

**Proposed accounting-ratios lesson (same proposal as filed from the Nigeria book).** What a ratio is (one item expressed in relation to another, for assessing performance and financial strength) and the first two: mark-up, gross profit over cost price, and margin, gross profit over selling price, each as a fraction, ratio or percentage.

**Proposed accounting-ratios lesson.** Converting between mark-up and margin: the same gross profit on different bases, so given the mark-up fraction the margin is found by adding the numerator to the denominator, and the reverse to return to mark-up.

**Proposed accounting-ratios lesson.** The liquidity and performance ratios: current (working capital) ratio, rate of stock turnover (cost of sales over average stock), acid test/quick ratio (current assets minus stock, over current liabilities) and return on capital (net profit over capital owned) — and what each is for: pricing and finding profit from mark-up/margin, judging whether short-term obligations can be met and whether resources lie idle, and comparing performance across periods and businesses.

**Opening part of the new lesson.** What financial statement analysis is, its purposes for management, investors and credit providers, and the three ratio families (profitability, liquidity, efficiency) as the book's own framework.

**Calculation part.** Profitability ratios worked and interpreted in the Malaysian way: gross profit percentage on sales (margin), gross profit percentage on cost of sales (mark-up / tokokan), net profit percentage on sales, and return on capital.

**Ratio part of the new lesson.** Interpreting financial statements with basic ratios (gross and net margins, mark-up, return on capital, current and acid-test ratios, inventory turnover) at the business-studies level.

**Ratio part of the new lesson.** Analysing profitability, efficiency and liquidity ratios from a business's financial statements, taught inside the business-functions chapter.

**Proposed accounting-ratios lesson.** Gross margin and gross mark-up and converting between them, net profit as a percentage of sales, rate of return on capital employed, expense ratios and asset turnover ratio.

**Profitability ratios.** Profitability measures: mark-up and mark-up percentage (selling price = cost + mark-up), gross profit percentage, expense percentages by category and net profit percentage; a falling mark-up is investigated for wrong prices, markdowns, stocktake errors or theft.

## Sub lesson 2 — Liquidity, efficiency and return — and judging with ratios

**Calculation and interpretation part.** Liquidity ratios: current ratio and acid-test ratio, what each measures about ability to pay, and the book's benchmark readings (e.g. 2:1 current ratio).

**Calculation and interpretation part.** Efficiency ratios: inventory turnover rate, debtors' collection period and creditors' payment period, read as how well the business manages stock and credit.

**Comparison part of the new lesson.** Comparing performance: using the ratios to compare a business across years and against another business and to judge its financial position.

**Judgement part of the new lesson.** Justifying a business's financial position from its financial statements.

**Proposed new lesson teaching accounting ratios and their interpretation.** Working-capital ratio (current ratio) and liquid (acid-test) ratio worked out from a company balance sheet to judge whether working capital is adequate.

**Proposed accounting-ratios lesson.** Rate of stock turnover: what it measures, how it is calculated from cost of sales over average stock, and the factors that push it up or down.

**Interpretation part of the proposed accounting-ratios lesson.** Using ratios properly: compare with past periods, with similar businesses and with the budget, and allow for the type of business before judging.

**Purpose of analysis.** Why statements are analysed: ratios and percentages let the accountant compare periods, other firms and industry norms and report trends for decision-making.

**Liquidity, equity and return ratios.** Stability and return measures: owner's/shareholders' equity ratio (below 0.5:1 means creditors own more than the owners), working capital and the current ratio, liquid (quick) capital and ratio excluding inventory and prepayments, and return on owner's equity against average equity.

**Analysing sole-trader statements.** First analysis of a sole trader's statements: sales trend, gross profit and net profit percentages, return on owner's equity against bank term-deposit rates, working capital, and the equity ratio with its 0.5:1 danger benchmark.

**Reading the statements.** Profitability analysis of the project: return on sales (average profit as a percentage of sales) and return on investment (each year's net profit compared with the initial investment) used to judge whether the venture earns enough (Ecuador's book.)

**Reading the venture's numbers.** Financial analysis of the venture: analysing its accounting information to get an objective view of its present situation and how it is expected to evolve; the plan's financial section records the product or service sold, its price in córdobas, the unit of sale, how much is made per month and whether sales are cash or credit (Nicaragua's book.)
**Financial statement analysis and ratios.** Financial statement analysis and its objectives: the concept of analysis and interpretation, who analyses the statements and with what objectives (Tanzania's book.)

**Financial statement analysis and ratios.** Financial ratios as the tool of analysis, by category: profitability ratios, liquidity ratios, efficiency/asset-turnover ratios (how well the business uses its assets to generate sales and cash), financial-leverage ratios (the capital structure — debt ratio, equity ratio, debt-to-equity ratio, times interest earned — and what each computed value means: a 46% debt ratio means lenders contributed… (Tanzania's book.)

**Financial statement analysis and ratios.** The limitations of financial ratios: ratios simplify analysis but rest on figures that can be distorted — inflation distorts the real values behind the figures, and the statements omit qualitative characteristics of the business (internally generated goodwill and brands are not recognised as assets) that matter to interpretation (Tanzania's book.)

**Ratio analysis.** Ratio analysis as a management tool: a quantitative method of analysing financial statements to evaluate performance, financial health and operational efficiency by comparing figures from the balance sheet and income statement (Bhutan's book.)

**Common-size statements.** Common-size (vertical) analysis: statements restated as percentages so periods can be compared line by line (Bhutan's book.)

**Analysis part.** Financial statement analysis as assessing the entity's performance with the analysis tools, made by management to obtain critical information (Bhutan's book.)

**Common-size part.** Common-size statement analysis: each line as a percentage so years compare directly (shown on a full statement of financial position) (Bhutan's book.)

**Interpretation part.** Ratio interpretation in depth: return on equity (interest and preference dividends excluded), and the receivables trade-off — longer credit terms raise sales but also bad debts, while pressing customers to pay quickly can damage sales (Bhutan's book.)

**Projected analysis part.** Analysing the projected statements: the net profit margin (also called percentage return on sales or margin on sales) calculated from revenue and cost of sales (Bhutan's book.)

**Analysing performance.** Unit 12.3 Assessing Business Performance: key performance indicators for profitability, liquidity and efficiency and calculating the ratios; financial against non-financial indicators; variances, trends and benchmarks (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Ratio analysis.** Strand 2 Unit 2: financial ratios, each with its formula — the current ratio, the debt-equity ratio, return on sales, return on equity, and accounts receivable turnover (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**The cash cycle.** Strand 2 Unit 2: the operating cycle — its definition and formula (inventory period plus receivables period), the inventory and receivables turnover formulas, the net operating cycle/cash conversion cycle (less the payables period), and how to shorten the cycle (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Ratio.** Vertical analysis of a sole trader's statements: ratios and percentages calculated from the statements (analysis) and then commented on (interpretation) to aid decisions and industry comparison (Fiji's book.)
