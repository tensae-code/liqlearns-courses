# Hands-on — Financial Statement Analysis and Ratios

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two shops each made a profit of 10,000 — but one used capital of 20,000 and the other 100,000. Which did better? A bare figure cannot say; a ratio can. Judging a business by figures set against each other is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: ratios and percentages read the financial statements: one figure set against another judges profitability, liquidity and efficiency, and lets a business be compared across years and against other businesses. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
