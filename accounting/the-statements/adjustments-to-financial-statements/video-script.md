# Video script — Adjustments to Financial Statements

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Business does not stop at midnight on balance day, so amounts owed, prepaid, earned or received in advance are adjusted so each period carries exactly its own revenues and expenses.

Accrual accounting records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, cash-based accounting only when cash moves; a transaction-by-transaction comparison shows why accrual gives the clearer picture. Balance-day adjustments exist because business does not stop at midnight on balance day: amounts owed, amounts prepaid, amounts owed to the business, amounts received in advance, closing inventory and depreciation must all be matched to the right period. Accrued expenses: wages incurred but unpaid at balance day are added to the expense and shown as a current liability, entered through the General Journal. Prepayments: the unused part of an expense paid in advance (insurance) is moved out of the expense into a Prepayments current asset.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
