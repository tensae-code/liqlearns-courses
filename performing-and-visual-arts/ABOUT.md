# About this course — Performing and Visual Arts

This file carries the course's provenance: what the course is,
which books it is built from, how Ethiopia's own books teach the
arts, and what is honestly missing. Lesson pages carry none of
this — a learner opens a lesson and goes straight to the doing.
The detail lives here, once, on purpose.

## What the course is

Performing and Visual Arts (ሥነ-ጥበባት) is the Ethiopian school
subject that teaches drawing, music, drama and dance as one woven
art. Ethiopia's books teach the three strands together from the
start — looking and listening first, then imitating (sound,
movement), then making (drawing, singing, acting) — and this
course keeps that way: the observe → imitate → make order runs
through its lessons. The course is organised by **levels, not
grades**: the school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, never as the course's
organisation.

Everything taught traces to a book and a printed page, named in
each lesson's sources. Teacher-guide material is always labelled
**(teacher guide — evidence)**; it never counts as pupil-taught.
Where a lesson rests only on teacher-guide evidence, its sources
say so and its acceptance count is 0 countries.

## How Ethiopia's books are shaped

Every primary book has the same five-chapter skeleton, but the
weight shifts as the child grows. Recorded here from each book's
own contents (printed pp. iii–iv), because this shape is part of
how Ethiopia teaches it:

- **Grade 6** — Ch. 1 "What Art Is" (a definition chapter that
  also carries the drawing foundation and the whole
  scale/key-signature theory block), Ch. 2 "Expressing Creativity"
  (rhythm reading, composition, still-life painting, assembling,
  stencil, calligraphy, expression through theatre, film and
  photography), Ch. 3 "Telling and Expressing Our Country's
  History through Art", Ch. 4 "Appreciating Indigenous
  Knowledge", Ch. 5 "The Relationship of Art with Other
  Subjects". Every chapter closes with a long summary, TWO ✓/✗
  self-check tables (the visual and music strands separated) and
  a multi-part closing exercise. (Grade 6 Student Textbook,
  printed pp. iii–iv.)
- **Grade 7** — the book turns from skills to knowledge about the
  arts: Ch. 1 "Artistic Awareness" (design elements and
  principles; traditional music and dances; theatre and film),
  Ch. 2 "Expressing Creativity" (painting and printing; music
  reading including qenet; performing traditional dance; theatre
  and film ideas), Ch. 3 "History and Culture" (Amhara music
  history and musicians; Saint Yared; modern music's beginning;
  great visual artists; indigenous knowledge), Ch. 4 "Aesthetic
  Usefulness", Ch. 5 "Relations with Other Subjects". Every
  chapter closes with a ✓/✗ self-check table, a summary and a
  closing exercise. (Grade 7 Student Textbook, printed pp.
  iii–iv.)
- **Grade 8** — the last primary book turns analytical and
  productive: Ch. 1 "Recognising Art" (organising pictures with
  the elements and principles; instruments, qenet and folk-dance
  categories; film and theatre preparation), Ch. 2 "Expressing
  Art Creatively" (dictation and sight-reading; dance
  performance; theatre and film; batik, silk-screen, casting and
  papier-mâché), Ch. 3 "Culture and History" (traditional music,
  instruments and dance in history; ancient monuments and
  handcraft; local histories for theatre and film), Ch. 4
  "Aesthetic Usefulness", Ch. 5 "Art Education's Relation with
  Other Subjects". Every chapter closes with a summary, a ✓/✗
  checklist and closing exercises. (Grade 8 Student Textbook,
  printed pp. iii–iv.)

## The books' own glossaries

The course glossary — the books' own definitions, carried here
once (lesson pages define their words as they meet them):

**Grade 2 Student Textbook (ሙዳዬ ቃላት, printed p. 95)** — arts;
performing arts; visual arts; note; music (a composition of
beats and sounds); drawing (a thing's surface/appearance);
dance; free dance; theatre (creative acting); photograph;
culture (a community's way of life/custom); value (a
manifestation of culture); appreciation (expressing feeling for
the arts).

**Grade 8 Student Textbook (ሙዳዬ ቃላት, printed p. 138)** —
element (አናስር) = design's foundations; principle (መርህ) =
design's laws; texture; value (ፍካት/ቫሊዩ) = tone level from black
to white; harmony (ሑባሬ); mould (ሞልድ) = the hollowed part we
fill with gypsum or liquid things to multiply a form; spectrum
(ስፔክትረም) = colour-band; art (ሥነ-ጥበባት) = holding painting,
music, theatre and drama; choreography (ኬሮግራፊ) = dance
arrangement system; note (ኖታ) = music sign; performing art =
holding music, dance, theatre and film; visual art = holding
painting, sculpture, photography, drawing; keyboard instrument
= holding beat and string characters; alto = women's thick
voice; soprano = women's thin voice; tenor = men's thin voice;
bass = men's thick voice; interval = music sound distance.

## Honest notes

- The Grade 1 riddles' answers are not printed in the student
  book; the course never invents them.
- Songs are taught by name, purpose and what they teach; lyrics
  are never transcribed (at most a one-line fragment a source
  itself quotes).
- Two book-teaching conflicts are carried openly in the lessons'
  sources: a Grade 3 closing exercise calls the atamo a church
  instrument while the same book's teaching places it in secular
  music (the teaching is followed); and a Grade 3 diagram draws
  the whole note dark while the other books teach the open oval
  (the open oval is taught).
