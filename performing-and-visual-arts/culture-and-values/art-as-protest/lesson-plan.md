# Lesson plan — Art as Protest

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Art as Protest
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** art as social, political and cultural protest and defence of democratic values — realist painting of the working class, American social realism, revolutionary sculpture, and a capital city built as protesting architecture.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Realism that protests** — 19th-century pictorial realism and the working class (Daumier, Millet, Courbet); American social realism (Hopper, Grant Wood, Dorothea Lange's photography, Lucien Freud).
2. **Sculpture revolutionaries and a protesting city** — Rodin and Giacometti as revolutionaries of sculpture; Brasília (Lúcio Costa, Oscar Niemeyer) as urbanism built to create a social, protesting architecture.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this course in the same title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what realism protested with, naming two artists.
2. Name the two sculpture revolutionaries and say what Brasília was built to be.
