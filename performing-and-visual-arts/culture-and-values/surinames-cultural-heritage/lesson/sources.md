Suriname's own way: one integrated arts course — Kunst- en cultuureducatie (KCE) — in six domains (Music, Dance, Visual Arts, Cultural Heritage, Theatre, Media), each domain a series of one-hour lessons built on the same frame: the IKA lesson shape (Introductie–Kern–Afsluiting, the core in three phases — warm-up, technique, own work and showing moment) and the Big Idea design process in four phases (orient, research, experiment, reflect), every series answering one research question and ending in a presentation; each domain is deliberately fused with another school subject (music with arithmetic, dance with geography, visual arts with history, theatre with language); the course's heart is Suriname itself — its many peoples' dances, dress, jewellery, heritage and theatre forms — held together by the Alakondre ideal, "Alakondre na wan, wan na Alakondre" (unity in diversity); assessment is by evaluation card and portfolio, process over product. Suriname's own way: the deepening year runs the same six domains on the same frame as leerjaar 7, but at competency levels B/C — pupils switch independently between the four design phases, master the craft itself, argue their choices, handle the emotions of others in their work, and present independently in an unknown space for an unknown audience; music gives meaning to a calculated music work built on the rhythmic patterns of Suriname's five peoples; dance travels a safe route from home through the Amazon interior into Brazil, in awasa, indigenous and Latin American steps; visual arts add hatching and pattern design to the clothing work and end in a fashion show instead of an exhibition; heritage places an object into another culture and performs what that does to its meaning as an oral art form; theatre adapts a known story into an Alakondre Mato; media builds the one-minute film on a storyboard and uploads it to YouTube. Named, not placed, as filed: each domain's begrippenlijst (glossary — Music printed p. 48, Dance pp. 87–88, Visual Arts pp. 128–129, Heritage pp. 158–159, Theatre pp. 197–199, Media pp. 223–224) — inventory only (content-bearing single terms from them are carried inside the placement lines where the lessons use them); each domain's evaluatiekaart (evaluation card — Music pp. 49–51, Dance pp. 90–92, Visual Arts pp. 130–131, Heritage pp. 160–162, Theatre pp. 200–202, Media pp. 225–227) — assessment apparatus, not teaching content; the closing appendices (the KCE lesson form, printed p. 229; deepening questions for preparing a lesson, p. 230; the general KCE evaluation card, p. 231) — teacher apparatus; the Bronnenlijst (sources list, printed pp. 232–234) — inventory only. | Suriname — Cultural Heritage Les 2 "Onderzoek doen naar cultureel erfgoed" — researching a heritage object or tradition in four steps — frame your questions (its name, material, origin, user, meaning, the stories attached to it), explore your sources (internet, encyclopedias, history books, a museum's collection, an archive, the people who work in heritage institutions), work out the answers, noting different answers where sources differ, and draw your conclusion; then judge the thing's intangible value — why it must be kept for later (bewaarnoodzaak) — and defend that judgement with arguments (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/researching-heritage`) | Book 1, printed pp. 139–144 |
| Suriname — Cultural Heritage domain introduction, deepened — heritage can be personal, local, regional, national and international at once, or all together; what counts as heritage changes over time and differs by place, and groups in society deploy it — or ignore it — as they need; this year the course works from the heritage of the cultures present in Suriname for more than a hundred years — Inheems, Marron, Chinees, Hindostaans and Javaans — and of the Creole community, born in Suriname itself from the mixing of peoples (slug first proposed in Book 1, leerjaar 7) (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/what-cultural-heritage-is`) | Book 2, printed pp. 133–136 |
| Suriname — Cultural Heritage Les 1 "Surinaams cultureel erfgoed" and Bijlage 1 "Afbeeldingen bij Les 1": Suriname's heritage by name, the deepening year's table — instruments: apinti, sekseki and the Hindostaans dhol; dishes: pom, saoto, tjauw min, her'heri, podosiri, roti and atyupo (peprewatra); head coverings: the Javanese mondolan, the indigenous pumari, the Creole angisa, the Marron felt hat (vilthoedje), the Hindustani orhani and the Chinese bridal headdress; dances: kumanti and papa dances, seketi, srimpi, the dragon dance, djarang kepang and launda ke nach; theatre: wajang, Ramlila and the folk plays A Sa Go, Natak and Ludruk; films: Purab Aur Paschim (1970), Wan Pipel (1976) and Kon hesi baka (1977–78); music: Aleke, gamelan, a Baithak Gana ensemble and a kasekoband; songs named: Sikkie Malinda, Rorac, Ala Pikin Nengre, Tembang Macapat, the gospel Prisiri and Jai Jai Sarnaam (Opo); feasts: Chinese New Year, Holi, Ketikoti and Id-Ul-Fitr (Bodo); rituals: Chinese ancestor veneration, a Hindu wedding, Muharram and winti, with pictured Winti vessels — a calabash bowl and an earthenware bowl with notched rim and holes; religion and monuments: Fort Zeelandia, the St. Petrus en Paulus cathedral, the Neveh Shalom synagogue beside the Ahmadiyya main mosque and the Arya Dewaker temple, all in Paramaribo; storytelling: Anansi, Akaraman, Mato and Birbal; and pictured art: indigenous ceramics and Nola Hatterman's painting "Op het terras" (1930), the Creole koto, and the Sranan poetry collection "Wortoe d'e tan abra" (slug first proposed in Book 1, leerjaar 7) (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/surinames-heritage-named`) | Book 2, printed pp. 140, 154–157 |
| Suriname — Cultural Heritage Les 2 "Onderzoek doen naar Surinaams cultureel erfgoed" (Researching Surinamese Cultural Heritage): critical source research — the four research steps now aimed at meaning — frame questions about why the object or tradition matters in its own culture (its use, its meaning, the stories attached to it, its link to that people's history or religion, why they keep it), explore sources critically (check a website against others, doubt and ask), work the information out by comparing sources — noting where they disagree or contradict each other — and conclude in your own argued form from the sources you find most credible; then the swap: research a partner's object and write two arguments for keeping it as Surinamese heritage (slug first proposed in Book 1, leerjaar 7) (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/researching-heritage`) | Book 2, printed pp. 145–148 |
| Suriname — Cultural Heritage Les 3 "Kennis overdragen in een kunstvorm en presentatie oefenen": the culture experiment — give your own heritage object or tradition a new application by placing it in another culture, and reason out what that does to its meaning and its intangible value — does the serious become ridiculous, the joyful serious? The book's worked examples: a traditional wrap-cloth of one culture remade into another culture's garment; gamelan music played on a sekseki from another culture; the dragon dance performed to papa-dance music; the song Sikkie Malinda set to another culture's melody; Ramlila performed in the style of wajang; Anansi given the leading role in a Birbal story (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/the-culture-experiment`) | Book 2, printed pp. 149–151 |
