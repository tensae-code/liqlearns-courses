## Sub lesson 1 — What cultural heritage is

By the **UNESCO definition** the book quotes, cultural heritage is **objects that are testimonies of the history and identity of a culture**. It comes in two kinds: **material heritage** — monuments, cultural landscapes, archaeological finds, books, museum objects — and **intangible, living heritage** — traditions, rituals, fashion, music and dance, stories, customs. Heritage education builds **historical awareness, identity, cultural self-awareness and cultural citizenship** — learning in, from and about your own surroundings. In the deepening year: heritage can be **personal, local, regional, national and international at once**; what counts as heritage **changes over time and differs by place**, and groups in society deploy it — or ignore it — as they need. The deepening year works from the heritage of the cultures present in Suriname for more than a hundred years — **Inheems, Marron, Chinees, Hindostaans and Javaans** — and of the **Creole community**, born in Suriname itself from the mixing of peoples.

## Sub lesson 2 — Suriname's heritage by name

The books name Suriname's own. Leerjaar 7: household things — the **prapi (dia) jug, stamper and dough roller**; dishes — **pom, podosiri, pepre watra, roti and bami**; instruments — the **apinti drum (apinti drong), sekseki and gamelan**; dress — the **omslagdoek and the koto**; the dances **djarang kepang (jaran kepang)** and **kawina music**; a **Ramlila** performance; the song-games **Seki Malinda and Makokketje**; the **Anansi spider stories**; the monuments pictured — the **St. Petrus en Paulus cathedral** and the **Neveh Shalom synagogue beside the Ahmadiyya main mosque** in Paramaribo, the **monument of 155 years of Chinese immigration**, and **Fort Nieuw-Amsterdam**; and the feasts — the **Divali procession, the Ketikoti celebration and Chinese New Year** — set against things that are **not** Surinamese heritage, such as the mobile phone, streetdance and the hamburger. Leerjaar 8's table adds, among much more: instruments **apinti, sekseki and the Hindostaans dhol**; dishes **pom, saoto, tjauw min, her'heri, podosiri, roti and atyupo (peprewatra)**; head coverings — the Javanese **mondolan**, the indigenous **pumari**, the Creole **angisa**, the Marron **felt hat (vilthoedje)**, the Hindustani **orhani** and the **Chinese bridal headdress**; dances **kumanti and papa dances, seketi, srimpi, the dragon dance, djarang kepang and launda ke nach**; theatre **wajang, Ramlila** and the folk plays **A Sa Go, Natak and Ludruk**; films **Purab Aur Paschim (1970), Wan Pipel (1976) and Kon hesi baka (1977–78)**; music **Aleke, gamelan, a Baithak Gana ensemble and a kasekoband**; feasts **Chinese New Year, Holi, Ketikoti and Id-Ul-Fitr (Bodo)**; rituals **Chinese ancestor veneration, a Hindu wedding, Muharram and winti**; religion and monuments — **Fort Zeelandia**, the cathedral, the synagogue beside the mosque and the **Arya Dewaker temple**, all in Paramaribo; storytelling — **Anansi, Akaraman, Mato and Birbal**; and pictured art — **indigenous ceramics** and **Nola Hatterman's painting "Op het terras" (1930)**, the Creole **koto**, and the Sranan poetry collection **"Wortoe d'e tan abra"**. Songs and stories are placed here by name and purpose only — never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Researching heritage — and the culture experiment

**Researching a heritage object or tradition takes four steps**: frame your questions (its **name, material, origin, user, meaning, the stories attached to it**); explore your sources (**internet, encyclopedias, history books, a museum's collection, an archive, the people who work in heritage institutions**); work out the answers, **noting different answers where sources differ**; and draw your **conclusion**. Then judge the thing's **intangible value** — why it must be kept for later (**bewaarnoodzaak**) — and **defend that judgement with arguments**. The deepening year aims the four steps at **meaning**, checks sources **critically**, and ends in the swap: research a partner's object and write **two arguments** for keeping it as Surinamese heritage. The **culture experiment** goes one step further: give your own heritage object or tradition **a new application by placing it in another culture**, and reason out what that does to its **meaning and intangible value** — does the serious become ridiculous, the joyful serious? The book's worked examples: a traditional wrap-cloth of one culture remade into another culture's garment; gamelan music played on a sekseki from another culture; the dragon dance performed to papa-dance music; the song Sikkie Malinda set to another culture's melody; Ramlila performed in the style of wajang; Anansi given the leading role in a Birbal story.
