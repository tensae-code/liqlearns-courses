## Sub lesson 1 — Traditional dress, people by people

The traditional dress of Suriname's peoples, garment by garment. **Indigenous dress: angisa** (shoulder cloth), **saja** (woman's under-skirt), **kamisa** (man's under-trousers). **Creole dress: koto, kotobere, yaki and angisa**. **Hindustani dress: sari, gharara, pagri, dhoti, payama and kurta**. **Javanese dress: djarik, klambi, topí, kathok komprang and sarong**. **Chinese dress: the qipao**. **Marron dress: pangi and kamisa**. The series' research question is: **how do you recognise, from a garment's design, which people it comes from?**

## Sub lesson 2 — Suriname's jewellery and what it means

Suriname's own jewellery and what it means. The **slavenboei or slavenarmband (slave bracelet)** — the well-known men's bracelet worn **for beauty and for faith**, its name taken from the **shackles put on enslaved people's arms**, first flat and later hollow. The other named pieces: **ogri ai krara** (evil-eye bead), **boto keti, kromanti ketting, the mattenklopper, bamboeketting, koningsschakelketting and pie-piet ketting**. The **Hindustani god pendant**, worn so the worshipped god **protects** the wearer, and the **Muslim Allah pendant in calligraphy**, worn **against evil eyes and for protection**.
