# The Macedonian Wedding Cycle

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — the Macedonian Wedding Cycle — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — the Macedonian wedding customs

the Macedonian wedding customs: kneading the little bread for the couple's luck, decking the houses, pinning ornaments on the wedding guests, blocking the bride's door until the groom pays, the bride seeing the groom through the ring ("Through the ring I see you, into your heart I enter"), and the bride's too-large shoes filled with money.

## Sub lesson 2 — the Galičnik wedding: five days of ceremony at Petrovden, kept under UNESCO protection

the Galičnik wedding: five days of ceremony at Petrovden, kept under UNESCO protection — inviting the dead relatives, shaving the groom, fetching the bride on horseback behind the flag-bearer, the bride filling vessels at the fountain and beginning the bridal dance, in the famed Galičnik costume.

## Sub lesson 3 — the wedding traditions of the Muslim and Albanian peoples

the wedding traditions of the Muslim and Albanian peoples: the bride-painting of Dolna Reka, the bajraktari processions of Skopje, the asking for the bride (sweet or bitter coffee giving the answer), the miraz, and the Albanian henna night (kana gece) when the bride leaves her handprints on the wall.

## Sub lesson 4 — Turkish, Bosniak, Vlach and Serbian wedding customs

Turkish, Bosniak, Vlach and Serbian wedding customs: the Turkish betrothal bundle and hamam; the Bosniak three-to-seven-day wedding; the Vlach sewing of the wedding flag and flour-sowing songs; the Serbian buklija invitation, shooting the apple from the gate, buying the bride, throwing the bouquet and the sieve of grain onto the roof.

## Sub lesson 5 — the wedding songs of every people: a song appointed for each moment of the rite

the wedding songs of every people: a song appointed for each moment of the rite — kneading the pogacha, shaving the groom, decorating the flag, fetching the bride, her farewell — in the Macedonian wedding, and the ordered song-groups of the Albanian, Turkish, Romani, Bosniak, Serbian and Vlach weddings.

## Do It Yourself

2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about North Macedonia's own way of teaching its arts?
