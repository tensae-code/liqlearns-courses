# Lesson plan — Folk Crafts of Ethiopia

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Folk Crafts of Ethiopia
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the crafts of the people's hands — Ethiopian folk handicrafts seen whole, then embroidery and woven grass, leather craft, the old ways of dressing, and the jewellery traditions with their named pieces — and what the crafts give back to society.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Our Arts and Cultural Heritage and Making with Clay and Paper — the learner knows their heritage and has made things by hand; this lesson meets the folk crafts as living trades with named pieces and a benefit to society.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Ethiopian folk handicrafts** — የህዝብ እጅ ሥራዎች — the people's hand work seen whole before its families are met one by one (Grade 5, printed p. 95).
2. **Embroidery and woven grass** — embroidered craft and woven-grass craft — thread worked into cloth, grass worked into useful beauty (Grade 5, printed p. 96).
3. **Leather craft and old dress** — the leather craft, and the old ways of dressing that the crafts served (Grade 5, printed p. 97).
4. **Jewellery: ማርዳ and ድሪ** — Ethiopian jewellery traditions, with the named pieces ማርዳ (marda) and ድሪ (diri) kept in their own names (Grade 5, printed p. 98).
5. **What the crafts give society** — the crafts' benefit to society (የህዝብ እጅ ሥራዎች ለህብረተሰቡ ያላቸው ፋይዳ) — the book's own closing of the craft chapter (Grade 5, printed pp. 112–114).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Our Arts and Cultural Heritage and Making with Clay and Paper. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what folk handicrafts are and name the chapter's craft families.
2. Tell embroidery from woven-grass work by material and method.
3. Name the leather craft and the old dress it served.
4. Name the jewellery pieces ማርዳ (marda) and ድሪ (diri).
5. State the crafts' benefit to society from the book's own chapter.

## What you need
If possible, real examples to look at: an embroidered cloth, woven grass work, a leather article, a traditional ornament — or their pictures.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Look at your hands. Somewhere near you, hands like yours made a cloth shine with thread, wove grass into something useful, or cut leather into beauty. This lesson names those crafts and the pieces they make. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Crafts blurred into one 'handmade things' → sort by material: thread on cloth (embroidery), grass (weaving), skin (leather), metal and bead (jewellery).
- Jewellery pieces confused → ማርዳ and ድሪ are named in Amharic in the book; learn them as names, the way you learn a person's.
