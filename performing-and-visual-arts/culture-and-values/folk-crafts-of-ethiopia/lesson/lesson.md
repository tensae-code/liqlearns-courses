# Folk Crafts of Ethiopia

## Do it first

Look at your hands. Somewhere near you, hands like yours made a cloth shine with thread, wove grass into something useful, or cut leather into beauty. This lesson names those crafts and the pieces they make.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ethiopian folk handicrafts

The chapter opens with the family name: **የህዝብ እጅ ሥራዎች — the people's hand work, Ethiopian folk handicrafts**. Before any single craft is taught, the book sets the whole family before the pupil: the things a people's hands make, from its own materials, by skills passed hand to hand. What follows in this lesson is the book's own order through the family — stitch, grass, leather, dress, and the shining pieces last.

## Sub lesson 2 — Embroidery and woven grass

Two fibre crafts stand together. **Embroidery**: **thread worked into cloth** — the stitched designs that edge and brighten woven garments. **Woven grass**: **grass worked into useful beauty** — coiled and woven into the containers and mats of daily life. One decorates what is worn; the other builds what is used; both begin with a plant or a thread and a pair of patient hands.

## Sub lesson 3 — Leather craft and old dress

**Leather craft** works the third material: **skin cut, stitched and finished** into the goods of daily life and ceremony. Beside it the book sets **old dress** — the old ways of dressing that the crafts together produced: the woven and embroidered cloth, the leather work, the ornaments of the next part, worn as one whole traditional outfit. Dress is where the craft family meets the body.

## Sub lesson 4 — Jewellery: ማርዳ and ድሪ

The shining members of the family: **Ethiopia's jewellery traditions** — and the book names pieces the way you name people: **ማርዳ (marda)** and **ድሪ (diri)**. Learn them in their own names, transliterated but never translated away: a jewellery piece's name carries its people, its material and its occasion inside it. The lesson keeps both names exactly as the book prints them.

## Sub lesson 5 — What the crafts give society

The chapter closes by turning the crafts outward: **የህዝብ እጅ ሥራዎች ለህብረተሰቡ ያላቸው ፋይዳ — the benefit of the people's hand work to society**. The crafts **earn their makers a living**, **carry a people's identity and memory in usable form**, and **turn local materials into local wealth**. This is why the book teaches children the crafts' names: a society that can name its crafts can keep them, buy them, and hand them on.

## Say it to your companion

Sort with your companion: give a material — thread, grass, leather, metal — and they name the craft family and one piece it makes.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw one craft piece from your area and label its material and craft.
2. Write the benefit list: three things the crafts give your community.
3. If a craftsperson works near you, ask (politely) what their craft's hardest step is; write the answer.
