# Lesson plan — Indigenous Knowledge of Ethiopia

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Indigenous Knowledge of Ethiopia
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's communities hold their own knowledge — grown at home over generations — and it is real knowledge: it farms, builds, heals and makes.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the culture lessons of this title — the learner already knows that every people has its own culture, dress, food and arts.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What indigenous knowledge is** — Ethiopia's many communities each have their own culture, language, belief, dress and food — and their own knowledge, grown inside that life (Grade 7, printed pp. 110–112).
2. **The named store — nine examples** — the book names nine examples: among them the Bale farmers' porcupine trick, Konso terracing, horn craft, and the building knowledge of Aksum, Lalibela and Gondar (Grade 7, printed pp. 111–113).
3. **Keeping the store** — indigenous knowledge lives only while it is practised and handed on — valuing it is part of keeping it (Grade 7, §3.6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Old Arts and Today's Arts and Folk Crafts of Ethiopia. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what indigenous knowledge is and where it lives.
2. Name the anchor examples of the book's nine and say what knowledge each holds.
3. Say how indigenous knowledge is kept, and do one keeping act.
## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
