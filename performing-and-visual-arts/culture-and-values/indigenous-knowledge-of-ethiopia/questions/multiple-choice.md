# Indigenous Knowledge of Ethiopia — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Indigenous knowledge is —
a) knowledge a community grows itself, in its own place, over generations  b) knowledge imported last year  c) only written knowledge
**Answer: a) knowledge a community grows itself, in its own place, over generations** — that is §3.6's definition.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The Grade 7 book says each Ethiopian community has its own —
a) culture, language, belief, dress and food  b) nothing of its own  c) imported customs only
**Answer: a) culture, language, belief, dress and food** — those are the section's named possessions.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The Bale farmers' example is a trick to —
a) keep porcupines from eating crops  b) call rain  c) scare birds only
**Answer: a) keep porcupines from eating crops** — that is the book's named farming example.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Konso's example is —
a) terracing — hillsides stepped so soil and water stay  b) flat farming only  c) irrigation by river
**Answer: a) terracing — hillsides stepped so soil and water stay** — terracing is Konso's named knowledge.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Indigenous knowledge is kept by —
a) practising it and handing it on  b) hiding it  c) forbidding questions
**Answer: a) practising it and handing it on** — practice and handing-on are its keeping.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** One keeping act a learner can do is —
a) ask an elder how the thing is done and watch the maker's hands  b) nothing  c) laugh at old ways
**Answer: a) ask an elder how the thing is done and watch the maker's hands** — asking and watching are the learner's keeping acts.
