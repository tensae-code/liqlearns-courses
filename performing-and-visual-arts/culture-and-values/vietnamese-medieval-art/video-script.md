This lesson is Vietnamese Medieval Art.

Vietnamese Medieval Art — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: A terracotta phoenix relief of the Lý age (11th–13th centuries), a stone tiger of the Trần age (13th century), a stone lion statue of the Champa culture (12th century), a wood relief in the Hạ Hiệp communal house in Hanoi (Lê restoration age, 17th century), a silk portrait of Trịnh Đình Kiên (about the 18th century), the lacquered-wood Quan Âm statue of Hội Hạ pagoda (Mạc age, 16th century) and a blue-and-white ceramic figure of a woman (Early Lê, 15th century); the common mark of Vietnam's medieval art is its conventional, stylised forming, whose main images are the creatures of belief and religion and the images of life and nature, and many heritages — village communal-house sculpture, worship statues in traditional sculpture — show simple, rustic shape and mass combined into an overall harmony; and, in application, analysing the national-treasure wooden statue of Queen Trịnh Thị Ngọc Trúc (17th century) — its composition and texture (dress, skin, hands and face) and its beauty.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
