# Lesson plan — Vietnamese Medieval Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Vietnamese Medieval Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** vietnamese Medieval Art — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Vietnam Grade 7 Mĩ thuật 7** — A terracotta phoenix relief of the Lý age (11th–13th centuries), a stone tiger of the Trần age (13th century), a stone lion statue of the Champa culture (12th century), a wood relief in the Hạ Hiệp communal house in Hanoi (Lê restoration age, 17th century), a silk portrait of Trịnh Đình Kiên (about the 18th century), the lacquered-wood Quan Âm statue of Hội Hạ pagoda (Mạc age, 16th century) and a blue-and-white ceramic figure of a woman (Early Lê, 15th century); the common mark of Vietnam's medieval art is its conventional, stylised forming, whose main images are the creatures of belief and religion and the images of life and nature, and many heritages — village communal-house sculpture, worship statues in traditional sculpture — show simple, rustic shape and mass combined into an overall harmony; and, in application, analysing the national-treasure wooden statue of Queen Trịnh Thị Ngọc Trúc (17th century) — its composition and texture (dress, skin, hands and face) and its beauty
2. **Vietnam Grade 10 Mĩ thuật 10 — Lí luận và lịch sử mĩ thuật** — Influenced by the art of the Lý period, the artefacts of the Trần period fine art (1226–1400) have a realistic character (tính hiện thực), shown with a liberal style (phong cách khoáng đạt) and strong, healthy shape-mass (hình khối chắc khoẻ) (the dragon on the Trần-period wooden door (Rồng trên cửa gỗ thời Trần); the Trần-period stone tiger statue (Tượng hổ đá thời Trần)); next, the Early Lê period fine art (thời Lê sơ, 1428–1527) strongly carries the Confucian character (mang đậm chất Nho giáo) with the temple (đền), shrine (miếu) and tomb (lăng mộ) architectural works… built much in Thanh Hoá; mixing into the common flow of the Vietnamese people's fine art but with the exemplary, standard character (sự khuôn mẫu, mực thước) strongly of Confucianism, this period's fine-art heritage has brought the separate form-creating conventions (quy ước tạo hình khác biệt); the decorative art stands out (nổi bật) with the dragon, cloud, flower and grass patterns of the four seasons (hoạ tiết rồng, mây, hoa cỏ bốn mùa)…, among which the blue-and-white flower ceramics (gốm hoa lam) develop strongly (phát triển mạnh) (the doctoral stele (Bia tiến sĩ), Văn Miếu, Hanoi; the decoration on blue-and-white flower ceramics (Trang trí trên đồ gốm hoa lam)); and the Mạc period fine art (1527–1592) has the folk-isation tendency (xu hướng dân gian hoá); the sculpture art reduces the ideal character (bớt tính lí tưởng), the Buddha-statue portraits (chân dung tượng Phật) become more and more close to humans (gần gũi với con người); besides, the communal-house carving (chạm khắc đình làng) develops carrying high form-creating value, rustic (mộc mạc), strong and healthy carving strokes (nét chạm chắc khoẻ), creating vivid mass (tạo khối sinh động) (the carving at Hữu Bổ communal house (Chạm khắc đình Hữu Bổ), Vĩnh Phúc; the Quan Âm Nam Hải statue (Tượng Quan Âm Nam Hải)); the Restored Lê period fine art (thời Lê trung hưng, 1533–1789) both develops the Buddha-statue sculpture and develops the carving art on the wood and stone materials…, and the statue of Quan Âm with a thousand arms and a thousand eyes (pho tượng nghìn tay nghìn mắt) in lacquered-wood material (chất liệu gỗ phủ sơn) appears (the statue of Quan Âm with a thousand eyes and a thousand arms (Tượng Quan Âm nghìn mắt nghìn tay), Bút Tháp Pagoda, Bắc Ninh; the carving on the stone corridor (Chạm khắc hành lang đá), Bút Tháp Pagoda, Bắc Ninh); and the Tây Sơn period fine art (1778–1802) carries a high realistic character (tính hiện thực cao); the portrait form-creating (tạo hình chân dung) of the religious and belief statues (tượng tôn giáo, tín ngưỡng) is shown clearly in the anatomical proportion (tỉ lệ giải phẫu), carving the portraits with the mood and feelings (tâm trạng, cảm xúc) like humans; in this stage, tombs (lăng mộ) also appear in many places across the country, with a system of round statues guarding the tombs (hệ thống tượng tròn canh giữ lăng mộ) (the ancestral statue (Tượng tổ), Tây Phương Pagoda, Hanoi; the statues of the marquis officials and animals (Tượng quan hầu và thú), Phú Đa Temple (đền Phú Đa), Vĩnh Phúc)
