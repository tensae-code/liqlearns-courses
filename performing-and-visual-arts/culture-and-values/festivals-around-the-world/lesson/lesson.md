# Festivals Around the World

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — Festivals Around the World — Syria's own teaching of this topic, kept as named parts: Teacher's Day, celebrated every March to honour the teacher; the Hijri New Year, tied to the Prophet's migration on 1 Muharram; and the Gregorian New Year tied to the birth of Christ. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — "The Holidays on Which Schools Close"

Teacher's Day, celebrated every March to honour the teacher; the Hijri New Year, tied to the Prophet's migration on 1 Muharram; and the Gregorian New Year tied to the birth of Christ.

## Sub lesson 2 — Enrichment "The Feasts and Occasions on Which Schools Close"

Mother's Day, at spring's start on 21 March — the children race to express their joy and love for their mothers, drawing gifts for them, making them with their hands or offering flowers; think of drawing an artwork to gift your mother; the Prophet's Birthday (al-Mawlid al-Nabawi) — decorations rise in the homes and markets, and Syrians exchange greetings; imagine and create a scene of the feast's appearances; and Glorious Christmas — people decorate their homes and the Christmas tree, and make a cave imitating the cave in which Jesus was born in Bethlehem in Palestine; in Syria every home has its Christmas tree, and the children wait for Baba Noel carrying gifts.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Syria's own way of teaching its arts?
