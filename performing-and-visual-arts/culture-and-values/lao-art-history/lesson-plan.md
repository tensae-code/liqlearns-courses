# Lesson plan — Lao Art History

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Lao Art History
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** lao Art History — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Arts & Crafts G11 teacher guide — Lao Art History** — the Sikhottabong period (6th–10th centuries) and the Lai Fong period (11th–13th), their Buddha images and the rock-carved Phra Siva Thep, with the spread of art along the Mekong; three great stupas in photographs and text — Wat Phou Champasak, That Ing Hang (about 25 m, rebuilt in the 16th century) and That Sikhottabong, and That Luang in Vientiane (45 m) with its building history; the four regional schools of Lao art — Luang Prabang (Wat Xieng Thong, its Buddha images), Vientiane, Champasak and Pakse — with photographs of temples and Buddha statues of each school; the two schools of the golden age (the Xaysetthathirath school and the Souriyavongsa school, 1638–1695), and its architecture — the Ho Phra Keo (built 1565), Wat Ong Teu and Wat Sisaket's ordination hall, in photographs; the art of the three successor kingdoms — Vientiane, Luang Prabang and Champasak — and of the Siamese period, with Wat Sisaket (built 1818) and its cloister of Buddha images described in detail
