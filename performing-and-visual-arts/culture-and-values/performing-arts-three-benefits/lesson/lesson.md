# Performing Arts — Three Benefits

## Do it first

Think of a feeling you could not say in plain words — but a song once said it for you. The Grade 8 book has a name for that service.

## Sub lesson 1 — The aesthetic benefit

The Grade 8 book (§4.1.1) names performing art's first benefit **the aesthetic benefit** — the benefit of beauty itself. Its example is exact: **poems with beautiful melodies express hidden feeling** — feelings a person cannot say plainly, a performed poem can carry out of hiding.

The benefit's reach in the book is wide: performing art serves **belief, governing skills, love, beauty and country** — the beautiful performance steadies the heart, trains the feelings, and makes the community's best things lovely enough to love. Beauty, in this book, is not a luxury; it is one of the ways a society stays good.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cultural (national) benefit

The second benefit (§4.1.2) is **the cultural — the national — benefit**. Performing arts, the book says, are **created from the community's experience and knowledge** and they **speak the community's own voice**: no other people can perform your people's life from the inside.

Because of that, performing art carries **identity and patriotism** as no imported art can. When a community performs its own experience, the young learn whose they are, and love of country is **transmitted** — not lectured, but sung, danced and acted into the next generation. The benefit opener (Chapter 4, printed p. 105) frames the set: performing arts enable the community's successful life — this cultural benefit is the middle of its three proofs.

## Sub lesson 3 — The three benefits together

Gather the set as the book builds it. Chapter 4 opens (§4.1, printed p. 105) by calling performing arts **branches enabling the community to have a successful life and a good way of living** — then proves it three times. **Aesthetic:** beauty carries hidden feeling (p. 105). **Cultural (national):** the community's own experience performed onward (pp. 105–106). **Psychological:** becoming, thinking and self-confidence strengthened, with healing and heroism (pp. 106–107) — that third benefit is taught in full in *How the Arts Help Us Learn*, where the Grade 8 book's own routing places its learning face.

Learn the three as one hand: beauty, belonging, and a steadied mind — what performing art gives the society that keeps it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: the Grade 8 book sorts performing art's benefits to society into three: the aesthetic, the cultural (national) and the psychological. Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write or say the lesson's key list from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. Show one part of the lesson with your hands, your voice or a drawing — as the part itself asks.
3. Teach the part you found hardest to someone at home, in three sentences.
