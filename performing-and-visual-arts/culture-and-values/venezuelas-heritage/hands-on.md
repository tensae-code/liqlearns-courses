# Hands-on — Venezuelas Heritage

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Petroglifos venezolanos
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the engraved stones left by Venezuela's first peoples across the country — drawings and symbols cut into rock, among the oldest art testimonies of the land. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Patrimonio Universal de Venezuela
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Venezuela's UNESCO World Heritage — Coro and its port La Vela (1993), Canaima National Park (1994), the Ciudad Universitaria de Caracas (2000), the Diablos Danzantes de Yare (2012) and the Parranda de San Pedro de Guatire y Guarenas (2013) — and why each was chosen. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Los objetos
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: heritage objects — movable material traces of human work: the instruments (cuatro, maracas, arpa), the traditional toys (gurrufío, trompo, papagayo) and the kitchen ware (budare, tinajero) a culture keeps. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Pueblos originarios de Venezuela y su arte
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the 44 named original peoples of Venezuela (Arawak, Caribe and Chibcha linguistic families), settled 12,000–15,000 years ago, and the character of their art — mainly mythical in function, made for magical, symbolic, ceremonial and festive practices, in harmony with their habitat. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Petroglifos de Venezuela
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: more than 500 petroglyph sites across the whole territory — savannas, mountains, riverbanks, jungle — serving as guides, territorial limits, meeting and ritual places; still used by Arawak shamans in initiation ceremonies, and heritage to be protected: never scrub, scrape, paint or mould them (slug first proposed in Book 1). Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## El saqueo cultural
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the book's recurring charge — heritage taken and still held abroad: the Kueka stone in Berlin, the Dresden Codex in Germany, Moctezuma's headdress in Vienna, Egypt's treasures in England, the USA and France, Iraq's legacy destroyed and looted since 2003 — and the peoples demanding their return. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.
