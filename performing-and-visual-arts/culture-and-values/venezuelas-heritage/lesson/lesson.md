# Venezuelas Heritage

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about this lesson's subject — venezuelas heritage. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Petroglifos venezolanos

The engraved stones left by Venezuela's first peoples across the country — drawings and symbols cut into rock, among the oldest art testimonies of the land.

## Sub lesson 2 — Patrimonio Universal de Venezuela

Venezuela's UNESCO World Heritage — Coro and its port La Vela (1993), Canaima National Park (1994), the Ciudad Universitaria de Caracas (2000), the Diablos Danzantes de Yare (2012) and the Parranda de San Pedro de Guatire y Guarenas (2013) — and why each was chosen.

## Sub lesson 3 — Los objetos

Heritage objects — movable material traces of human work: the instruments (cuatro, maracas, arpa), the traditional toys (gurrufío, trompo, papagayo) and the kitchen ware (budare, tinajero) a culture keeps.

## Sub lesson 4 — Pueblos originarios de Venezuela y su arte

The 44 named original peoples of Venezuela (Arawak, Caribe and Chibcha linguistic families), settled 12,000–15,000 years ago, and the character of their art — mainly mythical in function, made for magical, symbolic, ceremonial and festive practices, in harmony with their habitat.

## Sub lesson 5 — Petroglifos de Venezuela

More than 500 petroglyph sites across the whole territory — savannas, mountains, riverbanks, jungle — serving as guides, territorial limits, meeting and ritual places; still used by Arawak shamans in initiation ceremonies, and heritage to be protected: never scrub, scrape, paint or mould them (slug first proposed in Book 1).

## Sub lesson 6 — El saqueo cultural

The book's recurring charge — heritage taken and still held abroad: the Kueka stone in Berlin, the Dresden Codex in Germany, Moctezuma's headdress in Vienna, Egypt's treasures in England, the USA and France, Iraq's legacy destroyed and looted since 2003 — and the peoples demanding their return.
