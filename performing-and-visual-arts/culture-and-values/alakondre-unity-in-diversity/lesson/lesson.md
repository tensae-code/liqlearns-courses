# Alakondre Unity in Diversity

## Do it first

Quick start: from the title alone, guess what this lesson's work is. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Alakondre — one people, many cultures

Suriname's arts course is held together by one ideal: **Alakondre** — "Alakondre na wan, wan na Alakondre" (**Alakondre is one, one is Alakondre**): the country's many peoples and cultures as **one wealth**. The book matches it to the Hindustani **ekta** and the Javanese **sawiji**. The course's aims grow from it: **cultural self-awareness**, **making your own work together**, and **presenting it**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Alakondre deepened — total inclusivity

In the deepening year Alakondre is taken further. It was originally an **Afro-Surinamese word of the town-dwellers**, now taken over by **all Suriname's peoples**. In daily speech it names any mixture — Alakondre soup, Alakondre clothing — but its meaning is **total inclusivity**: the ideal of **many truths standing side by side, all equally true and equally valued**. **Sranan is its symbol** — by the book's glossary a language based on **all 22 languages** spoken or once spoken in Suriname, a language of everyone and of no one: the **"wan pipel" (one people)** idea.
