# Lesson plan — Sudanese Art Through the Ages

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Sudanese Art Through the Ages
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sudan's art from its oldest Nubian heritage to the Khartoum School — heritage, ancient Nubian art, Christian-era art, and Sudan's own modern school.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the visual-art and culture lessons before it in the ladder — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Design Lesson 3 "Sudanese Artistic Heritage"** — Sudan's artistic heritage studied from photographs — the woven birsh mat, the quffa basket, decorated jebena coffee pots and the Jirtig set — Sudan's society as a storehouse of authentic customs and values at the meeting of Africa's civilisations, and its folk heritage as what binds the nation together and keeps its authentic aesthetic and creative values alive; pupils draw the Jirtig tools from the pictures or from their own surroundings
2. **Book 1, Artistic Culture, "Art in the Ancient Nubian Era"** — Sudan's own oldest art — Nubian wall painting under Egyptian influence, Kerma pottery, the temples of Napata and Jebel Barkal, boats and cattle as subjects, and the technique: a gypsum ground with colours prepared from lime, sand, egg white and ground stone, violet much used and halos on religious figures; the works now kept in the Sudan National Museum, Khartoum
3. **Book 1, Artistic Culture, "Art in the Christian Era in Sudan"** — the art of Christian Nubia from the 6th century — the church as the mother art: Faras church in green brick coated with white gypsum, lotus carving and geometric church plans, later red sandstone and granite arches with carved friezes; sculpture held back by religious tradition to ornament units and tombstones, while wall painting carried the icons and Bible scenes of the Dongola churches, with calligraphic ornament where carving was barred
4. **Book 1, Artistic Culture, "Modern Plastic Art in Sudan"** — Sudan's own modern art — Bakht Al-Ruda's art teaching from the 1930s, teachers sent to Egypt and Britain, the design school of 1946 moved to the Technical Institute in 1951, and the qualified teachers who covered the coffeehouse walls and held exhibitions from the mid-1950s; above all the Khartoum School: Gordon Memorial College students discovering the Arabic letter's aesthetics and using calligraphy as a plastic element, panels smelling of the African and Arab environment, matured in Ibrahim Al-Salahi and Ahmed Mohamed Shibrain, praised by Osman Waqi Allah, and carried into sculpture, ceramics and illustrative drawing
