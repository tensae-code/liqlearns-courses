Verdict: COMPLETE — read in full, page by page: Drawing field (4 lessons), Design & Decoration field (5 lessons), Forming & Composition field (5 lessons) and the closing picture-models appendix. Sudan's own way: the book teaches in three fields — Drawing, Design & Decoration, Forming & Composition — anchored in the pupil's Sudan: Meroe's pyramids for light and shadow, the Jirtig tray and woven birsh mats for heritage, Nile-valley and Nubian pottery ornament for clay, and palm-frond and straw weaving for the loom. | Sub lesson 1 — Design Lesson 3 "Sudanese Artistic Heritage": Sudan's artistic heritage studied from photographs — the woven birsh mat, the quffa basket, decorated jebena coffee pots and the Jirtig set — Sudan's society as a storehouse of authentic customs and values at the meeting of Africa's civilisations, and its folk heritage as what binds the nation together and keeps its authentic aesthetic and creative values alive; pupils draw the Jirtig tools from the pictures or from their own surroundings (student book; original target `culture-and-values/sudanese-artistic-heritage`) | Book 1, printed p. 20 |
| Sub lesson 2 — Book 1, Artistic Culture, "Art in the Ancient Nubian Era": Sudan's own oldest art — Nubian wall painting under Egyptian influence, Kerma pottery, the temples of Napata and Jebel Barkal, boats and cattle as subjects, and the technique: a gypsum ground with colours prepared from lime, sand, egg white and ground stone, violet much used and halos on religious figures; the works now kept in the Sudan National Museum, Khartoum (student book; original target `culture-and-values/ancient-nubian-art-of-sudan`) | Book 2, printed p. 9 |
| Sub lesson 3 — Book 1, Artistic Culture, "Art in the Christian Era in Sudan": the art of Christian Nubia from the 6th century — the church as the mother art: Faras church in green brick coated with white gypsum, lotus carving and geometric church plans, later red sandstone and granite arches with carved friezes; sculpture held back by religious tradition to ornament units and tombstones, while wall painting carried the icons and Bible scenes of the Dongola churches, with calligraphic ornament where carving was barred (student book; original target `culture-and-values/christian-era-art-of-sudan`) | Book 2, printed p. 11 |
| Sub lesson 4 — Book 1, Artistic Culture, "Modern Plastic Art in Sudan": Sudan's own modern art — Bakht Al-Ruda's art teaching from the 1930s, teachers sent to Egypt and Britain, the design school of 1946 moved to the Technical Institute in 1951, and the qualified teachers who covered the coffeehouse walls and held exhibitions from the mid-1950s; above all the Khartoum School: Gordon Memorial College students discovering the Arabic letter's aesthetics and using calligraphy as a plastic element, panels smelling of the African and Arab environment, matured in Ibrahim Al-Salahi and Ahmed Mohamed Shibrain, praised by Osman Waqi Allah, and carried into sculpture, ceramics and illustrative drawing (student book; original target `culture-and-values/khartoum-school-and-modern-sudanese-art`) | Book 3, printed p. 31 |
