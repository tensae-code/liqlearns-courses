# Lesson plan — Dominican Carnival

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Dominican Carnival
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Dominican carnival — the country's popular festival of masquerades, comparsas, dances and rejoicing: its characters (the Guloyas of San Pedro de Macorís among them), its costumed comparsa troupes, and the costumes and masks (caretas) made for it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the Dominican carnival is** — carnival as a popular festival of masquerades, comparsas, dances and rejoicing — and valuing the cultural expressions of one's surroundings.
2. **Carnival characters and comparsas** — the Guloyas, typical folklore characters of San Pedro de Macorís, and the country's traditional carnival characters; the comparsa — the costumed troupe — and the principal comparsas of the country, researched and shared.
3. **Dressing for carnival: costume and masks** — designing and making a carnival costume from an old shirt covered in strips of newspaper or snack bags; carnival masks as a central element of the festival — the named careta types (Pepinera, Joyera) and the ritual fuete that accompanies the costume.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
