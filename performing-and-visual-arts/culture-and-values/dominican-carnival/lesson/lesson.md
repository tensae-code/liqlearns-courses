# Dominican Carnival

## Do it first

Quick start: Masks in the street. Music in the street. Everybody dancing. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the Dominican carnival is

**Carnival** is a **popular festival of masquerades, comparsas, dances and rejoicing**. The Grade 2 book teaches the **Dominican carnival** as the country's own celebration, and asks the learner to **value the cultural expressions of their surroundings** — the festival belongs to the streets and the people in them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carnival characters and comparsas

The carnival has its **characters**: the **Guloyas**, typical folklore characters of **San Pedro de Macorís**, stand among the **traditional carnival characters of the country**, known and valued as popular tradition. And it has its **comparsas** — **costumed troupes**; the class researches the country's **principal comparsas** and shares what it finds.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dressing for carnival: costume and masks

For carnival you **make your dress**: the Grade 4 book designs a **carnival costume from an old long-sleeved shirt**, covered in **overlapping strips of newspaper or snack bags**, pasted in circular rows from the hem up. The Grade 10 book studies the **mask** — the *careta* — as **a central element of the festival**, with named types such as the **Careta Pepinera** and the **Careta Joyera**, and the ritual **fuete** — an inflated cow-bladder whip — that accompanies the costume: *the other human face, to the sound of the streets*.
