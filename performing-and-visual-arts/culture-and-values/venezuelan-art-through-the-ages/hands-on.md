# Hands-on — Venezuelan Art Through the Ages

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Arte cinético venezolano
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Venezuela's kinetic art — works that move or seem to move, in the visual-kinesthetic language: Carlos Cruz-Diez's Fisicromía and his Chromosaturation chamber, Gego's Reticulárea, Alejandro Otero's Abra Solar in Plaza Venezuela. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Juan Félix Sánchez
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the book's example of the essentially aesthetic human — Juan Félix Sánchez of Mérida (1900–1997), farmer, painter, sculptor, engineer, architect and weaver, builder of the chapel of San Rafael de Mucuchíes, whose every work carries love, respect and harmony toward others and the environment. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Arte colonial venezolano: panorama
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Venezuela's colonial art (16th c.–1810) — European Baroque imposed through evangelisation, yet made with local materials and the spontaneous imprint of pardo, criollo and school painters; sacred images copied from European prints, and syncretism where native and African features enter the Christian image. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Arquitectura colonial venezolana
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: colonial architecture — simple churches with monumental curved façades and Islamic-origin wooden par y nudillo roofs; the grid town around its plaza; houses of brick and adobe with patios, corridors, balconies and grilled windows; and the star-planned coastal castles raised against pirates. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Escultura colonial e imaginería
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: colonial sculpture — the imaginería: polychromed, gold-leafed wooden saints, full-size, half-size and dressable processional images made to inspire veneration; and the goldsmiths' sacred vessels in silver and gold set with precious stones. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Pintura colonial venezolana
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: colonial painting — oil and tempera on canvas and cedar or mahogany boards; Virgins after Murillo, patron saints, purgatory and hell; painted by artisans and copyists, pardos and mulatos whose names were often lost, with indigenous and enslaved hands preparing boards, cloths and pigments; the named masters: Lerma y Villegas, Juan José Landaeta, the Painter of Tocuyo, Juan Pedro López. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Artes musicales en la Venezuela colonial
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: music in colonial Venezuela — the 18th-century flowering: La Filarmónica society in Caracas, cathedral chairs and music schools, ensembles of trumpets, chirimías and percussion, the first cathedral organ (1710); and the people's unwritten music — improvised, played by ear (tocar de oído, de guataca), inseparable from song and dance at feasts. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Artes escénicas en la Venezuela colonial
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the scenic arts of the colony — processions, dances within comedies and autos sacramentales; the first licence to perform a comedy in Caracas (1600); theatre in house corrals and for civic feasts; the Coliseo, Caracas's first theatre (1784, wood and bahareque, candle-lit, 1,500–1,800 seats); Andrés Bello's poem-drama Venezuela Consolada (1804). Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Juan Lovera y la pintura republicana
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Juan Lovera, who broke with religious painting to give the republic its civic images — El tumulto del 19 de abril de 1810 and La firma del Acta de la Independencia (1838), painting himself in as witness; the first half-century's painters, draughtsmen and lithographers, Carmelo Fernández drawing Bolívar's portrait that still appears on Venezuela's coinage. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Pintura académica de la segunda mitad del siglo XIX
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the great-format academic history painting of the Guzmán Blanco era, launched by the 1883 Bolívar centenary exhibition — Martín Tovar y Tovar, Antonio Herrera Toro, Cristóbal Rojas and Arturo Michelena painting battles, heroes and also poverty and death (Miranda en la Carraca, Vuelvan caras, Primera y última comunión). Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Escultura venezolana del siglo XIX
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: 19th-century Venezuelan sculpture under neoclassical academic rules — equestrian statues of heroes raised as patriotic propaganda, and funerary monuments (busts, angels, reliefs, Christs in marble); Eloy Palacios, Pérez Mujica, De la Cova and Lorenzo González trained in France, working in bronze. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Arquitectura venezolana del siglo XIX
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the neo-styles of the Guzmán Blanco rebuilding — Juan Hurtado Manrique's neo-Gothic Palacio de las Academias, Santa Capilla and Templo Masónico; Alejandro Chataing's theatres, Capitolio and Panteón Nacional works, the Arco de Carabobo, and the country's first reinforced-concrete construction. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Panorama del siglo XX venezolano
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the two great tendencies that determine Venezuela's 20th-century visual arts — the figurative, representing images of social and natural reality, and the abstract, progressively freeing itself from the real world's references. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## El Círculo de Bellas Artes y Armando Reverón
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the Círculo de Bellas Artes (1912) — artists under the Gómez dictatorship breaking with academic realism for landscape, still life and portrait painted outdoors; Armando Reverón's synthesis of tropical light into whites and ochres in his self-built world at El Castillete; Manuel Cabré's lifelong painting of the Waraira Repano. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## La Escuela de Caracas
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the Escuela de Caracas — the group that concentrated on local themes, landscape and nature: Marcos Castillo, López Méndez, Rafael Ramón González, Elisa Elvira Zuloaga, Antonio Alcántara, Luis Alberto Egea. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Los Disidentes
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Los Disidentes (1940s–50) — Venezuelans in Paris (Otero, Manaure, González Bogen, Guevara Moreno) proclaiming in their magazine the abandonment of figurative tradition for geometric abstraction, neoplasticism and constructivism. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Realismo social
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: social realism (1940s–70s) — art of Venezuela's sociocultural reality in kinship with Mexican muralism: Rengifo, Bracho, Poleo and Pedro León Castro carrying painting out of the museums onto great public murals of the people's struggles. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## El Taller de Arte Realista
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Gabriel Bracho's Realist Art Workshop — realist painting as an instrument of social emancipation; Claudio Cedeño's caricature, Armando Barrios and Nicolás Piquer painting Afro-descendant life; Centeno Vallenilla's criollo and indigenous themes; sculptors Francisco Narváez and Alejandro Colina. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Abstracción en Venezuela
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Venezuelan abstraction — realist references dissolved by synthesis into geometric forms, lines and colour stains, in conceptual representation and pure abstraction (Otero, Soto, Cruz-Diez, Manaure, Carreño, Gramcko, Pardo). Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Cinetismo
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: kineticism — Jesús Soto's Penetrables, entered by the spectator, and his Esfera Caracas, inspired by the llanos' "sun of the deer" at sunset; Cruz-Diez's fisicromías, flat colour surfaces that move virtually as the spectator walks — kin to op art (slug first proposed in Book 1). Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Pintura figurativa no realista
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: non-realist figurative painting of the late 20th century — conceptual, expressionist and surrealist figuration (Mario Abreu, Jacobo Borges, Alirio Palacios, Alirio Rodríguez); the Taller Libre de Arte; and informalist groups such as El Techo de la Ballena, leaving everything to the unforeseen and the chance of the pictorial matter. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Escultura y artes del fuego
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: sculpture experimenting with new materials in public space (Gego's environmental aerial structures, Zitman, Valera, Carreño), and the fire arts — ceramics, enamel, goldwork and glass — rooted in Venezuela's origins and long dismissed as "minor arts". Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Arquitectura moderna en Venezuela
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: oil-boom modern architecture — Carlos Raúl Villanueva's Museo de Bellas Artes (1938, beginning national art collecting) and his Ciudad Universitaria de Caracas, a synthesis of architecture, landscape and integrated art (Calder's Acoustic Clouds in the Aula Magna); the Bauhaus echo in the architects who followed him. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.
