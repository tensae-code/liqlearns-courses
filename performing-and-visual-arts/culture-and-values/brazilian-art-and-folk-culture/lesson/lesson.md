# Brazilian Art and Folk Culture

## Do it first

Look at the sub lesson list. Pick the part you already know something about, and say what you know. Then start at Sub lesson 1 — every part builds on the one before it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What culture and popular culture are

What culture and popular culture are; folk culture made by the people and handed down orally (artesanato, festas, cantigas de roda, Festas Juninas) against erudite culture (Folha 5 "A arte é para todos?", Brazil's *ARTE — Ensino Médio*, printed p. 83).

## Sub lesson 2 — Brazilian folk art

Brazilian folk art: Mestre Vitalino's clay figures of Northeastern life, and the carrancas, great carved heads on São Francisco river boats; carving a miniature carranca in soap or chalk (Folha 5, Brazil's *ARTE — Ensino Médio*, printed p. 85).

## Sub lesson 3 — The French Artistic Mission (1816) and Neoclassicism

The French Artistic Mission (1816) and Neoclassicism: Pedro Américo's Independência ou Morte in an imported style; Debret's watercolours recording the daily life of Black, Indigenous and white Brazilians (Folha 14 "Arte brasileira: uma ilustre desconhecida", Brazil's *ARTE — Ensino Médio*, printed p. 218).

## Sub lesson 4 — Romantic indianism

Romantic indianism: Medeiros' Iracema, the Indigenous heroine as the image of the new nation (Folha 14, Brazil's *ARTE — Ensino Médio*, printed p. 220).

## Sub lesson 5 — Paraná's art

Paraná's art: Alfredo Andersen, "father of Paraná painting"; the Paranismo movement and its araucaria symbols; Lange de Morretes' geometric pinha pavement in Curitiba; stylising your own city's symbol into a repeating pattern (Folha 15 "Arte do Paraná ou arte no Paraná?", Brazil's *ARTE — Ensino Médio*, printed p. 236).
