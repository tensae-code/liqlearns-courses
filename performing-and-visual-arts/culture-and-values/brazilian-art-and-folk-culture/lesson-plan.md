# Lesson plan — Brazilian Art and Folk Culture

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Brazilian Art and Folk Culture
**Place in the curriculum:** Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** brazilian Art and Folk Culture — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier culture and values lessons — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What culture and popular culture are** — what culture and popular culture are; folk culture made by the people and handed down orally (artesanato, festas, cantigas de roda, Festas Juninas) against erudite culture
2. **Brazilian folk art** — Brazilian folk art: Mestre Vitalino's clay figures of Northeastern life, and the carrancas, great carved heads on São Francisco river boats; carving a miniature carranca in soap or chalk
3. **The French Artistic Mission (1816) and Neoclassicism** — the French Artistic Mission (1816) and Neoclassicism: Pedro Américo's Independência ou Morte in an imported style; Debret's watercolours recording the daily life of Black, Indigenous and white Brazilians
4. **Romantic indianism** — Romantic indianism: Medeiros' Iracema, the Indigenous heroine as the image of the new nation
5. **Paraná's art** — Paraná's art: Alfredo Andersen, "father of Paraná painting"; the Paranismo movement and its araucaria symbols; Lange de Morretes' geometric pinha pavement in Curitiba; stylising your own city's symbol into a repeating pattern

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
