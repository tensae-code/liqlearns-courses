## Sub lesson 1 — Vietnam Grade 6 Mĩ thuật 6

The heritage — jewellery of animal bone from the Hòa Bình culture; a human face carved on the rock wall of Đồng Nội cave (Hòa Bình culture); a stone axe of the Tràng An culture (about 300,000 years before the Common Era, by the book's dating); a stone pestle and grinding slab of the Hòa Bình culture; the cultures Tràng An, Hòa Bình (about 10,000 years BC) and Bắc Sơn (10,000–8,000 years BC); Vietnam's prehistoric art survives on stone, earth, animal bone and shells, with geometric patterns or simple engraved lines showing human faces and animals — and the excavated objects show an early aesthetic sense bound up with the tools of labour, like the stone axe and pestle; the heritage of the cultures Phùng Nguyên, Đồng Đậu, Gò Mun, Đông Sơn, Sa Huỳnh and Óc Eo — a bronze figure of the Đông Sơn culture, a pottery jar of Óc Eo, a pottery lamp of Sa Huỳnh — and above all the Đông Sơn bronze drum: every drum has a beautiful shape and balanced proportions, each a harmony of a part-sphere, a cylinder and a truncated cone, decorated with a system of patterns arranged in bands turning counter-clockwise, like the rotation of the Earth, carrying many deep meanings

## Sub lesson 2 — Vietnam Grade 10 Mĩ thuật 10 — Lí luận và lịch sử mĩ thuật

Vietnamese fine art (mĩ thuật Việt Nam) passes through thousands of years of history with many values preserved and kept to the later generations (các thế hệ sau này); Vietnamese fine art of the prehistoric and early-historic periods (thời kì tiền sử và sơ sử) was formed from the practical activities attached to making utensils (chế tác đồ dùng) (applied fine art (mĩ thuật ứng dụng)); the Đông Sơn bronze drum (Trống đồng Đông Sơn) is considered one of the most important archaeological artefacts (hiện vật khảo cổ học quan trọng nhất) found in Southeast Asia in general and Vietnam in particular (the lamp plate decorating a deer statue (Đĩa đèn trang trí tượng hươu), bronze (đồng), Đông Sơn culture; the stone-engraved painting of the horned human face (Tranh khắc đá hình mặt người có sừng), Hoà Bình culture, in the Đồng Nội cave (hang Đồng Nội); the cow statue (Tượng bò), fired earth (đất nung), Đồng Đậu culture, 3,500 years ago); and by the medieval period, the fine art of the Lý period (1009–1225) marks the development of the home country's Buddhist art (nghệ thuật Phật giáo nước nhà); besides the unique architectures (kiến trúc độc đáo), the sculpture and carving art (nghệ thuật điêu khắc, chạm khắc) of this stage is quite developed, the form-creating material mainly is stone (chất liệu tạo hình chủ yếu là đá) (the Buddha statue of the Lý period (Tượng Phật thời Lý), Phật Tích Pagoda, Bắc Ninh; Chùa Một Cột, Hanoi — the pagoda was built from the Lý period and restored through many periods (được trùng tu qua nhiều thời kì))

## Sub lesson 3 — Vietnam Grade 10 Art — Sculpture

Vietnam's traditional form-creating art appeared from the carvings and engravings on cave walls in the Hoà Bình culture period (Hoà Bình, Quảng Bình, Thái Nguyên) through the Đông Sơn culture (about 800–200 BC), lasting across Phú Thọ, Yên Bái, Hoà Bình, Thanh Hoá, Nghệ An and Hà Tĩnh with its centre at the Hùng Temple area; the preserved fine-art heritage shows the nation's special values through its long history of building and defending the country — a plaster cast of the engraved image on the Đồng Nội cave wall (Hoà Bình culture, about 10,000 years BC, Lạc Thuỷ, Hoà Bình), the Đông Sơn bronze statue of two people carrying each other (Thanh Hoá), fired-earth artefacts at the Chăm towers (7th–14th century, Mỹ Sơn, Quảng Nam) and the wooden relief of Chu Quyến communal house (about 17th century, Ba Vì, Hanoi)

## Sub lesson 4 — Vietnam Grade 11 Mĩ thuật 11 — Thiết kế công nghiệp

About 3,000 years ago, the residents of the Phùng Nguyên and Đồng Đậu cultures used jewellery in a diverse and very meticulous way — the main types are bracelets, earrings and bead strings, the raw materials chosen being stones with beautiful colours and fine grain, and the crafting technique had especially reached a high peak, some bracelets and earrings creating quite perfect round shapes with smooth, polished surfaces (a tube-shaped nephrite (đá ngọc) bracelet of the Đồng Đậu culture, about 3,000–3,500 years ago); entering the country-building period, some jewellery used as amulets (bùa hộ mệnh) became indispensable objects in spiritual rituals, the main materials being coloured stone, agate, bronze and glass, and in the archaeological sites of the Đông Sơn, Sa Huỳnh, Đồng Nai and Óc Eo cultures many extremely meticulously and exquisitely crafted jewellery items have been discovered — they not only made the user luxurious and noble but showed the wealth, strength and power of the person at the head, the bronze hairpins, the bracelets with many small bells, the agate and coloured-glass earrings also showing the period's diversity of jewellery types, so that we can vaguely imagine the jingling sound from the small bells on the bronze armlets and leg ornaments of Đông Sơn residents following the dance steps of that time's spiritual rituals (a two-animal-head stone earring of the Sa Huỳnh culture, about 2,000–2,500 years ago)

## Sub lesson 5 — Vietnam Grade 11 Mĩ thuật 11 — Thiết kế thời trang

In Vietnam, by the documents and the archaeological artefacts from the Đông Sơn culture period, the Vietnamese people used the accessories going with clothes such as head hats (mũ đội đầu), belts (thắt lưng),… (a head hat and the accessories on the woman statue image at the short sword hilt (chuôi kiếm ngắn), Đông Sơn culture)
