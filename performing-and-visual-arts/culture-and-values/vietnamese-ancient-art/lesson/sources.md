| Vietnam — Lesson 3 — Vietnamese fine art (mĩ thuật Việt Nam) passes through thousands of years of history with many values preserved and kept to the later generations (các thế hệ sau này); Vietnamese fine art of the prehistoric and early-historic periods (thời kì tiền sử và sơ sử) was formed from the practical activities attached to making utensils (chế tác đồ dùng) (applied fine art (mĩ thuật ứng dụng)); the Đông Sơn bronze drum (Trống đồng Đông Sơn) is considered one of the most important archaeological artefacts (hiện vật khảo cổ học quan trọng nhất) found in Southeast Asia in general and Vietnam in particular (the lamp plate decorating a deer statue (Đĩa đèn trang trí tượng hươu), bronze (đồng), Đông Sơn culture; the stone-engraved painting of the horned human face (Tranh khắc đá hình mặt người có sừng), Hoà Bình culture, in the Đồng Nội cave (hang Đồng Nội); the cow statue (Tượng bò), fired earth (đất nung), Đồng Đậu culture, 3,500 years ago); and by the medieval period, the fine art of the Lý period (1009–1225) marks the development of the home country's Buddhist art (nghệ thuật Phật giáo nước nhà); besides the unique architectures (kiến trúc độc đáo), the sculpture and carving art (nghệ thuật điêu khắc, chạm khắc) of this stage is quite developed, the form-creating material mainly is stone (chất liệu tạo hình chủ yếu là đá) (the Buddha statue of the Lý period (Tượng Phật thời Lý), Phật Tích Pagoda, Bắc Ninh; Chùa Một Cột, Hanoi — the pagoda was built from the Lý period and restored through many periods (được trùng tu qua nhiều thời kì)) (student book; original target `culture-and-values/vietnamese-prehistoric-art`) | Book 2, printed p. 26 |

| Vietnam — Bài 1, Did you know — the beauty of ancient Vietnamese jewellery — about 3,000 years ago, the residents of the Phùng Nguyên and Đồng Đậu cultures used jewellery in a diverse and very meticulous way — the main types are bracelets, earrings and bead strings, the raw materials chosen being stones with beautiful colours and fine grain, and the crafting technique had especially reached a high peak, some bracelets and earrings creating quite perfect round shapes with smooth, polished surfaces (a tube-shaped nephrite (đá ngọc) bracelet of the Đồng Đậu culture, about 3,000–3,500 years ago); entering the country-building period, some jewellery used as amulets (bùa hộ mệnh) became indispensable objects in spiritual rituals, the main materials being coloured stone, agate, bronze and glass, and in the archaeological sites of the Đông Sơn, Sa Huỳnh, Đồng Nai and Óc Eo cultures many extremely meticulously and exquisitely crafted jewellery items have been discovered — they not only made the user luxurious and noble but showed the wealth, strength and power of the person at the head, the bronze hairpins, the bracelets with many small bells, the agate and coloured-glass earrings also showing the period's diversity of jewellery types, so that we can vaguely imagine the jingling sound from the small bells on the bronze armlets and leg ornaments of Đông Sơn residents following the dance steps of that time's spiritual rituals (a two-animal-head stone earring of the Sa Huỳnh culture, about 2,000–2,500 years ago) (student book; original target `culture-and-values/vietnamese-prehistoric-art`) | Book 4, printed p. 7 |
