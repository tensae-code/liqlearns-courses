This lesson is Vietnamese Ancient Art.

Vietnamese Ancient Art — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: The heritage — jewellery of animal bone from the Hòa Bình culture; a human face carved on the rock wall of Đồng Nội cave (Hòa Bình culture); a stone axe of the Tràng An culture (about 300,000 years before the Common Era, by the book's dating); a stone pestle and grinding slab of the Hòa Bình culture; the cultures Tràng An, Hòa Bình (about 10,000 years BC) and Bắc Sơn (10,000–8,000 years BC); Vietnam's prehistoric art survives on stone, earth, animal bone and shells, with geometric patterns or simple engraved lines showing human faces and animals — and the excavated objects show an early aesthetic sense bound up with the tools of labour, like the stone axe and pestle; the heritage of the cultures Phùng Nguyên, Đồng Đậu, Gò Mun, Đông Sơn, Sa Huỳnh and Óc Eo — a bronze figure of the Đông Sơn culture, a pottery jar of Óc Eo, a pottery lamp of Sa Huỳnh — and above all the Đông Sơn bronze drum: every drum has a beautiful shape and balanced proportions, each a harmony of a part-sphere, a cylinder and a truncated cone, decorated with a system of patterns arranged in bands turning counter-clockwise, like the rotation of the Earth, carrying many deep meanings.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
