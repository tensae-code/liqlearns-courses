| Kazakhstan — § 7. Қазақстан сәулет өнері (заманауи және тарихи) (Kazakhstan's architecture — contemporary and historical) — what it teaches: each epoch of Kazakhstan architecture's development has its own peculiarity — it was connected with the social-economic state, construction's development and varied historical epochs' material and spiritual culture; Kazakhstan architecture's many-centuries history takes its beginning from the late Palaeolithic epochs, and national architecture, formed in the Middle Ages, develops until today; medieval Kazakhstan's architecture: the VII–XII centuries' construction takes an important place in architecture's history and in national traditions' formation — this epoch's cities and settlements consisted of a цитадель (citadel — бекініс, қоныс) and a шахристан (shahristan — the territory where the city's economic and public life concentrated); between the beginning of the X century and the XIII century, in connection with the Islam religion's wide spread, mosques and mausoleums began to be built in the cities, and the architecture monuments around Taraz show that epoch's architecture level — their oldest is the X–XI centuries' burnt-brick-built Бабажа қатын кесенесі (Babaja Khatun mausoleum, Zhambyl oblast), whose form comes in a cube-like shape; the XI–XII centuries' Айша бибі кесенесі (Aisha Bibi mausoleum, Zhambyl oblast) is also one of architecture's prominent samples — the building is one-chambered, its form is finished square, and its corners are raised through column-supports; the pictured monuments: Babaja Khatun and Aisha Bibi (both early Karakhanid style) (student book; original target `culture-and-values/kazakhstan-architecture-heritage`) | Book 7, printed p. 28 |
| Kazakhstan — § 7 (continued). Қазақстан сәулет өнерінің кезеңдері (The periods of Kazakhstan's architecture) — what it teaches: one of the specially-built buildings of the XIV–XV centuries' construction is the Қожа Ахмет Ясауи кесенесі (Khoja Akhmet Yassawi mausoleum, early Amir Temur style) — the mausoleum consists of a tomb chamber (көрхана) and rooms of varied purpose besides it; in XIX-century Kazakhstan varied styles met and a special architectural culture formed — alongside Central Asia's traditional constructions, buildings in the neoclassicism, eclectica and modern European styles began to be built, public buildings and new-type mosques and madrasas increased, and in public and administrative buildings the neoclassical tone dominated: the Oral (Uralsk) city's 1896-built Commercial Bank building was built in that sample, and the 1892-built Zharkent mosque-madrasa was based on the eclectica style, in which Central Asian and Chinese architecture masters harmonised; in the XX century, with new materials and constructions arising, by rejecting eclectica national architecture began to develop in a completely new direction — construction's being convenient and firm was specially regarded, and the constructivism direction took speed: the Kazakh ASSR's former Government House raised in Almaty in 1931 (now the Kazakhstan-British Technical University) belongs to such a building; in 1982 another special sample of architecture rose in Almaty — the М. О. Әуезов атындағы Қазақ мемлекеттік академиялық драма театры (M. O. Auezov Kazakh State Academic Drama Theatre, industrial-romantic style), and the Ш. Уәлихановтың «Алтынемел» мемлекеттік мемориалды музейі (Sh. Ualikhanov "Altynemel" state memorial museum, 1985, in Shankhai village, Almaty oblast) can also be a revived sample of national architecture; independent Kazakhstan's architecture entered development's new stage after independence was proclaimed — in 1992 the decision was taken to build the ҚР Президенті Кешенін (the RK President's Complex), the present First President's Residence in Almaty city, a new turning point in national architecture's development; in this epoch's architecture the stylisation method dominated, varied historical styles and mixed kinds were used, especially dominating in religious buildings — its bright example is the М. Жүсіп атындағы мешіт (M. Zhusup mosque) built in Pavlodar city in 2001 (Central Asia neoregionalism); independence and national consciousness's growth also appeared in the architecture of the country's new capital — Nur-Sultan city — where in the capital's contemporary architecture the Eastern tradition and the West's bold ideas met, and today Nur-Sultan is one of the world's most beautiful capitals; the Қиіз үй (felt yurt) is the masterpiece of the world's architecture — the yurt's inner space is decorated suiting its inhabitants' taste, comfortable and convenient in field conditions, and yurts' volumes are also varied: alongside small yurts used everyday there are big орда (orda) and palaces (student book; original target `culture-and-values/kazakhstan-architecture-heritage`) | Book 7, printed p. 29 |
