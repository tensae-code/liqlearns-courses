Kazakhstan architecture's many-centuries history takes its beginning from the late Palaeolithic epochs, and national architecture, formed in the Middle Ages, develops until today; medieval Kazakhstan's architecture: the VII–XII centuries' construction takes an important place in architecture's history and in national traditions' formation — this epoch's cities and settlements consisted of a цитадель (citadel — бекініс, қоныс) and a шахристан (shahristan — the territory where the city's economic and public life concentrated); between the beginning of the X century and the XIII century, in connection with the Islam religion's wide spread, mosques and mausoleums began to be built in the cities, and the architecture monuments around Taraz show that epoch's architecture level — their oldest is the X–XI centuries' burnt-brick-built Бабажа қатын кесенесі (Babaja Khatun mausoleum, Zhambyl oblast), whose form comes in a cube-like shape; the XI–XII centuries' Айша бибі кесенесі (Aisha Bibi mausoleum, Zhambyl oblast) is also one of architecture's prominent samples — the building is one-chambered, its form is finished square, and its corners are raised through column-supports; the pictured monuments: Babaja Khatun and Aisha Bibi (both early Karakhanid style); what it teaches: one of the specially-built buildings of the XIV–XV centuries' construction is the Қожа Ахмет Ясауи кесенесі (Khoja Akhmet Yassawi mausoleum, early Amir Temur style) — the mausoleum consists of a tomb chamber (көрхана) and rooms of varied purpose besides it; in XIX-century Kazakhstan varied styles met and a special architectural culture formed — alongside Central Asia's traditional constructions, buildings in the neoclassicism, eclectica and modern European styles began to be built, public buildings and new-type mosques and madrasas increased, and in public and administrative buildings the neoclassical tone dominated: the Oral (Uralsk) city's 1896-built Commercial Bank building was built in that sample, and the 1892-built Zharkent mosque-madrasa was based on the eclectica style, in which Central Asian and Chinese architecture masters harmonised; in the XX century, with new materials and constructions arising, by rejecting eclectica national architecture began to develop in a completely new direction — construction's being convenient and firm was specially regarded, and the constructivism direction took speed: the Kazakh ASSR's former Government House raised in Almaty in 1931 (now the Kazakhstan-British Technical University) belongs to such a building; in 1982 another special sample of architecture rose in Almaty — the М. О. Әуезов атындағы Қазақ мемлекеттік академиялық драма театры (M. O. Auezov Kazakh State Academic Drama Theatre, industrial-romantic style), and the Ш. Уәлихановтың «Алтынемел» мемлекеттік мемориалды музейі (Sh. Жүсіп атындағы мешіт (M.
