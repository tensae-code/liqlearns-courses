# Our Community's Values

## Do it first

Watch your community for one day, in your mind. A guest arrives — what happens? Two neighbours quarrel — who settles it? Lunch comes — who eats, and from what? The good, repeated, honoured ways a community **does** things have a name in Grade 2's book: **cultural values**. This lesson names them — from the book's own list.

## Sub lesson 1 — Naming our values

Grade 2 defines cultural values in one working sentence: **cultural values are what the community does**.

Then it lists what this community does — learn the list as the book gives it: **receiving guests; mutual respect and love; the shimglina system** — elder mediation and reconciliation, where the elders settle quarrels and make peace; **eating together from one mesob; helping each other; loving one's country, and the feeling of heroism; various community activities; crafts**, and the like.

The section opens with a **strip of pictures of values in action**, and its exercise gives the practice folded into this sub lesson: **from the pictures, name the cultural values you know in your area**. The pictured scenes include a traditional painting of women with coffee and mesob scenes, people **greeting an elder**, community gatherings, people **feasting together around a mesob**, a woman **giving to a girl**. Every value on the list can be **seen** — that is why the arts can teach them.

## Sub lesson 2 — What our community values

The chapter that holds the list opens wider. Its aims: **state local cultural values**, and appreciate and perform the **music, dances, colours, drama and theatre that express them**. Its introduction names even the chapter's history: the story of the Battle of Adwa stands inside the values chapter, because **loving one's country and heroism are values**, and Adwa is their story.

Its prior-knowledge questions are for every learner: **name good deeds known in your area; say how cultural values are expressed.**

The chapter's review, folded in here, checks the whole title of lessons: cultural values can be expressed by **music, theatre and drawing — all of them**; and its short answer is the learner's own list: **list the cultural values practised in your area**. The summary adds the everyday faces of the same values: **dress, eating, dance, coffee-making, and expressions of national pride** — values you can wear, taste, dance and brew.

## Sub lesson 3 — Values in art — being an example at home

Grade 3 brings the values home, with a word worth keeping: **exemplarity**.

**Exemplarity means the good, community-accepted actions parents show their children in the family** — **fair division of work, charitable deeds, respect, helping each other**, and the like.

Its picture is the values list at the family table: **eating together**.

A value sung in a song can be forgotten by evening. A value **shown at home, every day**, becomes the child's own way. That is why this lesson — and the whole values teaching — ends not with a list, but with an example: be one.

## Say it to your companion

Read the book's values list to your companion. For each value, they must point to (or remember) a picture or a real moment that shows it. Miss none of the list.

## Do It Yourself

1. List the cultural values practised in your area — the review's own short-answer task.
2. Draw one value in action: greeting an elder, sharing a mesob, helping a neighbour.
3. Do one exemplary action at home today: a fair share of work, a charitable deed, a helping hand — without being asked.
4. Ask an elder how shimglina settles a quarrel. Listen well; retell it at home.
