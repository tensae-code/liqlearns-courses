# Easter in Song and Custom

## Do it first

Quick start: hold an egg in your hand. Before anyone paints it — what could its painting say? Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — We celebrate Easter: painting eggs

At Easter, eggs are **painted**. The egg is a **symbol of life and nature's renewal**. The **red** of Christian Easter eggs stands for the **fire's purifying power** and for **Christ's blood**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Încondeiere: the wax-pattern eggs

**Încondeiere** is the **wax-pattern egg art** of the folk masters of **Bucovina and Maramureș**. Its motifs are **geometric**, **cosmic** (stars, Sun, Moon), **floral** (carnation, fir sprig, wheat ear) and **Christian** (cross, fish) — **repeated and set symmetrically**, so the egg suggests **perfection and harmony**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dyes from plants

Eggs are încondeiate with **beeswax** and dyed with **vegetable colours**: **red** from raw beetroot juice or boiled red-onion leaves; **yellow** from orange, lemon, onion peel and carrot; **green** from boiled nettle juice or spinach with vinegar.

## Sub lesson 4 — Cheerful Easter eggs: the egg doll

A blown egg becomes a **doll**: **pierce the egg at both ends and blow out the contents**, **draw eyes, nose and mouth**, **fix it on a cardboard roll** with glue, use **thread strands for hair** and **dress the doll in a piece of fabric**. The page's project is a **little bee made from an egg**.
