# Dance in Society

## Do it first

Think of the last celebration you saw. Was there dancing? Who danced, and when in the day did it begin? This lesson is about that place dance holds.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dance's place in the community (teacher guide — evidence)

*This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence — it teaches how the teacher's book frames dance in society.* The Grade 5 guide (§4.1.3) gives dance a **place in the community**: dance is not an extra at the community's occasions but one of the ways the community **gathers, celebrates, mourns and remembers together**.

Where the people are together, the guide's framing runs, their dance is with them — at festivals, at weddings, at the occasions that mark a life. To study dance in society is to study the community itself, moving.

## Sub lesson 2 — The customary folk dance (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's next section (§4.1.4) names the dance this lesson exists for: the **customary folk dance** — ልማዳዊ የሃገረሰብ ውዝዋዜ — the dance a people **keeps by custom**: learned without lessons, done the people's own way, at their own occasions, in their own dress and to their own music.

"Customary" is the load-bearing word. A customary folk dance belongs to the whole people, is guarded by custom rather than by any school, and changes only as the people themselves change. When you see one danced truly, you are seeing the community's memory with its feet.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: dance holds a place in the community's life — and the people's customary folk dance is that place made visible (teacher-guide evidence). Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write or say the lesson's key list from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. Show one part of the lesson with your hands, your voice or a drawing — as the part itself asks.
3. Teach the part you found hardest to someone at home, in three sentences.
