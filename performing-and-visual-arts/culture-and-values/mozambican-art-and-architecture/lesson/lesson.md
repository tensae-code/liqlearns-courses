# Mozambican Art and Architecture

## Do it first

Quick game: name a painting or a wall you have seen in a public place. Who made it? What story does it tell? Keep your answer — this lesson is about your own country's painting and building.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mozambican painting and its painters

Mozambique's painting has **three phases**. First, the **prehistoric rock paintings** — hunting and worship — at named sites province by province: in Tete (Chiloane, Chifumbazi, Cachombo), Manica (Monte Chimbanda and Monte Chinhamapere), Nampula (Riane, Monapo, Nacavala, Campote, Murrupula, Mogovolas, Ribaué) and Niassa (Marrupa). Second, the **colonial phase**, painting the rising national consciousness and the struggle for independence — Malangatana, 1971. Third, the **independence and contemporary phase** — great portraits and murals on public walls, like João Craveirinha's Heroes' Square mural, 1979.

Its outstanding painters: **Malangatana Valente Ngwenha, Elias Abdula Naguib, Noel Langa, Roberto Chichorro and João Timane**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mozambican architecture: the traditional house and the city

**Traditional (rural, popular) architecture** builds the house in **three forms**: a **circular plan with a conical roof**, a **quadrangular plan with a pyramidal four-water roof**, and a **rectangular plan with one or two waters** — built of thatch, grass, mud, wood, bamboo, stakes and other local materials, its knowledge passed from generation to generation.

**Urban architecture** builds in conventional materials — block, brick and cement. The outstanding architect is **José Forjaz** (BCI bank headquarters, Maputo, 2017).
