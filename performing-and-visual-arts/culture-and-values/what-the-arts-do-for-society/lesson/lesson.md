# What the Arts Do for Society

## Do it first

Look at your town in your mind. One thing there is harmful. One thing there is beautiful. This lesson is about the arts as the tools a society uses to speak about both — and it begins by asking the plainest question of all: what IS art?

## Sub lesson 1 — What is art?

The Grade 6 book opens its whole course by asking **what art is** — and answers with people. **Sakadeg** is presented as defining art through **the skilled person practising a craft** — art as mastery in hands. **Rumi** shows art's **two faces in literature and in the arts** — art as both the making and the made. And **ብልህነት (akilignnet)** is named: art as **ingenuity** — the cleverness that finds a way. Three doors into one house: art is skill, art is expression, art is ingenuity.

## Sub lesson 2 — The role of the arts in society

What do the arts **do**? The Grade 4 lessons answer in a chain. The arts hold a **role in life** and carry **culture and heritage** — the Lalibela churches and the festivals in pictures are heritage you can point at. The arts walk through **history** with us, and they go **to work**: arts in **industries** and arts in **professions** — the designer's, the builder's, the potter's, the musician's daily bread. Art expresses feelings, passes heritage down, gathers a community at its festivals, and earns its makers a living: four roles, one craft family.

## Sub lesson 3 — Art speaks for society — and against harm

The Grade 5 book gives art a voice: **'Art Speaks for Society'** — art as a **lens on community life, expressing feeling and passing heritage**, and **harmful practices** are named plainly for children to know them. Beside it stands the sorting lesson: **Useful and Harmful Traditions** — some traditions benefit people and land and are kept with pride; some harm, and art is one of the voices that says so. And where does art speak loudest? In **dances and songs against alcohol, drugs and AIDS** — and in the **songs for forests** that the Grade 4 book sings with the professionals' chapter: art defending the land it lives on.

## Sub lesson 4 — Art for current issues

Each grade takes the theme further: **Art for Current Issues**. Grade 6 applies art to **deforestation, pollution and girls' education** — the issues named in its own chapter. Grade 7 gives the theme a whole chapter (Chapter 5). Grade 8 works it in full: **the role of art in reducing harmful practices and solving social problems**, with the community's own list — **flood, erosion, land degradation, and early marriage through abduction** — and **art works against alcohol and drug abuse**. The pattern across the grades is the lesson: as the pupil grows, the issues grow with them, and art stays the tool for speaking.

## Sub lesson 5 — Nature through art

The last word belongs to the first artist. **Nature through art**: **appreciating nature's own art forms** — the shapes and colours of trees, mountains, rivers and sky; **drawing natural things** from life; and **nature songs**, the singing that answers what the eye has seen. The books' chain closes here: art begins in skill, serves society, speaks on its issues — and everywhere it goes, it is copying, honouring and caring for the artwork it was born inside: the natural world.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion one current issue. They must answer with an art form and a message: 'a poster — plant trees, the forest is our roof.' Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a poster for one current issue in your area, in the emblem-and-poster lesson's way.
2. Write three lines: one useful tradition of your area, one harmful one, and what art could say about each.
3. Draw one artwork of nature near your home — a tree, a river, a hill — from life.
