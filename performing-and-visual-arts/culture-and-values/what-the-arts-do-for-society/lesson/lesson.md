# What the Arts Do for Society

## Do it first

Look at your town in your mind. One thing there is harmful. One thing there is beautiful. This lesson is about the arts as the tools a society uses to speak about both — and it begins by asking the plainest question of all: what IS art?

## Sub lesson 1 — What is art?

The Grade 6 book opens its whole course by asking **what art is** — and answers with people. **Sakadeg** is presented as defining art through **the skilled person practising a craft** — art as mastery in hands. **Rumi** shows art's **two faces in literature and in the arts** — art as both the making and the made. And **ብልህነት (akilignnet)** is named: art as **ingenuity** — the cleverness that finds a way. Three doors into one house: art is skill, art is expression, art is ingenuity.

## Sub lesson 2 — The role of the arts in society

What do the arts **do**? The Grade 4 lessons answer in a chain. The arts hold a **role in life** and carry **culture and heritage** — the Lalibela churches and the festivals in pictures are heritage you can point at. The arts walk through **history** with us, and they go **to work**: arts in **industries** and arts in **professions** — the designer's, the builder's, the potter's, the musician's daily bread. Art expresses feelings, passes heritage down, gathers a community at its festivals, and earns its makers a living: four roles, one craft family.

## Sub lesson 3 — Art speaks for society — and against harm

The Grade 5 book gives art a voice: **'Art Speaks for Society'** — art as a **lens on community life, expressing feeling and passing heritage**, and **harmful practices** are named plainly for children to know them. Beside it stands the sorting lesson: **Useful and Harmful Traditions** — some traditions benefit people and land and are kept with pride; some harm, and art is one of the voices that says so. And where does art speak loudest? In **dances and songs against alcohol, drugs and AIDS** — and in the **songs for forests** that the Grade 4 book sings with the professionals' chapter: art defending the land it lives on.

## Sub lesson 4 — Art for current issues

Each grade takes the theme further: **Art for Current Issues**. Grade 6 applies art to **deforestation, pollution and girls' education** — the issues named in its own chapter. Grade 7 gives the theme a whole chapter (Chapter 5). Grade 8 works it in full: **the role of art in reducing harmful practices and solving social problems**, with the community's own list — **flood, erosion, land degradation, and early marriage through abduction** — and **art works against alcohol and drug abuse**. The pattern across the grades is the lesson: as the pupil grows, the issues grow with them, and art stays the tool for speaking.

## Sub lesson 5 — Nature through art

The last word belongs to the first artist. **Nature through art**: **appreciating nature's own art forms** — the shapes and colours of trees, mountains, rivers and sky; **drawing natural things** from life; and **nature songs**, the singing that answers what the eye has seen. The books' chain closes here: art begins in skill, serves society, speaks on its issues — and everywhere it goes, it is copying, honouring and caring for the artwork it was born inside: the natural world.

## Sub lesson 6 — The arts at work — the eight plates

[Picture 14001 — the arts at work — eight plates]

The Grade 4 book teaches this lesson's theme in **eight plates** (Figures 3.16–3.23) — walk them as the book hangs them. **Fig. 3.16, social:** people helping, discussing, eating together. **Fig. 3.17, economic:** the mill system and cotton spinning. **Fig. 3.18:** religious teaching. **Fig. 3.19, nature:** the Tis Abay falls. **Fig. 3.20:** the printing of the country's money. **Fig. 3.21:** children expressing feelings and wishes. **Fig. 3.22, social play:** the coffee ceremony and games. **Fig. 3.23:** honouring custom and culture.

Read the set as one sentence: there is no corner of the society's life — work, faith, money, play, feeling, custom — where the arts are not already at work. The plates are the proof, hung in a row.

## Sub lesson 7 — The Unit 3 practice set — blessing, visiting, expressing

The Grade 4 book closes the unit with a **practice set** (Activity 3.2) — do it as the book sets it. **First:** act out the **family blessing** — the blessing spoken at coffee, at meals and in discussion — with learners taking the family's parts. **Second:** with the teacher, **visit local historic places**, and **express them** four ways: in **drawing**, in **song or poem**, in **theatre** and in **dance**.

One visit, four arts. That is the unit's whole teaching turned into a day: society's places, met by society's arts, in the learner's own hands.

## Sub lesson 8 — Why the community needs its arts; cross-cutting issues performed (teacher guide — evidence)

*This part stands on teacher-guide evidence.* The Grade 5 guide's §4.2 teaches **introducing the arts to the community** — and inside it, **the necessity of visual and folk/craft arts within the community**: why a community *needs* its arts, not merely enjoys them. A town without its pictures and its crafts loses its memory, its teaching and its pride at once.

The guide's Chapter 5 (§5.1) then sends the performing arts to the community's hardest topics: **performing songs, dances and theatre about cross-cutting issues — traffic, HIV, addictive drugs and the like**. The stage and the song carry what lectures cannot: a warning the whole community will stop to watch.

## Sub lesson 9 — Theatre, film and photography for social topics — and Qaqe Wirdiwet

The Grade 6 book (§5.2) gives the performing and screen arts their social brief: **theatre, film and photography, centred on the human, treat varied social topics** — among the art media, these are the ones that put a human face on an issue.

The book names a working example: **"Qaqe Wirdiwet"**, a **women's-rights theatre work** — a play standing for women's rights before its community. Around it the book sets its practicals: **theatre and short films** on **traffic, gender and cleanliness**. An issue performed is an issue the audience must look in the face — that is the section's whole argument.

## Sub lesson 10 — Current issues sung and pictured — the Grade 7 set

[Picture 14004 — the Traffic Light chant teaches the law]

The Grade 7 book (§5.4) sends the arts to current issues with named works. The **"Traffic Light" chant** (printed p. 163) teaches traffic-light law — placed here by title, subject and purpose only: when the red light shows, the car stops; the chant makes the law memorable. The **"Plants" chant** (printed pp. 164–165) sings green plants as **a guarantee for human life** — planting and protecting them is its teaching, named and summarised here, never transcribed.

And the section closes in a picture: **Figure 8 (Chapter 5)** shows **the effect climate change exerts** (printed p. 168) — the issue drawn, so a learner can see what the words mean. Chant, chant, picture: the Grade 7 set works in every art it has.

## Sub lesson 11 — Performing art's benefits — the opener; art for current issues

The Grade 8 book opens Chapter 4 (§4.1) with the claim the next lesson proves in detail: **performing arts are art branches enabling the community to have a successful life and a good way of living** — branches that grow a special ability and an attractive expression, hold national unity, and build the community's psychological structure. Learn it as an opener: three benefits are coming, and this sentence is their door.

The same book closes the theme (§5.2, printed pp. 134–135): **art has a high benefit for introducing current issues easily to the community and increasing the community's awareness** — the opener's promise, applied. What performing art does for society, it does first by making society's own questions impossible to ignore.

## Sub lesson 12 — Arts make content easy; art, the chief tool; the issue list

Three books' statements belong together here, and this part carries their own pages. The Grade 5 book (§5.1.1, printed pp. 119–120): **children learn concepts through song, melody, drawing and theatre while enjoying themselves — they grasp easily and remember long**; linking the subjects this way is how content becomes easy. Its §5.2 (printed p. 125) raises the claim: **art is a chief tool for raising society's consciousness while entertaining** — and names the urgent issues, traffic accidents among them.

The Grade 7 book works the same ground twice. Its §5.3.1 (printed pp. 160–161) shows **music helping teach other subjects** — Amharic and English letters taught by varied chants; mathematics' four operations carried in song. Its §5.4 (printed p. 162) lists the **current issues** plainly: issues that occur in the community, nationally and regionally, harm daily life, and need attention — and sets the visual and performing arts to them.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion one current issue. They must answer with an art form and a message: 'a poster — plant trees, the forest is our roof.' Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a poster for one current issue in your area, in the emblem-and-poster lesson's way.
2. Write three lines: one useful tradition of your area, one harmful one, and what art could say about each.
3. Draw one artwork of nature near your home — a tree, a river, a hill — from life.
