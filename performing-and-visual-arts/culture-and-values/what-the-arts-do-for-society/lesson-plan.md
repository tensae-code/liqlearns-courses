# Lesson plan — What the Arts Do for Society

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** What the Arts Do for Society
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** art at work in the world — what art is, in the books' own definitions; the role of the arts in a society (expressing feelings, passing heritage, gathering community); art speaking against harm and for the current issues of our day; and nature herself read as an artwork and re-made through art.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Our Arts and Cultural Heritage and Festivals and Their Arts — the learner knows their own heritage and festivals; this lesson asks what the arts DO, for a whole society and for the issues of today.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What is art?** — the Grade 6 opening chapter — Sakadeg's definition (a skilled person practising a craft), Rumi's two faces of art in literature and the arts, and ብልህነት (akilignnet) as ingenuity (Grade 6, printed pp. 1–2).
2. **The role of the arts in society** — art's role in life; culture and heritage (p. 46); heritage in pictures — Lalibela and the festivals (pp. 47–49); arts in history and arts at work — industries and professions (pp. 55–60) (Grade 4, printed pp. 46–60).
3. **Art speaks for society — and against harm** — Grade 5's 'Art Speaks for Society' — art's lens on community life; Useful and Harmful Traditions; art against alcohol, drugs and AIDS; environment songs (Grade 5, printed pp. 108–112 and 129–130; Grade 4, printed pp. 90–91).
4. **Art for current issues** — the issues of our day as art's subjects — deforestation, pollution and girls' education named across the grades; art works against flood, erosion, drugs and alcohol (Grade 6, printed pp. 105–106; Grade 7, Ch. 5; Grade 8, printed pp. 134–135).
5. **Nature through art** — appreciating nature's art forms; drawing natural things; nature songs (Grade 4, printed pp. 90–95).

6. **The arts at work — the eight plates** — the Grade 4 book's eight 'arts at work' plates, described one by one: society helping and eating together, the mill and cotton spinning, religious teaching, Tis Abay, the printing of money, children's feelings, the coffee ceremony and games, honouring custom (Grade 4, printed pp. 56–60).

7. **The Unit 3 practice set — blessing, visiting, expressing** — the Grade 4 book's Activity 3.2: act the family blessing at coffee, meals and discussion; visit local historic places and express them in drawing, song/poem, theatre and dance (Grade 4, printed p. 60).

8. **Why the community needs its arts; cross-cutting issues performed (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 5 guide: the necessity of visual and folk/craft arts within the community (§4.2), and performing songs, dances and theatre about cross-cutting topics — traffic, HIV, addictive drugs (§5.1) (Grade 5 teacher guide, structural placements).

9. **Theatre, film and photography for social topics — and Qaqe Wirdiwet** — the Grade 6 book: theatre, film and photography, centred on the human, treat varied social topics — including the women's-rights theatre work 'Qaqe Wirdiwet' (Grade 6, printed p. 107).

10. **Current issues sung and pictured — the Grade 7 set** — two named chants — the Traffic Light chant and the Plants chant, placed by name and purpose only — and climate change pictured in Figure 8 (Grade 7, printed pp. 163–168).

11. **Performing art's benefits — the opener; art for current issues** — the Grade 8 book opens its benefits teaching: performing arts enable a successful life and a good way of living (printed p. 105) — and links art with current issues for the whole community (printed pp. 134–135).

12. **Arts make content easy; art, the chief tool; the issue list** — the Grade 5 book: children learn concepts through song, melody, drawing and theatre — easily, and remembered long (pp. 119–120) — and art as chief tool for society's consciousness, with its urgent issues named (p. 125); the Grade 7 book: music helping teach other subjects (§5.3.1, pp. 160–161) and the current-issues list (§5.4, p. 162).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Our Arts and Cultural Heritage and Festivals and Their Arts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what art is, in the Grade 6 definitions and the Sakadeg/Rumi faces of the word.
2. List the roles of the arts in society from the Grade 4 lessons.
3. Say how art speaks for society — and how it works against alcohol, drugs and AIDS.
4. Make art for a current issue: deforestation, pollution, girls' education, or another the class names.
5. Read nature as an artwork and show it through drawing and a nature song.

## What you need
Eyes for your own area: its issues, its traditions, its nature — and paper for a poster of your own.

## If the learner is stuck
- 'Art' narrowed to pictures only → the books' definitions are wide: music, dance, drama, sculpture and painting together; and art as a lens on society, not decoration.
- Confuses useful and harmful traditions → the book's own test: traditions that benefit people and land are kept; those that harm are questioned — art is one of the voices that questions.
