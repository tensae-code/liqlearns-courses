# Dance, Culture and Society

## Do it first

Before any instrument or paint existed, what art could a human already do? This lesson starts with that oldest answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — What culture is — and dance, the oldest art (teacher guide — evidence)

*This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence — from the Grade 8 guide's surviving body pages.* The guide begins with a definition worth memorising: **culture is the characteristics and knowledge of a group — its language, religion, food, traditions, values, music and arts**.

Inside that culture, the guide gives dance its full dignity: **dance is the oldest, most primal art** — before instruments, before paint, the body itself moved in pattern. The guide names dance's **purposes** in society and gives **dance education** its own definition: teaching a person to know, do and value that oldest art as part of their culture.

## Sub lesson 2 — Culture's dimensions and identity (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's next part takes culture apart to see how it holds together. Culture has **three dimensions**: the **material** — the things a group makes and uses; the **normative** — its rules, values and expectations; and the **symbolic** — its meanings, signs and stories. A dance touches all three at once: the costume (material), the occasions and rules of dancing (normative), and what the dance *means* (symbolic).

The guide then names **five cultural dimensions — problem areas** in which groups differ, among them **power distance** (how a group lives with authority), **uncertainty avoidance** (how it faces the unknown) and **individualism/collectivism** (whether the person or the group comes first). From these dimensions a group builds its **cultural identity** — the shared answer to "who are we?" that its dances, among all its arts, perform in public.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: culture is a group's characteristics and knowledge; dance — the oldest, most primal art — carries it; and culture itself has dimensions, material, normative and symbolic, in which identity lives (teacher-guide evidence). Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.
