# Cultural Heritage and Collections

## Do it first

Quick start: say in one sentence what you think this lesson is about — heritage and collections: what a collection is, how museums communicate it, heritage tangible and intangible — and how a community gathers, registers and exhibits its own heritage. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Collections and heritage, tangible and intangible

From collecting to heritage: a **collection** is a gathered set of things kept together, and **museums communicate collections** to their public. **Heritage** is wider: **tangible** heritage you can touch — objects and instruments — and **intangible** heritage you carry in practice — traditions and skills. The Grades 6–7 guide sets the class to make an **inventory of the region's own heritage, including what is in danger of disappearing**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colombia's own heritage anchors

Two anchors carry the idea home. The **Muisca raft** and the **El Dorado legend** stand for Colombia's gold-working heritage in the younger book. The visual-arts guide holds up **Colombia's own rock paintings of Río Guayabero as the first images of this land — heritage to know and value**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cultural management — the gestor cultural

The older book names the profession: heritage is kept by **cultural management** — administering a cultural organisation's resources so its public is best served. The person who does it is the **gestor cultural**.

## Sub lesson 4 — The heritage project — gather, register, exhibit

The older course ends in a real project: **formulate a cultural project** — definition, justification, objectives, methodology, resources, chronogram, evaluation — to **gather the community's cultural and musical heritage** (what music each generation of the community hears), **register it in written, visual and audio formats**, keep the project **logbook (bitácora)**, and **exhibit the findings to the community**.
