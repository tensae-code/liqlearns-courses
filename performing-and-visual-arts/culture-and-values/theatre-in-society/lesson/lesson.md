# Theatre in Society

## Do it first

Think of a feast day when a story was acted or sung before everyone. This lesson looks at theatre's place in the community — three ways.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theatre from faith (teacher guide — evidence)

*This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence.* The Grade 4 guide (§§3.3–3.3.3) treats **the role of theatre art in society** — and it looks at that role **three ways**. The first is **from faith**: theatre stands inside the community's religious life — sacred stories acted, feast-day performances, the community's beliefs made visible before the whole community.

Acted faith is not decoration on belief. When a community **sees** its sacred story performed, the story is held in common in a way hearing alone does not give: children learn it, elders are honoured by it, and the faith is renewed in public.

## Sub lesson 2 — Theatre from culture (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's second standpoint is **from culture**. A play wears its community: its **customs, dress, speech, work and occasions** walk onto the stage with the actors. Theatre in society is therefore a **mirror and a storehouse** of culture — the community sees its own way of life performed, judged, laughed at and kept.

This is why a people's theatre can never simply be swapped for another people's: the culture on the stage *is* the content. Change the customs in the play and you have changed the play's society.

## Sub lesson 3 — Theatre from history (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's third standpoint is **from history**. Theatre takes the community's **past** — its heroes, its hard years, its victories — and **acts it**. Performed history does what a told history cannot: it puts the ancestors on their feet, in voice and body, before descendants who never met them.

Hold the three standpoints together, as the guide sets them: theatre in society is the community **believing together** (faith), **recognising itself** (culture) and **remembering together** (history). One stage, three services.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: the Grade 4 guide sees theatre's place in the community three ways: from faith, from culture and from history (teacher-guide evidence). Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.
