# Houses Then and Now

## Do it first

Look at your own home. What is it made of? Wood? Stone? Mud? Tin? Now think of the oldest house you know, and the newest. Houses have a story, like dress — the book teaches houses inside its culture chapter, because **a house is an artwork**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Houses in old times

Grade 3's culture chapter gives houses one honest sentence, and this sub lesson stands on it: **houses are artworks made from different things, for dwelling**.

In old times, a house was made from what the place itself offered, by the hands of the family and the community, in the ways the grandfathers and grandmothers knew. Figure 4 shows houses **in old times**: their shapes and materials belong to their land and their time, and they sheltered the same family life our houses shelter now.

An old house is not a failed new house. It is the artwork of its time — made well, from what there was, for dwelling.

## Sub lesson 2 — Houses today

The book's second half of the sentence carries the change: houses' **making has improved and grown from time to time**.

Figure 4 sets the two side by side — **houses in old times and now** — so the eye can travel between them: new materials beside old, new shapes beside old, the same need underneath: a place to dwell.

The lesson the culture chapter teaches with dress, it teaches again with houses: old and new are **one story**. We learn from the old making, we build with the new, and we respect both — because both are artworks made for the same human thing: dwelling, together.

## Say it to your companion

Walk (or imagine walking) past two houses with your companion: one old, one new. Each of you says two true sentences: what it is made of, and how you know its time.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw Figure 4 from the lesson in your own way: old-times houses on one side, today's on the other.
2. Ask an elder what houses were made of when they were a child. Remember one material.
3. Look at your own home: name every material you can find in its walls and roof.
4. Say the book's sentence from memory: 'Houses are artworks…' — finish it.
