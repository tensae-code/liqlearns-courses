# Lesson plan — Old Arts and Today's Arts

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Culture and Values · **Lesson:** Old Arts and Today's Arts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Culture and Values (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** culture expressed in the arts — the ancient arts handed down unspoiled, today's ways of keeping them, and why culture must be protected and passed on.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole Culture and Values title — dress, houses and festivals are its evidence.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Expressing culture in the arts** — The chapter opener: many peoples, one country — each with its food, dress and dance — and the duty to preserve them.
2. **Old arts** — Ancient arts defined: unspoiled, unrevised, expressing feeling at joy, grief, work and daily life.
3. **Today's arts — and protecting culture** — The review fold: from mouth-to-mouth to cassette and CD — and culture cared for, generation to generation.

4. **The culture chapter opens — the Grade 1 aims (opening fold)** — the Grade 1 book's Chapter 3 aims: list the kinds of culture in the area, perform its dances and songs, and list its traditional artworks (Grade 1, printed p. 36).

5. **Music as culture — folklore** — the Grade 8 book: music is a main part of the social functions that make a community stand as a community — music as folklore (Grade 8, printed pp. 80–81).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Dress and Festivals and Their Arts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what culture shows: a society's way of life — food, dress, dance, traditional song.
2. Define ancient arts in the book's words: long-connected, unspoiled and unrevised, used to express feeling.
3. Give the review's great change: old arts passed mouth to mouth; today they are recorded and travel easily.
4. Say culture's ruling: it must be cared for and protected, to pass generation to generation.

## What you need
A song or story someone taught you by mouth — your first evidence.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Who taught you your favourite song? Did you read it, or did someone **sing it to you**, the way they were sung to? For most of Ethiopia's arts, for most of time, the answer is: mouth to mouth, heart to heart. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts of lesson/lesson.md, in order.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9011]: a picture strip in three parts — a weaver at handicraft work, a wedding dance, and farmers singing at debo work together — the student names each as an old art expressing feeling.
   - ADD AUDIO: an elder's voice (recorded) telling how a song reached them — the student retells the chain: who taught whom.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion one old art of your area — a song, a craft, a dance — and how it reached you: by mouth, or by recording? Then say the book's ruling about culture together.. (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks 'old' means 'finished' → the definition says existing from old times TO NOW; the old arts are alive wherever they are still used.
- Thinks recording makes caring unnecessary → the book's ruling stands after the recording sentence: culture must be cared for and protected — recordings pass easily, but people must still learn and do.
