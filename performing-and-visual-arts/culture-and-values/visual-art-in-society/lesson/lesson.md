# Visual Art in Society

## Do it first

Walk your street in your mind and look at its walls, doors, clothes and signs. Whose place does it look like? This lesson is about that.

## Sub lesson 1 — Visual art in society — culturally (teacher guide — evidence)

*This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence.* The Grade 4 guide (§§3.4–3.4.2) treats **the role of visual art in society** — and, like its theatre teaching, it looks two ways. The first is **culturally**: visual art is the community's identity **made visible in public** — the painted wall in the town, the woven pattern, the carved door, the dressed festival. A stranger learns whose town this is by looking.

Culturally, then, visual art does for the eye what the customary dance does for the body: it **says who the community is**, in forms the community itself made and keeps.

## Sub lesson 2 — Visual art in society — historically (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's second way is **historically**. Long after their makers are gone, a society's visual works **remain** — its paintings, carvings, buildings and crafts are the **record** by which later ages know it: what it valued, how it lived, what it could make.

Join the two ways and the lesson is complete. Culturally, visual art tells the community's own members — and every stranger passing — **who we are**. Historically, it tells the unborn **who we were**. No other art stays behind to speak in both directions at once; the visual is the society's longest memory.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: the Grade 4 guide treats the role of visual art in society two ways: culturally and historically (teacher-guide evidence). Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

2. Show one part of the lesson with your hands, your voice or a drawing — as the part itself asks.
3. Teach the part you found hardest to someone at home, in three sentences.
