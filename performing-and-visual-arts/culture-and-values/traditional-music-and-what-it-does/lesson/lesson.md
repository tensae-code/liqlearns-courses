# Traditional Music and What It Does

## Do it first

Name one thing your community wears, eats, sings and celebrates that a stranger would know it by. You have just listed marks of its culture — this lesson starts there.

## Sub lesson 1 — What culture is

Begin where the Grade 5 book (§3.1.1) begins: with the word **culture** itself. Culture is **a society's marks**: its **language, religion, art, dress, food, history and ceremonies** — the sum of its **spiritual and material products**, **passed from generation to generation**.

Read the list slowly. Everything a society is, it *shows* — in what it speaks, believes, makes, wears, eats, remembers and celebrates. Traditional music stands inside the list, not beside it: music is one of the marks by which a society can be known.

## Sub lesson 2 — Every society makes its music

The book's second step: **every society creates and uses its own music**. No society is music-less, because music does work societies need done: it **entertains**, it **teaches**, it **keeps the group's order**, and it **praises God**.

And it is the society's feeling carried in sound: traditional music **carries joy, grief and desires**, and it **lives with the society** — born in its occasions, changed as it changes, never a stranger's property. When you hear a people's traditional music, you are hearing the society itself, in its own voice.

## Sub lesson 3 — Traditional music's uses

Now the book's uses list — learn it whole. Traditional music **expresses and introduces a place's cultural events easily** — hear the music and you have met the occasion. It **carries views, feelings, desires, wishes, love, hate, liking and heroism**, and so works as a **tool of social communication** — a society talking to itself. It **transmits knowledge, skill, experience, thought, religious order, ethics and cultural values** — a school with no walls. And it works for the whole country: it **creates national feeling, guards the borders psychologically, encourages heroism and plants love of country**.

Count the uses as you learn them: introduce, communicate, transmit, unite. One song can do all four before it ends.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: culture is a society's marks, and traditional music is one of its strongest: it entertains, teaches, keeps order, praises God, and carries the society's joy, grief and desires. Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write or say the lesson's key list from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. Show one part of the lesson with your hands, your voice or a drawing — as the part itself asks.
3. Teach the part you found hardest to someone at home, in three sentences.
