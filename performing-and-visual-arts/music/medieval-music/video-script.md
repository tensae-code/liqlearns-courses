This is North Macedonia's own lesson.

**the Middle Ages, sacred music: the Gregorian chant.** the Middle Ages, sacred music: the Gregorian chant — one-voice Latin chant sung without instruments, the foundation of Western notation, polyphony and music education; the first polyphony, organum, and the Notre Dame school of Leonin and Perotin growing from two voices to four; notation — neumes, Guido d'Arezzo's four-line system and solmisation from the hymn to St John; the Mass becoming the great polyphonic work.

**the Eastern church.** the Eastern church: John Kukuzel, "the Angel-Voiced" (14th century), Byzantine theorist, composer and chanter who reformed the Eastern neumatic notation with signs for duration and dynamics, making church singing readable for centuries.

**secular music of the Middle Ages: the travelling poet-musicians.** secular music of the Middle Ages: the travelling poet-musicians — troubadours, trouvères, minnesänger — singing in the people's language of love, heroism, nature and daily life; the motet, madrigal, ballade and canzonetta; the court dances pavane (slow and stately) and galliard (quick and leaping).

That is Medieval Music — North Macedonia's way, kept by name.
