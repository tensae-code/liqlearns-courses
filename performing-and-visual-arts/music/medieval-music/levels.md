# Medieval Music — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — the Middle Ages, sacred music: the Gregorian chant
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the Middle Ages, sacred music: the Gregorian chant — one-voice Latin chant sung without instruments, the foundation of Western notation, polyphony and music education; the first polyphony, organum, and the Notre Dame school of Leonin and Perotin growing from two voices to four; notation — neumes, Guido d'Arezzo's four-line system and solmisation from the hymn to St John; the Mass becoming the great polyphonic work.

## Sub lesson 2 — the Eastern church
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the Eastern church: John Kukuzel, "the Angel-Voiced" (14th century), Byzantine theorist, composer and chanter who reformed the Eastern neumatic notation with signs for duration and dynamics, making church singing readable for centuries.

## Sub lesson 3 — secular music of the Middle Ages: the travelling poet-musicians
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: secular music of the Middle Ages: the travelling poet-musicians — troubadours, trouvères, minnesänger — singing in the people's language of love, heroism, nature and daily life; the motet, madrigal, ballade and canzonetta; the court dances pavane (slow and stately) and galliard (quick and leaping).
