# Appreciating Indigenous Knowledge in Music

## Do it first

Hold or picture a woven cloth: gabi or netela. Its pattern, its borders, its strength were worked out by makers who learned from makers before them. That carried, practised knowledge — in cloth, in song, in instruments — is indigenous knowledge. This lesson learns to appreciate it properly: by looking closely and answering with art.

## Sub lesson 1 — Indigenous knowledge in music, defined

The Grade 6 book's Chapter 4 appreciates **indigenous knowledge** — the knowledge a community itself has built and carried across generations, in its crafts, its instruments and its music. In music it lives in **how instruments are made and played, which songs belong to which occasions, and how melodies are remembered without writing**. None of it is lesser knowledge for being unwritten: it is tested knowledge, proved by centuries of use. Appreciation begins with that recognition.

## Sub lesson 2 — Exercise 4.1 — appreciating weaving in poem, music and eskista

**Exercise 4.1 (printed p. 95)** sets the appreciation to work: **visit a local weaving workplace and ask what clothes weaving can make; then express your appreciation of what you saw in POEM, MUSIC and ESKISTA**. If there is no weaving workplace nearby, the book's own fallback stands: **ask family or neighbours about the woven clothes they wear**, and express the appreciation the same way. The weaver's knowledge is honoured by answering it with the arts themselves.

## Sub lesson 3 — Exercise 4.2 — appreciating the traditional instruments

**Exercise 4.2** turns the same appreciation to the **traditional instruments**: the kebero's hollowed trunk and hide, the masenqo's single string, the washint's bamboo — each is indigenous knowledge made solid, knowledge of materials, of sound and of the occasions each instrument serves. Appreciate them as the exercise directs: name the instrument, its materials, its maker-knowledge and its occasion — and let the appreciation itself be performed, not only written.

## Sub lesson 4 — The guide's appreciation teaching (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 6 teacher guide carries the same chapter from the teacher's side (**teacher guide — evidence**): appreciation is led by **visiting, asking and performing back** — the learner does not only receive the community's knowledge but answers it with poem, song and dance, so the knowledge is honoured in its own currency. Evidence only, and labelled so; the exercises themselves stand on the pupil book above.

## Say it to your companion

Define indigenous knowledge in music for your companion, then appreciate one woven cloth or instrument aloud for them.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a four-line appreciation poem for a woven cloth you have seen.
2. List three pieces of indigenous knowledge in your own home and who carries each.
