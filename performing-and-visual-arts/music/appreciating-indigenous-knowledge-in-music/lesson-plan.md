# Lesson plan — Appreciating Indigenous Knowledge in Music

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Appreciating Indigenous Knowledge in Music
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous knowledge honoured through music — what it is, how weaving and the traditional instruments carry it, and how appreciation is expressed in poem, music and eskista.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History — the learner knows the music is old and shared; here the knowledge inside it is named and appreciated on purpose.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Indigenous knowledge in music, defined** — §4.2's music side: the knowledge a community carries in its songs, instruments and occasions, defined and placed.
2. **Exercise 4.1 — appreciating weaving in poem, music and eskista** — visit a weaving workplace (or ask family/neighbours), then express appreciation of woven clothes in poem, music and eskista.
3. **Exercise 4.2 — appreciating the traditional instruments** — the instruments themselves appreciated: their making, materials and makers honoured as indigenous knowledge.
4. **The guide's appreciation teaching (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 6 guide's Chapter 4 side: how the teacher leads appreciation of indigenous knowledge — evidence, labelled.

5. **The Grade 6 chapter opens — appreciating indigenous knowledge (chapter opening — Grade 6)** — the Grade 6 book's Chapter 4 opener and aims: appreciate indigenous knowledge through visual art, through theatre/film/photography, in melodies and poems, and by eskista; know traditional instruments as products of indigenous knowledge (Grade 6, printed p. 85).

6. **The Grade 6 chapter closes — honouring the knowledge and its owners (chapter closing — Grade 6)** — the Grade 6 book's Chapter 4 summary, its TWO self-check tables (music and visual strands separated) and its closing exercise — 10 true/false and 2 performance answers (Grade 6, printed pp. 95–98).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what indigenous knowledge means in music, in the book's framing.
2. Carry out Exercise 4.1: appreciate weaving in poem, music and eskista.
3. Carry out Exercise 4.2's appreciation of the traditional instruments.
4. Say what the teacher guide adds, as labelled evidence.

## What you need
A weaving workplace to visit if one is near — or family and neighbours who wear woven clothes; any traditional instruments the school holds.

1. **Do it first** — look at a woven cloth closely — its pattern was designed by knowledge no book taught its maker — and say one true sentence of appreciation. (lesson/lesson.md).
