## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Народни инструменти** — Народни инструменти — the folk instruments of the country's peoples in three families: string (tambura, kemane, gusla, çifteli, kanun), wind (gajda, kaval, zurla, grneta-clarinet, dvojanka, duduk, šupelka) and percussion (tapan, daire, tarabuka); and by making — hand-made instruments of natural materials, unique to a region, against factory-made instruments of the ensembles.
2. **Народни музички инструменти** — Народни музички инструменти — the folk instruments in four families (idiophonic, membranophonic, chordophonic, aerophonic); the idiophones: dzvonci-klopotarci, the bells hung on goats, sheep, cows and horses, and the klepalo, the wooden or iron signal board struck at the church.
3. **Аерофонични** — Аерофонични — the aerophones: the shupelka, the short flute of walnut, cornel, ash or maple; the gajda, the bagpipe at the foundation of Macedonian music culture, played with a pair of kavals, tambura and percussion; and the kaval itself, the long open flute of ornamented ash wood in three lengths.
4. **Кордофонични** — Кордофонични — the chordophones: the tambura, the long-necked lute carved from a single piece of wood; the kemene-gusla, the pear-shaped three-stringed bowed instrument held upright; and the violin (kemane), which entered the čalgija bands in the 19th century and is now heard all over Macedonia.
5. **Мембрафонични** — Мембрафонични — the membranophones: the dajre, goatskin stretched on a wooden hoop with pairs of metal discs that jingle at the wrist's flick; and the tapan, the great double-headed drum of walnut wood and goat or sheep skin, the most widespread instrument in every region.
6. **Инструментални состави** — Инструментални состави — the instrumental ensembles sorted: village ensembles, ensembles of traditional untempered instruments, town ensembles, oriental ensembles, zurla-tapan ensembles, and mixed ensembles of tempered and untempered instruments.
7. **Селски инструментални состави** — Селски инструментални состави — village ensembles: gajda, kaval, three-stringed gusla and tambura with a membranophone, usually the small tapan — named by their make-up: gajdarski, kavaldziski, tajfi — their melodies mostly single-voiced.
8. **Ориентални состави** — Ориентални состави — чалгии — the čalgija: the oriental ensemble of Persian-Arab chordophones and tempered European instruments with the dajre — kemane (violin), greneta (clarinet), lauta, ut, kanon and dajre, led by the clarinettist or violinist; the Ohrid čalgija the exception, with two violins, one or two lautas and dajre.
