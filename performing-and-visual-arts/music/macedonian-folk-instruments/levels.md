# Macedonian Folk Instruments — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Народни инструменти
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Народни инструменти — the folk instruments of the country's peoples in three families: string (tambura, kemane, gusla, çifteli, kanun), wind (gajda, kaval, zurla, grneta-clarinet, dvojanka, duduk, šupelka) and percussion (tapan, daire, tarabuka); and by making — hand-made instruments of natural materials, unique to a region, against factory-made instruments of the ensembles.

## Sub lesson 2 — Народни музички инструменти
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Народни музички инструменти — the folk instruments in four families (idiophonic, membranophonic, chordophonic, aerophonic); the idiophones: dzvonci-klopotarci, the bells hung on goats, sheep, cows and horses, and the klepalo, the wooden or iron signal board struck at the church.

## Sub lesson 3 — Аерофонични
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Аерофонични — the aerophones: the shupelka, the short flute of walnut, cornel, ash or maple; the gajda, the bagpipe at the foundation of Macedonian music culture, played with a pair of kavals, tambura and percussion; and the kaval itself, the long open flute of ornamented ash wood in three lengths.

## Sub lesson 4 — Кордофонични
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Кордофонични — the chordophones: the tambura, the long-necked lute carved from a single piece of wood; the kemene-gusla, the pear-shaped three-stringed bowed instrument held upright; and the violin (kemane), which entered the čalgija bands in the 19th century and is now heard all over Macedonia.

## Sub lesson 5 — Мембрафонични
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Мембрафонични — the membranophones: the dajre, goatskin stretched on a wooden hoop with pairs of metal discs that jingle at the wrist's flick; and the tapan, the great double-headed drum of walnut wood and goat or sheep skin, the most widespread instrument in every region.

## Sub lesson 6 — Инструментални состави
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Инструментални состави — the instrumental ensembles sorted: village ensembles, ensembles of traditional untempered instruments, town ensembles, oriental ensembles, zurla-tapan ensembles, and mixed ensembles of tempered and untempered instruments.

## Sub lesson 7 — Селски инструментални состави
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Селски инструментални состави — village ensembles: gajda, kaval, three-stringed gusla and tambura with a membranophone, usually the small tapan — named by their make-up: gajdarski, kavaldziski, tajfi — their melodies mostly single-voiced.

## Sub lesson 8 — Ориентални состави
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Ориентални состави — чалгии — the čalgija: the oriental ensemble of Persian-Arab chordophones and tempered European instruments with the dajre — kemane (violin), greneta (clarinet), lauta, ut, kanon and dajre, led by the clarinettist or violinist; the Ohrid čalgija the exception, with two violins, one or two lautas and dajre.
