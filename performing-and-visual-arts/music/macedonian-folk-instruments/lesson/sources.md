**Book 1:** *Музичка уметност за I година гимназиско образование (Musical Art for Year I of Gymnasium Education)* (student book, Macedonian) — North Macedonia, Литера Гроуп (Litera Group), Skopje; year 2025; ISBN 978-608-4855-74-3; authors Simona Zafirovska and Teuta Alimi Selmani; approved by the Ministry of Education and Science (decision no. 26-934/1) Grade: Grade 10 (student/teacher: STUDENT) — gymnasium year I filed as grade 10. PDF pages: 122 (120 printed pages per CIP; read in place from phase3-staging, size and pages verified against the record; cover seen). Verdict: COMPLETE. North Macedonia's own way: a full music-history arc (first peoples, antiquity, Middle Ages, Renaissance, Baroque) is taught beside a complete theory course, and the heritage unit treats every people's singing styles, oro dances and folk instruments as one shared treasury — with the UNESCO-listed Glasoečko two-part singing, the Kopačkata oro and the Macedonian gajda named as protected heritage. Excluded per standing rules: Речник (the glossary), printed pp. 113–116 — glossary/word list, inventory-only; the bibliography (pp. 117–118) is not content. Printed song texts ("O sole mio", "We Will Rock You", the folk-song listening examples) are summarised, never transcribed. Read in full in the 2026-11-16 Art expansion read. **Book 2:** *Музичка уметност за III година гимназиско образование (изборен предмет) (Music Art for Year III of gymnasium education, elective subject)* (student book, Macedonian) — North Macedonia, Министерство за образование и наука на Република Македонија (Ministry of Education and Science), Skopje; year 2010 (CIP), copy in hand is the 2013 printing; ISBN 978-608-226-255-0; author Suzana Tnokova Kuzmanovska; Ministry approval decision no. 22-4030/1 of 29.06.2010 Grade: Grade 12 (gymnasium Year III, elective) (student/teacher: STUDENT). PDF pages: 104 (102 printed pages per CIP plus covers; image-only scan read page by page from rendered sheets; read in place from phase3-staging, never deleted). Verdict: COMPLETE. North Macedonia's own way: half the book is Macedonia itself — its musical folklore and the science that studies it, folk songs sorted by their work, costumes of the four regions, every folk instrument family, the oro dances region by region (Teshkoto, Kopachka, Rusalii), village and chalgija ensembles, Macedonian opera singers, composers and conductors, the first Macedonian opera and ballet, Badnik customs, Cyril and Methodius, and the country's festivals. Excluded per standing rules: the quotations page (printed p. 3) and the photo spreads (pp. 4, 5, 11, 37, 53) carry no teachable text; the "listening at the pupils' suggestion" page (p. 71) is an activity, not content; the Задача task boxes throughout are exercises inside the placed chapters, not placed separately. Song lyrics are summarised, never transcribed. Read in full in the 2026-11-16 Art expansion read. Placements at liqlearns-courses-work/expansion-art-2026-11-16/north-macedonia-placements.md.

| North Macedonia — Народни музички инструменти — the folk instruments in four families (idiophonic, membranophonic, chordophonic, aerophonic); the idiophones: dzvonci-klopotarci, the bells hung on goats, sheep, cows and horses, and the klepalo, the wooden or iron signal board struck at the church (student book; original target `music/macedonian-folk-instruments`) | Book 2, printed p. 21 |
| North Macedonia — Аерофонични — the aerophones: the shupelka, the short flute of walnut, cornel, ash or maple; the gajda, the bagpipe at the foundation of Macedonian music culture, played with a pair of kavals, tambura and percussion; and the kaval itself, the long open flute of ornamented ash wood in three lengths (student book; original target `music/macedonian-folk-instruments`) | Book 2, printed p. 22 |
| North Macedonia — Кордофонични — the chordophones: the tambura, the long-necked lute carved from a single piece of wood; the kemene-gusla, the pear-shaped three-stringed bowed instrument held upright; and the violin (kemane), which entered the čalgija bands in the 19th century and is now heard all over Macedonia (student book; original target `music/macedonian-folk-instruments`) | Book 2, printed p. 23 |
| North Macedonia — Мембрафонични — the membranophones: the dajre, goatskin stretched on a wooden hoop with pairs of metal discs that jingle at the wrist's flick; and the tapan, the great double-headed drum of walnut wood and goat or sheep skin, the most widespread instrument in every region (student book; original target `music/macedonian-folk-instruments`) | Book 2, printed p. 24 |
| North Macedonia — Инструментални состави — the instrumental ensembles sorted: village ensembles, ensembles of traditional untempered instruments, town ensembles, oriental ensembles, zurla-tapan ensembles, and mixed ensembles of tempered and untempered instruments (student book; original target `music/macedonian-folk-instruments`) | Book 2, printed p. 38 |
| North Macedonia — Селски инструментални состави — village ensembles: gajda, kaval, three-stringed gusla and tambura with a membranophone, usually the small tapan — named by their make-up: gajdarski, kavaldziski, tajfi — their melodies mostly single-voiced (student book; original target `music/macedonian-folk-instruments`) | Book 2, printed p. 39 |
| North Macedonia — Ориентални состави — чалгии — the čalgija: the oriental ensemble of Persian-Arab chordophones and tempered European instruments with the dajre — kemane (violin), greneta (clarinet), lauta, ut, kanon and dajre, led by the clarinettist or violinist; the Ohrid čalgija the exception, with two violins, one or two lautas and dajre (student book; original target `music/macedonian-folk-instruments`) | Book 2, printed p. 39 |
