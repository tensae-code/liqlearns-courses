# Composing Music

## Sub lesson 1 — Composing from cells, patterns and ostinatos

Andorra's primary music programme builds creating as one side of a triangle — **create, perform, analyse**. A young composer starts from small pieces of music: **rhythmic and melodic cells** (tiny patterns), **patterns and ostinatos** (patterns that repeat), and **musical question-and-answer phrases** — a question phrase that sounds unfinished (suspensive) and an answer phrase that finishes it (conclusive). From those small pieces, learners **compose and improvise simple pieces**, growing to pieces made **with objects, instruments and digital devices**, and use **soundtracking of images and videos** as a stimulus to compose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Experiment and improvise

Andorra's batxillerat music programme separates three acts that are often confused. **Experimentation** is **ephemeral, playful exploration of sound where error is allowed** — nothing has to last. **Improvisation** is making music on the spot, but **built on pre-established patterns** — rhythmic, melodic, harmonic and formal patterns the improviser agrees or chooses first.

## Sub lesson 3 — Composition — the lasting product

**Composition** is different again: it is the **willed, lasting product** — a piece meant to stay. It is kept so it can be played again: **in scores in every form, or in sound and audiovisual recording**. Experiment, improvise, compose: play with sound, build on a pattern on the spot, then fix the piece you choose to keep.
