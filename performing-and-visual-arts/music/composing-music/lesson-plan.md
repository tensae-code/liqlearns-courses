# Lesson plan — Composing Music

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Composing Music
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** making your own music — composing and improvising from rhythmic and melodic cells, patterns and ostinatos, and question-and-answer phrases; and, higher up, the three different acts of experimenting, improvising and composing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Composing from cells, patterns and ostinatos** — creating simple pieces in Andorra's primary programme — composing and improvising from rhythmic and melodic cells, question-and-answer phrases and patterns, growing to pieces made with objects, instruments and digital devices, with soundtracking of images and videos as a stimulus.
2. **Experiment and improvise** — at batxillerat, sound experimentation as ephemeral, playful exploration where error is allowed, and improvisation built on pre-established rhythmic, melodic, harmonic and formal patterns.
3. **Composition — the lasting product** — composition as the willed, lasting product, kept in scores in every form or in sound and audiovisual recording.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this course in the same title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how a piece can be built from cells, patterns and ostinatos, and what a question-and-answer phrase pair is.
2. Tell experimentation and improvisation apart, in the programme's own way.
3. Say what makes composition different from experimentation and improvisation, and how a composition is kept.

## What you need
Your voice, simple instruments or objects, and a way to record or write down your piece.
