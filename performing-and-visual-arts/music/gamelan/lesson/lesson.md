# Gamelan

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — The gamelan of Indonesia and Malaysia — Balinese and Malay gamelan, their gong orchestras and repertoire, played in ensemble. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Malay gamelan of Pahang

Unit 1 Apresiasi: Gamelan — Malay gamelan of Pahang: saron baron, saron pekin, gambang kayu and kromong carry the melody, gendang the beat, gong marks time, kenong marks phrases; Hornbostel-Sachs classes; played at royal and official ceremonies; Unit 1 Gamelan Melayu — Malay gamelan as a performed ensemble: its instruments and discipline, inside the unit Perpaduan dan Keharmonian (filed under the Book 2 lesson slug music/gamelan-melayu).

## Sub lesson 2 — Performing named gamelan pieces from scores

Units 1–3 — performing named gamelan pieces from scores: Timang Burung, Togok and Topeng, and reviewing a gamelan performance (ulasan dan penilaian persembahan gamelan); Units 3–4 Gamelan — performing the gamelan pieces Perang and Kunang-kunang Mabuk (filed under the Book 10 lesson slug music/gamelan-repertoire).

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson holds, part by part, in your own words. Companion checks: did every part get named, with its Malaysian name kept where the book gives one?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Malaysia's own way of teaching its arts?
