# Lesson plan — Traditional Singing Styles of North Macedonia

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Traditional Singing Styles of North Macedonia
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** How North Macedonia's peoples sing traditionally — one-part and two-part singing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **how the peoples of the country sing: Macedonian one-part and two-part singing** — how the peoples of the country sing: Macedonian one-part and two-part singing — glasoečko echo singing, ukano, and bordun (drone) singing, the second voice at the upper third or fifth, three-part singing in places, ornaments throughout; Albanian throat singing, rhythm, polyphony and heterophony, men's epic and women's lyric songs; Turkish melismatic singing on modal makam scales; Roma singing of rhythm, virtuosity and free melody; Vlach melismatic singing with its leading line.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
