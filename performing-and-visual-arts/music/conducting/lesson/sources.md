| Vietnam — Topic 3 Bài 1 — the preparatory movement (động tác lấy đà), the singing-performance preparation shown through the conductor's two hands' signal — the opening lấy đà leads the choir or orchestra to start singing or playing — for an entry at the beat's head the movement is performed one beat before (in Trịnh Công Sơn's "Nối vòng tay lớn", the 2-beat pattern with the lấy đà on beat 1 starting the singing on beat 2, the stroke running continuously from the beat-1 point to the beat-2 point); for an entry at the beat's light part the movement is emphasised into the beat's head (in Nguyễn Đức Toàn's "Biết ơn chị Võ Thị Sáu", the lấy đà on beat 2 starting the singing in beat 2's second half); and the interleaved lấy đà between beats, whose dual function is both to give the choir breath for a new music idea or sentence and to ensure the sound's fullness — in the two-part "Hát trong mưa" the right hand cues the introduction, the left hand cues part 1's entry, the right hand part 2's, and both hands together cue both parts into a new section (student book; original target `music/conducting-and-conducting-technique`) | Book 9, printed p. 33 |
| Vietnam — Topic 3 Bài 1 — the ending movement (động tác kết thúc), the conductor's signal for all performers to stop at the same time — at the piece's last beat an up movement signals the performers to prepare to stop, then the down beat stops them, the movement ending with the thumb closing loosely against the index finger and the other fingers — practised on Văn Cao's "Ngày mùa" (Harvest Day), where at beat 3 of the last note both hands make the up movement together and beat down on the next beat (student book; original target `music/conducting-and-conducting-technique`) | Book 9, printed p. 36 |
| Vietnam — Topic 3 Bài 1 — describing legato and staccato sound — legato is beaten continuously, softly and flowingly, for sounds that resound connected without stopping — the wrist loosened, the hand moving always behind the arm (practised on Trần Chung's "Bài ca Trường Sơn" in the 4-beat pattern, with breath cues given by đà movements at the named bars, the hand moving a little slower than the arm, no bouncing or jerking at the beats, and the conductor singing along with the melody); staccato is beaten short and neat, for separated, non-resounding sounds — mainly wrist force combined with hand and finger movement (practised on Trần Kiết Tường's "Bài ca chiến thắng", taking đà on beat 3 to start the singing on beat 4, the dotted notes beaten short and neat) (student book; original target `music/conducting-and-conducting-technique`) | Book 9, printed p. 37 |
| Vietnam — Topic 3 Bài 1 — describing intensity (sắc thái) — the intensity signs p (light), mp (moderately light), mf (moderately strong) and f (strong) are described by the amplitude of the conductor's two hands — a wide amplitude for strong, a narrow amplitude for light — applied by conducting an excerpt of Hoàng Việt's "Nhạc rừng" (Forest Music) (student book; original target `music/conducting-and-conducting-technique`) | Book 9, printed p. 38 |
