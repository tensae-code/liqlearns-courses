# History of Indigenous Music

## Do it first

Indigenous music has a history as long as the people who carry it: chant systems, occasion songs, instruments proved by centuries. The teacher guide tells that history from the teacher's side. This lesson carries the guide's line as evidence — and labels honestly where the guide itself could only be read in outline.

## Sub lesson 1 — The guide's history of indigenous music (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 7 teacher guide teaches the **history of indigenous music** from the teacher's side (**teacher guide — evidence**): the music a community builds and carries itself — chant, occasion song and traditional instruments — running from the old chant systems through the azmari and regional traditions to the present keepers. The pupil-book history you already own (Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History; Amhara Music History) is the same river seen from the bank; the guide names it as the teacher's map of that river.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading evidence honestly (teacher guide — evidence)

Honesty is part of this lesson's content: the Grade 7 guide's **body was read to printed p. 109 only**, and its **Units 4–5 are placed from contents and syllabus** — those parts are **EVIDENCE-LIMITED (teacher guide — evidence)** and are labelled so wherever this course uses them. A learner who knows which parts of a source stand firm and which stand in outline knows how to trust a book — a history skill as real as any date.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the guide's history line and one honest limit of the guide itself.
