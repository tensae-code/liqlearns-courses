## Sub lesson 1 — The Haja' Rhythm and the Sama'i Darij Rhythm

Arab music holds many rhythms differing in their beats; the Haja' rhythm (dum–tak–tak in two) is used mostly in religious chants, and the Sama'i Darij rhythm (dum–tak–tak–tak in three) in instrumental and sung pieces — both played from the pictured beat charts by clapping the dum and patting the thighs for the tak.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Haja' Rhythm and the Ayyub Rhythm

Rhythmic patterns are among the features of Arabic music, rich in rhythmic patterns; the dum and tak strokes on the tabla and on the body (dum by clapping, tak by patting the thighs) — the pictured charts of the Fox (in two), Waltz and Sama'i Darij (in three) rhythms revised, then the Haja' chart (in two, its two tak strokes beamed together) and the Ayyub chart (in three, dum–dum–tak with a quaver figure) performed on the body or an instrument in connected form, and telling the rhythms apart by listening.
