# Turkmen National Instruments

## Do it first

Say what you already know about this lesson's subject — one thing you have seen, heard or done — before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four national instruments

The Turkmen book pictures and names the country's own instruments before any world instrument appears:

- **Dutar** — the two-stringed lute, made of mulberry wood (the book's riddle names it so).
- **Gyjak** — the bowed spike fiddle.
- **Gargy tüýdük** — a reed flute.
- **Dilli tüýdük** — a tongue flute.

Listen to the **"Dutar" movement** from Nury Halmämmedow's symphonic pictures **"Türkmenistan"** — the instrument itself, singing inside an orchestra. The book also sets riddles whose answers are instruments — the dutar, and the **deprek** played at weddings.

## Sub lesson 2 — The instruments in the Russian-track book

The Russian-track book pictures Turkmenistan's own instruments too: the **gopuz**, the **dutar** and the **gijak (gyjak)**.

It explains the player-words: a **dutarist** is a person who plays the dutar — just as a **pianist** plays the piano.

Listen to Nury Halmämmedow's **"Слепой дождик" (Blind Rain)** and say which instruments played.
