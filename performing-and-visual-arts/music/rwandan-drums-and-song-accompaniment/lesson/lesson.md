# Rwandan Drums and Song Accompaniment

## Do it first

Start with the first part below — do it or make it before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sounding instruments: drum, can and jerry can

Primary 2's second music unit accompanies every song with **sounding instruments — drum, can and jerry can — respecting the song's rhythm**: politeness songs, hygiene songs, unity-and-reconciliation and environment songs, Rwandan culture songs and religious songs, each allied with instruments (and, in the guide's third unit, with dance).

## Sub lesson 2 — Drums and calabash sockets

Primary 3 goes further: songs are allied with **drums and socket calabashes** in one performance, with suitable voice and dance — religious songs, social-harmony songs, anti-drug songs, songs against youth vagrancy, and child-rights songs, connecting song and instruments **without discordance**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ingoma rhythms for Intore

The **ingoma** drums give the different rhythms (**umurishyo w'ingoma**) that **balance the Intore dancer's movements**. Drummers play together in groups; learners take up a drum, learn to play it, and play together to produce the different beats under loud singing and clapping.
