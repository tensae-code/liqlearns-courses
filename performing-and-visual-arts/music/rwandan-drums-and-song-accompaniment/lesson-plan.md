# Lesson plan — Rwandan Drums and Song Accompaniment

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Rwandan Drums and Song Accompaniment
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Rwanda's song accompaniment in three steps: sounding instruments (drum, can, jerry can), then drums with calabash sockets, then the ingoma rhythms that carry the Intore dance.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sounding instruments: drum, can and jerry can** — Primary 2's second music unit accompanies every song with **sounding instruments — drum, can and jerry can — respecting the song's rhythm**: politeness songs, hygiene songs, unity-and-reconciliation and environment songs, Rwandan culture songs and religious songs, each allied with instruments (and, in the guide's third unit, with dance).
2. **Drums and calabash sockets** — Primary 3 goes further: songs are allied with **drums and socket calabashes** in one performance, with suitable voice and dance — religious songs, social-harmony songs, anti-drug songs, songs against youth vagrancy, and child-rights songs, connecting song and instruments **without discordance**.
3. **Ingoma rhythms for Intore** — The **ingoma** drums give the different rhythms (**umurishyo w'ingoma**) that **balance the Intore dancer's movements**.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
