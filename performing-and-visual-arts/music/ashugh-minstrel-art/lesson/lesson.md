## Sub lesson 1 — Theme 2

Theme 2: Armenian ashugh art (teacher-guide evidence) — the textbook presents three ashughs, Sayat-Nova at their head; ashugh art, though Armenian culture, must not be confused with folk music proper.

## Sub lesson 2 — Armenian ASHUGH art

Armenian ASHUGH art — gusan, taghergu (hymn-singer), ashugh: the first Armenian ashughs are Naghash Hovnatan (1661-1722) and Baghdasar Dpir; an ashugh must weave verses in set measures and improvise in competitions before the crowd.

## Sub lesson 3 — The ashughs' world

The ashughs' world — Ashugh Shirin's "Gardener, what are you doing" heard from the Shoghaken ensemble; mockery and riddle-songs were loved themes (the riddle-singer Ashugh Jalali); an ashugh from another town needed the guild's (hamqarutyun) permission to perform.

## Sub lesson 4 — ARMENIAN ASHUGH ART

ARMENIAN ASHUGH ART — gusan, taghergu, ashugh: is it folk music or another reality?; the RANKS of the ashughs; taghachaputyun — the metre of the tagh.

## Sub lesson 5 — Gusans and ashughs in the art-history line

Gusans and ashughs in the art-history line — tied to folk heroic and historical songs that raised the young on the ancestors' example; the ashugh art's great contributors Shirin, Jivani, Sheram, and its 20th-century gusans (Ashot, Shahen, Bagrat, Leyli); listening: Gusan Ashot, "Hayrik".
