# Lesson plan — How Music Expresses and Makes Us Feel

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** How Music Expresses and Makes Us Feel
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** How Music Expresses and Makes Us Feel — Armenia's own teaching of this topic, kept as named parts: The expressive means of music — a letter from Mozart; his "Lullaby" heard: composers choose how music speaks
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The expressive means of music** — The expressive means of music — a letter from Mozart; his "Lullaby" heard: composers choose how music speaks
2. **Theme 1** — Theme 1: What does music tell about — musical intonation: the same bird-song sounds hungry, then fed; music speaks in intonations like speech
3. **One mood or many** — One mood or many — Schumann's "Grandfather Frost" from the "Album for the Young": a work of one part or several, one mood or changing
4. **Romanticism's turn** — Romanticism's turn — artists began to dream unrealised dreams of life: Schubert's "Ave Maria" heard
5. **IMPRESSIONISM** — IMPRESSIONISM — after the painters' 1874 Paris exhibition the critics mocked them with the name; music takes the same path of impressions; Pan's flute named from the Greek god
6. **Theme 2** — Theme 2: Contemporary arrangements of classical and folk works — the videos for this theme are many for one hour: the teacher chooses and presents (teacher-guide evidence)
7. **Genres transform with society (teacher-guide evidence)** — Genres transform with society (teacher-guide evidence) — the lessons follow the transformations of music art and its separate genres alongside society's development, turning both to classical music and to other forms such as jazz
8. **THE FOUR KINDS OF LOVE AND MUSIC** — THE FOUR KINDS OF LOVE AND MUSIC — love is among the most important human feelings: Rachmaninoff the brilliant pianist; another kind in the Greeks is PHILIA — the love of friends; Bach's "Capriccio" in six small parts describes in music not an event but feelings; Grieg's song that later became a piano piece of the same name
9. **The LOVE theme (teacher-guide evidence)** — The LOVE theme (teacher-guide evidence) — the theme of love is treated through the ancient Greeks' distinction of love's four kinds — storge, philia, eros, agape — and then presented in music in the same way
