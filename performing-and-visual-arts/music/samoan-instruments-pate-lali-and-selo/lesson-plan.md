# Lesson plan — Samoan Instruments: Pate, Lali and Selo

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Samoan Instruments: Pate, Lali and Selo
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Samoa's own instruments in the classroom — the pātē (slit drum) and lali children play and hear, and the homemade instruments Samoan children make and play, from the selo and coconut shells to PVC horns.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of its title — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The pātē and lali** — Samoa's slit drums named among the instruments children play and hear — the staccato of pātē and lali — and PVC-made horns that call families together to worship.
2. **Homemade instruments** — the selo, wood block and coconut shells, and homemade instruments from household things like fala and tins — with the ukulele in the senior years.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of the Music title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Samoa's pātē and lali and describe their sound.
2. Name the selo and the homemade instruments of the Samoan classroom, and what they are made from.

## What you need
Your ears and your hands; coconut shells, a wood block or tins if they are at hand.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the pātē and lali are the same as any drum → they are Samoa's own slit drums, named in the programme among the instruments children play and hear, known by their staccato sound.
- Thinks instruments must be bought → Samoa's programme names homemade instruments from household things — fala and tins — alongside the selo, wood block and coconut shells.
