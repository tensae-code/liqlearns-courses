# Samoan Instruments: Pate, Lali and Selo

## Do it first

Tap the table: short — short — short-long. Sharp little sounds, each one cut off clean. You have just made the kind of sound this lesson is about. Whose instruments sound like that?

## Sub lesson 1 — The pātē and lali

Samoa's primary Expressive Arts programme names Samoa's own instruments among the instruments children play and hear: the **pātē** and the **lali** — Samoa's slit drums. The programme points to **the staccato of pātē and lali** — their short, sharp, cut-off sound — as a sound quality children learn to know.

The same programme names **PVC-made horns** — horns made from PVC pipe — that **call families together to worship**: an instrument with a job in the village's life, not only in the classroom.

## Sub lesson 2 — Homemade instruments

Beside the named instruments stand the ones children make and gather themselves: the **selo**, the **wood block** and **coconut shells**, and **homemade instruments from household things like fala and tins**. In the senior years the **ukulele** joins the classroom instruments.

Samoa's way is plain: the instruments of the place — slit drum, shell, tin and pipe — are real instruments, played and heard by name.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: which two slit drums does Samoa's programme name, and name two homemade instruments and what they are made from. Companion checks: pātē and lali? Household things — fala and tins?

## Do It Yourself

1. Name Samoa's two slit drums and the word that describes their sound.
2. Find one household thing that can be an instrument. Play a short pattern on it and write what you made it from.
3. Write one sentence: what job do the PVC horns do in Samoa?
