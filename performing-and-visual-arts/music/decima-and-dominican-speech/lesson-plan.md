# Lesson plan — Décima and Dominican Speech

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Décima and Dominican Speech
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the décima espinela — the ten-verse sung stanza — and the Dominican Spanish it is sung in: the regions' own voices (cibaeño, sureño, capitaleño), learned by interviewing the people who speak them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How the Dominican regions speak** — Dominican Spanish in its regions — cibaeño, sureño, capitaleño, with their letter-swaps and Taíno, African, Andalusian and Canarian roots — learned by interviewing people about how their region speaks.
2. **The décima espinela** — a ten-verse octosyllabic stanza (rhyme abbaaccddc) created by Vicente Espinel in 1591 — sung here as Juan Luis Guerra's merengue built from décimas, practised in karaoke in the three regional voices with hats and scarves (song named and summarised only).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what how the Dominican regions speak teaches, in the book's own way.
2. Say what the décima espinela teaches, in the book's own way.

## What you need
A song built from décimas to listen to (Juan Luis Guerra's merengue is the book's), a hat and a scarf for the karaoke voices.
