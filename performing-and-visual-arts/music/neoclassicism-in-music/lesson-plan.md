# Lesson plan — Neoclassicism in Music

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Neoclassicism in Music
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Neoclassicism — the return to clarity, balance and the forms of Haydn, Mozart and Bach with modern harmony, Prokofiev among its voices.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Neoclassicism** — Neoclassicism: after romantic excess, a return to clarity, balance and the forms of Haydn, Mozart and Bach filled with modern harmony — Igor Stravinsky (1882–1971), from the Russian ballets "The Firebird", "Petrushka" and "The Rite of Spring" to neoclassical discipline; Prokofiev's "Classical" Symphony beside him.
2. **Сергеј Сергеевич Прокофјев (1891–1953)** — Сергеј Сергеевич Прокофјев (1891–1953) — Prokofiev: composing from early childhood, the Petrograd Conservatory at thirteen (Lyadov, Rimsky-Korsakov, Esipova), the Rubinstein prize of 1914, the years abroad; the opera "War and Peace" after Tolstoy, the ballets "Romeo and Juliet" and "Cinderella", seven symphonies and five piano concertos.
