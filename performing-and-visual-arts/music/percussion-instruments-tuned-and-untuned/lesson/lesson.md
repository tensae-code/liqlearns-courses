# Percussion Instruments: Tuned and Untuned

## Do it first

Quick start: say one thing you already know — a sound, a song, a name — that belongs to this lesson's world. Keep it in mind; the parts will meet it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The percussion section in the orchestra

Percussion is the orchestra's **heartbeat**: sound made by **striking, scraping or shaking** — 'the hitting of one body against another' — giving **rhythm and tone colour**, from solid bodies and stretched membranes (drums). The family divides: **tuned** instruments (glockenspiel, xylophone, vibraphone, and even timpani) play **singable set pitches**; **untuned** ones (conga, cowbell, cymbals) give **indefinite pitch**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The family's full span

The family's full span runs to anything hit, shaken or scraped, **from claves to a wind machine**, with keyboard instruments (**piano, celesta**) counted in. Across a sixteen-instrument list the tuned members (xylophone, timpani, piano) are sorted from the untuned (bass drum, cymbals, castanets). Maltese percussionist **Renzo Spiteri** is pictured as a living example.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the parts in order, one sentence each. Companion checks: did every part get its sentence, and did the order hold?
