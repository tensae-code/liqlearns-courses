# Lesson plan — European and Balkan Folk Music Heritage

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** European and Balkan Folk Music Heritage
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** european and Balkan Folk Music Heritage — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **musical heritage in the region I** — musical heritage in the region I — Bulgaria: open-throat diaphonic women's singing, the great kaba gaida, gadulka and kaval, the fast rachenitsa (7/16) and pajduško dances, and the kukeri, masked men dancing with heavy bells to drive out evil; Greece: klephtic songs of the fight against the Ottomans, the bouzouki and Cretan lyra, sirtaki, the solo zeibekiko and Crete's pentozali.
2. **musical heritage in the region II** — musical heritage in the region II — Albania: northern Geg epic songs against southern Tosk iso-polyphony over a drone (UNESCO), çifteli and lahuta; Kosovo's shota dance; Serbia: the kolo, gusle epics, frula and the Guča trumpet; Montenegro: epic gusle recitation and the oro danced as a two-storey human tower; Croatia: UNESCO-listed klapa singing, bećarac wit, tamburica and the Dubrovnik linđo; Bosnia: the sevdalinka love song with its oriental melismas, played on saz and šargija.
3. **Europe's folk heritage, West and Centre** — Europe's folk heritage, West and Centre: French regional songs with bagpipe and fiddle; British and Irish ballads, reels and jigs, the Scottish bagpipe and Irish bodhrán; German and Austrian Lied, yodelling, ländler and waltz; Czech, Slovak and Slovenian polkas; Polish and Hungarian csárdás.
4. **Europe's folk heritage, East, North and South** — Europe's folk heritage, East, North and South: Romania's acrobatic călușari and the hora, with the pan flute and cimbalom; Russia's balalaika, domra and garmon; Ukraine's hopak and bandura; the North's hardanger fiddle, Finland's kantele and Kalevala epic singing; Italy's tarantella, Spain's flamenco and Portugal's fado.
