# Lesson plan — Beat Instruments

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Beat Instruments
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a beat (percussion) instrument is — sound by striking, clashing or beating with a stick — and three of them closely: አታሞ (atamo), ከበሮ (kebero) and ነጋሪት (negarit).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Our Instruments (the kebero is already known as a drum) and on listening to sounds around us.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a beat instrument is** — the definition, and the chapter's set of six: atamo, kebero, bell, qachil, tsenatsil, negarit.
2. **አታሞ — atamo** — the short drum of wood or tin and hide, for secular music, mostly used by girls and women.
3. **ከበሮ — kebero** — wood with cattle hide at front and back; it serves in various spiritual chants.
4. **ነጋሪት — negarit** — the great proclamation drum of old — decrees and coronation festivals at the palace.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Our Instruments. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what makes an instrument a beat instrument.
2. Name the chapter's six beat instruments.
3. Tell what the atamo, the kebero and the negarit are made of and what each is used for.

## What you need
Your hands for clapping the beat. A table or a book serves as a practice drum.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — clap, tap the table, stamp: make three sounds by striking. You have just played beat instruments (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition and set, then atamo, kebero and negarit one by one (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the atamo with the church instruments → the atamo is for secular (everyday, non-church) music; the church set is the next lesson.
- Mixes kebero and negarit → both are drums, but the negarit is the great palace drum of proclamations, beaten with a large stick.
