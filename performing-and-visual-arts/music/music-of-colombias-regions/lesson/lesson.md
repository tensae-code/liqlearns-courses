# Music of Colombia's Regions

## Do it first

Quick start: say in one sentence what you think this lesson is about — Colombia heard region by region: its own ensembles — vallenato, gaitas, marimba de chonta, arpa llanera — and the musics of the Caribbean, the Pacific, the Orinoquía and the Andes. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ensembles of Colombia

Colombia's own ensembles, named one by one: the **conjunto vallenato**, the **conjunto de gaitas**, and the **Pacific ensemble whose most representative instrument is the marimba de chonta**; then ensembles of instruments and voice, **rock's arrival in Colombia**, the **llanero ensemble of arpa llanera, cuatro and capachos**, the **Cauca ensemble of transverse flutes**, and **indigenous Colombian ensembles**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Caribbean — bands, cantaoras and drums

The Caribbean (Atlántico) region: its **chibcha, arawak and caribe roots**; the **bands of European wind and percussion instruments that play porro, puyas and bullerengues**; the **cantaoras** and their cultural practice; **"La Gota Fría"**; and the **guacharaca, bird and instrument in one**. The music guide adds the coast's **African-origin drum** — a tensed skin over a cylindrical or conical box, struck with hands or sticks — at home in the **tambora and caja vallenata**. Songs are placed by name and purpose only.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Pacific — marimba de chonta and currulao

The Pacific's most representative instrument is the **marimba de chonta**. The older book teaches it by doing: a **word-rhythm ostinato — each line is the pulse — is learned by the whole group, and over it one student improvises a spontaneous melody on the marimba**. The music guide hears the coast's **marimba ensembles, where the cununo accompanies the currulao**, one of the greatest expressions of the coast's folklore, and its other rhythms: **contradanza, aguabajo, jota chocoana, makerule, patacoré, juga**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Orinoquía — joropo, arpa, cuatro and capachos

The llanos: the **joropo is the base of all its rhythms**, danced by a couple; the **pasaje llanero is a slow joropo where singer and harp alternate**; the **arpa, melodic instrument par excellence, developed from the European harp**; the **cuatro** that accompanies it; and the ensemble of **arpa, cuatro and capachos**. The music guide adds the region's own rhythms and the **indigenous communities of the Eastern Plains, who keep their own musical expressions, different from llanera music**.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Andean region and Jorge Veloza

The music guide finds the **Andean region on the map and hears it — its musical rhythms, instrumentation, songs and composers** — and meets its popular master **Jorge Veloza, exponent of the region's music**, met again among the percussion instruments.
