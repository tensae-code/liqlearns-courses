# Lesson plan — Fukera and Shilela: Boast and Answer

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Fukera and Shilela: Boast and Answer
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's hero-poetry performed aloud — fukera, the hero's boast in short hurried lines; shilela (qererito), the companions' answering praise in long lines; and zefen, the community's song — what each form is, how they answer each other, and the heroes they remember.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Kinds of Traditional Music — the learner has already met shilela and fukera as praise chanting; this lesson teaches the two forms apart, line by line and hero by hero.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Fukera: the boast** — a fukera performer expresses heroism holding weapons, sticks, farm tools or flags, moving side to side (Grade 5, printed p. 109).
2. **Shilela and qererito: the answer** — the companions' answering chant — "we are with you, you are not alone"; the high place of fukera and qererito in Ethiopian society (Grade 5, printed pp. 109–110).
3. **Zefen, and the three classroom uses** — zefen — song whose melody and poem are the community's own; fukera, shilela and zefen used for unity, for mutual respect, and for patience when people clash (Grade 5, printed pp. 110–111).
4. **Poetry for heroes: the exemplars** — fukera and qererito defined against each other — long lines against short and hurried; exemplars for Emperor Tewodros, Dejazmach Belay Zeleke, and both forms for Lt. General Haylu Kebede, by hero and themes only (Grade 6, printed pp. 70–71).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Kinds of Traditional Music and Acting Our History. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe a fukera performance: what the performer holds, how he moves, what he cries.
2. Say what shilela/qererito answers, and what the answer gives the hero and the hearers.
3. Tell fukera from qererito by their lines: short and hurried against long and pausing.
4. Name zefen and say whose its melody and poem are.
5. Name the heroes of the book's exemplars — Tewodros, Belay Zeleke, Haylu Kebede — by themes only.

## What you need
Your voice, courage, and companions to answer you — fukera is never performed alone.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Stand tall. Say one true, proud thing about someone you admire — short and strong. Now let a friend answer you with praise of the same person, longer and slower. You have just performed the shape of this lesson: boast, and answer. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses the two forms → fukera is ONE hero boasting, short lines, hurried; qererito is the MANY answering, long lines, with pauses to reflect.
- Thinks the exemplars must be memorised word for word → they are placed by hero and themes only; learn the shape, honour the hero, do not recite what the lesson does not print.
