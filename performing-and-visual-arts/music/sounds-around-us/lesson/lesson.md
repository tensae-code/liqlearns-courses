# Sounds Around Us

## Do it first

Close your eyes for one minute and just listen. Do not talk. When the minute ends, name every sound you heard — a voice, a door, a bird, a car, your own breathing. That listening is where this whole Music course begins: before we sing anything, we hear.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sounds around us

Think of your walk to school this morning. What sounds did you hear on the way? A car passing. A cow calling. A bird in a tree. People talking. A door closing.

Now think of your home. What sounds do you know inside your home? Water pouring. A pot on the fire. A baby crying. A radio playing. Feet on the floor.

Listening is this: hearing a sound and naming the thing that makes it. Every sound around us is made by something — a person, an animal, a bird, a machine, the wind, the rain. When you can name the maker, you are really listening.

[Picture 9450 — a road to school: a house, a car, a cow and a bird, each with its sound]

The first music skill is this careful listening. We also listen to simple songs the same way: hear the song all the way through, take in its message, and then say what you heard — what the song was about, and whether it sounded happy or sad, beautiful or plain. Listen first. Judge after. Sing last.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sound and imitating sounds

Now we can say it properly: **sound is anything heard by the ear.**

Around us there are many different sounds, and they come from three big groups:

- **Birds** — chirping and singing, often in the morning.
- **Animals** — domestic animals at home, like the cow, the goat and the dog, and wild animals far away.
- **Music instruments** — sounds people make on purpose, to play music.

After listening comes imitating. Making a sound back is how your ear teaches your voice. Try it: imitate the sound of a domestic animal you know. Now a wild animal. Now a bird. Your class does it together — one imitates, the others guess the maker.

Hearing, naming, imitating — that is the road every song in this unit walks on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion three sounds from your road to school and three sounds from inside your home. Then imitate one animal sound and one bird sound, and let your companion guess the makers. Finish by saying what sound is, in your own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. On your next walk to school, count the different sounds you hear. Name the maker of each one when you arrive.
2. At home, stand in the doorway with your eyes closed. List five sounds inside your home and what makes each one.
3. Play the guessing game with your family: one person imitates an animal or a bird, everyone else names the maker.
