# Qenet: Ethiopia's Scales

## Do it first

Sing the eight-sound ladder you know. Now sing it again but SKIP the 4th and 7th sounds — five sounds will carry the whole song. You have just sung in Tizta, Ethiopia's best-loved qenet.

## Sub lesson 1 — Qenet: Ethiopia's scale system

A **qenet** (ቅኝት) is a **scale — a sound-arrangement system**. Ethiopia's main qenet are **Tizta, Bati, Anchi Hoye Lene and Ambassel**. They are **pentatonic — five sounds** — with a modal major/minor character, and Ethiopian music is **not exhausted by these four**: they are the main doors, not the whole house. The teacher guide adds the modes' family: tizita major and minor, bati major and minor, anchihoye, and ambassel in its types 1 and 2 — and it prints the Tizita mode's notes as an answer key: **C–E–F–G–A–C**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building Tizta major and Bati major

Build the qenet from the ladder you know. **Tizta major**: from a diatonic major scale, **omit the 4th and 7th sounds** — worked from C major, and shown on the piano. Its practice melodies are named by title only: **"አንች ልጅ" (Anch Lij)** and **"እኛም አለን ሙዚቃ" (We Also Have Music)**. **Bati major**: **omit the 2nd and 6th sounds**; its melody is titled **"ሸግየ ሸጊቱ" (Shegiye Shegitu)**. Two different leavings-out, two different songs' souls — that is why the modes are not interchangeable.

## Sub lesson 3 — Solfeggio and pitch names (teacher guide — evidence)

**Solfeggio** — singing pitches by their syllables — began with **Guido d'Arezzo** about a thousand years ago, from the hymn *Ut Queant Laxis*: **do-re-mi-fa-sol-la-si**. Sing it **movable-do** (the tonic is always Do) or **fixed-do**; name pitches on the piano's white keys and on the G-clef staff. Then perform in meter: **4/4 runs S-W-M-W** (strong, weak, medium, weak) and **3/4 runs S-W-W**; clap and count along. The guide's game: the **number-pyramid** — sing 1, then 121, then 12321, up the full scale and back.

## Sub lesson 4 — Kodály hand signs and music time (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 8 guide teaches solfeggio through the **Kodály method**: every scale degree is sung with a **hand sign at a rising body level** — **Do** at belly-button, **Re** slightly higher, **Mi** near heart level, **Fa** (thumb-down turn), **Sol** at chin, **La** (arched hand, fingers down), **Ti** at eye level pointing up, high **Do** at the forehead — visual, auditory and bodily learning at once. With the beat: the beat is music's pulse; **simple meter** (duple, triple, quadruple) stands against **compound meter** (3/8, 6/8, 9/8). Count **6/8 two ways** — six eighth-note beats, or two dotted-quarter beats. **2/2** (alla breve): the half note is one beat. **2/4**: two quarter notes per bar. And sing the **Anchihoye and Ambassel** scales from the staff, as the guide prints them.

## Sub lesson 5 — Sight reading and dictation

Reading music is proved two ways: **SIGHT READING** — sing or play written music you have never seen — and **DICTATION** — write down what you hear. The book's practice: complete bars with the missing note or rest; add the barlines; build five bars for each given signature, in the **simple** time signatures (2/4, 3/4, 4/4 — top number beats per bar, bottom number the note type). Then **rhythmic dictation**: in 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4, write **only the rhythm** of the sounds the teacher plays. The Grade 8 book carries the same pair onward as its music-reading chapter. Proof by eye, proof by ear — a reader has both.

## Say it to your companion

Build a qenet for your companion: sing a five-sound ladder and let them name which diatonic sounds you left out — Tizta or Bati?

## Do It Yourself

1. Build G-major Tizta and D-major Bati on the staff, as the book's exercise sets.
2. Sing the scale with Kodály hand signs, up and down, without missing a sign.
3. Take a rhythmic dictation: a partner claps a bar in 4/4; write only the rhythm.
