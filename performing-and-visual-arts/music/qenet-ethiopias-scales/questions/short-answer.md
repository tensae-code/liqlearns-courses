# Qenet: Ethiopia's Scales — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Name the four main qenet.
**Answer: Tizta, Bati, Anchi Hoye Lene and Ambassel.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Name one Tizta practice melody by its title.
**Answer: "አንች ልጅ" (Anch Lij) or "እኛም አለን ሙዚቃ" (We Also Have Music).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** In the number-pyramid exercise, what comes after 121?
**Answer: 12321 — the pyramid grows up the scale and comes back down.**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** What are the two ways to count 6/8?
**Answer: Six eighth-note beats, or two dotted-quarter beats.**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Which time signatures does this practice use?
**Answer: The simple ones — 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4.**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** Name the five interval kinds that form qenet.
**Answer:** Major, perfect, minor, augmented and diminished.

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** Why must a minor never be guessed from its major?
**Answer:** Same family name, different interval law — the formation must be built from the base by its own pattern.

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** From any base, what decides a qenet's formation?
**Answer:** Its interval pattern — the law applied from the base sound.

**9. (Sub lesson 9)** Name the chant the chapter sets among its melodies.
**Answer:** The ስራ chant.

**10. (Sub lesson 10)** What standing do the G7 and G8 guide parts have in this lesson?
**Answer:** Teacher-guide evidence — labelled so; the pupil book teaches, the guides evidence.

**11. (Sub lesson 16)** Do it on the staff: build G-major in Tizta, as the Grade 7 exercise directs. What have you built when it is done?
**Answer:** The G-major scale ordered by the Tizta qenet, written on the staff — the exercise's own building item, done in this lesson's qenet work.

**12. (Sub lesson 16)** Do it on the staff: build D-major in Bati, as the Grade 7 exercise directs.
**Answer:** The D-major scale ordered by the Bati qenet, written on the staff.

**13. (Sub lesson 16)** I can… read music in which two qenet, per the Grade 7 Chapter 2 self-check?
**Answer:** Tizta and Bati — applying written notes, rests and beats in both.
