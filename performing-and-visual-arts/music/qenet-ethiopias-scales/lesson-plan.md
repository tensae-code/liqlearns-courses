# Lesson plan — Qenet: Ethiopia's Scales

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Qenet: Ethiopia's Scales
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's own scale system — ቅኝት (qenet): the pentatonic modes Tizta, Bati, Anchi Hoye Lene and Ambassel, how Tizta and Bati major are built by leaving sounds out, solfeggio and the Kodály hand signs, and reading music by sight and by dictation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Scales, Pitch and Key Signatures — the learner can already build diatonic major and minor scales; this lesson meets Ethiopia's pentatonic system beside them.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Qenet: Ethiopia's scale system** — a qenet is a scale / sound-arrangement system; the main qenet are Tizta, Bati, Anchi Hoye Lene and Ambassel — pentatonic, with modal major/minor character (Grade 7, printed pp. 56–57).
2. **Building Tizta major and Bati major** — Tizta major: omit the 4th and 7th sounds of the major scale; Bati major: omit the 2nd and 6th; each with its practice melodies, named only (Grade 7, printed pp. 57–67).
3. **Solfeggio and pitch names (teacher guide — evidence)** — solfeggio from Guido d'Arezzo; movable-do and fixed-do; singing in 3/4 and 4/4 with strong/weak beat patterns; the number-pyramid exercise (Grade 7 teacher guide, printed pp. 44–51).
4. **Kodály hand signs and music time (teacher guide — evidence)** — each scale degree sung with a hand sign at a rising body level; simple and compound meter; counting 6/8 two ways; 2/2 and 2/4 (Grade 8 teacher guide, printed pp. 13–18).
5. **Sight reading and dictation** — solfege's two parts — sight reading and dictation; rhythmic dictation in 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4; melody sight-reading sets (Grade 7, printed pp. 49–56).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Scales, Pitch and Key Signatures. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define qenet (ቅኝት) and name the four main modes.
2. Build Tizta major (leave out 4 and 7) and Bati major (leave out 2 and 6).
3. Sing solfeggio syllables and place the Kodály hand signs from Do to high Do.
4. Count and clap 6/8 two ways, and say what 2/2 and 2/4 mean.
5. Prove reading two ways: sight reading and dictation.

## What you need
Your voice, paper staves, and hands for the Kodály signs.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Sing the eight-sound ladder you know. Now sing it again but SKIP the 4th and 7th sounds — five sounds will carry the whole song. You have just sung in Tizta, Ethiopia's best-loved qenet. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes Tizta and Bati → Tizta takes out 4 and 7; Bati takes out 2 and 6. Sing both from the same tonic and hear the difference.
- Hand signs drift → the body is the ladder: Do at belly-button, rising degree by degree to high Do at the forehead.
