| Vietnam — Study Topic 1, Lesson 1 "Triads and the main triads in the major and minor modes" — the triad (hợp âm ba) has a structure of three sounds combined together by thirds (quãng ba) — the lowest sound is called the root (âm gốc, sound 1), the second sound the middle (âm giữa, sound 3) and the topmost sound the top note (âm ngọn, sound 5); the major triad (hợp âm ba trưởng) is a triad of three sounds combined in the structure of thirds in which from the root to sound 3 is a major third and from sound 3 to sound 5 is a minor third — it has a healthy (khoẻ khoắn) character and a fresh, bright colour, and its symbol is the capital letters (examples C, F, G, with guitar fingerings); the minor triad (hợp âm ba thứ) has from the root to sound 3 a minor third and from sound 3 to sound 5 a major third — it carries a soft (mềm mại) character and a dark, gloomy (u tối) colour, and its symbol is the letters accompanied by the letter m (the abbreviation of the word minor: thứ), as Am and Dm; the diminished triad (hợp âm ba giảm) is a triad of three sounds combined in the structure of two minor thirds stacked on each other, from the root to sound 5 being a diminished fifth — it carries a dissonant (nghịch) character and a dark, gloomy, heavy colour, and its symbol is the letters dim placed beside the letter, as A dim and D dim; the major, minor and diminished triads can be built on any sound degree (the basic degrees and the altered degrees) in the major or minor mode following the regulated intervals of each chord kind, and the chords are practised on the guitar (student book) | Book 11, printed p. 5 |
| Vietnam — Study Topic 1, Lesson 1 — the main triads in the major and minor modes (các hợp âm ba chính) — in the major mode there are three main triads, the triads built on degrees I, IV and V, which are major triads carrying a consonant (thuận), healthy character with a fresh, bright colour (in C major: degree I is C major, degree IV is F major, degree V is G major); in the minor mode there are three main triads, the triads built on degree I (the tonic, chủ), degree IV (the subdominant, hạ át) and degree V (the dominant, át), which are minor triads carrying a consonant, soft character and a dark, gentle (dịu êm) colour (in natural A minor: I is Am, IV is Dm, V is Em); but the main triad in the minor mode, when the harmonic minor is used, because degree VII is raised a semitone, the main triad of degree V is a major triad (in harmonic E minor, the degree-V triad is B major); and the main triads in the major mode, when the harmonic major is used, because degree VI is lowered a semitone, the main triad at degree IV is a minor triad (harmonic C major has the degree-IV triad F minor, Fm); based on the specific key, the main triads can be built on all the different keys (student book) | Book 11, printed p. 8 |
| Vietnam — Study Topic 1, Lesson 2 "The secondary triads in the major and minor modes" — besides the main triads, in the major and minor modes there are also the secondary triads (hợp âm ba phụ), which have characters and colours different from the main triads, contributing to creating more rich, diverse colour for the accompaniment part of music works; in the major mode there are four kinds of secondary triad built on degrees II, III, VI and VII — the secondary triads built on degrees II, III and VI are minor triads carrying a dark colour with a soft, consonant character (in C major: II is Dm, III is Em, VI is Am), and the secondary triad built on degree VII is a diminished triad with a dissonant character and a dark, gloomy colour (B dim); in the minor mode there are four kinds of secondary triad built on degrees II, III, VI and VII — the secondary triad built on degree II is a diminished triad carrying a dissonant character, not consonant, with a dark, gloomy colour, and the secondary triads built on degrees III, VI and VII are major triads carrying a bright colour, consonant, with a healthy character (in natural A minor: III is C major, VI is F major, VII is G major); and if it is the harmonic minor, because degree VII is raised a semitone, the secondary triad of degree VII is a diminished triad (G sharp dim); the lesson opens with two harmonisations of Huy Du's song "Đường chúng ta đi" (The Road We Travel, words from Xuân Sách's poem), the second adding the secondary triads to the accompaniment (student book) | Book 11, printed p. 10 |
| Vietnam — Study Topic 1, Lesson 3 "The dominant seventh chord" (Hợp âm bảy át) — the dominant seventh chord is a chord built on degree V (át) of a major mode or a harmonic minor mode; its structure is a major triad plus a minor third above; it carries a dissonant character, not consonant (không thuận tai), with a bright colour; its symbol is the capital letter accompanied by the number 7 (G7, E7, with guitar fingerings); in the accompaniment part of a song, the colour of the dominant seventh chord is heard very clearly and it usually resolves (giải quyết) to the tonic triad of degree I — shown in Phú Quang's song "Em ơi Hà Nội phố" (Oh Hanoi Streets, words from Phan Vũ's poem), whose accompaniment uses E7 resolving to Am; the way of building it: when building the dominant seventh chord depending on a specific key, one needs to determine the degree-V sound of that key and build the dominant seventh chord following the principle: a major triad plus a minor third above (in harmonic E minor, the dominant seventh chord is the sounds B – D sharp – F sharp – A, the B seventh chord, B7); the chord is practised with Phạm Tuyên's song "Màu cờ tôi yêu" (The Colour of the Flag I Love, words Diệp Minh Tuyền), accompanied first by triads without the seventh chord and then with the seventh chord added, and applied to Văn Cao's "Làng tôi" (My Village) (student book) | Book 11, printed p. 14 |
