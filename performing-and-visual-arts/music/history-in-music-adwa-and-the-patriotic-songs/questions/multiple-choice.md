# History in Music: Adwa and the Patriotic Songs — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.


**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The march to Adwa was summoned with…
  a) Mesenqo, negarit, embilta, atamo, kebero, trumpet and the like b) Printed letters only c) Church bells alone
**Answer: a) Mesenqo, negarit, embilta, atamo, kebero, trumpet and the like** — the book's own instrument roll for the summons.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Shilela and qererito at the march worked by…
  a) Praising heroes and shaming the fearful b) Teaching the alphabet c) Marking the harvest
**Answer: a) Praising heroes and shaming the fearful** — praise for the brave, shame for the fearful — courage in sound.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** “Though My Bones Be Crushed” is sung by…
  a) Tilahun Gessesse b) Ejigayehu Shibabaw c) Aster Aweke
**Answer: a) Tilahun Gessesse** — the book's exemplar ሀ names Tilahun Gessesse.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The song's purpose is…
  a) Patriotic rousing — the singer offered to the country's defence b) A wedding celebration c) A children's counting game
**Answer: a) Patriotic rousing — the singer offered to the country's defence** — its title itself is the offering: though my bones be crushed.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The song “Adwa” is sung by…
  a) Ejigayehu Shibabaw b) Tilahun Gessesse c) Mahmoud Ahmed
**Answer: a) Ejigayehu Shibabaw** — the book's second exemplar names Ejigayehu Shibabaw (Gigi).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** The pair of exemplars together teaches that Ethiopian history is…
  a) Kept in song as well as in writing b) Kept only in foreign books c) Forgotten after one generation
**Answer: a) Kept in song as well as in writing** — named singers carry the victory in named songs.
