# Lesson plan — History of Western Concert Music

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** History of Western Concert Music
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The history of Western concert and orchestral music — its forms, halls and institutions, from the early orchestras to today.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier music lessons — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Music's beginnings** — music's beginnings: sound as a link to the gods, the shaman as first musician, work songs (Brazil's washerwomen, US cotton pickers) feeding blues and jazz
2. **The medieval church** — the medieval church: Gregorian chant (monodic, in unison) against the troubadours' secular folk music, from which polyphony begins
3. **Baroque and Classical** — Baroque and Classical: luthiers and Stradivari, Bach, the sonata form's musical discourse, Mozart and Beethoven
4. **Romantic and modern** — Romantic and modern: Verdi and Brazil's Carlos Gomes (O Guarani); Debussy, Stravinsky, Villa-Lobos' Bachianas Brasileiras, Schoenberg's twelve-tone system, Cage's chance sounds, Stockhausen's electronic studio

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
