# Performing Kinds of Music

## Do it first

Stand in two groups, facing each other. Group One sings a short line. Group Two answers with a line of its own — same beat, new words or sounds. Swap. You have just performed music the way this lesson teaches it: kinds of music are known by singing them, not only by naming them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The kinds of music, performed (teacher guide — evidence)

In the Grade 2 teacher guide, the kinds of music first met by listening in Chapter 1 come back in Chapter 2 as things to **do**: the class sings and performs them (§2.1). A kind of music is not finished when you can name it — it is finished when your voice can make it. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence: the guide is a scan with no text layer, so it is cited at section level only, exactly as it was read.

## Sub lesson 2 — Answering songs: two groups, one beat (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide pictures the performance plainly: **a class singing in two groups answering each other**. One group offers a line; the other group answers — and the beat never breaks between them. Listening, answering and keeping time together are the whole skill. Practise it with any short line the class knows: short offer, true answer, shared beat.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how performing a kind of music teaches more than naming it, and sing them one answering line.

## Do It Yourself

1. Lead one round of two-group answering singing with your family.
2. Write the names of the kinds of music your class performed, and one line about how each felt to sing.
