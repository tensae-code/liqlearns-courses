| Vietnam — Lesson 8 (Appreciation) — Huế Royal Court Music (Nhã nhạc Cung đình Huế) — nhã nhạc means "elegant music" (âm nhạc tao nhã), arising in the 15th century in our country; by the 19th century nhã nhạc was distilled and developed to its peak with richness and diversity of genre, unique in the combination between ritual (lễ) and music (nhạc); under the Nguyễn dynasty, nhã nhạc was considered the National Music (Quốc nhạc) — the official music used in the court's sacrificial ceremonies and many solemn rituals — so it is called Huế Court Nhã nhạc; the instruments in the court orchestra are made more skilfully and finely than the instruments used among the folk, comprising all the colour sounds: the clear sound, the opaque sound, the high sound, the low sound, the bronze sound, the iron sound, the silk sound, the bamboo sound; the court orchestra is organised into two bands: the đại nhạc (great music), performed in important rituals such as the Nam Giao sacrifice (lễ tế đàn Nam Giao), the Miếu sacrifice (tế Miếu) and the Grand Court assembly (Đại triều) — the great-music band has a large volume, far-carrying, mainly using the kinds of drums, the oboes together with the percussion and lightly-blown instruments; and the tiểu nhạc (small music), which has pieces and pieces of a relatively more stable form and bears elegant, cheerful colours, its volume not echoing loudly and strongly like the great music, often used in the court's banquets, festivals and Lunar New Year (Tết Nguyên đán), many small-music pieces also having words; the small-music instruments include the drum, the cymbals (não bạt), the clappers (phách), the sênh tiền (coin clappers), the flute (sáo), the đàn tì bà and the đàn nguyệt, and some small-music pieces are "Lưu thuỷ – Kim tiền", "Phẩm tiết" and "Nguyên tiêu"; in 2003, Huế Court Nhã nhạc, Vietnam's first representative, was inscribed by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity (from 2008 transferred into the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity); and listening to the work "Lưu thuỷ – Kim tiền" (court music, excerpt) (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 33 |
