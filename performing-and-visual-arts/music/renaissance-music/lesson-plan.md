# Lesson plan — Renaissance Music

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Renaissance Music
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Renaissance music — balance, clarity and equal-voice polyphony, the mass, motet and madrigal, and Palestrina its master.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **the Renaissance** — the Renaissance: balance, clarity and expression; polyphony where all voices are near-equal, and imitation passing a motif from voice to voice; the mass, motet, madrigal and chanson; instruments stepping out as soloists — lute, viola da gamba, harpsichord, flute; consorts of like instruments; music printing (Venice, 1501) putting notes within everyone's reach.
2. **Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, master of the Italian polyphonic school** — Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, master of the Italian polyphonic school: over 100 masses and many motets; the "Missa Papae Marcelli" whose clear words persuaded the Vatican to keep polyphony in church; the "Palestrina style" as the name for pure, balanced church polyphony.
3. **Orlando di Lasso of the Flemish school, music director in Munich** — Orlando di Lasso of the Flemish school, music director in Munich, composer of more than 2,000 works in four languages — masses, motets, psalms, madrigals, canzonettas, chansons; his double-choir "Echo", where one choir answers the other as an echo, and the merry madrigal "Matona mia cara".
4. **Орландо ди Ласо (1532–1594)** — Орландо ди Ласо (1532–1594) — Orlando di Lasso: with Palestrina the head of the Franco-Flemish polyphonic school and Europe's most famous musician of his century; kidnapped three times as a boy for his beautiful voice; Naples, Rome (maestro di cappella at St John Lateran at twenty-one), and from 1556 the Munich court; some 2,000 works — 530 motets, 175 madrigals, 150 chansons — crowned by the "Lagrime di San Pietro".
5. **"Audite nova!"** — "Audite nova!" — Lasso's four-voice a cappella piece in full score (cantus, altus, tenor, bassus) — Renaissance choral polyphony read and sung.
