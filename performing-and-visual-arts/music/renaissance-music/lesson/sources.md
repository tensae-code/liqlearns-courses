**Book 1:** *Музичка уметност за I година гимназиско образование (Musical Art for Year I of Gymnasium Education)* (student book, Macedonian) — North Macedonia, Литера Гроуп (Litera Group), Skopje; year 2025; ISBN 978-608-4855-74-3; authors Simona Zafirovska and Teuta Alimi Selmani; approved by the Ministry of Education and Science (decision no. 26-934/1) Grade: Grade 10 (student/teacher: STUDENT) — gymnasium year I filed as grade 10. PDF pages: 122 (120 printed pages per CIP; read in place from phase3-staging, size and pages verified against the record; cover seen). Verdict: COMPLETE. North Macedonia's own way: a full music-history arc (first peoples, antiquity, Middle Ages, Renaissance, Baroque) is taught beside a complete theory course, and the heritage unit treats every people's singing styles, oro dances and folk instruments as one shared treasury — with the UNESCO-listed Glasoečko two-part singing, the Kopačkata oro and the Macedonian gajda named as protected heritage. Excluded per standing rules: Речник (the glossary), printed pp. 113–116 — glossary/word list, inventory-only; the bibliography (pp. 117–118) is not content. Printed song texts ("O sole mio", "We Will Rock You", the folk-song listening examples) are summarised, never transcribed. Read in full in the 2026-11-16 Art expansion read. **Book 2:** *Музичка уметност за IV година гимназиско образование (изборен предмет) (Music Art for Year IV of gymnasium education, elective subject)* (student book, Macedonian) — North Macedonia, Министерство за образование и наука на Република Македонија (Ministry of Education and Science), Skopje; year 2010 (CIP), copy in hand is the 2015 printing; ISBN 978-608-226-254-3; author Suzana Tnokova Kuzmanovska; Ministry approval decision no. 22-4028/1 of 29.06.2010 Grade: Grade 13 (gymnasium Year IV, elective) (student/teacher: STUDENT). PDF pages: 96 (96 printed pages per CIP including covers; image-only scan read page by page from rendered sheets; read in place from phase3-staging, never deleted). Verdict: COMPLETE. North Macedonia's own way: the year's theory is carried on Macedonian backs — Teshkoto opens the book (in Blazhe Koneski's poem), Shuplevski, Skalovski and Prokopiev are the composer portraits, the folk songs are Bitola's, Christmas and Easter are told in Macedonian custom, and the ballet whose story is retold in full is Prokopiev's "Labin i Dojrana". Excluded per standing rules: the quotations page (printed p. 3) and the orchestra photograph (p. 4) are front matter; pure practice pages are not placed — the children's-instrument playing exercises (p. 20), the melodic exercises (pp. 38, 44), the syncopation/contratan/triplet drill examples (p. 53) and the recorder exercises (p. 65); the jazz (p. 60) and rock (p. 61) spreads are photo-and-listening pages whose teachable text duplicates Book 7's jazz and rock chapters; printed pp. 92–96 are a blank endpaper and blank staff paper. The Johann Strauss chapter (p. 72) repeats Book 7's Strauss biography and is placed here as printed in this book. Song lyrics are summarised, never transcribed.
