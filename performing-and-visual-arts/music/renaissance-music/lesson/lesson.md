# Renaissance Music

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — Renaissance music — balance, clarity and equal-voice polyphony, the mass, motet and madrigal, and Palestrina its master. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — the Renaissance

the Renaissance: balance, clarity and expression; polyphony where all voices are near-equal, and imitation passing a motif from voice to voice; the mass, motet, madrigal and chanson; instruments stepping out as soloists — lute, viola da gamba, harpsichord, flute; consorts of like instruments; music printing (Venice, 1501) putting notes within everyone's reach.

## Sub lesson 2 — Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, master of the Italian polyphonic school

Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, master of the Italian polyphonic school: over 100 masses and many motets; the "Missa Papae Marcelli" whose clear words persuaded the Vatican to keep polyphony in church; the "Palestrina style" as the name for pure, balanced church polyphony.

## Sub lesson 3 — Orlando di Lasso of the Flemish school, music director in Munich

Orlando di Lasso of the Flemish school, music director in Munich, composer of more than 2,000 works in four languages — masses, motets, psalms, madrigals, canzonettas, chansons; his double-choir "Echo", where one choir answers the other as an echo, and the merry madrigal "Matona mia cara".

## Sub lesson 4 — Орландо ди Ласо (1532–1594)

Орландо ди Ласо (1532–1594) — Orlando di Lasso: with Palestrina the head of the Franco-Flemish polyphonic school and Europe's most famous musician of his century; kidnapped three times as a boy for his beautiful voice; Naples, Rome (maestro di cappella at St John Lateran at twenty-one), and from 1556 the Munich court; some 2,000 works — 530 motets, 175 madrigals, 150 chansons — crowned by the "Lagrime di San Pietro".

## Sub lesson 5 — "Audite nova!"

"Audite nova!" — Lasso's four-voice a cappella piece in full score (cantus, altus, tenor, bassus) — Renaissance choral polyphony read and sung.
