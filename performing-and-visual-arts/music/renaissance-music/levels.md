# Renaissance Music — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — the Renaissance
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the Renaissance: balance, clarity and expression; polyphony where all voices are near-equal, and imitation passing a motif from voice to voice; the mass, motet, madrigal and chanson; instruments stepping out as soloists — lute, viola da gamba, harpsichord, flute; consorts of like instruments; music printing (Venice, 1501) putting notes within everyone's reach.

## Sub lesson 2 — Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, master of the Italian polyphonic school
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, master of the Italian polyphonic school: over 100 masses and many motets; the "Missa Papae Marcelli" whose clear words persuaded the Vatican to keep polyphony in church; the "Palestrina style" as the name for pure, balanced church polyphony.

## Sub lesson 3 — Orlando di Lasso of the Flemish school, music director in Munich
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Orlando di Lasso of the Flemish school, music director in Munich, composer of more than 2,000 works in four languages — masses, motets, psalms, madrigals, canzonettas, chansons; his double-choir "Echo", where one choir answers the other as an echo, and the merry madrigal "Matona mia cara".

## Sub lesson 4 — Орландо ди Ласо (1532–1594)
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Орландо ди Ласо (1532–1594) — Orlando di Lasso: with Palestrina the head of the Franco-Flemish polyphonic school and Europe's most famous musician of his century; kidnapped three times as a boy for his beautiful voice; Naples, Rome (maestro di cappella at St John Lateran at twenty-one), and from 1556 the Munich court; some 2,000 works — 530 motets, 175 madrigals, 150 chansons — crowned by the "Lagrime di San Pietro".

## Sub lesson 5 — "Audite nova!"
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: "Audite nova!" — Lasso's four-voice a cappella piece in full score (cantus, altus, tenor, bassus) — Renaissance choral polyphony read and sung.
