# Lesson plan — Saint Yared

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Saint Yared
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Saint Yared, the 6th-century father of Ethiopian church music — his three chant styles Ge'ez, Ezil and Araray, and the notation he built to keep them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History, where Yared appears as evidence; here the pupil book's own Yared teaching stands as its own lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Yared and his three chant styles** — the 6th-century father of Ethiopian church music; Ge'ez, Ezil and Araray — three melodic styles for the church's seasons and feasts.
2. **Yared's notation: keeping the chant** — the notation symbols Yared's system carries — music written so it can be taught, checked and handed on exactly.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History; Church Instruments. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell who Saint Yared was and when he lived.
2. Name his three chant styles and his notation's purpose.
3. Say why Yared stands at the head of Ethiopian music history.

## What you need
Only listening ears — chant is named and described, never transcribed.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep styles and qenet apart: Ge'ez/Ezil/Araray are church chant styles (Yared); Tizta/Bati/Anchi Hoye/Ambassel are the secular qenet scales.
