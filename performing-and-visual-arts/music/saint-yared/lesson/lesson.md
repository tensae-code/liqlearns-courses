# Saint Yared

## Do it first

Church chant is not one mood. Some feasts are solemn and heavy; some are light with joy. Fifteen centuries ago one musician built a system wide enough for all of them — and a way of writing it down so it could never be lost. His name is Yared.

## Sub lesson 1 — Yared and his three chant styles

**Saint Yared**, the **6th-century** father of Ethiopian church music, built its chant system in **three melodic styles: Ge'ez, Ezil and Araray** — the solemn style and its lighter companions, fitted to the church's seasons and feasts. The Grade 7 book gives Yared his own place in its history chapter (§3.2): before the azmari, before the bands, stands the cantor-scholar whose system the church still sings. Every tsenatsil shaken in Church Instruments rings inside a musical house Yared built.

## Sub lesson 2 — Yared's notation: keeping the chant

A chant system carried only in memory can drift; Yared's system carries **its own notation symbols** — signs that fix the chant's movement so it can be **taught, checked and handed on exactly**, singer to singer, century to century. That is why Ethiopian church music can claim a written line back to the 6th century: the notation is the bridge. The pupil book's Yared teaching ends where every Ethiopian music history begins — with respect for the man who made the country's music keepable.
