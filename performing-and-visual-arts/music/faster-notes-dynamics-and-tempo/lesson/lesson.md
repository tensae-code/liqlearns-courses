# Faster Notes, Dynamics and Tempo

## Do it first

Clap four slow beats. Now clap twice inside each beat — clap-clap, clap-clap — without speeding the beat itself. You have just played eighth notes inside quarter beats. Keep that feeling; the lesson names it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beat measures sound

Under every note in this lesson stands the **beat**. The Grade 4 book defines it: **beat measures the time-length of sounds** and makes measured movement possible; music is made by **joining sound and beat**. **Notes are the signs that stand for sounds**, and they are measured by beat — whole, half and quarter, the values you know, with the eighth and the dotted notes of this lesson joining them. Practise sounding "ta" or "la" for each value, using both hands as the measuring tool.

## Sub lesson 2 — The eighth note

The **eighth note** is new here: a single eighth note sounds for **half a beat** — two eighths fill the room of one quarter note. It carries a **flag**, and eighths travelling together are often **beamed** in pairs. The book's exercises play eighths, mix **eighth notes with rests**, and finish by having you **write your own** — a rhythm is yours when you can both play it and write it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dotted notes

A **dot placed after the note-head adds HALF the note's value**. Work the book's three sums: a **dotted whole = 4 + 2 = 6 beats**; a **dotted half = 2 + 1 = 3 beats**; a **dotted quarter = 1 + ½ = 1½ beats**. The rule never changes: find the note's value, halve it, add it on. The exercise writes and plays dotted notes and sums given examples — dot arithmetic you can hear.

## Sub lesson 4 — Dynamics and tempo: loud–soft, fast–slow

**Dynamics** are the signs that command a sound's **loudness — its growth and shrinkage**. Learn the book's six-row table: **P (piano) = low volume; PP (pianissimo) = very low; F (forte) = high; FF (fortissimo) = very high; < crescendo = rise gradually from low to high; > decrescendo = sink gradually from high to low**. The wedge signs are mouths: opening, the sound grows; closing, it sinks. The exercise sings a known song **using the signs** — the page conducts, the voice obeys.

**Tempo** is the beat's **speed**, measured by the **metronome** — made in 1815, the book notes. Its commands: **Andante = calm pace; Moderato = medium speed; Allegro = fast; Allegretto = very fast, as the book prints it**. (The book's own table puts Allegretto above Allegro; the lesson keeps the book's ordering, and says so.) The exercise judges the tempo of songs the teacher performs: name the speed-word that fits what you hear.

## Sub lesson 5 — Noise or music? The seven sounds

Not every sound is music. **Noise** (ሁካታ) is sound whose speed **cannot be measured** and which **disturbs** — breaking glass, tin struck with wood, thunder. **Musical sound can be measured, is understood, gives feeling and has pitch** — human voices, instruments. And music's sounds are **seven**: named by the English letters **A–G** and by the Italian sol-fa names — **C = Do, D = Re, E = Mi, F = Fa, G = Sol, A = La, B = Si** — with Do taken as the starting base.

## Sub lesson 6 — Moving to note values

Music moves the body in measured values. Movement to music **steadies posture, breathing and blood circulation**, and the book writes the movements in the very notes of this lesson: the **WALKING beat in QUARTER notes** and the **RUNNING beat in EIGHTH notes**, with stretching and swaying beats between. Write other notes — a dotted half's long stride — and move to them the same way: your feet read music too.

## Say it to your companion

Clap a rhythm for your companion that mixes quarter notes, eighth notes and one dotted note. They must write it down and clap it back exactly.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write and play a line mixing eighth notes and eighth rests.
2. Work the three dotted sums on paper and clap each one.
3. Sing the seven sounds up and down, once in letters and once in sol-fa.
