# Lesson plan — Faster Notes, Dynamics and Tempo

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Faster Notes, Dynamics and Tempo
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the rest of the note family and how music is performed — the eighth note, dotted notes that stretch by half, the loud-and-soft signs (dynamics), the speed signs (tempo), telling noise from musical sound, the seven sounds A–G and Do–Si, and moving your feet to note values.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Reading Music on the Staff and Notes and Rests — the learner can already read whole, half and quarter values; this lesson completes the values and adds the performance signs.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Beat measures sound** — beat measures the time-length of sounds and makes measured movement possible; notes are the signs that stand for sounds, measured by beat (Grade 4, printed p. 2).
2. **The eighth note** — a single eighth note is half a beat — new at Grade 5; playing eighths and mixing eighth notes with rests (Grade 5, printed pp. 20–21).
3. **Dotted notes** — a dot after the note-head adds half the note's value: dotted whole 6, dotted half 3, dotted quarter 1½ (Grade 5, printed pp. 21–22).
4. **Dynamics and tempo: loud–soft, fast–slow** — the volume-changing signs — P piano, PP pianissimo, F forte, FF fortissimo, crescendo and decrescendo (Grade 5, printed p. 23). tempo is measured by the metronome; Andante calm, Moderato medium, Allegro fast, Allegretto very fast as the book prints them (Grade 5, printed pp. 23–24).
5. **Noise or music? The seven sounds** — noise (ሁካታ) cannot be measured and disturbs; musical sound can be measured and has pitch; music has seven basic sounds, A–G and Do–Si (Grade 5, printed pp. 24–25).
6. **Moving to note values** — movement to music steadies posture, breathing and circulation; the walking beat in quarter notes, the running beat in eighth notes (Grade 5, printed p. 124).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Music on the Staff and Notes and Rests. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play eighth notes: two inside one quarter beat, half a beat each.
2. Work dotted sums: a dot adds half the note's value.
3. Read the dynamics signs P, PP, F, FF, crescendo and decrescendo.
4. Read the tempo words Andante, Moderato, Allegro and Allegretto as the book gives them, and name the metronome.
5. Tell noise from musical sound, and name the seven sounds in letters and in sol-fa.

## What you need
A drum or table to tap, paper and pencil, and your voice.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Clap four slow beats. Now clap twice inside each beat — clap-clap, clap-clap — without speeding the beat itself. You have just played eighth notes inside quarter beats. Keep that feeling; the lesson names it. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts a dotted half as 4 → the dot adds HALF of the note, not a whole note: half is 2, its half is 1, total 3.
- Confuses dynamics and tempo → dynamics is HOW LOUD; tempo is HOW FAST. A song can be loud and slow at once.
