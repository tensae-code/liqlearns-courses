# The Regional Culture Groups

## Do it first

A single singer can be forgotten. A group keeps a repertoire the way a granary keeps seed — together, on purpose, year after year. The book names the Amhara region's granaries of song. Learn their names and places as one roll before this lesson explains their work.

## Sub lesson 1 — The named groups and their places

The Grade 7 book names the region's organised music-keepers: the **Wollo Lalibela Kinet**, **Wo/ro Sahin's music school in Gondar**, the **Fasiledes culture group (Gondar)** and the **Gish Abay culture group (Gojjam)**. A **kinet** (culture group) gathers singers, players and dancers into one company that performs the region's songs and dances as a kept repertoire — at festivals, on stages and for visitors — so the music is practised, taught and handed on, not left to chance memory.

## Sub lesson 2 — What a culture group keeps alive

A culture group keeps three things alive at once: the **repertoire** (the songs and dances themselves, performed whole), the **teaching** (younger members learn inside the company, as the Lalibelawoch learned inside families), and the **occasions** (festivals and stages where the region hears itself). Where the azmari carried music alone from place to place, the group carries it **together, in one place, on purpose** — the modern, organised form of the same keeping.
