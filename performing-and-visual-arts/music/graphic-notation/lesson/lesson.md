# Graphic Notation

## Do it first

Quick start: say one thing you already know — a sound, a song, a name — that belongs to this lesson's world. Keep it in mind; the parts will meet it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Symbol grids and drawn pitch scores

**Symbols stand for sounds.** In Malta's Year 1 work a **4×4 symbol grid** is read in different directions — blanks mean **silence, a rest** — and then pupils fill blank grids with symbols of their own and **play each other's compositions**. Drawn shapes work as pitch scores too: a **rising line** means a continuous sound rising, a **falling line** a sound falling, a **flat line** a sound staying at one pitch, and **dot patterns** mean short sounds at different pitches.

## Sub lesson 2 — Story improvisation and the body-percussion chart

Music is also improvised **to a narrated story**: choose an instrument to depict each character or situation, play it in time, and vary **tempo and pitch**; groups then compose one line each of a nursery rhyme. A numbered **4×4 body-percussion chart** is read as a score by four groups — **(1) clap, (2) snap, (3) stamp, (4) slap** — with omitted numbers as rests; pupils then invent their own symbols and compose body-percussion and classroom-sound pieces for the class to play.

## Sub lesson 3 — Composing with notes — Lub-Dab-Rest

Composition also grows from conventional notes: around the heartbeat pattern **clap-clap-rest (ta-ta-sa)** — the "Lub-Dab-Rest" — pupils copy a composition, then create their own with **expression and dynamics** and perform each other's, and compose by counting in the Math Rabbit program.

## Sub lesson 4 — Percussion Jam Band — the box score

A percussion ensemble performs from a **box score**: the class's instruments are sorted into **high, medium and low** pitched sounds, each box is **one beat**, a coloured box means **play** and a blank box means **rest**, and parts A and B are layered together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the parts in order, one sentence each. Companion checks: did every part get its sentence, and did the order hold?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the parts' names from memory, in order.
2. Do the hands-on activity once more, teaching it to someone else.
3. Write one sentence: what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
