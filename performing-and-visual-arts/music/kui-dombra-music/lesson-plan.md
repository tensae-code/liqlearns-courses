# Lesson plan — Kui: Dombra Music

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Kui: Dombra Music
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The kui — Kazakhstan's instrumental dombra pieces — in its tokpe and shertpe manners, for the seasons and the homeland..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 1 Kulmanova Music** — the kui, the Kazakh instrumental piece, heard through the year's seasons; kui as the Kazakh instrumental story
2. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Valiullina Music, contents pp. 141-142** — the instrumental story-piece of the Kazakh steppe
3. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Kulmanova Music, contents pp. 142-143** — kui players compete: listening to instrumental stories
4. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Gorchakova Music, contents** — kui heard inside a Russian-medium year
5. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Orazalieva Music** — the instrumental story of the steppe
6. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 NIS Music** — kui in the NIS year
7. **Kazakhstan Grade 3 Kulmanova Music KZ** — admonition and counsel" and "Aitys — a contest of words" — the terme genre of sung counsel and the aitys improvisation duel of akyns (paired contest, improvisation, winning and losing); the wandering singer-poets of the steppe and the küishi masters
8. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Music (Gorchakova)** — источник вдохновения — the kui: listening to Kurmangazy Sagyrbayuly's kui «Алатау», a dombra portrait of the mountains of his native land; the symphonic kui: Erkegali Rakhmadiev's «Дайрабай», the first symphonic kui — not an arrangement but an author's orchestral work grown from a folk kui; transposition (переложение): Kurmangazy's kui «Адай», the galloping horse and the heroic spirit of the Adai clan, compared in three performances — dombra, orchestra and electronic instruments
9. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Music (Kulmanova)** — Қазақстанның астанасы» — A. Zhaimov's kui «Еркін елім» (My Free Land): how a kui's title and performance open its content; the capital's history and its songs; T. Tokzhanov's kui «Шабыт» (Inspiration): listening for the mood a kui carries; асыл мұра» — cultural heritage defined; the folk kui «Ақсақ құлан» (The Lame Kulan) and its legend in brief — the dombra itself tells the khan of his son's death; Magauya Khamzin's solo and the Turan ethno-folklore ensemble compared; National Dombra Day (2018) and UNESCO's 2014 listing of the kui art; Makhambet Utemisuly's kui «Жұмыр-Қылыш» heard in traditional, classical and estrada manners; Dauletkerei's kui «Қорұғлы» played by the Aldaspan rock group on electric dombra — the national instrument changed by electronic music
10. **Kazakhstan Grade 5 Music (Kulmanova)** — tokpe (bold strokes of the joined fingers up and down the strings) against shertpe (plucking finger by finger); Kurmangazy's kui «Адай» in three lives — solo dombra, the Kurmangazy orchestra, and A. Ensepov's modern estrada version; Tattimbet's kui «Сарыжайлау» in the shertpe tradition: Abiken Khasenov, the Otyrar Sazy orchestra and violinist Zhamilya Serkebaeva compared; its melody sung on "la" and played from notation; how modern arrangements add violin, guitar and synthesizer to the kui; musical style defined; the «Песня о домбре» (V. Vidman); Kurmangazy's kui «Сарыарка»: can a kui wake patriotism? — mood words chosen for it (free, happy, solemn, festive); set against the song «Даркан Дала» (Erkimbekov/Mauленов) as sung by Bibigul Tuleгенова and Ermek Serkebaev
11. **Kazakhstan Grade 6 Music (Musakhajaeva)** — what it teaches: the two great kui manners — tokpe of the west and shertpe of the east and centre; Tattimbet, founder of shertpe («Қосбасар», «Саржайлау»), and Kurmangazy, «Күй атасы», father of the kui («Балбыраун», «Кішкентай»); kui melodies sung in two voices
