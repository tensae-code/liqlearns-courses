# Honduran Music

## Do it first

Name a song that belongs to your country — not a song from far away, your own. Who makes it? What does it sound like? You have just done what Honduras's Grade 7 book does first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our music: Honduran music

The Grade 7 Educación Artística book's second block is Música, and its second sequence is **Nuestra música — our music: Honduran music**. Honduras's own music is met the way the book meets it: through **its makers and its sounds** — the people who make the music of Honduras, and the sound that music carries.

## Sub lesson 2 — Today's sounds in Honduras

The Grade 7 book does not leave Honduran music in the past. Its fourth music sequence, **Los sonidos actuales en Honduras — today's sounds in Honduras**, studies **the musical genres current in Honduras today**: the living sounds of the country now.

## Sub lesson 3 — Historical evolution of Honduran music

The Grade 8 book opens its music block with the **Evolución histórica de la música hondureña — the historical evolution of Honduran music**: the **periods** of Honduras's music, followed **from its beginnings to today**. One country's music, read as a history with periods — earlier sounds, later sounds, and the sounds of now that Sub lesson 2 named.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what is Honduran music, and what are the two ways the books study it? Companion checks: its makers and sounds? The genres of today and the periods from the beginnings?

## Do It Yourself

1. Name one piece of music from your own country and say who makes it and what it sounds like.
2. Write two headings — "today's sounds" and "earlier periods" — and put one example you know under each.
3. Write one sentence: why does a school book teach a country's own music by name?
