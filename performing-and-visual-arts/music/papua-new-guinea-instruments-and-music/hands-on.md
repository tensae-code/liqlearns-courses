# Hands-on — Papua New Guinea Instruments and Music

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Kundu drums and local sound makers
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: The Grade 3 guide sorts factory-made instruments from home-made ones, then meets the local sound makers: kundu drums, rattles, coconut-shell percussion and shakers — thump-thumping, the simple shaker, the beat cylinder — with melody and pattern played together.

## Instruments as each community's craft
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: In Grade 4 the instruments return as crafted objects: musical instruments from different communities, met as things each community makes.

## Hearing PNG's instruments
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: Grade 5 listens closely: distinguishing the sounds of Papua New Guinea's instruments — garamut, kundu, bamboo, rattles and guitar — by their duration, learning the families of instruments, and performing a traditional dance using the instruments.

## The PNG music industry and song collections
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: Grades 6–7 collect the nation's music: a class collection of known songs — church hymns, popular songs and songs from the PNG music industry — at least one performed to the class, and songs sung from printed notation in church hymnals, with the National Anthem sung in two- and three-part harmony.
