# Papua New Guinea Instruments and Music

## Do it first

Look at the sub lesson list. Pick the part you already know something about, and say what you know. Then start at Sub lesson 1 — every part builds on the one before it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kundu drums and local sound makers

The Grade 3 guide sorts factory-made instruments from home-made ones, then meets the local sound makers: kundu drums, rattles, coconut-shell percussion and shakers — thump-thumping, the simple shaker, the beat cylinder — with melody and pattern played together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Instruments as each community's craft

In Grade 4 the instruments return as crafted objects: musical instruments from different communities, met as things each community makes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hearing PNG's instruments

Grade 5 listens closely: distinguishing the sounds of Papua New Guinea's instruments — garamut, kundu, bamboo, rattles and guitar — by their duration, learning the families of instruments, and performing a traditional dance using the instruments.

## Sub lesson 4 — The PNG music industry and song collections

Grades 6–7 collect the nation's music: a class collection of known songs — church hymns, popular songs and songs from the PNG music industry — at least one performed to the class, and songs sung from printed notation in church hymnals, with the National Anthem sung in two- and three-part harmony.
