# Papua New Guinea Instruments and Music — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kundu drums and local sound makers
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Grade 3 guide sorts factory-made instruments from home-made ones, then meets the local sound makers: kundu drums, rattles, coconut-shell percussion and shakers — thump-thumping, the simple shaker, the beat cylinder — with melody and pattern played together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Instruments as each community's craft
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: In Grade 4 the instruments return as crafted objects: musical instruments from different communities, met as things each community makes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hearing PNG's instruments
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Grade 5 listens closely: distinguishing the sounds of Papua New Guinea's instruments — garamut, kundu, bamboo, rattles and guitar — by their duration, learning the families of instruments, and performing a traditional dance using the instruments.

## Sub lesson 4 — The PNG music industry and song collections
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Grades 6–7 collect the nation's music: a class collection of known songs — church hymns, popular songs and songs from the PNG music industry — at least one performed to the class, and songs sung from printed notation in church hymnals, with the National Anthem sung in two- and three-part harmony.
