# Chinese Traditional Instruments

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about chinese traditional instruments. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — China Grade 3 Music Vol.1

The suona — a Hebei folk tune played by different-sized suonas in turn over a folk band, picturing the lively jujube-harvest scene; the suona is a loud, bright Chinese wind instrument; Purple Bamboo Tune played by a Chinese folk ensemble — a Shanghai-opera tune in Jiangnan style picturing festival joy; memorising its melody and joining in with body percussion; China's bamboo instruments — bamboo board zithers high/middle/low and bamboo tube zithers that form a bamboo orchestra, and a Miao lusheng tune; trying to make a bamboo instrument yourself

## Sub lesson 2 — China Grade 3 Music Vol.2

The hulusi — a Dai and Yi wind instrument with a gourd body, soft and beautiful in tone — heard in Phoenix-Tail Bamboo under the Moonlight, a Dai melody from Xishuangbanna

## Sub lesson 3 — China Grade 4 Music Vol.1

A Chinese folk-ensemble movement from the East China Sea Fishermen's Song suite — telling which sections the dizi flute and the erhu lead, and matching speeds and expressions to the fishermen's scenes; the pipa concerto The Little Sisters of the Grassland — the Mongolian girls Longmei and Yurong guarding the flock in a snowstorm; the pipa is China's pear-shaped, four-string plucked lute of crisp, soft tone and rich technique; Golden Snake Dance — Nie Er's folk-ensemble arrangement of a folk tune, with folk gongs and drums, picturing Dragon Boat Festival races; its last section uses folk call-and-response （一领众和）, and pupils design gong, drum and cymbal parts for it; a zhongruan solo for the Yi people's Torch Festival night — the ruan is a Chinese plucked lute with four strings played with a plectrum, soft and beautiful; Thunder in a Drought — a Cantonese-music folk-ensemble piece (adapted by Yan Laolie from a Cantonese-opera tune) picturing joy when rain ends a long drought

## Sub lesson 4 — China Grade 4 Music Vol.2

A folk-ensemble piece whose flowing flute sound carries you to the water towns of Jiangnan; the bamboo dizi is China's transverse bamboo flute — bangdi bright and crisp, qudi soft and lyrical; Spring on Tianshan, a pipa solo — spring water unfreezing and Uyghur people dancing to the hand drum to welcome spring; Wushu, a folk-ensemble piece in three sections — moving through martial-arts actions with the music and completing its theme

## Sub lesson 5 — China Grade 5 Music (jianpu) Vol.2

Two plucked-lute solos from the suite Sketches of Northern Ethnic Life — "Reindeer" on the yueqin (moon lute) and "Winter Hunt" on the liuqin, both composed by Liu Dehai; the Qiang flute — a transverse bamboo flute of China's southwest minority peoples, high, bright and a little mournful; listening to Dance of the Yao People — a folk-orchestra piece in three parts picturing a Yao festival night of song and dance; keeping its rhythms with stamps, finger-snaps and claps; listening to "Golden Pheasants Leave the Mountain" — a Tujia percussion ensemble piece in the da-liuzi band form (liuzi gongs, head gong, second gong and horse gong), its five sections painting the pheasants' day from dawn to homecoming; listening to the ancient guqin solo "Three Variations on the Plum Blossom" — its theme returns three times; the guqin itself: some 4,000 years old, the instrument of Boya who found his one true listener, and the qin recording of Flowing Water that Voyager carried into space; listening to the guzheng solo "Fishermen's Song at Dusk" — sunset on the lake, sails coming home, fishermen singing; its title is a line of Tang poet Wang Bo

## Sub lesson 6 — China Grade 6 Art Vol.1

Meeting China's ancient instruments — a Warring-States bronze bell set (bianzhong), the clay xun, the ruan, and a painted lacquer drum on a tiger-and-bird stand

## Sub lesson 7 — China Grade 6 Music Vol.1

The erhu — Horse Racing crowds galloping horses into one solo, using the erhu's many techniques to neigh and gallop; the erhu itself, China's two-stringed bowed instrument of long history and rich expression

## Sub lesson 8 — China Grade 6 Music Vol.2

Gada Meilin on the morin khuur — the Mongolian hero's folk song reborn as a solo whose horse-head fiddle tone fills it with the vast grassland; the morin khuur itself, named for the horse's head carved on its scroll

## Sub lesson 9 — China HS Ensemble

The Chinese national orchestra — Peng Xiuwen's Flying Clouds, a Peking-opera aria rebuilt for national orchestra keeping opera's metre system and civil/martial band while gaining symphonic breadth; and the rehearsal of Golden Snakes Dance (Nie Er's arrangement of the dragon-boat tune): percussion introduction, mixed-timbre theme, the plucked section's "strike" against "roll", dynamics swelling to the climax; the unit closes by mapping the orchestra's seating and tabulating the peoples' instruments (Miao, Uyghur, Tibetan, Kazakh, Yi, Mongolian…)
