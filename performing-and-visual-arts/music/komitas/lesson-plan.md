# Lesson plan — Komitas

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Komitas
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Komitas — Armenia's own teaching of this topic, kept as named parts: Who creates music? Komitas — the composer pictured with a bird in his hands; his children's song "Payte dziuk" (The wooden horse) sung and heard in his own "Mankakan nvagner" (Children's pieces)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Who creates music? Komitas** — Who creates music? Komitas — the composer pictured with a bird in his hands; his children's song "Payte dziuk" (The wooden horse) sung and heard in his own "Mankakan nvagner" (Children's pieces)
2. **Komitas on music education (teacher-guide evidence)** — Komitas on music education (teacher-guide evidence) — the guide's introduction quotes Komitas's 1912 Bera Yesayan lecture: music as the balanced education of the child's feelings
3. **A letter from Komitas** — A letter from Komitas — "Hello, little friend, I am Komitas; as a child they called me Soghomon": the collector of Armenia's songs introduces himself (with Artsrunyan's painting "The song-gatherer")
4. **Komitas as the founder of the method (teacher-guide evidence)** — Komitas as the founder of the method (teacher-guide evidence) — Catholicos Vazgen I links Mashtots and Komitas: both gave the nation its letters, one of language, one of song; sad songs are never to be given to children — all must be lively
5. **"Hayr mer" (Հա՜յր մեր, Komitas)** — "Hayr mer" (Հա՜յր մեր, Komitas) — the book opens with Komitas's setting, sung before all the year's themes
6. **A second letter from Komitas** — A second letter from Komitas — composer, choirmaster, teacher, collector of Armenian folk song: his portrait in paint and in words
7. **How does the folk song reach us** — How does the folk song reach us — people sang in their daily life, not for an audience; collectors record them: hear and learn Komitas's noted song "The incense tree" (Խնկի ծառ)
8. **The year's plan in hours (teacher-guide evidence)** — The year's plan in hours (teacher-guide evidence) — Armenian national songs and dances take 16 hours, incl. Komitas's "Hoy Nazan" in choral arrangement and the military songs with the Karno Kochari danced shoulder to shoulder
9. **Komitas's "Hov areq" (Հով արեք)** — Komitas's "Hov areq" (Հով արեք) — hear the folk song first in one voice, in Komitas's own performance, then in many voices
10. **KOMITAS, a full chapter** — KOMITAS, a full chapter — Komitas Vardapet (1869-1935) holds a special place in Armenian folk music: he travelled the settlements, heard and noted songs and tunes on the spot, studied and classed them; his sharakan for the Hripsime virgins hides their names in its first letters
11. **Armenian folk dance-song "Habrban" (Հաբրբան)** — Armenian folk dance-song "Habrban" (Հաբրբան) — arranged by Komitas: the boy's and girl's call-and-answer sung as a little scene ("Sing like an ashugh")
