# Hearing Each Instrument

## Do it first

Close your eyes. Your companion will imitate the sound of an instrument — the boom of a drum, the slide of a bow, the call of a flute. Can you name the instrument by its sound alone, without seeing it? That is this whole lesson: ears first.

## The song that listens

There is a song called **እኛም አለን ሙዚቃ** — *We Too Have Music*. Its verses do a kind thing: each verse sings, and then it LEAVES A SPACE. In the space, one instrument sounds by itself. Your job is to listen in the space and know who is playing.

The instruments take their turns:

- **መሰንቆ (masenqo)** — the bow slides on its one string; the sound is long and singing.
- **ክራር (krar)** — the strings are touched one by one; the sound is short and bright.
- **ከበሮ (kebero)** — boom, boom; the drum under everything.
- **ዋሽንት (washint)** — the flute's high, sweet line in the air.

And when the song comes back in the later book, a fifth friend has joined: **ጥሩንባ (trumpet)** — bright and bold, like a call across a field. Musical activities — song, children's songs and dance — can be accompanied by various instruments, and this song is where you learn to hear them apart.

Listen for the WAY each one sounds, not just its name. A drum cannot slide. A flute cannot boom. Each sound fits only its own instrument.

## Say it to your companion

Sing *We Too Have Music* together — or any song you both know. At each verse's end, one of you imitates an instrument's sound in the space, and the other names it. Five sounds, five names: masenqo, krar, kebero, washint, trumpet.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play the listening game with a friend: eyes closed, name the sound. Play five rounds.
2. Write the five names in order of the sounds you like most: መሰንቆ, ክራር, ከበሮ, ዋሽንት, ጥሩንባ.
3. Next time you hear real music, listen for one instrument at a time and name it to yourself.
