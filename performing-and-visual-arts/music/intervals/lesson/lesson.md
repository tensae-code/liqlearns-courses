# Intervals

## Do it first

Sing one sound. Now sing a higher one. How far did you jump? Was it a small step or a wide leap? Measuring that jump — exactly — is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What an interval is

Albania's Grade 7 music guide teaches **intervals** as its own topic: an interval is **the distance between two sounds**. The distance is counted **from the first sound**: **1–2, 1–3**, and so on, up to **the octave, 1–8** — the widest distance this lesson builds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building, singing and playing intervals

An interval is not only named — it is made. The guide's work is **naming, building, singing and playing simple intervals inside the octave**: hear two sounds, count their distance and name it; be given a name, and build that distance with your voice or on an instrument, and sing or play it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what is an interval, and what is the widest one this lesson builds? Companion checks: a distance between two sounds? The octave, 1–8?

## Do It Yourself

1. Sing two sounds a small distance apart, then two sounds a wide distance apart. Write the count for each.
2. Build the octave from a starting sound, counting every step out loud.
3. Write one sentence: why must an interval have two sounds, not one?
