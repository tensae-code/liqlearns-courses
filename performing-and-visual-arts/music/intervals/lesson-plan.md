# Lesson plan — Intervals

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Intervals
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an interval is the distance between two sounds — how the distances are named and counted, from 1–2 up to the octave 1–8, and how simple intervals are built, sung and played.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of its title — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What an interval is** — the distance between two sounds, counted from the first sound: 1–2, 1–3, and so on up to the octave, 1–8.
2. **Building, singing and playing intervals** — naming, building, singing and playing simple intervals inside the octave.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of the Music title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an interval is: the distance between two sounds.
2. Name simple intervals inside the octave, counted 1–2 up to the octave 1–8.
3. Build, sing and play a simple interval when it is named.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks an interval is a single sound → an interval is a distance: it needs two sounds, a first and the sound counted up from it.
- Counts the octave as something outside the lesson → the octave, 1–8, is the widest interval this lesson builds.
