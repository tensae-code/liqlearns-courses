# Kinds of Music Around the World

## Do it first

Quick game: I name a place, you name a kind of music that comes from there. Jamaica! The USA! If a different kind jumped out for each place — you already know this lesson's way: a kind of music is told by **where it comes from** and **who made it famous**.

This lesson comes from a Cyprus Year 6 teacher unit, "Favourite Music" — its reading table of world music kinds. It is teacher unit material, taught here as evidence.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reggae

**Reggae** comes from **Jamaica**. It began in the **1960s**, and the name the table gives for who made it famous is **Bob Marley**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Jazz

**Jazz** comes from the **USA**. It began in the **early 20th century**, and the name the table gives for who made it famous is **Louis Armstrong**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gospel

**Gospel** is **Christian music** — the music of **churches and concerts**. The table places it in the **USA, Ireland and Britain**.

The same table also has a traditional kind — every country has its own traditional music. That kind is taught in its own lesson, Kinds of Traditional Music.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three kinds, and for each one give its home and its famous name — or, for gospel, where it is sung. Companion checks: Jamaica and Bob Marley for reggae? USA and Louis Armstrong for jazz? Churches and concerts for gospel?

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the table: three rows — reggae, jazz, gospel — with where each comes from and who made it famous.
2. Add a fourth row of your own: a kind of music from your own country, told the same way — where it comes from and who is known for it.
3. Write one sentence: why does knowing where a kind of music comes from help you understand it?
