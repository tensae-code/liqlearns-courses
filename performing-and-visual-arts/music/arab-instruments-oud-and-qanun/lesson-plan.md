# Lesson plan — Arab Instruments: Oud and Qanun

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Arab Instruments: Oud and Qanun
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Arab world’s plucked instruments, above all the oud and the qanun, and how each is built and played — with the nay, the Arab reed flute, stocked from the same placements.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The oud** — the ancient Arab string instrument: its deep bowl body and short bent neck, its five double strings and their tuning names (Yakah, ʿAshiran, Dukah, Nawa, Kirdan), its parts drawn and named, and how it is played with the risha plectrum
2. **The qanun** — a trapezoid sound-box carrying some 78 strings in courses of three, played with picks on both index fingers, its tuning levers raising and lowering each course — the instrument that sets the tuning for the whole Arab ensemble
3. **The nay** — the end-blown reed flute of the Arabs: an open tube of cane with six finger holes and a thumb hole, made in seven named sizes (37.5 to 68 cm), one for each degree of the maqam
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
