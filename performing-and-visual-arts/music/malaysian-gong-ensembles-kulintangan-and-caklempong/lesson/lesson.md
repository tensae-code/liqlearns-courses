# Malaysian Gong Ensembles: Kulintangan and Caklempong

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — Malaysia's gong ensembles — kulintangan and caklempong — their instruments, pieces and playing together. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Minangkabau caklempong of Negeri Sembilan

Unit 2 Apresiasi: Caklempong — Minangkabau caklempong of Negeri Sembilan: gereteh (15 bonang) plays the main melody, tingkah and sauwa (8 bonang) play ostinato, gendang ibu/anak the beat, with serunai, bangsi and salong winds; played at weddings and feasts; Units 1–2 — recognising and playing the instruments of the caklempong ensemble from scores, with a musician's discipline, and performing folk songs in the ensemble.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kulintangan, the gong-chime ensemble of Sabah's KadazanDusun, Bajau

Unit 5 Kulintangan — kulintangan, the gong-chime ensemble of Sabah's KadazanDusun, Bajau and Murut peoples: its knobbed gongs in a row and its place in celebration; Unit 6 Ensembel Kulintang — playing in a kulintang ensemble (filed under the Book 10 lesson slug music/kulintangan).

## Sub lesson 3 — Performing named caklempong pieces, Anak Burung Baniong and Cantik

Units 1–2 Caklempong — performing named caklempong pieces, Anak Burung Baniong and Cantik Manis, with the ensemble musician's discipline (filed under the Book 4 lesson slug music/playing-in-a-caklempong-ensemble).

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Malaysia's own way of teaching its arts?
