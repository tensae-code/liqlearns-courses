# Amhara Music History

## Do it first

Every region's music has a family tree of people. The Amhara book names them: pairs who sang from place to place, azmari who made the tej houses ring, groups that kept whole towns singing. This lesson learns their names — because a history without names is only a rumour.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Lalibelawoch and the azmari trade

The Grade 7 book opens its Amhara music history (§3.1, printed p. 77) with the **Lalibelawoch**: **mostly male–female pairs who tie the craft to faith; the craft is hereditary, and they move place to place**. Beside them stands the **azmari**, the wandering singer-musician of the tej houses, weddings and theatres — improvising praise and gentle mockery on masenqo and voice. The book names azmari: **Asefa Abate, Bahru Kanie, Derbaba Abun and Alemayehu Fanta** — names carried into the next lessons of this cluster.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bands, theatres and the music's widening road

The book's history walks the music from the tej house onto the **stage**: azmari and singers entered **bands and theatre houses**, where the region's melodies met new instruments and paying audiences, without leaving the old craft behind. The lives in Musicians of the Amhara Region — next door — each walk some part of this road: theatre first employments, private bands, national stages. History here is not dates alone; it is **venues changing, and the music surviving every change**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 7 chapter opens — history and culture (chapter opening — Grade 7)

The Grade 7 book opens its third chapter, *History and Culture*, by placing the arts inside time. Its aims:

- **the cultural and historical context of art** — no artwork stands outside its people's story;
- **regional musicians' histories** — this lesson's ground;
- **Saint Yared**;
- **indigenous handcraft, paintings and ancient writings**;
- **expressing indigenous knowledge in theatre and film**.

This lesson and its history neighbours keep them: the musicians here, Yared in his own lesson, the painters in theirs, and the stage-and-screen telling in the drama lessons.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Grade 7 chapter closes — check, summary and exercise (chapter closing — Grade 7)

The Grade 7 book closes its history chapter with a **self-check table (6 items)**, a **summary** and a **closing exercise of 7 true/false and 7 short answers**. The check ranges over the chapter the aims opened: art in its cultural and historical context, the regional musicians, Saint Yared, indigenous handcraft, paintings and ancient writings, and indigenous knowledge told on stage and screen. Mark each item ✓ or ✗, and re-study a cross in the lesson that owns it — the musicians here, Yared and the painters in theirs.

The chapter also set two practice tasks before its close, and they belong to this cluster's work: **Task-1** (Yared's works and success; Beshah Tekle Mariam's contributions; a piece on Prof. Ashenafi Kebede) sits in the Yared and musicians lessons, and **Task-2** (the great painters — are they the only ones? where do their ideas come from? your own vision) sits in the painters lesson. The exercise's fourteen written items sit in this part's questions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion who the Lalibelawoch are and name two azmari; let them tell you the road to the theatre.
