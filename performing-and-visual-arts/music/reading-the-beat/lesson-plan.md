# Lesson plan — Reading the Beat

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Reading the Beat
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sight-reading rhythm: the beat as a movement-act done with time — read by letters under notes, by off-staff melodies, and by sol-fa rows, always with a beat instrument keeping time.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Notes and Rests and Scales, Pitch and Key Signatures — the learner reads notes on the staff; here reading steps OFF the staff and runs on letters, syllables and the beat alone.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the beat is** — §2.1.1: the beat is a movement-act done with time; reading the beat is music's most fundamental thing — heartbeat, atamo and kebero at festivals and weddings.
2. **Reading notes by their letters** — Exercise 2.1 part 1: read note rows keeping time values, calling the letters written under the notes.
3. **Off-staff melodies** — Exercise 2.1 part 2: melodies read off the staff by their letters, played with beat instruments — and never memorised by their words.
4. **Sol-fa rows** — Exercise 2.1 part 3: read half-note sounds naming do re mi fa sol la, with a beat instrument; then substitute your own letters.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Notes and Rests; Reading Music on the Staff. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the beat as the book defines it and name its festival examples.
2. Read note rows by the letters under the notes, keeping time values.
3. Read an off-staff melody without memorising it by its words.
4. Read sol-fa rows (do re mi fa sol la) with a beat instrument.

## What you need
A beat instrument — atamo, kebero or clapped hands — and the book's letter rows copied on paper or board.

## If the learner is stuck
If reading stalls, shrink the row to four sounds and slow the beat — accuracy at a slow beat beats speed with stops.
