# Reading the Beat — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.


**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The beat, in the book's definition, is…
  a) A movement-act done with time b) A printed line on the staff c) The loudest sound in a song
**Answer: a) A movement-act done with time** — §2.1.1 — movement, done with time.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Which keeps the beat at festivals and weddings?
  a) The atamo and the kebero b) Only the singer's memory c) The printed programme
**Answer: a) The atamo and the kebero** — the book's festival beat-keepers, with the heartbeat as the body's own.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** In Exercise 2.1 part 1, the reader calls aloud…
  a) The letters written under the notes b) The page numbers c) Only the first note
**Answer: a) The letters written under the notes** — letters under the notes carry the pitch order.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** While reading the row, what must be kept?
  a) Each note's time value b) Complete silence c) The same pitch throughout
**Answer: a) Each note's time value** — the note shapes carry the time; the letters carry the order.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Off-staff reading gives the melody by…
  a) Its letters alone b) A full staff with clefs c) The teacher singing first
**Answer: a) Its letters alone** — part 2 removes the staff; the letters carry the melody.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** The book's warning for this form is…
  a) Do not memorise the melody by its words b) Always memorise the words first c) Never use instruments
**Answer: a) Do not memorise the melody by its words** — read the letters as music, or the skill is only memory.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** The sol-fa row is read in which note value?
  a) Half notes b) Whole notes only c) Quarter rests
**Answer: a) Half notes** — part 3 reads half-note sounds on the next clef.

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** After the printed row, the reader…
  a) Substitutes their own letters and reads on b) Stops reading for the day c) Erases the beat instrument
**Answer: a) Substitutes their own letters and reads on** — own letters, same reading — the skill transfers.

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** In Grade 6 Chapter 2's pre-talk, a beat is first shown by…
  a) Clapping and kebero strokes b) Reading a poster c) Drawing a staff
**Answer: a) Clapping and kebero strokes** — the pre-talk's own examples — show a kebero beat you know.

**10. (Sub lesson 5)** Beats in this chapter are read where?
  a) Written outside the staff, with varied time signs b) Only inside songs' words c) Nowhere — beats are never written
**Answer: a) Written outside the staff, with varied time signs** — read beats written outside the staff — this lesson's own ground.

**11. (Sub lesson 6)** The book's examples for high and low pitch are…
  a) Women's thin voices (high, G clef) and men's thick voices (low, F clef) b) Children's voices for both c) Instruments only, never voices
**Answer: a) Women's thin voices (high, G clef) and men's thick voices (low, F clef)** — the summary's own examples.

**12. (Sub lesson 6)** The Chapter 2 closing exercise's performance tasks ask learners to…
  a) Play the numbered rhythmic sight-readings with a beat instrument, and sing the three staff melodies alone and in groups b) Only copy definitions c) Draw the staff
**Answer: a) Play the numbered rhythmic sight-readings with a beat instrument, and sing the three staff melodies alone and in groups** — the exercise's own performance half.
