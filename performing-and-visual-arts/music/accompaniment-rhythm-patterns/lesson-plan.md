# Lesson plan — Accompaniment Rhythm Patterns

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Accompaniment Rhythm Patterns
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The characteristic rhythm patterns (tiết điệu) that accompany songs and dances, and how to find the right pattern for a piece in a given metre.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Vietnam Grade 10 Chuyên đề học tập Âm nhạc 10** — The rhythm pattern (tiết điệu) is the characteristic rhythm (tiết tấu) of the dances; the accompaniment rhythm pattern (tiết điệu đệm) is performing the harmony following the rhythm of the dances, used to support and accompany the melody of a music work such as a song or a wordless piece, and the accompaniment rhythm pattern has the role of completing the artistic image and the content of the music work; to form an accompaniment rhythm pattern for a song or piece, three basic elements are needed — rhythm, metre type and chord: rhythm is the relation of duration of the sounds following each other, the sounds connecting and interacting with each other creating the sound shapes (âm hình) and the different rhythm-shape groups, contributing to creating an accompaniment rhythm pattern with characteristic rhythm shapes or rhythm groups, and the rhythm element helps determine an accompaniment rhythm pattern with characteristic rhythm shapes or rhythm groups; the metre type (loại nhịp) is the showing of the beats of the metre by a certain duration — for example 2/4 metre is the metre type with two beats, each beat equal to a quarter note (nốt đen), and 6/8 metre is the metre type with six beats, each beat equal to an eighth note (nốt móc đơn) — and the metre-type element helps the determining and choosing of the rhythm pattern fitting the song or piece; besides the elements above, the accompaniment rhythm pattern must also fit the characteristics of the thematic material of the music work as well as the mode characteristics of the song or piece; some common accompaniment rhythm patterns: the Polka pattern — the characteristic rhythm of the Polka pattern is the off-beat (nghịch phách) sound shapes belonging to 2/4 metre, carrying a fresh, cheerful, bouncy (nảy gọn) character; the March pattern — March is the march rhythm pattern (the marching rhythm, nhịp đi) carrying a healthy character, the rhythm of March usually being the neat, clipped sound shapes in 2/4 or 4/4 metre, and the March pattern fits accompanying the songs carrying a solemn, majestic (hùng tráng) character; the Waltz pattern — the Waltz is a dance in triple metre (3/4 or 3/8) popular in many countries in the world, and the rhythm of the Waltz pattern is graceful (uyển chuyển), lilting (nhịp nhàng) and elegant (duyên dáng) with a slightly fast tempo; the Boston pattern — the Boston pattern is like the Waltz dance but has a slower tempo, so it is also called the slow Waltz or Waltz Boston; Boston is of 3/4 metre, and its characteristic rhythm is usually the eighth-note spread-chord (rải) sound shapes, and the Boston pattern fits very well the soft, deep (sâu lắng) lyrical songs and pieces; the Ballad pattern — the Ballad is a music genre rich in lyrical, romantic, gentle character, and the Ballad pattern usually has a slow tempo with the vertical-column chord (hợp âm cột dọc) sound shapes and the spread chords belonging to 2/4 or 4/4 metre; the Slow Rock pattern — its most characteristic sound shapes are the spread-chord sound shapes in 6/8 metre, sometimes 4/4 metre; and the Disco pattern — the Disco pattern has the vertical-column chord sound shapes with a slightly fast, flexible rhythm, belonging to 2/4 or 4/4 metre; the listening example is V. Monti's Czardas; determining the tempo of the song or piece — the songs and pieces in 2/4 metre are very common, and usually 2/4 metre has three tempo kinds: slow, moderate and fast; for a song with a slow or moderate tempo, the Ballad pattern should be chosen (Trọng Đài's song "Hà Nội đêm trở gió", Hanoi on a Windy Night, words from Chu Lai's poem); and determining the musical character — the musical character of the song or piece is closely related to the determining of the accompaniment rhythm pattern, and with the songs and pieces belonging to 2/4 metre, besides determining the tempo, one needs to base on the musical character of each specific work to choose and determine the most fitting accompaniment rhythm pattern: for a song or piece with a fresh, cheerful, bright musical character, choose the Polka accompaniment pattern (Hoàng Hoà's song "Thanh niên làm theo lời Bác", The Youth Follow Uncle Hồ's Words); for a song or piece with a lyrical musical character in 2/4 metre, the Slow Rock pattern should be chosen (Nguyễn Đức Toàn's song "Tình em biển cả", Your Love, the Vast Sea); and for a song with a flexible rhythm and a beat close to the marching beat, one can choose the March pattern or combine the two patterns Polka and March (Huy Thục's song "Bác đang cùng chúng cháu hành quân", Uncle Is Marching with Us, and Trịnh Công Sơn's "Nối vòng tay lớn"); the practice piece is Hoàng Hiệp's "Trở về dòng sông tuổi thơ" (Returning to the River of Childhood); the songs and pieces in 3/4 metre with a slow tempo — slow and leisurely (chậm rãi, thong thả), with a soft, lyrical musical character — should choose the Boston pattern (Hồ Bắc's song "Làng tôi", My Village); and the songs and pieces in 3/4 metre with a moderate and fast tempo should choose the Waltz pattern; the listening excerpts are H. Mancini's "Moon River" (words J. Mercer) and the English folk song "We Wish You a Merry Christmas", and the examples are Trịnh Công Sơn's song "Để gió cuốn đi" (Let the Wind Carry It Away) and Patty Hill and Mildred J. Hill's "Happy Birthday"; the practice piece is N. Mouskouri's "Over and Over", whose musical character is determined and whose chosen pattern is then used to accompany it on the guitar; the songs and pieces written in 4/4 metre with a slow and moderate tempo and a lyrical, gentle musical character are usually close to the Ballad and Slow Rock patterns (the listening excerpt is Paul de Senneville's "A Comme Amour", and the examples are Yến Dung's song "Ngày xưa ơi", Oh Days of Old, and Trần Long Ẩn's "Một đời người một rừng cây", One Human Life, One Forest of Trees); a song or piece with a moderate tempo and a solemn musical character, following the marching beat, can choose to be accompanied with the March pattern (Diệp Minh Tuyền's song "Hát mãi khúc quân hành", Singing Forever the Marching Song); and the songs and pieces in 4/4 metre with a fast tempo and a lively, flexible musical character are usually accompanied with the Disco pattern (Hoàng Hà's song "Đất nước trọn niềm vui", The Country in Complete Joy); the practice piece is Đỗ Minh's "Chào mừng Đảng Cộng sản Việt Nam" (Welcoming the Communist Party of Vietnam), whose chosen pattern is used to accompany it on the guitar; the songs and pieces in 6/8 metre with a slow and moderate tempo, with a graceful, flowing rhythm (tiết tấu uyển chuyển, dàn trải) and a soft, lyrical melody, are usually accompanied with the Slow Rock pattern (the listening song is Trương Quý Hải's "Hà Nội mùa vắng những cơn mưa", Hanoi in the Season Without Rains, words from Bùi Thanh Tuấn's poem, marked Slow rock, and the example is Trịnh Công Sơn's "Nhớ mùa thu Hà Nội", Remembering Hanoi's Autumn); and the songs and pieces in 6/8 metre with a fast rhythm and tempo, fresh and lively, are usually accompanied with the Waltz pattern (Văn Cao's song "Mùa xuân đầu tiên", The First Spring); the practice piece is Thế Song's "Nơi đảo xa" (At the Far Island), for which the musical character is analysed, the chords placed and the accompaniment rhythm pattern determined
2. **Vietnam Grade 10 Âm nhạc 10** — The Waltz rhythm pattern usually accompanies songs written in 3/4 or 3/8 metre (its basic sound pattern shown for both); the main triads of G major pressed in first position (the G, C and D chord diagrams with their finger placements and the open-string and not-played signs), and the right hand's way for each chord — finger p plays the chord's bass string on beat 1 and fingers i+m+a play strings 3+2+1 together on beats 2 and 3; the pattern practised across the three chords and applied to accompany the song "Đếm sao" (Counting Stars, excerpt) by Văn Chung, with the choice of adding body movements or percussion with the guitar; the Fox rhythm pattern accompanies songs of a cheerful, lively character, usually written in 2/4 metre (its basic sound pattern shown); the main triads and the dominant seventh chord of E minor pressed in first position (the Em, Am and B7 chord diagrams), and the right hand's way for each chord — finger p plays the bass string on beat 1, fingers i+m+a play strings 3+2+1 together in the second half of beat 1, finger p plays the second bass string on beat 2 and i+m+a the three strings in its second half; the pattern practised across the chords and applied to accompany "Cachiusa" (Katyusha), music by M. I. Blanter, Vietnamese words by Phạm Tuyên
3. **Vietnam Grade 11 Âm nhạc 11** — The Slow tune (điệu Slow) is usually used to accompany the songs with a gentle, slow character, written in 6/8 metre and 4/4 metre; for the guitar, the Slow tune has many different showing ways, and the most characteristic sound figure is the spread-chord (rải hợp âm) sound figures in the 6/8 and 4/4 types; and the basic sound figure of the Slow rhythm in 6/8 metre, and the basic sound figure of the Slow rhythm in 4/4 metre; and the practice: a) 6/8 metre: the song "Màu hoa đỏ" (The Red Flower Colour) — the way to press some chords: to accompany the song "Màu hoa đỏ" in the A minor key (giọng La thứ), one can choose six chords: C major (Đô trưởng (C)); E major (Mi trưởng (E)); F major (Pha trưởng (F)); D minor (Rê thứ (Dm)); E minor (Mi thứ (Em)); A minor (La thứ (Am)) (the chord diagrams); the right-hand plucking way (cách gảy tay phải): for the chords F, E and Em — finger p plucks string 6 (beat 1), finger i plucks string 3 (beat 2), finger m plucks string 2 (beat 3), finger a plucks string 1 (beat 4), finger m plucks string 2 (beat 5), finger i plucks string 3 (beat 6); for the chord Dm — finger p plucks string 4 (beat 1), finger i plucks string 3 (beat 2), finger m plucks string 2 (beat 3), finger a plucks string 1 (beat 4), finger m plucks string 2 (beat 5), finger i plucks string 3 (beat 6); for the chords C and Am — finger p plucks string 5 (beat 1), finger i plucks string 3 (beat 2), finger m plucks string 2 (beat 3), finger a plucks string 1 (beat 4), finger m plucks string 2 (beat 5), finger i plucks string 3 (beat 6); practise accompanying the Slow rhythm in 6/8 metre (the p i m a m i patterns); and accompany the song: "Màu hoa đỏ", music and words Thuận Yến (with the chord signs Am, C, Dm, F, G, E, Em); b) 4/4 metre: the song "Người thầy" (The Teacher) — the way to press the chords on the music: to accompany the song "Người thầy" in the A minor key, one can choose the chords: A minor (La thứ (Am)), D minor (Rê thứ (Dm)), E minor (Mi thứ (Em)), F major (Pha trưởng (F)), C major (Đô trưởng (C)), G major (Son trưởng (G)), E major (Mi trưởng (E)), A seventh (La bảy (A7)), B seventh (Si bảy (B7)), D seventh (Rê bảy (D7)) (the chord diagrams); the right-hand plucking way: for the chords Am and C; A7; B7 — finger p plucks string 5 (beat 1), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 1), fingers m+a pluck strings 2+1 (beat 2), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 2), finger p plucks string 5 (beat 3), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 3), fingers m+a pluck strings 2+1 (beat 4), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 4); for the chords Dm and D7 — finger p plucks string 4 (beat 1), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 1), fingers m+a pluck strings 2+1 (beat 2), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 2), finger p plucks string 4 (beat 3), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 3), fingers m+a pluck strings 2+1 (beat 4), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 4); for the chords F and G; E; Em — finger p plucks string 6 (beat 1), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 1), fingers m+a pluck strings 2+1 (beat 2), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 2), finger p plucks string 6 (beat 3), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 3), fingers m+a pluck strings 2+1 (beat 4), finger i plucks string 3 (the second half of beat 4); practise accompanying the Slow rhythm in 4/4 metre; and accompany the song: "Người thầy" (The Teacher), music and words Nguyễn Nhất Huy, with the performance guidance: practise carefully each music sentence at a slow tempo, ensuring when changing the accompaniment chords there is no detachment (không bị rời tiếng); practise changing the chords from A minor (Am) over to F major (F), needing to keep the common notes; and accompany at the correct tempo and evenly, pressing into the bar head to support the singing part better; 4. **Vietnam Grade 12 Music** — Practising accompanying the Ballad rhythm (tiết điệu Ballad) — the Ballad rhythm has a gentle, lyrical character fitting slow melodies in 4/4, with its basic sound figure shown; how to press the chords (diagrams for C, D, E, G, A7, E7, Am, Dm, Em, Am7); the right-hand plucking way by chord family (for C, Am, Am7, A7 finger p plucks string 5 on beat 1, then i–m–i–a–i–m–i across strings 3, 2, 3, 1, 3, 2, 3 through the bar; for D, Dm the thumb starts on string 4; for G, Em, E, E7 on string 6); combining string-hooking plucking with strumming (the down-stroke plucking many strings from above, the up-stroke from below, and the chord-plucking rhythm figure); practising accompanying Xuân Phương's "Mong ước kỉ niệm xưa" and "Tình thơ" (guidance: strong emphasis on beat 1 and slightly strong on beat 3, the first down-stroke full of the chord's sounds, later down-strokes allowed to drop one or two low strings, up-strokes on two or three high strings); changing left-hand chords while keeping their common sounds (G to Em keeping the Si note at string 5 fret 2; C to Em keeping the Mi note at string 4 fret 2); applying the skill to set chords and accompany Trần Tiến's "Mùa xuân gọi"
