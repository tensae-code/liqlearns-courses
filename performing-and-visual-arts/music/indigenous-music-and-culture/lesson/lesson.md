# Indigenous Music and Culture

## Do it first

Take the music out of a wedding in your mind. The dancing stops. The praise stops. The feast feels flat. Now put the music back. Indigenous music is not decoration on culture — it is one of the beams the culture stands on. The guide teaches exactly that joining.

## Sub lesson 1 — Music inside its occasions (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 7 teacher guide places **indigenous music inside its culture (teacher guide — evidence)**: the songs belong to **occasions — birth, wedding, worship, work and festival** — and to the **dress, dance and values** that share those occasions. A wedding song out of its wedding is a specimen; in its wedding it is a working part of the people's life. The guide's teaching keeps specimen and life apart — and always chooses the life.

## Sub lesson 2 — How music and culture hold each other (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's joining runs both ways (**teacher guide — evidence**): **culture gives music its occasions and meanings** — which song, for which feast, praising which value — and **music gives culture its memory and feeling**, carrying names, histories and praises that no list could hold. Where this guide's later units stand on contents and syllabus only, they are **EVIDENCE-LIMITED** and labelled so. Hold both directions and you hold the lesson: neither the song nor the culture stands alone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two directions of the joining — culture to music, music to culture.
