This lesson is Guitar and Ukulele.

The guitar and the ukulele — what each is, how it sounds, and its place among the instruments. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: The guitar (đàn guitar) and the ukulele — the guitar is an instrument widely popular in life, which arose many centuries ago and which the Spanish later improved into the kinds of guitar of today; the wooden guitar (guitar gỗ, the acoustic guitar) has six strings and a resonating box made of wood, with a resonant, deep and warm sound fitting the classical and chamber music styles; when playing, whether sitting or standing, the player usually holds the guitar across the lap horizontally, the left hand pressing the strings at the frets and the right hand plucking the strings with the fingertips; the electric guitar (guitar điện) is fitted with an amplifier and is often used in electronic bands, the player using a plastic pick to strike the strings, and it can show different emotional shades; the ukulele appeared in Hawaii at about the 19th century, became widespread in America at the beginning of the 20th century, and from there spread over the world; the ukulele's shape and material are like a wooden guitar's but smaller, and it usually has four strings; the player may sit or stand, one hand pressing the keys and one hand plucking the strings; the ukulele's sound is clear and bright, and combined with the techniques of strumming (gảy) and slapping (vỗ) it is very effective at making a bustling, fresh and lively colour.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
