# Lesson plan — Guitar and Ukulele

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Guitar and Ukulele
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The guitar and the ukulele — what each is, how it sounds, and its place among the instruments.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Vietnam Grade 8 Âm nhạc 8** — The guitar (đàn guitar) and the ukulele — the guitar is an instrument widely popular in life, which arose many centuries ago and which the Spanish later improved into the kinds of guitar of today; the wooden guitar (guitar gỗ, the acoustic guitar) has six strings and a resonating box made of wood, with a resonant, deep and warm sound fitting the classical and chamber music styles; when playing, whether sitting or standing, the player usually holds the guitar across the lap horizontally, the left hand pressing the strings at the frets and the right hand plucking the strings with the fingertips; the electric guitar (guitar điện) is fitted with an amplifier and is often used in electronic bands, the player using a plastic pick to strike the strings, and it can show different emotional shades; the ukulele appeared in Hawaii at about the 19th century, became widespread in America at the beginning of the 20th century, and from there spread over the world; the ukulele's shape and material are like a wooden guitar's but smaller, and it usually has four strings; the player may sit or stand, one hand pressing the keys and one hand plucking the strings; the ukulele's sound is clear and bright, and combined with the techniques of strumming (gảy) and slapping (vỗ) it is very effective at making a bustling, fresh and lively colour
2. **Vietnam Grade 10 Âm nhạc 10** — Theme 2 Instruments — melody practice on the melody instruments, chord practice connecting the D, G and C chords on the ukulele by a rhythm pattern, and ensemble practice of an excerpt of "Ơi cuộc sống mến thương" with recorder, melodica, ukulele and percussion parts under the C–G–D–G chords, body movements used to accompany the ensemble; its structure in nine named parts (headstock, tuning keys, nut, neck, frets, body, sound hole, strings, saddle); the order of the open strings (string 1 Mi, the thinnest; 2 Si; 3 Son; 4 Rê; 5 La; 6 Mi, the thickest) and the basic notes of the first hand position across the strings; and the sitting postures for playing — the classical posture (left foot on a footstool, the guitar held at four support points) and the accompaniment-singing posture (right foot crossed over the left foot, the guitar held at three support points); the finger-sign convention (left hand index 1, middle 2, ring 3, little 4; right hand thumb p, index i, middle m, ring a; open-string notes signed 0); the right hand cupped round, hooking the strings with thumb, index, middle and ring fingers, the wrist straight and the strumming force from the finger joints; the left hand cupped round, the thumb behind the neck, the fingers pressing perpendicular to the frets inside the fret; the first position (fingers 1–4 pressing in turn at frets 1–4 on all six strings) and the notes on strings 5 and 4, with practice exercises; tuning takes open string 1 (Mi) as the standard — fret 5 of string 2 equals open string 1 (turning the key clockwise to lower a string that is too high, counterclockwise to raise one too low), fret 4 of string 3 equals open string 2, and fret 5 of strings 4, 5 and 6 equals the open string above each; the guitar, easily affected by climate and weather, is kept in a dry, airy place away from strong impacts, at suitable temperature and humidity (50%–70%), and cleaned before and after use; then the first-position notes on strings 3 and 2, practised with exercises; the sign p uses a moderate, light strumming force of the right hand; the sign f a stronger strumming force from the right hand's finger joints together with a stronger pressing force of the left hand's fingers; then the first-position notes on strings 6 and 1, and a first-position practice combining the note positions on all six strings through "Happy Birthday" (Patty Hill & Mildred J. Hill); for notes on two neighbouring strings (1 and 2, or 2 and 3), strum fingers i and m simultaneously; for notes on two strings not beside each other, strum finger p simultaneously with one of the remaining fingers (i, m, a); practised in exercises and in a practice built on melody material from Văn Cao's song "Ngày mùa" (Harvest Day)
3. **Vietnam Grade 11 Chuyên đề học tập Âm nhạc 11** — Diệp Minh Tuyền's "Hát mãi khúc quân hành" in Quang Tùng – Xuân Cung's transcription for guitar solo (two sections of two phrases each) — the first phrase of section 1 uses the detached-sound technique (rời tiếng), the second phrase the connected-sound technique (liền tiếng), with emphasis on each bar's beat head and attention to the rests; section 2 uses a dotted rhythm pattern whose tempo must be kept exactly; the performance state is serious, the tempo moderate, the guitar sound full, strong and firm, the player concentrated and confident on stage (the lesson opens by watching Ana Vidovic's guitar solo concert excerpts)
4. **Vietnam Grade 11 Âm nhạc 11** — Pressing, plucking legato (legato) is an important technique when performing the guitar (đàn guitar); this technique creates even, continuous sound (liền mạch), not being cut off (không bị ngắt âm), soft and natural; and the practice of pressing, plucking legato: Practice exercise No.1 (with the finger signs p, m, i); and Practice exercise No.2: "Greensleeves" (excerpt of the melody part (trích phần giai điệu)), English folk song (Dân ca: Anh), arranged for guitar (Chuyển soạn cho đàn guitar): Quang Tùng – Xuân Cung, with the performance guidance: keep the pressing fingers until the end of the bar, move the hand when moving over to a new bar; and in the piece there is the note F sharp (Pha thăng) (f♯1) pressed at string four, fret four, and the note G sharp (Son thăng) (g♯1) pressed at string three, fret one; and the application: choose the nuances and the pressing fingers, plucking fingers fittingly to practise the music passage of "Greensleeves" (excerpt of the melody part); the technique of pressing, plucking detached (non-legato) is a technique usually used on the guitar; this technique creates the difference compared with the technique of pressing, plucking legato (legato); and the practice: Practice exercise No.1 (note: in the piece there is the note B flat (Si giáng) (b♭1) fixed pressed at string three, fret three); and Practice exercise No.2: "Tiếng chày trên sóc Bom Bo" (The Sound of the Pestle in Bom Bo Village) (excerpt), author: Xuân Hồng, arranged for guitar: Quang Tùng – Xuân Cung, with the performance guidance: pluck detached (gảy rời tiếng) the single notes after which there is a rest sign, and use the pressing finger or the plucking finger to make the detachment (làm rời tiếng); and the note F sharp (f♯) is pressed at string six, fret two, the note F sharp (f♯1) is pressed at string four, fret four, and the note F sharp (f♯2) is pressed at string one, fret two; and the application: apply the knowledge already learned to show the practice piece "Con kênh xanh xanh" (The Green Canal), music and words Ngô Huỳnh, arranged for guitar: Quang Tùng – Xuân Cung; the technique of plucking with two fingers on one string is using finger i and finger m to pluck alternately continuously (watch and listen to the video of the piece "Asturias" – I. Albeniz, and point out the difference of sound between the technique of plucking with two fingers on two strings and the technique of plucking with two fingers on one string); and the practice: Practice exercise No.1; and Practice exercise No.2: "Làng tôi" (My Village) (excerpt), music and words Văn Cao, arranged for guitar: Lê Đức Sơn, with the performance guidance: make the melody part (bè giai điệu) stand out clearly by the way of emphasising into finger p, and the plucking force between the remaining fingers must be even; and the application: apply the knowledge already learned, improvise (ứng tác) adding into part two of the music sentence already given, combining the technique of plucking with two fingers on one string to show that music sentence; this is a commonly seen technique of the guitar; the technique uses finger number 1 to press from two to six guitar strings on the same fret (cùng một phím); and when performing the barring technique, finger number 1 needs to be straight, the pressing force stronger than normal; and the practice: Practice exercise No.1; and Practice exercise No.2: an excerpt of the finger exercise in the B minor key (giọng Si thứ) – F. Sor, with the performance guidance: the second bar's two Si notes in part two are tied sounds (âm nối) so do not pluck again; and in the piece there is the note E sharp (Mi thăng) (e♯2) pressed at string one, fret one and the note Si (b1) pressed at string three, fret four, the note C sharp (Đô thăng) (c♯2) pressed at string two, fret two, and the note A sharp (La thăng) (a♯1) pressed at string three, fret three; and the application: based on the knowledge already learned, apply playing the sound stacks (chồng âm)
5. **Vietnam Grade 12 Music** — Practising guitar solo works (độc tấu) — the left-hand legato (hammer-on) technique on guitar (Step 1: the first note is plucked by the right hand like a normal note; Step 2: the next note is sounded by the left-hand finger pressing strongly into the fret, absolutely not using the right hand, so the sound rings without plucking), introduced by watching guitar works built on Vietnamese folk music material ("Inh lả ơi", "Xe chỉ luồn kim" by Tạ Tấn) + Solo practice piece No.1 "Trống cơm" (a Northern folk song), arranged for guitar by Quang Tùng + Solo practice piece No.2 "Fandanguillo" (excerpt), a Spanish folk dance, arranged for guitar by Tạ Tấn (with the barré at bar 12 — finger 1 pressing five strings at fret 2, the finger straight and the force stronger than normal — and the lower part notes at bars 2, 4, 6, 8 plucked strongly to make the melody clear)
