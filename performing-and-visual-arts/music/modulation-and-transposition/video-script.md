This lesson is Modulation and Transposition.

Changing key inside a piece (modulation) and moving a piece to a new key (transposition). — as China's school books teach it: How keys stand to each other and how music moves between them — near keys (one signature apart, or sharing a tonic's family: every major or minor key has six) against distant keys; modulation, the move from one key or mode to another, in its near forms (parallel major–minor, same-gong changes) and far forms (same tonic new mode, same mode new tonic, wholly new tonic and mode), with Schubert's Linden Tree and Mozart's Children's Piano Piece as the worked journeys; tonicisation, the brief borrowing of another key inside a phrase that never settles, as in Liu Jie's My Motherland; and transposition proper — moving a whole piece by interval with a new signature, moving it by changing the clef alone while the notes stay put, and writing for transposing instruments, whose written note is not their sounding note (the B-flat horn, the F horn, the E-flat saxophone).

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
