**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Armenian Sacred Music and Sharakan Hymns

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Armenian medieval music** — Armenian medieval music: the first spiritual songs; the authors of the sharakans — Armenia's own hymn tradition and its named hymn-writers
2. **Theme 1** — Theme 1: The sources of Armenian sacred music — SHARAKANS, TAGHS, GANDZES: the syllabus table sets Komitas Aghtsetsi's arrangement, Grigor Narekatsi's hymns, Khachatur Taronatsi, Nerses Shnorhali's "Aravot luso", Makar Yekmalyan and Komitas's liturgy settings side by side
3. **The development of Armenian music, 8th-15th centuries** — The development of Armenian music, 8th-15th centuries — formed in the 4th-5th centuries with Christianity's adoption (301) and the alphabet (405): the first hymn-singers (կցուրդներ) arose in the 5th century, their beginnings tied to Mesrop Mashtots, Sahak Partev, Movses Khorenatsi and Hovhannes Mandakuni; Sahak A Partev (348-439)
4. **The hymn-writers** — The hymn-writers — in the 7th-8th centuries new sharakan collections: Komitas A Aghtsetsi, Anania Shirakatsi (610-685, who wrote sharakans for the feasts), Sahak G Dzoroporetsi, Stepanos Syunetsi; the sharakan is sung to this day
5. **Chapter 1** — Chapter 1: ARMENIAN SACRED MUSIC (Հայ հոգևոր երաժշտություն) (teacher-guide evidence) — the grade-10 course opens with Armenia's own sacred music before turning to Europe
