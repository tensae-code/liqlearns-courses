# Lesson plan — Dominican Folk Music and Instruments

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Dominican Folk Music and Instruments
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Dominican folk music and its instruments — the güira, tambora and acordeón that carry merengue, bachata, mangulina and perico ripiao; the inventory of the island's folk rhythms; and playing those rhythms until they are not lost.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Dominican folk instruments** — the principal Dominican instruments — güira, tambora and acordeón — used in bachata, merengue, mangulina and Perico Ripiao; researching the instruments of Dominican rhythms and the Dominican percussionists (Guarionex Aquino, David Almengod, Fellé Vega, Isidro Bobadilla, Dante Cucurullo) with their experimental instruments, and fabricating Dominican instruments to play on film.
2. **The folk rhythms and their inventory** — folk music defined — traditional, passed generation to generation orally, no known authors, born of the island's Taíno, Spanish and African mix — and an inventory and classification of Dominican folk rhythms: merengue, mangulina, carabiné, palos, yuca, bambulá, bachata, perico ripiao, pambiche, atabales; Dominican popular and folk music as cultural ancestry, its regional traditions recognised and valued.
3. **Playing the rhythms** — researching how the güira and tambora are played in traditional merengue and practising their patterns until the rhythm is not lost; merengue's rhythmic patterns played with two pencil-shaped sticks (palillos); assembling a percussion set for bachata, learning the bongó and güira patterns from reference videos.
4. **Merengue as composition** — Dominican merengue as musical composition: the experts' lists of the best merengue singers studied as the genre's canon.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
