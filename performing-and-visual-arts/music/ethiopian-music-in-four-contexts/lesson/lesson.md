# Ethiopian Music in Four Contexts

## Do it first

Wedding song. Church chant. National anthem. Work song sung for gain and goods. Four musics, four different jobs in the people's life. A researcher named Zenebe Bekele sorted ALL Ethiopian music by those jobs. This lesson learns his four boxes — and how to sort truly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Zenebe Bekele's four categories

The Grade 8 book teaches the researcher's sorting (§1.2.1, printed p. 13): **the music researcher Zenebe Bekele classifies Ethiopian music into four main categories by its ideas and performance contexts** — **SOCIAL** (melodies expressing ideas arising at **birth, wedding, death** and similar events), **RELIGIOUS** (chants sung in varied religious systems, and **zar music — zar shouting songs** — performed in customary worship), **POLITICAL** (**national anthems are included here**), and **ECONOMIC** (music bound to work, production and livelihood). The sorting key is never the instrument or the region — it is **what the music is about and where it is performed**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting music truly

Sort with the key in hand. A **wedding song** celebrates a life-event: **social**. **Zar shouting songs** in customary worship: **religious**. The **national anthem** at a state occasion: **political**. A **work song** pacing the threshing floor, bound to production: **economic**. A hard case teaches most: a song can be sung in two contexts — then sort it by the context you are asked about, and say so. Sorting truly means naming the category AND its reason, every time.

## Say it to your companion

Name the four categories for your companion and sort a wedding song, a chant, an anthem and a work song for them.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four categories and one example of each from your own area.
2. Write two sentences: why the sorting key is idea-and-context, not instrument or region.
