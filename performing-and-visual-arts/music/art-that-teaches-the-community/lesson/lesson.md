# Art That Teaches the Community

## Do it first

Stand at a quiet crossing with your teacher. Stop. Gather your thoughts. Look both ways. Cross only when it is clear. You have just done what this lesson's first song teaches every student to do. Songs can teach a whole community to stay alive — here is how the books do it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Traffic safety and “The Careful Student”

The Grade 5 book teaches traffic accidents by their **causes** (printed p. 125): **drivers' ethical faults, carelessness and lack of skill; vehicles' technical faults; road-building problems; and pedestrians' own road-use problems** — and the answer is to **respect the traffic law**. Its song is **“The Careful Student”** (printed pp. 125–126): the careful student **gathers his thoughts crossing the road, knows the traffic lights and laws, and listens to elders**. The Grade 6 book reprints the song as **ጠንቃቃ ተማሪ (The Careful Student)**, credited **words Solomon Gela, tune Ezra Abate** (Grade 6, printed pp. 110–111). The songs are placed by name, teaching and credit only — their words are not transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — HIV/AIDS and the song “So AIDS Does Not Catch Us”

The book teaches HIV/AIDS plainly (printed p. 127, with the **HIV/AIDS ribbon** pictured): **HIV/AIDS weakens the white blood cells that defend the body, until the body cannot resist disease; it is a disease of humans only, and it is known only by a blood test.** Its prevention song is **“So AIDS Does Not Catch Us”** (printed pp. 127–128) — pupils must be careful of the terrible disease, which has infected people worldwide while medicine has found no cure; the song's whole purpose is careful prevention. The song is placed by name and purpose only — its words are not transcribed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Doping: the stimulant-drugs warning

The book's third warning is **doping** (printed pp. 128–129, with a doping photograph): **stimulant drugs are banned substances that artificially and temporarily excite muscle growth and improve performance**. Athletes are checked by **doping tests**, and the punishment runs **from suspension up to lifetime expulsion** from sport. The book's fairness point matters most: doping **harms clean athletes**, who lose honestly-earned places to a chemical lie. Taught exactly in the book's framing — a warning, not an instruction.

## Sub lesson 4 — Make your own teaching art

The chapter closes by handing the work to the class (printed p. 130): **compose and perform your own songs on HIV/AIDS and traffic, in pairs or groups, then discuss how the message landed**; and **prepare short theatre pieces on traffic, HIV, harmful practices and trade tax**. The test of a teaching song is not its beauty — it is whether the listeners can repeat the lesson after hearing it once. Make yours short, plain and unforgettable.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four cause-groups of traffic accidents and the book's HIV/AIDS definition — then hum where the prevention song would go.

## Do It Yourself

1. Compose a four-line teaching song on road safety with a partner and perform it.
2. Draw a poster: the red ribbon and one plain prevention sentence.
