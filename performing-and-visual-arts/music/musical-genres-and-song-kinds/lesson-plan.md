# Lesson plan — Musical Genres and Song Kinds

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Musical Genres and Song Kinds
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The variety of musical genres — the ballad, cantata and oratorio among them — and the genres of song and instrumental music told apart by character.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Balada** — Unit 1, Balada — the folk ballad and its kinds (fantastic, haiduc, historical, family, shepherd's), with "Miorița" sung by Nicolae Sulac.
2. **Varietatea genurilor muzicale** — Unit 4, Varietatea genurilor muzicale — music's genres sorted by their means: vocal genres, instrumental genres, and the dramatic genres where arts join; song, dance and march are the primary genres.
3. **Cantata și oratoriul** — Unit 4, Cantata și oratoriul — the cantata (arias, recitatives, duets, choruses) against the oratorio, bigger and dramatic; Handel, master of the oratorio, and the "Hallelujah" chorus from "Messiah".
4. **Genurile instrumentale** — Unit 4, Genurile instrumentale — how instrumental music describes: the sonata (Beethoven's "Spring" Sonata), the concerto (Vivaldi's "Seasons", three movements fast–slow–fast), quartet, suite and symphony.
5. **creative listening** — Unit 2, creative listening — the ballad "Miorița" heard in Nicolae Sulac's singing, and the verses picked out where man's bond with nature speaks.
6. **Clasificarea genurilor** — Unit 3, Clasificarea genurilor — the classification of instrumental genres three ways: by means (vocal, instrumental, dramatic), by forces (solo, chamber of 2–30 players, symphonic), and by destination (cultivated, folk, light/jazz/entertainment).
7. **summary chart** — Unit 3, summary chart — the families of instrumental genres side by side: solo (piece, prelude, nocturne, dance, sonata), chamber (trio to septet) and orchestral (concert, suite, symphony, poem, overture).
