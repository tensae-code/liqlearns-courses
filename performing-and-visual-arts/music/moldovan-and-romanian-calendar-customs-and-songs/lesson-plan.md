# Lesson plan — Moldovan and Romanian Calendar Customs and Songs

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Moldovan and Romanian Calendar Customs and Songs
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Romania's calendar customs sung and played through the year — Caloianul and Paparuda, the rain-calling customs of the weeks after Easter, and the other old customs dedicated to the Earth and the Sun.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Caloianul and Paparuda: calling the rain** — two very old Romanian customs practised after Easter into June to call the rain and well-being — the Caloian, a kind of theatrical representation played by children, who model a clay doll and sing for the rain gate to open; and Paparuda, a dance performed by girls dressed in leaves and flowers, going house to house singing to call the rain.
2. **More customs for the Earth and the Sun** — the other old Romanian customs dedicated to the Earth and the Sun: Călușul, Sânzienele, Rusaliile, Drăgaica and Cununa grâului (the Wheat Crown).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Caloianul and Paparuda are the same custom → both call the rain after Easter into June, but the Caloian is the children's theatrical play with a modelled clay doll, sung for the rain gate to open, while Paparuda is the girls' dance in leaves and flowers, house to house.
