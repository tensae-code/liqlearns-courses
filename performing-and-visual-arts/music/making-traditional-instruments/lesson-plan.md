# Lesson plan — Making Traditional Instruments

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Making Traditional Instruments
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how Ethiopia's traditional instruments are made — the maker-knowledge of materials, parts and building that the Grade 7 book's aesthetic-usefulness chapter teaches.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Our Instruments and Ethiopia's Instrument Families — the learner knows the instruments by sight and sound; here their making is the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Materials: the maker's first knowledge** — trunk and hide for the kebero; bamboo for the washint; wood, skin and string for the lyres — materials chosen for their voices.
2. **Building and the knowledge it keeps** — the building order — hollowing, stretching, stringing, tuning — and making as indigenous knowledge handed maker to maker.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Our Instruments; Beat Instruments; Church Instruments. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the materials the makers choose for drum, flute and lyre, and why.
2. Describe the making of one traditional instrument in ordered steps, as far as the book teaches it.
3. Say why instrument-making is itself indigenous knowledge.

## What you need
Safe local materials to handle and compare — wood, hide, bamboo, string — no sharp tools needed for the lesson itself.
