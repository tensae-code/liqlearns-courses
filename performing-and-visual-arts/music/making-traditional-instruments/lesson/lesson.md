# Making Traditional Instruments

## Do it first

Every instrument begins as a material with a voice inside it: a trunk that booms, a bamboo stem that sings, a hide that tightens a drum's speech. The maker knows which material holds which voice, and how to free it. That knowing — tested over centuries — is what this lesson honours.

## Sub lesson 1 — Materials: the maker's first knowledge

The Grade 7 book's making teaching begins with **materials** (§4.3's instrument-making): the **kebero** from a **hollowed great trunk, hide and cloth**; the **washint** from **bamboo**; the **masenqo and krar** from **wood, skin and string**. No material is accidental: trunk-depth gives the drum's boom, bamboo's hollow gives the flute's breath, hide and string give the lyres their singing tension. The maker's first knowledge is a knowledge of voices hidden in things.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building and the knowledge it keeps

Building follows the material: **hollow** the trunk true, **stretch** the hide even, **string** the lyre to its singing tension, **tune** by ear against the songs the instrument must serve. None of these steps stands in a factory manual; the knowledge passes **maker to maker**, proved each time an instrument sounds right at its occasion. That is why this lesson stands beside Appreciating Indigenous Knowledge in Music: the workshop and the songbook keep the same knowledge in two forms.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the materials of drum, flute and lyre, and the building order — hollow, stretch, string, tune.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw one instrument and label its materials on the drawing.
2. Write the four building steps in order and one sentence on how the knowledge passes on.
