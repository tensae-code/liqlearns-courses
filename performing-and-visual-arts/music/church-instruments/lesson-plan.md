# Lesson plan — Church Instruments

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Church Instruments
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the three metal church instruments — ደወል (bell), ቃጭል (qachil) and ፅናፅል (tsenatsil) — what each is made of and what each serves.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Beat Instruments — the learner knows the six-name set, and these three are its church members.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Beat Instruments. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three church instruments: ደወል (bell), ቃጭል (qachil), ፅናፅል (tsenatsil).
2. Say what each is made of.
3. Say what each serves — the church, and spiritual melody.

## What you need
Your ears. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — listen in your mind to a church morning: what metal sounds ring and shake there? Name what you can (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes qachil and tsenatsil → both are iron church instruments; the tsenatsil (the sistrum) is the one that is shaken, and it can also be brass or silver.
- Says the bell is a drum → the bell is iron and large; it rings, it is not beaten like a drum skin.
