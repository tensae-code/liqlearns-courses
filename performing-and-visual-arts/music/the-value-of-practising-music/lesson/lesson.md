# The Value of Practising Music

## Do it first

Sing or play one short line. It wobbles. Now practise only its hardest corner three times, slowly. Sing the whole line again. Hear that? The line stands straighter. That difference — made in two minutes — is the value this lesson is about.

## Sub lesson 1 — What practice gives the learner (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 4 teacher guide opens its music chapter with the **value of practising music (§4.1)**: a note value known on paper is not yet music — **practice is what turns knowledge into ability**. The learner who practises gains steadiness (the beat stops wandering), accuracy (the right sound for its full value) and confidence (performing before others without fear). This part is teacher-guide evidence and is labelled so.

## Sub lesson 2 — What practice gives the community (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's teaching reaches past the learner: **a community with practising musicians has music ready when life calls for it** — songs for festivals and celebrations, chant for worship, and teaching songs that carry the community's lessons to its children. Practice done alone in a corner returns as music everyone shares. That is why the guide gives practice a chapter opening of its own. (Teacher-guide evidence, labelled.)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what practice gave you the last time you truly practised something — musical or not.

## Do It Yourself

1. Practise one song for ten minutes today, slowly and truly.
2. Write one sentence: what will your practised music give your family or community?
