# The Voice and Its Types

## Do it first

Quick start: from the title alone, guess what this lesson's work is. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The voice's anatomy and registers

The La voix sequence opens with the **voice's anatomy**: the **larynx, the pharynx, the rib cage (cage thoracique), the mouth and nose cavities** — and the **different vocal registers**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four mechanisms of the voice

How the voice makes its sounds — the **four laryngeal mechanisms**: **I « fry » (voix craquée/strohbass)**, **II chest voice (voix de poitrine)**, **III head voice/falsetto (voix de tête)**, **IV whistle voice (voix de sifflet, beyond 1,500 Hz, rarely used in singing)** — and **vocalises**, the exercises singers practise.

## Sub lesson 3 — The types of voice

The **types of voice and their classification** — **soprano, tenor, baritone, bass** and the rest — told apart by **tessitura** and by **timbre** (a round timbre, a throaty voice).

## Sub lesson 4 — The ear's role in singing

The **ear captures the sound message** and the brain, having memorised it, **commands the larynx and vocal cords** and sets the muscles for the pitch heard. **Tessitura** is the span from the lowest to the highest note a voice can give — with exceptional tessituras named (Mariah Carey's near-five octaves into whistle voice; Adam Lopez's record) — and the voice is **warmed up in scales, 10 to 30 minutes**.
