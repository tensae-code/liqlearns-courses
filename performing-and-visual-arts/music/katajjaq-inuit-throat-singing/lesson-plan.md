# Lesson plan — Katajjaq: Inuit Throat Singing

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Katajjaq: Inuit Throat Singing
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** katajjaq, Inuit throat singing — two singers face to face, making sounds with throat, mouth and nose — nearly lost, and now returning.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **ᑲᑕᔾᔭᕐᓂᖅ — two singers, face to face** — **Katajjaq** is Inuit throat singing: **two singers stand face to face** and make sounds with the **throat, mouth and nose**.
2. **Nearly lost, and its return** — Katajjaq was **nearly lost** in the years when Inuit were moved off the land.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks throat singing is ordinary singing with words → katajjaq is made of sounds — throat, mouth and nose — copying nature and carrying stories.
- Thinks katajjaq died out → it was nearly lost, but young people across the Arctic learn, teach and perform it today.
