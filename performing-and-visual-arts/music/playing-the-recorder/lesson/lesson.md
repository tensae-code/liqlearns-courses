# Playing the Recorder

## Do it first

Quick start: from the title alone, guess what this lesson's work is. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — First technique: posture, breath and tongue

The initiation séance teaches the **basics of recorder technique**: **posture, breath (souffle), tonguing (coup de langue), attack (attaque) and breathing (respiration)** — the body is set before the notes begin.

## Sub lesson 2 — The notes, step by step to the full octave

Séance by séance the learner comes to **know the notes on the recorder**: **fingerings (doigtés) and tablature**, first **sol–la–si**, growing (fa, mi, ré) to the **full octave do–ré–mi–fa–sol–la–si–do** — playing little pieces containing the known notes **by heart** and recognising those notes on the staff.

## Sub lesson 3 — Playing together: the programme's instrumental practice

The EEA programme's instrumental practice runs on its **three instruments — recorder (flûte à bec), voice and percussion** — for **interpretation, improvisation, reproduction and creation**, playing together in **small and large groups**, judged in end-of-year **auditions and recitals**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Stronger technique and the B-flat

The EEA3 sequence keeps recorder practice in nearly every séance: **strengthening the basic technique** and learning a new note — **B-flat (Si bémol)** — on the recorder.
