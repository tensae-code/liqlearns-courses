# History of African and World Music

## Do it first

You know one country's music history deeply now. The guide's last history step widens the lens twice: first to a continent — Africa's musics, centuries deep — then to the world. This lesson carries the guide's two-lesson map of those histories, as evidence: Grade 10's learners meet it from a guide because no Grade 10 student book exists.

## Sub lesson 1 — The history of African music (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 teacher guide's history unit opens with **the history of African music (Lessons 22–23, first part; teacher guide — evidence)**: the continent's music carried across centuries — its **instruments and styles** laid on a timeline, the guide's pictured form. Ethiopia's own line — Yared's chant, the bone-and-bamboo antiquity of African instruments, the regional traditions — stands inside this continental history as one of its oldest written chapters. Evidence only: the guide names the lesson's scope, and this lesson carries that scope without inventing its details.

## Sub lesson 2 — The history of world music (teacher guide — evidence)

The unit's second half is **the history of world music (teacher guide — evidence)**: the musics of the world's peoples laid in their historical order, Africa's line among them — not an appendix but a foundation, since the world's oldest instruments and some of its oldest written music stand on African and near-Eastern ground this course has already visited (the Hurrian Hymn and Seikilos Epitaph of the origins lesson). The guide's order is itself the teaching: **Ethiopian history (Grade 9) widens to African history, and African to world** — each lens honest about the one inside it.
