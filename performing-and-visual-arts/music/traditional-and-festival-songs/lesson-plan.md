# Lesson plan — Traditional and Festival Songs

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Traditional and Festival Songs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the songs the people themselves made — the girls' Abebayehush of the Meskel season, the full New Year song, the boys' Hoya Hoye, the Buhe bonfire song, and the songs of weddings and life's occasions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the festivals the learner already lives — Meskel, New Year, Buhe, weddings. The songs are the people's own (የህዝብ): words and melody credited to the people.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Abebayehush and Adey** — "አበባየሁሽ" (Abebayehush), the girls' call-and-response song and game of the Meskel season, and the short fragment "አደይ" (Adey — the yellow daisy) sung after it.
2. **The full New Year song and Hoya Hoye** — "አበባዬ ሆይ" (Abebaye Hoy) — Grade 1's song returned complete, with its lemlem responses and closing blessings — and "ሆያ ሆዬ" (Hoya Hoye), the boys' house-to-house New Year visiting song of good wishes.
3. **Buhe** — "ቡሄ በሉ" (Say Buhe) — the call-and-response festival song of the Buhe bonfire — and its game melody Hoya Hoye, sung by boys with sticks at house doors in the evening of Debre Tabor day.
4. **Wedding songs and life's occasions** — songs and dances carry the community's feeling at weddings, religious festivals, grief, baptism, work and more; the wedding praise song "ሙሽራየ" and the songs of your own area's weddings.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Family Songs and Songs of Nature — singing in call and response. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sing Abebayehush in call and response and name its season (Meskel) and its singers (girls).
2. Tell Adey, the yellow-daisy fragment, as the piece sung after Abebayehush.
3. Sing the full Abebayehush (Abebaye Hoy) with its lemlem responses and closing blessings.
4. Sing Hoya Hoye's visiting part and say what New Year songs carry: the community's good wishes.
5. Sing the Buhe response and name its day and its bonfire.
6. Name life's occasions that carry their own songs, and perform a wedding song of your area in a group.

## What you need
Your voice and a group to answer you. For Buhe, an unlit pretend bonfire of sticks is enough inside class.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the words → go back to the call-and-answer shape: the teacher (or the audio) sings a short piece, the learner answers with the same piece. Short pieces first, whole song after.
- Loses the tune → hum the song together before singing words; the tune first, the words ride on it.
- Sings but cannot say what the song teaches → ask the lesson's own question: what is this song about, and what does it ask us to do? Every song in this unit teaches something that can be said in one sentence.
