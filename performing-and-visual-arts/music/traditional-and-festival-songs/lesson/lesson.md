# Traditional and Festival Songs

## Do it first

Think of the last festival you celebrated. Who sang? What did the singing do — did it wish people well, thank, praise, remember? In this lesson every song belongs to a day, and every day has its song.

## Sub lesson 1 — Abebayehush and Adey

When the Meskel season comes and the yellow daisies open, the girls sing **"አበባየሁሽ"** — *Abebayehush*.

It is a call-and-response song and a game together: one voice calls, the others answer. Its world is the season's world — the flowers, gathering wood, building a house, counting stars, mother's scolding, and the great Demera fire of Meskel.

Right after it, the book sings a short fragment: **"አደይ"** — *Adey*, the yellow daisy itself. A small song for the small flower that starts the season.

## Sub lesson 2 — The full New Year song and Hoya Hoye

In Grade 3 the Abebayehush returns in its complete traditional form, as **"አበባዬ ሆይ"** — *Abebaye Hoy* — printed in full as repertoire at the New Year (Enqutatash) festival picture: the same song, now whole, with the **"ለምለም" (lemlem)** responses and the closing blessing verses for the household.

New Year songs carry the community's good wishes — at New Year, and at Genna, Meskel, Timket and Eid too. The boys' New Year song is **"ሆያ ሆዬ"** — *Hoya Hoye*: the traditional visiting song, sung going house to house, wishing each home well. Its words and melody are credited to **the people's (የህዝብ)** — nobody owns it; everybody does.

## Sub lesson 3 — Buhe

Buhe is celebrated on **Debre Tabor day**, and its song is **"ቡሄ በሉ"** — *Say Buhe* — a call-and-response festival song, words and melody the people's, sung at the Buhe bonfire: the children carry their sticks and the fire is lit, and the singing answers back and forth across the flames.

In Grade 3 the book joins the two: Buhe's own game melody **is** Hoya Hoye — the boys' house-to-house song with its **"ሆ" (ho)** responses, sung with sticks at the house doors in the evening. One melody serves the bonfire and the visiting — that is how the people's songs travel.

## Sub lesson 4 — Wedding songs and life's occasions

Songs and dances are the arts by which the community expresses its feeling — at **weddings**, at **religious festivals**, at **grief**, at **baptism**, at **work**, and at more besides. Every occasion has its songs, and each kind has its own kind of melody.

The wedding is a family festival given great value: when children reach marriage, relatives and neighbours gather in joy, with ululation and shouting. The book prints a wedding praise song as repertoire — **"ሙሽራየ"**, *my bridegroom, my bride* — wishing the couple well on the road of marriage.

Your turn, in groups: perform the wedding celebration songs sung at weddings in your own area. Your area's songs are repertoire too.

## Say it to your companion

Sing Abebayehush's answer part to your companion, then Buhe's. Tell your companion: which song belongs to Meskel, which to the bonfire day, which goes house to house — and what all New Year songs carry.

## Do It Yourself

1. Learn the response part of Abebayehush and sing it with friends in a circle, call and answer.
2. At the next festival in your area, listen for its song. Who sings it? What does it wish?
3. Ask an elder to sing you a wedding song of your area. Learn its wish for the couple and sing it back.
