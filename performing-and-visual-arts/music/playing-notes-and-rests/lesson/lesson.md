# Playing Notes and Rests

## Do it first

No instrument yet. Clap: 1 — 2 — 3 — 4, sounding every beat. Now do it again with your hands moving but making NO sound for all four beats. Then mix: sound, sound, silence, sound. If your count never stopped, you are ready — this lesson takes the signs you can draw and puts them under your hands.

## Sub lesson 1 — Playing whole notes and whole rests

The notes and rests of the last lesson are now **played on beat (percussion) instruments**. A whole note on the drum is one sound held through **four beats**; a whole rest is **four beats of counted silence** beside it.

[GIF 9622 — playing notes and rests on a drum]

The favourite way to practise is **relay (ቅብብሎሽ)**: one player plays, the next answers, sound handing over to silence and back — whole notes mixed with whole rests, each player keeping the count for the next. Groups also write four whole notes and play them **accompanied by a local beat instrument**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Playing half notes and their mixes

Now the half note joins: **two beats** of sound — and two half notes fill exactly one whole note's time. Play half notes **mixed with whole rests**: sound for two, silence for four, sound for two. Then play with rests **in groups, to local beat instruments** — your area's own drums are the book's chosen instruments.

## Sub lesson 3 — Playing quarter notes and mixing everything

The quarter note plays for **one beat**. One book exercise matches **movements from daily life** to the note's value — a step, a clap, a tap — each movement lasting exactly its sign's beats. Mix quarters with their rests, then build the full line: whole, half and quarter notes and rests together, and **play the shown notes and rests for your classmates**, as the first practice of this work asked.

The rule over everything: **the count never stops.** Sound is played; silence is played; both are music.

## Say it to your companion

Count a line aloud — “whole note… whole rest… half, half… quarter, quarter, quarter, quarter” — while your companion plays it on a table or book. Then swap: your companion counts, you play. Both lines with the count unbroken?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a line of six signs mixing notes and rests. Play it on any beat surface.
2. Play the relay game with a friend: whole note, whole rest, two half notes — hand over without losing the count.
3. Perform your line for someone at home and teach them one sign.
