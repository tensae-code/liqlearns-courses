# Moldovan and Romanian Composers

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — Moldova and Romania's composers — Flechtenmacher, Musicescu and their school. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Alexandru Flechtenmacher

Unit 3, Alexandru Flechtenmacher — the overture "Moldova" and "Hora Unirii" with Vasile Alecsandri; he founded the Conservatory.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gavriil Musicescu

Unit 3, Gavriil Musicescu — the choral master: his two Choral Concertos, folk songs arranged for choir, and a rich repertoire for school choirs ("Ca o zi de primăvară").

## Sub lesson 3 — Ciprian Porumbescu

Unit 3, Ciprian Porumbescu — the "Ballad for Violin and Piano" and the patriotic songs, among them "Trei culori".

## Sub lesson 4 — Ștefan Neaga

Unit 3, Ștefan Neaga — the symphonic poem "Nistru", the first Moldovan symphonic work in the golden fund, and the five-generation Neaga dynasty of lăutari and composers.

## Sub lesson 5 — George Enescu

Unit 3, George Enescu — the Romanian Rhapsodies Nos. 1 and 2, built on folk motives heard from lăutari in childhood.

## Sub lesson 6 — Eugen Doga

Unit 3, Eugen Doga — the instrumental piece "Sonet" and the song "Codrii mei frumoși": a composer of symphonic, ballet, film and children's music.

## Sub lesson 7 — Muzica pentru copii

Unit 4, Muzica pentru copii — Eugen Doga's music for children: the film songs of "Maria, Mirabela" on Grigore Vieru's verses.

## Sub lesson 8 — Constantin Rusnac

Unit 1, Constantin Rusnac — the concert piece "Sărbătoreasca" ("Poem about Moldova") for folk orchestra: sârba-dance outer parts around a tender middle.

## Sub lesson 9 — Muzica și teatrul

Unit 1, Muzica și teatrul — Eugen Doga's ballet "Luceafărul", on Eminescu's poem, libretto by Emil Loteanu, in two acts and seven scenes.

## Sub lesson 10 — Piesa "La joc"

Unit 3, Piesa "La joc" — Mihail Jora's orchestral suite "Priveliști moldovenești" (Moldavian Landscapes, 1924), built on a folk melody, and its second piece "La joc" (At the Dance), where trumpet calls summon the village to the dance.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson holds, part by part, in your own words. Companion checks: did every part get named, with its Moldovan name kept where the book gives one?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Moldova's own way of teaching its arts?
