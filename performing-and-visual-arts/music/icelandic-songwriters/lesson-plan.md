# Lesson plan — Icelandic Songwriters

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Icelandic Songwriters
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Iceland's popular songwriters — Sigfús Halldórsson, whose Litla flugan (1952) woke Icelandic record publishing; Ingibjörg Þorbergs, the first Icelandic woman to record a song of her own; and the guide's closing lives of the people who made the music.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the listening and songs lessons — the learner already listens, sings and keeps a beat.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sigfús Halldórsson** — Sigfús Halldórsson — Litla flugan (1952), the catchy song that woke Icelandic record publishing; and the surprise of Hvers vegna? — a popular song played on bassoon and harpsichord.
2. **Ingibjörg Þorbergs** — Ingibjörg Þorbergs — the patients' request-programme host, clarinettist and arranger, and the first Icelandic woman to record a song of her own (Á morgun).
3. **The people who made the music** — The guide closes with short lives of Iceland's popular songwriters, lyricists, singers and bandleaders — Oddgeir Kristjánsson, Ási í Bæ, Haukur Morthens, Ellý Vilhjálms, the KK Sextet and many more — each told by origins, first music, work and instruments.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

3. Say what the people who made the music teaches, in your own words.
