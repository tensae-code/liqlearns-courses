# Video script — Angolan Instruments

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Ngoma — Angola's drum.** The batuque/ngoma drum — its names across Angola's languages (Ngoma in Kimbundu, Kikongo, Cokwe and Ngangela; oñgoma in Umbundu) and how it is played. In the ensemble picture the drum family stands part by part: ngoma base and ngoma solo, mukindu and mpwita. The membrane drums (membranophones) are the ngoma/oñgoma batuque played with the hands; the puíta, a friction drum; and the cikhuvu, carved from a single wood block, sounding like a hippopotamus's grunt, played with sticks of virgin rubber.

**Kisanji — the plucked lamellophone.** The kisanji, Angola's plucked lamellophone — pictured in the Kituxi ensemble alongside mukindu, ngoma base, ngoma solo, dikanza, hungu and mpwita, and described closely: made of wood or gourd with metal lamellae, played with the fingers.

**Hungu — the gourd-resonated musical bow.** The hungu, Angola's gourd-resonated musical bow, named among the traditional percussion ensemble instruments and pictured among Angola's traditional percussion instruments.

**Angola's idiophones — saxi, bavugu, kaca-kaca, ngongi.** Angola's idiophones, sounded by the instrument's own body: the saxi hand rattle (a short stick through a hollow sphere of maboque fruit, tin or other material filled with stones and grains), bavugu rows of dried hollow fruits tied on dancers' legs, the kaca-kaca cylindrical maraca with a soft rain-like sound, and the ngongi bell of metal or coconut shell.

That is Angolan Instruments — Angola's own books teach it this way.
