# Hands-on — Angolan Instruments

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Ngoma — Angola's drum
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the batuque/ngoma drum — its names across Angola's languages (Ngoma in Kimbundu, Kikongo, Cokwe and Ngangela; oñgoma in Umbundu) and how it is played. In the ensemble picture the drum family stands part by part: ngoma base and ngoma solo, mukindu and mpwita. The membrane drums (membranophones) are the ngoma/oñgoma batuque played with the hands; the puíta, a friction drum; and the cikhuvu, carved from a single wood block, sounding like a hippopotamus's grunt, played with sticks of virgin rubber. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Kisanji — the plucked lamellophone
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the kisanji, Angola's plucked lamellophone — pictured in the Kituxi ensemble alongside mukindu, ngoma base, ngoma solo, dikanza, hungu and mpwita, and described closely: made of wood or gourd with metal lamellae, played with the fingers. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Hungu — the gourd-resonated musical bow
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: the hungu, Angola's gourd-resonated musical bow, named among the traditional percussion ensemble instruments and pictured among Angola's traditional percussion instruments. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Angola's idiophones — saxi, bavugu, kaca-kaca, ngongi
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: angola's idiophones, sounded by the instrument's own body: the saxi hand rattle (a short stick through a hollow sphere of maboque fruit, tin or other material filled with stones and grains), bavugu rows of dried hollow fruits tied on dancers' legs, the kaca-kaca cylindrical maraca with a soft rain-like sound, and the ngongi bell of metal or coconut shell. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.
