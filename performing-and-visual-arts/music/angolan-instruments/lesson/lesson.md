# Angolan Instruments

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about this lesson's subject — Angola's own traditional instruments, named in their own languages. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ngoma — Angola's drum

The batuque/ngoma drum — its names across Angola's languages (Ngoma in Kimbundu, Kikongo, Cokwe and Ngangela; oñgoma in Umbundu) and how it is played. In the ensemble picture the drum family stands part by part: ngoma base and ngoma solo, mukindu and mpwita. The membrane drums (membranophones) are the ngoma/oñgoma batuque played with the hands; the puíta, a friction drum; and the cikhuvu, carved from a single wood block, sounding like a hippopotamus's grunt, played with sticks of virgin rubber.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kisanji — the plucked lamellophone

The kisanji, Angola's plucked lamellophone — pictured in the Kituxi ensemble alongside mukindu, ngoma base, ngoma solo, dikanza, hungu and mpwita, and described closely: made of wood or gourd with metal lamellae, played with the fingers.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hungu — the gourd-resonated musical bow

The hungu, Angola's gourd-resonated musical bow, named among the traditional percussion ensemble instruments and pictured among Angola's traditional percussion instruments.

## Sub lesson 4 — Angola's idiophones — saxi, bavugu, kaca-kaca, ngongi

Angola's idiophones, sounded by the instrument's own body: the saxi hand rattle (a short stick through a hollow sphere of maboque fruit, tin or other material filled with stones and grains), bavugu rows of dried hollow fruits tied on dancers' legs, the kaca-kaca cylindrical maraca with a soft rain-like sound, and the ngongi bell of metal or coconut shell.
