# Video script — Syrian and Arab Musicians

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This is Syria's own lesson.

**Lesson 5 "Syrian Creators".** Syria's own music-makers — Suleiman al-Issa, "poet of childhood", whose poem is sung here, and the Syrian composer Muti' al-Masri who set it.

**Lesson 11 (listening).** Sayed Darwish, the Egyptian Arab renewer of music, whose works carried social and national causes — his anthem "Biladi" and songs named and heard.

**Lesson 13 (composer).** Riad al-Sunbati, Egyptian musician famed for setting the Arabic qasida poem to music, with 539 vocal works across Arabic opera, operetta, sketch, monologue, qasida and taqtouqa.

**Lesson 2 (listening).** "Layali al-Uns" sung by the Syrian singer Amal al-Atrash (Asmahan), words by the Egyptian poet Ahmad Rami, composed by the Syrian singer-composer Farid al-Atrash.

**Lesson 7 "From My Homeland".** the Syrian musician Adnan Abu al-Shamat (1934-2011) of Damascus — oud and double-bass player who studied the Sama dance's rules and composed qasaid and muwashshahat for the great singers — and researching the Arab music scholars al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, al-Razi and al-Kindi.

**Lesson 12 (masters).** Sabah Fakhri, Syria's voice in world festivals — master of Aleppo qudud and Andalusian muwashshahat who sang ten hours without break into the Guinness records — and Hamza Shakkour, singer of religious muwashshahat and munshid of Damascus's Umayyad Mosque.

**Lesson 17 (listening).** taqasim on the oud by Mohamed al-Qasabji, the Egyptian oud master who composed for the giants of Arabic singing, Umm Kulthum and Asmahan among them.

**Lesson 19 "From the Sea of Music".** the song "Tik Tik Tik, Ya Umm Suleiman" — its author and composer are the Rahbani brothers, Assi and Mansour Rahbani.

**Lesson 6 "From My Country".** "Did you know? The music researcher Ilham Abu al-Suoud is the first Syrian woman who composed songs and anthems for children, and she won a number of international prizes for childhood melodies".

**Lesson 17 "Children of the World".** "Did you know? The Syrian composer Suhail Arafa was born in Damascus; he composed many songs for the most important Arab singers, among them 'Min Qasioun Atallu Ya Watani', 'Ya Dunya' and 'Sabah al-Khayr Ya Watana'".

**Lesson 12 (composer).** the vocal composition is by the Egyptian musician Mohamed Osman, master of the Arabic song's classical forms.

**Lesson 5 "Musician of the East".** Farid al-Atrash — a Syrian musician and one of the leading figures of Arabic music, a singer and composer and among the world's most brilliant oud players, holder of the French Order of Merit beside Beethoven and Chopin and the Cedar Medal in Lebanon; the song sung just before, "Ya Zahra fi Khayali" (words: Ma'moun al-Shinnawi), is by a Syrian composer from my country whose fame reached the whole world.

**Lesson 8 (music culture).** Samir Helmi — a Syrian singer and composer from the city of Aleppo who sang many emotional and patriotic songs, composed for a group of artists, and has a collection of children's melodies; he took part in singing in several Syrian plays such as "Kasak Ya Watan" and "Ghurba".

That is Syrian and Arab Musicians — Syria's way, kept by name.
