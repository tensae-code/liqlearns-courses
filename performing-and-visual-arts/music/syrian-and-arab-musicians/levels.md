# Syrian and Arab Musicians — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 5 "Syrian Creators"
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Syria's own music-makers — Suleiman al-Issa, "poet of childhood", whose poem is sung here, and the Syrian composer Muti' al-Masri who set it

## Sub lesson 2 — Lesson 11 (listening)
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sayed Darwish, the Egyptian Arab renewer of music, whose works carried social and national causes — his anthem "Biladi" and songs named and heard

## Sub lesson 3 — Lesson 13 (composer)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Riad al-Sunbati, Egyptian musician famed for setting the Arabic qasida poem to music, with 539 vocal works across Arabic opera, operetta, sketch, monologue, qasida and taqtouqa

## Sub lesson 4 — Lesson 2 (listening)
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: "Layali al-Uns" sung by the Syrian singer Amal al-Atrash (Asmahan), words by the Egyptian poet Ahmad Rami, composed by the Syrian singer-composer Farid al-Atrash

## Sub lesson 5 — Lesson 7 "From My Homeland"
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the Syrian musician Adnan Abu al-Shamat (1934-2011) of Damascus — oud and double-bass player who studied the Sama dance's rules and composed qasaid and muwashshahat for the great singers — and researching the Arab music scholars al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, al-Razi and al-Kindi

## Sub lesson 6 — Lesson 12 (masters)
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sabah Fakhri, Syria's voice in world festivals — master of Aleppo qudud and Andalusian muwashshahat who sang ten hours without break into the Guinness records — and Hamza Shakkour, singer of religious muwashshahat and munshid of Damascus's Umayyad Mosque

## Sub lesson 7 — Lesson 17 (listening)
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: taqasim on the oud by Mohamed al-Qasabji, the Egyptian oud master who composed for the giants of Arabic singing, Umm Kulthum and Asmahan among them

## Sub lesson 8 — Lesson 19 "From the Sea of Music"
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the song "Tik Tik Tik, Ya Umm Suleiman" — its author and composer are the Rahbani brothers, Assi and Mansour Rahbani

## Sub lesson 9 — Lesson 6 "From My Country"
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: "Did you know? The music researcher Ilham Abu al-Suoud is the first Syrian woman who composed songs and anthems for children, and she won a number of international prizes for childhood melodies"

## Sub lesson 10 — Lesson 17 "Children of the World"
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: "Did you know? The Syrian composer Suhail Arafa was born in Damascus; he composed many songs for the most important Arab singers, among them 'Min Qasioun Atallu Ya Watani', 'Ya Dunya' and 'Sabah al-Khayr Ya Watana'"

## Sub lesson 11 — Lesson 12 (composer)
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the vocal composition is by the Egyptian musician Mohamed Osman, master of the Arabic song's classical forms

## Sub lesson 12 — Lesson 5 "Musician of the East"
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Farid al-Atrash — a Syrian musician and one of the leading figures of Arabic music, a singer and composer and among the world's most brilliant oud players, holder of the French Order of Merit beside Beethoven and Chopin and the Cedar Medal in Lebanon; the song sung just before, "Ya Zahra fi Khayali" (words: Ma'moun al-Shinnawi), is by a Syrian composer from my country whose fame reached the whole world

## Sub lesson 13 — Lesson 8 (music culture)
Sub lesson 13 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Samir Helmi — a Syrian singer and composer from the city of Aleppo who sang many emotional and patriotic songs, composed for a group of artists, and has a collection of children's melodies; he took part in singing in several Syrian plays such as "Kasak Ya Watan" and "Ghurba"
