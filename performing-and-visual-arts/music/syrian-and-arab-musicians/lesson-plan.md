# Lesson plan — Syrian and Arab Musicians

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Syrian and Arab Musicians
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Syria's creators and the Arab world's musicians, with the listening that goes with them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lesson 5 "Syrian Creators"** — Syria's own music-makers — Suleiman al-Issa, "poet of childhood", whose poem is sung here, and the Syrian composer Muti' al-Masri who set it
2. **Lesson 11 (listening)** — Sayed Darwish, the Egyptian Arab renewer of music, whose works carried social and national causes — his anthem "Biladi" and songs named and heard
3. **Lesson 13 (composer)** — Riad al-Sunbati, Egyptian musician famed for setting the Arabic qasida poem to music, with 539 vocal works across Arabic opera, operetta, sketch, monologue, qasida and taqtouqa
4. **Lesson 2 (listening)** — "Layali al-Uns" sung by the Syrian singer Amal al-Atrash (Asmahan), words by the Egyptian poet Ahmad Rami, composed by the Syrian singer-composer Farid al-Atrash
5. **Lesson 7 "From My Homeland"** — the Syrian musician Adnan Abu al-Shamat (1934-2011) of Damascus — oud and double-bass player who studied the Sama dance's rules and composed qasaid and muwashshahat for the great singers — and researching the Arab music scholars al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, al-Razi and al-Kindi
6. **Lesson 12 (masters)** — Sabah Fakhri, Syria's voice in world festivals — master of Aleppo qudud and Andalusian muwashshahat who sang ten hours without break into the Guinness records — and Hamza Shakkour, singer of religious muwashshahat and munshid of Damascus's Umayyad Mosque
7. **Lesson 17 (listening)** — taqasim on the oud by Mohamed al-Qasabji, the Egyptian oud master who composed for the giants of Arabic singing, Umm Kulthum and Asmahan among them
8. **Lesson 19 "From the Sea of Music"** — the song "Tik Tik Tik, Ya Umm Suleiman" — its author and composer are the Rahbani brothers, Assi and Mansour Rahbani
9. **Lesson 6 "From My Country"** — "Did you know? The music researcher Ilham Abu al-Suoud is the first Syrian woman who composed songs and anthems for children, and she won a number of international prizes for childhood melodies"
10. **Lesson 17 "Children of the World"** — "Did you know? The Syrian composer Suhail Arafa was born in Damascus; he composed many songs for the most important Arab singers, among them 'Min Qasioun Atallu Ya Watani', 'Ya Dunya' and 'Sabah al-Khayr Ya Watana'"
11. **Lesson 12 (composer)** — the vocal composition is by the Egyptian musician Mohamed Osman, master of the Arabic song's classical forms
12. **Lesson 5 "Musician of the East"** — Farid al-Atrash — a Syrian musician and one of the leading figures of Arabic music, a singer and composer and among the world's most brilliant oud players, holder of the French Order of Merit beside Beethoven and Chopin and the Cedar Medal in Lebanon; the song sung just before, "Ya Zahra fi Khayali" (words: Ma'moun al-Shinnawi), is by a Syrian composer from my country whose fame reached the whole world
13. **Lesson 8 (music culture)** — Samir Helmi — a Syrian singer and composer from the city of Aleppo who sang many emotional and patriotic songs, composed for a group of artists, and has a collection of children's melodies; he took part in singing in several Syrian plays such as "Kasak Ya Watan" and "Ghurba"
