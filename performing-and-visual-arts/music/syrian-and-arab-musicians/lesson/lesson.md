# Syrian and Arab Musicians

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — Syria's creators and the Arab world's musicians, with the listening that goes with them.. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 5 "Syrian Creators"

Syria's own music-makers — Suleiman al-Issa, "poet of childhood", whose poem is sung here, and the Syrian composer Muti' al-Masri who set it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lesson 11 (listening)

Sayed Darwish, the Egyptian Arab renewer of music, whose works carried social and national causes — his anthem "Biladi" and songs named and heard.

## Sub lesson 3 — Lesson 13 (composer)

Riad al-Sunbati, Egyptian musician famed for setting the Arabic qasida poem to music, with 539 vocal works across Arabic opera, operetta, sketch, monologue, qasida and taqtouqa.

## Sub lesson 4 — Lesson 2 (listening)

"Layali al-Uns" sung by the Syrian singer Amal al-Atrash (Asmahan), words by the Egyptian poet Ahmad Rami, composed by the Syrian singer-composer Farid al-Atrash.

## Sub lesson 5 — Lesson 7 "From My Homeland"

the Syrian musician Adnan Abu al-Shamat (1934-2011) of Damascus — oud and double-bass player who studied the Sama dance's rules and composed qasaid and muwashshahat for the great singers — and researching the Arab music scholars al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, al-Razi and al-Kindi.

## Sub lesson 6 — Lesson 12 (masters)

Sabah Fakhri, Syria's voice in world festivals — master of Aleppo qudud and Andalusian muwashshahat who sang ten hours without break into the Guinness records — and Hamza Shakkour, singer of religious muwashshahat and munshid of Damascus's Umayyad Mosque.

## Sub lesson 7 — Lesson 17 (listening)

taqasim on the oud by Mohamed al-Qasabji, the Egyptian oud master who composed for the giants of Arabic singing, Umm Kulthum and Asmahan among them.

## Sub lesson 8 — Lesson 19 "From the Sea of Music"

the song "Tik Tik Tik, Ya Umm Suleiman" — its author and composer are the Rahbani brothers, Assi and Mansour Rahbani.

## Sub lesson 9 — Lesson 6 "From My Country"

"Did you know? The music researcher Ilham Abu al-Suoud is the first Syrian woman who composed songs and anthems for children, and she won a number of international prizes for childhood melodies".

## Sub lesson 10 — Lesson 17 "Children of the World"

"Did you know? The Syrian composer Suhail Arafa was born in Damascus; he composed many songs for the most important Arab singers, among them 'Min Qasioun Atallu Ya Watani', 'Ya Dunya' and 'Sabah al-Khayr Ya Watana'".

## Sub lesson 11 — Lesson 12 (composer)

the vocal composition is by the Egyptian musician Mohamed Osman, master of the Arabic song's classical forms.

## Sub lesson 12 — Lesson 5 "Musician of the East"

Farid al-Atrash — a Syrian musician and one of the leading figures of Arabic music, a singer and composer and among the world's most brilliant oud players, holder of the French Order of Merit beside Beethoven and Chopin and the Cedar Medal in Lebanon; the song sung just before, "Ya Zahra fi Khayali" (words: Ma'moun al-Shinnawi), is by a Syrian composer from my country whose fame reached the whole world.

## Sub lesson 13 — Lesson 8 (music culture)

Samir Helmi — a Syrian singer and composer from the city of Aleppo who sang many emotional and patriotic songs, composed for a group of artists, and has a collection of children's melodies; he took part in singing in several Syrian plays such as "Kasak Ya Watan" and "Ghurba".

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Syria's own way of teaching its arts?
