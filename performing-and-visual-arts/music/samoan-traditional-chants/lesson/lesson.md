# Samoan Traditional Chants

## Do it first

How did Samoa remember its past before everyone could read? One answer sings: the song itself was the record. This lesson meets the chants that carried it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Faatufugaga, Faiva o le Vao and Sami

The Years 11–12 Music Teacher Guide's History and Research unit names **Faatufugaga, Faiva o le Vao and Sami**, and the **traditional chants**: pupils **understand the traditional songs that came from them** — studying each song's **rhythmic patterns, instruments, purposes, storylines and links to tasks**. A chant is never only a tune: it has a job, a story and a place in work and ceremony.

## Sub lesson 2 — Singing in its traditional context

The guide's work ends in performance and judgment: **perform a song within its traditional context**, and **review the role of the song in oral history** — the song as one of the ways a people without written records keeps its past and hands it on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: name the three chant sources, and name three things studied about their songs. Companion checks: Faatufugaga, Faiva o le Vao, Sami? Patterns, instruments, purposes, storylines, task links?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three names: Faatufugaga, Faiva o le Vao and Sami.
2. Choose one traditional song you know by name and list its purpose and its storyline in one sentence each — by summary only.
3. Write one sentence: what is a song's role in oral history?
