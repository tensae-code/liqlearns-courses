# Lesson plan — Samoan Traditional Chants

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Samoan Traditional Chants
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Faatufugaga, Faiva o le Vao and Sami, and Samoa's traditional chants — the songs that came from them, their patterns, purposes and storylines, and the song's role in oral history.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of its title — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Faatufugaga, Faiva o le Vao and Sami** — the traditional songs that came from them — their rhythmic patterns, instruments, purposes, storylines and links to tasks.
2. **Singing in its traditional context** — perform a song within its traditional context, and review the role of the song in oral history.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of the Music title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Faatufugaga, Faiva o le Vao and Sami.
2. Say what is studied about their songs, and what a song does in oral history.

## What you need
Your voice and a companion; a song your own family knows, held by name and purpose only.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a chant is only its tune → the guide studies the whole song: rhythmic patterns, instruments, purposes, storylines and links to tasks.
- Thinks singing a traditional song anywhere is the same → the guide asks for the song performed WITHIN its traditional context.
