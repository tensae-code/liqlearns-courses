# Moving in Note Values

## Do it first

Stand up. Sound a long “ta” and take four slow steps — that is a whole note walking. Sound a shorter “ta” for two steps — a half note. One step, one short sound — a quarter note. Your legs have just read music.

## Sub lesson 1 — Moving in rows, sounding the values

The Grade 4 book teaches note values with the whole body first (§1.1.1, printed p. 6): **pupils line up in three (four) rows and move to the given note values while sounding them**. The value is not only counted — it is walked and sung at once. Whole note: long sound, long movement. Half note: half as long. Quarter note: one beat, one step. When the sound, the step and the count agree, the value is truly known.

## Sub lesson 2 — Moving in circles, arms raised

The same teaching turns the rows into **three circles, arms raised above the head** — the class moves as rings, still sounding the given values. Raised arms make every learner visible: if one circle shortens a whole note, everyone sees it. Finish by mixing the values: the caller names whole, half or quarter, and the circles move and sound exactly that value, no more, no less.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why moving to a value teaches it better than only counting it — then walk them a half note.

## Do It Yourself

1. At home, mark four floor steps and walk whole, half and quarter values, sounding each.
2. Teach the rows-and-circles game to two friends.
