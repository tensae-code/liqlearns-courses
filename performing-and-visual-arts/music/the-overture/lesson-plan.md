# Lesson plan — The Overture

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** The Overture
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The overture — the orchestral opening that sets the stage — heard in Beethoven's Coriolan.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Tabloul muzical** — Unit 3, Tabloul muzical — Tchaikovsky's overture-fantasy "Romeo and Juliet": Shakespeare's tragedy told in three themes — Friar Lorenzo's Good, the families' Enmity (sword-strokes in the cymbals), and Love (English horn for Romeo, flutes for Juliet).
2. **Uvertura "Coriolan"** — Unit 3, Uvertura "Coriolan" — Beethoven's "Coriolan" overture, in sonata form: the hero's defiant unison theme against the pleading lyrical theme of his mother and wife.
3. **Uvertura "Ștefan cel Mare"** — Unit 3, Uvertura "Ștefan cel Mare" — Iacob Mureșianu's overture "Ștefan cel Mare" (1883), the first Romanian symphonic poem, on Bolintineanu's poem "The Mother of Stephen the Great": the oboe's uneasy cantilena, the battle, the Victory theme.
4. **Uvertura națională "Moldova"** — Unit 3, Uvertura națională "Moldova" — Alexandru Flechtenmacher's national overture "Moldova": the first proof in Romanian music that folk song and dance motives can feed the largest symphonic forms — a sunrise over the land, then a fan of contrasting folk dances.
