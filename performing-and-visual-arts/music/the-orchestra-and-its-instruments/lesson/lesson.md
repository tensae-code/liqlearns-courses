# The Orchestra and Its Instruments

## Do it first

Quick start: say one thing you already know — a sound, a song, a name — that belongs to this lesson's world. Keep it in mind; the parts will meet it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meeting the instruments ears-first

Malta meets the orchestra **ears-first**: every instrument is met with its **recorded sound**, recognised by its **timbre**, and only then sorted into its family, the range widening year by year. The first four, by sight and sound, are **violin, trumpet, flute and drums**, each coloured while its sound is heard.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting by family

After listening, instruments are **sorted by family**: woodwind (flute, clarinet) coloured one way, percussion (drum, triangle) another, and eleven names — cello, trombone, clarinet, triangle, flute, tuba, horn, violin, bassoon, timpani, double bass — fixed in a wordsearch. The full system is the **four families — Strings, Woodwinds, Brass, Percussion** — applied to sixteen named instruments including tambourine, cymbals, recorder, castanets and wooden blocks.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grieg — In the Hall of the Mountain King

Inside a story, each instrument's sound paints a step: pupils sort the heard instruments — clarinet, trombone, cymbals, bassoon, violin, oboe, timpani, French horn — into the four families, then match the story's **six steps** to the instruments that paint them, from **pizzicato violins sneaking** to the **whole orchestra** throwing Peer out.

## Sub lesson 4 — Britten — The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra

Britten's guide teaches the orchestra's **seating plan and sections**: twenty-six named instruments placed in their family sections and coloured by family, each section known by its marks — **cup mouthpieces and bells** for brass, **reeds** for woodwind, **four strings** (forty-seven on the harp) for the largest section, strings, and **hit, shaken or scraped** for percussion.

## Sub lesson 5 — Telling near-identical instruments apart

Close cousins are told apart: the string family's **pitch order** (violin highest, double bass deepest) and how the harp differs; woodwind **reeds** — double reed oboe and bassoon, single reed clarinet, none for flute and piccolo, the piccolo the flute's 'little brother'; brass — the trombone's **slide** against valves, and the tuba's deepest voice.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the parts in order, one sentence each. Companion checks: did every part get its sentence, and did the order hold?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the parts' names from memory, in order.
2. Do the hands-on activity once more, teaching it to someone else.
3. Write one sentence: what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
