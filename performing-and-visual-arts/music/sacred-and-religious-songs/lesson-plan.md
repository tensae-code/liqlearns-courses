# Lesson plan — Sacred and Religious Songs

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Sacred and Religious Songs
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sacred and religious song — the faith chants and religious nashid, devotional songs for children, and music wedded to the holy word.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **the faith chants** — "Forsaking the Idols", a chant of the Prophet, Gabriel's coming and the first revealed word "Read" (printed p. 8); "To the Generous", a chant of praise, prostration and the declaration of faith (printed p. 9); "Ablution", the chant of performing wudu whose water is light on the face (printed p. 9); "Like the Bird", the narrative chant of the Prophet's life — the cave of Hira, Gabriel and the call to Islam (printed p. 12); "Son of the Believers", the five-year-old in kindergarten learning his religion — cleanliness is of faith and prayer the pillar of religion (printed p. 13); "My Lord, My Lord", the child's prayer — an Arab Muslim child serving his homeland with effort and resolve (printed p. 13)
2. **The Religious Chant (Al-Nashid Al-Dini)** — religious chanting is an art that treats religious subjects; Islamic religious chanting began in the time of the Prophet Muhammad and includes remembrance of God, tahlil, tasbih and praise of the Prophet; reading and listening to the chant "Tala' al-Badru Alayna" (The Full Moon Has Risen over Us) and explaining its meanings, and the occasion it suits (summary only — no words transcribed)
