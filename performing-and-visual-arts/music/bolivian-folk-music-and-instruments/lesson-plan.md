# Lesson plan — Bolivian Folk Music and Instruments

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Lesson:** Bolivian Folk Music and Instruments
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bolivia's folk, criolla and mestiza music — estudiantinas, taquirari and chobena — and its pre-Columbian and autochthonous instruments and ensembles.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bolivia's folk music and its makers** — Criolla, mestiza and folk music, the estudiantinas, the representative ensembles (Los Kjarkas, Savia Andina, Trio Los Cambitas), the composers of the national repertoire, and the artists who carry indigenous knowledge and care for Mother Earth — all placed by name, title and theme only.
2. **The instruments of Bolivia** — Organology and the Hornbostel-Sachs families read through Bolivia's own instruments: the pre-Columbian instruments, the quena, quenacho and tarka, the guitar, the school banda de guerra, and the instruments of the Bolivian east.
3. **Taquirari and chobena** — Two rhythms of the Bolivian east, both in 2/4: the Taquirari, the dance of the arrow, and the slower, calmer Chobena of the Chiquitania.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
