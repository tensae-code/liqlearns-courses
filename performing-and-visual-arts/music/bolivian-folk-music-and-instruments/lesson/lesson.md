# Bolivian Folk Music and Instruments

## Do it first

Name one thing from Bolivia that belongs in this lesson — a song, a dance, a pot, a carving, a cloth. Say where it comes from and who makes it. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bolivia's folk music and its makers

Criolla, mestiza and folk music, the estudiantinas, the representative ensembles (Los Kjarkas, Savia Andina, Trio Los Cambitas), the composers of the national repertoire, and the artists who carry indigenous knowledge and care for Mother Earth — all placed by name, title and theme only.

## Sub lesson 2 — The instruments of Bolivia

Organology and the Hornbostel-Sachs families read through Bolivia's own instruments: the pre-Columbian instruments, the quena, quenacho and tarka, the guitar, the school banda de guerra, and the instruments of the Bolivian east.

## Sub lesson 3 — Taquirari and chobena

Two rhythms of the Bolivian east, both in 2/4: the Taquirari, the dance of the arrow, and the slower, calmer Chobena of the Chiquitania.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson holds, part by part, in your own words. Companion checks: did every part get named, with its Bolivian name kept?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Bolivia's own way of teaching its arts?
