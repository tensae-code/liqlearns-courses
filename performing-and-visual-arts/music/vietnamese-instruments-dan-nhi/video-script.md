This lesson is Vietnamese Instruments: Đàn Nhị, Đàn T'rưng, Drums and Clappers.

Vietnam's own instruments — the đàn nhị two-string fiddle, the đàn t'rưng bamboo xylophone, the trống con and trống cái drums, and the thanh phách clappers. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: The trống con, Vietnam's small hand drum (leather head, wooden body, about 20 cm across) — meeting it, striking rhythm patterns from pictures, and drumming the accompaniment to "Tổ quốc ta"; trống con again — striking pictured rhythm patterns and drumming the accompaniment to the new-friend song; the trống cái, Vietnam's great festival drum — its stick, body and head named in a labelled picture, its booming "tùng" at festivals and in the lion dance, and reading its rhythm patterns aloud; the story of the thanh phách — visiting a folk-instrument maker's house to watch the wooden clappers being made, hearing their cheerful "cách cách" sound, and reading a children's rhyme with phách accompaniment; playing the thanh phách — two bamboo or wooden bars struck together — striking pictured rhythm patterns and accompanying the song "Gà gáy".

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
