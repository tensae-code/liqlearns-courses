| Vietnam — Theme 2 appreciation — the đàn nhị — Vietnam's traditional fiddle, also called đờn cò, nhị líu or cò ke from place to place: two strings run from the tuning pegs (trục dây) down the neck (cần đàn) to the round body (bầu đàn), and the player draws and pushes the bow (cung vĩ) — bamboo or wood strung with horsehair or nylon — across them; its sound is bright and smooth (réo rắt, mượt mà), able to be gentle and deep or lively and joyful; it accompanies singing, plays alone, and joins other instruments; listening to Thao Giang's đàn nhị solo "Tình quê hương" (Love of the Homeland) (student book; original target `music/dan-nhi-vietnamese-two-string-fiddle`) | Book 3, printed p. 18 |

| Vietnam — Theme 5 appreciation — the đàn t'rưng of Tây Nguyên — a self-sounding percussion instrument made of bamboo tubes long and short, thick and thin, with a node at one end and the other end cut slanting; the tubes are tied together into an instrument across, with cord, and hung on a frame; struck with beaters, the tubes make high and low sounds; its timbre is slightly dull, not loud, but carries far and is quite special — hearing the t'rưng one feels the murmuring of streams, the pouring of waterfalls, the rustling of the bamboo forest in the wind; the people of Tây Nguyên often use the t'rưng in community cultural life — solo, in ensemble, and accompanying singing — and today it is brought into the national folk orchestra and can combine with other instruments (student book; original target `music/dan-trung-bamboo-xylophone`) | Book 4, printed p. 41 |
