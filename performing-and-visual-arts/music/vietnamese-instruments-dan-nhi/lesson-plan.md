# Lesson plan — Vietnamese Instruments: Đàn Nhị, Đàn T'rưng, Drums and Clappers

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Vietnamese Instruments: Đàn Nhị, Đàn T'rưng, Drums and Clappers
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Vietnam's own instruments — the đàn nhị two-string fiddle, the đàn t'rưng bamboo xylophone, the trống con and trống cái drums, and the thanh phách clappers.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Vietnam Grade 1 Âm nhạc 1** — The trống con, Vietnam's small hand drum (leather head, wooden body, about 20 cm across) — meeting it, striking rhythm patterns from pictures, and drumming the accompaniment to "Tổ quốc ta"; trống con again — striking pictured rhythm patterns and drumming the accompaniment to the new-friend song; the trống cái, Vietnam's great festival drum — its stick, body and head named in a labelled picture, its booming "tùng" at festivals and in the lion dance, and reading its rhythm patterns aloud; the story of the thanh phách — visiting a folk-instrument maker's house to watch the wooden clappers being made, hearing their cheerful "cách cách" sound, and reading a children's rhyme with phách accompaniment; playing the thanh phách — two bamboo or wooden bars struck together — striking pictured rhythm patterns and accompanying the song "Gà gáy"
2. **Vietnam Grade 3 Âm nhạc 3** — The folk drum ensemble (dàn trống dân tộc) — a unique performance form of Vietnam's traditional music, the great trống cái and the small trống con playing together: leather heads, wooden shells, sticks that strike the head or the shell for far-carrying sounds; listening to Phạm Tuyên's "Nổi trống lên các bạn ơi!" (Raise the Drums, Friends!); "Suối đàn t'rưng" (The T'rưng Stream, Trần Chánh Trực) — Tây Nguyên, the land of endless mountain-and-forest sounds: trickling streams, sighing wind, birds gliding in the highland sun — played on the t'rưng, the Highlands' great bamboo xylophone
3. **Vietnam Grade 5 Âm nhạc 5** — The đàn nhị — Vietnam's traditional fiddle, also called đờn cò, nhị líu or cò ke from place to place: two strings run from the tuning pegs (trục dây) down the neck (cần đàn) to the round body (bầu đàn), and the player draws and pushes the bow (cung vĩ) — bamboo or wood strung with horsehair or nylon — across them; its sound is bright and smooth (réo rắt, mượt mà), able to be gentle and deep or lively and joyful; it accompanies singing, plays alone, and joins other instruments; listening to Thao Giang's đàn nhị solo "Tình quê hương" (Love of the Homeland)
4. **Vietnam Grade 7 Âm nhạc 7** — The đàn t'rưng of Tây Nguyên — a self-sounding percussion instrument made of bamboo tubes long and short, thick and thin, with a node at one end and the other end cut slanting; the tubes are tied together into an instrument across, with cord, and hung on a frame; struck with beaters, the tubes make high and low sounds; its timbre is slightly dull, not loud, but carries far and is quite special — hearing the t'rưng one feels the murmuring of streams, the pouring of waterfalls, the rustling of the bamboo forest in the wind; the people of Tây Nguyên often use the t'rưng in community cultural life — solo, in ensemble, and accompanying singing — and today it is brought into the national folk orchestra and can combine with other instruments
5. **Vietnam Grade 12 Music** — "Kể chuyện ngày mùa" (Telling the Story of the Harvest Season), a famous work of Thao Giang (1948–2023) written for đàn nhị solo together with the folk music orchestra — besides the opening, cadenza and ending, three main sections; built on ancient chèo material, it carves a vivid picture of the Vietnamese countryside in harvest season (a slow, graceful opening evoking the peaceful homeland fields; a flexible, cheerful first section describing labour on the fields and farmers carrying rice back to the village; a settled, slow, gentle second section; a cadenza showing personal technique, exploiting the đàn nhị's special techniques to imitate life's sounds such as the cock crowing and birdsong; a flexible, cheerful third section leading to the prosperous, happy life); performed successfully by many artists, among them People's Artist Thế Dân; expressing the music's emotion through body movement
