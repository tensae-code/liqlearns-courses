# Scales, Pitch and Key Signatures

## Do it first

Sing up: do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, si, do. You have climbed a ladder of eight sounds and arrived at the first sound's twin, one floor up. That ladder is a scale — this lesson takes it apart to see how its steps are built.

## Sub lesson 1 — The major scale: the ladder of sounds

A **scale** is **the ordering of sounds in a system and sequence — the LADDER of sounds**, going up and coming down. The **major scale** is the main foundation scale, from which the others are formed. Its steps: the **HALF step is found only from 3→4 and from 7→8**; **all other steps are WHOLE**. Eight letter-pairs of the practice set to label whole or half: G→A, A→B, B→C, C→D, D→E, E→F, G→F, A→G. The book builds the same scale on both staves — C major and F major, each in the G clef and the F clef.

## Sub lesson 2 — The minor scale: three kinds, and counting back three

**Minor scales sound soft** in tone and character, and they divide into **three kinds: NATURAL minor, HARMONIC minor and MELODIC minor** — at this level only the **natural minor** is built. Its trick is beautiful: find it by **counting BACK THREE sounds from its major**. From C major (C D E F G A B C), counting back 3-2-1 lands on **A** — so A natural minor uses the same sounds, starting from A. The book pictures the D natural minor, and its exercise builds E, A and F natural minors on both clefs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Pitch: high and low

**Pitch** (የድምፅ ቃና) is the sound-character expressed by **thick and thin**. Sounds written on the **G clef's** lines and spaces are **HIGH**; those on the **F clef** are **LOW** — which is why the class divides by voice character to sing the two clefs and report their difference. Practise as the book sets it: sing C→F in quarter-note lengths; G→C in half notes; G→C ascending **and** descending. Reading staff notes and rests keeping time value, joined with the beat, is **sight-singing** — the skill this whole music strand has been walking towards.

## Sub lesson 4 — Key signatures: sharps and flats in order

To build a major or minor scale on a new starting sound, its steps must keep their whole/half pattern — and the **key-signature signs** do that keeping. They are **SHARP and FLAT**: written **on the music at the staff's head**, in their **correct positional order**, for both clefs. The book's plates show F major's and G major's signatures placed on the G and F staves; its exercise writes the signatures, in correct order, on given staves for **D major, E major and A-flat** on both clefs.

## Sub lesson 5 — Scales and key signatures in the guide (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 6 teacher guide teaches the same ground as the teacher's side of the desk: the **diatonic major scale**, the **diatonic minor scale**, and **key signatures (የቁልፍ ምልክቶች)** — how sharps and flats at the staff head set the scale. It adds the practice this lesson's singing grows into: **counting rhythm with spoken syllables**, practising counting written rhythms, and **reading notes written on the treble and bass staffs** — the pitches of thin (high) and thick (low) sounds. Evidence only, and labelled so; the ladder itself you have already climbed from the student book.

## Say it to your companion

Sing a major scale to your companion but stop on the 7th sound. They must sing the last step home and say whether it was whole or half.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build the F major and G major scales on paper, marking W and H between the sounds.
2. Find the natural minor of G major by counting back three, and write its ordering.
3. Write the key signature of D major on both clefs, signs in their correct positions.
