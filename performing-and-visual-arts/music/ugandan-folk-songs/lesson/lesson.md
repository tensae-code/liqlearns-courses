# Ugandan Folk Songs

## Do it first

Quick game: before you read on, say what you already know about this lesson's work — its name, its look, its story. Then read and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Every group its own tradition, handed down by mouth

Uganda's performing-arts books open with the country's own inheritance: **every ethnic group has its own musical tradition**, **orally transmitted for centuries** — learned by hearing and singing, not from a page. (Songs are placed by name and kind only, never transcribed.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Vocal tones: Kuhugura and eggono

Groups use **different vocal tones**. In **Bunyoro** the tone quality is called **Kuhugura** and in **Buganda** **eggono** — the same lesson, two named voices of two peoples.

## Sub lesson 3 — Pentatonic songs with traditional instruments

Ugandan music **usually follows the pentatonic scale** — a **few groups use hexatonic** — and **vocal music is accompanied by traditional instruments**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Perform a folk song of your environment

The term's work: **identify and perform a folk song depicting your natural environment**, with **appropriate vocal characteristics and instrument accompaniment** — the song, the voice and the instruments of your own place, together.
