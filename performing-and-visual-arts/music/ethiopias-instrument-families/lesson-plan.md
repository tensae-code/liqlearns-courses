# Lesson plan — Ethiopia's Instrument Families

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Ethiopia's Instrument Families
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's traditional instruments family by family at Grade 8 depth — the kebero as history-teller at their head, with dibe, atamo, negarit and tsenatsil among the beat-givers that add feeling to cultural musics.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Our Instruments, Beat Instruments and Church Instruments — the first meetings; here the families stand complete and deep.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The kebero: history-teller** — the Grade 8 depth: thick and thin sound; hollowed great trunk, hide and cloth; festivals and funerals; a rare instrument passing history generation to generation.
2. **The beat-giving family: kebero, atamo, negarit, dibe, tsenatsil** — the instruments that give more feeling to cultural musics at social and religious systems, named as one family roll.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Our Instruments; Beat Instruments; Church Instruments. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the kebero fully: sound, making, uses, and its history-telling standing.
2. Name the beat-giving family — kebero, atamo, negarit, dibe, tsenatsil — and its work at social and religious occasions.
3. Place the string and wind families beside the beat family from earlier lessons.

## What you need
Any real instruments the school holds; otherwise the pictures and names carry the lesson.
