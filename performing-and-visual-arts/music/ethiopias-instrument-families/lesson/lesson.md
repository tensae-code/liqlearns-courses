# Ethiopia's Instrument Families

## Do it first

One drum that booms low and taps high. A drum whose whole body is a history book. Instruments in this lesson are not listed — they are known: what they are made from, where they serve, what they remember. Begin with the drum that does all three best.

## Sub lesson 1 — The kebero: history-teller

The Grade 8 book's family teaching gives the **kebero** its fullest portrait: a **percussion instrument giving thick and thin sound, accompanying spiritual chant**; it is **long-lived**, and **its whole body and playing are history-tellers — a rare Ethiopian instrument passing history generation to generation**. It is **made from a hollowed great trunk, hide (ጠፍር) and cloth**, and **used at varied festivals and funeral ceremonies** — celebration and mourning carried by the same drum, exactly as the people's life carries both.

## Sub lesson 2 — The beat-giving family: kebero, atamo, negarit, dibe, tsenatsil

Around the kebero stands its family — **the beat-givers that give more feeling to cultural musics at social and religious systems: kebero, atamo, negarit, dibe and tsenatsil**. You know them from Beat Instruments and Church Instruments; the Grade 8 teaching binds them into one roll with one job: **feeling, in time** — the pulse under chant and celebration that lets a gathering feel together. Beside them stand the families you already own: **strings** (begena, masenqo, krar) and **winds** (washint, embilta, meleket) — three families, one music.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the kebero's whole portrait in four sentences, then recite the beat-family roll.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three-family chart from memory and check it.
2. Write two sentences on why a drum can be a history-teller.
