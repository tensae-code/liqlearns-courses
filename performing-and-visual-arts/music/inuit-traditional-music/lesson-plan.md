# Lesson plan — Inuit Traditional Music

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Inuit Traditional Music
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the traditional music of the Inuit of Nunavut — katajjaq, drum dancing, pisiit and square dancing — songs that gather people, tell stories and celebrate.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Music everywhere in Nunavut** — Songs are lovely to hear and are **heard everywhere in Nunavut** — on radio and computer, from grandparents and elders singing the old songs, and as dance music at feasts.
2. **Closing — what music does** — **Music is a fine way to gather people together, to tell stories and to celebrate.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks traditional music means only old songs → the book's forms are sung and danced today — at feasts, in schools and around the world.
- Mixes up the four forms → katajjaq = throat singing; qilaujjarniq = drum dancing; pisiit = personal songs; suluvinniq = square dancing.
