# Tsymbaly of Belarus

## Do it first

Before you read: say or sketch what you already know about this lesson's subject — Belarus's tsymbaly — the hammered dulcimer at the front of its folk orchestra, and the folk instruments' new composed life around it. Start there; the lesson builds on it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The tsymbaly at the front of the orchestra

In the **State Academic Folk Orchestra of the Republic of Belarus named after I. Zhinovich**, the chief place belongs to the Belarusian **tsymbaly** — a hammered dulcimer, its strings struck with sticks. The Grade 2 book gives the instrument a whole visit, with Luksha's poem "Tsymbaly" — the instrument is loved enough to have poems written to it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The tsymbaly's new life in song

The Grade 4 book returns to the folk instruments' **new life**: the composed song "Tsymbaly" (words Tsitavets, music Voinskaya) stands beside the folk song "Zajhraj zha mne, dudarochku" and the ancient folk song "Vaitouna" — and the children sing **second voices (padgaloski)** under a song, the folk way of enriching one melody with answering voices below it.
