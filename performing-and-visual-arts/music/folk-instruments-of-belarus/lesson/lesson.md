# Folk Instruments of Belarus

## Do it first

Before you read: say or sketch what you already know about this lesson's subject — Belarus's folk instruments, made for centuries by folk masters and tied to the people's life — dudka, tsymbaly, harmonik, buben and skrypka. Start there; the lesson builds on it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Five folk instruments

The Grade 4 book's chapter "Belarusian folk instruments" meets the instruments folk masters have made for centuries, their playing tied to the people's life, with tunes that imitate the wind, birdsong and animal voices:

- **Dudka** — the shepherds' beloved pipe; its players are called **dudary**.
- **Tsymbaly** — struck with wooden sticks; its sound is compared to the hum of a waking pine forest.
- **Harmonik** — the instrument of dance tunes and refrains.
- **Buben** — of no fixed pitch, played loud, bright, hushed or mysterious.
- **Skrypka** — the people's queen of music.

Folk ensembles never have fewer than three players — tsymbaly, dudka and buben; or skrypka, tsymbaly and harmonik. The chapter ends with a quadrille: count its timbres, and decorate it with the class's own instruments.
