# Notes and Rests

## Do it first

Sing “ta” and hold it. Count on your fingers while you hold it: 1 — 2 — 3 — 4. Stop. Now count 1 — 2 — 3 — 4 again, in complete silence, fingers moving, no sound. You have just performed the two longest signs in music. This lesson gives them — and their four brothers — their drawings and their names.

## Sub lesson 1 — ሙሉ ኖታ the whole note and the whole rest

A **note** is **the drawing of music (የሙዚቃ ሥዕል)** — a sign that shapes and holds sound. Every note has its own shape, its own way of being played, and its own time value. And every note has a partner: its **rest**, the sign for its silence.

The **whole note (ሙሉ ኖታ)** is an **oval, egg-shaped open note head** — open, with nothing inside it and no stem. It takes the **highest time value** in the music system: it is played by sounding “ta” or “la” **without stopping through four equal beats**.

Its partner, the **whole rest**, is counted **four equal beats with no sound**. Its sign is a **filled block hanging from a line** — it hangs down.

## Sub lesson 2 — ግማሽ ኖታ the half note and the half rest

The **half note (ግማሽ ኖታ)** is made by adding **one fixed vertical line — a stem — on the right side of the whole note**, drawn top to bottom. The stem halves the value: the half note is counted **two beats**, sounded “ta” or “la” through two equal beats.

The **half rest** is counted **two equal beats without sound**. Its sign is a filled block **sitting on a line** — it sits up. Hanging is whole; sitting is half. Never swap them.

## Sub lesson 3 — The quarter note and the quarter rest

The **quarter note** holds a “ta” or “la” sound for **one beat**. Its head is **filled** — black — with a stem.

The **quarter rest** is counted **one beat without making sound**. Its sign is its own quick squiggle, drawn in Picture 9611.

[Picture 9611 — rests, silent beats]

Remember the order of worth: the whole note takes the highest time value; the half note takes the next-highest; the quarter note takes one beat. Two half notes fill a whole note's time; a half note equals two quarter notes.

## Sub lesson 4 — All the signs together, and the Note-Values Song

Notes and rests together, one rule for all: **notes are played while sounding; rests are played without sound — and both are counted, always.** Beat (ምት) measures the time; the pattern the signs make is the rhythm (ስልተ-ምት).

There is a song that carries the whole lesson: **ሙዚቃ አጠናለሁ — I Study Music**. Its verses teach the three values in song — sounding the voice for four beats is the whole note, for two beats the half note, for one beat the quarter note. Sing it, and you carry 4, 2, 1 in your voice.

## Say it to your companion

Draw the six signs for your companion — no labels. Companion names each sign and its value, and says “sounded” or “silent” for each. Six signs, six values: 4, 4, 2, 2, 1, 1.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw all six signs in order: whole note, whole rest, half note, half rest, quarter note, quarter rest.
2. Write the beats under each: 4, 4, 2, 2, 1, 1.
3. Play a line of your own: whole note — whole rest — two half notes — four quarter notes, counting aloud, sounding the notes and keeping the rests silent.
