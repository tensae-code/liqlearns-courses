# Lesson plan — Reading Music on the Staff

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Reading Music on the Staff
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** music's notebook — the staff (ስታፍ) of five lines and four spaces, the G clef and the F clef that name its sounds, and the signs that complete it: bars, accidentals, ledger lines, time signatures and the stem rule for reading a melody.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Notes and Rests — the learner already knows the whole, half and quarter notes and rests as signs with values; this lesson puts those signs on the staff, where the Grade 4 book turns notation technical.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The staff: five lines, four spaces** — the staff (ስታፍ) is where notes and rests are placed and read; five lines and four spaces, counted from the bottom up (Grade 4, printed pp. 22–23).
2. **The two clefs: G and F** — the G (treble) clef rises from the staff's second line; copy its writing again and again (Grade 4, printed p. 24). the F (bass) clef rises from the fourth line, its two dots around that line; it serves low, thick sounds (Grade 4, printed pp. 24–25).
3. **Bars and bar lines; whole and half steps** — a bar line divides notes of equal beat; a bar is the space between two bar lines; double bar lines close a finished piece; the semitone is music's smallest distance (Grade 5, printed pp. 46–47).
4. **Accidentals: sharp, flat, natural** — the signs that raise, lower or restore a sound — sharp, flat, double sharp, double flat, natural (Grade 5, printed pp. 47–48).
5. **Ledger lines: notes beyond the staff** — sounds above or below the staff are written on short ledger lines — four plates: G clef above and below, F clef above and below (Grade 5, printed pp. 50–51).
6. **Time signatures and reading a melody** — the top number counts beats in a bar, the bottom names the beat's note-kind — 4/4, 3/4, 2/4; the stem rule and how a note sits on a line or in a space (Grade 5, printed pp. 52–54).

7. **Music as written language: the sign inventory (Grade 8 depth)** — music is a written language; its sign inventory — staff, clefs, notes, rests, accidentals and the rest — gathered as one alphabet.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Notes and Rests and Playing Notes and Rests. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a staff is: five lines and four spaces, counted from the bottom up.
2. Draw the G clef starting from line 2 and say it serves high (thin) sounds.
3. Draw the F clef from line 4 with its two dots and say it serves low (thick) sounds.
4. Divide music into bars, and say what a double bar line does.
5. Name the accidentals — sharp, flat, double sharp, double flat, natural — and what each does.
6. Read notes on ledger lines, read 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4, and apply the stem rule.

## What you need
Paper ruled with five-line staves (draw them), a pencil, and a drum or clapping hands for the beats.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts the staff from the top → always begin at the bottom line: music counts its house from the ground floor.
- Confuses the clefs → G rises from line 2 for thin, high sounds; F sits on line 4 for thick, low sounds — and its two dots hug line 4.
