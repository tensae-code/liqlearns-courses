# Reading Music on the Staff — true or false

Say whether each sentence is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Staff lines and spaces are counted from the bottom up.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** The G clef serves middle-to-thin (high) sounds.
**Answer: True**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The F clef serves thick, low sounds.
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Double bar lines close a finished piece.
**Answer: True**

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** A double sharp raises a sound by a whole tone.
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 5)** Every ledger-line note carries its own short line.
**Answer: True**

**7. (Sub lesson 6)** A note head above the 3rd line takes its stem pointing down.
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** The staff has six lines.
**Answer: False** — the book's own false item — the staff has five lines.

**9. (Sub lesson 8)** The Grade 4 Chapter 2 closing exercise includes practical items, not only written ones.
**Answer: True** — its practicals are: draw both clefs on a staff, and perform a chosen regional dance.

**10. (Sub lesson 9)** Grade 5 Chapter 2 asks learners to appreciate children's theatres and stage simple ones.
**Answer: True** — that is one of the opener's own aims.

**11. (Sub lesson 11)** Music, in the Chapter 2 summary, is a writable and readable field of art and science.
**Answer: True** — the summary's opening claim.

**12. (Sub lesson 11)** Batik is a new factory method invented for this book.
**Answer: False** — the summary calls batik an ancient community method for clothing design, still worked by the ancient method.
