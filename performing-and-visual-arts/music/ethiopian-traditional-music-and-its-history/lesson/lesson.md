# Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History

## Do it first

Hear, in your mind, the last traditional song you heard — at a festival, a wedding, on the radio. Who taught it to the singer? Nobody signed it. It travelled mouth to ear across generations. Hold that thought: it is the first definition in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What Ethiopian traditional music is

**Traditional music bears a people's identity**: it is systematic **poem + melody, transmitted orally, with no individual creator ownership** — the society itself is the author. Folk and modern music are told apart by poem, melody, rhythm pattern, instruments and context of use. **Sacred** music stands in four branches: Christian Ge'ez/Ezil/Araray chants; Islamic manzuma/neshida/baro; indigenous-religion irecha/magano; and folk-belief music. **Secular** music serves life's occasions — including the **festival songs**: hoya hoye, እንቁጣጣሽ (Enqutatash), ቡሄ በሉ, አበባየሆሽ (Abebayehush). Secular music is mostly pentatonic, but communities use **scales of two to seven sounds**. Its marks: no class or gender distinction; oral transmission; integrated with the culture; transmitted over long ages. Heroism music — fukera, shilela and qererito — has its own lesson next door.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saint Yared and the birth of Ethiopian notation (teacher guide — evidence)

**Saint Yared**, the 6th-century father of Ethiopian church music, built its system of **three melodic styles: Ge'ez, Ezil and Araray** — with their own **notation symbols**; the guide argues his system came before Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven. The guide then walks the music outward: the **first folk-music recordings** under Haile Selassie (the 1924 Jerusalem Armenian orphan band, which became a military band); the professional bands — **Meder Tor Orchestra (1935), Police Orchestra (1936), Kibur Zebegna (1947)**; and the **1960s flowering**, with Tilahun Gessesse and Mahmoud Ahmed named. It also maps the music by geography: **Northern** (Gondar, Gojjam, Tigray, Shoa, Wollo, Gurage, Welega — church melody reflected in secular song; harp, lyre, tambourine); **Eastern** (Harar Oromo, Harari, Somali, Afar, Bale — majority-Muslim music and dance; Wollo manzuma); **Southern** (Wolayta, Gamo Gofa, Kaffa, Sidama, Konso).

## Sub lesson 3 — Origins of music and of instruments (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 8 guide reaches the deepest past. Music stands in **every known living and ancient culture**; its first written forms include the **Hurrian Hymn No. 6** (the earliest melody) and the **Seikilos Epitaph** (1st century AD — the oldest complete surviving composition, engraved on a gravestone in Turkey). For instruments: the oldest in the record is a **vulture-bone flute** from a European cave; **mammoth-bone and swan-bone flutes** are 30,000–37,000 years old; the oldest drum is **China's Neolithic Alligator Drum**, clay and alligator hide, used in ritual; Africa gives the **musical bow**, its player's mouth as resonator. Music before language, the guide suggests: a language of emotion that imitates feelings.

## Sub lesson 4 — Instrument families: strings, winds, drums and shaken (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 6 guide teaches Ethiopia's instruments **family by family**, partly through printed quizzes with answer keys. **Strings** (የክር መሳሪያዎች): begena, masenqo and krar — the guide tests the **parts and playing of the begena**, the masenqo's string material, and the string instruments' posts and bridges, naming **ድርድር, ብርኩማ, ገበቴ, ቀምበር, መቃኛ** in its matching exercise. **Winds** (የትንፋሽ መሳሪያዎች): the **washint — a bamboo flute with holes** (the guide's key confirms it is made from bamboo/reed) — embilta, meleket and others. **Percussion and shaken/struck instruments**: the **negarit**, whose old role was **sending messages**, the **atamo**, the kebero — and instruments **sounded by shaking in the hand and striking with the finger**, the tsenatsil among them.
