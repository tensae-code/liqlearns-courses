# Lesson plan — Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Ethiopian Traditional Music and Its History
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what Ethiopian traditional music is — folk and modern, sacred and secular, its scales and festival songs — and its long history: Saint Yared and church music, the music of the three regions, the origins of music and instruments, and Ethiopia's instrument families taught one by one.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Kinds of Traditional Music and Our Instruments — the learner has already met fukera, shilela, the masenqo, kebero, washint and krar; this lesson gives the tradition its definition, its map and its history.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What Ethiopian traditional music is** — traditional music bears a people's identity — systematic poem and melody, transmitted orally, with no individual creator ownership; folk against modern; sacred in four branches and secular by occasion; scales of two to seven sounds (Grade 7, printed pp. 14–20).
2. **Saint Yared and the birth of Ethiopian notation (teacher guide — evidence)** — Saint Yared (6th c.), father of Ethiopian church music; his three melodic styles Ge'ez, Ezil, Araray with notation symbols; the first folk-music recordings and professional bands (Grade 7 teacher guide, printed pp. 2–7).
3. **Origins of music and of instruments (teacher guide — evidence)** — music in every culture — the Hurrian Hymn and the Seikilos Epitaph; the oldest instruments — bone flutes, the Alligator Drum, the musical bow (Grade 8 teacher guide, printed pp. 41–44).
4. **Instrument families: strings, winds, drums and shaken (teacher guide — evidence)** — Ethiopia's string family with the guide's printed quiz — begena's parts, the string instruments' bridges and posts (ድርድር, ብርኩማ, ገበቴ, ቀምበር, መቃኛ); winds — washint of bamboo; drums and shaken instruments — negarit, atamo, tsenatsil (Grade 6 teacher guide, §3.1, printed pp. 59–68).

5. **Modern music begins: Ashenafi Kebede and Beshah Tekle Mariam** — “Modern Music Begins in Ethiopia” — Professor Ashenafi Kebede's life and work; Beshah Tekle Mariam beside him.
6. **The Grade 7 and Grade 8 tellings: chant characters, definitions, and music as old as humankind** — G7: the chant types' characters (p. 91) and pp. 92–94; G8: traditional instruments defined as material heritage (p. 15); music as old as humankind and instruments' development (pp. 12–15).
7. **The modern-instruments contrast (Grade 7 telling)** — Grade 7's modern-music opening set against the traditional craft — the contrast Modern Instruments teaches from Grade 8.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Kinds of Traditional Music and Our Instruments. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define Ethiopian traditional music and tell folk from modern, sacred from secular.
2. Name Saint Yared, his three melodic styles and their notation, and the first bands.
3. Compare the music of the northern, eastern and southern regions.
4. Tell the origins story: the oldest instruments and the first written melody and song.
5. Name Ethiopia's instrument families and the tested parts of the string family.

## What you need
Your ears and voice; a map of Ethiopia if one is at hand; any local instrument to look at closely.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses sacred and secular → sacred serves worship in its four branches (church chant, manzuma/neshida, irecha/magano, folk-belief music); secular serves life's occasions — joy, grief, heroism, hunting, work.
- Cannot sort the regions → North: church melody reflected in secular song. East: majority-Muslim music and dance, Wollo manzuma. South: Wolayta, Gamo Gofa, Kaffa, Sidama, Konso.
