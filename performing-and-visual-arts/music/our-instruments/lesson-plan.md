# Lesson plan — Our Instruments

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Our Instruments
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** four of Ethiopia's instruments by name, look and sound-word — መሰንቆ (masenqo), ከበሮ (kebero), ዋሽንት (washint), ክራር (krar).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the child's own world — songs heard at home and at festivals. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **መሰንቆ — masenqo** — the single-string fiddle, met with its bow.
2. **ከበሮ — kebero** — the drum that plays with singing.
3. **ዋሽንት — washint** — the long flute.
4. **ክራር — krar** — the lyre, and the four instruments pictured together.

5. **The regional instrument roll and the negarit's history (Grade 6 depth)** — the Grade 6 history chapter's roll — mesenqo, negarit, embilta, atamo, kebero, trumpet — and the negarit as the proclamation drum of the summons.
6. **Washint, mesenqo and kebero at Grade 6 depth** — the three friends deepened: washint the bamboo flute of the march and melody; mesenqo the azmari's bowed string; kebero the thick-and-thin drum of chant and celebration.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: singing songs in class — the instruments are first met inside a song. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four instruments: መሰንቆ (masenqo), ከበሮ (kebero), ዋሽንት (washint), ክራር (krar).
2. Point to each instrument in a picture and say its name.
3. Say which instrument is a drum, which is a flute, and which two are played with strings.

## What you need
Your eyes and ears. Paper and a pencil for drawing the instruments.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — sing or hum a song you know, and name every instrument you can hear playing with songs like it (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the four instruments one by one, then pictured together (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9600]: a picture of the four instruments in a row, each named in Amharic and in letters — the student points and names each one.
   - ADD AUDIO: each instrument's sound in turn — the student names the instrument before the name is given.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name the four instruments and say one thing about each (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — made later, when videos are made.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, true-or-false — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw the four instruments from memory and name them (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes masenqo and krar → both sing with strings, but the masenqo is one string played with a bow; the krar is a lyre with many strings.
- Calls every drum a kebero → the kebero is the big drum of this lesson; other drums come in the Beat Instruments lesson.
