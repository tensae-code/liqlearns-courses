# Our Instruments

## Do it first

Sing a little of a song you know — or hum one. Now think: when people sing together, what plays with the singing? Name every instrument you can think of. This lesson meets four of them, the first four instruments of our music.

## Sub lesson 1 — መሰንቆ masenqo

The **መሰንቆ (masenqo)** is a fiddle with ONE string. The player draws a bow across the string, and the string sings. It is small enough to hold, and its sound carries the song.

## Sub lesson 2 — ከበሮ kebero

The **ከበሮ (kebero)** is a drum. Its deep boom keeps the song strong. When you hear boom, boom under the singing, that is the kebero.

## Sub lesson 3 — ዋሽንት washint

The **ዋሽንት (washint)** is a long flute. The player blows into it and covers and opens its holes with the fingers, and it sings a high, sweet line over the song.

## Sub lesson 4 — ክራር krar, and the four together

The **ክራር (krar)** is a lyre. It has many strings over a round body, and the fingers touch the strings to sing.

Now hold all four together: መሰንቆ (masenqo) — one string and a bow. ከበሮ (kebero) — the drum. ዋሽንት (washint) — the long flute. ክራር (krar) — the lyre.

## Sub lesson 5 — The regional instrument roll and the negarit's history (Grade 6 depth)

The four friends of this lesson march in bigger company in the Grade 6 book: the history chapter's roll of the country's instruments — **mesenqo, negarit, embilta, atamo, kebero, trumpet and the like** — the very instruments that summoned the march to Adwa (History in Music tells that story). Among them the **negarit** carries a history of its own: the **proclamation drum**, whose beat once carried the ruler's summons and decrees across the land — a drum that was also a messenger. When you meet the negarit in Beat Instruments, remember: its voice once moved armies and assemblies.

## Sub lesson 6 — Washint, mesenqo and kebero at Grade 6 depth

Grade 6 depth on three friends. The **washint**: the **bamboo flute** whose breath-line rides over march and melody alike — shepherd's instrument and soldier's companion. The **mesenqo**: the **one-stringed bowed lyre of the azmari**, the praise-singer's whole orchestra in one hand — the instrument Lema Gebre Hiwot never parted from. The **kebero**: the drum of **thick and thin sound** that accompanies **spiritual chant** and celebration — in the history chapter it beats under the summons, in the church it beats under the chant, one drum serving both worlds.

## Say it to your companion

Name the four instruments for your companion. For each one, say one true thing: how it looks, or how it is played. Your companion checks: four names, four true things?

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the four instruments from memory, one on each quarter of a page.
2. Write each name under its drawing: መሰንቆ, ከበሮ, ዋሽንት, ክራር.
3. Sing a short song and tap the kebero's part with your hands on your knees.
