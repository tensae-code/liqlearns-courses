# The Elements of Music

## Do it first

Sing a line you know. Sing it again, much louder, then soft. Again — faster, then slower. Again — high, then low. The song never changed, but the music did, four times. Each change touched one element of music. This lesson names them all.

## Sub lesson 1 — The six elements (pupil book)

The Grade 5 pupil book names the elements as one list (§1.2, printed p. 16): **beat, melody, harmony, structure, dynamics (with the alteration signs), and tone colour**. Every song you have sung is built of these six. The book then sets Grade 5's scope inside the set: **notes and rests and their sounded values** — the element-work this grade performs, while the whole set is named so nothing is mistaken for the whole of music.

## Sub lesson 2 — The eight elements (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 5 teacher guide teaches the same ground as **eight elements (teacher guide — evidence)**: **rhythm (ስልተ-ምት), dynamics (ኃይለ ድምፅ — the loud/soft power of sound), tempo, pitch (የድምፅ መጠን/ቃና), melody (ዜማ), harmony (ህብረት ሙዚቃ), timbre and duration**. Set the two lists side by side: melody, harmony and dynamics stand in both; the pupil book's beat grows into the guide's rhythm, tempo and duration, and its tone colour is the guide's timbre. Two lists, one music — the guide simply cuts the loaf into more slices. (Evidence, labelled.)

## Say it to your companion

List the six elements for your companion; let them answer with the guide's eight and the matches between them.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write both lists from memory and draw lines joining the elements that match.
2. Take one song and write one sentence about each of its six elements.
