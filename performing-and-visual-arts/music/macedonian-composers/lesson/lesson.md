# Macedonian Composers

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — North Macedonia's composers — Prokopiev, Bužarovski and their generation — who built the country's musical life and its comic opera. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dimitrije Bužarovski, composer, musicologist and conductor

Dimitrije Bužarovski, composer, musicologist and conductor, and his comic opera "Деспина и мистер Докс" (1991): Beaumarchais' Barber re-set in an Ohrid boarding house, its arias built on reworked folk songs, the emigrant Mister Doks come home to find a bride.

## Sub lesson 2 — Trajko Prokopiev (1909–1979) of Kumanovo

Trajko Prokopiev (1909–1979) of Kumanovo, composer and conductor who built Macedonia's musical life — first director of the Skopje music school, conductor of the symphony orchestra and opera, director of the "Tanec" ensemble; his ballet "Лабин и Дојрана" (1958) on the myth of Lake Dojran, its main theme a kaval melody in uneven 5/8, the flute imitating the folk kaval.

## Sub lesson 3 — the Macedonian Composer School: born in the 20th century on the foundation of folk music

the Macedonian Composer School: born in the 20th century on the foundation of folk music — uneven rhythms, modal scales, ornamented melody — and raised by new institutions: the Macedonian Philharmonic, the Opera and Ballet, and the Skopje Music Academy.

## Sub lesson 4 — the founders (after 1945)

the founders (after 1945): Stefan Gajdov, Trajko Prokopiev (the choral cycle "Kumanovki", the first Macedonian ballet "Labin i Dojrana"), Todor Skalovski, Živko Firfov, Gligor Smokvarski, Blagoja Ivanovski, Vlastimir Nikolovski and Kiril Makedonski (the first Macedonian opera "Goce") — folk tradition wedded to art-music craft.

## Sub lesson 5 — the second group (1960–1980), affirmation and modernism

the second group (1960–1980), affirmation and modernism: Toma Prošev, Tomislav Zografski, Mihajlo Nikolovski, Risto Avramovski and Stojan Stojkov — modernist techniques with folklore as inspiration rather than quotation, each with a personal style.

## Sub lesson 6 — the third group (from the 1980s), contemporary expression

the third group (from the 1980s), contemporary expression: Tome Mančev, Dimitrije Bužarovski, Goce Kolarovski, Jana Andreevska, Pande Šahov, Vulnet Tairi and Fatos Lumani — international careers, electronic and computer sound, multimedia and no single ruling style.

## Sub lesson 7 — Македонски композитори

Македонски композитори — the Macedonian composers in portrait: Vlastimir Nikolovski, Kiril Makedonski, Todor Skalovski, Toma Prošev, Petre Bogdanov Kochko, Risto Avramovski, Stojan Stojkov, Zhivko Firfov, Ljubomir Brandjolica, Sotir Golabovski, Dimitrije Bužarovski and Goce Kolarovski.

## Sub lesson 8 — Кирил Македонски (1925–1984)

Кирил Македонски (1925–1984) — Kiril Makedonski: composer of the first Macedonian opera, "Goce" — the first step toward a national operatic style — and of "Ilinden" and "Car Samuil", four symphonies and the symphonic poem "Tancharka", his operas breathing with Macedonian folklore motifs.

## Sub lesson 9 — Димитрије Бужаровски (1952)

Димитрије Бужаровски (1952) — Dimitrije Bužarovski of the fifth generation: composer, musicologist and pioneer of computer and electronic music in Macedonia; over 50 works — symphonies, operas, oratorios, ballets — his style moving from neoclassicism through aleatorics to a multi-genre mix of serious music, pop, rock, jazz and folklore.

## Sub lesson 10 — Драган Шуплевски (1933–2001)

Драган Шуплевски (1933–2001) — Dragan Shuplevski: choral conductor, pedagogue and composer — first conductor of Radio Skopje's Children's Choir, conductor of the Radio Choir 1968–1990, founder of the Academic Choir "Mirche Acev" (1960); over 80 choral arrangements and more than 20 original choral works in the Macedonian folk spirit, and three capriccios for violin and piano.

## Sub lesson 11 — Македонски композитори

Македонски композитори — the Macedonian composers in portrait: Trajko Prokopiev, Todor Skalovski, Stefan Gajdov, Mihajlo Nikolovski, Vlastimir Nikolovski, Dragan Shuplevski, Toma Prošev, Tome Manchev, Dimitrije Bužarovski, Stojan Stojkov, Zhivoin Glishikj and Goce Kolarovski.

## Sub lesson 12 — Тодор Скаловски (1909–2004)

Тодор Скаловски (1909–2004) — Todor Skalovski of Tetovo: music teacher, wartime conductor in Skopje and Bitola, co-founder of the symphony orchestra, the opera and the Macedonian Philharmonic; his choral works — above all "Makedonsko oro", which catches the rebellious rhythm of Teshkoto — "Makedonska humoreska", "Pesna za Goce", "Bitolka", "Makedonski diptih", "Todorka mlada robinka", and songs for children's choirs.

## Sub lesson 13 — Трајко Прокопиев (1909–1979)

Трајко Прокопиев (1909–1979) — Trajko Prokopiev of Kumanovo: began on the duduk, then flute, then violin in Skopje (1915), singing in the "Vardar" society; Belgrade's Music School, Prague's Conservatory, opera conductor 1947–1951; of the older generation who sought their expression in the national melos — the lyricist, master of choral miniatures on folklore themes and of film music; his last works the ballet "Labin i Dojrana" and the opera "Razdelba".
