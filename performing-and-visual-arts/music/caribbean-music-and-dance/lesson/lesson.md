# Caribbean Music and Dance

## Do it first

Open a social studies text about the Caribbean. Before you read a single paragraph, point: where is the heading? Where is a picture? Where is its caption? Those features are a map — they tell you where the text keeps its information. If you can read the map, you can find what the text teaches about **music and dance in the Caribbean**.

This lesson comes from Jamaica's Grade 4 National Standards Curriculum Guide for the Exploratory Core — its Social Studies / Language Arts work. It is a teacher curriculum guide, taught here as evidence.

## Sub lesson 1 — Finding music and dance in a text

A social studies text is built with **features** — headings, pictures, captions and the other helps a text gives its reader. Jamaica's guide sets this work: **use the features of a social studies text to locate** information — here, information **regarding music and dance in the Caribbean**.

So the first skill is locating: let the heading tell you the topic, let the pictures and captions show you what the page is about, and go straight to where the music and dance information lives.

## Sub lesson 2 — Extracting what the text says plainly

Locating is only half. The guide's second word is **extract**: take out the **explicit information** — what the text states plainly, in its own terms — regarding music and dance in the Caribbean.

Explicit means stated, not guessed. If the text says it, you may extract it. If the text does not say it, it does not belong in your extraction, however true it may be somewhere else.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: which features you used to locate the information, and the explicit information you extracted about Caribbean music and dance. Companion checks: did the features really point there? Is every extracted line stated plainly in the text?

## Do It Yourself

1. Take a new page of a social studies text. Name its features out loud before reading it.
2. Locate its information about music and dance, using those features.
3. Write your extraction: the explicit information only, in short lines, and underline any line you are unsure the text states plainly — then check it.
