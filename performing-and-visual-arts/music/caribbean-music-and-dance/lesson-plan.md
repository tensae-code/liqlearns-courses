# Lesson plan — Caribbean Music and Dance

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Caribbean Music and Dance
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** music and dance in the Caribbean can be learned from a social studies text itself — use the features of the text to locate and extract the explicit information it gives about Caribbean music and dance, as Jamaica's Grade 4 Exploratory Core guide sets the work (teacher curriculum guide — evidence).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the songs and traditional-music lessons; this lesson is labelled teacher curriculum guide — evidence throughout, because its Jamaica source is a teacher's curriculum guide, not a student textbook.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Finding music and dance in a text** — use the features of a social studies text (headings, pictures, captions and other text features) to locate where it tells about music and dance in the Caribbean.
2. **Extracting what the text says plainly** — pull out the explicit information the text gives regarding Caribbean music and dance, in the text's own terms.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Kinds of Traditional Music. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Use a social studies text's features to find its information about Caribbean music and dance.
2. Extract and state the explicit information the text gives about that music and dance.

## What you need
A social studies text about the Caribbean and a friend to check your findings.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — open a social studies text and point to the features that tell you where its music and dance information lives (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — finding, then extracting, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of an open social studies text with its features marked — heading, picture and caption — the student names each feature and what it points to.
3. **Say it to your companion** — state the information you extracted, plainly (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — made later, when videos are made.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — extract the music and dance information from a new page (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE (described only) — an open social studies text with heading, picture and caption marked → the student names each feature.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student listens and answers.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the whole text hunting blindly → start from its features: headings, pictures and captions point to where the music and dance information is.
- Adds ideas the text never said → this lesson extracts *explicit* information only: what the text states plainly, in its own terms.
