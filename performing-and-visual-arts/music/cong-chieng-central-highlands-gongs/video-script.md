This lesson is Cồng Chiêng: Gongs of the Central Highlands.

The cồng chiêng gong culture of Vietnam's Tây Nguyên Central Highlands. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: The cồng chiêng gongs of Tây Nguyên (the Central Highlands) — Vietnam has a rich, long-standing folk music, and besides the folk songs of every region there are unique folk instruments, the Central Highlands being famous for instruments made of bamboo and stone; in particular the Space of the Cồng Chiêng Culture of Tây Nguyên was inscribed by UNESCO in 2005 as a Representative Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity; the cồng chiêng is a self-sounding percussion instrument of the peoples of Tây Nguyên and some other peoples (Mường, Chăm, Khmer…), made of bronze, round in shape, in many sizes large and small, thin-walled, with a boss in the middle or without; the player strikes it with a wooden beater whose boss end is wrapped in cloth, or with the fist; wide-diameter gongs have a sound that rumbles like thunder, small-diameter ones a high, clear sound; and for some peoples of Vietnam the cồng chiêng is a sacred instrument, used on occasions of worshipping the spirits and at folk festivals.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
