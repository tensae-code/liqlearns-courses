# Lesson plan — Kazakh Composers and Singers

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Kazakh Composers and Singers
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Kazakhstan's named masters — composers Bakhytzhan Baikadamov, Dauletkerei the kui philosopher and Gaziza Zhubanova, and folk singer Kulyash Baiseitova..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Music (Gorchakova)** — folk vocal, the singing manner of a people's own tradition; the lullaby as everyone's first song; the Armenian folk song "Swallow" in Kulyash Baiseitova's folk manner, its virtuoso bird-imitation built on pauses; Bakhytzhan Baikadamov, founder of choral singing in Kazakhstan and first arranger of kuis and folk songs, who also recalculated the dombra's construction; his song «Девушка-водовоз» ("The Water-Carrier Girl") sung with noise instruments imitating hoofbeats
2. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Music (Kulmanova)** — Gaziza Zhubanova (1927–1993), daughter of Akhmet Zhubanov, composer of operas, oratorios and ballets; her ballet «Хиросима» (State Prize 1970) in summary: a Japanese family's peaceful life, the bomb, and the requiem ending — its main theme is the Japanese folk song «Сакура», sung here in Kazakh translation
3. **Kazakhstan Grade 6 Music (Musakhajaeva)** — what it teaches: Dauletkerei Shygaiuly, «Bapas kuishi» — founder of the lyrical-philosophical tokpe kui: «Топан», «Өттің дүние», «Жігер» and his gallery of women's portraits in kui; his kui «Байжұма» heard and analysed
