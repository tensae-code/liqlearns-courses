| Vietnam — Chủ đề 3, Bài 4 — Music appreciation — an overview of the history of Vietnamese music (sơ lược về lịch sử âm nhạc Việt Nam): the history of Vietnamese music goes through three large periods, those being: the period of building the country and keeping the country (thời kì dựng nước và giữ nước), the period of building an independent, autonomous feudal nation (thời kì xây dựng quốc gia phong kiến độc lập tự chủ), and the French colonial period and the struggle to gain independence, unification and build socialism (thời kì Pháp thuộc và đấu tranh giành độc lập, thống nhất xây dựng chủ nghĩa xã hội); 1. Music of the period of building the country and keeping the country (about from the 2nd millennium BC to the early 10th century): music of the Hùng Vương – An Dương Vương era (from the 2nd millennium BC to the 2nd century BC) — this is the period laying the foundation for the nation's cultural and music traditions: the music of the residents of Văn Lang – Âu Lạc is closely attached to labour life, many different music forms have formed and there are enough of the instrument types belonging to the families: the body-resonating family (họ tự thân vang) (bronze drums (trống đồng), stone instruments (đàn đá), gongs (cồng chiêng)), the membrane-ringing family (họ màng rung) (the types of skin drums (trống da)), and the air family (họ hơi) (gourd khene (khèn bầu), conch (tù và) and trumpets made of horn (kèn bằng sừng)) (a Đông Sơn bronze drum (Trống đồng Đông Sơn)); music of the era of Northern domination and fighting Northern domination (from the 2nd century BC to the early 10th century) — under Northern domination (Bắc thuộc), in the Northern region, the element of Việt – Hán cultural and art exchange appeared, the music and instruments of the Han people being imported into Vietnam such as: the pair of small cymbals (cặp chũm choẹ), the double-reed shawm (kèn đám kép),…, and in the Southern region, music has influence from Indian culture, imported instruments appearing such as the transverse flute (sáo ngang) and the Ghi năng drum (trống Ghi năng), in this stage the professional music foundation beginning to form and develop; 2. Music of the period of building an independent, autonomous feudal nation (the 10th century to the middle of the 19th century): the stage from the 10th century to the 15th century — under the dynasties of the Ngô, Đinh, Tiền Lê, Lý, Trần and Hồ houses, music developed brilliantly and there was exchange, absorbing from Chinese and Indian music, and in this stage two music streams (hai dòng nhạc) formed, folk music (âm nhạc dân gian) and court music (âm nhạc cung đình), court music and the chèo stage (sân khấu chèo) being born under the Lý dynasty; the stage from the 15th century to the middle of the 19th century — under the Hậu Lê and Nguyễn dynasties, the national folk music had new development steps: court music formed the folk music organisations such as Nhã nhạc (elegant music), Đại nhạc (great music), Tiểu nhạc (small music),…; folk music in the North has the Bát âm ensemble (dàn Bát âm), in the South there is the Ngũ âm ensemble (dàn Ngũ âm) of the Khmer people used in the festivals and funerals; some instruments imported from abroad appear such as đàn tranh, đàn tì bà, đàn nhị, hồ,…; and many traditional singing genres develop such as hát Cửa đình (communal house singing), Chèo, Tuồng, Ca Huế, Huế court elegant music (Nhã nhạc cung đình Huế),… (Huế court elegant music (Nhã nhạc cung đình Huế)); 3. Music of the French colonial period and the struggle to gain independence, unification and build socialism (from the middle of the 19th century to now): the French colonial stage (from the middle of the 19th century to before 1945) — this is the stage of spontaneous development of Vietnamese music before the strong influence of Western cultural art, especially French music, outstanding being the birth of the new music department (bộ phận nhạc mới), changing the basic face of our country's music: the composition movement by the Western European method and the birth of Reformed music (âm nhạc Cải cách) appeared, Vietnamese music absorbed the elements from the West and formed a new music foundation bearing the national style and soul (phong cách và tâm hồn dân tộc), many new songs being composed based on the major scale (thang âm, điệu thức trưởng), the Western major-minor system (thứ phương Tây) and the five-tone scale system (hệ thống thang năm âm), and composition tendencies appeared such as the romantic tendency (khuynh hướng lãng mạn), the patriotic epic tendency (khuynh hướng hùng ca yêu nước) and the revolutionary tendency (khuynh hướng cách mạng) with the famous songs such as "Bình minh" (Dawn) by musician Nguyễn Xuân Khoát – poem Thế Lữ, "Tiếng gọi thanh niên" (Call of Youth) by musician Lưu Hữu Phước, "Cùng nhau đi hồng binh" (Together Go, Red Soldiers) by musician Đinh Nhu, "Du kích ca" (Guerrilla Song) by musician Đỗ Nhuận,…; the stage of struggling to gain independence, unification and build socialism (from 1945 to now): the stage from 1945 to 1975 — in 1945, the August Revolution succeeded, revolutionary music (âm nhạc cách mạng) was officially born, the professional music foundation in the North also formed and developed, typical such as the songs "Bộ đội về làng" (Soldiers Return to the Village) by musician Lê Yên, "Trường ca sông Lô" (The Lô River Epic) by musician Văn Cao, "Cấy chiêm" (Planting the Fifth-Month Rice) by musician Tô Vũ – poem Quách Vinh, "Đóng nhanh lúa tốt" (Thresh Quickly, Good Rice) by musician Lê Lôi – poem Huyền Tâm, the cantata (thanh xướng kịch) "Nguyễn Văn Trỗi" by musician Đàm Linh, the opera (nhạc kịch) "Cô Sao" (Miss Sao) and "Người tạc tượng" (The Sculptor) by musician Đỗ Nhuận, "Bên bờ K'rông Pa" (On the Bank of the K'rông Pa) by musician Nhật Lai,…; in the South, many musicians succeeded with the songs on the resistance topic, typical being the songs "Lên ngàn" (Up to the Mountains) by musician Hoàng Việt, "Tiểu đoàn 307" (Battalion 307) by musician Nguyễn Hữu Trí, "Con kênh xanh xanh" (The Green Canal) by musician Ngô Huỳnh, "Tình đồng chí" (Comradeship) by musician Minh Quốc,…; and the instrumental music genres develop such as symphony, concerto, sonate; the stage from 1975 to now — from 1975, Vietnamese music achieved many new achievements, the composition topics mainly in this stage being praising the Party, Uncle Ho, the glorious feats of arms (chiến công lẫy lừng) of the resistance war against America, and love of homeland and country, typical being the songs such as: "Viếng lăng Bác" (Visiting Uncle Ho's Mausoleum) by musician Hoàng Hiệp, "Lời Bác dặn trước lúc đi xa" (Uncle Ho's Words Before Going Far Away) by musician Trần Hoàn, "Mùa xuân trên Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh" (Spring in Ho Chi Minh City) by musician Xuân Hồng, "Bài ca không quên" (The Unforgettable Song) by musician Phạm Minh Tuấn, "Quê hương" (Homeland) (music Giáp Văn Thạch, poem Đỗ Trung Quân),…; the field of training and performance achieved many new achievements, one of the most brilliant achievements being that in the year 1980, People's Artist Đặng Thái Sơn won first prize in the international piano competition named Chopin in Poland, the event being admired and esteemed by world friends; and from the year 2000 to now, music is rich and diverse in topics and content, the topics of history, love of homeland and country and national traditions always being highly proposed, many songs about protecting the sea and islands (bảo vệ biển đảo) encouraging the national spirit, many artists having performed successfully music works domestically and abroad, admired by the public, and the Classical chamber, folk and light music streams (dòng nhạc Cổ điển thính phòng, dân gian và nhạc nhẹ) developing in a balanced, harmonious way; and listening to the Aria Cô Sao by musician Đỗ Nhuận: "Aria Cô Sao" is a singing excerpt (khúc hát) in the opera (vở nhạc kịch) "Cô Sao" by musician Đỗ Nhuận — the opera includes 3 acts (3 màn), its content telling about the struggle of the compatriots and soldiers of the Northwest region (vùng Tây Bắc) against the French, the Japanese and their henchmen (bọn tay sai) and praising the beautiful love of the Thái ethnic girl named A Sao with Hà (a Viet Minh political prisoner (một tù nhân chính trị Việt Minh)); in 1965, the opera was premiered (công diễn) for the first time at the Hanoi Opera House (Nhà hát Lớn Hà Nội); later, "Aria Cô Sao" was separated into an independent work and performed on many professional music stages domestically and abroad; and the melody of the work has a lyrical, narrative character (trữ tình, tự sự) combined with the words showing lingering, yearning moods (tâm trạng day dứt, khắc khoải), the rhythm and tempo being changed continuously reflecting the inner agitation (sự xáo động nội tâm) (student book;
