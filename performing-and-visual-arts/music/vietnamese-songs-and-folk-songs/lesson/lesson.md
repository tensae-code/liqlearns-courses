## Sub lesson 6 — Vietnam Grade 6 Âm nhạc 6

"Mưa rơi" (The Rain Falls) — a folk song of the Khơ-mú (Khmu) people, a small ethnic group living mainly in Vietnam's Northwest, collected and transcribed by Tô Ngọc Thanh: rain makes the trees fresh and the buds crowd the branches, the forest is beautiful with a hundred flowers, butterflies flutter, the boys and girls laugh beside the terraced fields and the cuckoo calls — a cheerful, optimistic melody picturing the beautiful, peaceful space of the mountain homeland; sung in relay between two groups and with percussion accompaniment; "Bác Hồ – Người cho em tất cả" (Uncle Hồ — He Who Gives Me Everything), music by Hoàng Long and Hoàng Lân, words after the poem by Phong Thu — a lilting, clear melody holding children's innocent feelings before the beautiful nature and the dear village: the morning sun, the moon-sister, the trees, rivers and fields all give, the soldier gives courage and the teacher her lessons, but the one who gives the child everything — who brings the light, the dreams and a new, bright, free and happy life — is Bác Hồ Chí Minh; the children's gratitude to him, in relay groups and unison; "Việt Nam quê hương tôi" (Vietnam, My Homeland) by Đỗ Nhuận — an invitation to come to Vietnam: the blue sea stretching to the horizon, the waves lapping, the casuarina wind on the shore, sails rising over the waves into the bright pink sunrise

## Sub lesson 7 — Vietnam Grade 7 Âm nhạc 7

The song "Lí kéo chài" (The Net-Hauling Lí), a folk song of the South (dân ca Nam Bộ) with new words by Hoàng Lân — a cheerful melody whose words picture the enthusiastic, optimistic labour of the fishing folk of the Southern countryside, hauling in the boat for many shrimps and fish; it is sung in the call-and-response form (xướng – xô): the leader (xướng) is one person with a ringing, strong voice, and the response (xô) is the group, singing while they work the movements of the labour, with the hauling actions as accompaniment; the folk songs of some regions of Vietnam — folk song is a precious heritage in the treasury of Vietnamese folk music of many generations: arising from the spiritual life needs of working people, folk songs are songs the people themselves created, polished and handed down by oral transmission through many generations; and because they differ in geography, climate, language, ways of labour and living environment, Vietnam's folk-song regions are rich in genre, unique in melody (làn điệu) and performance space: the northern mountain peoples (Tày, Nùng, Thái, Dao, Mông and others), whose folk melodies are usually clear and cheerful with simple, rustic words close to the images of mountain forest and of life and labour on the upland fields — "Xoè hoa" (Thái folk song), "Mưa rơi" (Khơ-mú), "Gà gáy" (Cống Khao); the northern midlands and Red River delta, whose melodies are lyrical, heartfelt and clear with simple words tied to the life of the wet-rice people — hát trống quân, hát xoan, hát ru, hát dô, hát đúm, hát quan họ, đồng dao; Central Vietnam, rich in melody with its own beauty tied to each locality's language — "Đi cấy" (folk song of Thanh Hoá), "Lí tiểu khúc" (folk song of Nghệ Tĩnh), the folk songs of Bình Trị Thiên, and South-Central Vietnam with its hò, lí, ví and hát dặm melodies, hát giao duyên, hát ru and labour folk songs joined to farming, fishing, weaving and mat-weaving; the Central Highlands (Tây Nguyên), whose folk songs are at times strong, joining the sound of the cồng chiêng gongs and the folk dances, at times whispering confidences in simple, plain words, and at times heartfelt with love in lullabies for children — "Ru em" (Xơ-đăng folk song), "Hát mừng" (Hrê); and the South (Nam Bộ), with its garden countryside and dense web of rivers and canals — the lí melodies, hò Đồng Tháp, and the folk melodies of the Chăm, Hoa and Khmer peoples, such as the song "Chim sáo"; "Inh lả ơi", a folk song of the Thái people — spring comes to the northwestern mountains and forests, a thousand flowers smile; the reading is practised for pitch and rhythm and read with words set and with movement (the book notes that Inh lả and Noọng are ways of addressing young girls)

## Sub lesson 8 — Vietnam Grade 8 Âm nhạc 8

The song "Việt Nam ơi" (Oh Vietnam), music and words by Bùi Quang Minh — its joyful, proud melody is like a panoramic picture of a beautiful Vietnam changing day by day to join and develop together with the world; the song has a two-section form and an opening phrase (câu đầu); the song "Ngàn ước mơ Việt Nam" (A Thousand Vietnamese Dreams), music and words by Nguyễn Hồng Thuận (excerpt) — it brings the optimistic message of life of Vietnam's young generation, always dreaming and burning with all their might for their passion in order to head toward success; the Quan họ folk songs of Bắc Ninh (Dân ca Quan họ Bắc Ninh) — formed and developed in the old Kinh Bắc region (now Bắc Ninh province), a distinctive art form of that thousand-year civilised land; Quan họ is both a form of exchange singing (hát giao duyên) and a genre of ritual folk song bound to the custom of forming sworn friendships (kết nghĩa, kết bạn); the liền anh (the male singers) in áo the, khăn xếp and ô lục soạn, together with the liền chị (the female singers) in four-panel or five-panel dress, khăn mỏ quạ scarves and nón thúng quai thao hats, sing answering verses to each other, plain and deeply felt, at village festivals and new-house celebrations; each singing session (canh) usually begins with the giọng lề lối (giọng cổ, the old style), continues with the giọng vặt (giọng vụn) and ends with the giọng giã bạn (the farewell style); many Quan họ songs have been brought onto the stage and have become familiar to audiences all over the country and internationally, such as "Gọi đò", "Người ơi đừng về", "Ngồi tựa mạn thuyền" and "Cây trúc xinh"; in 2009, Dân ca Quan họ Bắc Ninh was inscribed by UNESCO as a Representative Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity; the listening song "Khách đến chơi nhà" (A Guest Comes to Visit the House) is one of the representative Quan họ songs, showing the cultural beauty in the tactful, delicate and affectionate way of behaving of the Kinh Bắc people; the song "Nơi ấy Trường Sa" (That Place Is Trường Sa), music by Phạm Tuyên, words adapted from a poem by Vũ Thị Khương — it has cheerful, bustling melodic touches, and its image-rich words tell of beautiful nature where the soldiers guard the homeland's sea and islands day and night; the song has a two-section form; the song "Nơi đảo xa" (At the Far-Off Island), music and words by Thế Song (excerpt) — with optimistic, earnest feeling and a melody like surging waves, together with the image of the naval soldier, the song shows love of homeland and country and the resolve to protect every sacred inch of the Fatherland's land; the folk song "Soi bóng bên hồ" (Reflecting One's Image beside the Lake) — a folk song of the Giáy people, collected, recorded and adapted by Nguyễn Tài Tuệ; the song has a cheerful, clear melody with images of the nature and the beautiful life of the northern mountain region of the country

## Sub lesson 13 — Vietnam Grade 11 Âm nhạc 11

"Dâng Người tiếng hát mùa xuân" (Offering Him the Spring Song): "Dâng Người tiếng hát mùa xuân" by musician Nguyễn Văn Thương (1919 – 2002) is one of the typical songs about beloved Uncle Ho, its music character lyrical and clear (trữ tình, trong sáng), its words showing the people's respectful heart (lòng tôn kính) towards His great merit (công lao to lớn), the generations today and tomorrow also shoulder to shoulder, side by side, dedicating their youth to keep the nation's independence and freedom and build the country ever richer and more beautiful; the lesson opens with body-relaxing movements (turning the neck, turning the shoulders, bending the body) helping relax the body, mouth and face muscles, and voice warm-up (khởi động giọng) patterns 1 and 2 (vocalise), with the guidance: place the sound gently, the mouth opens wide, the lower jaw drops softly, and practise the sound pattern ascending and descending stepwise by seconds in a fitting voice range; and the singing guidance: place the sound gently, the mouth opens naturally, the lower jaw drops softly, take a deep breath at the idea-breaks, sentences and sections, regulate the breath evenly (điều tiết hơi đều đặn), sing stronger into the head of each beat, and show exactly the pitch and duration of the piece; and the practice: choose one singing sentence in the song and show that singing sentence by the vocalise singing way, and show the song by different singing forms; and the application: perform the song combined with accompaniment music and auxiliary movements (động tác phụ hoạ); "Lí qua kêu": "Lí qua kêu" is a familiar folk song (bài dân ca) of the people of the South (Nam Bộ), recorded (kí âm): Hoa Sim — in each folk song sentence there contains cheerful, witty, humorous feelings (tình cảm vui tươi, dí dỏm, hài hước); and the folk song has the use of some dialect words (từ địa phương) such as: dia (về (return)), nam đáo nữ phòng (nam đến phòng nữ (the man comes to the woman's room)), tắt (nhất định, chắc chắn (definitely, certainly)); the lesson first reads the words by the rhythm, and has voice warm-up patterns 1 and 2 (on "Mi i mè è ma a mô ô" and "Nô ô…"), with the guidance: place the sound gently, the mouth opens naturally, the lower jaw relaxes softly, the face fresh (nét mặt tươi tắn), and practise the sound pattern ascending and descending stepwise by minor seconds in a fitting voice range; and the singing guidance: take a deep breath at the single rests and double rests (dấu lặng đơn, lặng đen), place the sound gently, pronounce the words clearly, sing with stronger emphasis into the head of each beat, the mouth opens naturally, relax the body when singing, and sing exactly the rhythm at beats 1 and 3, melisma (luyến) fully the notes of the song words "quạ", "nam", "nữ", "họ", "chẳng"; and the practice: practise singing the melodies below fluently, and show the folk song "Lí qua kêu" combined with beating thanh phách (clappers) or self-made instruments; and the application: choose one among the topics about homeland, country and friendship and set new words for the song "Lí qua kêu"; an overview of the history of Vietnamese music (sơ lược về lịch sử âm nhạc Việt Nam): the history of Vietnamese music goes through three large periods, those being: the period of building the country and keeping the country (thời kì dựng nước và giữ nước), the period of building an independent, autonomous feudal nation (thời kì xây dựng quốc gia phong kiến độc lập tự chủ), and the French colonial period and the struggle to gain independence, unification and build socialism (thời kì Pháp thuộc và đấu tranh giành độc lập, thống nhất xây dựng chủ nghĩa xã hội); 1. Music of the period of building the country and keeping the country (about from the 2nd millennium BC to the early 10th century): music of the Hùng Vương – An Dương Vương era (from the 2nd millennium BC to the 2nd century BC) — this is the period laying the foundation for the nation's cultural and music traditions: the music of the residents of Văn Lang – Âu Lạc is closely attached to labour life, many different music forms have formed and there are enough of the instrument types belonging to the families: the body-resonating family (họ tự thân vang) (bronze drums (trống đồng), stone instruments (đàn đá), gongs (cồng chiêng)), the membrane-ringing family (họ màng rung) (the types of skin drums (trống da)), and the air family (họ hơi) (gourd khene (khèn bầu), conch (tù và) and trumpets made of horn (kèn bằng sừng)) (a Đông Sơn bronze drum (Trống đồng Đông Sơn)); music of the era of Northern domination and fighting Northern domination (from the 2nd century BC to the early 10th century) — under Northern domination (Bắc thuộc), in the Northern region, the element of Việt – Hán cultural and art exchange appeared, the music and instruments of the Han people being imported into Vietnam such as: the pair of small cymbals (cặp chũm choẹ), the double-reed shawm (kèn đám kép),…, and in the Southern region, music has influence from Indian culture, imported instruments appearing such as the transverse flute (sáo ngang) and the Ghi năng drum (trống Ghi năng), in this stage the professional music foundation beginning to form and develop; 2. Music of the period of building an independent, autonomous feudal nation (the 10th century to the middle of the 19th century): the stage from the 10th century to the 15th century — under the dynasties of the Ngô, Đinh, Tiền Lê, Lý, Trần and Hồ houses, music developed brilliantly and there was exchange, absorbing from Chinese and Indian music, and in this stage two music streams (hai dòng nhạc) formed, folk music (âm nhạc dân gian) and court music (âm nhạc cung đình), court music and the chèo stage (sân khấu chèo) being born under the Lý dynasty; the stage from the 15th century to the middle of the 19th century — under the Hậu Lê and Nguyễn dynasties, the national folk music had new development steps: court music formed the folk music organisations such as Nhã nhạc (elegant music), Đại nhạc (great music), Tiểu nhạc (small music),…; folk music in the North has the Bát âm ensemble (dàn Bát âm), in the South there is the Ngũ âm ensemble (dàn Ngũ âm) of the Khmer people used in the festivals and funerals; some instruments imported from abroad appear such as đàn tranh, đàn tì bà, đàn nhị, hồ,…; and many traditional singing genres develop such as hát Cửa đình (communal house singing), Chèo, Tuồng, Ca Huế, Huế court elegant music (Nhã nhạc cung đình Huế),… (Huế court elegant music (Nhã nhạc cung đình Huế)); 3. Music of the French colonial period and the struggle to gain independence, unification and build socialism (from the middle of the 19th century to now): the French colonial stage (from the middle of the 19th century to before 1945) — this is the stage of spontaneous development of Vietnamese music before the strong influence of Western cultural art, especially French music, outstanding being the birth of the new music department (bộ phận nhạc mới), changing the basic face of our country's music: the composition movement by the Western European method and the birth of Reformed music (âm nhạc Cải cách) appeared, Vietnamese music absorbed the elements from the West and formed a new music foundation bearing the national style and soul (phong cách và tâm hồn dân tộc), many new songs being composed based on the major scale (thang âm, điệu thức trưởng), the Western major-minor system (thứ phương Tây) and the five-tone scale system (hệ thống thang năm âm), and composition tendencies appeared such as the romantic tendency (khuynh hướng lãng mạn), the patriotic epic tendency (khuynh hướng hùng ca yêu nước) and the revolutionary tendency (khuynh hướng cách mạng) with the famous songs such as "Bình minh" (Dawn) by musician Nguyễn Xuân Khoát – poem Thế Lữ, "Tiếng gọi thanh niên" (Call of Youth) by musician Lưu Hữu Phước, "Cùng nhau đi hồng binh" (Together Go, Red Soldiers) by musician Đinh Nhu, "Du kích ca" (Guerrilla Song) by musician Đỗ Nhuận,…; the stage of struggling to gain independence, unification and build socialism (from 1945 to now): the stage from 1945 to 1975 — in 1945, the August Revolution succeeded, revolutionary music (âm nhạc cách mạng) was officially born, the professional music foundation in the North also formed and developed, typical such as the songs "Bộ đội về làng" (Soldiers Return to the Village) by musician Lê Yên, "Trường ca sông Lô" (The Lô River Epic) by musician Văn Cao, "Cấy chiêm" (Planting the Fifth-Month Rice) by musician Tô Vũ – poem Quách Vinh, "Đóng nhanh lúa tốt" (Thresh Quickly, Good Rice) by musician Lê Lôi – poem Huyền Tâm, the cantata (thanh xướng kịch) "Nguyễn Văn Trỗi" by musician Đàm Linh, the opera (nhạc kịch) "Cô Sao" (Miss Sao) and "Người tạc tượng" (The Sculptor) by musician Đỗ Nhuận, "Bên bờ K'rông Pa" (On the Bank of the K'rông Pa) by musician Nhật Lai,…; in the South, many musicians succeeded with the songs on the resistance topic, typical being the songs "Lên ngàn" (Up to the Mountains) by musician Hoàng Việt, "Tiểu đoàn 307" (Battalion 307) by musician Nguyễn Hữu Trí, "Con kênh xanh xanh" (The Green Canal) by musician Ngô Huỳnh, "Tình đồng chí" (Comradeship) by musician Minh Quốc,…; and the instrumental music genres develop such as symphony, concerto, sonate; the stage from 1975 to now — from 1975, Vietnamese music achieved many new achievements, the composition topics mainly in this stage being praising the Party, Uncle Ho, the glorious feats of arms (chiến công lẫy lừng) of the resistance war against America, and love of homeland and country, typical being the songs such as: "Viếng lăng Bác" (Visiting Uncle Ho's Mausoleum) by musician Hoàng Hiệp, "Lời Bác dặn trước lúc đi xa" (Uncle Ho's Words Before Going Far Away) by musician Trần Hoàn, "Mùa xuân trên Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh" (Spring in Ho Chi Minh City) by musician Xuân Hồng, "Bài ca không quên" (The Unforgettable Song) by musician Phạm Minh Tuấn, "Quê hương" (Homeland) (music Giáp Văn Thạch, poem Đỗ Trung Quân),…; the field of training and performance achieved many new achievements, one of the most brilliant achievements being that in the year 1980, People's Artist Đặng Thái Sơn won first prize in the international piano competition named Chopin in Poland, the event being admired and esteemed by world friends; and from the year 2000 to now, music is rich and diverse in topics and content, the topics of history, love of homeland and country and national traditions always being highly proposed, many songs about protecting the sea and islands (bảo vệ biển đảo) encouraging the national spirit, many artists having performed successfully music works domestically and abroad, admired by the public, and the Classical chamber, folk and light music streams (dòng nhạc Cổ điển thính phòng, dân gian và nhạc nhẹ) developing in a balanced, harmonious way; and listening to the Aria Cô Sao by musician Đỗ Nhuận: "Aria Cô Sao" is a singing excerpt (khúc hát) in the opera (vở nhạc kịch) "Cô Sao" by musician Đỗ Nhuận — the opera includes 3 acts (3 màn), its content telling about the struggle of the compatriots and soldiers of the Northwest region (vùng Tây Bắc) against the French, the Japanese and their henchmen (bọn tay sai) and praising the beautiful love of the Thái ethnic girl named A Sao with Hà (a Viet Minh political prisoner (một tù nhân chính trị Việt Minh)); in 1965, the opera was premiered (công diễn) for the first time at the Hanoi Opera House (Nhà hát Lớn Hà Nội); later, "Aria Cô Sao" was separated into an independent work and performed on many professional music stages domestically and abroad; and the melody of the work has a lyrical, narrative character (trữ tình, tự sự) combined with the words showing lingering, yearning moods (tâm trạng day dứt, khắc khoải), the rhythm and tempo being changed continuously reflecting the inner agitation (sự xáo động nội tâm)

## Sub lesson 14 — Vietnam Grade 12 Music

"Hành khúc Thanh niên Cộng sản Hồ Chí Minh" (March of the Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth), music and words Văn Dung (1936–2022; author of the familiar revolutionary songs "Những bông hoa trong vườn Bác", "Đường Trường Sơn xe anh qua", "Bài ca đường 9 chiến thắng") — a youthful, healthy melody whose words urge union members and youth to live and work by Uncle Ho's ideology to build an ever richer, more beautiful Vietnam; voice warm-ups, singing guidance, practising the melody's high-register notes and far interval leaps, performing in different singing forms and with body movement; "Ta tự hào đi lên, ôi Việt Nam!" (We Proudly Go Up, Oh Vietnam!), music Chu Minh, words from the poem of Hoàng Trung Thông — an epic song (hùng ca) praising the golden victory milestones of the Vietnamese nation in the resistance war against America, composed in 1972 as the capital Hanoi and the Northern provinces faced the B.52 aircraft campaign; a majestic, spacious melody at a moderate beat carrying the nation's resilient will and pride, awakening millions of hearts towards the struggle for national liberation and independence; "Hoa đào bên suối" (Peach Blossom by the Stream), a folk song of the Giáy people (dân ca Giáy), collected and recorded by Nguyễn Tài Tuệ — a singing tune of the Giáy ethnic people whose sweet melodies and clear, dreamy words blend the listener into the fresh, beautiful natural scenery of the Northwest mountains and forests; voice warm-ups and singing guidance (stronger emphasis into the first note of the melisma words, exact rhythm figures, full melisma notes); practising melodies with much melisma; setting new words for the folk song; singing songs bearing folk material (hát ca khúc mang chất liệu dân gian) — the characteristics of this singing way (musicians exploit some folk music element to form a new melody line; a soft, lyrical character, melodies with many melisma and grace sounds, nonsense syllables, auxiliary words and syncopation rhythms; profound, rustic, simple words close to the public; the singer shows sweetness and profundity, valuing the melisma and grace sounds and clear pronunciation; opening examples "Đất nước lời ru" by Văn Thành Nho, "Chiếc khăn Piêu" by Doãn Nho, "Khúc hát sông quê" by Nguyễn Trọng Tạo, "Dáng đứng Bến Tre" by Nguyễn Văn Tý) + "Chiều sông Thương" (Afternoon on the Thương River), music An Thuyên (1949–2015), words from the poem of Hữu Thỉnh (born 1942) — a lyrical, soft melody bearing chèo material, clear words rich in imagery (the autumn afternoon, the brown river carrying sunlight far away, the lush green rice fields) evoking the peaceful countryside with its prosperous, hopeful life (guidance: melisma at bars 11, 13, 17, 19, 20, two-part voice blending at the chorus, a graceful, flowing walking figure) + "Quê hương ba miền" (Homeland of Three Regions), music and words Thanh Sơn (1938–2012) — one of the few songs converging the folk-song characteristics of all three regions, North, Central and South, its words evoking the countrysides and beautiful famous landscapes of Vietnam (guidance: showing each region's folk character correctly) + practising the regional folk colours (Northern folk-style melisma material and chèo syncopation-rhythm material in "Chiều sông Thương"; ca Huế melisma material and Southern folk-song melisma material in "Quê hương ba miền"); singing Vietnamese folk songs (hát dân ca Việt Nam) — the characteristics of the folk-song singing way (folk songs are songs created by the working people, transmitted from generation to generation with a heritage character, attached to the cultural and spiritual life of the people in every region of Vietnam; this one grows from the folk verse of climbing the Thiên Thai mountain and seeing a pair of phoenixes eating ripe mangoes, a verse that from North to Centre to South became many repertoires with the same content and different melodies; singing guidance) + "Cây trúc xinh" (The Pretty Bamboo) (a Quan họ Bắc Ninh folk song), collected and recorded by Nguyễn Ngọc Oánh (a soft, lyrical melody, its words taken from the verse of the pretty bamboo growing beside the communal house; the "chị Hai" image in it also appears in many other Quan họ songs, a characteristic of the Kinh Bắc countryside; guidance for a sweet, profound performance with soft, graceful, flowing gestures; setting new words for either song)
