# Anthems and Songs That Teach

## Do it first

Stand up, straight and still, as if a flag were being raised. That posture is part of an anthem before a single note is sung. Sit down — now let us learn what anthems are, and which ones are ours.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our School and Protect Our Heritage

**"Our School" (ትምህርት ቤታችን)** praises the **school compound** and teaches **caring for it** — watering plants, working together, keeping it beautiful **for the next generation**. Then the song of heritage: **"Our Heritage Shall Be Protected"** — heritages are the **nation's wealth and identity marks**, to be guarded by all, with an azmari refrain. It names the treasures: **Aksum, Lalibela, the Sof Omar caves, Jegol and the Fasil castles**. A song that lists what must not be lost is a guard posted in melody.

## Sub lesson 2 — Our Region's Anthem and Our Country's Anthem

A region, too, sings itself. The **Amhara regional anthem**, **"የታታሪ ህዝቦች" (The Diligent Peoples)**, sings the region as archive of history and cradle of culture, its peoples building together in **peace, justice and equality**. Above it stands the **Ethiopian national anthem**: **citizenship's honour, peoples' freedom and equality**, a cradle of culture and heritage, and a trust — 'we shall protect you, we have a trust'. Both anthems are printed in full in the book and are **placed here by name and role only**. The national anthem is sung **standing**, at school and on the country's great occasions — your posture in the Do It First was its first lesson.

## Sub lesson 3 — Honour the Elders: The Elders' Counsel

**"The Elders' Counsel" (የአረጋውያን መዝሙር)** is a praise song with azmari refrains. Its teaching: **elders' counsel is sweet as honey**. Elders were **yesterday's teachers, boundary-keepers, enemy-frighteners, doctors, farmers, singers and drivers** — every duty the community needed, they carried it first. The song's command is simple: **honour them and listen, to be fruitful**. A community that stops listening to its elders cuts its own root.

## Sub lesson 4 — There Are Professionals; Protect the Forests

**"There Are Professionals"** appreciates the makers: professionals in **every kebele and woreda** whose products serve from square to home — the **carpenter's table and chair, the weaver's gabi and netela, the blacksmith's gold earrings, the builder** — all professionals **equal in their work**. (The book credits the song: melody Alemayehu W/Yohannes, poem Asefa Bekele; the Grade 6 book meets the same song again.) **"Let Us Protect the Forests"** sings the forest as the base of **building, farming tools and the weaver's gear**: plant seedlings everywhere, water and tend them, **make planting a culture** — and the Grade 5 book answers with **"The Forest Is for My Life"**: the forest as medicine for hunger and protection of soil and water, with **"Good Ethics"** beside it — let lying and gossip end, let work be honoured.

## Sub lesson 5 — Festival songs of the year

The year itself has a songbook. **NEW YEAR**: women hold flowers and sing **Enqutatash**, announcing the year and wishing **peace and love**. **MESKEL** (Meskerem 17) is counted as New Year by the Gurage community: the Awi wear jano, carry cut wet trees to relatives' homes as gifts, eat the traditional abzi food and celebrate singing **"eyohe"**. **GENNA**: young men hold bent gena sticks, hold contests, then celebrate singing and dancing. **EID AL-FITR**: Muslims **share with those who have not** (zakat al-fitr) and give gifts, and the **menzuma** is presented. Four festivals, four musics — you met their family in Traditional and Festival Songs; here they stand in the year's own order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one song from this lesson and its whole message in two sentences. They must say whether it is an anthem, a praise song or a teaching song — and why.
