# Pese Samoa and Fa'asala

## Do it first

Say a sentence out loud. Hear how your voice rises and falls with the words? In Samoa's senior composition lesson, that rise and fall is not an accident — it builds the song.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a pese Sāmoa is

The Years 11–12 Music Teacher Guide's composition example teaches the **pese Sāmoa** by its characteristics: **its melody is determined by its text**; it is **modal with a strong tonal centre**; it **uses two chords only (usually I and V)**; **the melody can begin on any pitch and ends on the tonic**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building one together

The guide gives **the authentic way**: **one melody, another part to support it, and a fa'asala descant over the main melody**. The class work follows it step by step: **the class composes a melody together, supports it with another part, and each pupil writes their own fa'asala** — the crowning line is each composer's own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: list the pese Sāmoa's four characteristics, and name the three parts of the authentic way. Companion checks: text-led melody, modal, two chords, ends on the tonic? Melody, support, fa'asala?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four characteristics of a pese Sāmoa.
2. Speak a line of your own words and mark where your voice rises and falls.
3. Write one sentence: what is a fa'asala, and where does it sing?
