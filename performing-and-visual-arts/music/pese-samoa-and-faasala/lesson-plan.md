# Lesson plan — Pese Samoa and Fa'asala

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Music · **Lesson:** Pese Samoa and Fa'asala
**Place in the curriculum:** Music (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** composing a pese Sāmoa — a Samoan song whose melody is determined by its text, modal with a strong tonal centre and two chords only — and crowning it with a fa'asala descant.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of its title — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a pese Sāmoa is** — its characteristics — the melody is determined by the text, it is modal with a strong tonal centre, it uses two chords only (usually I and V), the melody can begin on any pitch and ends on the tonic.
2. **Building one together** — the authentic way has one melody, another part to support it, and a fa'asala descant over the main melody; the class composes a melody together, supports it, and each pupil writes their own fa'asala.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the characteristics of a pese Sāmoa.
2. Say the three parts of the authentic way and what a fa'asala is.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the melody comes first and words are fitted later → in a pese Sāmoa the melody is DETERMINED BY THE TEXT: the words lead.
- Thinks more chords means a better song → the pese Sāmoa uses two chords only, usually I and V.
