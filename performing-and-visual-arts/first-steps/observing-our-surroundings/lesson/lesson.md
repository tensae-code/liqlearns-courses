# Observing Our Surroundings

## Do it first

Stand at your door or your window. Look slowly, all around. What do you see? A house. A tree. An animal. A person walking. Now close your eyes for three breaths and only listen. What do you hear? You have just done the first art skill of this course: **observing our surroundings** (አካባቢን ማስተዋል).

## Sub lesson 1 — Observing our surroundings

Our **surroundings** (አካባቢ) are everything around us. The book's own summary names them: **houses, animals, people, birds, plants, cars and carts**.

Observing means looking with care, on purpose. The chapter's aims are the observer's jobs: name the **sounds** of the things around you, name their **colours**, name their **shapes**; talk about the pictures you observe; sing back a song you have heard; and imitate the movements and sounds of things, of family members, of animals and of plants.

Some things around us have their own **sounds**, and some things **move** from place to place — the book's summary asks us to notice which are which. A hen has a sound and moves. A house has no sound of its own and stays. Noticing that difference is observing.

The chapter closes with its review, folded in here: imitate a seen animal's movement, a heard animal's sound, a family member's voice — and sing a favourite song together. Observing first, imitating second: that order runs through this whole course.

## Sub lesson 2 — Watching and telling what happens

Observing grows a second step: watch something **happening**, and **tell the action**.

In the book's picture page, a girl pushes her classmate in a wheelchair, and two children read a book together. Nothing there is just a 'thing' — each picture is somebody **doing** something. The observer's sentence is always the same shape: **who** — **is doing what**.

Practise it anywhere. A woman is carrying water. A boy is sweeping the yard. Oxen are pulling a plough. First watch in silence. Then tell it, one true sentence at a time. The Grade 1 teacher guide teaches the same skill as finding the **actions and the message** in what we watch (teacher guide — evidence).

## Say it to your companion

Watch one thing happening near you with your companion. Take turns: one watches and tells — who, doing what — and the other listens and says if the telling is true and complete.

## Do It Yourself

1. At your door, name five things in your surroundings, with each one's colour and shape.
2. Close your eyes and listen. Name three sounds and what makes each one.
3. Watch one action for a full minute. Tell it in one sentence: who is doing what.
4. Sort six things around you: which have their own sound, and which move from place to place?
