# Appreciating Ethiopian Traditional Dance

## Do it first

Watch dancers in your mind at a festival. You have joined such a dance. This lesson asks you to do the other half: appreciate it as art.

## Sub lesson 1 — Traditional dance as an art (teacher guide — evidence)

*This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence — a single guide page, taught as the slim evidence it is.* The Grade 3 guide (p. 73) sets **Ethiopian traditional dance art** (የኢትዮጵያ ባህላዊ የውዝዋዜ ጥበባት) before the learner **to be appreciated as an art**: the country's traditional dances are not only customs to join but **artworks to value** — watched with the same respect a painting is watched with.

Appreciating, here, has its usual shape in this course: look closely, name what the dancers do, and say what it is worth — the steps' skill, the costume's craft, the occasion's meaning. The guide's point is the placement of dance itself: among the arts, honoured as one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: the Grade 3 guide's appreciation of Ethiopia's traditional dance as an art — the country's dance, valued as art (teacher-guide evidence). Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write or say the lesson's key list from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. Show one part of the lesson with your hands, your voice or a drawing — as the part itself asks.
3. Teach the part you found hardest to someone at home, in three sentences.
