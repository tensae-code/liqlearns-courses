# Appreciating and Valuing the Arts

## Do it first

Hold two pictures side by side in your mind — one you love, one that leaves you cold. Now say WHY, in words another person could argue with. That 'why' is aesthetic judgement, and this lesson is its school.

## Sub lesson 1 — Aesthetic value: judging beauty

The Grade 7 student book's judgement chapter: **aesthetic value (የውበት እሴትና ፍርድ) — value and judgement of beauty**. Its terms: **criteria** for judging, **judgement** itself, **valuing artwork**, the relations of **art and nature**, and the figures of the judgement world — **art critics** and **art movements**. The discipline underneath all of them: a judgement is not a shrug. It is a claim about a work, carried by **reasons** drawn from the work itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Appreciation in Grades 7–8 (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)

The senior guides continue appreciation as its own study. Grade 7's later units (from its **contents**): **heredity in art, the aesthetics of art, appreciating an artwork, and good aesthetic education** — appreciation taught as a skill with its own lessons. Grade 8's (from its **contents**): **the process of valuing, aesthetician philosophers** (named in its syllabus as Plato, Aristotle, Kant, Hegel and Marx), and **art's place in society**. Both guides' files end before these units' bodies, so these topics stand on **contents and syllabus evidence only** — EVIDENCE-LIMITED, exactly as labelled.

## Sub lesson 3 — Appreciating visual practices (teacher guide — evidence)

The federal guide's appreciation turns systematic: **appreciating visual-art practices** — **perceiving** the work, **analysing** it, and **judging** it; **what in an artwork deserves appreciation**; **aesthetic taste**; **educating society aesthetically**; and **assessing aesthetic value**. Five moves, in order: look, take apart, judge, taste, teach. Appreciation here is not a feeling that visits you; it is a practice with steps.

## Sub lesson 4 — Cultural diversity in folk art (teacher guide — evidence)

Beside appreciation stands its widest lesson: **cultural diversity in the arts**. The guide teaches **folk arts** — their **meaning** and **historical development** — then **cultural diversity** itself, and **diversity in folk art**: in **form, in function, in material, in context** — and the **expressions of diversity** in the arts. The four diversities are the valuing tool of this whole course: when you meet another people's art, ask its form, its function, its material and its context before you dare a judgement.

## Sub lesson 5 — Aesthetic valuing and African aesthetics (teacher guide — evidence)

The senior strand closes at home: **aesthetic valuing** as its own lesson, and **an introduction to African aesthetics — valuing beauty in the African context**. After the philosophers of other continents (Grade 8's syllabus), the final word is Africa's own: beauty judged with Africa's materials, functions and meanings in the judge's hand. The course's first steps end where they should — the learner able to value the art of their own continent in its own terms, with reasons.

## Say it to your companion

Judge one artwork each, with reasons. Your companion must answer your reason — agree and add, or disagree and counter — before the next work may be judged.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a five-line appreciation of one local craft piece, with three reasons.
2. List three folk arts of three different peoples and one value in each.
3. Judge your own class's exhibition: best work, and the reason, in writing.
