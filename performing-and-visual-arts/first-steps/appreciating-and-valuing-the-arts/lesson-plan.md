# Lesson plan — Appreciating and Valuing the Arts

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Appreciating and Valuing the Arts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** learning to judge and honour art — aesthetic value and how beauty is judged, the appreciation and valuing topics of the senior grades, appreciating the visual practices around us, cultural diversity in folk art, and African aesthetics: valuing art in African terms.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands at the head of the course as the appreciation strand's senior capstone — every earlier lesson made or met art; this one teaches judging it and valuing it, the way the senior guides teach.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Aesthetic value: judging beauty** — aesthetic value and judgement (የውበት እሴትና ፍርድ) — criteria, judgement, valuing artwork, art–nature relations, art critics and movements (Grade 7, printed pp. 127–131).
2. **Appreciation in Grades 7–8 (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)** — Grade 7 Units 4–5 appreciation topics — heredity in art, art and aesthetics, appreciating an artwork, good aesthetic education; Grade 8 Units 4–5 — the process of valuing, aesthetician philosophers, art's place in society (Grade 7 teacher guide contents, printed pp. 110–149; Grade 8 teacher guide contents, printed pp. 57–102).
3. **Appreciating visual practices (teacher guide — evidence)** — appreciating visual-art practices — perceiving, analysing and judging, what in an artwork deserves appreciation, aesthetic taste, educating society aesthetically, assessing aesthetic value (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 32–33, printed pp. 56–58).
4. **Cultural diversity in folk art (teacher guide — evidence)** — folk arts' meaning and historical development; diversity in folk art's form, function, material and context; expressions of diversity (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 34–37, printed pp. 58–64).
5. **Aesthetic valuing and African aesthetics (teacher guide — evidence)** — aesthetic valuing; an introduction to African aesthetics — valuing beauty in the African context (Grade 10 teacher guide, Lessons 33–34, printed pp. 57–61).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: every built lesson of this course — appreciation grows on making and knowing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what aesthetic value is and judge a work with reasons.
2. Name the appreciation topics of the Grade 7–8 units (evidence-limited where the guides are truncated).
3. Appreciate visual practices: say what in a work deserves valuing.
4. Value cultural diversity in folk art as a strength.
5. Say what African aesthetics asks: art valued in African terms.

## What you need
Artworks to judge: reproductions, local crafts, performances the class has made — and the courage to give reasons for a judgement.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Hold two pictures side by side in your mind — one you love, one that leaves you cold. Now say WHY, in words another person could argue with. That 'why' is aesthetic judgement, and this lesson is its school. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md), with these add-on slots at their exact places:

## If the learner is stuck
- 'I just like it' with no reason → the lesson's discipline: every judgement carries a because — point to line, colour, craft, feeling or meaning and argue from it.
- Judging another culture's art by home rules alone → the diversity part: folk art is valued inside its own people's terms first, then compared.
