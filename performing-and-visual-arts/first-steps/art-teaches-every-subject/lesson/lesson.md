# Art Teaches Every Subject

## Do it first

Name your hardest subject. Now ask: could a song, a picture or a play carry one piece of it to you? This lesson is the oldest book's answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Art education and every subject

The Grade 8 book's links chapter (§5.1) builds toward a closing claim this lesson carries: **art education relates to every kind of subject** — performing art with natural science, social science, mathematics and language; visual art with the same wide set — **directly or indirectly**, art serves them all.

That is the claim the parts of *How the Arts Help Us Learn* prove piece by piece. This short lesson stands at the chapter's end for its last word — the relation that makes the set complete.

## Sub lesson 2 — Art education and special-needs education

The chapter's very last sub-section (ሠ, printed p. 133, with its Figure 61) turns to the learners every other section could miss: **art education's relation with special-needs education**.

Its teaching is the chapter's kindest logic. A learner who cannot take a subject by the usual road — by long text, by quick hearing, by still sitting — may take it **by art's roads**: by doing, by picturing, by singing, by handling. Art education does not only decorate the curriculum for these learners; it can be the **way in**. The Grade 8 book ends its links chapter here on purpose: the set is not complete until every learner is inside it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: art education reaches every subject — and every learner, including learners with special needs, for whom art is a road in (Grade 8 evidence). Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write or say the lesson's key list from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. Show one part of the lesson with your hands, your voice or a drawing — as the part itself asks.
3. Teach the part you found hardest to someone at home, in three sentences.
