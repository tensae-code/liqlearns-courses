# Lesson plan — Looking at Pictures

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Looking at Pictures
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** to read a picture the way we read a page — say what it shows, and say what it tells us.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Observing Our Surroundings — the same careful looking, turned onto a picture.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What a picture shows** — Naming and recognising everything in the picture — the visual-perception skill.
2. **What a picture tells us** — The picture's message: the family scenes, and what we understood from them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Observing Our Surroundings. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Look at a picture slowly and name everything it shows.
2. Say what the people in a picture are doing.
3. Tell the picture's message — what you understood from it.
4. Say what things you do in your own surroundings, like the people in the picture.

## What you need
Any picture — in a book, on a wall, or the picture in this lesson.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Look at the picture in this lesson for a slow count of ten. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts of lesson/lesson.md, in order.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9003]: a picture of a mother serving food to her children at home — the student names who, where and what, then tells the picture's message.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice asking the picture questions slowly — 'Who is there? … Where are they? … What are they doing? … What does it tell us?' — the student answers each aloud.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Show your companion a picture. (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists things but finds no message → ask 'why did the picture-maker show THIS moment? What does it make you feel or know?'
- Guesses without looking → send the eyes back: 'Point at the part of the picture that tells you that.'
