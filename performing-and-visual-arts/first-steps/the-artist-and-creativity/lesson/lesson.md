## Sub lesson 1 — Художник и творчество. Професията на художника (The Artist and Creativity. The Artist's Profession)

why people create: art as the striving to understand the world, oneself and the relations between people, and to express it in signs and images; the ability to create art is some 35,000 years old, and the first creators were probably shamans and priests (the mythical Thracian priest-king and singer Orpheus, whose lyre charmed even the gods — told in summary); creativity as discovery rather than making — Michelangelo "freeing" the figure from the amorphous mass of marble (his Dying Slave, 1513–1516), and the Pygmalion story (Gérôme's painting, 1890) as the emblem of the creative process; the great creators called geniuses, bearing a gift from a higher power that finds expression in moments of inspiration — inspiration as a sudden revelation of the imagination that almost always comes after long, strenuous work on a problem; virtuoso mastery of technique (Max Ernst, Picasso's Weeping Woman, 1937) as the mark of the great masters, who reach their peak at different ages; and the map of the artist's professions — architecture (civil, landscape, industrial, transport and interior), the decorative arts in synthesis with architecture (ceramics, woodcarving, metalwork, mosaic, stained glass), design (graphic, spatial, advertising, product, interior, digital, fashion and textile), fine art without utilitarian function together with its new forms (art photography, performance, installation), the artist-teacher whose mission is to develop the creativity of the pupils — without creativity a person becomes an instrument executing another's will — and the students of art themselves: art historians who research and teach, and art critics who interpret and evaluate contemporary works

## Sub lesson 2 — Живот и творчество на художника (The Life and Work of the Artist)

the artist as a public personality formed by a definite environment and conditions, who in turn shapes his society: some creators are strongly socially engaged (Goya, Delacroix, Picasso), others deliberately distance themselves from reality (the Romantic, Cubist, Expressionist and Surrealist authors); the creative path (творчески път) — the consecutive stages in an author's creative development — and the creative process — the specific activity through which a person's abilities are maximally expressed in works distinguished by novelty and originality; the discoveries artists give the world (the Van Eyck brothers perfecting oil technique, Leonardo's ideas for the metro, the diving suit, flying machines and the tank, Uccello and Dürer exploring optics, perspective and plastic anatomy, the Impressionists embodying the discoveries of physics); Rembrandt, who after the failure of "The Night Watch" (1642) lost his position in society, withdrew to Antwerp and there began the most mature stage of his path; Van Gogh, unrecognised in life, whose works today cost millions; the stages of the creative process — accumulated life and creative experience and the gathering of information, the birth of the idea (the artist choosing from many ideas the one that will reach the end), realisation (the final result often differing from what was expected, and that moment giving the greatest satisfaction), and the last stage, when the work reaches those it is meant for; and the four aspects by which creativity is studied — the person, the process, the product and the environment ("press") — the same NEW lesson as proposed from the Grade 9 book (The Artist and Creativity), here stocked with the artist's biography and the stages of the creative process
