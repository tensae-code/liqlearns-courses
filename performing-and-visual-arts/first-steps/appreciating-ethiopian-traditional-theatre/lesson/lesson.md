# Appreciating Ethiopian Traditional Theatre

## Do it first

The appreciation set is almost complete: music valued, dance valued. This lesson values the third — the people's own stage.

## Sub lesson 1 — Traditional theatre as an art (teacher guide — evidence)

*This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence.* The Grade 3 guide (pp. 74–76) completes its appreciation set — music, dance, now **theatre**: **Ethiopian traditional theatre**, the community's own stage tradition, set before the learner **to be appreciated as an art**.

Traditional theatre holds what the community holds: its stories, its speech, its humour and its judgements, acted by its own people in its own places. To appreciate it is to see that a people's stage — however simple its boards — is an art with its own masters and its own memory, not a rehearsal for somebody else's theatre.

## Sub lesson 2 — The named play — 'እኔ ነኝ ውሮ' (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide does not leave traditional theatre general: it names a **traditional play extract — "እኔ ነኝ ውሮ"** (§4.3.1, printed p. 75; the title as printed). This lesson places the play the way the course places every named work: **by name and purpose only** — a traditional play, named in the teacher's book as the example through which learners meet and appreciate traditional theatre. Its text is not transcribed here.

A name like this is a handle on a heritage. When you meet the play performed — or an elder who knows it — you will already know what you are seeing: Ethiopia's traditional theatre, with its own named works, appreciated as art.
