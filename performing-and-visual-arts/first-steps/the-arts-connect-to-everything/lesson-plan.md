# Lesson plan — The Arts Connect to Everything

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** The Arts Connect to Everything
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the arts holding hands with every other subject — visual and performing arts woven into the whole curriculum, and the arts' place in society and in education: proof that this course was never a decoration on the school, but one of its threads.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands at the head of the course as the connection strand's capstone, resting on the federal guides' own closing units — the learner has the whole course behind them and now sees its threads run into every other subject.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Visual arts with other subjects (teacher guide — evidence)** — the integration of visual arts with other subjects — visual-art concepts and skills serving the whole curriculum (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 39–40, printed pp. 69–71).
2. **Performing arts with other subjects (teacher guide — evidence)** — the integration of performing arts with other subjects — drama, song and movement carrying other subjects' content (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 41–45, printed pp. 71–77).
3. **The arts in society and in education (teacher guide — evidence)** — performing and visual arts in society — concepts and theories of their role — and in education (Grade 10 teacher guide, Lessons 35–42, printed pp. 61–78).
4. **Connections in Grades 7–8 (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)** — Grade 7 Unit 5 — performing and visual arts' relation with other subjects, and good aesthetic education; Grade 8 Unit 5 — values education through the arts (Grade 7 teacher guide contents, printed pp. 110–149; Grade 8 teacher guide contents, printed pp. 57–102).

5. **Visual art's links — the map, the scale law, technology, and teaching's institutions** — the Grade 7 book's §5.2 set: the link map (p. 152 in the book's own pagination; the audit brief lists p. 148 — see sources), enlarging and reducing by the law of scale, social science and technology, and visual art in the teaching process (printed pp. 152–156).

6. **Grade 8 Unit 5 — the arts in other subjects (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 8 guide's Unit 5, placed from its contents only (the guide's body ends at p. 56): music with other subjects (pp. 82–87), dance in other subjects (pp. 88–93), music connected to learning (pp. 89–90), theatre and film in other subjects (pp. 94–95), visual arts in other subjects (pp. 96–102) (Grade 8 teacher guide, contents, printed pp. 82–102).

7. **Visual art and natural science — the spectrum** — the Grade 8 book: visual art's clear relation with natural-science education — spectrum-colour creation, things' natural forms, and colour-combination reactions (Grade 8, printed p. 128).

8. **The Grade 6 chapter opens — art among the other subjects (chapter opening — Grade 6)** — the Grade 6 book's Chapter 5 opener and aims: visual art's relation with other subjects; visual works protecting heritage and teaching useful issues; music's link with other subjects; learning heritage, health and the forest through chant (Grade 6, printed p. 99).

9. **The Grade 6 chapter closes — art in daily life and every subject (chapter closing — Grade 6)** — the Grade 6 book's Chapter 5 summary, its TWO self-check tables and its closing exercise — 4 true/false and 2 short answers, one a creative group task on two local religious festivals (Grade 6, printed pp. 112–115).

10. **The Grade 7 chapter opens — decorating, and art among the subjects (chapter opening — Grade 7)** — the Grade 7 book's Chapter 5 opener and aims: decorate the home and environment with pottery and ceramic works; explain art's relations with other subjects; understand art's role and benefit in the teaching process (Grade 7, printed p. 148).

11. **The Grade 7 chapter closes — check and exercise (chapter closing — Grade 7)** — the Grade 7 book's Chapter 5 self-check table (3 named items) and closing exercise — 3 true/false and 3 short answers (Grade 7, printed pp. 168–169).

12. **The Grade 8 chapter opens — art education among the subjects (chapter opening — Grade 8)** — the Grade 8 book's Chapter 5 opener and aims: understand art education's relation with varied subject kinds and its benefit for teaching other subjects easily; its relation with current issues; appreciate works in both families; make the community grasp current issues through the arts (Grade 8, printed p. 120).

13. **The Grade 8 chapter closes — art relates to everything (chapter closing — Grade 8)** — the Grade 8 book's Chapter 5 summary, its ✓/✗ checklist (7 items) and its closing exercises: explain art's relation with four subjects, and make its relation with current issues in picture and chant (Grade 8, printed pp. 136–137).

## If the learner is stuck
- 'Art is separate' → hunt the hiding places: every diagram is drawing, every story is drama's cousin, every pattern is design. The guides' integration lessons exist because the connections are real.
- Overclaiming from the truncated units → Grade 7–8 Unit 5 topics are named from contents only and labelled EVIDENCE-LIMITED; the lesson does not pretend to their bodies.
