# The Arts Connect to Everything

## Do it first

Open any other schoolbook — science, language, mathematics. Find one picture, one diagram, one story, one song. You have just found this course hiding inside that one. This lesson maps those hiding places.

## Sub lesson 1 — Visual arts with other subjects (teacher guide — evidence)

The federal guide devotes lessons to **the integration of visual arts with other subjects** — visual-art concepts and skills **serving the whole curriculum**. Every diagram a science pupil reads was drawn; every map a geography pupil uses was designed; every letterform a language pupil writes was shaped. The visual strand of this course is therefore not one subject among others: it is the **picture-language all the others speak**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Performing arts with other subjects (teacher guide — evidence)

Five lessons — the guide's longest integration run — teach **the integration of performing arts with other subjects**: drama, song and movement **carrying other subjects' content**. A history scene acted is history remembered in the body; a multiplication song is arithmetic that sings itself; a health drama teaches what a paragraph cannot. Performing art is the memory and the feeling of the curriculum — the subjects enter through it and stay.

## Sub lesson 3 — The arts in society and in education (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 guide closes the whole federal course with the arts' **place**: **the performing and visual arts in society** — the **concepts and theories** of their role in the community's life — and **the performing and visual arts in education**. It is the grown-up form of this course's own Culture and Values unit: art expressing, remembering, gathering, earning, speaking — now studied as society's structure, and as the school's own foundation.

## Sub lesson 4 — Connections in Grades 7–8 (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)

The connection theme is already present in the middle guides — named here from their **contents pages only**, because those files are truncated: Grade 7's Unit 5 teaches **performing and visual arts and their relation with other subjects**, and **good aesthetic education**; Grade 8's Unit 5 turns to **values education through the arts**. Even unfinished, the contents tell the story: from Grade 7 upward, the curriculum itself insists the arts connect to everything — and to the values a school exists to teach.

## Sub lesson 5 — Visual art's links — the map, the scale law, technology, and teaching's institutions

The Grade 7 book (§5.2) draws visual art's links as a **map**: visual art **expresses thought, feeling, desire, view, knowledge and identity**, and — directly or indirectly — it has a high relation with the other subjects around it. Learn the map first; the next three steps walk its roads.

**Mathematics:** knowing artistic concepts strengthens the thinking and reasoning used in maths, and by the **law of scale** we **enlarge and reduce** pictures exactly — the map-reader's and the designer's shared law. **Social science and technology:** a country's **history and social order are shown, told, introduced and passed down** through visual works — and the same visual skill serves the technology that prints, builds and machines. **The teaching process itself:** for teachers, visual art enables **activity-based, unforgettable, participatory, concrete teaching** — and the institutions of education, from the classroom wall to the textbook, run on it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Grade 8 Unit 5 — the arts in other subjects (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)

*This part is EVIDENCE-LIMITED: the Grade 8 guide's body in the library file ends at printed p. 56, so Unit 5 stands on its contents and syllabus only — the contents lines themselves are what is taught here.* The contents promise five connections. **Music with other subjects and cross-cutting issues** (pp. 82–87): connect, relate and apply music's concepts across the curriculum. **Dance in other subjects** (pp. 88–93): popular styles of dance, the characteristics of cultural dance, the elements of dance, and dance's significance inside music. **Music connected to learning** (pp. 89–90): integrating music and science, the way of learning music history, and the very meaning of subject integration. **Theatre and film/video in other subjects** (pp. 94–95). **Visual arts in other subjects and cross-cutting issues** (pp. 96–102).

Learn these as the guide's own table of promises: each line names a connection the full guide teaches. Where the body is missing, this lesson says so — the contents are evidence of the connection's place, not of its full text.

## Sub lesson 7 — Visual art and natural science — the spectrum

The Grade 8 book (§5.1.2) gives visual art its science road: visual art has a **clear relation with natural-science education** in **spectrum-colour creation** — the colours of the spectrum, in their order (red, orange, yellow, green and onward, as a prism spreads them) — in the drawing of **things' natural forms**, and in **colour-combination reactions**, what colours do to each other when they meet.

Learn the meeting plainly. Science names what light does; visual art **does it on paper** — splits it, orders it, mixes it and watches the reaction. The spectrum is a science fact you can paint, and a painting lesson you can measure.
