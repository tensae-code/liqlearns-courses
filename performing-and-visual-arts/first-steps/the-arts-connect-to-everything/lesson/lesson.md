# The Arts Connect to Everything

## Do it first

Open any other schoolbook — science, language, mathematics. Find one picture, one diagram, one story, one song. You have just found this course hiding inside that one. This lesson maps those hiding places.

## Sub lesson 1 — Visual arts with other subjects (teacher guide — evidence)

The federal guide devotes lessons to **the integration of visual arts with other subjects** — visual-art concepts and skills **serving the whole curriculum**. Every diagram a science pupil reads was drawn; every map a geography pupil uses was designed; every letterform a language pupil writes was shaped. The visual strand of this course is therefore not one subject among others: it is the **picture-language all the others speak**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Performing arts with other subjects (teacher guide — evidence)

Five lessons — the guide's longest integration run — teach **the integration of performing arts with other subjects**: drama, song and movement **carrying other subjects' content**. A history scene acted is history remembered in the body; a multiplication song is arithmetic that sings itself; a health drama teaches what a paragraph cannot. Performing art is the memory and the feeling of the curriculum — the subjects enter through it and stay.

## Sub lesson 3 — The arts in society and in education (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 guide closes the whole federal course with the arts' **place**: **the performing and visual arts in society** — the **concepts and theories** of their role in the community's life — and **the performing and visual arts in education**. It is the grown-up form of this course's own Culture and Values unit: art expressing, remembering, gathering, earning, speaking — now studied as society's structure, and as the school's own foundation.

## Sub lesson 4 — Connections in Grades 7–8 (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)

The connection theme is already present in the middle guides — named here from their **contents pages only**, because those files are truncated: Grade 7's Unit 5 teaches **performing and visual arts and their relation with other subjects**, and **good aesthetic education**; Grade 8's Unit 5 turns to **values education through the arts**. Even unfinished, the contents tell the story: from Grade 7 upward, the curriculum itself insists the arts connect to everything — and to the values a school exists to teach.

## Sub lesson 5 — Visual art's links — the map, the scale law, technology, and teaching's institutions

The Grade 7 book (§5.2) draws visual art's links as a **map**: visual art **expresses thought, feeling, desire, view, knowledge and identity**, and — directly or indirectly — it has a high relation with the other subjects around it. Learn the map first; the next three steps walk its roads.

**Mathematics:** knowing artistic concepts strengthens the thinking and reasoning used in maths, and by the **law of scale** we **enlarge and reduce** pictures exactly — the map-reader's and the designer's shared law. **Social science and technology:** a country's **history and social order are shown, told, introduced and passed down** through visual works — and the same visual skill serves the technology that prints, builds and machines. **The teaching process itself:** for teachers, visual art enables **activity-based, unforgettable, participatory, concrete teaching** — and the institutions of education, from the classroom wall to the textbook, run on it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Grade 8 Unit 5 — the arts in other subjects (EVIDENCE-LIMITED, teacher guide — evidence)

*This part is EVIDENCE-LIMITED: the Grade 8 guide's body in the library file ends at printed p. 56, so Unit 5 stands on its contents and syllabus only — the contents lines themselves are what is taught here.* The contents promise five connections. **Music with other subjects and cross-cutting issues** (pp. 82–87): connect, relate and apply music's concepts across the curriculum. **Dance in other subjects** (pp. 88–93): popular styles of dance, the characteristics of cultural dance, the elements of dance, and dance's significance inside music. **Music connected to learning** (pp. 89–90): integrating music and science, the way of learning music history, and the very meaning of subject integration. **Theatre and film/video in other subjects** (pp. 94–95). **Visual arts in other subjects and cross-cutting issues** (pp. 96–102).

Learn these as the guide's own table of promises: each line names a connection the full guide teaches. Where the body is missing, this lesson says so — the contents are evidence of the connection's place, not of its full text.

## Sub lesson 7 — Visual art and natural science — the spectrum

The Grade 8 book (§5.1.2) gives visual art its science road: visual art has a **clear relation with natural-science education** in **spectrum-colour creation** — the colours of the spectrum, in their order (red, orange, yellow, green and onward, as a prism spreads them) — in the drawing of **things' natural forms**, and in **colour-combination reactions**, what colours do to each other when they meet.

Learn the meeting plainly. Science names what light does; visual art **does it on paper** — splits it, orders it, mixes it and watches the reaction. The spectrum is a science fact you can paint, and a painting lesson you can measure.

## Sub lesson 8 — The Grade 6 chapter opens — art among the other subjects (chapter opening — Grade 6)

The Grade 6 book opens its fifth chapter, *The Relationship of Art with Other Subjects*, with aims that run in both directions — art serving the subjects, and the subjects carried by art:

- **visual art's relation with other subjects**;
- **visual works' contribution to protecting our heritage**, and **using visual works to teach useful issues**;
- **theatre, film and photography's heritage contribution**;
- **music's link with other subjects**;
- **learn about heritage, health and the forest's importance through chant**, and **compose melodies and poems on varied topics**.

This lesson is the chapter's home: the Grade 6 aims stand at its head, in the book's own order, beside the teacher-guide evidence the lesson already carries.

## Sub lesson 9 — The Grade 6 chapter closes — art in daily life and every subject (chapter closing — Grade 6)

The Grade 6 book closes its fifth chapter with a summary that starts in the street: **visual works are communication ways in daily life**, and **design contributes in architecture, clothing, jewellery, wood and metal design and technological creations — visual art's basic relation with science**. Visual art also **ties to culture and history and raises social topics**. Music's relations are just as direct: **with mathematics, counting note durations and silences; with language, since all speech and letters rest on sound** — and **music shapes good citizens through songs of patriotism and family love**.

Check yourself in the book's two tables. Table one (6 items) is music: **music links with varied subjects; sing "Knowledge Blossoms" correctly; know and sing "There Are Professionals"; have sung "Our Historical Heritage" and "The Careful Student" and know what they teach; my music choice's positive and negative effect on my future life; subjects can be learned through chant**. (All songs named, never transcribed.) Table two (5 items) is visual and stage: **visual works help protect heritage; visual art relates with other subjects; visual art teaches traffic, gender, cleanliness and health; theatre, film and photography teach the same; I can make teaching visual works**.

The exercise's second short answer is a group doing: **choose two local religious festivals, present their singing and eskista studied in groups, then explain what you learned from the rite, what the eskista's movement process relates to, and what your instruments could be made from if the plants they grow from did not exist.** Do it with your group. Its written items sit in this part's questions.

## Sub lesson 10 — The Grade 7 chapter opens — decorating, and art among the subjects (chapter opening — Grade 7)

The Grade 7 book opens its fifth chapter, *Relations with Other Subjects*, with one making aim and two knowing aims:

- **decorate the home and environment with pottery and ceramic works**;
- **explain art's relations with other subjects**;
- **understand art's role and benefit in the teaching process**.

This lesson — the relations chapter's home — keeps the second and third aims. The decorating aim lives in the pottery and ceramics work of the visual-art lessons; it is named here so the chapter opens whole, as the book opens it.

## Sub lesson 11 — The Grade 7 chapter closes — check and exercise (chapter closing — Grade 7)

The Grade 7 book closes its fifth chapter with a short, exact ending: a **self-check table of 3 items** and a **closing exercise of 3 true/false and 3 short answers**. The three can-do items are the whole chapter:

- **I can decorate home and environment with pottery and ceramics**;
- **I can state visual art's links, benefits and teaching use**;
- **I can say current issues' nature, divisions and benefit**.

Mark each ✓ or ✗ the book's way. A cross on the first sends you to the pottery work; a cross on the second or third sends you back into this lesson — the links and the current issues are its own ground. The exercise's six written items sit in this part's questions.

## Sub lesson 12 — The Grade 8 chapter opens — art education among the subjects (chapter opening — Grade 8)

The Grade 8 book — and with it the whole primary course — opens its last chapter, *Art Education's Relation with Other Subjects*, with aims that look outward:

- **understand art education's relation with varied subject kinds**;
- **understand art education's benefit for teaching other subjects easily**;
- **understand art education's relation with current issues**;
- **appreciate works made in visual and performing arts**;
- **know how to make the community grasp current issues easily, using visual and performing arts**.

This lesson is the chapter's home — its relations and current-issues work is the lesson's own ground.

## Sub lesson 13 — The Grade 8 chapter closes — art relates to everything (chapter closing — Grade 8)

The Grade 8 book closes its last chapter — and the primary course — with a **summary**, a **✓/✗ checklist of 7 items** and **closing exercises**. The summary's claim is the widest in the series: **art relates to all subject kinds, directly or indirectly**, and **by visual and performing arts, maths, language, natural science and social science can easily be taught**. Then it counts art's benefits in a long roll: **high benefit for speech ability, self-confidence, expressing creativity, easy communication, thinking ability, ethics, working-together culture, knowing local and historical people's history, making the community grasp current issues, and growing the country's economy**.

Run the final checklist ✓ or ✗ across those claims. The closing exercises are explanations and a making: **explain art's relation with natural science, social science, language and maths; make art's relation with current issues in pictorial explanation and chant; and explain, with examples, art's contribution to transmitting current issues' messages.** The making is a doing — a picture and a chant, made with this lesson's work. The explanations sit in this part's questions.
