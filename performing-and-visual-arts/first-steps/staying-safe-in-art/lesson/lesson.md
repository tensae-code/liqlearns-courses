# Staying Safe in Art

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about staying safe in art. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dress for safety first

Before the making starts, the safety page says: wear the protective coat so colours and cleaning fluids cannot reach your clothes or skin, wear gloves with liquids and colours, and put on the mask and goggles whenever the material flies or smells strong — the volatile materials, the cleaning liquids and the pungent colours.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sharp tools, heat and strong smells

Scissors, craft knives and the clay- and wood-carving tools are handled with care, and a hammer or saw is never used without the teacher. Heating devices and hot glue are the teacher's job — ask for help. Work where the air moves when materials smell strong, and keep away from your schoolmates when a task needs sharp tools — one Grade 2 book makes the pupil pledge it: "I am responsible."

## Sub lesson 3 — Tidy up, wash, and work at home with a parent

When the work ends, tidy the workspace and wash your hands — after every activity, not only the messy ones. Art work at home is done only under a parent's supervision, or a responsible family member's. Safety is not a page to skip: Saudi Arabia prints it at the front of every art book, in every grade and every term.
