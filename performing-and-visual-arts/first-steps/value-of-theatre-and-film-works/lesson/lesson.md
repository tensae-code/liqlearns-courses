# Value of Theatre and Film Works

## Do it first

Some books reach us with pages missing. This lesson teaches you to read what a contents page can honestly tell — and to say, plainly, what it cannot.

## Sub lesson 1 — The value of theatre and film works — the contents line (EVIDENCE-LIMITED)

*This lesson is EVIDENCE-LIMITED and says so everywhere it must: the Grade 8 guide's library file is truncated — its body ends at printed p. 56 — so Unit 4 stands on the guide's **contents and syllabus only**. The contents name this section plainly: **the value of theatre and film works** (printed pp. 72–75).*

What the contents line itself states is what this lesson teaches: that in the guide's valuing unit, theatre and film works are **valued** — taken as works with worth to be judged, the way this course's appreciation lessons teach — and that this section holds its place in the unit's order among the four arts. Anything beyond the contents line would be invention, and this lesson does not invent.

## Sub lesson 2 — The comparison the contents set (EVIDENCE-LIMITED)

The contents give this section its task as well as its title: **compare and contrast** — theatre and film works set beside **dance and music**, their likenesses and differences judged. That single contents instruction is taught here as it stands: the guide's valuing method is comparative — a work's value is sharpened by standing it next to a neighbouring art's works and asking what each does that the other cannot.

The lesson goes no further than the contents go. When the guide's body for Unit 4 is found, this lesson will be rebuilt from it; until then the contents line, honestly labelled, is the whole evidence — and it is enough to know the section exists, what it values, and how it works.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson in your own words: the Grade 8 guide's Unit 4 contents name the value of theatre and film works and set its comparison — taught here, slim and honestly, from the contents lines alone (EVIDENCE-LIMITED). Then let your companion ask you one question about any part of it, and answer from the lesson.
