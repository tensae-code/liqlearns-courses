# What the Arts Are

## Do it first

Think of all the beautiful things people make and do. A drawing on a wall. A song your mother sings. A dance at a festival. A photograph of your family. A little play at school. All of these are **the arts**. Before anyone explains, try this: which of them do you know with your **eyes only**, and which must somebody **do** in front of you? Keep your answer — the lesson sorts it with you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Visual arts — the arts we see

Some arts stay still and let our eyes do all the work. These are the **visual arts** — the arts we know by **seeing**.

At the start they are two: **drawing** — a thing's picture made by hand on paper, canvas or wall — and **photograph** — a picture taken with a camera. The Grade 3 book widens the family: the visual branch also holds **film** and **video**, the moving pictures we watch.

A drawing of a horse is still there tomorrow. A photograph of your family can hang on the wall for years. That is the mark of a visual art: it is **made, and then it stays**, and our eyes visit it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Performing arts — the arts we watch being done

Other arts do not stay still. They **happen** — somebody performs them, and we watch and listen while they happen. These are the **performing arts**.

They are four: **music** — sounds shaped into song and tune; **dance** — the body moving to music; **theatre** and **drama** — people acting a story or a person, in front of others. The Grade 3 book says it in one line: the performing branch is the art that **includes action and movement**.

When the song ends, the music is gone — unless someone performs it again. That is the mark of a performing art: it lives **while it is being done**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Identifying and expressing the arts

Now the sorting game — the work the books call **identifying the arts**. Someone draws a picture: visual. Someone sings: performing. A photograph is taken: visual. Children act a story: performing.

And the books join a second word to the first: identifying **and expressing**. We do not only name the arts — we **express** them: we draw, we sing, we dance, we act. Every lesson after this one is you expressing one of the arts yourself.

## Sub lesson 4 — The two families restated — the Grade 6 opening

The oldest books open by saying the two families again, in full. The **performing arts** are **theatre, drama, music and dance**. The **visual arts** are **painting, sculpture, drawing, design and photography** — and the book adds the newer screen arts the younger books had begun to name.

Say the lists slowly and sort each art as you say it. This is the same house you met in Sub lesson 1 and Sub lesson 2 — the Grade 6 book simply hangs every member's name on the door.

## Sub lesson 5 — Comparing the two families — the Grade 8 matching

The Grade 8 book sets the two families side by side and asks you to **compare** them. **Visual arts** are the arts by which we express idea, inner feeling and messages by **photograph, painting, drawing and sculpture** — works that stay and are seen. **Performing arts** express the same ideas and feelings **in front of an audience, while they happen** — in music, dance and theatre.

Do the book's matching work: here is an art — photograph. Which family, and by what is it known? Here is another — a dance. The rule never changes: **stays and is seen — visual; happens and is watched — performing.**

## Sub lesson 6 — The Grade 6 chapter opens — what art is (chapter opening — Grade 6)

The Grade 6 book opens its first chapter, *What Art Is*, by raising the level of the whole subject. Its aims:

- **know what art is** — a definition chapter, not only a doing chapter;
- **drawing as the basic skill of visual art**, and **draw varied pictures with drawing**;
- **tell major from minor scales**, and **tell low from high pitch**;
- **build major and minor scales on the G and F clefs using key-signature signs**;
- **know theatre and film**.

This lesson keeps the first aim — what art is. The drawing lesson keeps the second, the scales lesson keeps the middle three, and the theatre lessons keep the last.

## Say it to your companion

Name the two families for your companion and give two arts in each. Then mime one performing art without a sound, and let your companion guess which family it belongs to — and say how they know.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw one visual art happening: a friend drawing, or a camera taking a photograph.
2. Perform one performing art for ten breaths: hum a song, dance four steps, or act picking fruit from a tree.
3. Walk around your home. List everything that is a visual art. Say the list aloud.
4. Watch or remember a festival moment. Which performing arts were in it? Name them.
