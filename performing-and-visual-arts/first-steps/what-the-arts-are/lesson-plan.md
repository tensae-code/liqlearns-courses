# Lesson plan — What the Arts Are

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What the Arts Are
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the arts are two families — the visual arts, which we know by seeing, and the performing arts, which we know by watching and hearing them done.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the child's own world — the drawings, photographs, songs, dances and little plays they already know from home and school.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is

student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Visual arts — the arts we see** — Drawing and photograph — and, as the older books add, film and video — are arts we know by seeing.
2. **Performing arts — the arts we watch being done** — Music, dance, theatre and drama happen in front of us, with voice, body and movement.
3. **Identifying and expressing the arts** — Tell the two families apart, and begin to express each one yourself.

4. **The two families restated — the Grade 6 opening** — the Grade 6 book opens by restating the two families with their full member lists (Grade 6, printed pp. 1–2).

5. **Comparing the two families — the Grade 8 matching** — the Grade 8 book compares visual and performing art side by side — match each art to how it expresses (Grade 8, printed p. 108).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Caring for Your Book — the course opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the arts: music, dance, theatre, drama, drawing and photograph.
2. Sort the arts into the two families: visual arts and performing arts.
3. Say why drawing and photograph are visual, and why music, dance, theatre and drama are performing.
4. Show one art of each family with your own hands or voice.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls a song a visual art because we 'see' the singer → the song itself is heard, not seen; it happens and ends — performing.
- Calls a photograph performing because a person took it → what matters is how we know the art: the photograph stays and is seen — visual.
