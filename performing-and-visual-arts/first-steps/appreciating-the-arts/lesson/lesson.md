# Appreciating the Arts

## Do it first

Think of a song you love. How do you **show** that you love it? You sing it. A drawing you love? You look long, and you draw your own. A dance you love? Your feet answer. The books have a strong word for all of this — **appreciating the arts** — and their meaning may surprise you: appreciation, in these books, is something you **do**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Visiting and looking

Grade 3 opens its appreciating chapter with a definition and a journey. **Appreciate** means **visiting and looking** at different art products — photographs, drawings, theatre, drama, music, dance, dress, architecture works, handicraft products — **and giving appreciation**. And appreciation **contributes to the art's growth**: the art we visit and honour grows.

The practice has its own Amharic name: the **visit** — ጉብኝት (gubignit). A visit is a **journey, alone or in a group, from place to place**, to **enjoy and know** man-made or natural **historic heritages**, drawing works and handicraft products.

The chapter's named branches to visit and appreciate: **drawing, sculpture** (ኪነ ቅርፅ), **handicraft, photograph, architecture, music, dance, theatre**. Eight doors — the visit walks through all of them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Showing what we love

Grade 2's chapter teaches the same truth from the doing side. Its aims: identify **how the arts are appreciated**, appreciate them, and **show appreciation by doing** — the chapter covers **music, dance, drawing, theatre and photograph**, each appreciated **by performing it**.

Its closing summary gives the course's definition, and it is the one to remember: **appreciation is expressing one's inner feeling for the arts by doing them.**

Its closing questions check exactly that: **appreciation can be expressed in dance** — true. **A poem presented on stage by acting** is theatre. **Acting a person's character** is acting (ትወና). **The image obtained with a camera** is a photograph. And the short-answer question that sums the chapter: **by what kinds of art is appreciation expressed?** — by all of them, done.

## Sub lesson 3 — Saying which we love — and why

Appreciation also learns to **choose and say why**. The photograph review folded into this lesson gives the practice: two photographs — the **Sof Omar** cave, an entrance in rock, and the **Tis Abay Falls**, a wide waterfall. The book's activity is one honest sentence: **say which of the photographs pleased you.**

Grade 3's chapter summary, folded in with it, says what all this serving is for: the arts serve a community's life in many ways — people use them to **express thought, communicate, express appreciation and pass messages**; and a community's custom, dress, dance, drawing work and pottery are **appreciated and passed to the generation** through drama, theatre, music, photograph and painting.

Its closing check: **a visit means going around looking at historic places** — true; **photography's benefit to society is great** — true. Appreciation, done well, is how a people's arts survive into the next generation's hands.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Grade 1 chapter closes — what we appreciated (closing fold)

The Grade 1 book closes its chapter on appreciating cultural practices with its own summary — and the summary is a doing list. **Traditional dress, traditional dance, eating together and helping each other** were appreciated **by performing them**: we wore the dress, we danced the dance, we ate together, we helped.

That is the book's whole teaching about appreciation in one line: you do not appreciate a practice only by looking at it — you appreciate it by **taking part in it, well**. The chapter's review asks the same of you: name a cultural practice you appreciate, and say **why** — the reason is part of the appreciation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion which art you love most, and show it — sing it, step it, draw it — do not only say it. Then each of you names one photograph or place you would visit, and why.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a small visit: walk to one artwork, craft, building or historic place near you. Look long. Say what you appreciated.
2. Show appreciation by doing: perform one art today — a song, a dance, a drawing, a small acting — for someone.
3. Look at two photographs (in a book or at home). Say which pleases you, and give two reasons.
4. Write the definition from memory: 'Appreciation is …' — then check it against the lesson.
