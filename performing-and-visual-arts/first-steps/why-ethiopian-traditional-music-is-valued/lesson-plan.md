# Lesson plan — Why Ethiopian Traditional Music Is Valued

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Why Ethiopian Traditional Music Is Valued
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Grade 3 guide names Ethiopian traditional music's marks of value: it survived the centuries without foreign influence, its instruments exist only in Ethiopia, and it stands as an emblem of Ethiopia (teacher-guide evidence).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the Grade 3 guide's appreciation chapter (printed pp. 67–72).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **It survived the centuries (teacher guide — evidence)** — Ethiopian traditional music survived the centuries without falling under foreign influence — the guide's first mark of value (Grade 3 teacher guide, printed pp. 67–72).
2. **Instruments only here — an emblem of Ethiopia (teacher guide — evidence)** — its instruments are found only in Ethiopia, its many kinds live in different localities, and the music stands as an emblem of Ethiopia (Grade 3 teacher guide, printed pp. 67–72).
