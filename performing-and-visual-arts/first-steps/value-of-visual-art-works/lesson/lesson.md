# Value of Visual Art Works

## Do it first

Some books reach us with pages missing. This lesson teaches you to read what a contents page can honestly tell — and to say, plainly, what it cannot.

## Sub lesson 1 — The value of visual art works — the contents line (EVIDENCE-LIMITED)

*This lesson is EVIDENCE-LIMITED and says so everywhere it must: the Grade 8 guide's library file is truncated — its body ends at printed p. 56 — so Unit 4 stands on the guide's **contents and syllabus only**. The contents name this section plainly: **the value of visual art works** (printed pp. 76–81).*

What the contents line itself states is what this lesson teaches: that in the guide's valuing unit, visual art works are **valued** — taken as works with worth to be judged, the way this course's appreciation lessons teach — and that this section holds its place in the unit's order among the four arts. Anything beyond the contents line would be invention, and this lesson does not invent.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tools and messages on monuments and artifacts (EVIDENCE-LIMITED)

The contents give this section its field as well as its title: **tools and messages on monuments and artifacts** — the value of visual art works is read where the works themselves carry it: in the tools that made them and the messages carved and built into monuments and artifacts. That contents instruction is taught here as it stands: to value a visual work, the guide sends the learner to the monument and the artifact, to read what the maker left in the stone and the thing.

The lesson goes no further than the contents go. When the guide's body for Unit 4 is found, this lesson will be rebuilt from it; until then the contents line, honestly labelled, is the whole evidence — and it is enough to know the section exists, what it values, and how it works.
