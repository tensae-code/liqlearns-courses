# How the Arts Help Us Learn — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 2)** Name three arts ways into language.
**Answer:** Any three of: lines loosening the hand for the fidel; learning letters in drawing and song; stories in visible drawing; poems in melody; short writings and stories in drama; distinguishing fidel shapes and sizes.

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** Draw and answer: 4 birds + 3 birds = ? Write the number.
**Answer:** 7

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** Name two things Grade 2's science pictures show.
**Answer:** Land (a landform, መልክዓምድር) and flowers — and the shapes of made things.

**4. (Sub lesson 5)** What is the practice at the end of Grade 2's social-studies part?
**Answer:** Tell the teacher what you understood about learning history and social values from the picture and the song.

**5. (Sub lesson 6)** What does the Grade 3 closing exercise check?
**Answer:** Whether the learner can judge claims about how the arts relate to and help other subjects.

**6. (Sub lesson 7)** List the Grade 4 chapter's three aims.
**Answer:** Learn other subjects' content through art products; appreciate the method; use drawing, music, drama and song practically while learning.

**7. (Sub lesson 8)** What travels, in the guide's §5.1?
**Answer:** The visual elements — shapes, forms and colours — from art into mathematics and the other subjects.

**8. (Sub lesson 9)** How can people communicate besides speech, in §5.1?
**Answer:** By pictures — visual communication; one picture can carry a thousand words' worth of message.

**9. (Sub lesson 10)** How does the Yekatit 12 monument help us learn?
**Answer:** It stands open in public, recalling the 1936 invasion's harm, teaching history and identity to every generation that passes.

**10. (Sub lesson 11)** Name the four columns of the Grade 6 table.
**Answer:** Science (sense organs, breathing), mathematics (counting songs), citizenship (respect), language (letter chants).

**11. (Sub lesson 12)** State the listening rule of §5.3.
**Answer:** Choose the music you hear as you choose the food you eat — take what nourishes the mind, refuse what is unsuited to your age.

**12. (Sub lesson 13)** Name the two fields §5.2 joins to visual art.
**Answer:** Social activities (the arranged, beautified home) and history (the record carried by made things).

**13. (Sub lesson 14)** Name three of music's Grade 7 links.
**Answer:** Any three of: language; mathematics (scale, multiplying notes); physics (rhythm in time); chemistry; environmental/social science; therapy; art.

**14. (Sub lesson 15)** Name the three changes performing art brings in teaching.
**Answer:** Physical change, mental change and behavioural change.

**15. (Sub lesson 16)** Why must a person strengthen thinking and self-confidence, in §4.1.3?
**Answer:** To succeed in social life — and performing art is named as a strengthener of both.

**16. (Sub lesson 17)** How do science and performing art help each other here?
**Answer:** Science gives the facts (sound, breath, health); the art carries them to the community, as the named AIDS chant does.

**17. (Sub lesson 18)** Name two social-science links and two mathematics links of visual art.
**Answer:** Social: expressing culture/heritage, landscape study, history from pictures/monuments, social benefit (any two). Maths: scale, solid geometric images, perimeter and area by design (any two).

**18. (Sub lesson 19)** Name the three worked relations of §5.1.1.
**Answer:** Performing art with social science (local culture), with mathematics (imaginative calculation, time indicators), and with language (communication, culture's main tool).

**19. (Sub lesson 20)** How do shapes count quantities, in the exercise's example-world?
**Answer:** Shapes stand for amounts — a learner counts and reckons with pictured shapes, so drawing carries the number work.
