# Lesson plan — How the Arts Help Us Learn

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** How the Arts Help Us Learn
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the arts make every other school subject easier, clearer and unforgettable — language, maths, science and social studies all learn better with song, drawing, dance and drama inside them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on How the Arts Belong Together — if the arts complete each other, they can also carry other subjects.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **How the arts help every subject** — The principle: learning through melody, dance, drawing and drama — and what it grows in the learner.
2. **Arts for language** — Lines, letters and songs; stories in drawing; poems in melody; stories in drama.
3. **Arts for maths** — Drawing maths shapes; shapes, fractions and picture sums; number songs.
4. **Arts for science** — Surroundings, cleanliness, sounds and care of the world — taught in drawing, music and drama.
5. **Arts for social studies** — A community's life, history, culture and values — taught through drawing, music and theatre.

6. **The Grade 3 closing exercise — check the chapter (closing fold)** — the Grade 3 book's Chapter 5 closing exercise, folded in as this lesson's check: what music can and cannot do between subjects (Grade 3, printed pp. 84–85).

7. **Learning other subjects through art — the Grade 4 aims** — the Grade 4 book's chapter aims: learn other subjects' content through art products, appreciate the method, and use drawing, music, drama and song while learning (Grade 4, printed p. 80).

8. **Shapes, forms and colours across the curriculum (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 4 guide's syllabus: the visual elements — shapes, forms and colours — applied across the curriculum, mathematics first (Grade 4 teacher guide, Ch. 5 §5.1, syllabus, printed p. 93).

9. **Pictures that speak — visual communication** — the Grade 6 book: people communicate besides speech — 'one picture is worth a thousand words' — and drawing is the most ancient communication (Grade 6, printed p. 100).

10. **A monument that teaches — Yekatit 12** — the Grade 6 book's monument: the Yekatit 12 memorial — a history-and-identity memorial for the generations (Grade 6, printed p. 103).

11. **Music's links in four columns — the Grade 6 table** — the Grade 6 book's §5.3 table: music linked column by column with science, mathematics, citizenship and language (Grade 6, printed p. 108).

12. **Choose music like food** — the Grade 6 book's listening rule: as we choose the food we eat, the music we hear must be chosen — and music unsuited to the child's age is refused (Grade 6, printed p. 109).

13. **Visual art in social life and history** — the Grade 7 book: we arrange, beautify, organise and design the home's utensils — visual art inside social activities, and inside history (Grade 7, printed p. 154).

14. **Music's links — language, number, science and healing** — the Grade 7 book's §5.3 set: music with language, maths, environmental and social science; rhythm with physics and chemistry; the scale with maths; music as therapy; music with art (Grade 7, printed pp. 156–159).

15. **Performing art and the child's social growth** — the Grade 7 book: in teaching, performing art brings physical, mental and behavioural change in children (Grade 7, printed pp. 133–135).

16. **The psychological benefit — confidence and a settled mind** — the Grade 8 book's third benefit of performing art: strength of becoming, thinking and self-confidence — healing, heroism and respect (Grade 8, printed pp. 106–107).

17. **Performing art and natural science — the Grade 8 account** — sound creation, highness and lowness, breath, vibration and oscillation; the instrument qenet; a named AIDS chant as the working example (Grade 8, printed pp. 122–123).

18. **Visual art with social science and with mathematics — the Grade 8 pair** — social science: benefit, culture, heritage, landscape and history from pictures and monuments; mathematics: scale, solid forms, perimeter and area by design (Grade 8, printed pp. 129–130).

19. **Art relates to every subject — the Grade 8 chapter set** — the Grade 8 book's Chapter 5 opening set: art as expression of what we see, hear and wish; performing art with social science, with mathematics (its time indicators), and with language (Grade 8, printed pp. 121–127).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: How the Arts Belong Together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why lessons taught through the arts are not forgotten.
2. Give the arts' way into language: lines and letters, stories in drawing, poems in melody, stories in drama.
3. Give the arts' way into maths: shapes, fractions, picture sums and number songs.
4. Give the arts' way into science and social studies, with an example of each.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and one song you know.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Sing the alphabet — or the fidel — as a song. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts of lesson/lesson.md, in order.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9006]: a picture of one classroom where the arts carry four subjects — a child singing the fidel, a picture sum on the board, a drawing of land and flowers, children eating around a mesob — the student names which subject each corner teaches.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice singing a short counting song, then asking a picture-sum question — the student answers aloud before the voice goes on.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 14002]: a drawn picture of a public memorial monument — the student says what history it teaches and to whom.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Teach your companion one small thing through an art: sing a count, draw a shape-sum, or act a story moment. (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks arts are only for the arts lesson → give the fidel-song test from Do it first: the tune carried the letters.
- Thinks picture sums are 'not real maths' → the books use them to balance and count; the number answer still gets written under the picture.
