# How the Arts Help Us Learn

## Do it first

Sing the alphabet — or the fidel — as a song. Easy, yes? Now try to say the same letters as a plain list, no tune. Harder. The tune **carried** the letters into your memory and kept them there. That carrying is this lesson: the Grade 2 book says children learn other subjects through the arts **without boredom**, and the Grade 3 book says it makes them **understand their surroundings deeply**.

## Sub lesson 1 — How the arts help every subject

Grade 2 opens the teaching: the arts have a **great role** in learning other subjects. By **melody, dance, drawing and drama**, children learn **without boredom** and grow their talent.

And it names what arts learning grows: **deep observation, language ability, social relations, decision-making, readiness and creativity** — and it makes lessons **unforgettable, picture-drawing and clear**.

Grade 3 states the same principle for its own year: teaching the arts combined with other subjects helps us **appreciate artworks, develop creativity, strengthen body movement, teach contents with enjoyment, and understand our surroundings deeply**. Its chapter aims add the learner's inside growth: the arts develop **self-confidence, mental growth and mutual relations**, remove anxiety, and grow **speaking and memory** ability. The Grade 2 teacher guide carries the same teaching — each art's contribution to the other subjects — at section level (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — Arts for language

Language is the first subject the arts carry, and the books are exact about how.

From the **drawing** branch, we learn to write the Amharic and English letters **beautifully** — and the hand is loosened for the fidel with **different lines**, practised in order. Learning **letters**, and **stories in visible drawing** and **in song**, makes them **quick to know and unforgettable**.

Grade 3 widens it: the arts distinguish **fidel shapes and sizes** easily, show **stories in drawing**, teach **poems in melody**, and show and teach **short writings and stories in drama**. A story you have acted is a story you own.

## Sub lesson 3 — Arts for maths

Maths comes in through the hands and the eyes.

Grade 2: the arts help teach maths **irreplaceably** — through the **shapes of natural and man-made things**, by **replacing maths numbers with pictures of things** to balance and count, and through **music**. Its own activity: draw the pictured maths shapes by design, and, following the examples, **replace maths numbers with pictures**, design maths shapes and colour them.

Grade 3: make **basic shapes, fractions and graphs**, **calculate using drawings**, and **sing number songs** — so the lesson is unforgettable. Its drawings show numbers' quantity distinguished by drawing, and sums calculated with pictures. A sum you can see is a sum you can check.

## Sub lesson 4 — Arts for science

Science is the world observed — and observing is the arts' first skill.

Grade 2: messages about **the surroundings, natural and man-made things**, and keeping **one's own, the family's and the area's cleanliness**, are taught in drawing, music and drama so children grasp them and **do not forget** them. Its picture work: **land and flowers in pictures** — a landform (መልክዓምድር) and flowers of different shapes and colours — and the **shapes of made things**.

Grade 3: **personal hygiene care, people's basic needs, distinguishing different sounds, care of the surroundings, the breathing system** and other activities are taught through the arts. Science sung and drawn stays learned.

## Sub lesson 5 — Arts for social studies

Social studies is the community's own life — and the arts are the community's own voice.

Grade 2: a community's **way of life, eating, helping**, and its whole **history, culture, custom and values** are taught to children through **drawing, music and theatre**; the country's **landform, people's settlement and daily activities** can likewise be learned through the arts. Its picture work: **values in pictures** — pictures of social and historical values — and its practice: tell the teacher what you understood about **learning history and social values** from picture and song.

The circle closes: the arts grew out of the community's life — and they hand that life back to its children, unforgettable.

## Sub lesson 6 — The Grade 3 closing exercise — check the chapter (closing fold)

The Grade 3 book ends its chapter on the arts' relationships with a **closing exercise** — and its true/false items are this lesson in miniature. Try two of them the book's way. *Music's relationship with other subjects is low* — **false**: this whole lesson exists because the relationship is high. *Different messages can be transmitted by music* — **true**: a song can carry a lesson, a warning or a memory.

Work the exercise as the book intends: every item is a claim about how the arts help learning, and your job is to judge it against what the chapter taught — not to guess. A closing exercise is the book checking that the joining of arts and subjects stayed with you.

## Sub lesson 7 — Learning other subjects through art — the Grade 4 aims

The Grade 4 book opens this chapter with its aims stated plainly: **learn other subjects' content through art products**; **appreciate the method** — see that learning-through-art is a real way, worthy of respect; and **use drawing, music, drama and song practically while learning** the other subjects, mathematics among them.

Notice the order. First the content travels by art. Then the learner *values* the road it travelled. Then the learner takes the road themselves, with their own hands and voice. That three-step aim is the shape of this whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 8 — Shapes, forms and colours across the curriculum (teacher guide — evidence)

*This part stands on teacher-guide evidence — the guide's syllabus table for Chapter 5.* Its first connection (§5.1) is **connecting shapes, forms and colours with mathematics and other subjects**: the visual elements the learner already knows from art are **applied across the curriculum — mathematics first**.

The guide's point is practical. A shape learned by drawing is a shape recognised in a maths lesson; a colour order learned in art is a pattern read in a chart. The teacher's syllabus plans for the elements to *travel* — and this lesson is where the learner watches them travel.

## Sub lesson 9 — Pictures that speak — visual communication

The Grade 6 book opens its links chapter (§5.1) with a question: **how can people communicate besides speech?** Its answer is the visual one. **"One picture is worth a thousand words"** — a single picture can carry what a long speech struggles to say. And the book gives drawing its full age: **drawing is the most ancient communication** — people pictured before they wrote.

The book's own example is near home: the picture printed on **tea-leaf packaging** (Figure 57) speaks to a buyer who may never read a word of its text. That is visual communication at work — art helping every subject, and every stranger, understand.

## Sub lesson 10 — A monument that teaches — Yekatit 12

[Picture 14002 — Yekatit 12 memorial]

The Grade 6 book (§5.1, Figure 61) sets one public monument before the learner: the **Yekatit 12 memorial**. It is a **history-and-identity memorial for generations** — a work of visual art standing in public, recalling the grave harm done to Ethiopians in the **1936 Italian invasion**, so that those who never lived it still know it.

Learn the lesson the monument teaches about art and learning. A book must be opened; a monument **stands open**. It teaches history to everyone who passes — art doing the work of a history lesson, in stone, in the street, every day.

## Sub lesson 11 — Music's links in four columns — the Grade 6 table

The Grade 6 book (§5.3) prints music's links as a **four-column table** — read it column by column, as the book lays it out. With **science**: the sense organs that hear, and the breathing system that sings. With **mathematics**: counting in song — the book's example counts **ten green bottles**. With **citizenship**: respect, sung and practised together. With **language**: the chant **"Ha Hu Hi I sing"** — letters carried by melody.

Before the table, the book sets its pre-task: **sing two chants learned in Grades 1–5, in groups, with a soft accompaniment**. Do that first, as the book orders — the table explains links your own voice has already made.

## Sub lesson 12 — Choose music like food

The Grade 6 book (§5.3) gives listening a rule you can hold in one hand: **listening to music can be likened to eating food**. As we **choose the food we eat** — we do not eat everything set before us — so **the music we hear must be chosen** too.

The book gives the rule its reasons. Chosen well, music has a **positive effect on children's minds**. And the choosing includes a refusal: the book counsels **refraining from music unsuited to the child's age**. A listener is not a bucket to be filled; a listener is a person choosing a diet for the mind.

## Sub lesson 13 — Visual art in social life and history

The Grade 7 book (§5.2) finds visual art at work in **social activities**: we **arrange, beautify, organise and design the home's utensils** — every set table and ordered shelf is visual art serving social life, giving the eye order and pleasure at once.

And the same section joins visual art to **history**: the things a people made and arranged are how later ages know them. The utensil on the shelf today is the history evidence of tomorrow. Art helps social studies twice — by beautifying the life lived now, and by carrying its record forward.

## Sub lesson 14 — Music's links — language, number, science and healing

The Grade 7 book (§5.3) walks music through the timetable. Music **relates to language, mathematics, environmental science, social science and more**, and **supports teaching them** — a chant holds letters, a rhythm holds counting.

Then it goes deeper, link by link. With **physics**: the beautiful melody of sounds is made by **rhythmic movements measured in time** — rhythm is physics you can hear; the section joins music to **chemistry** in the same passage. With **mathematics**: the **scale** and combined sounds, and the method of **multiplying notes**. Then **music therapy** — music used to heal and settle the mind — and **music and art** together, each performing art strengthening the visual. Learn the set as the book prints it: music is not a break from the subjects; in the Grade 7 book it is one of their roads.

## Sub lesson 15 — Performing art and the child's social growth

The Grade 7 book (§4.2.2) states what performing art does to the learner: in teaching, **performing art brings physical, mental and behavioural change** — the body grows surer, the mind grows readier, and conduct grows with both. Its picture (Figure 39) shows children in that change, performing together.

This is the deepest claim in this lesson. The arts do not only carry other subjects' content — they **change the learner who carries it**. A child who has sung the lesson, acted the lesson and danced the lesson is not the same child who only heard it.

## Sub lesson 16 — The psychological benefit — confidence and a settled mind

The Grade 8 book (§4.1.3) names the **psychological benefit** of performing art. For a person to succeed in social life, the book says, they must strengthen the power of **becoming, thinking and self-confidence** — and performing art strengthens all three.

The benefit runs further in the book's account: performing art can **heal** the troubled mind, wake the **feeling of heroism**, and grow **respect for social and religious laws**. Stand on a stage and your thinking steadies; sing with others and courage comes easier. This is how the arts help us learn the hardest subject of all — ourselves.

## Sub lesson 17 — Performing art and natural science — the Grade 8 account

The Grade 8 book (§5.1.1) gives performing art's relation to **natural science** its fullest statement: the relation lives in **sound creation**, in sound's **height and lowness**, in **breath use**, in **vibration and oscillation** — the physics inside every note — and in the instrument **qenet**, the tuning systems instruments are built by.

Its working example is a chant the book names — the AIDS chant **"ኤድስ እንዳያጠቃን"** — placed here by name and purpose only: a performing artwork carrying a science-and-health message to the community. Science gives the art its facts; the art gives the facts a road into every ear.

## Sub lesson 18 — Visual art with social science and with mathematics — the Grade 8 pair

The Grade 8 book (§5.1.2) pairs visual art with two subjects in turn. With **social science**, the relation is in **social benefit, expressing culture, expressing historical heritages, and studying landscape** — and in learning **history from pictures** and from **sculptures and monuments**, written down for those who come after.

With **mathematics**, visual art has, the book says, a great role: by **scale** — enlarging and reducing pictures; by setting **solid geometric images** before the learner; and by **perimeter and area worked out through design**. The drawing board is a maths instrument: every enlargement is a proportion, every design a measurement.

## Sub lesson 19 — Art relates to every subject — the Grade 8 chapter set

The Grade 8 book opens Chapter 5 (§5.1) with the widest claim in this lesson: **art relates to every subject** — art is among the main ways a human **expresses and reflects what he sees, hears and will wish**: his idea, his feeling, his hope (printed pp. 121–122).

Then §5.1.1 works the performing side, subject by subject. With **social science** (pp. 124–125): performing art is a **local culture** in which community members focus on their community's life together. With **mathematics** (pp. 125–126): music education expresses **varied calculations by thinking imaginatively and performing them** — and the notes' **time values stand as indicators**, counting made audible. With **language** (pp. 126–127): the book frames it first — **language creates communication for humans and is culture's main tool; there is no knowledge and no culture carried without it** — and performing art is one of language's oldest carriers. Learn the set as one staircase: the claim, then the three worked relations.

## Sub lesson 20 — The Grade 4 chapter closes — the arts inside every subject (chapter closing — Grade 4)

The Grade 4 book closes its fifth chapter, *The Arts' Relationship with Other Subjects* — the chapter this whole lesson carries — with a summary and a 13-item exercise. Its items are this lesson in miniature: **shapes counting quantities** (art inside number), **the sense organs at work in drama** (art inside the body's own learning), **a song expressing cleanliness** (art inside health), and **posters teaching against flood and deforestation** (art inside caring for the land).

It also fixes a name this course uses everywhere: flat shapes arranged in order are called a **pattern**. And it ends with a drawing task — **draw the missing picture** — a doing, carried here as this part's practice: when a set of pictures lacks its next one, draw the one that belongs. The written items sit in this part's questions.

## Say it to your companion

Teach your companion one small thing through an art: sing a count, draw a shape-sum, or act a story moment. Then explain the principle: why did the art make it stick?

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a picture sum: draw 3 mangoes + 2 mangoes, and write the answer in numbers under it.
2. Loosen your hand with lines, then write one fidel beautifully, three times.
3. Sing a number song and count back with it.
4. Draw one value of your community — helping, greeting, sharing a mesob — and tell what it teaches.
