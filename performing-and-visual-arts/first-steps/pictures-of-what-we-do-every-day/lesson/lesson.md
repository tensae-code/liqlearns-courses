# Pictures of What We Do Every Day

## Do it first

Think of your day today, from the moment you woke. What did you do first? And then? The book draws a whole day in pictures, one action at a time — and it asks you to **list what you do each day, and perform one chosen activity**. Walk through the pictures below and check: is this your day too?

## Sub lesson 1 — Getting up

The day begins in bed. The book's first picture shows a girl **getting up** — sitting up in her bed, the day starting. Every daily-activity picture works the same way: one true action, drawn so clearly that you can name it at a glance. Say it with its name: **getting up**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Washing

Next, water. Two children **wash their faces**, the water running down, and hands are washed too. In the morning song that sings this whole day, the order is sung plainly: get up, **wash face and hands**. Clean hands and a clean face carry the day well.


## Sub lesson 3 — Cleaning teeth

At the basin, two children **clean their teeth**. Small circles, up and down — the teeth are cleaned every day, morning and night, so they stay strong.


## Sub lesson 4 — Combing hair

Three girls in colourful dresses **comb and arrange their hair**, and a boy combs his. The comb moves through the hair in strokes, until the hair lies neat. Combing is the day's small making-beautiful.

## Sub lesson 5 — Eating

Four eating pictures: a boy eating at a table; a girl eating; a **family eating together around a mesob**; and a coffee-ceremony gathering. Eating is a daily activity we do alone sometimes — and together at our best. The mesob picture is the Ethiopian heart of this sub lesson: food shared from one mesob.

## Sub lesson 6 — Going to school

Children in uniform **walk to school** — the Ethiopian flag flies over the school; one pair crosses the road at a **zebra crossing**; two boys walk hand in hand; a line of pupils enters the school gate; inside, two pupils stand before the desks and the blackboard. The zebra crossing is the safe place to cross: stop, look, cross on the stripes.

## Sub lesson 7 — Reading books

A girl **reads under a tree**. Two boys **share one book**, reading together. Reading is a daily activity too — the quietest one of the day, and the one this whole course is built on.

## Sub lesson 8 — Helping at home

A photograph shows a woman and a girl **pounding grain** with a tall mortar and long pestle — helping parents. Other drawings show children **sweeping** and clearing rubbish, and a woman wringing washed clothes over a tub. Helping at home is part of the day, not an extra: the day-picture book puts it beside eating and playing.

## Sub lesson 9 — Playing with friends

In the schoolyard, by the flagpole, children **play ball** and **skip**; a circle of children holds hands in a **ring game**; two girls dance, holding hands. Playing with friends is the day's joy — and it closes the book's day of pictures, as it closes so many real days.

## Say it to your companion

Lay the day out for your companion in order, naming each activity. Then let your companion perform one activity without words — you guess which picture of the day it is.

## Do It Yourself

1. List everything you did today, in order, from waking to now.
2. Draw your own day in four pictures: morning, school, home, play.
3. Perform one daily activity for a friend, as the book asks — no words allowed.
4. Tomorrow, watch for the zebra crossing on your road. Cross only on the stripes, looking both ways.
