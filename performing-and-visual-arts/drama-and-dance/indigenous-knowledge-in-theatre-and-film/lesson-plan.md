# Lesson plan — Indigenous Knowledge in Theatre and Film

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Indigenous Knowledge in Theatre and Film
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous knowledge itself — community-guarded knowledge from long interaction with the environment, with its thirteen natures — and its use as theatre and film material: riddles, tales, natural conservation, conflict resolution.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Indigenous Knowledge on Stage and Screen (the pupil lesson) — this guide lesson deepens it with the knowledge's thirteen natures.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **What indigenous knowledge is — and its thirteen natures (teacher guide — evidence)** — community-guarded knowledge from long interaction with the environment; adaptive, cumulative, dynamic, holistic, humble, intergenerational, invaluable, moral, observant, relative, responsible, spiritual, unique.
2. **Ethiopian forms as material (teacher guide — evidence)** — riddles, tales, natural conservation, conflict resolution — used as material for theatre and film.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Indigenous Knowledge on Stage and Screen. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define indigenous knowledge as the guide does.
2. List the thirteen natures.
3. Name Ethiopian forms used as theatre and film material.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names one thing their community guards and hands down — a seed, a story, a way of settling quarrels — before the thirteen natures are listed (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition first, then the thirteen natures, then the Ethiopian forms used as material (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 14406]: a board of the thirteen natures — the student reads them and picks three to test against a known piece of knowledge.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion five of the thirteen natures, and one Ethiopian form that can become a play.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Pick one nature — say, intergenerational — and write how it shows itself in one piece of knowledge from your area.
