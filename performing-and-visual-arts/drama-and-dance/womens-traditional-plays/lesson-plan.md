# Lesson plan — Women's Traditional Plays

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Women's Traditional Plays
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the plays only women play — Shadey of Wag Himra, Ashendye of Lasta Lalibela, Solel and Engicha Neqela — met by their peoples and places, their pictured players, and the study the book sets: asking their melodies and poems from the women who keep them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Traditional Games and Stories and Folktales — the learner knows the community's games and tales; this lesson meets the women's own play tradition, kept and performed by women and girls.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four women's plays: Shadey, Ashendye, Solel, Engicha Neqela.
2. Place Shadey in Wag Himra and Ashendye in Lasta Lalibela.
3. Say what the Shadei celebration is and when it runs (Hamle 16–26).
4. Describe the book's study: girls' groups present the four plays, asking their melodies and poems; mixed groups present Nefijele and write its history's connections.

## What you need
Respect, first of all — these plays belong to their keepers; girls' groups to study and present, and elders or culture offices to ask, where the book directs.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the places → Shadey flowers grow in Wag Himra (Seqota's country, from the dance lesson); Ashendye belongs to Lasta Lalibela.
- Thinks the plays are open to all performers by right → the book presents them as women's plays, studied with respect and presented by girls' groups, asking their keepers.
