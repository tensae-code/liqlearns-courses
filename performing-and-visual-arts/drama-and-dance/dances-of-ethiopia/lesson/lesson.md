# Dances of Ethiopia

## Do it first

Think of a dance from YOUR area. Now think: is it the only dance in Ethiopia? Not even close. This country dances in many ways — leaping with sticks in one place, dancing before a tower in another, shoulders dancing in the north. Open the map. We travel by dance.

[Picture 9706 — four dance panels: Afar dancers in white with curved daggers; Somali dancers leaping with sticks; Gondar women dancers in embroidered white dress; a Raya Kobo group of women with a drummer]

## Sub lesson 1 — Afar — and Somali and the South

The **Afar** traditional dance: dancers in white, curved daggers at the waist, dancing before a tower.

The **Somali** traditional dance: dancers in white **with sticks, leaping** — the leap is its signature. Beside it, the **Southern** region's traditional dance — different again.

Your work: state the differences you see between the Somali and Southern dances — and then perform both styles. Looking first, naming the difference, then dancing: that order is respect.

[Picture 9706 — four dance panels: Afar dancers in white with curved daggers; Somali dancers leaping with sticks; Gondar women dancers in embroidered white dress; a Raya Kobo group of women with a drummer]

## Sub lesson 2 — Tigray — and comparing dances

The **Tigray** traditional dance stands beside the others for comparison.

Comparing dances is a skill: look at the **dress**, and look at the **music instruments** used. State the differences in dress and in instruments between the Somali and Tigray dances.

Every comparison teaches the same truth: the differences are not mistakes. Each place dances its own life.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Amhara region — Gojjam, Gondar, Awi, Kemise, Shewa

Now the Amhara region's own set. The **Gojjam** traditional dance — dancers in white, outdoors. The **Gondar** traditional dance — women dancers in white embroidered dress, hair in **albaso** style, caught mid-dance.

Then **Awi**, then **Kemise** (the Oromo of Wollo), then **Shewa** — each pictured, each its own.

The region's dances are named: Gojjam, Gondar, Shewa, Wollo, Awi, Wag Himra and Kemise — and dance itself is artistic movement of the body, to please oneself and others, differing culture to culture, with many dances inside one culture.

## Sub lesson 4 — Seqota, Wollo, Argoba, Raya — and choosing one to perform

The set concludes: **Seqota**, **Wollo**, **Argoba** culture, and **Raya Kobo** — a group of women in traditional dress, with a drummer.

Now the performer's choice: choose a regional traditional dance you appreciate, and perform it in a group. Describe the dress used by the dances of your own area. And hear the wider survey with it: the country's folk dances are taught region by region, each nation and nationality with its own culture — a whole country's worth of dancing, of which this lesson's photographs are the doorway.
