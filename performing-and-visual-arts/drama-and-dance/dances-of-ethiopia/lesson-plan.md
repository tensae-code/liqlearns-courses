# Lesson plan — Dances of Ethiopia

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Dances of Ethiopia
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia holds many dances, differing from place to place — Afar, Somali, Southern, Tigray, and the Amhara region's Gojjam, Gondar, Awi, Kemise, Shewa, Seqota, Wollo, Argoba and Raya — told apart by their dress, their instruments and their steps, and each one performable with respect.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Circle Dances and Traditional Steps — the learner can already dance in a circle and with a partner. This lesson widens the map to the whole country.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Afar — and Somali and the South** — Afar (white, curved daggers, the tower); Somali (sticks, leaping) and the South — differences stated, both styles performed.
2. **Tigray — and comparing dances** — Tigray beside Somali: comparing dances by dress and by instruments.
3. **The Amhara region — Gojjam, Gondar, Awi, Kemise, Shewa** — the Amhara set opens: Gojjam outdoors in white; Gondar women in embroidered dress, albaso hair; Awi; Kemise (Oromo of Wollo); Shewa.
4. **Seqota, Wollo, Argoba, Raya — and choosing one to perform** — Seqota, Wollo, Argoba, Raya Kobo with its drummer; choose one dance and perform it in a group; the country's region-by-region survey.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Circle Dances and Traditional Steps. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the Afar, Somali, Southern and Tigray dances with a marker for each.
2. Compare two dances by dress and instruments.
3. Name the Amhara region dances: Gojjam, Gondar, Awi, Kemise, Shewa, Seqota, Wollo, Argoba, Raya.
4. Describe the dress of your own area's dance.
5. Perform one appreciated regional dance in a group.

## What you need
An open space for performing. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner recalls a dance from their own area, then learns it is one of many, before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the book's own tour — Afar; Somali and the South; Tigray compared; then the Amhara set in its pictured order, ending in the performer's choice (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9706]: four dance panels — Afar, Somali, Gondar, Raya Kobo — the student matches each panel to its dance and names its marker.
   - ADD AUDIO: the drum and music of two different regional dances, one after the other (slot only — no audio file is made in this build) — the student says which dance each music belongs to, and why.
3. **Say it to your companion** — compare two dances for your companion — dress first, then instruments — and name all nine Amhara-region dances.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — describe, in writing or drawing, the dress of your own area's dance, and one step of it.

## If the learner is stuck
- The names blur together → anchor each name to ONE pictured marker: Afar–daggers, Somali–leaping sticks, Gondar–albaso hair, Raya–the drummer.
- Comparisons come out as 'better/worse' → steer back to the book's two lenses: dress and instruments. Difference, not rank.
- The Grade 7–8 guides carry these dances' step-by-step techniques (eskesta counts, Tigray two-beat steps); those are their own lessons' stock, named in sources.md, not duplicated here.
