# Icyivugo: Rwandan Self-Praise Poetry

## Do it first

Start with the first part below — do it or make it before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — What ibyivugo are

An **icyivugo** (plural **ibyivugo**) is Rwanda's self-praise poem — a poem in which the speaker praises great deeds, their own or another's, in lines with their own structure and rhymes. It is **not an ordinary song**: it is recited, by heart, with facial expressions, gestures and dance movements that bring out what the words say. The model patriotism icyivugo in the book is spoken by an **intore**, a foremost hero of Kanyarwanda.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reciting ibyivugo on great themes

The book recites ibyivugo theme by theme. **Gukora Cyane (Working Hard)** praises wealth earned through hard work, cooperatives and learning. An **environment** icyivugo praises the environment and urges its protection — learners say who speaks, who is praised, and what was created for Rwandans to enjoy. **Uburinganire n'ubwuzuzanye** praises a husband and wife sharing property and work and raising children who respect God and country, and is performed with dance, facial expression and hand actions.

## Sub lesson 3 — Composing your own icyivugo

Composing has clear steps: **think of a person who has done great things; write the story of what they did; change the story into lines following the model poem; check the words sit well together; read aloud to check the uniform rhythm.** The class composes on the book's themes — patriotism, hard work and success, environmental sustainability, gender equality — and, in the teacher's guide, on **inclusive education**: every learner included. Composed ibyivugo are then staged in a concert performance before an audience (teacher guide evidence).
