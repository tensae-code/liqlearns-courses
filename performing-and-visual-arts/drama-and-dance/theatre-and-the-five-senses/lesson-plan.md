# Lesson plan — Theatre and the Five Senses

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Theatre and the Five Senses
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Theatre through the senses — sight and hearing first, then smell, touch and taste on stage.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theatre and Our Senses of Sight and Hearing** — sight and hearing help us understand a play — we watch the actors with our eyes and listen to their voices with our ears, and so tell shapes, sizes and colours in the play
2. **Theatre and the Senses (Smell, Touch and Taste)** — the other senses help actors present their characters — smell tells pleasant and bad smells, touch tells things rough, smooth, cold and hot (a potter at his wheel pictured), and taste tells sweet and bitter
3. **I Listen and Remember** — distinguishing between sounds by way of memory; listening-and-remembering is the ability to recall the sounds — of human, animal and inanimate things — that I heard in my life, and to reproduce them; recalling sounds and reproducing them helps the actor recall the situations, feelings and emotions of the character he represents; imitating every pictured sound (children, a lion, a cat, an owl) once in a sad voice and once in an angry voice
4. **I See and Remember** — distinguishing between the characters and their movements in the play by way of memory; seeing-and-remembering is the ability to recall the images, movements and feelings stored in the memory; memory helps the actor recall the movements and behaviour of the character he represents in the play and remember them — matching a rider's stored kit (saddle, helmet, horseshoe, whip, boots) from memory
5. **I Feel and Remember** — distinguishing between the characters' emotions and their feelings in the play by way of memory; feeling-and-remembering is the ability to recall past events and the feelings and emotions I went through while those events happened — memory helps the actor recall the feelings of the character he represents in the play and her emotions; whether the memory holds negative feelings and positive ones, and naming the feelings in the pictures (pain, fear, cold and shivering, joy, sadness)
