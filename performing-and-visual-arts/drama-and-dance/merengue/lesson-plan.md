# Lesson plan — Merengue: Dance of the Dominican Republic

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Merengue: Dance of the Dominican Republic
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** merengue — the Dominican Republic's national dance and treasure: its joyful rhythm, its classified variants, and its place as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Merengue, the nation's treasure** — merengue as the Dominican Republic's treasure and part of its national identity: a joyful, contagious rhythm of celebration, with its National Day on 26 November; dancing it at the school dance festival.
2. **The variants of merengue** — the variants of Dominican merengue classified — Fradique Lizardo's eight variants, by tambora strokes, instruments, character and region; the Cibaeño of the Cibao among them — appreciated, then danced: recognising rhythms, melodies, lyrics and interpreters, and dancing at least two variants from videos.
3. **Merengue, heritage of humanity** — dancing merengue as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recorded for the course blog; and researching Dominican merengue as dance into a mural of images, press cuttings, drawings and texts.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
