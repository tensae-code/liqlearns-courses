# Mato: Surinamese Storytelling Theatre

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — What a Mato is

**Mato** is Suriname's own **storytelling theatre** — a **frame tale (raamvertelling)**: a fictional story built on **ondrofeni, life experience**, and **facts of daily life**, so that the listeners **go home with something to think on**. It is **total theatre** — the telling is **regularly interrupted by song, dance, music or another story**. Its parts are the **Syatu mato**, a **riddle that opens the evening and tests the listeners' intelligence**, and the **Langa mato, the tale itself**. Its figures are the **Matoman or Gi-man**, the **chief storyteller who also wrote the main story**, and the **Pikiman**, through whom the tale is told to the public, who **confirms each part** — "Na so a de" (so it is), "Na leti so" (truly so), "Kweti kweti" (not at all) — and who **directs the piece**. It is **performed in a circle**, and among the **Ndyuka the Anansitori falls under the Mato genre**. The book names beside it the **Du theatre**, the comic entertainment the enslaved performed in slavery time. Stories are placed here by name, structure and summary only — never transcribed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Playing a Mato — rules, roles and writing

A Mato is played by its rules. The **Syatu mato** — a joke or riddle — **opens to catch the audience's attention**. The **Matoman starts the main tale and holds its line through every interruption**. A player who wants to join in with their own story, song or dance calls **"mi ben dape" (I was there)**, and the **Pikiman**, director and keeper of order, answers **"yu ben dape, sa yu syi?" (you were there — what did you see?)**; that joining-in is the **koti**. The Matoman and Pikiman each have an assistant — the **Mato-basiya and Piki-basiya**. The class works **Alakondre fasi**, drawing on all Suriname's cultures and languages — **Surinaams-Javaans, Sarnami, Chinees, Portugees, Sranan** — with **Dutch as the main language**. A **known story** (Anansi, Aladdin, Little Red Riding Hood are named) is adapted in **four groups writing around one big sheet — beginning, two middles, end** — answering the **3 W's** for their part, the story set in the **Amazon or Caribbean region**: the book's example turns Hansel and Gretel into "Anansi is lost in the Amazon forest! Who does he meet? Will he ever come back to Paramaribo?"
