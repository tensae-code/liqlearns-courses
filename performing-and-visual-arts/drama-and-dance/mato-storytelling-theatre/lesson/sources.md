| Suriname — Theatre domain introduction: the Mato — Mato, Suriname's own storytelling theatre — a frame tale (raamvertelling): a fictional story built on ondrofeni, life experience, and facts of daily life, so that the listeners go home with something to think on; it is total theatre, the telling regularly interrupted by song, dance, music or another story; its parts are the Syatu mato, a riddle that opens the evening and tests the listeners' intelligence, and the Langa mato, the tale itself; its figures are the Matoman or Gi-man, the chief storyteller who also wrote the main story, and the Pikiman, through whom the tale is told to the public, who confirms each part — "Na so a de" (so it is), "Na leti so" (truly so), "Kweti kweti" (not at all) — and who directs the piece; it is performed in a circle, and among the Ndyuka the Anansitori falls under the Mato genre; the book names beside it the Du theatre, the comic entertainment the enslaved performed in slavery time (teacher guide — evidence) | Book 1, printed pp. 164, 167–168 |
| Suriname — Theatre Les 1 "Speltraining: kennismaking met Mato" (Play Training: Meeting the Mato): the rules, roles and writing — how a Mato is played — the Syatu mato (a joke or riddle) opens to catch the audience's attention; the Matoman starts the main tale and holds its line through every interruption; a player who wants to join in with their own story, song or dance calls "mi ben dape" (I was there), and the Pikiman, director and keeper of order, answers "yu ben dape, sa yu syi?" (you were there — what did you see?); that joining-in is the koti; the Matoman and Pikiman each have an assistant, the Mato-basiya and Piki-basiya; the class works Alakondre fasi, drawing on all Suriname's cultures and languages — Surinaams-Javaans, Sarnami, Chinees, Portugees, Sranan — with Dutch as the main language; and the known story (Anansi, Aladdin, Little Red Riding Hood are named) is adapted in four groups writing around one big sheet — beginning, two middles, end — answering the 3 W's for their part, the story set in the Amazon or Caribbean region: the book's example turns Hansel and Gretel into "Anansi is lost in the Amazon forest! Who does he meet? Will he ever come back to Paramaribo?" (teacher guide — evidence) | Book 1, printed pp. 173–178 |
