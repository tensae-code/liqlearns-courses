# Lesson plan — How Animals and People Move

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** How Animals and People Move
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every mover has its own way — a family walks together, each person in their own gait; a horse gallops, a hen walks, a cat walks softly, and a bird takes off into flight — and you learn each way by watching, then doing it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on How Things Move — the learner already knows the three ways: two legs, four legs, wings. This lesson looks closer, mover by mover.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **How people move** — a family walking together holding hands — mother, father, daughter, small child — each with their own gait.
2. **How animals move** — horses galloping, a hen walking, a cat prowling softly, a donkey standing — each animal's own way.
3. **Show how each one moves** — the six-picture practice set — bird, kid goat, sheep, hen, donkey, cow with calf — and the bird's take-off.

4. **Dances copied from plants and animals** — The Grade 4 guide's Chapter 5 (§5.3) turns the copying around into making: **creating dances by imitating the movements of plants and animals** (ከዕፅዋትና ከእንስሳት እ

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: How Things Move. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Walk in a family line, holding hands, keeping the group together.
2. Gallop like a horse, walk like a hen, prowl like a cat.
3. Show how the kid goat, sheep, donkey and cow with calf move.
4. Show a bird's take-off, step by step.
5. Say each mover's own way in words.

## What you need
A safe open space. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner walks like an older person, then like a small child, then creeps like a cat, before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — people first — the family walking together, each gait different; then the animals one by one; then the six-picture practice set and the bird's take-off (lesson/lesson.md).

6. **Do It Yourself** — at home, watch one animal for a full minute, then show its movement to your family.

## If the learner is stuck
- Copies every animal the same way → slow down and watch one detail: the hen's head bobs; the cat's feet are silent; the horse leaves the ground.
- The take-off is just arm-waving → rebuild it in order: crouch, push with the legs, spread, beat, lift. The legs start it.
- This lesson's family line is also the seed of the next lessons: people moving like people becomes acting.
