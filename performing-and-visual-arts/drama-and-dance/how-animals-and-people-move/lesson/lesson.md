# How Animals and People Move

## Do it first

Look at the animals and people you know. Walk the way your grandmother walks. Now walk the way a small child walks. They are not the same walk! Now creep softly, softly, like a cat. This lesson watches how people move, and how each animal moves, one by one.

[Picture 9701 — a six-animal picture grid: a yellow bird on a branch, a kid goat, a sheep, a hen, a donkey, a cow with her calf]

## Sub lesson 1 — How people move

A family walks together — mother, father, daughter, and the small child — holding hands, seen from behind as they go.

Watch closely: they all walk, but no two walks are the same. The father's steps are long. The mother's steps are steady. The small child's steps are quick and short, almost a run, to keep up. That own-way of walking is called a person's **gait**.

[Picture 9701 — a six-animal picture grid: a yellow bird on a branch, a kid goat, a sheep, a hen, a donkey, a cow with her calf]

[GIF 9752 — a family walking together holding hands, each person's gait slightly different]

Walk with your family or your class in a line, holding hands. Feel how the group must slow down and speed up so everyone stays together.

## Sub lesson 2 — How animals move

Now the animals, one by one. Three **horses gallop** — all four legs leaving the ground together, fast and strong. A **hen walks**, head bobbing with each step. A **cat walks** softly and low, placing each foot with care — a quiet prowl. A **donkey** stands still and strong.

[Picture 9701 — a six-animal picture grid: a yellow bird on a branch, a kid goat, a sheep, a hen, a donkey, a cow with her calf]

Copy each one. Gallop in place like the horse — bounce, bounce, all together. Walk like the hen, with little head bobs. Prowl like the cat, soft and low. Then stand like the donkey: still, strong, patient.

## Sub lesson 3 — Show how each one moves

Here are six movers in pictures: a yellow **bird** on a branch, a **kid goat**, a **sheep**, a **hen**, a **donkey**, and a **cow with her calf**.

Your work: show the class how each pictured animal moves. The kid goat skips and hops. The sheep walks calmly with the flock. The cow walks slowly and heavily, her calf trotting beside her. And the bird — the bird does the most exciting move of all: it **takes off**. It crouches, pushes with its legs, spreads its wings wide, beats them hard, and lifts into the air.

## Sub lesson 4 — Dances copied from plants and animals (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 4 guide's Chapter 5 (§5.3) turns the copying around into making: **creating dances by imitating the movements of plants and animals** (ከዕፅዋትና ከእንስሳት እንቅስቃሴዎች ውዝዋዜዎችን መቅዳት) — nature as choreography.

The syllabus joins it to singing with beat-following movement and role play to express nature, and singing about nature — stars, rivers, birds. You have learned how plants sway and animals move; now their movements become your dances.
