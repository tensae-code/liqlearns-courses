# Lesson plan — Indigenous Knowledge on Stage and Screen

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Indigenous Knowledge on Stage and Screen
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how indigenous knowledge becomes theatre and film: what the knowledge is — learned from the people, handed down generation to generation — and how writers and theatre makers use it as their starting point and reference.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the culture lessons — the learner knows Ethiopia's crafts and customs as content; this lesson shows them becoming plays and films.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **What indigenous knowledge is** — the cultural knowledge communities use to ease daily life's ups and downs; learned from the people, handed down generation to generation.
2. **What the knowledge includes** — tool-making, farm implements, traditional medicines, handcrafts, star and season study, philosophy, reconciliation systems.
3. **How stage and screen use it** — film and book writers, history researchers and theatre makers use the knowledge as a starting point and reference.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Finding Ideas for Theatre and Film. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define indigenous knowledge in the book's words.
2. Name what it includes — tool-making, medicines, star study, seasons, reconciliation and more.
3. Say how theatre and film makers use it.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names one thing elders know that no school book taught them, before 'indigenous knowledge' is defined (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition first, then what the knowledge includes, then how film and theatre makers use it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion: what is indigenous knowledge, and name two things it includes.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Choose one piece of your community's knowledge — a terrace method, a medicine, a reconciliation custom — and write how a play could show it.
