# Theatre and Film Through History

## Do it first

Name the last play or film you saw. Now ask: how old is the art you were watching? Older than your town? Older than the printing press? By the end of this lesson you will place theatre and film on one long timeline.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theatre and film, and their participants

**THEATRE** comes from the Greek **"theatron", a place of seeing**: in early times actors showed entertaining plays in such places, and the art grew from there. **FILM** began after the 19th century as **photography, sound and visual inventions** grew and modernised: with image-and-sound technology it **overcame drama's limits of place, time** — a film travels where its actors cannot. The participants: the **ACTOR** — theatre's main and most visible part, embodying the character with body and voice; the **DIRECTOR (አዘጋጅ)** — who unifies all the performance parts and the preparation; the **WRITER**; and the **AUDIENCE** — watching in the theatre place, and in some theatres made itself a part of the performance.

The Grade 7 student book grounds this in its own first telling (§1.3, printed p. 24): theatre comes from the Greek **'theatron'**, and the **first dramatic performance was in 6th-century BC Greece**. (Fig. 22 shows the outsides of theatre performance places.) The history in this lesson therefore begins with a date and a place: Greece, twenty-six centuries ago, a people gathering to watch a story acted.

## Sub lesson 2 — Types of theatre and of film

Theatre's types (Grade 7 book): **comedy, dramatic, musical, tragedy and community theatre** — dramatic theatre reaches audiences by stage, by sound and image, or by story. **Film** is **successive moving images with sound**, shown via television, cinema or other screens; its elements include plot, structure, character, composition and film-beauty. Its **nine types**: **adventure, drama, romance, comedy, action, documentary, investigative, scientific and animation**. Same family names — comedy lives in both arts — but the stage plays it live and the screen plays it forever.

## Sub lesson 3 — Theatre and film in Ethiopia (teacher guide — evidence)

Ethiopia's own story, from the guide: **theatre** — **Fabula** (an animal comedy) by **Tekle Hawariat Tekle Mariam (1913)**, probably the first original African play; **Hager Fikir Theatre** (founded **1935**, Piazza) — the **oldest indigenous theatre in Africa**, with patriotic association, radio broadcasts and tours; the **National Theatre** (begun as Cinema Marconi, completed **1955**, 1,260 seats); and roots in Axumite and church passion plays, fukera war songs, and azmari and areho wandering performers as the first actors. **Film** — introduced under **Menelik II**; the first cinema hall **Seytan Bet ('House of the Devil', 1923)**; nationalisation in 1974 shrank cinemas to under twenty; **Haile Gerima's 'Harvest: Three Thousand Years'**; and the Ethiopian Film Center (1978), becoming the **Ethiopian Film Corporation**. The Grade 7 student book adds the first Ethiopian film by name: the **1957 E.C.** black-and-white **«ሒሩት አባቷ ማነው»** — and why Ethiopian film has not grown: high cost, political instability, too few professionals.

## Sub lesson 4 — History of Ethiopian theatre and film (teacher guide — evidence)

The federal guide roots Ethiopian theatre **in prehistory and everyday public life — plays, rituals, church ceremonies and school drama** — theatre as the **oldest form of performing art**, and then the history of **modern theatre**. Film arrived with Ethiopia's **modernisation** — the introduction of the motion picture — followed by the history of **local film production** and **contemporary Ethiopian cinema**, including the **opening of film schools**. Guide evidence, labelled so; the dates and names stand in the sub lesson before this one.

## Sub lesson 5 — African and world theatre and film (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 guide takes the story continental. **Theatre**: the history of **African theatre** and the **history of theatre in the world**. **Film**: **African film as a product of African societies' historical experience** — defining and interpreting African experience — through its focus areas: **African film in the colonial era** and **African film in the post-independence era**; and the history of **world film** beside it. Ethiopia's story of the last sub lessons is one river in this wide water: every people's cinema begins by telling its own experience.

## Sub lesson 6 — What film art is — and how it began (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 3 guide teaches film from its root (§2.3): **what film art is (የፊልም ጥበብ ምንነት)** — its nature as an art — and **how film art began (አጀማመር)** — its beginnings.

Hold the two together: film is the youngest of the arts in this course, an art born from a device — and this guide treats its nature and its beginning as the first things a learner should grasp about it.

## Sub lesson 7 — Theatre — collaborative and improvised (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 7 guide's account of theatre (printed pp. 14–16) adds two working truths. Theatre is a **collaborative live art** — combining **words, voice, movement and visual elements**, made by many people together, live before its audience. And it works in two modes: **improvised** work, made in the moment, and **scripted** work.

The guide also gathers the word's wider meanings: theatre is **the building, the activity, and the institution** — and as communication it carries **real-life experience through gesture, speech, song, music and dance**. The word itself comes from the Greek **theatron/theaomai** — a place for viewing, to see.

## Sub lesson 8 — Film's seven preparation stages

[Picture 14403 — film's seven preparation stages]

Film crosses the limits of drama and theatre — of place, time and reach — and the Grade 8 book gives its making as **seven preparation stages** (§1.3): **(1) Writing** — the writer sets down the film's idea; **(2) Pre-preparation** — the writer plans the budget and issues the work timetable, and actors are chosen; **(3) Implementation time** — entering the work per the plan; **(4) Photography** — the part holding the photography-taking laws; **(5) Sound recording** — after everything is corrected, recording the actors' voices; **(6) Post-preparation** — evaluating the made film; **(7) Distribution (showing)** — after evaluating, if correct, showing it on varied media.

## Sub lesson 9 — Local histories — theatre and film's foundation

Theatre and film stand on real life. The Grade 8 book (§3.3) teaches that their **historical foundation is on social activities performed in the real world**: the community's **historical transmission, cultural systems and language** make theatre and film arise as one field — performing arts that take the community's artistic values, founded in **speech, movement and materials**, and present them again, **bounded by time and place and imitated**, raising the community's consciousness.

Its named local histories include the **shimglina (ሽምግልና, elder-mediation) system, Timket celebration, the mass (ቅዳሴ) system, the mewlid system, traditional medicine, the mirqat (ምርቃት, blessing) system, the wedding system and the mourning (ለቅሶ) system**. Writing theatre and film from them can teach **peace, unity and mutual understanding**, pass history generation to generation, and make the community own the art as 'mine'.
