# Lesson plan — Theatre and Film Through History

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Theatre and Film Through History
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two stage-and-screen arts across time — what theatre and film are and who takes part in them, theatre's types and film's nine types, Ethiopia's own theatre houses and cinema story, and the long history of theatre and film in Ethiopia, Africa and the world.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Theatre on a Stage and Video: Moving Pictures — the learner knows the stage and the camera; this lesson gives both arts their past.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

2. **Types of theatre and of film** — theatre's types — comedy, dramatic, musical, tragedy, community; film defined by successive moving images with sound, and its nine types (Grade 7, printed pp. 25–29).
3. **Theatre and film in Ethiopia (teacher guide — evidence)** — Fabula (1913) by Tekle Hawariat; Hager Fikir Theatre (1935); the National Theatre (1955); Ethiopia's cinema from Menelik II to the Film Corporation and Haile Gerima (Grade 7 teacher guide, printed pp. 17–25).
4. **History of Ethiopian theatre and film (teacher guide — evidence)** — theatre rooted in prehistory and everyday public life — plays, rituals, church ceremonies, school drama; film arriving with modernisation, local production, film schools (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 7–11, printed pp. 12–17).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Theatre on a Stage and Video: Moving Pictures. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what theatre is (theatron) and name its participants: actor, director, writer, audience.
2. Name the types of theatre and the nine types of film.
3. Tell Ethiopia's theatre story: Fabula, Hager Fikir Theatre, the National Theatre.
4. Tell Ethiopia's film story: Menelik's era, Seytan Bet, the Film Corporation, Haile Gerima.
5. Place African and world theatre and film history: colonial and post-independence African film.

## What you need
Attention for a long story; a timeline strip of paper helps the class hold the centuries.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes theatre and film → theatre is LIVE, in a place, before an audience; film is recorded — projected audio-video that travels without its actors.
- Loses the Ethiopian dates → anchor them: 1913 Fabula, 1935 Hager Fikir, 1955 National Theatre completed, 1923 first cinema hall Seytan Bet.
