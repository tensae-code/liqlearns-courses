# Lesson plan — Euripides' Helen

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Euripides' Helen
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Euripides' tragedy Helen studied in full — prologue, parodos and episodes.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Πρόλογος και Πάροδος (στ. 1-436) (Prologue and Parodos)** — Helen herself tells her sufferings — the judgement of the three goddesses, the phantom, Menelaus gone to war for a shadow; Teucer's news; then the chorus joins her lament for relentless fate
2. **Α΄ Επεισόδιο και Β΄ Επεισόδιο (στ. 437-1219) (Episodes 1-2)** — Menelaus, Troy's conqueror, arrives a ragged shipwreck; the recognition of husband and wife; from recognition to the escape plan — the prophetess Theonoe's choice
3. **Στάσιμα (στ. 1220-1285, 1425-1499, 1593-1652) (The chorus songs)** — the chorus sings between the acts — the futility of war and the gods' unexplored will; the Mother goddess (Demeter) myth, where reversal restores balance; the chorus' wishes for the escaping ship
4. **Γ΄ και Δ΄ Επεισόδιο (στ. 1286-1592) (Episodes 3-4)** — the invented plan is put into practice — Helen tricks King Theoclymenus with a fake funeral rite at sea for the "dead" Menelaus; the machinery of salvation is completed
5. **Έξοδος (στ. 1653-1870) (Exodus)** — the messenger reports the escape and the fight aboard; Theoclymenus rages; the Dioscuri appear as gods from the machine and close the story
