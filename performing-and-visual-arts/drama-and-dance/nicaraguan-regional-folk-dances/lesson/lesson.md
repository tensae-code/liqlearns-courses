# Nicaraguan Regional Folk Dances

## Do it first

Name a dance from where you live. Nicaragua's Grade 4 guide does this for the whole country at once: it names the country's three regional folk-dance traditions outright, region by region. Can you match each dance to its region by the end?

## Sub lesson 1 — El Güegüense — the Pacific

**El Güegüense** is the folk dance of the **Pacific** region. The guide teaches it with its **history**, its **basic steps**, its **costumes** and its **props** (utilería) — a dance is learned whole, not as steps alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — La Polka — the North-Central region

**La Polka** is the folk dance of the **North-Central** region. It too is taught with its history, its basic steps, its costumes and its props — the same whole-dance way, for a different region's tradition.

## Sub lesson 3 — Palo de Mayo — the Atlantic

**Palo de Mayo** is the folk dance of the **Atlantic** region, taught with its history, basic steps, costumes and props. The three dances together are Nicaragua's regional map in dance — and the guide sets them for **presentation in school activities**, so learners dance their country's traditions before their own community.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three dances and their regions. Companion checks: Güegüense — Pacific? Polka — North-Central? Palo de Mayo — Atlantic? And for each: history, steps, costumes, props?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three dances with their regions, from memory.
2. Choose one dance and list what the guide teaches for it: history, basic steps, costumes, props.
3. Write one sentence: why does the guide teach a country's dances region by region?
