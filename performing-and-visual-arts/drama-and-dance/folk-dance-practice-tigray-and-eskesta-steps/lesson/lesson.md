# Folk Dance Practice: Tigray and Eskesta Steps

## Do it first
Step: right leg out — fix. Left leg toward it — fix. Now the mirror. Count it: one-two, one-two. You are already inside the guide's practice. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — The shared one-two leg pattern (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Both dances in this practice share one **leg pattern**, counted **one-two**: move the **right leg and fix** it; bring the **left leg toward it and fix**; then **mirror** the whole thing on the other side.

Repeat right and left; the pattern also **scrolls**, and moves **forward and backward**. Legs first — the pattern must hold before anything joins it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tigray coordination — and eskesta's fast count (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

For the **Tigray dance**, the legs keep the one-two pattern while **shoulder and neck coordinate** with them — three parts of the body agreeing on one count.

For **Amhara eskesta**, the same leg pattern is counted **fast** one-two, with the same **shoulder and neck coordination**. Same road, two speeds: Tigray steady, eskesta fast.

## Say it to your companion
Count the pattern aloud for your companion while you step it: right and fix, left toward it and fix, mirror.

## Do It Yourself
Practise the one-two pattern for ten counts daily until your shoulders and neck can join without breaking the steps.
