# Intore Dance

## Do it first

Start with the first part below — do it or make it before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Intore, the war dance

**Intore** is Rwanda's war dance. It was danced to **encourage warriors going to war or hunting**, and to **celebrate victory at the King's court**. Reading a performance, learners name the dancers' costume part by part — head, body, legs — and the feelings their faces express.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Chosen Ones

**Intore means 'Chosen Ones' or 'Leaders'** — male dancers **elected by the king (Umwami)** for physical strength and good behaviour, and educated in palace conduct as leaders. The **Intore Dance Troupe**, among Rwanda's oldest music and dance groups, performed at the mwami's court and today performs across the country and at the National Museum in Huye. Learners observe, imitate the movements in groups with singing and clapping, and perform on stage for audience feedback. **Today females also perform Intore.**

## Sub lesson 3 — Performing Intore to umurishyo w'ingoma

A full performance matches the dance (**umuhamirizo**) to the drum rhythms (**umurishyo w'ingoma**) and to ibyivugo, on a stage before an audience. The unit also teaches why dances are recorded (video camera, CDs) and how to research a local performing group — its name, founding, dances, benefits and joining requirements.
