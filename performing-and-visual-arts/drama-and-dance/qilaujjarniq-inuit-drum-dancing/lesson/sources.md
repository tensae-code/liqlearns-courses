| Sub lesson 1 — ᕿᓚᐅᔾᔭᕐᓂᖅ (qilaujjarniq): drum dancing, an old custom — songs sung while the qilaut (frame drum) is beaten with the katuk (drum stick); the qilaut was once also a shaman's tool, and disagreements between men could be settled by song through drum dancing instead of anger (original target `music/qilaujjarniq-inuit-drum-dancing`) | Book 1, printed p. 8 |
| Sub lesson 2 — Two regional ways: drum dancing differs across the Arctic — in the east one singer sings to a big drum with caribou skin wrapped on it, in the west the drummers beat the drum's rim with thin wooden beaters and a whole group sings together (original target `music/qilaujjarniq-inuit-drum-dancing`) | Book 1, printed p. 11 |
| Sub lesson 3 — Drum dancing today: drum dancing is still performed more in the west than the east, and is used today for celebrations and happy occasions; schools and communities teach children drum dancing so they learn their ancestors' customs (original target `music/qilaujjarniq-inuit-drum-dancing`) | Book 1, printed p. 12 |
