# Lesson plan — Literary Elements of Drama

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Literary Elements of Drama
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Aristotle's six literary elements of theatre — plot, theme, character, dialogue, music/rhythm, spectacle — with the modern additions, and plot's own structure: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, denouement.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Elements of Drama — the guide's practice list; this lesson gives drama's literary anatomy and its plot mountain.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Aristotle's six — and the modern additions (teacher guide — evidence)** — plot, theme, character, dialogue, music/rhythm, spectacle; modern additions: convention, genre, audience; plus exposition, conflict, suspense, language/diction, style, soliloquy, monologue.
2. **Plot structure — the mountain (teacher guide — evidence)** — initial incident, preliminary event, rising action, climax, falling action, denouement.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Elements of Drama. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List Aristotle's six elements and the modern additions.
2. Name plot's stations in order.
3. Place a story's moments on the plot mountain.

## What you need
The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells a known story and stops at its most breathless moment, before 'climax' is named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Aristotle's six first, then the modern additions, then plot structure as a mountain (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 14402]: a diagram of the plot mountain: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, denouement — the student places a known story's moments on it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion Aristotle's six, and walk a story up and down the plot mountain.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Take any story and write its five plot stations in one line each.
