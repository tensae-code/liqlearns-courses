# Literary Elements of Drama

## Do it first
Tell a story and feel it *climb* — things tighten, tighten — until the one moment everything hangs on. Then it falls and settles. That climb has names. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Aristotle's six — and the modern additions (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

**Aristotle** named theatre's literary elements, and the guide keeps his six: **plot, theme, character, dialogue, music/rhythm and spectacle**.

Modern theatre adds three: **convention, genre and audience**. Around them the guide gathers the working vocabulary: **exposition, conflict, suspense, language/diction, style, soliloquy** (a character speaking thoughts alone) and **monologue** (one speaker's long speech).

## Sub lesson 2 — Plot structure — the mountain (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

[Picture 14402 — the plot mountain]

Plot has a shape — a mountain. It opens with the **initial incident** and **preliminary event**, then climbs its **rising action**, stands on the **climax** — the highest, most breathless moment — descends its **falling action**, and settles in the **denouement**, where the story's knots are untied.

The **exposition** lays the world's facts before the climb. Learn the mountain once, and you can map every story you will ever meet.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion Aristotle's six, and walk a story up and down the plot mountain.

## Do It Yourself
Take any story and write its five plot stations in one line each.
