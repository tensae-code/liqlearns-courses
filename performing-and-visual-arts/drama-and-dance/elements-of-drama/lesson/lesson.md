# Elements of Drama

## Do it first
Freeze! Two actors hold a quarrel mid-shout. What is alive in that frozen moment? Two characters, a relationship, a situation, voices, tension… You have started this lesson's list already. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — The elements of drama (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

To express ideas in theatre, the guide names the **elements of drama**: **role/character, relationships, situation, voice, movement, focus, tension, space, time, language/dialogue, symbol, audience, mood and atmosphere**.

Read the list as a maker's checklist. A scene weak in one element — no tension, no focus — feels weak exactly there. Strong scenes feed them all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dramatic action — introduction, conflict, resolution (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

The elements are carried by **dramatic action**, and the guide gives its shape: **introduction → conflict → resolution**.

The audience meets the people and their world (introduction). Something collides — a want against a want (conflict). And the collision settles, one way or another (resolution). Every story you love rides these three moves.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion five elements of drama and the three moves of dramatic action.

## Do It Yourself
Take a story you know and label it: its characters, its situation, its conflict, its resolution.
