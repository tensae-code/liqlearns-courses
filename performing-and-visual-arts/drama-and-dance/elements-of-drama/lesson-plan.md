# Lesson plan — Elements of Drama

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Elements of Drama
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the elements of drama the guide lists for expressing ideas in theatre: role/character, relationships, situation, voice, movement, focus, tension, space, time, language/dialogue, symbol, audience, mood and atmosphere — and dramatic action: introduction, conflict, resolution.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Elements of Theatre — the learner knows theatre's elements; this lesson gives drama's own element list from the Grade 7 guide.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Elements of Theatre. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the elements of drama.
2. Tell dramatic action's three moves.
3. Label the elements in a scene you watch or make.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner freezes in one dramatic moment — a quarrel, a farewell — and the class names everything alive in that frozen moment (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the element list in groups — people, means, air — then dramatic action's three moves (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion five elements of drama and the three moves of dramatic action.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Take a story you know and label it: its characters, its situation, its conflict, its resolution.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
