# The Elements of Theatre

## Do it first

Freeze. Where are you? Who are you pretending to be? What just happened, and what happens next? You have just named a setting, a character and a plot without knowing it. This lesson gives every part of a play its name.

## Sub lesson 1 — Setting, character and theme

Theatre presents actors' conflicts, actions and atmospheres on a stage before an audience — human life presented and interpreted. Its elements here: **SETTING (መቼት)** is the **place** where the story happens **plus the time** when it happens; the playwright may state the time outright or let it be guessed from the characters' condition. **CHARACTER**: beings the playwright creates — **people, animals, or abstract ideas** like kindness or wickedness — who speak, listen, eat and drink like people; the actor must **analyse** the character before playing it. **THEME (ጭብጥ)** is **local** — from one era and place's events — or **universal**, crossing era, culture, time and place: love, hate, honour, fame.

## Sub lesson 2 — The actor's three tools: body, voice, mind

**Actors** are real people who understand the story and, **leaving their own character, take on the character's**. What is expected of them: **learn songs, dances and movements; attend rehearsals and grow ability; heed the director's directions; work together**. And an actor needs **three tools**: **BODY** — fit for every movement; theatre needs much breath, so physical exercise; **VOICE** — words and letters reaching listeners clearly, without piling up; **MIND** — picturing the character, feeling, focus. Body carries, voice reaches, mind believes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Two kinds of stage, and the audience

Stages come in **two kinds**. The **FRONTAL stage (ፊት ለፊት መድረክ)** faces the audience — the kind in the country's theatre houses — with changing, makeup and curtain rooms **behind** it; it is **raised above the seats**, making distance but making actors loom large; actors must play **full-face or half-face**, never a full back. The **ROUND stage (ክብ መድረክ)** — the arena — is built in the hall's **middle with the audience seated around it**; actors and audience are close. And the **audience** completes the art: theatre happens **in the interaction of actors and audience** — the children, adults and elders who watch the story performed.

## Sub lesson 4 — The director's job

The **director** leads and controls the **whole performance process from first to last**. The book's list: decide **which action happens on which part of the stage**; **run the cast's rehearsals** before production; organise **costumes, set design and light**; and **check the actors' movements** — walking, turning, positioning — for fitness and for matching the message. When many elements must become one play, one pair of eyes holds the whole: the director's.

## Sub lesson 5 — Building the stage world: set, costumes, sound, light, props

The stage world is built: **SET** in two ways — a **PAINTED set** (the back wall covered with a painting or curtain resembling the place — a shore picture for a waterside setting) or a **BUILT set** (materials — carton, wood, metal — a house actually built). **COSTUMES**: dress fitting the context and setting; costume **strengthens the actor's power to play the character in the right feeling**, and a costume team prepares it. **SOUND** must be prepared for **every scene** — a sad scene without its sound gives a far weaker feeling. **LIGHT** makes the performance attractive and complete — for suspenseful and dream scenes, **dim, flickering light** brings the event near the watcher's heart. **PROPS** are two kinds: **HAND props** carried and moved (a cane — ከዘራ — an umbrella) and **FIXED props** that stay in one place (a buffet, a cupboard). And the **script (ስክሪፕት)** is the author's writing on paper showing in detail what the actors are to perform.

## Sub lesson 6 — Elements and genres of theatre in the guides (teacher guide — evidence)

The guides set the same elements in a row: **actors/performers (ተዋንያን), audience (ታዳሚዎች), the organiser/director (አዘጋጅ), the performance place/stage (የመከወኛ ቦታ), artistic inputs/materials (ጥበባዊ ግብዓቶች), and the play script (ጽሁፈ-ተውኔት)**. The Grade 6 guide adds theatre's **composition/writing (ድርሰት)**, its **(technical) genres (የተውኔት ዘውጎች)**, and **how theatre is prepared and staged (የተውኔት አዘገጃጀት)** — the whole journey from writing to stage, which the next lesson walks step by step.

## Say it to your companion

Set your companion a tiny scene: give them a setting, a character and a theme — no words. They act thirty seconds; you name every element you saw at work.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a character card for one character: person, animal or idea — and how you know.
2. Sketch both stage kinds and label where the audience sits in each.
3. List a play's needs under five heads: set, costumes, sound, light, props.
