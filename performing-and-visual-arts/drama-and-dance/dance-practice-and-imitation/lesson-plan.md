# Lesson plan — Dance Practice and Imitation

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Dance Practice and Imitation
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** three linked skills from the Grade 3 guide: practising dance for real, finding the message or theme (ጭብጥ) in drama, video and photograph, and imitation (አከታተብ/ማስመሰል) — copying movements and actions as a drama skill.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the movement and dance lessons — the learner can move and dance; this lesson adds practice discipline, theme-finding and imitation.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Dance practice (teacher guide — evidence)** — የዳንስ ተግባራዊ ልምምድ — dance learned by practical, repeated practice.
2. **Finding themes — in drama, video and photograph (teacher guide — evidence)** — identifying messages and themes (ጭብጥ) in drama, video and photograph.
3. **Imitation — copying as a drama skill (teacher guide — evidence)** — አከታተብ/ማስመሰል — copying movements and actions exactly, as a trained drama skill.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Circle Dances and Traditional Steps. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what dance practice is and why dances are practised, not just performed once.
2. Find the theme (ጭብጥ) in a drama, a video or a photograph.
3. Imitate a movement exactly, as a trained drama skill.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner copies a partner's three movements exactly — hands, head, steps — before imitation is named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — dance practice first, then finding themes, then imitation as a named drama skill (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion one theme you found in a story or picture today, and show one movement you imitated.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Practise one short dance every day for three days, and write down what got easier.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
