# Indigenous Dance Artists

## Do it first
Every community knows its dancers — the ones called for when the dance must be done *right*. Who are they, as artists? This lesson names them. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so — and it is honest about what the guide does not print.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who indigenous dance artists are (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

**Indigenous dance artists** are the **performers of the dances of hunting, gathering, agricultural and domesticated cultures** — the people who carry a culture's dances in their bodies and perform them for the community.

A hunting culture dances its hunt; a farming culture dances its harvest. The artist is the one the community trusts to do that dance truly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Identifying Ethiopia's performers (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

How do we identify **Ethiopia's indigenous dance performers**? The guide's method is **discussion and video comparison**: talk about the dances, watch them, compare performers and styles — and the artists emerge by their mastery.

Honesty matters here: the guide prints **no single artist biography**. The artists are known the way their communities know them — by watching them dance.

Tell your companion who an indigenous dance artist is, and how the guide says to identify Ethiopia's.

## Do It Yourself
Ask in your community who performs its traditional dances best, and write down what people say makes them artists.
