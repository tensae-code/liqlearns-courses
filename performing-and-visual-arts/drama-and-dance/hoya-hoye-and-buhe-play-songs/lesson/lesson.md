# Hoya Hoye and Buhe: Traditional Play Songs

## Do it first

Form a circle. Clap a simple beat together — steady, not fast. That beat is the floor both of today's games stand on. Keep it going while a leader calls, and answer together: you are already inside the first game.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hoya Hoye: a traditional play song

The Grade 4 book's games begin with a choice: **choose a local traditional music game and perform it in groups by its rules**, in **simple beat**. The printed game song is **Hoya Hoye**, the children's traditional play song — a **call-and-response** game: a call goes out, and the players answer together, 'Hoya Hoye ho!' as the placement names its cry. The rules are the game's skeleton; the beat is its floor; the circle is its house.

## Sub lesson 2 — Buhe Belu: the second game

The second game of the set is **"Buhe Belu"** — a **Buhe-season children's game song** with **leader (አዝማች) lines and group answers**. The leader carries the lines; the group carries the answers; the game carries the season — Buhe, the festival of bonfires and fresh bread that you met in the festival lessons. Two games, one shape: a leader, a group, an answer — and a season or a circle to play them in.

## Say it to your companion

Lead one call of the Hoya Hoye game; your companion answers with the group answer and then leads the next call. Judge each other: was the beat kept?

## Do It Yourself

1. Teach the Hoya Hoye game to a younger child or a friend, by its rules.
2. Draw the two games: the circle with its leader, and the Buhe game at dusk.
3. Write the two games' rules in your own words, one line each.
