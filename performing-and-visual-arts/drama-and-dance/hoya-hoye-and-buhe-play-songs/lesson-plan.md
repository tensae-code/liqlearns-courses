# Lesson plan — Hoya Hoye and Buhe: Traditional Play Songs

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Hoya Hoye and Buhe: Traditional Play Songs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the children's traditional play songs as games — Hoya Hoye, the circle play song with its call-and-response, and Buhe Belu, the Buhe-season game song with its leader (አዝማች) lines and group answers — played by their rules, in groups.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Traditional Games and Circle Dances and Traditional Steps — the learner can already play the traditional games; this lesson gives the two play songs their own home, as the Grade 4 book places them as games performed to a simple beat.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Games and Traditional and Festival Songs. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play the Hoya Hoye game in a group by its rules, to a simple beat.
2. Say what call-and-response means inside the game.
3. Play the Buhe Belu game as the second game: leader (አዝማች) lines and group answers.
4. Say which season the Buhe game belongs to.

## What you need
A circle of players, open ground, and a simple beat to play to.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Form a circle. Clap a simple beat together — steady, not fast. That beat is the floor both of today's games stand on. Keep it going while a leader calls, and answer together: you are already inside the first game. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- The circle's beat breaks → stop, re-clap the simple beat alone for eight counts, then restart the game on it; the beat is chosen before the game, as the book's section places it — dances and games in simple beat.
- The group answers raggedly → shorten the leader's call; an answer can only be together if the call is short enough to hear whole.
