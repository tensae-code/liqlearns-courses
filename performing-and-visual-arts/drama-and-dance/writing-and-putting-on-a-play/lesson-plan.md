# Lesson plan — Writing and Putting On a Play

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Writing and Putting On a Play
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** from an idea to opening night — plot built on cause and effect with flashback, the script's qualities and where play ideas come from, the five starting things (script, costumes, place, props, rehearsal), the rehearsal ladder, writing a script and a screenplay, and the shapes stories take: one act, three acts, the whole arc.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Elements of Theatre — the learner can already name setting, character, plot and theme; this lesson makes a whole play from them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Elements of Theatre and Acting Together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain plot as cause and effect in order, and how flashback (ምልሰት) reorders the telling.
2. Give the script's qualities: concise, clear, short sentences, language fitting each character.
3. Name the three sources of play ideas and the five starting things of making theatre.
4. Climb the rehearsal ladder from table read to dress rehearsal.
5. Tell the story shapes: exposition to resolution; one-act, two-act, three-act; and the screenwriter's vocabulary.

## What you need
Paper and pencils, a space, simple costumes and props, and a story worth telling.

## If the learner is stuck
- A plot that is just 'and then…' → chain it: every event needs its CAUSE before it and its EFFECT after it, credibly linked.
- Dialogue that all sounds like one person → the script rule: language fitting each character's personality; read each line aloud in that character's voice.
