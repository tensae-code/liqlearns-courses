# Circle Dances and Traditional Steps

## Do it first

Join hands with friends and make a circle. Now, all together, walk the circle round — slowly first. Feel it? Nobody leads alone; the circle itself moves you. That turning circle is the oldest dance there is. This lesson teaches it properly — and the traditional steps that grow from it.

[Picture 9712 — children holding hands in a circle in traditional dress, turning together]

## Sub lesson 1 — Circle dances to a simple beat

Make a circle. Hold hands. The song is **እንሽከረከራለን** (Enshikerekeralen — We Go Round in a Circle).

[Picture 9712 — children holding hands in a circle in traditional dress, turning together]

To a simple beat, the circle **turns round** — everyone stepping together, singing and dancing as one ring. The beat is the boss: feet land on it together, or the ring wobbles.

[GIF 9753 — a circle of children holding hands, turning round together to a simple beat]

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional dance steps

Now two dancers, **facing each other**, mid-dance. A traditional dance has STEPS — movements that belong to it, learned and passed on, done **in rhythm**.

Face your partner. Step as they step. Answer their movement with yours, the way the pictured dancers do — the dance lives between the two of you.

[GIF 9754 — two dancers facing each other, stepping a traditional dance in rhythm]

## Sub lesson 3 — Traditional songs and dances together

In a group, sing the traditional songs and **perform the traditional dances and games as the culture prescribes** — the way they are done, not any way at all.

This is the circle and the pair grown up: a whole group, in traditional dress, dancing in a circle — song carrying the steps, steps carrying the song. When you perform them as prescribed, you are not inventing. You are receiving, and passing on.

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion why the beat is the boss of the circle, and what 'as the culture prescribes' means..
