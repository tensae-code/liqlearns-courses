# Lesson plan — Circle Dances and Traditional Steps

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Circle Dances and Traditional Steps
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** holding hands in a circle, a group can turn together to a simple beat — and two dancers facing each other can carry a traditional dance's steps in rhythm; together, song and dance are one cultural act.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on How Things Move — the learner's body already walks, runs and turns to a beat. This lesson joins bodies together: the circle, the pair, the group.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Circle dances to a simple beat** — እንሽከረከራለን (Enshikerekeralen — We Go Round in a Circle): the hand-held circle turning to a simple beat.
2. **Traditional dance steps** — two dancers facing each other — the traditional steps, in rhythm, answered between partners.
3. **Traditional songs and dances together** — the group performance: traditional songs and dances performed as the culture prescribes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: How Things Move. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Turn in a hand-held circle to a simple beat.
2. Keep feet together on the beat while singing.
3. Dance traditional steps facing a partner, in rhythm.
4. Perform a traditional song and dance in a group as prescribed.
5. Say why the steps are learned, not invented.

## What you need
A simple beat — a drum, clapping, or a sung pulse. An open space. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner's circle forms and turns slowly before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the circle first, then the facing pair's steps, then the whole group's prescribed performance (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- The circle wobbles → slow the beat right down and count the steps aloud together; speed is earned, not started with.
- Partners drift out of rhythm → face each other and watch the FEET, not the faces, until the steps lock.
- 'As prescribed' feels strict → explain the gift inside it: a prescribed dance is a message from the grandparents; receiving it exactly is how it survives.
