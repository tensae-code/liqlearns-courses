# National Dances and Cultural Drama

## Do it first
Every area has its dance, and every dance carries something — a dress, a feast day, a way of greeting. Name one dance from your area and the custom it carries. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — The country's dances (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

You have met the dances region by region. The guide now gathers them: **the country's dances** (§3.2.1) — one nation holding many dances, each belonging to its people and place.

Held together, they teach something no single dance teaches alone: a country can be one, and its cultures many, at the same time.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cultural face of drama (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Drama is not only entertainment. The guide names its **cultural aspect** (§3.3): a play shows how a people live — their greetings, their work, their celebrations, what they honour and what they avoid.

That is why a community's own stories make its truest drama: the culture is already inside them, waiting to be acted.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion one dance of the country and one piece of culture it carries.

## Do It Yourself
Ask an elder which dance belongs to your area's biggest celebration, and write down what they tell you.
