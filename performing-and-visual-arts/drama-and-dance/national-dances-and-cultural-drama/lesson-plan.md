# Lesson plan — National Dances and Cultural Drama

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** National Dances and Cultural Drama
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the country's dances as one family (§3.2.1), and drama's cultural face (§3.3) — how drama carries a people's culture.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Dances of Ethiopia — the learner knows the regional dances; this lesson gathers them as the nation's dances and adds drama's cultural aspect.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before

1. **The country's dances (teacher guide — evidence)** — §3.2.1: the nation's dances, held together as one topic.
2. **The cultural face of drama (teacher guide — evidence)** — §3.3: drama's cultural aspect — drama carries a people's way of life.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Dances of Ethiopia. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Speak of the country's dances as one national family with many members.
2. Say what the cultural aspect of drama means.
3. Give one example of a custom carried by a dance or a drama.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names one dance from their own area and one custom that dance carries with it (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the nation's dances first, then the cultural aspect of drama (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion one dance of the country and one piece of culture it carries.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Ask an elder which dance belongs to your area's biggest celebration, and write down what they tell you.
