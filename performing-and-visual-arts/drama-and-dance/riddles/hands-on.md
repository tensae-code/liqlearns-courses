# Hands-on — Riddles

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The home riddle harvest
Elders are riddle libraries. Ask at home and in your neighbourhood: 'Do you know a riddle?' Collect every riddle offered — write the riddle, never an answer you have not seen the circle agree on. Bring the harvest to class: the class circle tests each one, clue by clue.

## Riddle-making workshop
In pairs, choose a hidden thing your class knows well — a jebena, a hen, a school bell. Build three clues: what it looks like, what it does, where it lives. No clue may use the thing's name or a piece of it. Exchange riddles with another pair. A riddle that is guessed on the first clue is too easy; one nobody can crack after fair guessing needs a kinder clue. Tune yours until it is just right.
