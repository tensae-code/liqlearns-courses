# Lesson plan — Riddles

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Riddles
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a riddle (እንቆቅልሽ, enqoqilish) is a speaking game: one voice challenges — 'Riddle, riddle — what?' — the other answers 'I know it', and the hidden thing must be guessed from its clues.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the child's own talk — riddles live in courtyards and kitchens, passed voice to voice. It is a short, single-skill lesson: the game form, played well.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Stories and Folktales — riddles are the stories' small cousins. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Ask a riddle in the proper form: 'Riddle, riddle — what?' — 'I know it.'
2. Work a riddle clue by clue, testing a guess against every clue.
3. Play both of the book's riddles.
4. Bring and ask a riddle from home.
5. Make a three-clue riddle of your own.

## What you need
Voices and a circle of guessers. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner is given the stone-above riddle cold, and feels the pull of it, before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the game's call-and-answer form first, then the book's two riddles as the practice set (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9711]: children playing the riddle game in a circle — the student points to the asker and the guessers and says each one's line.
3. **Say it to your companion** — run the riddle form with your companion and present the stone riddle for guessing.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — play both riddles at home tonight; bring one home riddle back to class.

## If the learner is stuck
- Guessers shout random answers → slow them with the clue test: 'Does your guess fit stone above? Stone below? A living soul between? All three, or it falls.'
- A learner demands the printed answer → honour the book: the answers are NOT printed in the student book, and inventing one would spoil the game and break the record (see sources.md). The circle's agreed, clue-tested answer is the lesson's answer.
- Tales and riddles together are the roots of theatre — the teacher guides say so; the actor's first stage was a storyteller's circle.
