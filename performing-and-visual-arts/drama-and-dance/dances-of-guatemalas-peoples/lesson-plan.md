# Lesson plan — Dances of Guatemala's Peoples

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Dances of Guatemala's Peoples
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the meanings of dances, and a first initiation into the choreographies and techniques of the dances of Guatemala's other peoples and of Mesoamerica — taught at theme level, because the Grade 4 activity pages are not in the PARTIAL file.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Dances carry meanings** — Guatemala's Grade 4 experience “Mi cuerpo habla” (My Body Speaks) teaches that **dances have meanings**: a dance is the body speaking, and each people's dance says something of that people.
2. **A first initiation into other peoples' dances** — The same experience sets a **first initiation into the choreographies and techniques of the dances of Guatemala's other peoples and of Mesoamerica** — a beginner's entering-in, learning the shaped movement (the choreography) and the way of doing it (the technique) of dances that are not your own people's, with respect for the peoples they belong to.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this course in the same title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say that dances carry meanings, and that learning a dance begins with its meaning.
2. Say what a first initiation into another people's dance asks — its choreography and technique, learned with respect.
