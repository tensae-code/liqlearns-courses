# Dance Composition and Modern Dance

## Do it first

Make the shortest dance you can: one step, one turn, one gesture — in that order. You have just composed. This lesson names what you used — body, space, time, force — and teaches composing on purpose.

## Sub lesson 1 — The elements of dance: body, space, partner (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 5 guide names **the elements of dance (የውዝዋዜ አላባዊያን)**: **posture founded on the body's shape**; the **use and handling of space** in movement; and **coordinating movement with a partner (ከአጣማሪ ጋር እንቅስቃሴ ማቀናጀት)**. The Grade 6 guide stands underneath: **body movement is the foundation of dance** — bend, stretch, turn, step — introduced before any regional style. And rhythm binds it: dancers **stepping exactly on a drum's beats** is rhythm felt and shown in movement. Body, space, partner, rhythm: the choreographer's first four tools.

## Sub lesson 2 — Composing a traditional dance (teacher guide — evidence)

The federal guide teaches **dance composition** directly: first **traditional dance in Ethiopia** itself; then **the basic elements of dance composition**; then **composition in dance — building a dance from its elements**. Its picture is dancers arranged in a **composed formation**, and its moving idea is the GIF beside this part: **a short dance phrase built element by element** — step, turn, gesture combined. A second guide part joins the performing side: the **compositional techniques of performance in music and dance** — the techniques used to **compose and perform** traditional music, and arranging movement into a **performable whole**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Modern dance: styles and techniques (teacher guide — evidence)

Grade 10's advance is **modern dance**. The guide first teaches **the difference between traditional and modern dance**, then **techniques and styles in modern dance** — and later returns to **the styles of modern dance performed and distinguished**, modern dance in performance answering its perception lessons. Its teaching pictures are contrasts: **a traditional dancer and a modern dancer in matching poses**, and **a row of modern dance styles in characteristic poses** — the same body, new questions: the lesson teaches the difference by looking, exactly as the guide does.

## Sub lesson 4 — Harmony and composition in music, for dancers too (teacher guide — evidence)

Dances are composed **on** composed music, so the guides' harmony work belongs in this lesson's foundation. Grade 9: **harmonic composition in traditional music** — what a harmonic composition is, its style and technique, **how traditional music is put together and performed harmonically**. Grade 10 goes modern: the **concept and functional theory of harmony**, **harmonising a melody with chords** — using **structural tones**, with tips for fitting chords to a melody — **chord leading (voice leading)**, and an **8-bar chord activity** as its worked example. The dancer hears a chord fit a melody the way a step fits a beat: composition is one craft wearing two arts.

## Say it to your companion

Compose a four-count phrase and teach it to your companion by elements: 'a step (body), travelling (space), with a turn (time)'. They must name each element as they learn it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Compose a dance phrase of step + turn + gesture and write its recipe.
2. Re-style one traditional phrase in a modern way and note two differences.
3. Draw your formation: where each dancer stands, and the path each one travels.
