# Lesson plan — Dance Composition and Modern Dance

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Dance Composition and Modern Dance
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** building dances on purpose — the elements of dance (body, space, partner; time, space, force), how a traditional dance is composed from its elements, the techniques of composing and performing, and how modern dance differs from traditional dance in its styles and techniques.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Folk Dance Styles and Eskesta and Moving to Music — the learner can already perform set styles; this lesson teaches making dances, and the modern dance the federal guides add.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The elements of dance: body, space, partner (teacher guide — evidence)** — posture founded on the body's shape; the use and handling of space in movement; coordinating movement with a partner (ከአጣማሪ ጋር እንቅስቃሴ ማቀናጀት) (Grade 5 teacher guide, §1.2, printed pp. 12–14).
2. **Composing a traditional dance (teacher guide — evidence)** — traditional dance in Ethiopia; the basic elements of dance composition; building a dance from its elements; the compositional techniques of performance in music and dance (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 17–19 and 26–27, printed pp. 28–31 and 44–47).
3. **Modern dance: styles and techniques (teacher guide — evidence)** — the difference between traditional and modern dance; techniques and styles in modern dance, in perception and in performance (Grade 10 teacher guide, Lessons 5–6 and 15, printed pp. 6–8 and 22–23).
4. **Harmony and composition in music, for dancers too (teacher guide — evidence)** — what a harmonic composition is, in traditional and in modern music — structural tones and fitting chords to a melody — the composing that dances are built on (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 14–16, printed pp. 25–28; Grade 10 teacher guide, Lessons 1–4 and 11–14, printed pp. 3–6 and 19–22).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Folk Dance Styles and Eskesta and Moving to Music. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the elements of dance in both teachings: body shape, space, partner — and time, space, force/energy.
2. Build a short dance from its elements, step by step.
3. Say what compositional techniques do in music and dance performance.
4. Tell modern dance from traditional dance and name what its styles and techniques study.

## What you need
Open floor, music or a drum, and a notebook for choreography notes.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Make the shortest dance you can: one step, one turn, one gesture — in that order. You have just composed. This lesson names what you used — body, space, time, force — and teaches composing on purpose. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- A 'composition' that is only copying → composition means choosing: which element, which order, which formation. Change one choice and the dance is yours.
- Confuses the two element lists → the Grade 5 guide teaches body/space/partner (the dancer's tools); the Grade 10 application list teaches time/space/force (the movement's qualities). Both are in the lesson, each under its own book.
