# Lesson plan — Genres of Theatre and Film

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Genres of Theatre and Film
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** genre in theatre and film: theatre genres analysed three ways — thematic, formative and technical — and film genres through their three conventions — narrative, stylistic, thematic — with learners categorising works into genres.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Elements of Theatre — genre was named among its topics; this lesson, from the Grade 9 guide's Lessons 20–21, teaches genre itself. There is no Grade 9 student book: guide evidence only.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Genre in theatre — three lenses (teacher guide — evidence)** — genre defined; theatre genres analysed in three parts — thematic, formative and technical — with common sub-genres (tragedy, comedy and others).
2. **Genre in film — three conventions (teacher guide — evidence)** — film genres through narrative, stylistic and thematic conventions; basic film genres (action, comedy, drama and others) with their characteristics; learners categorise works.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Literary Elements of Drama. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define genre.
2. Analyse theatre genres thematically, formatively and technically.
3. Read film genres through narrative, stylistic and thematic conventions.

## What you need
The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner sorts five known stories or films into piles by 'what kind of thing is this', before 'genre' is defined (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition first, then theatre's three genre lenses, then film's three conventions and basic genres (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion: theatre's three genre parts and film's three conventions.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Take three works you know and categorise each twice — once by theme, once by form — and notice how the answers differ.
