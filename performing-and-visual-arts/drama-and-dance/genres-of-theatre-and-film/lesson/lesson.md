# Genres of Theatre and Film

## Do it first
A funny story. A sad story. A story of heroes. Without knowing the word, you already sort works into kinds. The word is *genre* — and this lesson gives it its full anatomy. There is no Grade 9 student book; this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Genre in theatre — three lenses (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

**Genre** is a work's kind — the family it belongs to by its matter and manner. The guide analyses **theatre genres in three parts**: **thematic** genres (by what the work is about), **formative** genres (by its form) and **technical** genres (by its making).

Among the common sub-genres stand **tragedy and comedy** — the two oldest masks: one ending in loss, the other in laughter and repair.

## Sub lesson 2 — Genre in film — three conventions (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Film genres are read through **three conventions**: **narrative** (how the story is told), **stylistic** (how the film looks and sounds) and **thematic** (what it is about).

The basic film genres — **action, comedy, drama and others** — each carry their **characteristics**, the marks by which an audience knows the kind before the story even settles. The guide's closing work is the learner's: **categorise works into genres**, and defend the placing.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: theatre's three genre parts and film's three conventions.

## Do It Yourself
Take three works you know and categorise each twice — once by theme, once by form — and notice how the answers differ.
