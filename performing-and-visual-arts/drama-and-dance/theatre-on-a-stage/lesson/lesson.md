# Theatre on a Stage

## Do it first

Stand on a raised step, or at the front of a room. Raise your arms. Everyone else sits and watches you. Feel it? You are on a stage, and they are your audience. That raised, watched place changes everything. This lesson is about that place — and about performing on it well.

[Picture 9704 — a lone performer with arms raised before red curtains, with the silhouettes of the audience watching]

## Sub lesson 1 — Theatre on a stage

Theatre is **presented on a stage before an audience**. The picture shows it whole: one performer, arms raised, red curtains behind, and the dark shapes of the audience watching from in front.

[Picture 9704 — a lone performer with arms raised before red curtains, with the silhouettes of the audience watching]

Theatre is acting a person's character in imitation — and it can be presented at **school festivals** and on **public holidays**, where the whole community gathers. Use your talent: make theatre at school, and let your performance be your appreciation — your gift back to the watchers.

On a stage, everything grows: your voice must reach the last row, your movements must be seen from far away, and your character must be clear to people who cannot see your face closely.

## Sub lesson 2 — መነባንብ (menibanib) — recitation

One treasured form of stage performance is the **መነባንብ (menibanib)** — a public recitation: a reading performed aloud before listeners.

The picture shows a menibanib about the **Abbay** (the Blue Nile) being presented outdoors: the presenter stands and points at a photograph of the falls, and the audience sits before them, listening.

A recitation is not flat reading. The voice carries the words the way an actor carries a role — rising, falling, pausing — so that listeners SEE what the words describe. When you recite, perform the words; do not just say them.
