# Lesson plan — Theatre on a Stage

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Theatre on a Stage
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** theatre comes together on a stage before an audience — a performer, a place set apart, people watching — and one of its oldest stage forms is the መነባንብ (menibanib), a public recitation performed before listeners.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Playing a Person and Acting Together — the learner can play roles and build group scenes. This lesson gives those scenes their home: the stage, and the audience before it.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Theatre on a stage** — the stage whole: a performer before red curtains and a watching audience; theatre at school festivals and public holidays.
2. **መነባንብ (menibanib) — recitation** — መነባንብ (menibanib) — the public recitation: a performed reading, like the Abbay recitation before a seated outdoor audience.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Playing a Person and Acting Together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three things a stage performance needs.
2. Say how voice and movement change on a stage.
3. Say where theatre is presented — school festivals and public holidays.
4. Say what a መነባንብ (menibanib) is.
5. Recite a short passage as a performance, not a flat reading.

## What you need
A raised place or a marked front-of-room stage, and listeners. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands on a raised step with arms raised while others watch, before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the stage first — performer, curtains, audience — then the menibanib recitation as the stage's performed-reading form (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9704]: a lone performer with arms raised before red curtains, the audience in silhouette — the student names the performer, the stage and the audience in the picture.
   - ADD AUDIO: a strong recitation voice performing a passage about a river — the student marks where the voice rises, falls and pauses.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the stage to your companion — performer, stage, audience — and what a menibanib is.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — recite a passage you know by heart to your family tonight, standing, as a performance.

## If the learner is stuck
- Voice too small for the 'stage' → practise throwing the voice to a friend standing far away, one line at a time.
- Recitation comes out flat → choose ONE word per line to lift; the rest of the line will shape itself around it.
- Deeper theatre elements (setting, character, plot, script, theme) grow from this lesson in the later books; here the stage itself is the teaching.
