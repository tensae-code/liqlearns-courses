# Lesson plan — Dance Education and Indigenous Dance

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Dance Education and Indigenous Dance
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** dance as a human universal with named purposes — performing, healing, entertaining, celebrating, socialising, learning, worshipping, fitness, communicating — its deep history, and how to study a dance by watching: the Wolayta Seleme-Seleme circle dance as the viewing example.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the dance lessons whole — the learner can dance and name dances; this lesson studies dance itself.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Dance education and dance's purposes (teacher guide — evidence)** — dance education: performing, improvising, choreographing, critiquing, recording, viewing; purposes: performing, healing, entertaining, celebrating, socialising, learning, worshipping, fitness, communicating.
2. **Dance history — and studying dance by watching (teacher guide — evidence)** — Paleolithic rock paintings; ritual dances of birth, marriage, death, fertility, rain, harvest, mimicking animals and nature; the Wolayta Seleme-Seleme circle as viewing example; watch, note, compare.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Elements of Theatre. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name dance education's six activities.
2. List dance's purposes.
3. Use the watch–note–compare method on a dance.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner lists every reason people around them dance — a wedding, a prayer, a game, a healing — before the guide's purposes are named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — dance education's work first, then dance's purposes, then its history, then the video-observation method on the Wolayta example (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion four purposes of dance and the observation method's three moves: watch, note, compare.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Watch one dance — live or recorded — and write notes under four heads: art, music, dance, nature/food, as the guide's method does.
