# Dance Education and Indigenous Dance

## Do it first
Why do humans dance at all? Your grandmother dances, children dance, whole villages dance. List the reasons before the guide names them — you will find you knew most of them. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dance education and dance's purposes (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

**Dance education** is the study of dance by doing its full work: **performing, improvising, choreographing, critiquing, recording and viewing**.

Why does dance exist at all? The guide's purposes: **performing, healing, entertaining, celebrating, socialising, learning, worshipping, fitness and communicating**. Dance practised in **every human culture** carries all of these somewhere in its steps.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dance history — and studying dance by watching (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Dance is old beyond writing: **Paleolithic rock paintings** already show it. Early **ritual dances** marked **birth, marriage, death, fertility, rain and harvest**, their movements **mimicking animals and nature**; ancient civilisations then turned dance toward **aesthetic purpose**.

The guide's study method is **video observation**: watch the **Wolayta Seleme-Seleme circle dance**, **note** what you see, and **compare two Wolayta dance videos** across **art, music, dance, nature and food**. Watching carefully is a dance skill too.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion four purposes of dance and the observation method's three moves: watch, note, compare.

## Do It Yourself
Watch one dance — live or recorded — and write notes under four heads: art, music, dance, nature/food, as the guide's method does.
