# Tableau: Living Pictures

## Do it first

Quick game: with your group, show people inside a bank — without moving and without speaking. One of you is the teller behind the counter, one is waiting in line, one is guarding the door. Freeze! If someone watching can tell who is who — you have already made this lesson's first mode.

This lesson comes from Jamaica's Grade 6 National Standards Curriculum Guide for the Exploratory Core — its Social Studies work, which names the drama modes **"Tableau / still pictures"** and **"Thought tracking"**. It is a teacher curriculum guide, taught here as evidence.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tableau — still pictures

A **tableau** is a **still picture made by people**: a group freezes in position so that their bodies together show a scene, the way a painting does. Nothing moves and nobody speaks — the scene must be readable from the frozen shapes alone.

Jamaica's guide sets the mode to work: **use Tableau / still pictures to create scenes in a financial institution** — the tellers, customers and guards of a bank or similar place, held frozen so the class can read the scene.

## Sub lesson 2 — Thought tracking

**Thought tracking** opens the frozen picture from inside. The tableau stays frozen, but one figure at a time is given a voice — not to act or move, but to **speak the thoughts** that figure would be thinking inside the scene: what the waiting customer hopes, what the guard is watching for, what the teller is taking care of.

The guide pairs the two modes in the same Social Studies work, alongside role-playing the reading of the annual budget in Gordon House — the frozen picture and its spoken thoughts are two halves of one drama tool: the tableau shows the scene, thought tracking tells what the scene's people are thinking.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what is a tableau, and what is thought tracking? How do the two join? Companion checks: frozen still picture for tableau? Thoughts spoken while the figure stays frozen for thought tracking? A financial-institution scene as the guide's example?

## Do It Yourself

1. In a group, create a tableau of a scene in a financial institution. Hold it while a watcher reads the scene aloud.
2. Thought-track every figure in your tableau, one voice at a time, nobody moving.
3. Write one sentence: what did thought tracking tell the class that the frozen picture alone could not?
