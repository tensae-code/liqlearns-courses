# Lesson plan — Tableau: Living Pictures

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Tableau: Living Pictures
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two named drama modes — Tableau (still pictures) and Thought tracking — freezing into a still picture to show a scene, then speaking the thoughts of the figures frozen inside it, as Jamaica's Grade 6 Exploratory Core guide prescribes them for scenes in a financial institution (teacher curriculum guide — evidence).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the mime and first-plays lessons; this lesson is labelled teacher curriculum guide — evidence throughout, because its Jamaica source is a teacher's curriculum guide, not a student textbook.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Tableau — still pictures** — freeze with your group into a still picture that shows a scene, as the guide sets it: a scene in a financial institution.
2. **Thought tracking** — while the picture holds frozen, speak the thoughts of a figure inside it, one voice at a time.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Mime, Fables and First Plays. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Create a tableau — a frozen still picture — that shows a scene clearly.
2. Thought-track a figure in a tableau: speak that figure's thoughts while the picture stays frozen.

## What you need
A group to freeze with, and a scene to show.

## If the learner is stuck
- The picture keeps moving → a tableau is a *still* picture: once you freeze, nothing moves until the scene is released.
- Thought tracking feels like ordinary acting → the figure stays frozen; only the voice speaks, giving the thoughts inside that frozen figure's head.
