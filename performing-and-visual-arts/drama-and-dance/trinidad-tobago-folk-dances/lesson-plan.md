# Lesson plan — Trinidad Tobago Folk Dances

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Trinidad Tobago Folk Dances
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the folk dances of Trinidad and Tobago — the heritage strand of its Dance curriculum: French and African influences, Carnival dances, and Bhangra and Kollattam from its Indian heritage, with Bele, Limbo, Quadrille, Ghadka and Kalinda researched as folk forms.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Folk forms from many heritages** — The country's folk forms come from its many heritages — French and African influences, Carnival dances, and Bhangra and Kollattam from its Indian heritage.
2. **The named folk forms, researched** — Bele, Limbo, Quadrille/Jharoo, Ghadka and Kalinda are researched as folk forms — each with its own people, occasion and movement.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks folk dance is one dance → Trinidad and Tobago names many folk forms, from many heritages.
- Thinks research is not dance work → the curriculum itself sets these forms to be researched as folk forms.
