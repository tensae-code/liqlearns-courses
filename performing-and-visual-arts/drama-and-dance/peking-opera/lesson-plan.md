# Lesson plan — Peking Opera

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Peking Opera
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Peking opera, China's best-known opera form — its singing, painted faces, costumes and hats.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China Grade 3 Music Vol.2** — Learning to sing a Peking-opera aria — Li Tiemei's aria from the modern Peking opera The Red Lantern (xipi liushui rhythm, dan role), telling how her family serves the revolution
2. **China Grade 4 Art Vol.2** — Peking-opera headdresses — the tall, ornamented stage hats and crowns of opera costume, seen in a full stage photograph
3. **China Grade 4 Music Vol.2** — Peking opera's role types — sheng (male), dan (female), jing (painted-face) and chou (clown), divided by sex, age, character and status; its four skills are singing, speech, acting and acrobatic fighting; pupils hear arias, name each role type and imitate the performance; painted faces （脸谱） — the coloured patterns that show character: red for loyalty, black for uprightness, white for treachery; a song that names them — blue-faced Du Erdun, red-faced Guan Gong, yellow-faced Dian Wei, white-faced Cao Cao, black-faced Zhang Fei; singing a Peking-opera aria — Yang Zirong's aria from the modern opera Taking Tiger Mountain by Strategy (a sheng role), sung while imitating the character's stage movements; the opera's band — a folk-ensemble piece built on opera tunes (lead instrument the jinghu, with jing-erhu, sanxian and yueqin), and Jijifeng, a gong-and-drum pattern （锣鼓经） for hurried, tense action, played on drum-and-clappers, large and small gongs and cymbals; pupils try playing a Jijifeng together
4. **China Grade 5 Music (jianpu) Vol.2** — "My Homeland Is Beijing" — a "Beijing song" （京歌）: a modern song built wholly from Peking-opera singing tunes, in three parts, the last lifting the melody higher in opera fashion
5. **China HS Drama Performance** — The three marks of Chinese xiqu — comprehensiveness (singing, speech, acting and acrobatic fighting — chang, nian, zuo, da — woven into one), conventionality (every movement, tune, drum pattern and fight codified: dong you shi, nian you diao), and virtuality (a whip in the hand is a galloping horse, two banners a carriage, a small curtain a sedan chair — the stage suggests, the audience imagines); and the four role types — sheng (old, young, martial, child), dan (blue-cloth, flower, martial, old), jing the painted-face (colour telling loyal from treacherous), chou the white-nosed clown; xiqu in performance — Kunqu's Peony Pavilion ("All the flowers in their glory", Du Liniang's spring longing, body movement fitted word by word to the tune); Peking opera's At the Three-Fork Inn, a fight in pitch darkness played in full light by virtuality alone, the percussion band (wuchang) driving every move; and two short plays rehearsed by the class
