# Peking Opera

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about peking opera. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — China Grade 3 Music Vol.2

Learning to sing a Peking-opera aria — Li Tiemei's aria from the modern Peking opera The Red Lantern (xipi liushui rhythm, dan role), telling how her family serves the revolution

## Sub lesson 2 — China Grade 4 Art Vol.2

Peking-opera headdresses — the tall, ornamented stage hats and crowns of opera costume, seen in a full stage photograph

## Sub lesson 3 — China Grade 4 Music Vol.2

Peking opera's role types — sheng (male), dan (female), jing (painted-face) and chou (clown), divided by sex, age, character and status; its four skills are singing, speech, acting and acrobatic fighting; pupils hear arias, name each role type and imitate the performance; painted faces （脸谱） — the coloured patterns that show character: red for loyalty, black for uprightness, white for treachery; a song that names them — blue-faced Du Erdun, red-faced Guan Gong, yellow-faced Dian Wei, white-faced Cao Cao, black-faced Zhang Fei; singing a Peking-opera aria — Yang Zirong's aria from the modern opera Taking Tiger Mountain by Strategy (a sheng role), sung while imitating the character's stage movements; the opera's band — a folk-ensemble piece built on opera tunes (lead instrument the jinghu, with jing-erhu, sanxian and yueqin), and Jijifeng, a gong-and-drum pattern （锣鼓经） for hurried, tense action, played on drum-and-clappers, large and small gongs and cymbals; pupils try playing a Jijifeng together

## Sub lesson 4 — China Grade 5 Music (jianpu) Vol.2

"My Homeland Is Beijing" — a "Beijing song" （京歌）: a modern song built wholly from Peking-opera singing tunes, in three parts, the last lifting the melody higher in opera fashion

## Sub lesson 5 — China HS Drama Performance

The three marks of Chinese xiqu — comprehensiveness (singing, speech, acting and acrobatic fighting — chang, nian, zuo, da — woven into one), conventionality (every movement, tune, drum pattern and fight codified: dong you shi, nian you diao), and virtuality (a whip in the hand is a galloping horse, two banners a carriage, a small curtain a sedan chair — the stage suggests, the audience imagines); and the four role types — sheng (old, young, martial, child), dan (blue-cloth, flower, martial, old), jing the painted-face (colour telling loyal from treacherous), chou the white-nosed clown; xiqu in performance — Kunqu's Peony Pavilion ("All the flowers in their glory", Du Liniang's spring longing, body movement fitted word by word to the tune); Peking opera's At the Three-Fork Inn, a fight in pitch darkness played in full light by virtuality alone, the percussion band (wuchang) driving every move; and two short plays rehearsed by the class
