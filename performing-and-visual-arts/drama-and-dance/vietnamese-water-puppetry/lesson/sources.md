| Vietnam — Lesson 12 "Forming water-puppetry figures" — the forming of the water-puppet figures (nhân vật múa rối nước) — the fairy figures and chú tễu, the puppet figures in the water puppetry art, and the figures of the king, the mandarin and the soldier; in the water puppetry art, the puppet is made of wood, with a body part floating up on the water surface to perform, and the part sunk under the water surface keeping the puppet floating on top and being the place where the control system is installed for the puppet to move; the forming of the puppet is usually whimsical (ngộ nghĩnh) and bears a highly stylised character following the folk statue-carving tradition; the puppet's colours are usually brilliant; the stage in the water puppetry art is called the puppet house (nhà rối) or the water pavilion (thuỷ đình) and uses the water surface to perform the puppets, behind it there is a screen (màn che), and around it are decorated flags, fans, parasols (voi), dragons (long) and more; the performers stand behind the screen controlling the puppets; and water puppetry (múa rối nước) is a traditional folk stage art form, appearing from the wet-rice civilisation (nền văn hoá lúa nước); from an art bearing folk elements, water puppetry has become a traditional art and a unique, characteristic creation of Vietnam — puppetry exists in many countries of the world, but water puppetry exists only in Vietnam (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 50 |
