# Playing a Person

## Do it first

Watch the next person who passes you — really watch. How do they dress? How do they move? What do they carry? Now copy ONE thing about them: their walk, or the way they hold what they carry. You have started playing a person.

## Sub lesson 1 — Watch first — what a theatre game is

Theatre is a performing art that shows a community its own way of life, visibly, to the community itself.

A **theatre game** is acting that imitates a person's **character**. You imitate what you have observed: their **dress**, their **movement**, their **character**, their **actions**, their dance — matching what you observed to what you want to show.

So the rule of this whole lesson: **observe first**. No watching, no playing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Father, mother — and the teacher

To act a **father**, the book lists the needed things: a **gabi** (a wrap), a **hat**, a **stick**, short trousers, long trousers, a **coat**, and the like. Other characters need their own matching things. Look at the father and mother in the picture, then each of you act the father's role and the mother's role.

[Picture 9703 — the things of the roles: a gabi wrap, a hat, a stick and a coat for the father; a teacher's chalk and book; a painter's brush and easel]

To act the **teacher**: enter the class, chalk in hand, and teach. In groups, act the teacher for each other. (The teacher's praise song belongs to the music lessons; here the teacher is a role to play, with the teacher's own movements and care.)

## Sub lesson 3 — The soldier and the health worker

Act the **health worker** from the picture: the one who cares for the sick, who examines, who helps. Groups act the role from the picture's own details.

Act the **soldier** from the pictures: upright, disciplined, marching. In groups, act the soldier's role — the march in place, the straight back, the steady look.

## Sub lesson 4 — The artist — and seller and buyer

Act the **artist** — the painter at work, brush in hand before the canvas. Each of you act the artist's role for the teacher.

Then the market: in groups, act the **seller** and the **buyer** — the seller praising the goods, the buyer asking, bargaining, deciding. Two roles that need each other: a market scene is a small play already.
