# Lesson plan — Playing a Person

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Playing a Person
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** to play any person, first OBSERVE them — their dress, movement, character and actions — then dress the role with its own things and act what you observed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Playing Family Roles — father and mother return here as the first two roles of the wider set, now with the book's own costume lists.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Watch first — what a theatre game is** — theatre defined, and the theatre game: imitate a person's dress, movement, character, action and dance — observe first.
2. **Father, mother — and the teacher** — father and mother with the book's costume list (gabi, hat, stick, coat); the teacher entering with chalk to teach.
3. **The soldier and the health worker** — the health worker's caring role and the soldier's upright march, each acted from its picture.
4. **The artist — and seller and buyer** — the artist (painter) at the easel; seller and buyer bargaining in the market.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Playing Family Roles. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a theatre game is and what it imitates.
2. List the things needed to act a father.
3. Act father, mother and teacher.
4. Act the health worker and the soldier from their pictures.
5. Act the artist, and play seller and buyer in a bargain.

## What you need
Simple role things: a wrap (gabi), a hat, a stick, a coat, chalk, a brush. Borrowed, never bought.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner observes a passing person and copies one thing about them before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — observe-first rule; then the roles in the book's order — father/mother, health worker, soldier, teacher, artist, seller and buyer — taught in the lesson's four sub lessons (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9703]: the things of the roles — a gabi wrap, hat, stick and coat; chalk and a book; a brush and easel — the student matches each set of things to its role.
   - ADD AUDIO: a market bargain heard aloud — seller praising, buyer asking — the student names who is speaking at each line.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name the five things you observe before playing a person, and the father's things from the book's list.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — observe one worker in your area for five minutes today; tomorrow, act their role for your family.

## If the learner is stuck
- Acts a role nobody can name → go back to observation: pick the person's ONE clearest detail and build the role on it.
- No costume things at hand → movements and actions carry a role further than clothes; clothes are the helper, not the role.
- The book itself mislabels the artist's activity number — the lesson follows the roles' order, not the printed number (see sources.md).
