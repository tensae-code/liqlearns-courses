# Lesson plan — What Performing Art Does for a Community

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** What Performing Art Does for a Community
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** performing art's benefits: in social life — lightening shared work, binding people at weddings and festivals, carrying news and knowledge — and in children's growth: body, mind, behaviour, language and memory.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole Drama and Dance title — the learner can sing, dance and act; this lesson says what all that art does for the community that holds it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Art — the foundation of the community's house** — a people's identity-bearing art values live with them for ages, binding the community and bringing tolerance and living together.
2. **Performing art in social life** — lightening shared work (debo, farming), binding weddings, festivals and births, carrying information and indigenous knowledge, accompanying film and theatre.
3. **Performing art and children's growth** — physical, mental and behavioural change; creative power; language and speech; learning with enjoyment; memory; love of history, culture, environment and family; a well-formed body.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Making Art Together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how performing art lightens shared work.
2. Say how it binds people and carries information and indigenous knowledge.
3. List what performing art develops in children.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner recalls a time music or dance changed a hard day — work made lighter, a celebration made warmer — before benefits are listed (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the opener first — art as the community's foundation — then art in social life, then art in children's development (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion three things performing art does: one for work, one for celebrations, one for children.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Write about one celebration in your community and the performing art inside it: what would be lost without the art?
