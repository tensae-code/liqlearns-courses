# What Performing Art Does for a Community

## Do it first
Picture farmers working together all day under the sun — singing. Picture a wedding with no music. Which picture feels wrong? Performing art is not decoration on a community's life. This lesson counts what it actually does.

## Sub lesson 1 — Art — the foundation of the community's house

A people's **identity-bearing art values** live with them for ages, accumulated **generation to generation**. The book gives art a building's place: art is the **foundation of the community's house**, with great power to **bind its parts together** and to bring long ages of **tolerance and living together**.

A foundation is not decoration. Remove it, and the house does not merely look worse — it falls.

## Sub lesson 2 — Performing art in social life

Watch traditional music at work. It **lightens the load** of work needing wide energy and long time — **debo** collective work and farm work move to its rhythm. At **weddings, festivals and births** it develops people's relations and culture, and expresses the shared feeling of a group — the country-feeling and love that make peoples one.

It was used to **exchange information** in unlettered communities, passing ideas generation to generation; it **passes indigenous knowledge down**; it **accompanies the other arts** — film and theatre; and it plants a philosophy for the community's spiritual life.

## Sub lesson 3 — Performing art and children's growth

For children, the book's list is a whole education. Performing art brings **physical, mental and behavioural change**; gives power to **show and express creative works**; develops **language and speech** and easy communication; lets children **learn while enjoying themselves** and develops **memory**; grows their **love of their history, culture, environment and family**; and, by practice, gives them a **well-formed body**.

Singing, dancing and acting are not breaks from learning. For a child, they *are* learning — of the happiest kind.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion three things performing art does: one for work, one for celebrations, one for children.
