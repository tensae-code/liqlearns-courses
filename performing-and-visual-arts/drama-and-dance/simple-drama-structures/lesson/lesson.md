# Simple Drama Structures

## Do it first
Tell the smallest story you can: someone wants something, something happens, and it ends. You just used a play's simplest shape — a beginning, a middle and an end. This lesson is about recognising the simple shapes a play takes. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — A play has a shape (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

A play is not just people talking. A play **takes a shape** — the guide calls these the *simple drama structures* (ቀላል የትወና መዋቅሮች).

Learning drama begins with **recognising** those simple shapes: seeing how a little play is put together, the way you can see how a house is put together from its walls and roof.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three-beat shape (teacher guide — evidence)

[Picture 14400 — the three-beat shape — beginning, middle, end]

The first shape to practise recognising is the smallest one: a play that opens, moves and closes — a **beginning**, a **middle** and an **end**.

In the beginning we meet the people. In the middle, something happens to them. In the end, it finishes — happily or sadly, but finished. When you tell any tiny story, check: can your listener find all three beats?

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the three beats of your tiny story, and say which beat is which.

## Do It Yourself
Make a three-beat mini-play with friends: one beat to open it, one beat in the middle, one beat to close it.
