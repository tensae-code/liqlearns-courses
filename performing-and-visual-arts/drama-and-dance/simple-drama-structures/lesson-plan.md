# Lesson plan — Simple Drama Structures

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Simple Drama Structures
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the simple shapes a play takes — recognising the simple structures of drama, as the Grade 2 teacher guide's §1.3 names them (cited at section level: the guide is a scan read visually).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Acting Together — the learner has already made simple drama in a group; this lesson names the shapes that drama takes.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **A play has a shape (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide's §1.3: simple drama structures — recognising the simple shapes a play takes.
2. **The three-beat shape** — the simplest recognisable shape in practice: a beginning, a middle and an end.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Acting Together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say that a play takes a shape, and name the simplest shape's three beats.
2. Find the beginning, middle and end in a tiny story.
3. Build a mini-play on the three-beat shape.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells a tiny story in three beats — a beginning, a middle and an end — before the shapes are named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the guide's §1.3 topic in evidence form: recognising the simple shapes a play takes (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 14400]: a diagram of a play's simplest shape: beginning, middle, end — the student points to each beat in a tiny story.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion the three beats of your tiny story, and say which beat is which.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Make a three-beat mini-play with friends: one beat to open it, one beat in the middle, one beat to close it.
