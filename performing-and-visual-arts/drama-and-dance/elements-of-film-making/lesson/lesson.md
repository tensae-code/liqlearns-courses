# Elements of Film Making

## Do it first
Film the same arrival three ways — far, near, from above — and the story *feels* different each time, though nothing else changed. Who holds those choices? The filmmaker, with a toolkit this lesson names. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Camera — shots, angles, movements, focus (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

[Picture 14404 — four camera shots of the same actor]

The camera speaks first. Its **shots**: **wide, medium, close-up and point-of-view**. Its **angles**: **low and high**. Its **movements**: **pan** (turning in place) and **tracking** (travelling with the action). And its **focus**: in and out, **soft, deep and shallow** — choosing what the eye may rest on, and what melts away.

Four choices, made in every second of every film you have ever watched.

## Sub lesson 2 — Before the camera — mise-en-scène, light, colour (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Everything placed **before** the camera has a French name: **mise-en-scène** — production design, **costume**, **colour**, **lighting**, **performance**, **blocking** and **framing**, taken as one arranged whole.

Around it work **composition** and the **colour palette**; **setting and props**; **costume and make-up**; and **lighting — hard against soft** — each light telling a different emotional truth about the same face.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sound, editing, performance (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

A film is also *heard*. Its **sound** has kinds: the **composed score**, **dialogue**, **narration/voice-over** and **sound effects**.

Its **editing** chooses a pace — **rapid cutting** against **slow cutting** — and its **performance** is the actor's craft: **facial expression, body language and voice**. Camera, arrangement, sound, cut, performance: the toolkit is complete.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: four camera shots, two angles, and the three kinds of film sound.

## Do It Yourself
Storyboard a 30-second film on paper: choose each shot, its angle, its light and its sound, and write why.
