# Lesson plan — Elements of Film Making

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Elements of Film Making
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the filmmaker's toolkit as the guide lists it: camera shots, angles and movements; focus; mise-en-scène; composition and colour; setting, props, costume and make-up; lighting; sound; editing; and performance.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Video: Moving Pictures — the learner knows shot, scene and sequence; this lesson gives film making's full element list.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Camera — shots, angles, movements, focus (teacher guide — evidence)** — wide, medium, close-up, point-of-view shots; low/high angles; pan and tracking movements; focus in/out, soft, deep, shallow.
2. **Before the camera — mise-en-scène, light, colour (teacher guide — evidence)** — mise-en-scène (production design, costume, colour, lighting, performance, blocking, framing); composition; colour palette; setting and props; costume & make-up; hard vs soft lighting.
3. **Sound, editing, performance (teacher guide — evidence)** — sound — composed score, dialogue, narration/voice-over, sound effects; editing — rapid vs slow cutting; performance — facial expression, body language, voice.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Elements of Theatre. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the camera shots, angles, movements and focus kinds.
2. Say what mise-en-scène holds.
3. Name film's sound kinds and editing's pace choices.

## What you need
The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner films or imagines one moment — a friend arriving — three ways: far away, close up, and from above, before the toolkit is named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the camera first (shots, angles, movements, focus), then what stands before it (mise-en-scène, composition, colour, light), then sound, editing and performance (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 14404]: four frames of one actor — wide, medium, close-up, point-of-view — the student names each shot and says what it shows.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion: four camera shots, two angles, and the three kinds of film sound.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Storyboard a 30-second film on paper: choose each shot, its angle, its light and its sound, and write why.
