# Lesson plan — Acting Together

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Acting Together
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a group can build a small play together — taking characters from a story, making scenes before and after it, and passing a clear message to the people watching.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Playing Family Roles and Playing a Person — the learner can already step into one role. This lesson joins the roles together into a group play with a message.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Acting together in a group** — three children acting together — a play is a conversation of bodies and voices.
2. **Simple ways of making drama** — the book's four simple methods: a character in a situation; an idea from a story; a before/after scene; the character placed inside the event.
3. **Passing a message by drama** — drama passes a message, funny or sad — and builds self-confidence, speaking skill, body and mind.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Playing Family Roles and Playing a Person. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Act a scene in a group, listening to partners.
2. Name and use the four simple ways of making drama.
3. Rehearse a short drama taken from a book and show it.
4. Agree a message before acting, and carry it in every scene.
5. Say what drama builds in the actor.

## What you need
A story or short drama from a book. A small open space. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner's group acts a wordless helping scene before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — group acting first, then the four methods, then the message — the books' own climb (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading a very short story aloud — the group listens, then takes its characters and plays the story.
3. **Say it to your companion** — tell your companion the four ways of making drama, and the message your group's last scene carried.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — with friends, play the scene that happens AFTER a story you all know. What came next?

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- The group scene falls apart → shrink it: three actors, one happening, one minute. Listening, not size, is the skill.
- No message can be found → ask the group: what should the watchers think about on their way home? That is the message.
- Before/after scenes confuse → draw the story as three boxes: before — the story — after. Act one box at a time.
