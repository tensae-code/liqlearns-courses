# Lesson plan — Play: Every Craft Needs the Others

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Play: Every Craft Needs the Others
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a four-child play in summary: Aster, Halima, Kasa and Abdu argue about whose father's craft matters most, and settle it — every craft needs the others, and none stands above another.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner knowing the crafts around them — carpenter, weaver, blacksmith, potter, plumber, builder — from daily life.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **The argument — whose craft is greatest?** — Aster, Halima, Kasa and Abdu each claim their father's craft matters most.
2. **The settling — every craft needs the others** — Abdu and Halima settle it: every professional uses the others' products; none is above or below another.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Theatre Is. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the play's story in summary, naming Aster, Halima, Kasa and Abdu.
2. Say how the argument is settled.
3. Give your own example of two crafts that need each other.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says which craft in their own area they think matters most, and why, before the play's story is told (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the play's story in summary only — the argument, then the settling of it — then performing the idea in the learners' own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion the play's story: who argued, and how the argument ended.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Write down two crafts in your area and one way each one needs the other.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
