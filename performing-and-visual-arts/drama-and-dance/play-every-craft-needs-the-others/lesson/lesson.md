# Play: Every Craft Needs the Others

## Do it first
A carpenter makes chairs, tables and shelves. A weaver makes clothes. If you had to choose — whose work matters more? Hold your answer. Four children in a play argue about exactly this.

## Sub lesson 1 — The argument — whose craft is greatest?

In the play, four children — **Aster, Halima, Kasa and Abdu** — fall into an argument. Each one says: my father's work is the greatest.

The carpenter's side points to his work: chairs, tables and shelves — where would anyone sit, eat or keep things without them? The weaver's side answers with clothes: nobody goes out into the world unclothed. Each child argues for their own father's craft, and nobody gives way.

## Sub lesson 2 — The settling — every craft needs the others

**Abdu and Halima** settle the argument. Look, they say: the carpenter works wearing the weaver's clothes. The weaver works sitting on the carpenter's chair. Every professional uses the products of the others.

So the play ends in agreement: the blacksmith, the potter, the plumber, the builder — **none is above another, and none is below another. All support each other.**

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the play's story: who argued, and how the argument ended.

## Do It Yourself
Write down two crafts in your area and one way each one needs the other.
