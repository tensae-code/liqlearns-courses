| Bolivia — The Machetero of the Beni — the dance that identifies the Beni (in the native tongue, Chipiperono), the Gran Moxos region's own cultural representation: born in the colonial era of the Jesuit settlements over older aboriginal ceremonies danced to the sun, the moon, thunder, the rainbow, nature and the new dawn, venerating the jungle's animals — tiger, caiman, pink dolphin (bufeo), deer — as mythological beings (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/danzas-folcloricas-de-bolivia`) | Book 3, printed pp. 9–10 |

| Bolivia — Dance as a written record — a people without writing recorded its truths in movement: every movement of the originary dances has a meaning and is not there by chance; the dances are an instruction notebook (un cuaderno de instrucciones) that only needs interpreting, to be shared with those who come after (teacher compendium — evidence; original target `drama-and-dance/danzas-folcloricas-de-bolivia`) | Book 4, PDF p. 8 |
| Bolivia — The warrior dances of the altiplano — the Qhina-quinas, in hats decorated with coloured feathers and the khawa, a jaguar-skin corselet over the shoulders, the quena leading over tambor and bombo; the Chuqilas, in tanned leather on the back and white canvas pollerines, fighting as if attacked, with the kusillo, the funny character who accompanies them, the choreography ending in the capture of the vicuña; and the Jula Jula, vigorous and warlike, in march rhythm, played in two answering troops of 20 to 25 people blowing sikus of four and three tubes separately and responsorially, with no percussion at all (teacher compendium — evidence; original target `drama-and-dance/danzas-folcloricas-de-bolivia`) | Book 4, PDF p. 9 |
| Bolivia — The forms of folk dance music — the primary form (a segment, "part A", repeated continually — much used in autochthonous music and children's rounds); the binary form (A–B, two different melodic segments, the first often vocal and the second instrumental, or the reverse); and the ternary form (A–B–C, the first two alike in rhythmic pattern, the third clearly different — common in the cueca, the carnavalitos and the tonadas tarijeñas) (teacher compendium — evidence; original target `drama-and-dance/danzas-folcloricas-de-bolivia`) | Book 4, PDF p. 42 |
