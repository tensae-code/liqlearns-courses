# Bolivian Folk Dances

## Do it first

Name one thing from Bolivia that belongs in this lesson — a song, a dance, a pot, a carving, a cloth. Say where it comes from and who makes it. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dances of the west

Caporales, with its athletic turns and bells; the Tinku, the ritual encounter-combat of Macha in Potosi, an offering to Pachamama; the Tobas; the Chutas of La Paz carnival; and the Balseros of Rurrenabaque.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dances of the plains and valleys

The Taquirari, dance of the arrow, and the Chobena of the Chiquitania; the Machetero of the Beni; and the valleys' cueca, pujllay, bailecito, huayno and chacarera.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dance as a written record

A people without writing recorded its truths in movement: every movement of the originary dances has a meaning — the dances are an instruction notebook that only needs interpreting; and the forms of folk dance music: primary, binary and ternary.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson holds, part by part, in your own words. Companion checks: did every part get named, with its Bolivian name kept?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Bolivia's own way of teaching its arts?
