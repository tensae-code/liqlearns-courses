# Lesson plan — Bolivian Folk Dances

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Lesson:** Bolivian Folk Dances
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bolivia's folk dances by region — Caporales, Tinku and Tobas of the west and Taquirari and Chobena of the plains, with the Machetero of the Beni and the dances of the valleys.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Dances of the west** — Caporales, with its athletic turns and bells; the Tinku, the ritual encounter-combat of Macha in Potosi, an offering to Pachamama; the Tobas; the Chutas of La Paz carnival; and the Balseros of Rurrenabaque.
2. **Dances of the plains and valleys** — The Taquirari, dance of the arrow, and the Chobena of the Chiquitania; the Machetero of the Beni; and the valleys' cueca, pujllay, bailecito, huayno and chacarera.
3. **Dance as a written record** — A people without writing recorded its truths in movement: every movement of the originary dances has a meaning — the dances are an instruction notebook that only needs interpreting; and the forms of folk dance music: primary, binary and ternary.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
