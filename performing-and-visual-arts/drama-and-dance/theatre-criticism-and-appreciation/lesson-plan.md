# Lesson plan — Theatre Criticism and Appreciation

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Theatre Criticism and Appreciation
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Judging a play — theatrical taste, the theatre critic's work, criticism of a show and reviewing a traditional performance.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theatrical Taste** — theatrical taste (al-tadhawwuq al-masrahi) — the learned skill of understanding and appreciating a play in an artistic, critical way — and its six requirements: knowing theatre's basic elements, developing the sense of beauty, developing judgement and analysis, learning and practice, developing innovation and creativity, and precision of observation
2. **The Theatre Critic** — who the theatre critic is — a specialist of wide theatre culture who analyses works deeply, objectively and without bias — and the critic's four tasks: analyse the text, evaluate the actors' performance, evaluate the work's effect on its audience, and evaluate the directing as one integrated whole; practised in pairs on a shipwreck story, one pupil acting while the other criticises
3. **Criticism of the Theatrical Show** — how a show is criticised element by element — the text (clarity of idea and message, plot sequence, character building, language), the acting (body and voice expression, team harmony, silences, credibility), the directing (use of space, atmosphere, imagination) and the scenography (decor, lighting, costumes, makeup, sound and visual effects) — so strengths and weaknesses are found and the work improved
