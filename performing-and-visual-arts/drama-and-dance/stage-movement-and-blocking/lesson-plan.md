# Lesson plan — Stage Movement and Blocking

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Stage Movement and Blocking
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how an actor's stance (وقفة) works on stage — the kinds of stage stance and what each is used for, holding the correct stance inside a scene, and expressing feelings and character through stance alone, without words — as Egypt's Grade 8 Theatre Education student book teaches it in its lesson "My Stance Speaks".
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the acting lessons — the learner already plays a person and acts together with a group. This lesson currently stands on the Egypt stance placement only; its sources say so plainly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The kinds of stage stance** — the kinds of stance (وقفة) and what each kind is used for on stage.
2. **Stance speaks** — holding the correct stance inside a scene, and expressing feelings and character through stance alone, without words.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Playing a Person; Theatre on a Stage. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name that there are kinds of stage stance and say what a stance is used for.
2. Hold the correct stance inside a scene.
3. Show a feeling and a character through stance alone, without words.

## What you need
Space to stand and be seen, and classmates to watch and read your stance.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — stand as a guard, then as a tired traveller, without speaking: can your classmates read each one? (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the kinds of stance first, then stance as expression (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of actors holding different stances on a stage — the student says what each stance is telling the audience.
   - ADD GIF: one actor changing stance — proud, afraid, tired — without a word — the student names each feeling as it appears.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain why a stance must be held correctly inside a scene (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — made later, when videos are made.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — build a three-stance character study (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

- GIF (described only) — an actor changing stance without words → the student names each feeling.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student listens and answers.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks stance is just standing still → a stance is a chosen way of standing that the audience reads: it carries feeling and character.
- Keeps adding words → this lesson's second half is stance *alone*: if the watchers cannot read it silently, adjust the stance, not the speech.
