# Stage Movement and Blocking

## Do it first

Quick game: stand as a guard at a gate — no words. Now stand as a traveller who has walked all day — no words. If your classmates could read both, your body was already doing this lesson: it was speaking through how you stood.

This lesson comes from Egypt's Grade 8 Theatre Education student book, Lesson 2, "My Stance Speaks" — the actor's craft taught step by step, stance before speech.

## Sub lesson 1 — The kinds of stage stance

A **stance (وقفة)** is the way an actor stands on stage. It is not accidental: the book teaches that stances come in **kinds**, and **each kind is used for its own purpose** inside a performance.

Because the audience reads a stance before it hears a word, choosing the stance is part of acting itself: the right stance for the moment helps the scene say what it means; the wrong one argues against it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stance speaks

Inside a scene, the actor **holds the correct stance** — the stance the character and the moment need, held steadily while the scene goes on.

And a stance can carry the whole message alone: the book trains the actor to **express feelings and character through stance alone, without words** — pride, fear, tiredness, a whole person, shown by how they stand, so the audience reads the inside of the character from the outside of the body.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what is a stage stance, and what can it do without any words? Companion checks: kinds of stance, each with its use? Holding the correct stance inside a scene? Feelings and character expressed through stance alone?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose three characters and hold each one's stance for a watcher to read — no words allowed.
2. Play a short scene with a partner, holding your character's correct stance from start to finish.
3. Write one sentence: which was harder — finding the right stance, or holding it — and why?
