# Hands-on — Brazilian Folk and Popular Dances

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Samba born from the African umbigada dance and Portuguese modinhas
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: samba born from the African umbigada dance and Portuguese modinhas; Caribbean rumba, mambo, merengue and chá-chá-chá from the same mixing

## Frevo
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: frevo: Recife's carnival dance, the passistas balancing with little umbrellas, the fanfarra brass-and-percussion orchestra

## Fandango Paranaense
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: Fandango Paranaense: viola, rabeca and adufo with clog-stamping (tamanco) dances at communal harvest work parties (mutirão)

## Urban tribes (street dance
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: urban tribes (street dance, hip-hop, axé, pagode, samba, sertanejo, punks, hippies) and how the groups we join shape the dances we love

## The samba school parade
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: the samba school parade: one enredo joining music, fantasy costumes, floats and thousands dancing their own way; researching the kinds of samba (canção, choro, breque, gafieira, morro, carnavalesco)

## Indigenous dances of Brazil
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: Indigenous dances of Brazil: the caxiri ritual of the Uaupés river peoples — feather headdresses, flutes, two formations (slow interlaced line, fast solo) dancing all night around the caxiri vessel; re-enacting the slow formation

## Brazil's folk dances
Do the practice this part names, with real materials and a partner: Brazil's folk dances: frevo and maracatu of the Northeast, and the quadrilha of the Festas Juninas with its sanfona; organising a quadrilha from grandparents' memories
