# Carabiné Dance of the Dominican Republic

## Do it first

Quick start: On tiptoe! The caller calls, the circle turns. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The carabiné and where it comes from

The **carabiné** is a **Dominican folk rhythm and dance** from the **early-19th-century French occupation**: soldiers on duty are said to have **danced in coordination with the carbine on the shoulder**. Of **strong African influence**, it is an **ancestor of merengue and kompa**, played with **tambora, pandero and acordeón**, like the mangulina and the pambiche.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dancing the carabiné

The carabiné is danced **on tiptoe** — *en la punt'el pie* — under the **bastonero**, the caller. It opens with a **sway**; then the **gentlemen circle and change ladies until each finds his partner**, in the ordered turns called **ron entre ron** — circle upon circle — and it closes with the **reverencia**, a bow to the public. Her dress: a **skirt with two pleats, a side bun and a showy rose**; his: **blue jacket and trousers, a cane hat and a bow tie**.
