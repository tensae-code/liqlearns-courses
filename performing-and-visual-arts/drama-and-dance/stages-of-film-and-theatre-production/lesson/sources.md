Suriname's own way: one integrated arts course — Kunst- en cultuureducatie (KCE) — in six domains (Music, Dance, Visual Arts, Cultural Heritage, Theatre, Media), each domain a series of one-hour lessons built on the same frame: the IKA lesson shape (Introductie–Kern–Afsluiting, the core in three phases — warm-up, technique, own work and showing moment) and the Big Idea design process in four phases (orient, research, experiment, reflect), every series answering one research question and ending in a presentation; each domain is deliberately fused with another school subject (music with arithmetic, dance with geography, visual arts with history, theatre with language); the course's heart is Suriname itself — its many peoples' dances, dress, jewellery, heritage and theatre forms — held together by the Alakondre ideal, "Alakondre na wan, wan na Alakondre" (unity in diversity); assessment is by evaluation card and portfolio, process over product. Suriname's own way: the deepening year runs the same six domains on the same frame as leerjaar 7, but at competency levels B/C — pupils switch independently between the four design phases, master the craft itself, argue their choices, handle the emotions of others in their work, and present independently in an unknown space for an unknown audience; music gives meaning to a calculated music work built on the rhythmic patterns of Suriname's five peoples; dance travels a safe route from home through the Amazon interior into Brazil, in awasa, indigenous and Latin American steps; visual arts add hatching and pattern design to the clothing work and end in a fashion show instead of an exhibition; heritage places an object into another culture and performs what that does to its meaning as an oral art form; theatre adapts a known story into an Alakondre Mato; media builds the one-minute film on a storyboard and uploads it to YouTube. | Suriname — Media Les 1 "Mindmap" — making a mindmap in three steps — write the main subject large in a circle in the middle of unlined A3 or A4 paper; draw the thick branches around it for the sub-topics, each sub-topic in its own colour; work each sub-topic out in thin twigs of keywords, pictures and symbols, thinking freely, nothing too strange — with example film subjects named: an advertisement for Fernandes soft drinks' special offers, the grand opening of Suriname's most modern shopping centre, "Suriname in 20 years"; the mindmap is the guideline the whole film series is built on (teacher guide — evidence; original target `drama-and-dance/mindmapping-a-film-story`) | Book 3, printed pp. 209–212 |
| Suriname — Media Les 2 "Het omzetten van woorden in fragmenten/scènes" (Turning Words into Fragments/Scenes): the storyboard — a storyboard is a draaiboek (shooting script) of consecutive drawings — the whole story worked out in pictures so you can see exactly what must be filmed; the pupils draw 8 to 10 boxes on A4 (five rows of two), each box showing what happens, where, and who is in it, with the shot decided in the box — a close-up from nearby or a total shot — and any words to be spoken written in; a shot is one continuous take, and consecutive shots together form a scene or fragment; the shots' order is the timeline, and the finished storyboard is ticked off shot by shot while filming — the drawings need not be beautiful, only clear (teacher guide — evidence; original target `drama-and-dance/storyboarding-a-film`) | Book 3, printed pp. 213–215 |
| Suriname — Media Les 4: publishing and screening — the finished one-minute film is uploaded to a YouTube channel — the class's own or the group's, with a strong password, a well-chosen channel name and the thumbnail not forgotten — and then screened with a short spoken introduction by each duo before an invited audience of parents, neighbours and other pupils, who judge whether the assignment was carried out, whether the film's message came across, whether the title fits the story, and whether the media tips were kept (slug first proposed in Book 1, leerjaar 7) (teacher guide — evidence; original target `drama-and-dance/the-one-minute-film`) | Book 3, printed pp. 220–222 |
