# Stages of Film and Theatre Production

## Do it first
A film does not begin when the camera rolls — and it does not end when filming stops. Seven stages carry it from an idea to an audience. Shuffle them, guess their order — then meet the guide's.

## Sub lesson 1 — Film's stages, in order (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

A film is made in stages. **Story development** first. Then **pre-production** — planning, locations, casting, schedule and budget, held by the line/production manager. Then **production**, whose heart is **principal photography** — the camera rolls; the guide calls it the **most expensive phase**.

Then **wrap**: strike the set, return everything, keep **full records**. Then **post-production** — edit, visual effects, music, sound design, **picture lock**, delivery. And finally **distribution**: the film reaches its audience.

## Sub lesson 2 — Theatre's three phases — the contrast (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Theatre's production is told in **three phases**: **pre-production** — play selection and development; **production** — rehearsing; and **post-production** — **audience feedback**.

Set the two side by side and the arts' difference shows: a film is *finished* and then distributed; a theatre work is finished *by* its audience, night after night, and their feedback is its post-production.
