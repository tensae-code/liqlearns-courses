# Lesson plan — Stages of Film and Theatre Production

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Stages of Film and Theatre Production
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a film is made, stage by stage — story development, pre-production, production, principal photography, wrap, post-production, distribution — set against theatre production's three phases.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on From Script to Stage — the learner knows theatre's journey; this lesson gives film's seven stages and the contrast.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Film's stages, in order (teacher guide — evidence)** — story development → pre-production → production → principal photography → wrap → post-production → distribution.
2. **Theatre's three phases — the contrast (teacher guide — evidence)** — pre-production (play selection/development), production (rehearsing), post-production (audience feedback).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: From Script to Stage. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name film's production stages in order.
2. Say what happens at wrap and in post-production.
3. Contrast theatre's three phases.

## What you need
The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner orders seven jumbled stage-cards for making a film — guess first, before the guide's order is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — film's stages in order first, then theatre's three phases as the contrast (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Walk your companion through a film's stages, from story development to distribution.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Write your own tiny production plan: which stage are you in today for a class film, and what happens next?
