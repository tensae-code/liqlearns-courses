| Vietnam — Bài 1 Explore — stage art design defined — the field where the design artist creates, arranges and builds the scene (bối cảnh) serving the play's content, answering the artists' creation and the audience's aesthetic needs; stage art includes the image elements of a stage work — building the scene, lighting, props, make-up, actors' costumes (also called Design for Theatre, Scenography, Stage design, Set design, Production design); to design effectively the artist must establish an overall visual idea (ý tưởng thị giác tổng thể) for the play's spaces and scenes, and needs knowledge of painting, sculpture, photography, interior design and computer graphics plus understanding of costumes, make-up, sound, lighting and backdrops; the related professions: painter–sculptor–artist, filming–photography, graphic design, costumes–make-up–props–lighting, scene sketching; the scene designer works with the director and the design-production group, responsible for the scale models of the scene, perspective drawings, platform systems, structure drawings and everything relating to the play and the public (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/stage-and-film-design`) | Book 13, printed p. 5 |
| Vietnam — Bài 1 Explore — Vietnam's stage art history — beside the court art, many unique folk stage types are performed at the communal-house yard (sân đình), markets and lake surfaces with conventional methods (thủ pháp ước lệ) carrying their own colours; in 2003 Huế court Nhã nhạc was recognised by UNESCO as an intangible cultural heritage of humanity, its stage art design aesthetic, special and unique; in the early 20th century Western stage art imported into Vietnam was received and developed by artists Vũ Đình Long, Đoàn Phú Tứ, Thế Lữ together with painters Bùi Xuân Phái, Tô Ngọc Vân, Văn Cao, Phạm Văn Đôn…, forming the art of "bài trí sân khấu" (stage arrangement); from 1955 the Vietnamese stage changed and developed by leaps with directors Dương Ngọc Đức, Nguyễn Đình Nghị, Đình Quang… and painters Lương Đồng, Phùng Huy Bính, Doãn Châu…, and stage art grew from "bài trí sân khấu" to "thiết kế sân khấu" — today's stage art design subject, now a training branch at the Hanoi University of Theatre and Cinema, the University of Culture – Military Arts and the Ho Chi Minh City University of Theatre and Cinema (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/stage-and-film-design`) | Book 13, printed p. 6 |
| Vietnam — Bài 1 Did-You-Know — the wider history — in the 16th century a play's script paid no attention to the stage art element and scenery and props were not invested in for the actors; from the 17th century the branch developed strongly into many stage forms (spoken drama, music, dance drama…); in 1895 film art appeared and early films were shot directly from stage play scenes with act and scene changes like stage plays; film art design — staging scenes, props, make-up, costumes for realism, history, action, horror and fantasy films — plays an important decisive role in a film's success, building outdoor locations and studio scenes from simple to complex, real to illusory; the film art design artist, the "Chief Artist" (Art Director), is named in the film's titles with the director and main actors and wins the "Art Direction" and "Production Design" categories at Oscar, Cannes and Berlin; in 2009 the Oscar went to Avatar's art designers Rick Carter, Robert Stromberg and Kim Sinclair (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/stage-and-film-design`) | Book 13, printed p. 8 |
| Vietnam — Bài 1 Recognise — the main characteristics of stage art design, attached to the term "creating visual images" (tạo dựng hình ảnh thị giác) — stage space (không gian sân khấu) — the empty space on the performance stage created by the artist in the design drawing for the director to stage (space handling); stage composition (bố cục) — tight, attached, reasonably and beautifully arranged, highly structural, shown by platforms, scenery and objects, the artist using painting knowledge to create shape-mass, line and colour with high aesthetics, obeying geometry's far–near law and architecture parameters, or creating by other art schools (Svoboda's stage design for Othello); scene-building materials in two parts — the hard part (platforms, masses, panels, flat scenery) and the soft part (hanging backdrops, curtains, fine-art effects), very diverse and combinable with modern techniques such as dynamic light effects and light creating 3D effects on the backdrop; and the two space kinds that give the public its awareness of the play — static space (không gian tĩnh), describing the play's place and environment without joining the situations directly, and dynamic space (không gian động), changed by the director's handling, expanding the viewer's imagination and the play's illusory change (stage design for The Caucasian Chalk Circle) (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/stage-and-film-design`) | Book 13, printed p. 9 |
