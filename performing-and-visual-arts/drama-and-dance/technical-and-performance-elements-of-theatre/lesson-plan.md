# Lesson plan — Technical and Performance Elements of Theatre

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Technical and Performance Elements of Theatre
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** theatre's two craft families: the technical elements — scenery/set, costumes, props, lights, sound, make-up — and the performance elements — acting's face, body and voice crafts — held inside the general elements: performers, audience, director, playwright, acting space, design, text.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Elements of Theatre and Costume and Make-Up for Theatre; this lesson gives the guide's full technical/performance anatomy.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Technical elements (teacher guide — evidence)** — scenery/set (curtains, flats, backdrops, platforms), costumes, props, lights (placement, intensity, colour), sound, make-up/wigs/body paint.
2. **Performance elements — and the general elements (teacher guide — evidence)** — acting (face, body, voice), character motivation and analysis, empathy, breath control, vocal expression, inflection, projection, diction, gesture; general: performers, audience, director, playwright, acting space, design aspects, text.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Costume and Make-Up for Theatre. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the technical elements.
2. List the performance elements.
3. Name the general elements of theatre.

## What you need
The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner watches one imagined minute of a play twice — first with lights, set and sound, then stripped bare — and says what vanished (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the technical elements first, then the performance elements, then the general elements that hold them all (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion: three technical elements, three performance elements, three general elements.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — For one scene of a class play, write the technical list (set, props, lights, sound, make-up) and the performance work each actor must do.
