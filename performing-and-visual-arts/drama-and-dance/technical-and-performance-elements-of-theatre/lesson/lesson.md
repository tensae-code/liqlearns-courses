# Technical and Performance Elements of Theatre

## Do it first
A bare stage, an actor, daylight. Now add: a painted backdrop, a lamp's glow, a drum's sound, a costume. The play *grows* — and every addition has a name and a craft. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Technical elements (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

The **technical elements** build the play's world: **scenery/set** — curtains, flats, backdrops, platforms; **costumes**; **props**; **lights** — placed, and tuned by **intensity and colour**; **sound**; and **make-up, wigs and body paint**.

None of them acts. All of them make acting *believable* — they are the world's body, built by technicians and designers before the first line is spoken.

## Sub lesson 2 — Performance elements — and the general elements (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

The **performance elements** are the actor's crafts: **acting** of face, body and voice; **character motivation** and **character analysis**; **empathy**; **speaking** with **breath control, vocal expression, inflection, projection and diction**; and **nonverbal expression — gesture and facial expression**.

Holding both families stands the **general elements**: **performers, audience, director, playwright, acting space, design aspects and text**. Remove any one, and there is no theatre left to build or perform.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: three technical elements, three performance elements, three general elements.

## Do It Yourself
For one scene of a class play, write the technical list (set, props, lights, sound, make-up) and the performance work each actor must do.
