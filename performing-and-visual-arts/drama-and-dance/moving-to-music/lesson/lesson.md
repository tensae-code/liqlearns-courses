# Moving to Music

## Do it first

Imagine music playing — your favourite. Let your shoulders answer it. Let your feet answer it. Nobody taught you that movement; it came from the music through you. That is free movement. This lesson protects it, practises it — and then pairs it with a partner.

## Sub lesson 1 — Free movement to music

**Free movement to music** means: you express games, music and songs with whatever body movement or dance suits you — **without restriction, following your own wish**.

Hear the New Year music and the dances around you? In free movement, your body answers them your way. In groups, show the class the cultural games and dances you know from your area — free movement is also where your own area's dances enter the room.

The Grade 1 dance strand begins exactly here: moving the body to what is heard — free and guided body movement to music, swaying and stepping in a circle to a beat.

## Sub lesson 2 — Free dance movements

**Free dance movement** is body movement and dance done to music **without being limited to set dances**.

Look at the pictures of various traditional dances — then, in groups, show dances you know from your area. The set dances teach you the country's ways; free dance lets your own way speak. A dancer needs both.

## Sub lesson 3 — Partner dance with directions

Now the song **ዳንስ** (Dans — Dance) pairs you up. Choose **two by two**, hold hands, play with a friend.

The directions ARE the dance: **jump together, let go — right, left, forward, back; stamp knees and stamp the ground with the feet; forward together, jumping; up-down with hands raised; turn your partner round.**

[Picture 9707 — partner dance directions: pairs holding hands, arrows for right, left, forward and back, and a turning pair]

Then the practice: in pairs, perform a song you know combined with body movement, following the song and the picture. Directions turn two free dancers into one dancing pair.

## Sub lesson 4 — Dances that carry our values — and dancing your appreciation

A community's values do not live only in words. They live **in its dances**: perform a pictured traditional dance you like or know — the dance carries what the community honours.

And how do you show **appreciation** for a dance? You dance it. Individually or in groups, perform a dance you know for the class. Appreciation here is not applause from a chair; it is your body saying: this is worth keeping.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the difference between free dance and set dance, then call the partner directions from memory..

## Do It Yourself

1. At home, put on or sing a song and move freely for its whole length — your way, start to finish..
