# Styles and Approaches in Theatre and Film

## Do it first
Same story. Same events. Tell it grave and slow — it is a tragedy. Tell it quick and comic — the room laughs. Nothing changed but the *approach*. That approach has a name: style. There is no Grade 10 student book; this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — The concept of style in production (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

**Style**, in production, is the **approach** a work takes — the manner in which a production chooses to do everything it does: how its actors move and speak, how its world is dressed and lit, how its pace runs.

Two productions of the *same* play, in different styles, are two different experiences. Style is not the story. Style is the story's *way of walking into the room*.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing an approach — deliberately (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Because a production *can* take different approaches, style is a **choice** — the guide's lesson exists so makers choose **deliberately**, knowing the major approaches open to them, instead of falling into one by habit.

The maker's questions are: what approach serves this story, this audience, this place? Answer those, and the acting, the design and the pace follow — one style, held from first rehearsal to final curtain.
