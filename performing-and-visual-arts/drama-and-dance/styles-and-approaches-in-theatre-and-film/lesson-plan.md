# Lesson plan — Styles and Approaches in Theatre and Film

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Styles and Approaches in Theatre and Film
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the concept of styles and art approaches in theatre and film production — the major style approaches a production can take (Grade 10 guide, Lesson 10). There is no Grade 10 student book: guide evidence only.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Genres of Theatre and Film — genre sorted works by kind; this lesson turns to how a production is approached: its style.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Genres of Theatre and Film. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what style means in theatre and film production.
2. Show how one scene changes under two style approaches.
3. Name style as a production's deliberate choice.

## What you need
The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells one known story two ways — once grave and slow, once comic and quick — and feels the story change though its events did not (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the concept first — style as a production's chosen approach — then what choosing a style decides (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion what 'style' means for a production, in one sentence, with your two-tellings example.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Take a scene you know and write how two different style approaches would change its acting, dress and pace — events untouched.
