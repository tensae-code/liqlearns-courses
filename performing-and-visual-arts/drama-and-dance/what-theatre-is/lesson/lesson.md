# What Theatre Is

## Do it first
Think of a play you have seen — at school, at a festival, anywhere. What did you see? People acting. What did you hear? Words, and perhaps music. What was around them? A place built or arranged for the show, clothes, drawn or built things. Keep your list. This lesson gives that list its name.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theatre is art for an audience

Theatre is art presented to an audience. People prepare it and organise it, and other people come to watch it.

Theatre can be organised **with voice** — the actors speak and sing. It can also be organised **without voice** — the actors show everything with movement and faces, and the audience still understands. Both are theatre.

## Sub lesson 2 — The arts inside theatre

[Picture 14401 — the arts inside theatre]

One play holds many arts inside it. It holds **literature** — the story and its words. It holds **acting** — people becoming the story's people. It holds **music**, and **drawing**, and **architecture** — the built place of the play — and **sculpture**, the carved and shaped things.

That is why theatre feels so full: when you watch a play, you are meeting many arts at the same time, working as one.

## Sub lesson 3 — The director's main role

Many people work on a play, but one role holds it together: the **organiser — the director**. The director organises the whole work: the actors, the story, the music and the place, so that everything reaches the audience as one theatre.

When you watch a play and it feels like one whole thing, that is the director's work you are feeling.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion what theatre is, and name three arts that live inside one play.

## Do It Yourself
Watch any short performance and list which of theatre's inside-arts you could see in it.
