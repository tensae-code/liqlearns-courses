# Lesson plan — What Theatre Is

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** What Theatre Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what theatre art is: art presented to an audience, organised with voice or without voice, holding literature, acting, music, drawing, architecture and sculpture inside it, with the director in the main organising role.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own experience of watching a play or a school performance; it gives that experience its first full definition.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Theatre is art for an audience** — the definition: art presented to an audience, organised with voice or without voice.
2. **The arts inside theatre** — literature, acting, music, drawing, architecture and sculpture live inside one play.
3. **The director's main role** — the organiser/director plays the main role in holding a play together.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Acting Together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what theatre art is, in your own words.
2. Name the arts theatre contains: literature, acting, music, drawing, architecture and sculpture.
3. Say who plays the main organising role in theatre.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner watches or recalls a short play and names everything they saw and heard inside it, before any definition is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition first, then the arts theatre holds inside it, then the director's main role (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 14401]: a diagram of the six arts inside theatre under the director — the student names each art in a play they know.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion what theatre is, and name three arts that live inside one play.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Watch any short performance and list which of theatre's inside-arts you could see in it.
