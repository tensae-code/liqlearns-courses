# Lesson plan — Appreciating Indigenous Knowledge in Dance, Theatre and Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Appreciating Indigenous Knowledge in Dance, Theatre and Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** valuing home-grown knowledge across three arts: in dance, in traditional theatre — what traditional theatre (የትውፊታዊ ተውኔት) is, its characteristics and performances — and in visual art, with paper mosaic (የወረቀት ሞዛይክ) among its practices.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the appreciation lessons — the learner can appreciate art works; this lesson turns appreciation toward indigenous knowledge itself.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **In dance and in traditional theatre (teacher guide — evidence)** — valuing home-grown knowledge in dance (§4.2); what traditional theatre is (የትውፊታዊ ተውኔት ምንነት), its characteristics and performances (§4.3).
2. **In visual art (teacher guide — evidence)** — appreciating indigenous knowledge in visual art (§4.4): drawing historical/cultural pictures, making paper mosaic (የወረቀት ሞዛይክ).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Indigenous Knowledge in Theatre and Film. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what valuing indigenous knowledge in dance means.
2. Say what traditional theatre (የትውፊታዊ ተውኔት) is.
3. Name visual-art practices that carry indigenous knowledge, including paper mosaic.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names one home-grown thing of knowledge in a dance, a play or a picture from their area, before appreciation is framed (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — dance first, then traditional theatre — its meaning, characteristics and performances — then visual art and its practices (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion one way indigenous knowledge shows in each art: dance, theatre, visual art.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Make a small paper mosaic (የወረቀት ሞዛይክ) of a historical or cultural scene, as the syllabus practice names.
