# Appreciating Indigenous Knowledge in Dance, Theatre and Art

## Do it first
A dance your grandmother knows. A play form older than the town. A picture made the home way. Each holds knowledge grown *here*. This lesson teaches valuing it. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — In dance and in traditional theatre (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Appreciation here turns to **home-grown knowledge in dance** (§4.2): the community's traditional dances — played on its **traditional instruments** and danced in its own ways — are knowledge, not just entertainment.

**Traditional theatre (የትውፊታዊ ተውኔት)** is the community's inherited theatre form. The guide studies **what it is, its characteristics and its performances**: theatre grown from the people's own life, carrying their stories in their own manner.

## Sub lesson 2 — In visual art (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

In **visual art** (§4.4), indigenous knowledge is appreciated by *making*: **drawing historical and cultural pictures**, and making **paper mosaic (የወረቀት ሞዛይክ)** — a picture built from small paper pieces.

The home way of picturing — its subjects, its materials, its patience — is itself the knowledge. To practise it is to keep it alive.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion one way indigenous knowledge shows in each art: dance, theatre, visual art.

## Do It Yourself
Make a small paper mosaic (የወረቀት ሞዛይክ) of a historical or cultural scene, as the syllabus practice names.
