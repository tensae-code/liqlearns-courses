# How Things Move

## Do it first

Stand up. Walk across the room. Now run back, softly and safely. Now stretch both arms out wide and pretend to fly, very slowly. You just moved in three different ways. This lesson looks closely at how things move — you, the animals, and the birds.

## Sub lesson 1 — Moving on two legs

Some movers walk and run on **two legs**. You do. A child walks, then runs. A hen also moves on two legs — she walks, and she runs in her quick, busy way.

The song **እንቅስቃሴ** (Enqisqase — Movement) puts it plainly: the child and the hen move on two legs — walking and running. Watch your own legs as you walk: one foot, then the other. One, two. One, two. When you run, the same two feet go faster, and for a moment both feet leave the ground.

## Sub lesson 2 — Moving on four legs

Other movers run on **four legs**. The dog runs fast on four legs. The cat runs fast on four legs too.

Four legs can go very fast. The feet land in a pattern — front feet, back feet — and the body stretches and gathers, stretches and gathers. Try it on your hands and knees on a soft place: hands and knees are your four legs. Move slowly first. Then a little faster. Feel how different it is from two legs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Moving on wings

And one mover is different from all the rest. The **bird** does not walk or run to travel — the bird **flies**, with its wings spread wide.

Spread your arms like the bird's wings. Lift them, lower them, slowly and strongly. That up-and-down is how the bird pushes the air and rises. Two legs walk and run. Four legs run fast. Wings fly. Three ways of moving — and now you can show all three.

## Say it to your companion

Name the three ways of moving and show each one while your companion guesses the mover..
