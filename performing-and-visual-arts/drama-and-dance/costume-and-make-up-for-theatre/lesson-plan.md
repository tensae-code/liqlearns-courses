# Lesson plan — Costume and Make-Up for Theatre

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Costume and Make-Up for Theatre
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the practical craft of stage appearance: building theatre with clothing (አልባሳት) and make-up inputs — face painting and make-up materials (የገጽ ቅብና መዋቢያ ግብዓቶች) — the Grade 5 guide's longest theatre section.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Elements of Theatre — costume is already named as a stage element; this lesson teaches the craft of making it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Costume — clothing builds the character (teacher guide — evidence)** — using clothing (አልባሳት) to build theatre.
2. **Make-up and face painting (teacher guide — evidence)** — face painting and make-up materials (የገጽ ቅብና መዋቢያ ግብዓቶች) — the craft of the stage face.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Elements of Theatre. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how clothing (አልባሳት) is used to build theatre.
2. Name face painting and make-up materials (የገጽ ቅብና መዋቢያ ግብዓቶች) as stage craft.
3. Design a character's costume and make-up, with reasons.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner dresses or draws a classmate-character — an elder, a merchant, a bride — so others can guess who they are, before costume is named as a craft (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — costume first (clothing builds the character), then make-up and face painting and their materials (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion how costume and make-up together change an actor into a character.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Design a costume for one character in a story you know: list the clothes and one make-up change, and say why each fits the character.

Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
