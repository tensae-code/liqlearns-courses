# Costume and Make-Up for Theatre

## Do it first
An actor walks in wearing an elder's clothes, walking slowly. You knew who he was before he spoke. What told you? His clothes — and perhaps his face. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Costume — clothing builds the character (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's theatre craft begins with **clothing (አልባሳት)**. On stage, clothes are not decoration: they *build* the character. The audience reads age, work, wealth and occasion from dress before the actor says a word.

Choosing costume is therefore a maker's decision, like choosing words in a story: each piece must be true to the person being played.

## Sub lesson 2 — Make-up and face painting (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

The face is the other half of stage appearance. The guide names **face painting and make-up materials (የገጽ ቅብና መዋቢያ ግብዓቶች)** — the inputs used to paint the actor's face into the character's face.

Age lines make an elder. Colour can make health or sickness, joy or grief. Make-up, like costume, is a craft of truth: it must serve the character the story needs.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion how costume and make-up together change an actor into a character.

## Do It Yourself
Design a costume for one character in a story you know: list the clothes and one make-up change, and say why each fits the character.
