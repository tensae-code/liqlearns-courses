# Lesson plan — Arab Theatre

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Arab Theatre
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Arab theatre from its modern founders such as Marun al-Naqqash to Jordanian playwrights and the theatrical text.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Theatrical Text — A Creator from My Country** — Rukus bin Zayed Al-Uzaizi (born Madaba 1903, died Amman 2004), Arabic teacher and Jordan's first press correspondent, twice president of the Jordanian Writers Association and holder of the King Hussein Order of Excellence in Literature 1977, whose books on Jordan's people, customs and antiquities made him a founding figure of Jordanian letters
2. **Arab Theatre** — Arab theatre's growth — poetry recited at Suq Ukaz and the orator's theatrical presence behind it; Maroun Al-Naqqash, pioneer of organised Arab theatre, with his verse Al-Bakhil after Molière; Abu Khalil Al-Qabbani bringing folk heritage to the stage; Tawfiq Al-Hakim's Ahl Al-Kahf and Pygmalion; then Jordan's own theatre from the 1960s, its pioneers Nabil Sawalha and Khalid Al-Tarifi, Hani Snobar "father of Jordanian theatre", and the Jerash and Jordan theatre festivals
