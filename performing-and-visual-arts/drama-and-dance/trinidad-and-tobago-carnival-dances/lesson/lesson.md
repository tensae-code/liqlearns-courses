# Trinidad and Tobago Carnival Dances and Characters

## Do it first

Quick start: from the title alone, guess what this lesson's work is. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Canboulay and the origins of Carnival

**Canboulay** stands at Carnival's beginning in this curriculum. Inside the Canboulay scenario learners practise **meet/part and follow/lead**, and study the **French and African influences in Carnival's history**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carnival character dances

Two Carnival character dances are taught as movement: **Sailor Mas and Jab Molassie**, inside the dynamics and heritage work, with **Parang music and movement** — and **body isolations are taken from the Sailor and Jab Molassie dances** themselves.

## Sub lesson 3 — Traditional Carnival Characters

The **Traditional Carnival Characters** are drama content in Forms 2–3: **Baby Doll, Dame Lorraine, Indian Mas, Jab Molassie, Midnight Robber, Minstrels, Moko Jumbies, Pierrot Grenade, Sailor Mas, stick fighter and Burrokeet** — with **costume construction** for them. Learners research and present **at least four characters — origin, costume, behaviour, movement**.

## Sub lesson 4 — The makers who built its dance

Carnival's dance was built by named makers: **Beryl McBurnie, Jean Coggins-Simmons, Astor Johnson and Julia Edwards** are studied as **the people who built Trinidad and Tobago dance**, and in Form 3 a **dance icon or group is analysed**.
