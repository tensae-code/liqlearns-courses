# Finding Ideas for Theatre and Film

## Do it first
This week something made you laugh, or worried you, or surprised you. That moment could be a play. But where do playwrights *find* their ideas — all of them? The book answers, and it starts in ancient Greece.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first playwrights — the Greek writers

The first theatre scripts were written in **ancient Greece, around the 5th century BC**. The book names four writers.

**Aeschylus** — the father of tragedy writing. **Sophocles** — a writer of tragedies. **Euripides** — who wrote up to 25 plays in his lifetime. And **Aristophanes** — son of Philippus, the comedy writer, with up to 40 works, 11 of them complete for the stage.

Every playwright since has stood where they stood: with an idea, and an audience.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where ideas come from

Ideas for theatre and film come from **all around the writer**: society's current attention-grabbing issues; social life; culture, custom and values; political and economic issues; belief and civilisation; past and present history; the weakening of shared values; outside influences of thought, culture, belief and technology; and the natural and human-made things around us.

The script is then enriched with **belief, hope, truth, betrayal, love, hate, heroism, loyalty and patriotism**. The writer is the main stakeholder — but audience, actor, director and helpers must **collaborate** to bring the idea alive.

## Sub lesson 3 — Worked example — 'The Hopeful Student' (summary only)

The book prints a model play so you can watch an idea at work: **'ተስፈኛዋ ተማሪ' — The Hopeful Student** (summary only — the dialogue is never copied here).

**Alemitu**, a Grade-7 student in **Mersa town, North Wollo** — bright, humble, respected, active in charity and clubs — advises her classmates **Hana and Ahmed**: have a **goal**, follow your lessons and study, instead of merely counting class levels. Her own vision is to become a **medical doctor**. They accept her advice, and as break ends they head back to class together.

See the craft: one clear idea — *a goal makes study matter* — carried by people you can believe, in a place you know.
