# Lesson plan — Finding Ideas for Theatre and Film

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Finding Ideas for Theatre and Film
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** where theatre and film ideas come from: the first scripts and the four Greek writers, the sources of ideas all around us, and a model play — 'The Hopeful Student' — as a worked example, in summary only.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Writing and Putting on a Play — the learner knows how a play is built; this lesson teaches where its idea is found.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **The first playwrights — the Greek writers** — first theatre scripts in ancient Greece, ~5th century BC; Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, Aristophanes.
2. **Where ideas come from** — society's issues, social life, culture and values, history, belief — and the writer working with actor, director and audience.
3. **Worked example — 'The Hopeful Student' (summary only)** — the model play ተስፈኛዋ ተማሪ: Alemitu advises Hana and Ahmed to set a goal and study — summary only, dialogue never transcribed.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Writing and Putting on a Play. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four Greek writers and what each is known for.
2. List the book's sources of ideas for theatre and film.
3. Tell the model play 'The Hopeful Student' in summary, and say what it teaches.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells one true moment from this week that could become a tiny play, before idea-sources are listed (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the first playwrights first, then the book's list of idea sources, then the model play as a worked example — summary only (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion two places a play's idea can come from, and the model play's story in three sentences.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Write the idea for your own short play: its source, its people, and what changes for them.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
