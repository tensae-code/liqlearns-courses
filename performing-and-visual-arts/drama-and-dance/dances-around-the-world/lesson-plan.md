# Lesson plan — Dances Around the World

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Dances Around the World
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Folk and national dances of Europe and the wider world, met by their steps, music and peoples..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Valiullina Music, contents pp. 141-142** — the waltz danced and heard
2. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Gorchakova Music, contents** — dances of the world
3. **Kazakhstan Grade 5 Music (Kulmanova)** — the game-journey "Silk Road from Asia to Europe" opens in Berlin at a contest of national music: German, Austrian, Italian and Spanish costumes told apart, the countries found on the map; the Estonian folk song «У каждого свой музыкальный инструмент» (arr. Kh. Kirvite) — every neighbour plays something, bagpipe, pipe and horn — sung in two voices; the 3/4 conducting triangle learned, since a waltz lives in it; Germany: song kinds (counting, teasing, comic, game, dance, choral), the reinlender and polka, and instruments born in Germany — svirel, flute, cymbals, harp, xylophone, drum, violin, double bass; Austria: zither, alpine horn, vargan and flugelhorn pictured; the yodel, the Tyrolean shepherds' song that flips the voice from low chest to high register, tried against throat singing; the Austrian polka-polianka; how the Ländler turned into the waltz; Johann Strauss (1825–1899), the Waltz King, and his «Весенние голоса»; Italy, cradle of musical culture: Neapolitan songs; the barcarolle, the Venetian gondoliers' song, in Offenbach's «Сказки Гофмана»; the Sicilian tarantella — a creative game: invent movements for the tarantella, build its rhythm from cards and percussion, and dance it in a group; Italian instruments mandola, mandolin, guitarrón, tambourine, castanets; class project: a "Festival of Folk Music" — each group brings a people's song and dance — or stage a rite of one Turkic people
