# Theme in Film

## Do it first
One word for a whole film. Love? Death? Democracy? Try it on a film you know — hard, and strangely satisfying. This lesson defines what you were reaching for. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theme — the film's central idea (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

A film's **theme** is its **central, unifying idea** — statable in **one word or a short phrase**: love, death, democracy…

Small as it sounds, theme **regulates** the whole film: its **characters, story, photography, plot and genre** all answer to it. A multi-million project still **begins and ends in one sentence** — its theme.

## Sub lesson 2 — Story, plot and the decade (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

How is a theme built? The guide's equation: **story (action) + plot = character development that yields the theme**. The audience watches people *change*, and the change spells the theme.

And themes are not timeless accidents: they **reflect their decade's social, economic and political climate**. Read a period's films, and you read its worries and hopes.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion one film and its theme in one word — then defend your word.

## Do It Yourself
Watch any film or episode and write: its theme in one word, and two choices in it that the theme explains.
