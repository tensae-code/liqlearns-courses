# Lesson plan — Theme in Film

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Theme in Film
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a film's theme is: its central, unifying idea, statable in one word or short phrase, regulating characters, story, photography, plot and genre — and reflecting its decade's climate.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Dance Practice and Imitation's theme-finding — the learner can find a theme (ጭብጥ); this lesson gives film's theme its full account.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Theme — the film's central idea (teacher guide — evidence)** — theme = the central/unifying idea, statable in one word or short phrase; it regulates characters, story, photography, plot and genre.
2. **Story, plot and the decade (teacher guide — evidence)** — story (action) + plot = character development that yields the theme; themes reflect their decade's social, economic and political climate.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Dance Practice and Imitation. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define theme in film.
2. Say what theme regulates.
3. State a film's theme in one word or short phrase.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names a film or story they love and tries to say its whole point in one word, before 'theme' is defined for film (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition first, then what theme regulates, then who chooses it and how story and plot yield it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Give your companion one film and its theme in one word — then defend your word.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Watch any film or episode and write: its theme in one word, and two choices in it that the theme explains.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
