| North Macedonia — Ороводна народна традиција / Соборски ора — the oro tradition: dances made for joy and grief alike, shown off at the sabor gathering before the monastery gates — the men's ora (Toska, Sitna Lisa, Chuchuk, Postupano, Kopachka, Kalajdzisko, Komitsko), the women's (Zhensko Krsteno, Berovka, Kochansko, Tresenica, Zhensko Chamche) and the mixed (Lisolaj, Sirto, Velgoshko, Djangurica, Povrateno) (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 26 |

| North Macedonia — Женски ора — women's ora: the goodness, elegance and modesty of the Macedonian woman and girl; the holds — by hands, by handkerchief, by the little fingers (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 28 |
| North Macedonia — Мешани ора — mixed ora: men and women holding hands or handkerchiefs — the dance of togetherness, unity and equality (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 29 |
| North Macedonia — Обредни ора — ritual ora: dances and songs as regulators of order, health and fertility — the men's processions (Dzhamali, Vasilichari, Surovaskari, Babari, Eshkari, Mechkari, Rusalii), the women's processions (Vodicharski, Lazarski, Gjurgjovdenski, Dodolski) and the wedding cycle (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 30 |
| North Macedonia — Свадбен циклус — the wedding cycle's dance order: the zurli and tapani strike up first for the kum (godfather), who leads the Kumovo oro; then the Svatovsko for the matchmaker, and Svekrvino, Zetovsko and Nevestinsko in turn — the calm "ramni ora", the even dances (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/wedding-dances-oro`) | Book 4, printed p. 32 |
| North Macedonia — Македонски народни ора — the folk ora: named from places, people, crafts, animals, instruments and customs, open or closed, divided by region — East, West, North, South, Central; the East: Osogovka, the men's suite of outdancing and initiation; Sedenka, the choreographed village gathering where two bachelors win the beautiful Stojna; Vodarki, the girls' walk to the water (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 33 |
| North Macedonia — Западен регион — the West: Kopachka, the men's oro of Kopachija and Kichevo, rich in custom and costume; and Chifte Chamche, the old Bitola "heavy" oro of the chamchii dancers, danced also by Turks and Roma in their own variants (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 34 |
| North Macedonia — Тешкото — Teshkoto of Lazaropole: the most powerful Macedonian men's oro — slow and heavy at the start, speeding to furioso, the leader kneeling on the tapan; the dance with which the Tanec ensemble won first prize at Llangollen in 1950 (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 34 |
| North Macedonia — Централен и Северен регион — the Centre: Kalajdzisko, the tinsmiths' cheerful dance of Veles; Sred Selo, the Povardarie village-life choreography; the North: Svadba, the folk-drama of the Skopje wedding; Drachevka, Atanas Kolarovski's choreography to Trajko Prokopiev's music, picturing the Skopje village sabori (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 35 |
| North Macedonia — Жетварка / Русалии — Zhetvarka, the Ovche Pole harvest choreography with its rites for a safe and plentiful harvest; and the South's Rusalii of Gevgelija: the black-clad men's ritual dance of the Unbaptised Days, with great crosses at their necks, fighting evil house by house and gathering gifts for the village, the church and the hospital (student book; original target `drama-and-dance/oro-dances-of-macedonia`) | Book 4, printed p. 36 |
