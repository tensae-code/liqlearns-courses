# Video script — Oro: Circle and Wedding Dances of North Macedonia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This is North Macedonia's own lesson.

**Свадбен циклус.** Свадбен циклус — the wedding oro dances by role, to zurla and tapan: the godfather leads the "Kumovo oro", then the matchmaker's, the mother-in-law's, the groom's and the bride's oro.

**every people's oro with its own instruments.** every people's oro with its own instruments: Macedonian oro to zurla, gajda, kaval, tapan, tambura and šupelka; Albanian to tapan, daire, šupelka, çifteli and kaval; Turkish to daire, tapan, kaval and kanun; Roma to zurla, tapan, violin and saxophone; Serbian and Bosniak to šupelka, tapan and accordion; Vlach to kaval, violin and double bass — the named oro watched and compared: "Тешкото" (Macedonian), "Шота" (Albanian), "Коло" (Serbian) and the others.

**the wedding dances.** the wedding dances: Macedonian svadbarsko, teškoto, nevestinsko, svekrvino and pajduška, kept alive at the Galičnik Wedding festival; Albanian women's bridal dances with the daire; Turkish halay and the couple dance çiftetelli on kana night; the Romani čoček of improvised, energetic movement.

**Ороводна народна традиција / Соборски ора.** Ороводна народна традиција / Соборски ора — the oro tradition: dances made for joy and grief alike, shown off at the sabor gathering before the monastery gates — the men's ora (Toska, Sitna Lisa, Chuchuk, Postupano, Kopachka, Kalajdzisko, Komitsko), the women's (Zhensko Krsteno, Berovka, Kochansko, Tresenica, Zhensko Chamche) and the mixed (Lisolaj, Sirto, Velgoshko, Djangurica, Povrateno).

**Машки ора.** Машки ора — men's ora: struggle, togetherness and individuality shown through outdancing; the holds — by hand, shoulder, belt, or the shepherd's staff.

**Женски ора.** Женски ора — women's ora: the goodness, elegance and modesty of the Macedonian woman and girl; the holds — by hands, by handkerchief, by the little fingers.

**Мешани ора.** Мешани ора — mixed ora: men and women holding hands or handkerchiefs — the dance of togetherness, unity and equality.

**Обредни ора.** Обредни ора — ritual ora: dances and songs as regulators of order, health and fertility — the men's processions (Dzhamali, Vasilichari, Surovaskari, Babari, Eshkari, Mechkari, Rusalii), the women's processions (Vodicharski, Lazarski, Gjurgjovdenski, Dodolski) and the wedding cycle.

**Свадбен циклус.** Свадбен циклус — the wedding cycle's dance order: the zurli and tapani strike up first for the kum (godfather), who leads the Kumovo oro; then the Svatovsko for the matchmaker, and Svekrvino, Zetovsko and Nevestinsko in turn — the calm "ramni ora", the even dances.

**Македонски народни ора.** Македонски народни ора — the folk ora: named from places, people, crafts, animals, instruments and customs, open or closed, divided by region — East, West, North, South, Central; the East: Osogovka, the men's suite of outdancing and initiation; Sedenka, the choreographed village gathering where two bachelors win the beautiful Stojna; Vodarki, the girls' walk to the water.

**Западен регион.** Западен регион — the West: Kopachka, the men's oro of Kopachija and Kichevo, rich in custom and costume; and Chifte Chamche, the old Bitola "heavy" oro of the chamchii dancers, danced also by Turks and Roma in their own variants.

**Тешкото.** Тешкото — Teshkoto of Lazaropole: the most powerful Macedonian men's oro — slow and heavy at the start, speeding to furioso, the leader kneeling on the tapan; the dance with which the Tanec ensemble won first prize at Llangollen in 1950.

**Централен и Северен регион.** Централен и Северен регион — the Centre: Kalajdzisko, the tinsmiths' cheerful dance of Veles; Sred Selo, the Povardarie village-life choreography; the North: Svadba, the folk-drama of the Skopje wedding; Drachevka, Atanas Kolarovski's choreography to Trajko Prokopiev's music, picturing the Skopje village sabori.

**Жетварка / Русалии.** Жетварка / Русалии — Zhetvarka, the Ovche Pole harvest choreography with its rites for a safe and plentiful harvest; and the South's Rusalii of Gevgelija: the black-clad men's ritual dance of the Unbaptised Days, with great crosses at their necks, fighting evil house by house and gathering gifts for the village, the church and the hospital.

**Тешкото.** Тешкото — Teshkoto: Blazhe Koneski's poem of the dance (the zurli shriek, the tapan thunders, the grief pressed in the chest — summarised, not transcribed), and how it is danced: to zurli and tapani at a very slow tempo, arms lowered and held, then bent at the elbows as the tempo grows, until the leader climbs onto the tapan, wipes the drummer's face and passes the handkerchief so every dancer leads in turn.

That is Oro: Circle and Wedding Dances of North Macedonia — North Macedonia's way, kept by name.
