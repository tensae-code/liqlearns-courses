# Lesson plan — Mime, Fables and First Plays

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Mime, Fables and First Plays
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** theatre without words and theatre at its first step — what drama is, learned through a first named play; silent movement and mime; the animal-fable plays; silent drama and the monologue; and wordless performance with improvisation, creating on the spot.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Acting Together and Playing a Person — the learner can already act in a group and play roles with words; this lesson takes the words away and lets body and face speak.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What is drama? A first play (teacher guide — evidence)** — drama/theatre (ድራማ/ተውኔት) introduced through a named play — "ቤት የለሽ ጨዋታ" — performed and read in class (Grade 3 teacher guide, §1.2, printed pp. 8–14).
2. **Silent movement, fable plays and mime (teacher guide — evidence)** — perceiving movement without sound; three named fable plays; then mime (ድምፅ አልባ እንቅስቃሴ/ማይም) and movement as named skills (Grade 4 teacher guide, printed pp. 10–17).
3. **Silent drama and the monologue (teacher guide — evidence)** — soundless theatre (ድምፅ አልባ ተውኔት); the monologue — speaking alone (መነባነብ); a named piece "ግመል" (Grade 4 teacher guide, §2.3, printed pp. 38–39).
4. **Wordless performance and improvisation (teacher guide — evidence)** — speechless performances (ንግግር አልባ ትወናዎች) and sudden improvisation (ድንገቴ ፈጠራ) — creating on the spot, from a single prop (Grade 6 teacher guide, §2.3, printed pp. 46–49).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Acting Together and Playing a Person. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what drama is, with the first play as the example.
2. Perform silent movement: reaching, lifting and carrying an invisible load.
3. Name the fable plays and then mime (ድምፅ አልባ እንቅስቃሴ/ማይም) and movement as skills.
4. Tell silent drama from the monologue (መነባነብ) — no voice at all, and one voice alone.
5. Improvise: create a wordless scene on the spot from a single prop.

## What you need
Open space, and — for the fable plays — simple animal masks or headbands if the class makes them.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Without a single word, show your companion that you are lifting something very heavy. Then show them something very light. If they could tell the difference, your body speaks a language — this lesson is its school. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mime that nobody can read → exaggerate the invisible thing's properties: heavy is slow and straining; hot is quick and blowing. The watcher reads effort, not objects.
- Improvisation freezes → start from the prop: open it, lift it, fear it, carry it. One action always suggests the next.
