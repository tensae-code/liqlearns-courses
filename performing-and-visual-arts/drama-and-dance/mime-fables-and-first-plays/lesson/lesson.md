# Mime, Fables and First Plays

## Do it first

Without a single word, show your companion that you are lifting something very heavy. Then show them something very light. If they could tell the difference, your body speaks a language — this lesson is its school.

## Sub lesson 1 — What is drama? A first play (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 3 guide introduces **drama (ድራማ/ተውኔት)** the practical way: through **a named play — "ቤት የለሽ ጨዋታ"** (the title as printed) — **performed and read in class**. What drama is, is learned from inside a play: people taking parts, a story unfolding in action before watchers. The play is placed here by name and purpose only — the guide's way of meeting drama is the doing, and the lesson keeps it that way.

## Sub lesson 2 — Silent movement, fable plays and mime (teacher guide — evidence)

First, **understanding movement without sound (ድምፅ አልባ እንቅስቃሴ)** — perceiving silent movement, the doorway to mime. Then **drama through animal fables**: three named fable plays — **"ስለ ጦጣ ፍርድ"** (about the monkey's judgement), **"ስለ አንበሳና ስለ ነበር፤ ስለ ጅብና ስለ አህያ ባልንጀርነት"** (about the lion and the leopard; about the hyena and the donkey as companions), and **"ስለ አንድ ከበርቴ ሰው"** (about a certain man) — placed by title and subject, never transcribed. After the fables, **mime (ድምፅ አልባ እንቅስቃሴ/ማይም)** and **movement** are named as skills in their own right: the animal stories taught the class to act; mime teaches it to act in silence.

## Sub lesson 3 — Silent drama and the monologue (teacher guide — evidence)

Two opposite solitudes. **Silent drama (ድምፅ አልባ ተውኔት)** — soundless theatre, a whole play with **no voice at all**. And the **monologue (መነባነብ)** — **speaking alone**: one actor, one voice, holding the stage by words, with a named piece **"ግመል"** (the title as printed) as its example. Silence with many bodies, or speech with one: both strip theatre to a single element and make the class master it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Wordless performance and improvisation (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 6 guide joins mime's family: **wordless acting — speechless performances (ንግግር አልባ ትወናዎች)** — and **sudden improvisation (ድንገቴ ፈጠራ), creating on the spot**. Its picture is the whole method: an actor improvising a scene from **a single prop** — opening an invisible door and reacting to what is behind it. No script, no words, no warning: the actor's body, voice-less, and invention must carry everything. This is mime grown up into theatre-making itself.

## Say it to your companion

Mime one everyday action for your companion — pouring coffee, saddling a horse, opening a stuck door. They must name it before you may nod.

## Do It Yourself

1. Mime a three-action story with a beginning, a problem and an ending — no words.
2. Prepare one fable scene in a group: choose animals, fix the story's lesson, perform it.
3. Give a one-minute monologue as a character of your choice, under an imagined spotlight.
