# Lesson plan — Chinese Opera (Xiqu)

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Chinese Opera (Xiqu)
**Place in the curriculum:** Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Chinese opera beyond Peking opera — regional xiqu forms, their singing, acting and stage conventions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China Grade 5 Music (jianpu) Vol.2** — "Watching the Big Opera" — a song with a spoken prologue about a village opera stage, in Henan Yu-opera flavour: the Yu opera （豫剧/河南梆子） band of banhu, small sanxian and dizi, and singing in imitation of its style; "West Lake Beauty", a song in Yue-opera aria style; the Yue opera （越剧） of Zhejiang, Shanghai and Jiangsu — clear, gentle, beautiful singing with a Jiangnan air; an extension listens to the question-and-answer scene of the Yue opera Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai in Wu dialect
2. **China Senior Music 6 Music & Drama** — Xiqu performance — the role types （行当）, the means and conventions （程式） of performance, and the basic skills and special techniques, through Peking-opera and local-opera excerpts; the voice systems （声腔） and genres of xiqu — the four great voice systems, genre and regional culture, genre singing and dialect
