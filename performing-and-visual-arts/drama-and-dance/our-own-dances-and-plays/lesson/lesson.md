# Our Own Dances and Plays

## Do it first
Today you saw many movements — someone carrying water, a goat jumping, a seller calling. Take one movement and stretch it into a little dance of your own: three steps, repeated. You have just done what this lesson teaches. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Movements become our own dances (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

Before this, you learned by **perceiving** — watching how people and animals and things move. The guide's expression section (§2.2) turns that around: the movements you perceived now become **dances of your own**.

Take a movement you have really seen. Repeat it with the beat. Stretch it, turn it, make it yours. That is a dance you made — not copied, made.

## Sub lesson 2 — Play-shapes become our own plays (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

The same turn happens for drama. You learned to **recognise** the simple shapes a play takes. The guide's §2.3 — drama structures in expression — asks you to use them: build **little plays of your own** on those shapes.

Your play can come from your own day: a market moment, a game, a journey. Give it an opening, a middle and a close, and perform it with your friends.

## Say it to your companion
Show your companion your own three steps, and tell them what real movement they came from.

## Do It Yourself
With friends, make a two-minute play of your own and perform it for the class.
