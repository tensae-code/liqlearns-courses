# Lesson plan — Our Own Dances and Plays

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Our Own Dances and Plays
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** turning the movements you have perceived and the play-shapes you have recognised into your own dances and little plays — the Grade 2 guide's expression sections §2.2 and §2.3 (section-level evidence).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Simple Drama Structures and on the movement lessons — the learner has perceived movements and recognised play shapes; now they make their own.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Movements become our own dances (teacher guide — evidence)** — §2.2: the perceived movements turned into the children's own dances.
2. **Play-shapes become our own plays (teacher guide — evidence)** — §2.3: drama structures in expression — the recognised shapes turned into the children's own little plays.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Simple Drama Structures. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Turn a perceived movement into your own short dance.
2. Build a little play of your own on a recognised shape.
3. Perform your own dance or play for others.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner copies one movement they saw today — a person working, an animal moving — and turns it into three dance steps of their own (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the guide's two expression sections: movements/dances in expression (§2.2), then drama structures in expression (§2.3) (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Show your companion your own three steps, and tell them what real movement they came from.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — With friends, make a two-minute play of your own and perform it for the class.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
