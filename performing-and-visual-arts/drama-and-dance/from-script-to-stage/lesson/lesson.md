# From Script to Stage

## Do it first
Here is a script. There is the audience, waiting. Between the two lies a whole journey of work — most of it invisible on the night. List everything you think that journey holds. Then check your list against the guide's.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing and preparing (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

The work begins by **finding a script** — chosen for **artistic quality** and for **realistic resources**: a play you can truly stage. Ethiopian scripts are also weighed by **Ethiopian cultural values**.

Then the makers draw the **'nitty gritty' outline** of the whole work, form the **staff/crew** — director, choreographer, producer, musical director, public relations — set a **rough budget** (costumes, props, make-up, publicity), and fix the **initial artistic vision**: what this production will be.

## Sub lesson 2 — Casting, rehearsals, opening night (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

With the vision fixed comes **casting** — auditions choose who embodies each character. Then **rehearsals** climb their ladder: the **table read**, **blocking** (placing the action), **repeated rehearsal**, **special rehearsals** for song and dance, and the **dress rehearsal** in full costume.

**Publicity** carries the news out — and then, at last, **opening night**: the journey becomes a performance.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion through the journey: script, crew, budget, casting, rehearsals, opening night.
