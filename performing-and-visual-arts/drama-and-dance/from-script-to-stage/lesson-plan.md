# Lesson plan — From Script to Stage

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** From Script to Stage
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the whole work of theatre, script to stage: choosing a script, the outline, staff and crew, budget, artistic vision, casting, the rehearsal ladder — table read, blocking, repeated and special rehearsals, dress rehearsal — publicity and opening night.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Writing and Putting on a Play — the learner knows the ladder's outline; this lesson gives the guide's full production journey.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Choosing and preparing (teacher guide — evidence)** — find a script (artistic quality + realistic resources; Ethiopian cultural values); the 'nitty gritty' outline; staff/crew; rough budget; initial artistic vision.
2. **Casting, rehearsals, opening night (teacher guide — evidence)** — casting/auditions; table read, blocking, repeated rehearsal, special song/dance rehearsals, dress rehearsal; publicity and opening night.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Writing and Putting on a Play. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Walk the production journey in order.
2. Name the crew roles the guide lists.
3. Order the rehearsal kinds: table read, blocking, repeated, special, dress.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner lists everything that must happen between 'we have a play script' and 'the audience is watching', before the guide's journey is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — choosing the script and preparing (outline, crew, budget, vision), then casting and rehearsals, then publicity and opening night (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Walk your companion through the journey: script, crew, budget, casting, rehearsals, opening night.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Plan a class production on paper: your script choice, your crew, three budget items, your rehearsal order.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later).

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
