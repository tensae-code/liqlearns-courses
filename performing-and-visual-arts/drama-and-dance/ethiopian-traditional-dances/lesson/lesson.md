# Ethiopian Traditional Dances

## Do it first
Think of the dances you have seen. Some were at religious ceremonies. Some were at weddings and celebrations. Some mourned, some praised heroes. The book sorts them all — begin with your own list.

## Sub lesson 1 — Traditional dances defined — sacred and secular

Traditional dances are performed for **social and religious ceremonies**.

The **sacred** dances belong to the country's faith branches — the book counts four — danced as part of worship. The **secular** dances belong to life's occasions: **joy, grief, heroism and hunting**. A wedding dance and a mourning dance are both traditional dances; the occasion tells you which kind you are watching.

## Sub lesson 2 — Amhara dances by district

[Picture 14405 — Amhara dances by district]

The Amhara districts name their own dances. **Wollo** has **timtim**. **Gondar** has **disqna menṭiq**. **Gojjam** has **netela** and **zinab inqiṭqiṭ**. **North Shewa** has **wancha liqliqa** and **minjar**. **Raya** has **laleguma**. And **Wag Himra** is known for its **women's eskista**.

Dances also differ in *how* they move: with the feet, the shoulders, the neck, above the waist, below the waist or the whole body — jumping, lying, walking, standing — and men's and women's styles differ.

## Sub lesson 3 — The nation's dances

Beyond Amhara, every people names its dances. **Oromo:** Regeda, Shegoye, Fole, Dechisa, Shubisa, Tiri, Butula, Chasaroba, Gelo. **Tigray:** Tembien, Enderta, Telhit, Hora Sedesey. The **Gurage** have their own dances; the **South** names Silo, Lembo, Wolayta, Alaba, Mezhenger and Dorze; and **Somali:** Hayawa, Sidawoya, Buranbur, Danto.

One country — and its dances cannot be counted on one hand. Each name is a people saying: this is how we move, and this is who we are. (Names are spelled as the book prints them.)

## Sub lesson 4 — Where dances come from

Where does a dance movement come from? The book answers: from **nature, from animals, and from daily work**.

The **Gondar** dance grows from a **chicken drinking water** — head down, then neck up. A **Tigray** dance and Sidama's **Lembo** grow from **bird movement**. **Gurage, Gamo and Minjar** dances grow from **horse-riding, hunting and farming**.

So a traditional dance is a people's memory of their own life, kept in the body.
