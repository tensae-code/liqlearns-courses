# Lesson plan — Ethiopian Traditional Dances

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Ethiopian Traditional Dances
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's traditional dances whole: what they are — dances for social and religious ceremonies, sacred and secular — the named dances of the Amhara districts and of the nation's peoples, and where dance movements come from.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Dances of Ethiopia and Folk Dance Styles and Eskesta — the learner knows dances by region and technique; this lesson gives the Grade 7 book's whole-country account.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Traditional dances defined — sacred and secular** — dances performed for social and religious ceremonies; sacred dances in the four faith branches, secular by occasion.
2. **Amhara dances by district** — Wollo timtim; Gondar disqna menṭiq; Gojjam netela and zinab inqiṭqiṭ; North Shewa wancha liqliqa and minjar; Raya laleguma; Wag Himra women's eskista.
3. **The nation's dances** — Oromo, Tigray, Gurage, the South and Somali — the named dances of Ethiopia's peoples.
4. **Where dances come from** — movements grow from nature, animals and daily work — the Gondar dance from a chicken drinking water.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Dances of Ethiopia. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define traditional dances by their ceremonies and occasions.
2. Name Amhara dances by district — Wollo, Gondar, Gojjam, North Shewa, Raya, Wag Himra.
3. Name dances of the Oromo, Tigray, Gurage, Southern peoples and Somali.
4. Say where dance movements come from, with the Gondar chicken example.

## What you need
An open space for moving and acting. The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names every dance they have ever seen at a wedding, a festival or a religious ceremony, before the book sorts them (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition first — sacred and secular occasions — then how dances move and the Amhara names, then the nation's dances, then where movements come from (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 14405]: a table of Amhara districts and their named dances — the student matches districts to dances, including minjar for North Shewa.
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion: one sacred occasion, one secular occasion, and three named dances with their places.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Make a dance map of your own area: the dances danced there, their occasions, and one movement each is known by.
