# Lesson plan — Local Stories for Theatre and Film

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Local Stories for Theatre and Film
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** local stories as the road by which traditional and indigenous knowledge passes down the generations — with intercultural theatre mixing Ethiopia's cultures in one production, and theatre as a society's cultural sustainability.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the indigenous-knowledge lessons — the learner knows local histories as content (Grade 8 student book, §3.3, stocked in Theatre and Film Through History); this guide lesson gives the method.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Local stories — knowledge's road (teacher guide — evidence)** — local stories as the means of passing traditional/indigenous knowledge down the generations; Ethiopian forms — riddles, tales, natural conservation, conflict resolution — as theatre/film material.
2. **Intercultural theatre and cultural sustainability (teacher guide — evidence)** — mixing and matching Ethiopia's cultures and subcultures in one production; theatre as the mechanism of a society's cultural sustainability, connecting communities.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Indigenous Knowledge in Theatre and Film. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how local stories pass knowledge down.
2. Name the traditional forms used as material.
3. Explain intercultural theatre and cultural sustainability.

## What you need
The pictures in the lesson. Nothing else needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells the oldest story they know from their own area — who told it to them, and who told that person (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — local stories as knowledge's road first, then the Ethiopian forms named, then intercultural theatre and cultural sustainability (lesson/lesson.md).
   - (No picture slots beyond the described slots in the lesson text.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — Tell your companion how a local story carries knowledge down, and what intercultural theatre mixes.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Write down one local story from an elder, name the knowledge inside it, and outline the play it could become.

## If the learner is stuck
Go back to the Do it first moment and do it again slowly, together. The explanation only names what the learner has already done or seen.
