# Local Stories for Theatre and Film

## Do it first
Your oldest story has already travelled: from a grandparent's mouth to your parent's, to yours. That travelling *is* a technology — older than books. This lesson names it. It stands on teacher-guide evidence, and says so.

## Sub lesson 1 — Local stories — knowledge's road (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

**Local stories** are the means by which **traditional and indigenous knowledge passes down the generations**. The teacher's craft is to use Ethiopian traditional forms as theatre and film material: **riddles, tales, natural conservation and conflict resolution**.

The story is the vehicle; the knowledge is the passenger. Stage the story well, and the knowledge arrives in a new generation without ever being lectured.

## Sub lesson 2 — Intercultural theatre and cultural sustainability (teacher guide — evidence) (teacher guide — evidence)

**Intercultural theatre** mixes and matches **Ethiopia's cultures and subcultures in one production** — one stage holding many peoples' stories, dances and forms, each honoured in its own manner.

Through such work, **theatre becomes the mechanism of a society's cultural sustainability**: it **connects communities**, and a culture that is performed is a culture that continues.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion how a local story carries knowledge down, and what intercultural theatre mixes.

## Do It Yourself
Write down one local story from an elder, name the knowledge inside it, and outline the play it could become.
