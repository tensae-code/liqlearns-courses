# Acting Our History

## Do it first

Ask at home tonight — no, first, right now: what is the oldest true story of your country that you already know? Who told it to you? Histories live because people keep telling them. This lesson tells the greatest of them — and then acts it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Known histories — and the Victory of Adwa

**Known histories** are the events, the famous people who made history in their work, and the history of country and place — passed down **from generation to generation**, from grandparents to parents to children.

The greatest of them: the war of **Adwa**, in **1888 E.C.**, between Ethiopia and Italy. Led by **Emperor Menelik** and **Empress Taytu**, Ethiopia's patriot fathers defeated the invading enemy and sent it back. The victory is celebrated every year on **Yekatit 23**.

[Picture 9705 — Empress Taytu and Emperor Menelik, a traditionally dressed patriot warrior with spear and shield, and the Ethiopian flag]

At the Adwa Victory festival, the day is honoured the way the patriots earned it: with morale-stirring, hero-encouraging **fukera**, **shilela** and **qererito**, and with songs. The hero song calls the Ethiopian hero one who is never broken — **ዘራፍ!** (Zeraf!)

## Sub lesson 2 — Acting the patriots' history

A history that is only read can fade. A history that is **acted** is remembered in the body.

So: ask your families about Adwa, and tell the class what you heard — every family's telling adds a piece. Collect the melodies and the fukera sung at Adwa Victory time, and perform them in song and dance.

Then act the history of the patriot fathers and mothers in drama: the gathering, the march, the standing firm, the victory. You are not only learning the history. By acting it, you become one of the generation that passes it on.
