# Lesson plan — Acting Our History

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Drama and Dance · **Lesson:** Acting Our History
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Drama and Dance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** our country's known histories — above all the Victory of Adwa — live by being told from generation to generation, and acting them makes the telling unforgettable.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Acting Together — the learner can build group scenes. Here the group's story is the country's own.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Known histories — and the Victory of Adwa** — known histories defined; Adwa 1888 E.C. under Menelik and Taytu; the yearly Yekatit 23 celebration with fukera, shilela and qererito.
2. **Acting the patriots' history** — ask your families, collect the festival melodies and fukera, and act the patriot fathers and mothers in drama.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Acting Together and Stories and Folktales. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what known histories are and how they travel.
2. Tell the Adwa story: 1888 E.C., Italy, Menelik and Taytu, the victory.
3. Name the celebration day — Yekatit 23.
4. Name the festival arts: fukera, shilela, qererito and songs.
5. Act a part in the patriots' history.

## What you need
Family tellings — ask at home. Simple patriot things for acting: a stick for a spear, a cloth for a shield. Nothing bought.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names the oldest true country story they know, and who told it to them, before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — known histories first, Adwa at their centre; then the acting of the patriots' history, fed by family tellings (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9705]: Empress Taytu and Emperor Menelik, a patriot warrior with spear and shield, and the flag — the student names each figure and what they stand for.
   - ADD AUDIO: fukera and shilela chanted at Adwa Victory time (slot only — no audio file is made in this build) — the student feels the morale-raising rhythm and joins the response.
3. **Say it to your companion** — tell your companion the Adwa story in five lines, then the part you would act.
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — ask a grandparent or elder at home for their Adwa telling; bring one new detail back to class.

## If the learner is stuck
- The story is only dates → anchor it in the people: a leader, an empress, fathers and mothers who stood firm. Dates hang on people.
- No family telling available → the class tellings pool together; every learner may borrow the pool's details, named as class knowledge.
- Songs and chants are placed by name and purpose only — their verses are never transcribed (see sources.md).
