This lesson is Vietnamese Traditional Theatre.

Vietnam's traditional theatre forms, appreciated as a family of old stage arts. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: Overview of some traditional art forms — Chèo (a stage art with a long history, its singing developed strongly in the Red River Delta, a folk-character art close to farmers; performed on the communal house yard at village festivals, actors and audience able to exchange and dialogue; famous pieces "Xúy Vân giả dại", "Thị Mầu lên chùa" from "Quan Âm Thị Kính", "Lưu Bình, Dương Lễ"; very rich tune systems — Nói, Vỉa, Ngâm vịnh, Sắp hề gậy, Ra trò and Đường trường tunes, lyrical dialogue; lục bát and tứ tuyệt verse with many padding sounds; the chèo orchestra accompanying the singing and the stage background — melodic nhị, sáo, đàn bầu, thập lục beside percussion trống đế in the key role, trống ban, thanh la) + Tuồng (hát bội) (the stage art most popular in the South Central region, centred on Bình Định, Phú Yên, Khánh Hoà; performed at festivals and in feudal aristocratic life, especially the Nguyễn-era royal courts; heroic and tragic tone, the character's psychology always outstanding; conventional, gestural and exaggerated; plays "Hồ Nguyệt Cô hoá cáo", "Thủy Định Minh cầu cá", "Mạnh Lương bắt ngựa"; an orchestra of percussion (drums, thanh la), strings (nhị, hồ, tam, tứ, nguyệt) and winds (sáo, tiêu, kèn bầu) supporting the actors) + Cải lương (a national folk singing-drama formed from the Southern đờn ca tài tử movement — its early-20th-century development in repertoire, playing technique, singing and performance, combined with the ca ra bộ form's stage elements; 1917 officially marks cải lương's birth; its music founded on ritual music pieces, ca Huế and Central and Southern folk music; scripts from Nôm poem stories such as "Kim Vân Kiều" and "Lục Vân Tiên" or from tuồng plays, beside social plays such as "Đời cô Lựu" and "Tô Ánh Nguyệt"; an orchestra of traditional instruments plus the guitar phím lõm reformed from the Western guitar).

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
