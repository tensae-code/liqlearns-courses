# Performing and Visual Arts course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Built from the bottom, the same way as the other courses: a learner
does not start at the stage — they start with their eyes, ears and
hands. The path climbs:

**First Steps → Visual Art → Music → Drama and Dance → Culture and Values**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner
can jump in higher; the "start here instead" path points down this
ladder to the bottom).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **First Steps** — the arts before their names: caring for your
   book, what the arts are, observing the world with eyes and ears,
   looking at pictures and saying what they tell, the pictures of
   what we do every day, how the three arts belong together and help
   every other subject, and learning to appreciate the arts — and,
   higher up the same first steps, appreciating and valuing the arts
   with reasons, and the arts connect to everything: how art is
   woven through every school subject. The audit close-out added:
   art teaches every subject (the special-needs account included);
   why Ethiopian traditional music is valued; appreciating
   Ethiopian traditional dance and theatre (teacher-guide
   evidence); and the four value-of lessons — music works, dance
   works, theatre and film works, visual art works — carried as
   contents-only evidence from the truncated Grade 8 guide.
2. **Visual Art** — the hand and the eye: first marks and lines,
   shapes and forms, colouring, colours and mixing them, the crafts
   around us (metal, pottery, weaving), making with clay and paper,
   printing, painting then and now, sculpture and historic places,
   beautiful writing, and photographs — looking at them, and taking
   them to pass a message. The upper-grade depth: the elements of
   visual art and the colour wheel, drawing as the foundation
   skill, making with paper and pieces (papier-mâché, collage,
   mosaic), paints and how they work, cave art and church painting,
   the great painters of Ethiopia, houses of our country, shapes,
   forms and patterns in colour, emblems and posters that speak,
   and video — moving pictures. The audit close-out added:
   painting still life; telling history with illustration, and
   in words and pictures; double letters; the elements and
   principles of design; Lalibela — churches carved from one
   rock; decorating home and environment; design and
   perspective — near and far; composition in visual art; and
   the elements of design in 3D arts. The Art expansion added:
   symbolism in art (the Me-Symbol); mural painting, painted,
   tiled and in relief; digital and computer art (posterisation
   and a character built shape by shape on screen);
   impressionism and impasto dabs; comics — character design,
   with Kyle and Lulu; imaginative and expressive art — the
   fantasy landscape; and Maldivian crafts and dress —
   coconut-leaf weaving and the kasabu libaas (Maldives student
   book). The Art expansion added:
   history of art — art and power, art and mentality, and art
   and conflicts, taught through questions, not chronology;
   women in art history — women as model, patron and artist;
   and famous artists and masterpieces — the latest trends,
   from op and kinetic art to conceptual art and land art
   (Andorra programme documents, evidence). The Art expansion
   added: Maya art and techniques — the Maya people's own
   artistic techniques, handled instrumentally and
   procedurally; nature in art — recreating the relationship
   between the human being and nature through artworks; and
   graphic design — Publicarte, a designed magazine of the
   community's culture (Guatemala teacher guide, evidence,
   PARTIAL file). The Art expansion added: Inuit arts and
   crafts — beadwork, sewing, drawing and printmaking,
   stone carving, clothing, tools and jewellery taught as
   art, form by form; Baker Lake wall hangings — southern
   wool worked into Arctic designs; and contemporary Inuit
   art — new forms growing alongside the old (Canada
   student books, Nunavut). The Art expansion added:
   ancient Egyptian art — pyramids, the sphinx and paintings
   evaluated by their elements and principles; and basketry —
   coiling, plaiting, wicker and twining, patterned baskets
   for use and for sale (Uganda student book). The Art expansion
   added: kite making — kites designed and made to depict
   given themes, with weaving in natural and man-made
   materials; weaving — various items from natural
   materials, growing to complex and mixed-media weaving
   for decorative and useful objects; assemblage — bead
   jewellery from natural and man-made beads, 3D objects
   showing space and balance, and toys designed and made
   from wood; and Barbadian crafts and makers — the
   Historic and Marine museums, local artifacts collected
   and drawn, and Barbadian textile artists, sculptors,
   potters and basket weavers (Barbados curriculum
   guide, evidence). The Art expansion
   added: artists of Central America — Honduras's first
   artists and its current artists, known and valued by
   name, widening to the artists of all Central America,
   known by their works (Honduras student books). The Art expansion
   added: street art and graffiti — graffiti as urban
   painting, lettering and wall work as a public art
   form; and fashion design — textile design, pattern
   for cloth, and dress designed as applied visual art
   (Albania teacher guide, evidence).
3. **Music** — the ear and the voice: sounds around us, naming
   sounds, the songs (animals, family, nature, movement, festivals,
   school, counting, clean living, our country, colours), the
   instruments of Ethiopia — masenqo, kebero, washint, krar, atamo,
   negarite and the church instruments — hearing each one's sound,
   the kinds of traditional music, and reading music: whole, half
   and quarter notes and rests, played and sung. The upper-grade
   depth: reading music on the staff (the G and F clefs), faster
   notes, dynamics and tempo, scales, pitch and key signatures,
   qenet — Ethiopia's own scales, Ethiopian traditional music and
   its history, fukera and shilela — the boast and its answer, and
   anthems and songs that teach. The audit close-out added: the
   elements of music; moving in note values; reading the beat;
   art that teaches the community (traffic safety, HIV/AIDS and
   doping taught through song); the Adwa patriotic songs; Amhara
   music history and its musicians; Saint Yared; the regional
   culture groups; making traditional instruments; Ethiopian
   music in four contexts; Ethiopia's instrument families;
   modern instruments; appreciating indigenous knowledge in
   music; song: knowledge blossoms; and the evidence lessons —
   performing kinds of music, the value of practising music,
   history of indigenous music, indigenous music and culture,
   and the history of African and world music. The Art expansion
   added: kinds of music around the world — reggae, jazz and
   gospel told by where each comes from and who made it famous
   (Cyprus teacher unit material, evidence); and work songs and
   sea shanties — "Day-O", the Jamaican banana-boat song of the
   night-shift banana loaders (Cyprus teacher unit material,
   evidence, summary only). The Art expansion added: Caribbean
   music and dance — learning the Caribbean's music and dance
   from a social studies text itself, using its features to
   locate and extract what it says plainly (Jamaica teacher
   curriculum guide, evidence). The Art expansion added: the
   GCSE set works of the United Kingdom — Bach's Brandenburg
   Concerto No. 5 and the concerto grosso; the sonata and sonata
   form through Beethoven's 'Pathétique'; Baroque music through
   Purcell's 'Music for a While' — ground bass and word-painting;
   pop music through Queen's 'Killer Queen' — verse-chorus form
   and multi-tracking; music for the stage through 'Defying
   Gravity' from Wicked; film music — the cue, underscoring and
   the leitmotif — through John Williams' Star Wars; folk music
   in modern dress through Afro Celt Sound System's fusion
   'Release'; bossa nova through 'Samba Em Prelúdio'; and making
   music with computers and apps, carrying the Anthology's
   copyright and music-publishing chapter (United Kingdom
   student book, scores by summary only). The Art expansion
   added: composing music — building pieces from cells,
   patterns and ostinatos, and the three acts of experimenting,
   improvising and composing; and conducting — pulse, metre,
   tempo, cues, rehearsals and one unified musical idea
   (Andorra programme documents, evidence). The Art expansion
   added: Maya instruments and musical events — organising
   musical events especially with the instruments of Maya art;
   and the message in songs — listening first without the
   words, then with them, and saying what a song means
   (Guatemala teacher guide, evidence, PARTIAL file). The
   Art expansion added: the traditional music of Nunavut —
   its four forms and what music does for a community;
   katajjaq, Inuit throat singing, nearly lost and now
   returning; the coming of the Qallunaat (newcomers) and
   the new kinds it grew; pisiit — written Inuktitut songs
   sung together every Sunday; and contemporary Inuit
   music — many instruments, the internet, and new things
   added to the old songs (Canada student books, Nunavut). The
   Art expansion added: Ugandan folk songs — each ethnic
   group's own tradition, Bunyoro's Kuhugura and Buganda's
   eggono vocal tones, pentatonic songs with traditional
   instruments (Uganda student book; teacher guide, evidence). The
   Art expansion added: making and composing music —
   simple tunes from the notes B, A and G fitted to given
   rhythm patterns, with simple lyrics; the tuk band of
   Barbados — its instruments named and recognised, and
   written about with how they are played; building
   instruments from recycled and found materials —
   bottles, saw, comb, maracas and bamboo; composing
   calypso — simple calypso tunes on local topics, and
   calypsos for graduations, Crop Over and other social
   events; and the steel pan — playing simple tunes and
   taking part in a steel band group (Barbados curriculum
   syllabus, evidence). The Art expansion added: Honduran music — Honduras's
   own music through its makers and sounds, the genres
   current in Honduras today, and its historical periods
   from its beginnings to today; choral singing — voices
   harmonised to sing the country's music together, and
   choral direction (dirección coral); the human voice —
   the phonation apparatus and the classification of
   voices; and Latin American and Caribbean music — the
   region's genres valued and appreciated, and its
   instruments with how they are played (Honduras student
   books). The Art expansion added: intervals — the
   distances between sounds, counted from the first
   sound, 1–2 up to the octave 1–8, named, built, sung
   and played (Albania teacher guide, evidence).
4. **Drama and Dance** — the body: how things, animals and people
   move; playing family roles; acting together; playing a person;
   theatre on a stage; acting our history; circle dances and
   traditional steps; the dances of Ethiopia's peoples; moving
   freely to music; traditional games; stories and folktales told
   aloud; riddles. The upper-grade depth: the elements of theatre,
   writing and putting on a play, mime, fables and first plays,
   theatre and film through history, folk dance styles and
   eskesta, dance composition and modern dance, women's
   traditional plays, and the Hoya Hoye and Buhe play songs. The
   audit close-out added: what theatre is; the play where every
   craft needs the others (summary only); Ethiopian traditional
   dances; finding ideas for theatre and film; indigenous
   knowledge on stage and screen; what performing art does for
   a community; and the teacher-guide evidence lessons — simple
   drama structures, making art together, costume and make-up,
   the elements of drama and of film making, literary and
   technical elements of theatre, genres of theatre and film,
   and styles and approaches in theatre and film. The Art
   expansion added: tableau — living pictures, freezing into a
   still picture of a scene in a financial institution and
   thought-tracking the frozen figures (Jamaica teacher
   curriculum guide, evidence). The Art expansion added:
   interactive theatre — the audience taking part in the
   performance, ending in the "Rights and Duties" performance —
   and stage movement and blocking, taught through stage
   stances (وقفة) that speak without words (Egypt student
   books). The Art expansion also added: boduberu — the
   Maldives' traditional big-drum dance, met through Zaeky
   Zameel's ink drawing of it (Maldives student book, artwork
   study). The Art expansion added: dances of Guatemala's
   peoples — the meanings of dances, and a first initiation
   into the choreographies and techniques of the dances of
   Guatemala's other peoples and of Mesoamerica (Guatemala
   teacher guide, evidence, PARTIAL file). The Art expansion
   added: qilaujjarniq — Inuit drum dancing, east and west;
   suluvinniq — square dancing, the whalers' dance still
   danced today; preparing to perform — the stop-and-think
   routine for performance nerves; the Sila Rainbow Dance
   Competition — teams, judges and the community hall; and
   the dancer's body — water and stretching (Canada student
   books, Nunavut). The Art expansion added:
   Ugandan folk dances — each group's motifs, instruments,
   costumes and props, choreographed to its folk song
   (Uganda student book; teacher guide, evidence). The
   Art expansion added: puppet theatre — creating cartoon
   characters and stick puppets, and cutting, arranging
   and pasting paper-bag and sock puppets (Barbados
   curriculum guide, evidence).
5. **Culture and Values** — the arts as a people's memory:
   traditional dress then and now, houses then and now, the
   festivals and their arts (Meskel, Timket, Arafa, Genna, New
   Year, Adwa), old arts and today's arts, and the values our
   community holds, carried in art. The upper-grade depth: what
   the arts do for society, and the folk crafts of Ethiopia. The
   audit close-out added: indigenous knowledge of Ethiopia;
   traditional music and what it does; dance in society; music
   in society; theatre in society; visual art in society; dance,
   culture and society; and performing art's three benefits. The
   Art expansion added: art as protest — realism, social
   realism, revolutionary sculpture and Brasília's protesting
   architecture; and art of our country — the Romanesque of
   Andorra read as art and as religious power (Andorra
   programme documents, evidence). The Art expansion added:
   symbols of Guatemala's peoples — the guide's own
   iconography read as cultural art: the snail, the sun as
   nawal Ajpú, and the heart-planet with nawal Noj, wisdom
   (Guatemala teacher guide, evidence, PARTIAL file).
 The Art expansion added:
   national symbols of Central America — the national
   symbols of the Central American countries, and what
   they stand for (Honduras student book).
