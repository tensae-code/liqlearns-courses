# Performing and Visual Arts course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Built from the bottom, the same way as the other courses: a learner
does not start at the stage — they start with their eyes, ears and
hands. The path climbs:

**First Steps → Visual Art → Music → Drama and Dance → Culture and Values**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner
can jump in higher; the "start here instead" path points down this
ladder to the bottom).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **First Steps** — the arts before their names: caring for your
   book, what the arts are, observing the world with eyes and ears,
   looking at pictures and saying what they tell, the pictures of
   what we do every day, how the three arts belong together and help
   every other subject, and learning to appreciate the arts — and,
   higher up the same first steps, appreciating and valuing the arts
   with reasons, and the arts connect to everything: how art is
   woven through every school subject. The audit close-out added:
   art teaches every subject (the special-needs account included);
   why Ethiopian traditional music is valued; appreciating
   Ethiopian traditional dance and theatre (teacher-guide
   evidence); and the four value-of lessons — music works, dance
   works, theatre and film works, visual art works — carried as
   contents-only evidence from the truncated Grade 8 guide.
2. **Visual Art** — the hand and the eye: first marks and lines,
   shapes and forms, colouring, colours and mixing them, the crafts
   around us (metal, pottery, weaving), making with clay and paper,
   printing, painting then and now, sculpture and historic places,
   beautiful writing, and photographs — looking at them, and taking
   them to pass a message. The upper-grade depth: the elements of
   visual art and the colour wheel, drawing as the foundation
   skill, making with paper and pieces (papier-mâché, collage,
   mosaic), paints and how they work, cave art and church painting,
   the great painters of Ethiopia, houses of our country, shapes,
   forms and patterns in colour, emblems and posters that speak,
   and video — moving pictures.
3. **Music** — the ear and the voice: sounds around us, naming
   sounds, the songs (animals, family, nature, movement, festivals,
   school, counting, clean living, our country, colours), the
   instruments of Ethiopia — masenqo, kebero, washint, krar, atamo,
   negarite and the church instruments — hearing each one's sound,
   the kinds of traditional music, and reading music: whole, half
   and quarter notes and rests, played and sung. The upper-grade
   depth: reading music on the staff (the G and F clefs), faster
   notes, dynamics and tempo, scales, pitch and key signatures,
   qenet — Ethiopia's own scales, Ethiopian traditional music and
   its history, fukera and shilela — the boast and its answer, and
   anthems and songs that teach.
4. **Drama and Dance** — the body: how things, animals and people
   move; playing family roles; acting together; playing a person;
   theatre on a stage; acting our history; circle dances and
   traditional steps; the dances of Ethiopia's peoples; moving
   freely to music; traditional games; stories and folktales told
   aloud; riddles. The upper-grade depth: the elements of theatre,
   writing and putting on a play, mime, fables and first plays,
   theatre and film through history, folk dance styles and
   eskesta, dance composition and modern dance, women's
   traditional plays, and the Hoya Hoye and Buhe play songs.
5. **Culture and Values** — the arts as a people's memory:
   traditional dress then and now, houses then and now, the
   festivals and their arts (Meskel, Timket, Arafa, Genna, New
   Year, Adwa), old arts and today's arts, and the values our
   community holds, carried in art. The upper-grade depth: what
   the arts do for society, and the folk crafts of Ethiopia. The
   audit close-out added: indigenous knowledge of Ethiopia;
   traditional music and what it does; dance in society; music
   in society; theatre in society; visual art in society; dance,
   culture and society; and performing art's three benefits.
