# Lesson plan — Photography

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Photography
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a photograph is taken — the five guidance points and the two arrangements — and how photographs, and video after them, pass messages.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Photographs, its next-door neighbour — the learner has looked at photographs and told what they understood; now they stand behind the camera. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Simple ways of taking a photograph** — light, lens, film and print; the five guidance points.
2. **Vertical and horizontal pictures** — ቋሚ and አግዳሚ, portrait and landscape, and choosing by subject.
3. **Passing a message by photograph** — what photographs do for a country, and reading two message photographs.
4. **Passing a message by video** — sound plus moving image; watching, writing the message, drawing it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Photographs — the learner should look before they take. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how a photograph is made (light through a lens, image on film, printed on prepared papers) and give the five guidance points.
2. Choose vertical or horizontal arrangement by their subject, using both Amharic names.
3. List what photographs do for a community and country, and read a message photograph aloud.
4. Watch a video and present its message in writing, with a drawing of it.

## What you need
A camera or a phone camera if one is available; otherwise a paper frame (a rectangle cut from card) to practise framing, paper and pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner remembers being photographed, and looks at the light where a photograph of them might be taken (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the making of a photograph and its guidance; the two arrangements; messages by photograph; messages by video (lesson/lesson.md).

   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9318]: a picture of the same scene in horizontal and vertical frames — the student says which frame suits a standing subject and which a spread-out one.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9319]: a picture of two message photographs — children reading together; work shared equally — the student tells each photograph's message in one sentence.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the five guidance points — decide, choose the colour behind, control the light, keep it in the frame, know the arrangements — the student repeats them until they hold all five.
   - ADD IMAGE (described only — not made in this build): a picture of a class watching a screen together, the book's video-watching slot — the student says what they will do after watching (write the message; draw it).
3. **Say it to your companion** — the five guidance points in order, and a planned message photograph described (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, short-answer, true-or-false.
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — plan a photograph on paper; one subject in both arrangements; a video's message written and drawn (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- The subject escapes the frame → slow down at guidance point 4: frame first, check the edges, then press.
- Cannot choose an arrangement → ask what the subject does: stands (ቋሚ) or spreads (አግዳሚ)?
- Finds no message in a photograph → ask who is in it and what they are doing together; the doing is the message.
