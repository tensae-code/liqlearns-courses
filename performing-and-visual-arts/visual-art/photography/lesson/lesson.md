# Photography

## Do it first

Have you been photographed? With what? How did you feel — and when are you photographed: at festivals, at school, with family? Stand where a photograph of you might be taken and look at the light on your face. In the last lesson you looked at photographs. In this one, you learn how a photograph is taken — and how a photograph, and even a video, passes a message.

## Sub lesson 1 — Simple ways of taking a photograph

Photography methods mean **the guidance for how a photograph should be taken**. The photographic art is performed by letting light pass through a camera lens, forming an image on film, and printing it on prepared papers. Photography serves to capture man-made and natural things in image form — and to transmit messages.

[GIF 9344 — a camera raised, the scene framed, the shutter pressed, and the photograph appears]

When you take a photograph, the guidance is:

1. **Decide what to photograph.**
2. **Choose the colour behind the subjects.**
3. **Control the amount of light.**
4. **Take care the image does not go out of the set frame.**
5. **Know the two photograph arrangements** — horizontal and vertical (next sub lesson).

A photographer at work holds the camera to the eye, steady — deciding, framing, and only then pressing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vertical and horizontal pictures

A photograph can stand or lie:

- **Vertical** (ቋሚ) arrangement — the picture stands tall: taller than it is wide.
- **Horizontal** (አግዳሚ) arrangement — the picture lies wide: wider than it is tall.

[Picture 9318 — the same scene in a horizontal frame and in a vertical frame]

The teacher guides name these portrait and landscape photography (ቁምና ዝርግ ፎቶግራፍ): the standing frame suits standing subjects — a person, a church tower; the wide frame suits spread-out subjects — a landscape, a market, a team. Before you press, turn the frame in your mind and choose: does my subject stand, or does it spread?

## Sub lesson 3 — Passing a message by photograph

A photograph's benefit to a community or country is great. By photographs we can: **know history and pass it from generation to generation; introduce culture; explain ways of life; show dress culture and food culture; show famous people's works; know historic places; introduce rare animals and exemplary people.**

[Picture 9319 — two message photographs: children reading together; work shared equally]

Look at the two message photographs: children **reading together**, and **work shared equally**. Each one passes its message without a single word. Now the practice: look at photographs and tell the message you understood from each. A good photographer decides the message first — then takes the photograph that carries it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Passing a message by video

Video is a method of presenting a message **combining sound and moving image**. Films, documentaries, dramas, music and dances and other ideas are presented by video. Watching is a skill too: watch the video shown to you, then **present the message you understood — in writing** — and **make a drawing of the message you saw**. A photograph carries its message in one still moment; a video carries its message in sound and movement together. Both ask the same of you: watch carefully, and tell the message truly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the five guidance points for taking a photograph, in order. Then describe a photograph you would take to pass the message "our school reads together" — what would be in the frame, what colour behind, standing or wide?

## Do It Yourself

1. Plan a photograph on paper: draw its frame, mark what stands inside it, write the colour behind the subject and where the light comes from.
2. Take or find two photographs of the same subject — one vertical (ቋሚ), one horizontal (አግዳሚ) — and say which serves the subject better, and why.
3. Watch a short video with someone. Write the message you understood in two sentences, and draw the message in one picture.
