# Papua New Guinea Carving and Storyboards

## Do it first

Look at the sub lesson list. Pick the part you already know something about, and say what you know. Then start at Sub lesson 1 — every part builds on the one before it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Carved storyboards

The Grade 3 guide's craft background: carved storyboards depict everyday life and legends — a story carried in wood. Environmental print belongs to the same unit: the printed and carved signs of the learner's own place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Soft carving and miniature caves

Soft carving and foam carving come first, then miniature caves modelled in soap, foam and clay — small worlds built by cutting and shaping soft materials.

## Sub lesson 3 — Carving and modelling, safely

The Grade 4 guide teaches safety when handling carving tools, what can be carved, and the process of making a carving; then clay models, and models built from wood, sticks, shells, grass chunks, soap and useful materials found in the environment.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion this lesson's one thing in your own words, naming each sub lesson as you go. Companion checks: did every part get named?

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each sub lesson's practice once more, on your own.
2. Teach one part to someone else, the way this lesson taught it to you.
3. Write one sentence: what is this lesson's one thing?
