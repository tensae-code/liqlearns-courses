# Body Painting

## Do it first

Look at the sub lesson list. Pick the part you already know something about, and say what you know. Then start at Sub lesson 1 — every part builds on the one before it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Organic colours from nature

Papua New Guinea's Grade 3 guide sets environmentally friendly organic colours — made from flowers, leaves, clay, soil and charcoal — beside modern paints, and asks which kind a painting needs. With them learners paint in geometric arrangement and make narrative painting about their own life, and meet a local painter and their work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making traditional paint

The Grade 4 guide teaches plants used to make paint and the making of traditional paint itself, step by step, with the steps of making coconut oil — the binder and carrier of the tradition — taught as part of the same craft.

## Sub lesson 3 — Traditional designs, their meanings, and body painting

Traditional designs are not decoration only: the guides teach their meanings, and body painting using the traditional organic medium — the same colours, worn on the skin for dance and ceremony.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion this lesson's one thing in your own words, naming each sub lesson as you go. Companion checks: did every part get named?

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each sub lesson's practice once more, on your own.
2. Teach one part to someone else, the way this lesson taught it to you.
3. Write one sentence: what is this lesson's one thing?
