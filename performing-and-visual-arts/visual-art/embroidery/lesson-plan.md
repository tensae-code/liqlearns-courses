# Lesson plan — Embroidery

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Embroidery
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** embroidery as creating, by hand or machine, ornamental designs or figures on fabric, needing three elements — fabric, thread and needle; its types taught one by one: cross stitch, vagonite and Russian stitch, with ribbon embroidery named in the lesson's list.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Embroidery and its three elements** — fabric, thread and needle
2. **Cross stitch and vagonite** — cross stitch precise; vagonite for beginners
3. **Russian stitch** — the special needle, worked from the reverse, in 3D

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Visual Art lessons of this course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define embroidery and name its three elements.
2. Tell cross stitch, vagonite and Russian stitch apart by how each is worked and used.

## What you need
Fabric, thread and a needle — and, for Russian stitch, its special needle.
