# Lesson plan — Chinese Literati Poetry-Painting

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Chinese Literati Poetry-Painting
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The literati unity of poetry and painting — poems inside pictures and pictures inside poems in Chinese art.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China Grade 5 Art Vol.1** — Painting a poem — literati painting joins poetry and image: understand the poem's feeling, then paint the scene it carries (pupils illustrate Li Bai, Cao Zhi's Seven-Step Poem, Jia Dao and others)
2. **China Grade 6 Art Vol.1** — Poem, calligraphy, painting and seal fused in one work — Zheng Banqiao's orchid-and-rock and Shitao's poem-painting album show how the four arts widen a picture's world
3. **China HS Chinese Painting & Calligraphy** — The four-in-one work — poem, calligraphy, painting and seal composed together (a union Western painting does not have): Wang Mian's ink plum with his own poem of integrity, Zheng Banqiao hearing the people's suffering in bamboo, Li Keran's long prose inscription on his ox; the literati-painting tradition from Wang Wei through Su Shi and Zhao Mengfu; where inscriptions and seals sit so they complete the composition

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
