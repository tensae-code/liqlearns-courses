# Lesson plan — Inuit Arts and Crafts

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Inuit Arts and Crafts
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Inuit-made artworks come in many different forms — beadwork, sewing, weaving, drawing, carving, clothing-making and tool-making — and each is real art.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Many forms, all of them art** — Inuit-made artworks come in **many different forms** — beadwork, sewing, weaving, drawing, carving, clothing-making and tool-making — and **each is real art**.
2. **ᓴᐸᖓᓕᕆᓂᖅ — beadwork** — **Beadwork** is a form Inuit learned from other cultures and **made their own**.
3. **ᒥᖅᓱᕐᓂᖅ — sewing** — **Sewing** is an Inuit art.
4. **ᑎᑎᖅᑐᒐᐅᓂᖅ — drawing and prints** — **Drawing and printmaking** are Inuit art, shown by Kenojuak Ashevak's 1960 stonecut **"The Enchanted Owl"**.
5. **ᓴᓇᙳᐊᕐᓂᖅ — carving** — **Carving** in stone and ivory: Inuit carvers turn hard northern materials into animals full of life — the book shows a carved walrus (McCord Museum).
6. **ᐊᓐᓄᕌᑦ — clothing as art** — **Clothing** is Inuit art: the sealskin parka and the kamik with embroidered, beaded tops are designed artworks, made by community artists.
7. **Tools as art** — The **ulu**, the women's knife, is made as a **beautiful object** as well as a working tool — the book's own stated theme of tools as art.
8. **ᐃᒥᖅᑎᑦ — jewellery** — **Inuit jewellery-making**: silver worked at the bench into ulu-shaped and antler-form earrings and pendants.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks only painting and carving are art → the reader teaches beadwork, sewing, clothing, tools and jewellery as art too — each form is real art.
- Thinks beadwork is an old Inuit invention from the start → the book says plainly: Inuit learned beadwork from other cultures and made it their own.
