# Inuit Arts and Crafts — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Many forms, all of them art
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Inuit-made artworks come in many different forms — beadwork, sewing, weaving, drawing, carving, clothing-making and tool-making — and each is real art.

## Sub lesson 2 — ᓴᐸᖓᓕᕆᓂᖅ — beadwork
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Beadwork is a form Inuit learned from other cultures and made their own.

## Sub lesson 3 — ᒥᖅᓱᕐᓂᖅ — sewing
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sewing is an Inuit art.

## Sub lesson 4 — ᑎᑎᖅᑐᒐᐅᓂᖅ — drawing and prints
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Drawing and printmaking are Inuit art, shown by Kenojuak Ashevak's 1960 stonecut "The Enchanted Owl".

## Sub lesson 5 — ᓴᓇᙳᐊᕐᓂᖅ — carving
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Carving in stone and ivory: Inuit carvers turn hard northern materials into animals full of life — the book shows a carved walrus (McCord Museum).

## Sub lesson 6 — ᐊᓐᓄᕌᑦ — clothing as art
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Clothing is Inuit art: the sealskin parka and the kamik with embroidered, beaded tops are designed artworks, made by community artists.

## Sub lesson 7 — Tools as art
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The ulu, the women's knife, is made as a beautiful object as well as a working tool — the book's own stated theme of tools as art.

## Sub lesson 8 — ᐃᒥᖅᑎᑦ — jewellery
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Inuit jewellery-making: silver worked at the bench into ulu-shaped and antler-form earrings and pendants.
