# Inuit Arts and Crafts

## Sub lesson 1 — Many forms, all of them art

Inuit-made artworks come in **many different forms** — beadwork, sewing, weaving, drawing, carving, clothing-making and tool-making — and **each is real art**. Canada's Level 11 reader walks through the forms one by one, in photographs of finished work and of the makers at work.

## Sub lesson 2 — ᓴᐸᖓᓕᕆᓂᖅ — beadwork

**Beadwork** is a form Inuit learned from other cultures and **made their own**. It is slow, long work, and its finished pieces — beaded collars and necklaces — are beautiful.

## Sub lesson 3 — ᒥᖅᓱᕐᓂᖅ — sewing

**Sewing** is an Inuit art. Sewers are famed for small, beautiful sewn works — dolls, kamik tops and hanging pieces — made from cloth, sealskin and caribou skin.

## Sub lesson 4 — ᑎᑎᖅᑐᒐᐅᓂᖅ — drawing and prints

**Drawing and printmaking** are Inuit art, shown by Kenojuak Ashevak's 1960 stonecut **"The Enchanted Owl"**. At print centres such as Pangnirtung, drawings and prints are made and sold.

## Sub lesson 5 — ᓴᓇᙳᐊᕐᓂᖅ — carving

**Carving** in stone and ivory: Inuit carvers turn hard northern materials into animals full of life — the book shows a carved walrus (McCord Museum).

## Sub lesson 6 — ᐊᓐᓄᕌᑦ — clothing as art

**Clothing** is Inuit art: the sealskin parka and the kamik with embroidered, beaded tops are designed artworks, made by community artists.

## Sub lesson 7 — Tools as art

The **ulu**, the women's knife, is made as a **beautiful object** as well as a working tool — the book's own stated theme of tools as art.

## Sub lesson 8 — ᐃᒥᖅᑎᑦ — jewellery

**Inuit jewellery-making**: silver worked at the bench into ulu-shaped and antler-form earrings and pendants.
