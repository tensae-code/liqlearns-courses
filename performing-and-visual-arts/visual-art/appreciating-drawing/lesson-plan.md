# Lesson plan — Appreciating Drawing

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Appreciating Drawing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** appreciating drawing: knowing what drawing is — putting a thought down on paper, canvas or wall with pencil, colour, pen or charcoal — and showing that appreciation by drawing and by arranging your drawings for friends to see.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the drawing lessons of this unit — you appreciate best what your own hand has tried. The Grade 2 book places it in its Appreciating the Arts chapter: each art is appreciated by doing it.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the drawing lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.
- Next door: Appreciating the Arts in First Steps, where all the arts are appreciated together.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what drawing is: putting a thought down on paper, canvas or wall using pencil, colour, pen, charcoal and the like.
2. Name drawing materials found in your own area and use them.
3. Draw as the teacher shows, then arrange your drawings in order so friends can appreciate them — a first classroom exhibition.

## What you need
Your drawings from this unit, a wall or table to arrange them on, and drawing materials from your area — pencil, colour, pen or charcoal.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks at three of their own drawings from this unit and picks the one they would show a visitor — before appreciation is defined (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

- AUDIO — appreciation sentences modelled → the student says one true liked thing per drawing.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student hangs the exhibition along with it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what they like in a drawing → give sentence starters: "The colour…", "The shape…", "It makes me feel…". One true small thing beats big empty praise.
- Ashamed of their own drawing in the row → every drawer in this unit began with a free scribble; the row shows a journey, and journeys are what exhibitions are for.
