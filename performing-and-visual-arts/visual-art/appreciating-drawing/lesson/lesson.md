# Appreciating Drawing


## Do it first

Take out three drawings you made in this unit. Look at them slowly.

If a visitor could see only ONE, which would you show? Why that one?

You have just appreciated drawing — your own. This lesson gives that looking its name, its definition, and its ceremony: the exhibition.

## What drawing is

The book defines drawing plainly: drawing is **putting a thought down on paper, canvas or wall** using **pencil, colour, pen, charcoal and the like**.

A thought — that is the treasure. The hand makes marks; the marks carry a thought from inside one person to the eyes of another.

And how do you appreciate drawing? The book's answer is doing, not only looking: **use the drawing materials found in your area, and draw**. A person who has tried to draw an oval in one stroke appreciates another person's oval with new eyes.

## Showing our work

Then comes the ceremony — Activity 4.2: **draw as the teacher shows, then arrange your drawings in order so friends can appreciate them**.

[Picture 9162 — drawings pinned up in a row for friends to see]

Arranging is part of the art: which drawing opens the row? Which two sit side by side? Leave space between them, like words need spaces.

When the row is up, walk it slowly. Appreciating means giving each drawing your real attention — and being able to say one true thing you like about each: the careful colour, the closed shape, the feeling in it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Drawing is putting a thought down on paper, canvas or wall — with pencil, colour, pen or charcoal. We appreciate it by drawing, and by showing our drawings in a row for friends." Then show them your row.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the definition of drawing from memory, and list the drawing materials you can find in your area.
2. Arrange five drawings in a row at home or in class. Change the order once — say why the new order is better.
3. Visit your own exhibition: say one true thing you appreciate about each drawing, out loud.
