# Lesson plan — The Colour Wheel

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** The Colour Wheel
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the twelve-part colour wheel — primary, secondary and tertiary colours — and how colours relate: warm and cool halves, neutrals, and the opposites that sit across the wheel from each other.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Primary Colours and Mixing Colours — the learner can already name red, yellow and blue and mix green, orange and purple; this lesson builds the whole wheel the older books use.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Colour basics: light, surface, eye** — colour is the effect of light reflected from a thing; seeing colour needs light, a reflecting surface and the eye; a colour has a name, a purity and a value (Grade 5, printed pp. 8–9).
2. **The wheel and the secondary colours** — the wheel is a circle of twelve parts; primaries sit at equal distances; equal mixes of primaries make orange, green and violet (Grade 5, printed pp. 9–10).
3. **Tertiary colours and the colour value chart** — six tertiaries from a primary plus its neighbouring secondary, in equal amounts; one colour lightened with white and darkened with black (Grade 5, printed pp. 10–11).
4. **Warm and cool colours, and the neutrals** — warm colours feel like sun and fire, cool colours like water and air; black and white are neutrals, on no wheel and neither warm nor cool (Grade 5, printed p. 12).
5. **Opposite colours** — a colour's opposite sits directly across the wheel; the pairs named (Grade 5, printed pp. 12–13).

6. **One colour, and neighbours: monochromatic and analogous (Grade 6)** — colour character in full — warm/cool, monochromatic, complementary, analogous.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Primary Colours and Mixing Colours. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how colour is seen: light, a reflecting surface and the eye.
2. Build the wheel: three primaries, three secondaries between them, six tertiaries.
3. Name the warm half and the cool half of the wheel.
4. Say what black and white are: neutrals, not on the wheel.
5. Find any colour's opposite across the wheel and name the main pairs.

## What you need
Paints or coloured pencils in red, yellow and blue, paper, and a plate or lid to trace a circle.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Draw a big circle. Put red, yellow and blue on it, far apart from each other. Leave the spaces between them empty — by the end of this lesson you will fill every space and read the whole circle like a clock. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up secondary and tertiary → a secondary is primary + primary; a tertiary is primary + the secondary next to it. Count the parents.
- Cannot find an opposite → draw a straight line through the wheel's centre from the colour; the colour it lands on is the opposite.
