# Chiaroscuro: Light and Shadow

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about chiaroscuro: light and shadow. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Light and shadow on a round fruit

The spherical body is among the simplest shapes and recurs through all nature — fruits, vegetables, the patterns on living creatures' bodies. Where the light falls, the fruit is lit; turned away, it falls into shadow; and it is the passage from light to dark across the round surface that makes the flat drawing stand out as a solid thing.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the masters show

Many world and local artists drew spherical bodies as fruit still lifes. Paul Cézanne stands out for showing light and shadow's role in bringing out colour's beauty and strength, and light and shadow appear likewise in the Saudi artist Zahra Al-Ghamdi's painting — the same play of lit and dark, in a world master and in the Kingdom's own artist.

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing fruit in soft pastels

The practice: spherical fruits are drawn and shaded in soft pastels — look at the real fruit, fix where the light strikes and where the shadow gathers, and grade the colour from light to dark so the roundness appears. The shading is not decoration: it is how the eye is told the fruit is round.
