# Genres of Painting

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — The genres of painting — landscape, portrait, still life, battle and everyday-life (genre) painting, and how each is recognised. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Genuri ale picturii

G4 Ch. 4, Genuri ale picturii — the genres of painting: landscape, portrait (and self-portrait), still life, and composition — genre scenes, historical, religious and mythological compositions, each with its masters.

## Sub lesson 2 — categories of composition

Unit 3, categories of composition — the genre composition of everyday life, the mythological composition (Prometheus; Dubinovschi's "Strâmbă-Lemne"), and the symbolic sculptural composition.

## Sub lesson 3 — The everyday-life composition

Unit III §1 — the everyday-life composition: Dutch masters Vermeer ("The Milkmaid", its diagonal balancing the empty wall) and Pieter de Hooch; in Romanian art Grigorescu and Aman; in the 20th century Corneliu Baba, and in Bessarabia Igor Vieru, Rusu-Ciobanu, Glebus Sainciuc, Romanescu.

## Sub lesson 4 — The historical genre of composition

Unit III §1 — the historical genre of composition: great persons and memorable events, battle and war scenes with clear schemas that spotlight the idea — Velázquez's "Surrender of Breda", Aman's "Turkish Envoys at Vlad Țepeș".

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Moldova's own way of teaching its arts?
