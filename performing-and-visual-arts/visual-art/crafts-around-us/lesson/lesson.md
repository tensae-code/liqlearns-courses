# Crafts Around Us

## Do it first

Look around your home. Find one thing that was made by hand — a pot, a basket, a tool, a necklace. Hold it, or picture it clearly. Who made it? What is it made of? What do you use it for? This lesson walks through the crafts all around us, one craft at a time.

## Sub lesson 1 — Metal work

Metal workers shape iron and other metals into the tools daily life needs. Look at these metal-work products: a curved knife, a sickle for cutting, a pointed tool, and a hoe blade for digging the soil. Each tool's shape fits its job — the sickle curves to gather and cut stems, the hoe blade is wide and flat to turn the earth.

[Picture 9300 — metal tools: knife, sickle, pointed tool, hoe blade]

## Sub lesson 2 — Pottery

Potters shape clay soil and fire it hard. Two pots to know well: the black clay cooking pot, ድስት (dist), and the red decorated clay coffee pot, ጀበና (jebena). The dist cooks the family's food; the jebena brews and pours coffee. Clay, water, skilled hands and fire — that is pottery.

[Picture 9301 — a black dist with its lid, and a red jebena]

## Sub lesson 3 — Weaving

Weavers make cloth and baskets by passing threads and strips over and under each other. Look at a woven mesob — the basket table with its lid, where food is served — and a colourful round woven sefed tray. The pattern is not painted on afterwards: the weaving itself makes the pattern, strip by strip.

[Picture 9302 — a lidded mesob and a patterned sefed tray]

## Sub lesson 4 — Handicrafts old and new

Handicrafts — እደ ጥበብ — are the useful, beautiful things people make by hand or with simple materials, at home and in small workshops. The craft families are: pottery work, weaving work, wood work, jewellery work, basketry and sewing work, and metal work. Look at a silver necklace, an agelgil (a lidded food container), a mesob, and a three-legged barchuma stool.

[Picture 9303 — silver necklace, agelgil, and a barchuma stool]

These crafts also clothe and furnish a home: clay products, weaving, gold and silver jewellery, clothing, metal utensils, wooden decorations and household goods. Handicraft is a wide field — leather belts, bags and shoes belong to it too. And the crafts are still alive at the market stall, where makers sell what their hands have made.

[Picture 9330 — a market stall of baskets, pots, a jebena, tools and jewellery]

## Sub lesson 5 — Three families of pottery

Pottery — preparing clay soil for a purpose and firing it in high heat so it hardens and serves long — has three families. Earthenware: built thin, smoothed, fired at low heat; red, brown, black or tan; it holds liquids, and it is the weakest of the three. Stoneware: fired at very high heat until it is hard like stone; strong with heat, so it serves for stoves (ምድጃ), cooking and serving hot food. Ceramic ware: fired at the highest heat; very hard, with attractive designs in varied colours and a shining finish.

[Picture 9320 — the three pottery families: earthen, stone and ceramic]

## Sub lesson 6 — Crafts kept alive

Crafts survive because people keep making and teaching them. Pottery is kept alive mainly by women who inherit their mothers' skills; today many organise in associations and specialise — one makes figurines, another pots, another plates — so the art survives. The collectable crafts of our cultures include basketwork and agelgil, silver and crosses, textiles, religious paintings and manuscripts, woodwork, gourds and calabashes, stonework and pottery. When you buy, use and care for a handmade thing, you help keep its craft alive.

## Say it to your companion

Name one craft from each family — metal, pottery, weaving, wood, jewellery, basketry — and say what each thing is used for. Then tell your companion which pottery family a jebena belongs to, and why.

## Do It Yourself

1. Walk through your home and list every handmade thing you find, with its craft family.
2. Pick one craft thing and draw it carefully, showing the material it is made of.
3. Ask an older person who makes such things in your area, and what they learned from whom. Tell the class what you heard.
