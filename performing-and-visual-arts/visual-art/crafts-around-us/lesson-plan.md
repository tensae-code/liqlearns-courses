# Lesson plan — Crafts Around Us

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Crafts Around Us
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the crafts of our community — metal work, pottery and weaving first, then the whole handicraft family — what each is made of, and what it is for.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own home and market — the pots, baskets and tools they already use every day. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Metal work** — the metal tools of daily life: curved knife, sickle, pointed tool, hoe blade.
2. **Pottery** — clay fired hard: the black ድስት (dist) and the red ጀበና (jebena).
3. **Weaving** — patterns made by the weaving itself: the lidded mesob and the sefed tray.
4. **Handicrafts old and new** — እደ ጥበብ: the six craft families and what they give a home.
5. **Three families of pottery** — earthenware, stoneware and ceramic ware, told apart by heat and strength.
6. **Crafts kept alive** — how crafts survive: inherited skills, associations, and people who value handmade things (partly teacher-guide evidence — see sources.md).

7. **Ancient handcraft: art you use every day (Grade 8)** — fully indigenous materials, made by hand for use — coins, knockers, pottery, weaving, embroidery.
8. **Ancient weaving: clothes for every occasion (Grade 8)** — ሽመና from cotton, wool and thread — qemis to aklil, for councils, weddings, mourning and daily life.
9. **Ancient pottery and today's pottery works (Grade 8)** — from the Axumite era — insira, dist, masero, jebena, gan — pottery for food, income and culture.
10. **Stone tools and ancient jewellery (Grade 8)** — spear, knife, axe and the stone mill; ጌጣጌጥ — folk jewellery from local inputs, worn across the body.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Looking at Pictures (First Steps) — the learner can already say what a picture shows. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the metal-work products and say what each tool is for.
2. Tell a dist from a jebena and say what each is used for.
3. Explain how weaving itself makes the pattern on a mesob or sefed.
4. List the handicraft families and give one product of each.
5. Tell earthenware, stoneware and ceramic ware apart by firing heat and strength.
6. Say how pottery and the other crafts are kept alive today.

## What you need
One handmade thing from home to look at. Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes dist and jebena → the dist is the wide black cooking pot with a lid; the jebena is the round coffee pot with the long neck and spout.
- Thinks the mesob pattern is painted → the pattern is the weaving: coloured strips pass over and under, and the pattern grows as the weaver works.
- Mixes the pottery families → order them by heat: earthenware lowest and weakest, stoneware high and hard, ceramic highest and shining.
