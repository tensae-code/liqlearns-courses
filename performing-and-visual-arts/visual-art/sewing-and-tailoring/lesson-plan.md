# Lesson plan — Sewing and Tailoring

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Sewing and Tailoring
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the tailor's craft and first sewing — measuring, the pattern (tipar), basting (a însăila) and festoon stitch (a festona), the craft's own words, a sewn fish pencil-case, and buttons sewn as a heart.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The little tailor** — how the tailor works — he measures you with the tailor's centimetre, cuts the material following the pattern (tipar), bastes the pieces (a însăila — sewing with sparse stitches), and festoon-stitches the edges (a festona) so the material does not fray; the craft's words: a croi (to cut to a drawn outline), a retușa (to correct).
2. **The little fish pencil-case** — sew a fish-shaped pencil-case from two cloths — draw the fish on the cloth, cut it out, turn and stuff the shape, finish with a drawn eye and a cord.
3. **Buttons sewn as a heart** — decorate your headband or bag — gather coloured buttons from clothes that no longer fit and sew them one beside another in the shape of a heart on a bag.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks basting is the final stitching → basting (a însăila) is sewing with sparse stitches to hold the pieces first; the edges are then festoon-stitched (a festona) so the material does not fray.
