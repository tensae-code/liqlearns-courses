# Lesson plan — Double Letters

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Double Letters
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** double writing (ድርብ) done properly: on squared paper, and between two parallel guide lines, so letters and numerals sit true — neither too high nor too low.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands beside Beautiful Writing, where the learner met double (outlined) writing as one of calligraphy's methods. This lesson is the careful workshop of that one method: the paper and the lines that keep double letters honest.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Double writing on squared paper** — why the squares suit this practice best; the peer exchange that corrects it.
2. **Double writing between guide lines** — two parallel lines; letters and numerals resting true; reading sizes, widths and structures.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Beautiful Writing (Visual Art) — where double writing (ድርብ) is met among calligraphy's methods. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why squared paper suits double-letter practice best.
2. Work the peer exchange: comment on a companion's double writing, separating strong and weak sides, and correct faults freely.
3. Rule two parallel guide lines and write letters and numerals resting between them — not too high, not too low.
4. Read a piece of double writing for its letters' differing sizes, widths and structures.

## What you need
Squared paper, plain paper, a ruler, pencil — and patience: double writing is slow writing, done twice-looked.

## If the learner is stuck
- The twin lines drift apart → slow down and keep one steady distance all the way around the letter.
- Letters float or sink → check them against the guide lines: head and foot should rest in their places.
- Peer comments sting → the book's way is free correction: strong sides named, weak sides named, faults fixed — that is the kindness of the exercise.
