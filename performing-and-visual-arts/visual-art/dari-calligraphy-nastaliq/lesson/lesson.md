# Dari Calligraphy: Nastaliq

## Do it first

Start with the first part below — do it or make it before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Single letters, their movements and their dot measure

Grade 4 changes the hand from Naskh to Nastaliq. Each letter — آ، ب، ج، د، ر، س، ص، ط، ع، ف، ق، ک، گ، ل، م، ن، و، ه، ی — is calligraphed by the rules of the hand, counting its movements in the model line; س and ص come in two ways, toothed (دندانه‌دار) and stretched (کشش‌دار). Grade 5 adds the dot system: every letter is counted and placed in dots — how many dots the alif is made of, and where its madda sits.

## Sub lesson 2 — Whole words in Nastaliq

Then whole chosen words are written with the reed pen on the baseline — آدم (human), ادب (manners), راستی (truthfulness), صلح (peace), قانون (law), کتاب (book), مادر (mother), نان (bread), هنر (art) — and in Grade 5 the dotted words of patience (صبر), the teacher (استاد، معلم), the pen (قلم) and prayer (نماز).

## Sub lesson 3 — The words inside sentences

Each practised word is then written correctly inside a full sentence in Nastaliq on the baseline, with all its letter movements — the year's words carried from single letters to flowing written sentences.

## Sub lesson 4 — Combining letters — and the composed page: chalipa and siyah-mashq

Grade 6 combines the Nastaliq letters in every position of a word, then crowns the subject with two composition arts: chalipa, the diagonal crossed panel of set writing (کتابت), and siyah-mashq, the 'black practice' page where words are written again and again, overlapping in strong black strokes, until the practice page itself becomes the picture.
