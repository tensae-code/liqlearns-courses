# Vernacular Architecture of the Dominican Republic

## Do it first

Quick start: Old houses are documents. Read them like a reporter. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Vernacular architecture — the heritage houses

**Vernacular architecture** is a region's own building, and it **reflects the region's culture, history and ideology, from indigenous times on**. The Grade 5 book learns it as **fieldwork**: the learner works **as a reporter**, documenting the **heritage houses of their community** — the **Victorian house of Puerto Plata**, **colonial Santo Domingo** and the **country house of San Cristóbal** are named as examples.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the maqueta

Then the documented house is **rebuilt as a maqueta** — a **scale model on a cardboard base**, made from **used boxes, wooden sticks, papers and glue**, so that its **architectural details can be identified** in the model as they were found in the street.

## Sub lesson 3 — Models of the Colonial City

At Grade 8 the models become **models of our identity**: recreating the buildings that **represent a historical epoch of the country** — the **Colonial City of Santo Domingo** is pictured — **analysing their styles and elements** and building the maqueta **from waste materials**: **walls with tabs, doors and windows drawn, cut, folded and glued**.
