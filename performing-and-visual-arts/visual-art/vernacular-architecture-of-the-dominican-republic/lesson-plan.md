# Lesson plan — Vernacular Architecture of the Dominican Republic

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Vernacular Architecture of the Dominican Republic
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Dominican Republic's vernacular architecture — the heritage building of its regions, learned like a reporter and rebuilt as a maqueta: the Victorian house of Puerto Plata, colonial Santo Domingo, the country house of San Cristóbal, and the Colonial City's models of identity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Vernacular architecture — the heritage houses** — vernacular architecture reflects a region's culture, history and ideology from indigenous times on: working as a reporter to document the heritage houses of my community (Victorian house of Puerto Plata, colonial Santo Domingo, country house of San Cristóbal named as examples).
2. **Building the maqueta** — building a scale model (maqueta) of the documented house on a cardboard base, from used boxes, wooden sticks, papers and glue, so its architectural details can be identified.
3. **Models of the Colonial City** — architectural models of our identity: recreating the environment's buildings that represent a historical epoch of the country (the Colonial City of Santo Domingo pictured), analysing their styles and elements and building the maqueta from waste materials — walls with tabs, doors and windows drawn, cut, folded and glued.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
