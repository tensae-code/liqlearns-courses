# Shapes You Can Draw


## Do it first

Try two things before any teaching.

First: draw an egg shape — an oval — in ONE stroke, without lifting your pencil. Did the ends meet?

Second: draw four lines that join up into a box. Where did the last line have to go?

A shape is what you get when lines join and close. This lesson walks through every shape family the books teach — and ends by building a house and a car out of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Round and oval shapes

The first closed shapes in the book are **round and oval shapes** — filled ovals and ellipses in many colours, scattered like stones and grouped like eggs in a basket.

[Picture 9153 — coloured filled ovals and ellipses]

An oval is a circle that stretched. Draw it the proud way — **in one stroke**, starting at the top, going round, and coming home to where you started:

[GIF 9183 — a pencil drawing an oval in one stroke]

Fill a page: big ovals, small ovals, ovals standing, ovals lying down.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shapes made of lines

Grade 2 says it by showing it: **a shape is made of lines**. Its figure builds shapes line by line — each side a line you already know, until the last line closes the shape.

[Picture 9176 — shapes built from lines]

Watch the closing happen:

[GIF 9185 — lines joining up into a closed shape]

If the lines do not close, the shape leaks — colour would run out of it. Draw a triangle and a square slowly, side by side, and check the corners: did every line meet the next?

## Sub lesson 3 — Regular shapes

A **regular shape** is an even shape — its sides and corners match in an orderly way: the square with four equal sides, the even rectangle, the round circle.

[Picture 9175 — regular shapes: square, circle, triangle, rectangle, hexagon]

The book's activity is simple and grown-up: **copy the pictured shapes in your own way, and show the teacher**. Your own way — your hand, your size — but the shape must stay even and closed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Geometric shapes with a ruler

Grade 3 gives the family its name. An **open shape (ዝርግ ቅርፅ)** is a picture made on paper by **joining lines**. Open shapes divide into two kinds: natural and man-made.

The man-made, measured kind are the **geometric shapes** — shapes **made with a ruler**: the book names the **pyramid, circle, square and rectangle**.

[Picture 9155 — geometric shapes: pyramid, circle, square, rectangle]

The activity: join lines to draw different geometric pictures in pencil — then design enjoyable pictures by joining open shapes together.

## Sub lesson 5 — Open shapes from nature

The other kind of open shape is already drawn for you — by nature. **Natural open shapes** are the shapes found in nature: of **flowers, animals, leaves, people, clouds** and so on.

[Picture 9156 — open shapes from nature: flowers, a leaf, a cloud, a person]

You do not need a ruler for these; you need eyes. Look at a leaf: what line does its edge travel? Look at a cloud: where would your pencil go?

The activity joins the two kinds: **join different open shapes to make enjoyable pictures** — a flower's circle on a stem's line, a cloud over a house's square.

## Sub lesson 6 — Forms: cylinder, cube and cone

A shape is flat. A **form (ፎርም)** is a shape with body — drawing the **true appearance** of natural and man-made things with pencil, charcoal, colour and other things.

The basic forms in drawing are three: the **cylinder** (round and tall, like a cup), the **cube** (a box), and the **cone** (a point on a round base). The book renders them **shaded** — light on one side, dark on the other — because shading is what makes a flat drawing stand up.

[Picture 9157 — the basic forms: cylinder, cube and cone, shaded]

[GIF 9188 — a pencil shading a cylinder from light to dark]

The activity: draw the basic forms **repeatedly in pencil**, then **colour** your pencil design in a colour you enjoy.

## Sub lesson 7 — Joining and colouring geometric shapes

Now the geometric shapes stop standing alone. **Join them** — square to triangle, circle to rectangle — and **repeat them**, and the joined work becomes a **design**.

[Picture 9177 — joined and coloured geometric designs]

The book's activities walk it in order: join geometric pictures; **colour your joined work in a colour you enjoy**; then make designs by **joining and repeating** basic geometric open shapes.

A repeated shape is a pattern you already know from lines — now the pattern is built of shapes.

## Sub lesson 8 — Building a house from shapes

A house is shapes keeping company: a **square or rectangle** for the walls, a **triangle** for the roof, small rectangles for door and windows.

[Picture 9158 — a house built from shapes]

Watch the parts arrive and settle:

[GIF 9187 — shapes sliding together into a house]

The book's activity: **design regular houses from the pictures, then colour them**. Your house can stand anywhere — give it the roof and door of the houses you know.

## Sub lesson 9 — Building a car from shapes

A car is shapes too: a long **rectangle** for the body, a shape on top for the cabin and windows — and two **circles** that do the rolling.

[Picture 9159 — a car built from shapes]

The book's activity matches the house: **draw the car design, and colour it**. When your shapes roll, you have built a car out of geometry.

End of the lesson — look back: ovals, line-built shapes, regular shapes, geometric shapes, nature's shapes, solid forms, joined designs, a house, a car. Every one of them is lines that joined and closed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "A shape is lines that join and close. An oval is drawn in one stroke. Geometric shapes are measured with a ruler — pyramid, circle, square, rectangle. A form has body: cylinder, cube, cone. And joined shapes build designs, a house and a car." Point to each thing as you name it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw five ovals in one stroke each — big, small, standing, lying down.
2. Draw the three forms — cylinder, cube, cone — in pencil, shade one side of each, then colour one of them.
3. Build a house AND a car from shapes on one page, and write the shape-name beside every part.
