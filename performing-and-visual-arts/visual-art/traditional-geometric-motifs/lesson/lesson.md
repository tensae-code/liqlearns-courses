# Traditional Geometric Motifs

## Do it first

Before you read anything: try the first part below with your hands or your voice. What you manage — and where you stop — is exactly where this lesson starts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Traditional design L1, Geometric motifs in grids

Traditional geometric motifs — drawing geometric motifs inside square grids, the foundation of Iran's traditional design.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional design L2, The drum-in-drum motif

Drawing the طبل در طبل (drum-in-drum) motif on a diagonal grid and extending it into new motifs, as used in the tilework of Yazd's Jameh Mosque.

## Sub lesson 3 — Traditional design L3, The wave motif

Drawing the نقش موج (wave motif) across a diagonal-square grid until it covers a whole ground, then transferring and colouring it, as in Isfahan's Chaharbagh tilework.

## Sub lesson 4 — Decorative motifs L1, Chaharlangeh

چهارلنگه: drawing the four-part chaharlangeh motif of Iran's decorative design.

## Sub lesson 5 — Decorative motifs L2, The eight-knot and chaharlangeh

گره هشت و چهارلنگه: constructing the eight-knot (gereh) geometry with the chaharlangeh.

## Sub lesson 6 — Traditional design L1, Traditional design

طراحی سنتی: what traditional design is — nature's forms simplified into motifs, and the family of traditional arts those designs serve.

## Sub lesson 7 — Traditional design L2, Geometric motifs

آشنایی با نقش‌های هندسی: getting to know the geometric motifs of Iran's traditional arts, drawn with geometric shapes and lines on squared paper.

## Sub lesson 8 — Traditional design L3, Drawing the gereh

طراحی گره: drawing the gereh (knot) — a square and a diamond set on squared paper, built into the knot patterns of traditional design.
