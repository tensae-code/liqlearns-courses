| Serbia — "Signs and symbols" — by a picture, various messages and ideas can be conveyed, and in our everyday surroundings we constantly meet many different pictures — messages; it is important, whenever we see something, to ask ourselves — what does this mean? and also — what message does this carry to us?; Serbia's flags pictured — the flag of the Serbian Revolution of 1804, the flag of the King's Guard of 1904, and the flag of the Republic of Serbia (original target `visual-art/signs-symbols-and-pictograms`) | Book 2, printed pp. 46–47 |
| Mozambique — Lesson 8, visual codes: signs and signos — visual signs (sinais) carry a universal meaning anyone can read (traffic prohibition and obligation signs); signos carry their own meaning and replace what they represent, in four kinds: icons (they imitate the object: a map, a photograph, a building plan), indices (they relate to what they show and need an explanation: clouds for rain, smoke for fire, tyre marks for braking), symbols (understood by one group: the national emblem, the red ribbon, the white dove, a logo) and pictograms (their meaning is immediate, needing no explanation) (student book; original target `visual-art/signs-symbols-and-pictograms`) | Book 3, printed pp. 59–61 |

| Colombia — Project 1, Subproject 1, Momento 3 "Símbolos y representaciones": the symbolism of colour — colour's meaning changes from culture to culture (the many whites the Inuit distinguish to judge the ice; white as mourning in some Eastern cultures, black in Colombia's), colours felt as warm or cold; and Colombia's own geometric painter Carlos Rojas among the "great creators" (student book; original target `visual-art/art-that-speaks-in-symbols`) | Book 4, printed pp. 52–60 |
