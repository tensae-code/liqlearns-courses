# Signs, Symbols and Pictograms

## Do it first

Quick start: say in one sentence what you think this lesson is about — people understand each other by signs. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pictures that stand for subjects

A sign can be a **small picture that stands for a whole thing**. The Grade 2 exercise: **think up and make three small pictures for three different school subjects**, so that you and your classmates can recognise which subject each picture is for — think **what is typical for each subject**, on the basis of which it could be recognised.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sign systems — hands, dots and roads

People use different kinds of signs for mutual understanding, and **picture signs convey definite messages**. There is the **hand alphabet of sign language** for the deaf and **Braille's raised-dot letters** for the blind. **Traffic signs** serve understanding in traffic. And the rule of all signs: **the more clearly a sign is designed, the more people understand it.**

## Sub lesson 3 — Signs through history — and Serbia's flags

Through history people have used different signs, from **seals, coats of arms and flags** to the signs marking institutions — theatres, museums and the like (a seal from Mohenjo-daro and the sign of the National Museum in Belgrade are pictured). We meet picture-messages every day, so whenever we see something we should ask: **what does this mean? — and what message does this carry to us?** The Grade 3 book pictures Serbia's flags: the **flag of the Serbian Revolution of 1804**, the **flag of the King's Guard of 1904**, and the **flag of the Republic of Serbia**.
