# Lesson plan — Signs, Symbols and Pictograms

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Signs, Symbols and Pictograms
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** people understand each other by signs — small pictures that stand for subjects, the hand alphabet and Braille's raised dots, traffic signs, seals, coats of arms and flags — and the more clearly a sign is designed, the more people understand it; whenever you see a picture-message, ask: what does this mean?.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pictures that stand for subjects** — A sign can be a small picture that stands for a whole thing.
2. **Sign systems — hands, dots and roads** — People use different kinds of signs for mutual understanding, and picture signs convey definite messages.
3. **Signs through history — and Serbia's flags** — Through history people have used different signs, from seals, coats of arms and flags to the signs marking institutions — theatres, museums and the like (a seal from Mohenjo-daro and the sign of the National Museum in Belgrade are pictured).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
