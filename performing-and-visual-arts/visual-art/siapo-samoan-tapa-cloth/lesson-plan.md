# Lesson plan — Siapo: Samoan Tapa Cloth

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Siapo: Samoan Tapa Cloth
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** siapo, Samoa's tapa cloth — how it is made, where its symbols come from in nature, and how its patterns and legends become a learner's own painting or print.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of its title — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How siapo is made** — analyse the techniques, materials, design elements and processes used in making Samoan tapa.
2. **Where tapa symbols come from** — the natural sources that inspire tapa designs, and drawing traditional Samoan patterns with linear and curved lines.
3. **Designing from siapo and legend** — design a painting or print based on Samoan siapo and legend.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of the Visual Art title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how siapo is studied: its techniques, materials, designs and processes.
2. Say where tapa designs come from and make a pattern of linear and curved lines.
3. Design a painting or print based on siapo and legend.

## What you need
Paper and drawing tools; a leaf or natural object to study for a symbol.
