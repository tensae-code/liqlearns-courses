# Siapo: Samoan Tapa Cloth

## Do it first

Look at a leaf. Now imagine its shape repeated across a whole cloth, in straight lines and curved lines, until the cloth itself tells you where it came from. Samoa's artists have done exactly that for generations — on siapo.

## Sub lesson 1 — How siapo is made

Samoa's primary Expressive Arts programme teaches **siapo, Samoan tapa**, as a study of making: pupils **analyse the techniques, materials, design elements and processes used in making Samoan tapa**. The cloth is learned from the making outward — what it is made of, how it is worked, and how its designs are built.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where tapa symbols come from

The programme asks the design question directly: pupils **explain where tapa symbols come from — the natural sources that inspire tapa designs** — and **draw traditional Samoan patterns with linear and curved lines**, the two line families the patterns are built from.

## Sub lesson 3 — Designing from siapo and legend

The study ends in the learner's own work: **design a painting or print based on Samoan siapo and legend** — Samoa's cloth and Samoa's stories together as the ground of a new design.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what four things do pupils analyse in siapo making, and where do tapa symbols come from? Companion checks: techniques, materials, design elements, processes? Natural sources?

## Do It Yourself

1. List the four things analysed in the making of Samoan tapa.
2. Draw one traditional-style pattern using linear lines, and the same pattern using curved lines.
3. Sketch a small painting or print design based on siapo and a legend you know — by description and sketch only.
