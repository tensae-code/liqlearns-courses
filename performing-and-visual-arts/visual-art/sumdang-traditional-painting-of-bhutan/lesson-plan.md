# Lesson plan — Sumdang: Traditional Painting of Bhutan

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Sumdang: Traditional Painting of Bhutan
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bhutan's Sumdang traditional painting, step by step — draw a traditional picture, lay the basic colour inside the outline, mix it with white for the border line, draw the boundary lines with white, and apply black to the inner part.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Draw and lay the basic colour** — Draw a traditional picture, then apply the basic colour inside the outline.
2. **White at the border, boundary lines in white** — Mix the basic colour with white and apply it at the border line; then draw the boundary lines with white.
3. **Black to the inner part** — Apply black to the inner part — the last step of the Sumdang sequence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the colours can go in any order → Sumdang is a sequence: basic colour, then white-mixed at the border, boundary lines in white, black last inside.
- Thinks white is only for mixing → white works twice in Sumdang: mixed into the basic colour at the border, and pure for the boundary lines.
