# Lesson plan — Sculpture and Historic Places

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Sculpture and Historic Places
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sculpture — art you can touch and walk around — its two kinds (round and relief), its four making techniques with casting in full, and the monuments and artifacts that carry a country's past.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Making with Clay and Paper — modelling is already in the learner's hands; sculpture names what modelling belongs to and adds carving, assembling and casting. Sub lessons 5–6 stand on teacher-guide evidence, labelled so.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Appreciating sculpture and historic places** — slow looking, what made you happy, and drawing the places you know.
2. **Round and relief** — the two kinds: free-standing, walked around; or raised on a flat surface, front only.
3. **Four ways to make sculpture** — modelling, carving, assembling (in full), casting named.
4. **Casting: one form, many copies** — the mould, the candle example, and gypsum casting step by step.
5. **Sculpture with clay and papier-mâché** — the five steps, from teacher-guide evidence.
6. **Monuments and artifacts** — what an artifact and a monument are, from teacher-guide evidence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Making with Clay and Paper (modelling) and Painting Then and Now (appreciating). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Appreciate a sculpture aloud and draw a historic place they know.
2. Tell in-the-round from relief, with the viewing rule for each.
3. Name the four techniques and assemble a figure from found materials.
4. Give the gypsum-casting sequence, naming negative mould and positive cast.
5. Give the five papier-mâché steps and its biggest mistake (painting too soon).
6. Define artifact and monument, with Ethiopian examples.

## What you need
Paper and pencil/colours; found materials for assembling; a candle and a cup (with an adult) for the casting example.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes round and relief → ask one question: "Can I walk all the way around it?" Yes = in the round; no, front only = relief.
- Mixes negative and positive → the mould is the hollow copy (negative); what comes out of it is the form itself (positive).
- Thinks casting makes only one → the candle in the cup can be melted and set again; a mould exists precisely to multiply.
