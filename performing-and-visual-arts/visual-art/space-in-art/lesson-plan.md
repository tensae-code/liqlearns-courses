# Lesson plan — Space in Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Space in Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** space as art's subject — cosmonaut artists, stars and constellations in art, how artists imagined the future, and space-themed crafts..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Artistic Labour** — the starry sky: constellations and their hero names, Ptolemy's list, Dürer's printed star maps, Ulugbek's catalogue; making a "Starry Sky" lamp: a dark-paper cylinder pierced and drawn with a chosen constellation, foil stars glued on, a lamp inside — step photos; cosmonaut-artists: Alexei Leonov's space paintings, Vladimir Dzhanibekov, astronaut Alan Bean's moon landscapes; how people of the past imagined the future: the 1911 French postcard series "In the Year 2000" and which inventions came true
2. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Labour Training (Ormanbekova)** — magnets and weightlessness: an experiment on what magnets attract; a round space construction in which a cosmonaut floats, held off the base by repelling magnets

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
