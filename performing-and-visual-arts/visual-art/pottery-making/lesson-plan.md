# Lesson plan — Pottery Making

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Pottery Making
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** pottery whole — what pottery is, its beginnings and heritage, clays and ceramic materials, plaster moulds, and how clay vessels are made and fired.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Let's identify clay; let's create** — pottery as one of humanity's first crafts — ancient clay vessels pictured (fig. 5.1.1) and the grain-store jar (fig. 5.1.2), clay craft running from prehistory to the village potter
2. **clay types and materials** — knowing the earths — Kaolin white clay (the Boralesgamuwa and Meeyankodai deposits), ball clay, fire clay and red clay, then the potter's other materials: feldspar, silica sand, dolomite and talc, each pictured and placed
3. **Let's prepare moulds for making clay vessels** — mould-making whole — the mould materials (wood, clay, metal, cement, fibre, rubber, plaster of Paris) and a two-piece plaster mould built step by step around a bottle, soaped, poured 1½–2 inches thick, opened and cleaned

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
