# Great Painters of Ethiopia

## Do it first

Think of the best drawing or painting you have ever seen in real life. Who made it? If you do not know their name, that is normal — in church painting the painters' names rarely appear. Today you learn names worth knowing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Belachew Yimer

**Belachew Yimer** was born in **Debre Markos, Gojjam**. He is known for **traditional-style paintings**, among them **"The Wedding of the Mouse and the Cat"** and his **farming works** — the Grade 4 book shows his traditionally-styled farming painting. He is the book's first art professional: proof that the traditional style is not anonymous — it has masters, and their names can be learned.

## Sub lesson 2 — Laureate Afewerk Tekle

**Afewerk Tekle** is the honoured **World Laureate Master Artist**, born in **Ankober, Shewa, in 1927 E.C.** — as the Grade 4 book gives his year. His works named in the book: **"From Non-cooperation to Unity"**, **"African Union"** and **"Mother Ethiopia"**, which the book reproduces — a mother figure with child. Ethiopia's most honoured painter carries the country itself as his subject.

## Sub lesson 3 — More master painters (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 7 guide widens the hall. **Afewerk Tekle** again — his stained glass **"Total Liberation of Africa"** in Africa Hall covers 150 m², and in 1983 he became the first African member of the Russian Academy of Arts. **Alle Felege Selam Heruy** (1924–2016) founded the **Addis Ababa School of Art in 1957 — the first formal art school in Ethiopia**. **Gebre Kristos Desta** (1932–1981) **brought abstract expressionism and modern art to Ethiopia**. **Zerihun Yetmgeta** (b.1941) builds painting and printmaking from Orthodox icons and scrolls, with wood relief, parchment, bamboo, wax and gold. **Mezgebu Tesema** is the realist of rural life. **Tadesse Mesfin** (b.1953), professor at the Alle School, paints his market-women series. This part is teacher-guide evidence, labelled so.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ethiopian manuscripts (teacher guide — evidence)

Ethiopia's own **Ge'ez written tradition** is unique in sub-Saharan Africa. Its book is the **codex** (mäshaf, made of branna — parchment): **folded parchment leaves gathered in gatherings, sewn, and covered**. Sizes run from pocket books to volumes over 45 cm tall, and the writing is older than the literature — it begins on epigraphic monuments, carved inscriptions. The painters' art and the scribes' art grew in the same books: a manuscript page can carry Ge'ez script and a painted miniature side by side.
