# Lesson plan — Colouring Inside Shapes

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Colouring Inside Shapes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** colouring carefully so the colour stays inside the outline — first on outline ovals, rectangles and squares, then on a drawn fruit: the orange.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Shapes You Can Draw — the learner draws closed shapes; a closed shape is exactly what careful colouring needs. The Grade 1 book's word is "carefully", twice: draw carefully, then colour in carefully.

## Sub lessons
1. **Colouring the open shapes carefully** — the Grade 1 book's outline ovals, rectangles and squares to colour, with Exercise 6 — draw oval shapes in a colour you choose, carefully, then colour them in carefully.
2. **Drawing and colouring fruit: the orange** — the Grade 2 book's orange — drawing an orange's shape and colouring it, with its activity: colour the orange pictures in your exercise book.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Shapes You Can Draw — colour stays inside a shape that closes. Recommended, never forced.
- Next door: the Colours lessons, where colour itself becomes the subject.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Colour outline ovals, rectangles and squares so the colour stays inside the lines.
2. Draw oval shapes in a colour you choose and colour them in carefully, as Exercise 6 asks.
3. Draw an orange's round shape and colour it, as the Grade 2 activity asks.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and colours — crayons or coloured pencils. One outline shape page to start.

## If the learner is stuck
- Colour crosses the border → colour the edge band first with short strokes; the band guards the rest. Smaller strokes near the edge, bigger ones in the middle.
- White gaps everywhere → strokes must overlap a little, like roof tiles; go over the shape a second time in the same direction.
- Scribbly filling → this is the one place scribble does NOT belong: strokes one way only, back and forth like mowing grass.
