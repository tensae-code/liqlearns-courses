# Colouring Inside Shapes


## Do it first

Here is an outline oval, empty inside. Colour it in — as carefully as you can, before anyone shows you how.

Finished? Look at the edge. Did the colour stay home, inside the line — or did it wander out?

This lesson is the careful art: colour that fills a shape fully and stops at its border.

## Sub lesson 1 — Colouring the open shapes carefully

The book gives you shapes that are only outlines — **ovals, rectangles and squares** — waiting for colour.

[Picture 9154 — outline shapes waiting for colour]

The careful way: work **along the edge first**, slowly, then fill the middle with strokes that go **one way**, back and forth, until no white gaps are left. Careful does not mean slow forever — it means the colour obeys the border.

Watch it happen:

[GIF 9184 — colouring staying inside an outline, stroke by stroke]

Then Exercise 6, the book's own task, with its word said twice: **draw** different oval shapes in a colour you choose, **carefully** — then **colour them in, carefully**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Drawing and colouring fruit: the orange

Now the shape is a fruit. The Grade 2 book draws **an orange** — a round shape, its leaf on top — and colours it in its own colour.

[Picture 9160 — drawing and colouring an orange]

Draw the round shape first. Then colour it the careful way: edge first, strokes one way, full and even — orange all over, the way the fruit itself is orange all over.

The book's activity sends you to your exercise book: **colour the orange pictures** there. Every fruit you colour is the same careful skill, wearing a new shape.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Careful colouring means the colour stays inside the outline. Edge first, then fill — strokes one way, no white gaps." Show them your fullest, tidiest shape.

## Do It Yourself

1. Colour one outline rectangle and one outline square — edge first, no gaps, nothing outside.
2. Draw three oval shapes in colours you choose, carefully — then colour each one in, carefully (Exercise 6).
3. Draw an orange and colour it evenly; draw a second fruit shape of your own and colour that too.
