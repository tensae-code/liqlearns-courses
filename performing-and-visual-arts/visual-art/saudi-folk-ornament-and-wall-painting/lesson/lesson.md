## Sub lesson 1 — Al-Qatt Al-Asiri wall painting

In the Asir region in the south of the Kingdom, the folk artist decorated house walls, doors and windows with Al-Qatt Al-Asiri — line-shapes taken from the nature around him: mountains, sun, moon, crescent, stars, clouds, rain, lightning and grape clusters. He decorated vessels, embroidered clothes and engraved jewellery the same way. Al-Qatt Al-Asiri is registered as Saudi intangible cultural heritage with UNESCO.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saudi folk ornament: engraving, doors and walls

The Kingdom's folk ornament builds by repeating the circle, triangle, square and rhombus. Engraving (النقش), above all gypsum engraving, marks the doors and the inner and outer walls of the great houses — the Emirate Palace in Najran among them — in beautiful colours: blue, green, violet, red and yellow. Craftsmen lean on geometric and plant ornament and turn wooden doors into masterpieces.

## Sub lesson 3 — Islamic ornament and its uses

Islamic ornament — geometric, plant and calligraphic — ornaments mosques, doors, windows, furniture, Qur'an copies and artefacts of gypsum, ceramic, textile, metal and marble, each from simple geometric shapes. The folk craftsman cared for ornament on buildings and on pottery, palm-frond products, carpets, vessels, clothes and leathers alike, relying on repetition — among folk art's most important features.
