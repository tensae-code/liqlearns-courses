# Clay Toys of Belarus

## Do it first

Before you read: say or sketch what you already know about this lesson's subject — Belarus's own clay-toy tradition — the modelled clay toys (tsatski) of Nemanitsa, Zembin and Logoisk, and the masters a Belarusian child models after. Start there; the lesson builds on it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The clay toys of Belarus

Belarusian children learn modelling by looking at their own country's clay toys — the **tsatski** made by folk masters:

- **Nemanitsa** — the clay toys of **Rzhavutski** (1985).
- **Zembin** — the clay toys of **Shmatko** (1990).
- **Logoisk** — **Strelchanka's "Goat and Billy-Goat"** (2010).

These are not factory toys: each one is modelled by hand from clay, in the place where its maker lives, and each place's toys carry that place's hand. The Grade 1 book sets them in the clay chapter — "Secrets of a piece of clay" — right where the child is learning to build animals and figures from a rolled clay log, so the masters' toys are the models the child works toward.
