# Shapes, Forms and Patterns in Colour

## Do it first

Draw a circle, a square and a triangle. Colour each a different colour. Now close the book: which colour was the circle? Colour has made the shape unforgettable — that is this lesson's first secret.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shapes told by colour

The Grade 3 shapes and forms return, now **worked in drawing and colour**. A **shape** (ዝርግ ቅርፅ) is what joined lines make — a thing with width, the **closed outline** of a thing. The book's circles are **filled with colour — shapes can be told by colour**. **Natural shapes** are not uniform: read them from the **shadows and silhouettes** of trees, animals and birds, and from plants. **Geometric shapes** are measurable by straight, uniform lines: triangle, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram — and every household object carries its own shape, waiting to be read.

## Sub lesson 2 — Forms: geometric, natural, insect and man-made

A **form** is a thing's whole appearance — **width plus volume and depth**, in three dimensions: **sphere, cylinder, cone, cube**. Nature has forms too: the **plant form**, the **insect form**. **Man-made forms** have length, width and height of their own. The activity joins the two sub lessons: draw a favourite natural or man-made shape, and **show household utensils' shapes in different colour grades** — colour climbing light to dark across one shape.

## Sub lesson 3 — Patterns: shapes and colours in order

Now shapes go to work in **order**. The book draws shapes that belong in empty spaces **in sequence** — find the missing one. Arranging flat shapes in order **expresses feeling and makes ornament**: a **two-colour repeat pattern**, a pattern from **repeated shapes**, beauty made by joining papers and by joining pebbles. The same ordered shapes teach number: a **divided circle teaches fractions** — into how many parts is the circle divided, and what fraction is the blue part? And instruments arranged in order make patterns that teach counting: kebero in a row, masenqo in a row, and the total of both.

## Sub lesson 4 — Stories and letters through pictures

Pictures can carry **language** itself. The book shows a **pictorial drama**, a figure showing **things' positions**, and **English and Amharic letter-writing forms** side by side. Then a **pictorial composition** — a story told as an essay in pictures. The activity: **state the message of the pictures**, then **express it in drama and drawing**. A story that can be read in pictures can be told three ways: seen, acted and drawn.

## Sub lesson 5 — Perceiving and practising in our locality (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 2 teacher guide closes its perception chapter with **visual topics and the practices carried on in the locality** — perceiving, then **practising where you live**. Its picture is children practising an art activity **outdoors in their locality**. The contents type of the scan is degraded at these two entries, so this part is placed at **section level only** — its teaching is its title and its picture: perception is not finished in the classroom; it is practised on your own street, with your own place as the picture.

## Say it to your companion

Build a secret pattern of four shapes and colours. Your companion must continue it twice and say its rule. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw household utensils' shapes in different colour grades.
2. Make a two-colour repeat pattern from pebbles or paper joins.
3. Divide a circle into equal parts, colour one part, and say its fraction.
