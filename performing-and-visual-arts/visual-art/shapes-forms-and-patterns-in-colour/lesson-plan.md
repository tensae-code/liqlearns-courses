# Lesson plan — Shapes, Forms and Patterns in Colour

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Shapes, Forms and Patterns in Colour
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** shapes and forms worked in colour — telling shapes by their colour, building patterns of shapes and colours in order, learning fractions and stories through pictures, and practising perception in your own locality.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Shapes You Can Draw and Colouring Inside Shapes — the learner can already draw shapes and forms; this lesson works them in colour and in ordered patterns, the Grade 4 way.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Shapes told by colour** — shape is the closed outline made by joined lines; natural shapes from silhouettes; geometric shapes measured by straight lines (Grade 4, printed pp. 10–13).
2. **Forms: geometric, natural, insect and man-made** — form is width plus volume and depth — sphere, cylinder, cone, cube; plant and insect forms; man-made forms with their own length, width and height (Grade 4, printed pp. 14–15).
3. **Patterns: shapes and colours in order** — sequences with gaps to fill; two-colour repeats and repeated shapes; fractions through a divided circle (Grade 4, printed pp. 81–85).
4. **Stories and letters through pictures** — language through art — a pictorial drama, things' positions in figures, letter-writing forms, and a pictorial composition whose message is stated, then expressed in drama and drawing (Grade 4, printed pp. 88–89).
5. **Perceiving and practising in our locality (teacher guide — evidence)** — visual perception and local practice exercises closing the perception chapter, cited at section level from the scanned Grade 2 guide (§§1.4–1.5, printed pp. 12–22).

6. **Corridors and colour composition (Grade 4)** — the veranda plate and geometric forms — how placed corridors compose a coloured picture.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Shapes You Can Draw and Colouring Inside Shapes. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell shapes apart by colour and name natural and geometric shapes.
2. Tell a shape from a form, and name the solid forms: sphere, cylinder, cone, cube, pyramid.
3. Build and continue a pattern of shapes and colours in order.
4. Use a divided circle to show a fraction, and read a story told in pictures.

## What you need
Paper, coloured pencils or paints, and small flat things — pebbles, bottle caps, leaves — for building patterns.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Draw a circle, a square and a triangle. Colour each a different colour. Now close the book: which colour was the circle? Colour has made the shape unforgettable — that is this lesson's first secret. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses the pattern → say it aloud in words (shape + colour) as you build; the voice keeps the order.
- Mixes shape and form again → shapes are flat (length and width); forms stand up (length, width and height).
