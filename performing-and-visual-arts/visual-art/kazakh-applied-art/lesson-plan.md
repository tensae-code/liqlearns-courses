# Lesson plan — Kazakh Applied Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Kazakh Applied Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** kazakh Applied Art — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 1 Labour Guide** — Kazakh decorative-applied art: paper ornament clothing, and the "Zhuldyz (Star)" geometric ornament made twice, in study and in making
2. **Kazakhstan Grade 1 Labour Guide RU** — the applied arts named as a kind, with Kazakh work as the example
3. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Labour Guide RU** — national dishes; national jewellery (two lessons): the Kazakh tableware and silverwork made
4. **Kazakhstan Grade 2 Raupova Art Labour, Term 3 lessons** — national costume and the crafts of the people
5. **Kazakhstan Grade 3 Muratkyzy Labour** — the Kazakh vessels (torsyk, tostagan and their kin) and their ornament patterns drawn; Kazakh silver jewellery (earrings, pendants, rings) and the zerger's art, with master jeweller Darkembay Shokparuly; a button necklace and a bracelet made
6. **Kazakhstan Grade 3 Tolebiev Visual Art** — Kazakhstan", Lesson 7 "Decorative-applied art" — the koloner crafts: felt, leather, wood, bone, metal work of the Kazakh masters (Musrepov's painting of a craftswoman; a camel pencil-holder made); kiiz felt, tekemet, syrmak and tuskiiz: how each is made and what its colours mean (Bekeev's and Turakbaeva's felts)
7. **Kazakhstan Grade 3 Zhanaev Visual Art** — what applied art is; a "Kazakh eli" collage built from photographs and paper; tusti kilem and tysyr kilem, beskeste kilem embroidery; a carpet pattern drawn and a model carpet made; the zerger's art: Darkembay Shokparuly's jewellery drawn; Berik Alibay and Taimas Mukazhan named; sholpy hair ornaments
8. **Kazakhstan Grade 3 Adamkulov Labour** — a wool hat knitted; a tekemet felted (kiiz basu, the full wet-felting sequence); a kamshy whip plaited from leather; an alasha woven from thread on a loom; a kebezhe chest, a wooden bed (agash tosek), an arba cart and a kubi churn: what each was for and how it was ornamented, each rebuilt as a model
9. **Kazakhstan Grade 3 Syzdykov Labour** — tale characters modelled (Karashash sulu, Ailaker bai, Kambar ata, Kobylandy batyr; Agyimsaly Duelkhanov's tale paintings); national vessels (astau, shara, tostagan, kese, ozhau) and the Taikazan; a papier-mâché kese made and ornamented
10. **Kazakhstan Grade 3 Rabilova Visual Art** — the wooden kumys vessels (torsyk, ozhau, shara) and their making; a kumys set modelled; the zerger dynasty of Berik Alibay; a bracelet drawn with ornament elements; stylisation and the doodling technique (Ualikhanov's drawings)
11. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Mamyrova Visual Art** — the zerger's craft; a bracelet drawn with a light-spot (жарық нүктесі) to give it volume; kiiz basu: felt-making; tekemet and syrmak; wet felting and dry felting worked step by step; a picture "painted" with wool fibres
12. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Muratkyzy Labour** — models of the Kazakh swing and the cradle built from sticks, fabric and cardboard; a four-strand round whip braided; asyk figures made for the national game; a torsyk sewn from felt
13. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Ormanbekova Labour** — a sandyk chest modelled from plasticine with a relief lid; the kamizol's parts and a kamizol made to a drawn pattern; the Golden Man of Issyk and the zerger's chasing (shekimeleu) technique; an "Altyn adam" decoration embossed on foil
14. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Tulebiev Visual Art RU** — the kinds of Kazakh applied art (felt-making, weaving, embroidery, wood and bone work); the kalkan shield of the epos; a kalkan made from felt with a tekemet pattern; patchwork (kurak) explained; a korpe sewn from pieces; zoomorphic ornament motifs charted (ram's horn, bird's wing, camel's foot); a syrmak bag decorated; national dishes of the peoples (baursaki, echpochmak, karavai) modelled from salt dough
15. **Kazakhstan Grade 5 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — what it teaches: Kazakh household treasures as still-life subjects — torsyk flask, silver jewellery, saukele and kalpak headdresses, embroidered bags — their form, colour, texture and purpose
16. **Kazakhstan Grade 5 Artistic Labour (boys, Chukalin)** — what it teaches: Kazakh household treasures as still-life subjects — torsyk flask, silver jewellery, saukele and kalpak headdresses, embroidered bags — their form, colour, texture and purpose; what it teaches: applied art technique by material — metal is cast, forged, engraved and chased; wood is carved, turned and fret-sawn; clay is modelled; leather is burnt, embossed and plaited
17. **Kazakhstan Grade 6 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — what it teaches: yarn bombing — «knitted graffiti», street art grown from the oldest needlecraft: bright knitted covers dress benches, buses, bridges, cars and swings; pupils try it on their own yard, balcony or a room's interior and photograph the result; what it teaches: the homemade rag doll of cloth and fur scraps was traditionally faceless — by folk belief a doll with a face gained a soul and could harm a child, so the faceless doll was at once toy and amulet (оберег); by how a doll was dressed, people judged its maker's taste and mastery
18. **Kazakhstan Grade 7 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — what it teaches: the painted pottery of ancient Kazakhstan — nomads' wooden vessels painted with Kazakh ornament motifs gathered from the steppe's interweaving cultures; colour order is meaning: blue for the sky, red for sun and fire, yellow for reason and longing, black for the earth, green for youth and spring; the first ornamented clay vessels of the 5th millennium BC in the southern cities, Neolithic pottery mostly black-painted so the ornament's contours stand clear, patterns changing over time — stamped ornaments («grille in a circle», «spoked wheel») beside modelled ones; 14th–15th-century ceramics with smooth surfaces, many-coloured ornament and opposite-colour or geometric ground lines
19. **Kazakhstan Grade 8 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — what it teaches: Kazakh decorative-applied art samples count as folk-creativity products; the kinds in the scheme — зергерлік (jewellery), былғары өңдеу (leather processing), көркем ою (artistic carving), ұсталық кәсіп (blacksmithing), кестелеу (embroidery), тоқымашылық (weaving), кілем тоқу (carpet weaving), өру (plaiting); the зергер worked metal and made jewellery, the wood master worked wood; women occupied themselves with weaving, sewing, embroidery and ши plaiting; such works were mostly done in groups — uniting, folk masters made folk creativity together, taught their art and passed it from generation to generation
20. **Kazakhstan Grade 8 Artistic Labour (boys, Chukalin)** — what it teaches: the people's craft (халықтың қолөнері) is an important part of the Kazakhs' material and spiritual heritage; the Kazakhs' household items and crafts developed in accordance with the nomadic economy and were convenient for life; preparing the yurt's equipment, combing wool, making felt, sewing, embroidery, weaving, carpet weaving, rope twisting, ши weaving and other works were occupied by women; wood carving, leather processing, bone and stone carving, blacksmithing and jewellery works were characteristic of men; the зергер (jeweller) processed metal and prepared decoration items, the wood master (ағаш ұстасы) made varied things from wood; masters taught their children their craft's ins and outs and delivered it from generation to generation; the products characteristic of Kazakh decorative-applied art are distinguished by deep creative content and national character; the кісе белбеу (Kazakh national hunter's belt), көнек and саба leather vessels are pictured
21. **Kazakhstan Grade 9 NIS Art (Akhmetkalieva)** — what it teaches: applied art and craft (қолданбалы өнер мен қолөнер) is an inseparable part of any country's culture; some craft kinds have come down from father to son as a family profession, and some became a whole region's, district's or country's traditional cultural heritage; craft (қолөнер) is the art of making various items by hand with simple tools; through the craftsman's skill, items of very high quality and beauty come into the world; craft's many kinds have been known since old times: blacksmithing (темір ұсталығы), woodwork (ағаш ұсталығы), shoemaking (етікшілік), jewellery (зергерлік), tailoring (тігіншілік), weaving (тоқымашылық), leather processing (тері өңдеу), leather tanning (былғары илеу), strap processing (қайыс өңдеу) and other craft kinds were perfected together with humanity's centuries-long life experience; artistic craft (көркем қолөнер) develops on the basis of folk tradition, and its own national and cultural peculiarities are preserved in it and it masters other civilisations' achievements mixed in; artistic craft is also called decorative-applied art (декоративті қолданбалы өнер), because it develops together with each people's culture and aesthetic taste; the Uzbek khan-atlas (хан-атлас) silk fabric and the Slavic rushnik (рушник) — an embroidered towel counted a historical-symbolic heritage with life-and-custom patterns stitched on cloth — are pictured; Zeinelkhan Mukhamedzhan, the Kazakh artist who revived the кесте (embroidery) art, and his wife Gulzhai Gusman's «Көркем бізкесте» work are introduced; what it teaches: Kazakh craft divides into two by men's and women's work: smithing (ұсталық), jewellery (зергерлік), leather tanning (былғары илеу), leather processing (тері өңдеу), bone carving (сүйек жону), stone carving (тас қашау) are men's work, and sewing (тігін тігу) and weaving (тоқыма тоқу) are women's work; the yurt's frame (кереге, уық, есік, шаңырақ) and everyday-needed dishes and items were made by wood masters (ағаш ұсталары); the tools and equipment needed for farming and animal husbandry — horse gear (ат әбзелдері), the plough (соқа), the sickle (орақ), knives (пышақтар) — came from blacksmiths' (темір ұсталарының) hands; Kazakh зергерлік (jewellery) is also counted a branch of fine art (көркем өнер): zergers mostly used silver — the saying fixed in Kazakh oral literature «алтынмен аптап, күміспен күптеп» (gilded with gold, silvered with silver) is said in the zerger's honour; another kind of Kazakh craft is сүйекшілік (bone work) — masters who pattern household items' outside with bone and turn them into patterned chests (сандық) are called сүйек жону шебері (bone-carving master); leather-processing masters were also highly valued among craftsmen: they tanned cattle and horse leather, processed it, sewed winter clothes from the ready leather, prepared horse gear's leather items, made саба and мес vessels for pouring drinks and kymys; another valued kind of home craft is felting (киіз басу), preparing бау and басқұр, and weaving ши and кесте; the pictured items — астау, басқұр, торсық, кілемше, ши, ер-тоқым, киіз басу; today's masters: Serikkazy Nuraliev, who revived the былғары кілем (leather carpet) making tradition known more in the VIII–IX centuries; Ergali Satkenov, a universal master whose main work is wood carving and making relief sculptures and national-patterned decorations from bone; Marat Sarsenbaev, a clay master who teaches children to make clay items
