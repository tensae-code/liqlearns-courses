## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Vietnam Grade 3 Mĩ thuật 3** — The dress of Vietnam's Mông, Ê-đê and Chăm peoples — pleated skirts, shirts, sashes and hats whose woven patterns stylise flowers, leaves and animals into simple lines and shapes; each people's costume carries its own identity
2. **Vietnam Grade 8 Mĩ thuật 8** — Patterns formed by stylising symbolic animals — the embroidery on the Thái people's skirts stylised from the buffalo, and the embroidery on the Xơ Đăng people's cloth stylised from the monkey; and patterns stylised from symbolic geometry — the Pu Péo people's cloth patterns made of geometric figures, the Lô Lô Hoa people's cloth patterns mostly geometric shapes and simple lines, and the Chăm people's cloth patterns made from straight lines arranged by the principle of repetition; the traditional patterns on the costumes of Vietnam's ethnic minorities use lines and combinations of geometric shapes to create lively decorative motifs, created by the principles of balance and rhythm, making tendencies of movement in each specific product
3. **Vietnam Grade 11 Mĩ thuật 11 — Thiết kế thời trang** — The process of creating patterns on the traditional outfit of the H'Mông ethnic compatriots is recognised as a special folk art form with the stages: weaving fabric, drawing patterns in beeswax (vẽ hoa văn bằng sáp ong), dyeing and embroidering; this process is done meticulously by the manual method, and in that the technique of beeswax drawing and indigo dyeing (nhuộm chàm) on fabric is an extremely unique stage carrying a strong own cultural identity — Step 1: to perform, the H'Mông women must prepare the beeswax by cooking it melting on a small fire stove and keeping it hot continuously throughout the drawing process until it is complete; Step 2: to create the patterns, the H'Mông women use a special pen type with the pen nib made of copper and the pen handle made of bamboo, to dip the hot melting beeswax and draw the patterns on linen fabric (vải lanh); Step 3: when the drawing process is complete, the fabric sheet is taken to be dyed indigo many times until the desired colour is obtained; Step 4: after that, to melt the beeswax away, the fabric is dipped into hot water then taken to be dried in the sun, revealing the white patterns on the indigo background
