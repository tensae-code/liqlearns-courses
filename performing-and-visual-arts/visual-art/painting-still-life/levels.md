# Painting Still Life — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number, as before. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — What painting is — and what a still life is
Pass mark: define painting in both senses (the act of applying colour properly, and the finished work) and define still-life painting (a composed arrangement of simple objects around us, studied and painted).

## Sub lesson 2 — Colour comes from light — the spectrum
Pass mark: name the seven spectrum colours in order; explain that a ray of light passing broken through a triangular prism shows them; say that colour's source is natural or artificial light, and that without light all is dark.

## Sub lesson 3 — Doing the still-life painting — tools and four stages
Pass mark: list the tools (thick paper, watercolour, palette, clean water, cloth, pencil, eraser, sharpener, support board, the model arrangement) and work the stages in order: prepare/arrange the model, draw it lightly, paint gradually starting from the lightest colour and waiting for drying, finish.

## Sub lesson 4 — Paint the pictured still lifes — the practice set
Pass mark: paint an arranged group of objects in colour, working the four stages without being led; compare the painting with the model and say what the next painting will do better.

## Sub lesson 5 — Teacher-guide evidence — drawing first, and the Hawassa watercolour
Pass mark: say, as teacher-guide evidence, that drawing is the starting point of most artwork; name painting as pigment + medium and some of the equipment; tell how the guide's worked example — a Lake Hawassa watercolour by Solomon Wagaye — was painted step by step in watercolour method.
