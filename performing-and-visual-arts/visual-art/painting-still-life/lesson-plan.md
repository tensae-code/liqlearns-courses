# Lesson plan — Painting Still Life

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Painting Still Life
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** still-life painting: arranging simple objects around us, drawing them lightly, and painting them in colour — and where the colours themselves come from: light, broken into the spectrum.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Drawing: The Foundation Skill and the colour work of the earlier Visual Art lessons (primaries, mixing, the colour wheel). The learner arrives able to draw and to mix colour; this lesson puts the two together on one arranged group of objects.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **What painting is — and what a still life is** — painting as act and as work; the still life: simple objects, composed and studied.
2. **Colour comes from light — the spectrum** — the seven spectrum colours; the prism; why light is colour's source.
3. **Doing the still-life painting — tools and four stages** — the tools; arrange, draw lightly, paint from the lightest colour, finish.
4. **Paint the pictured still lifes — the practice set** — Exercise 2.4: three arrangements, ሀ, ለ and ሐ, painted in colour.
5. **Teacher-guide evidence — drawing first, and the Hawassa watercolour** — labelled evidence: drawing as the starting point of most artwork; painting media and equipment; the Lake Hawassa worked example.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what painting is — both the act and the work — and what a still life is.
2. Name the seven spectrum colours in order and say how a prism shows them.
3. List the still-life tools and work the four stages: arrange, draw, paint, finish.
4. Paint an arranged group of objects in colour, lightest colour first.
5. Say, from the teacher-guide evidence, why drawing comes before painting, and how the Hawassa watercolour was worked.

## What you need
Thick drawing paper, watercolour, a palette, clean water, a piece of cloth, pencil, eraser, sharpener, a support board — and simple objects to arrange as the model (a pot, a cup, fruit).

## If the learner is stuck
- The drawing fights the painting → draw lighter. The pencil drawing is only a map; the paint is the journey.
- Colours turn muddy → start from the lightest colour, and wait for each part to dry before the next touches it.
- The arrangement looks dull → move the objects, not the paper: a composed arrangement is half the painting.
