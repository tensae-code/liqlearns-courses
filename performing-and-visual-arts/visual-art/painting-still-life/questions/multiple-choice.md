# Painting Still Life — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The word "painting" names —
a) only the finished work  b) both the act of applying colour and the finished work  c) only the brushes and palette
**Answer: b) both the act of applying colour and the finished work** — painting names the ACT and the RESULT.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A still life is —
a) a painting of a composed arrangement of simple objects around us  b) a photograph of a market  c) a portrait of a person standing still
**Answer: a) a painting of a composed arrangement of simple objects around us** — the objects are arranged, composed, studied and painted.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The spectrum colours, in order, begin —
a) violet, indigo, blue…  b) red, orange, yellow…  c) green, blue, red…
**Answer: b) red, orange, yellow…** — red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A ray of light passing broken through a triangular prism shows —
a) only white light  b) the blended spectrum colours  c) black and white only
**Answer: b) the blended spectrum colours** — the prism breaks the light and the colours show.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The first stage of the still-life painting is —
a) paint the lightest colour  b) prepare/arrange the still-life model  c) sign the finished work
**Answer: b) prepare/arrange the still-life model** — stage ሀ: prepare the model arrangement.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** When painting begins, you start —
a) from the darkest colour  b) from any colour  c) from the lightest colour
**Answer: c) from the lightest colour** — start from the lightest colour, waiting for drying as you go.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Exercise 2.4 asks you to paint —
a) one arrangement, three times in pencil  b) the three pictured still-life arrangements in colour  c) the spectrum through a prism
**Answer: b) the three pictured still-life arrangements in colour** — the three arrangements ሀ, ለ, ሐ, in colour.

**8. (Sub lesson 5)** According to the teacher guide (evidence), drawing is —
a) only for beginners  b) the starting point of most artwork  c) separate from painting
**Answer: b) the starting point of most artwork** — drawing records ideas and plans finished works, for painters and sculptors alike.

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** The guide's worked watercolour example shows —
a) a mountain market  b) Lake Hawassa, by Solomon Wagaye  c) a portrait of a king
**Answer: b) Lake Hawassa, by Solomon Wagaye** — the Lake Hawassa watercolour, taught step by step.
