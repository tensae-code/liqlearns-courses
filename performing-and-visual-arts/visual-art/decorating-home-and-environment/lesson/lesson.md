# Decorating Home and Environment

## Do it first

Look at your home's entrance. Then look at the path to the kitchen. Are they plain — or has someone's hand beautified them? In Ethiopia, beautifying the home is old, skilled work. This lesson learns it the book's way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Decorating from ancient times to today

Decorating homes and environments — with **varied designs from natural and human-made inputs** — is practised **from ancient times**, in the world and in Ethiopia. Even the **cave-dwellers** beautified their caves' insides and surroundings, with **pictures, writings and utensils** placed, pasted and hung.

The local way is alive today: when **festivals** come, or the **rains** arrive, people beautify especially the **inside and outside of the home**, using **varied soils, cattle products and ash** as their "paint". **Outside**, the paths — from the main gate or house door to the **kitchen**, the **toilet**, the **garden** and the **outer gate** — are beautified with **stones, soil, sand and gravel** of varied content and size.

And today the work is also a profession: done with **scientific knowledge and skill**, helped by the **architecture and gardening professions** — interior and exterior decoration and gardening **employ wide manpower and earn high income**. The ancient hand-work and the modern profession are one practice, grown.

## Sub lesson 2 — Decorating with ceramics and pottery — benefits and the task

One material family leads this work: **ceramics and pottery** — the pots and clay works of the crafts lessons, turned to beautifying. The book's figure shows a home interior beautified with **varied ceramic and design products**. Why do it? The book gives **three benefits**.

**First**, making our home and environment **comfortable and pleasant for our living** — decoration is not vanity; it is the home made kind to live in. **Second**, creating a **better idea and motivation for the next generation** — children who grow among beautified things learn to beautify. **Third**, **wide job opportunity** for the professionals in the field — the decorator's skill feeds the decorator.

The book's task closes the lesson: **choose a site at home or school and beautify it — in small groups and groups — using easily available local inputs, applying the theory learned in class.** Theory, then a real site, then the work. Do it.

## Say it to your companion

Walk a short path at home or school with your companion — gate to door, door to kitchen. Each of you names three beautifyings you can see, and one place that asks for beautifying. Agree on the one place you would start.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Beautify a path** — Choose one path — from the gate or the house door toward the kitchen or the garden. Edge it with stones, soil, sand or gravel of varied content and size, in the book's way. Finish the whole path, however short.
2. **A ceramic corner** — Take the pots and ceramic pieces your home already has. Arrange them to beautify one corner or shelf: comfortable to live with, pleasant to look at. Change one thing at a time until the corner rests.
3. **The group task** — The book's own task: in small groups, choose a site at home or school and beautify it using easily available local inputs, applying the theory learned in class. Plan it, do it, and walk a visitor through it.
