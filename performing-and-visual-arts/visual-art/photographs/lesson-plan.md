# Lesson plan — Photographs

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Photographs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a photograph is — an image obtained using a photo camera — and how to look at photographs and tell what you understood, through Ethiopia's famous places, a famous athlete, a wild animal, work and seasons, and historic places.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Looking at Pictures (First Steps); photographs are the pictures a camera makes, and this lesson is the learner's first careful walk through real ones. It feeds Photography, next door, where the learner takes photographs themselves.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Looking at photographs** — the definition, and the look-and-tell way of learning.
2. **Famous places, people and animals** — Tis Abay Falls, Abebe Bikila, the Abbay Dam, the walia ibex.
3. **Work, seasons, land and sky** — a market, harvest, a landscape (መልክዓምድር), an aeroplane.
4. **Historic places in photographs** — Bete Giyorgis, Axum, Fasilides' castle, the Sof Omar cave — and saying which pleased you.
5. **Appreciating photographs** — a full photograph set, appreciated the honest way: what you understood, what you feel.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Looking at Pictures (First Steps). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a photograph and say how it differs from a drawing.
2. Name the famous place, person, building and animal of the first sets, as the photographs show them.
3. Tell what the work, season, land and sky photographs show, and where the camera stood.
4. Name the historic places in photographs and say which photograph pleased them, and why.
5. Appreciate a photograph aloud: what they understood, what they feel.

## What you need
One real photograph found in the learner's area (a print, a book photograph, or a phone photograph).

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks at one photograph for a slow count of twenty and tells what is in it (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition; the photograph sets in the books' order; historic places; appreciating (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Describes a photograph with things not in it → return to the rule: only what the camera caught. Ask "where do you see that in the photograph?"
- Cannot tell the historic places apart → church cut into rock = Bete Giyorgis; tall carved stone among ruins = Axum; castle towers = Fasilides at Gondar.
- Says nothing about a photograph → give the two sentence-starters: "I see…" and "It makes me feel…".
