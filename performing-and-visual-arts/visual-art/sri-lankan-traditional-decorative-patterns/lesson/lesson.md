# Sri Lankan Traditional Decorative Patterns

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about sri lankan traditional decorative patterns. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Let's study traditional decorative designs

Sri Lanka's traditional motif stock studied as designs — lotus flower, swan, lion and the named classical patterns, their structure analysed for reuse

## Sub lesson 2 — Let's create a decorative design for a purpose (wrapping/covering)

Turning a traditional pattern into a working design for a real use — a wrapping/covering pattern planned with natural and created motifs

## Sub lesson 3 — Let's create new designs from various patterns

Inventing new decorative work from studied patterns — pattern units combined and varied into original designs, with the annex's traditional mask designs as source material; follow-ons: drawing a Ves-mask-style design and a sanniya design

## Sub lesson 4 — Let's study the traditional decorative designs with female figures (Panchanaari)

The classical figure motifs — the Panchanaari (five-women) and Sapthanaari (seven-women) thorana paintings and the Panchanaari drawing, their interlaced-figure construction studied line by line

## Sub lesson 5 — Let's study traditional decorative designs (Narilatha and the animal motifs)

The motif bestiary — the Narilatha flower design, the Vahana Kuttam (an elephant built from human figures), Kirthi Mukha (the glory-face), the Makara (the composite water beast) and the Yaali, each drawn and dissected in the annexes
