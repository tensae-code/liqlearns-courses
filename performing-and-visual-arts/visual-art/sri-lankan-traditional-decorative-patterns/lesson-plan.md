# Lesson plan — Sri Lankan Traditional Decorative Patterns

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Sri Lankan Traditional Decorative Patterns
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sri Lanka's traditional decorative designs — its named motifs, female-figure (Panchanaari) and animal patterns, studied and created for a purpose.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Let's study traditional decorative designs** — Sri Lanka's traditional motif stock studied as designs — lotus flower, swan, lion and the named classical patterns, their structure analysed for reuse
2. **Let's create a decorative design for a purpose (wrapping/covering)** — turning a traditional pattern into a working design for a real use — a wrapping/covering pattern planned with natural and created motifs
3. **Let's create new designs from various patterns** — inventing new decorative work from studied patterns — pattern units combined and varied into original designs, with the annex's traditional mask designs as source material; follow-ons: drawing a Ves-mask-style design and a sanniya design
4. **Let's study the traditional decorative designs with female figures (Panchanaari)** — the classical figure motifs — the Panchanaari (five-women) and Sapthanaari (seven-women) thorana paintings and the Panchanaari drawing, their interlaced-figure construction studied line by line
5. **Let's study traditional decorative designs (Narilatha and the animal motifs)** — the motif bestiary — the Narilatha flower design, the Vahana Kuttam (an elephant built from human figures), Kirthi Mukha (the glory-face), the Makara (the composite water beast) and the Yaali, each drawn and dissected in the annexes
