# Making with Clay and Paper

## Do it first

Take a lump of soft earth or clay, or picture one in your hands. Squeeze it. Roll it. It changes shape and keeps the new shape — that is why people have made things from clay for thousands of years. In this lesson you model with clay, finish it with fire and colour, and make pictures from paper too.

## Sub lesson 1 — Clay modelling

Modelling means creating pictures and things by rolling and shaping clay and similar stuff, by hand and with tools — rolling, flattening, cutting. Modelling is forgiving: a work can be undone and remade with no material loss. Clay soil serves best.

The steps: **plan first** — design the thing you want to make on paper. **Knead the clay clean on a clean surface until it softens.** Then **roll the clay and, adding and taking away, work until the wanted shape is obtained.** Ready clay does not stick to your hands when you work it. Tools called shape-extractors (ቅርፅ ማውጫ) — made easily from local wood, plastic, bone or metal — help you take clay away and shape different forms.

You can make people, animals, birds, letters, candle and flower holders, and utensils. For a figure that must stand: plan and draw it, build a thin-wire and small-stick frame (an armature), dress the frame with clay by hand, cover the finished piece with plastic so it does not crack, and let it dry. Make the shape of an animal you enjoy!

## Sub lesson 2 — Drying, firing and decorating clay

Clay soil is special: it kneads (ይቦካ) and does not crack quickly in wind, so it is convenient for making forms. Home utensils — ጀበና (jebena), ድስት (dist), insira, gan, mitad and the like — are made of clay soil and fired in fire so they last long and stay hard.

The finishing chain: keep the wet finished work in a **windless, shaded place** to dry. When dry, **smooth and line it**. **Fire it** by packing dung and chaff inside and around it. Then **decorate**: brush on plant colours with a brush made from horsehair or qacha fibre; warmed plant gums (grar, zgba) brushed on can make it shine.

## Sub lesson 3 — Paper pictures: cut, fold and paste

Paper makes pictures too. Cut papers, join them and paste them to draw a picture, following a model. Or fold and stick papers to create a picture — a flat sheet becomes a house, a flower, an animal, by folding, cutting and pasting. Look at each piece before you paste: its shape and colour are part of your picture.

## Sub lesson 4 — Houses and pots of our area

Architecture (ኪነ ህንፃ) is house building — an art you can live inside. Look at the houses of your own area: their walls, roofs and shapes. Design the house buildings of your area and show your design. Then the potter's side: knead the soils of your area and make a pottery work you know, in imitation — a pot like the pots your home uses.

## Sub lesson 5 — A New Year gift card

A card you make yourself carries your own hand in it. Make a New Year gift card by colour-painting — paint it with bright colours, the way the season feels — and show it. A painted card is a small gift of art: someone keeps it because your hands made it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Shaping letters from materials

Letters are shapes, and shapes can be made from anything. Shape the Amharic letters from different materials — clay, sticks, stones, string, cut paper — and show them. A letter you build with your hands is a letter you will never forget how to write.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the clay chain in order — plan, knead, shape, dry, fire, decorate — and say which step they must never skip, and why. Then name one thing you would model, and one letter you would shape from sticks.

## Do It Yourself

1. Model an animal you enjoy from clay or soft earth, following the steps: plan it on paper first.
2. Make a paper picture by cutting, folding and pasting — no drawing allowed, only paper shapes.
3. Shape three Amharic letters from sticks or stones and lay them in a row. Read them aloud.
