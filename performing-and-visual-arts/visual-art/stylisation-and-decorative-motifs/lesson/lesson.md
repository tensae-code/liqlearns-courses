# Stylisation and Decorative Motifs

## Do it first

Name one thing that belongs in this lesson — Stylisation — turning natural forms into decorative motifs and building decorative compositions from them. Say what it is in your own words. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Compoziții decorative

G4 Ch. 3, Compoziții decorative — decorative composition: stylisation (simplifying a natural form by geometrising it, step by step), the decorative motif (geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic), and the two ordering principles rhythm (repetition) and symmetry.

## Sub lesson 2 — Principii de construire a compozițiilor decorative

Unit 3, Principii de construire a compozițiilor decorative — stylisation: simplifying a natural form by geometrising it into a decorative motif (geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic), built by rhythm and symmetry, as on Moldovan carpets and towels.

## Sub lesson 3 — Transformarea formelor din natură

Unit I §3, Transformarea formelor din natură — nature's structures (animal, vegetal, mineral) turned into art: Egyptians simplified the lotus flower into jewellery and lotus-shaped columns.

## Sub lesson 4 — Tratarea decorativă

Unit II §7, Tratarea decorativă — decorative treatment against pictorial: colour laid uniformly, surfaces with precise contours, no volume and no aerial perspective, motifs geometrised and simplified; stylisation "warm" with wavy cornerless lines, "cold" with straight angular ones.

## Sub lesson 5 — Crearea motivului decorativ

Unit II §7.2, Crearea motivului decorativ — making a decorative motif step by step: examine a tulip, draw it true, stylise it, then compose with it; the motif families — geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic, anthropomorphic (sun, man, birds, flowers, little horses).

## Sub lesson 6 — De la structura naturală spre structura plastică

Unit I §1, De la structura naturală spre structura plastică — turning a natural structure into a plastic one by the artist's procedures (study, construction, overlapping, sectioning, combining, stylisation, modelling, modulation); art may imitate nature faithfully or transform it into new decorative forms — Escher's "Lizards".

## Sub lesson 7 — Stilizarea ca procedeu artistic

Unit III §15, Stilizarea ca procedeu artistic — stylisation's three operations (simplify the form, geometrise it, substitute its colours) and the stylised form at work everywhere: coats of arms (Gheorghe Vrabie's State Coat of Arms of Moldova), pictograms, posters, packaging, fabrics — Klimt's "Tree of Life" against Aurel David's unstylised "Noon".
