# Lesson plan — Stylisation and Decorative Motifs

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Stylisation and Decorative Motifs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Stylisation — turning natural forms into decorative motifs and building decorative compositions from them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Compoziții decorative** — G4 Ch. 3, Compoziții decorative — decorative composition: stylisation (simplifying a natural form by geometrising it, step by step), the decorative motif (geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic), and the two ordering principles rhythm (repetition) and symmetry.
2. **Principii de construire a compozițiilor decorative** — Unit 3, Principii de construire a compozițiilor decorative — stylisation: simplifying a natural form by geometrising it into a decorative motif (geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic), built by rhythm and symmetry, as on Moldovan carpets and towels.
3. **Transformarea formelor din natură** — Unit I §3, Transformarea formelor din natură — nature's structures (animal, vegetal, mineral) turned into art: Egyptians simplified the lotus flower into jewellery and lotus-shaped columns.
4. **Tratarea decorativă** — Unit II §7, Tratarea decorativă — decorative treatment against pictorial: colour laid uniformly, surfaces with precise contours, no volume and no aerial perspective, motifs geometrised and simplified; stylisation "warm" with wavy cornerless lines, "cold" with straight angular ones.
5. **Crearea motivului decorativ** — Unit II §7.2, Crearea motivului decorativ — making a decorative motif step by step: examine a tulip, draw it true, stylise it, then compose with it; the motif families — geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic, anthropomorphic (sun, man, birds, flowers, little horses).
6. **De la structura naturală spre structura plastică** — Unit I §1, De la structura naturală spre structura plastică — turning a natural structure into a plastic one by the artist's procedures (study, construction, overlapping, sectioning, combining, stylisation, modelling, modulation); art may imitate nature faithfully or transform it into new decorative forms — Escher's "Lizards".
7. **Stilizarea ca procedeu artistic** — Unit III §15, Stilizarea ca procedeu artistic — stylisation's three operations (simplify the form, geometrise it, substitute its colours) and the stylised form at work everywhere: coats of arms (Gheorghe Vrabie's State Coat of Arms of Moldova), pictograms, posters, packaging, fabrics — Klimt's "Tree of Life" against Aurel David's unstylised "Noon".
