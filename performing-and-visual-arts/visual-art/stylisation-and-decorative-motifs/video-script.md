This is Moldova's own lesson.

**Compoziții decorative.** G4 Ch. 3, Compoziții decorative — decorative composition: stylisation (simplifying a natural form by geometrising it, step by step), the decorative motif (geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic), and the two ordering principles rhythm (repetition) and symmetry.

**Principii de construire a compozițiilor decorative.** Unit 3, Principii de construire a compozițiilor decorative — stylisation: simplifying a natural form by geometrising it into a decorative motif (geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic), built by rhythm and symmetry, as on Moldovan carpets and towels.

**Transformarea formelor din natură.** Unit I §3, Transformarea formelor din natură — nature's structures (animal, vegetal, mineral) turned into art: Egyptians simplified the lotus flower into jewellery and lotus-shaped columns.

**Tratarea decorativă.** Unit II §7, Tratarea decorativă — decorative treatment against pictorial: colour laid uniformly, surfaces with precise contours, no volume and no aerial perspective, motifs geometrised and simplified; stylisation "warm" with wavy cornerless lines, "cold" with straight angular ones.

**Crearea motivului decorativ.** Unit II §7.2, Crearea motivului decorativ — making a decorative motif step by step: examine a tulip, draw it true, stylise it, then compose with it; the motif families — geometric, vegetal, zoomorphic, anthropomorphic (sun, man, birds, flowers, little horses).

**De la structura naturală spre structura plastică.** Unit I §1, De la structura naturală spre structura plastică — turning a natural structure into a plastic one by the artist's procedures (study, construction, overlapping, sectioning, combining, stylisation, modelling, modulation); art may imitate nature faithfully or transform it into new decorative forms — Escher's "Lizards".

**Stilizarea ca procedeu artistic.** Unit III §15, Stilizarea ca procedeu artistic — stylisation's three operations (simplify the form, geometrise it, substitute its colours) and the stylised form at work everywhere: coats of arms (Gheorghe Vrabie's State Coat of Arms of Moldova), pictograms, posters, packaging, fabrics — Klimt's "Tree of Life" against Aurel David's unstylised "Noon".

That is Stylisation and Decorative Motifs — Moldova's way, kept by name.
