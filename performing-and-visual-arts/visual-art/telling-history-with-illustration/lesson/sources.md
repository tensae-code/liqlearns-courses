countries: Ethiopia

| Lesson part | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Sub lesson 1 — History in visual art: the visual arts (painting, illustration, sculpture and other methods) have a big role in remembering and teaching the country's history and passing it to generations; this part teaches telling history through illustration | Book 1, §3.1, printed p. 65 |
| Sub lesson 2 — Illustration: expressing in drawing a history, composition, report or tale presented in writing — preparing a descriptive drawing from literary works (books, narratives, newspapers, tales) to narrate and express; tools — watercolour, brush, pencil, pen, thick paper, and histories gathered from books, tales and other information; steps — identify the history part to illustrate, divide the history into main parts suited to picture-narration, draw the picture-narrative design, beautify with watercolour or pen giving light and shade, write the history beside the picture narrative in beautiful handwriting; practicals — group illustration of a patriot's history; discussion of how local paintings, sculptures, public monuments and old buildings tell the country's history | Book 1, §3.1.1, printed p. 66 |
| Sub lesson 3 — Telling history with photography: photography began at the end of the 19th century; it binds a thing to its moment and condition; every photograph records its time and condition; a photograph is an era's witness, memorial and sign; photographs of Emperor Menelik's beginnings of telephone, car, railway, school, architecture and medicine (Fig. 37); practical — bring old photographs, tell what you understood and the difference between its time and our present life | Book 1, §3.1.2, printed p. 67 |
| Sub lesson 4 — Telling history with theatre: theatre presenting the country's history, its setting and its characters on stage narrates unforgettably for the audience; named history plays — "ህንደኬ" (Hindeke), "የቴዎድሮስ ራዕይ" (The Vision of Tewodros), "ጣይቱ" (Taytu), "የቃቄ ውርደወት" (Qaqe Wirdiwet) — presented at the National Theatre, the Hager Fikir Theatre and other venues; practical — choose a one-moment scene from the country's great plays and tell the history and era it made you feel | Book 1, §3.1.3, printed p. 68 |

**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Ethiopia).**

Honesty notes: The named plays in Sub lesson 4 are placed by name and venue only, exactly as the placement gives them; no play text is transcribed. The Menelik-era photographs (the book's Fig. 37) are described, never copied. Ethiopia's age as a civilisation is taught in the book's own framing ("among the world's ancient civilisations, a country of many languages and astonishing cultures") and is carried as the book's teaching, not as a new claim.
