# Lesson plan — Telling History with Illustration

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Telling History with Illustration
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how visual art remembers a country's history and teaches it to the next generations — through illustration, through photography, and through theatre.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own drawing and on the history they have heard at home and at school. Nothing else is needed: the lesson teaches how a written history becomes a drawn one.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **History in visual art** — painting, illustration, sculpture and other methods remember the country's history and teach it.
2. **Illustration — drawing a written history** — what illustration is; its tools; its steps, ending with the history written beside the pictures.
3. **History in photographs — an era's witness** — photography binds a thing to its moment; the Menelik-era photographs; reading an old photograph.
4. **History on the stage — theatre tells it** — theatre presents history's setting and characters; the named history plays and their theatres.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Painting Still Life (Visual Art) — the drawing and watercolour hand this lesson uses. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Photographs (Visual Art) — looking at photographs before using them as history's witnesses. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what role the visual arts have in remembering and teaching history.
2. Define illustration and work its steps, from choosing the history part to writing the history beside the picture.
3. Explain how a photograph binds a thing to its moment, and read an old photograph for its time.
4. Name the history plays the book names and say how theatre tells history unforgettably.

## What you need
Pencil, pen, watercolour, brush, thick paper — and a history to tell: a patriot's story, a tale, or a report from a book or newspaper. Old photographs if your family has them.

## If the learner is stuck
- The history is too big to draw → divide it into main parts first; one picture per main part.
- The drawing looks empty → give it light and shade; a picture-narrative needs depth, not only outlines.
- You cannot find the history → ask for a patriot's history, a tale, or a report; illustration feeds on written and told accounts alike.
