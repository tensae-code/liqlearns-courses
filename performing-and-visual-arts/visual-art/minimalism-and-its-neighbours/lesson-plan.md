# Lesson plan — Minimalism and Its Neighbours

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Minimalism and Its Neighbours
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Minimalism and its late-1960s–70s neighbours — simple geometry, photorealism and the movements that followed pop.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **the movements of the late 1960s–70s, first group** — the movements of the late 1960s–70s, first group: Minimalism's simple geometry (Judd, Stella, Flavin); Photorealism painting reality like a photograph (Chuck Close, Estes, Flack); Arte Povera — Italy's "poor art" of simple everyday materials against consumer society (Pistoletto's "Venus of the Rags", Kounellis, Merz); Feminist art answering gender inequality (Judy Chicago, Barbara Kruger, Cindy Sherman).
2. **Минимализам** — Минимализам — Minimalism: strict geometry and repeated identical forms that make the viewer study what little is there — Judd, Andre, Morris.
3. **Хиперреализам** — Хиперреализам — Hyperrealism: the everyday painted and sculpted more real than real — shoppers and streets at life size, in real clothes — Hanson.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
