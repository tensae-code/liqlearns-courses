# Glazes and Kiln Firing

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about glazes and kiln firing. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — firing

Fire as the potter's partner — what firing changes in the clay body (strength, weight, colour), the national kiln's cross-section studied, and the village kiln firing with its great fire and two-day cooling

## Sub lesson 2 — glaze

Glaze understood — why glaze (no water absorption, hygiene, beauty), the firing ranges (900–1200 °C ordinary, to 1350 °C glassy), and the glaze triangle of feldspar, silica and flux with its ingredient list (calcium carbonate, borax, zinc oxide, dolomite, frits)

## Sub lesson 3 — close

Decorating after firing — the methods tree (brush painting, printing, stencil, spraying, sponging, dipping) worked through with photographs, plus sand decoration: coloured sand mixed with binder paste trickled over the pot in patterns
