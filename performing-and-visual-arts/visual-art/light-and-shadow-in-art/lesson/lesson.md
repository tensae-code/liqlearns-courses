# Light and Shadow in Art

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about light and shadow in art. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three areas where light falls

Natural and artificial light spread in diverging rays. When light falls on an opaque body in its path, three areas arise: the lit area facing the light; the unlit side of the body itself, called the real shadow (الظل الحقيقي); and the shadow the body throws on the surface beneath it, the cast shadow (الظل الساقط).

## Sub lesson 2 — Strong light, strong shadow

By light we know things' colours, shapes and shadows. The rule is direct: the stronger and brighter the light, the stronger the shadow; the weaker the light, the lighter the shadow. The artist who wants deep, firm shadows seeks a strong light; soft light gives soft shadows.

## Sub lesson 3 — Black absorbs, white reflects

A body that absorbs all the light falling on it looks black, and a body that reflects it all looks white. Between those two ends lies every colour and every grade of shading the artist can draw — light and shadow are the measure of them all.
