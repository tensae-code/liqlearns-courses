# Bolivian Ceramics

## Do it first

Name one thing from Bolivia that belongs in this lesson — a song, a dance, a pot, a carving, a cloth. Say where it comes from and who makes it. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ceramics as Bolivia's cultural identity

Bolivian ceramics rooted in the origin arts — a fusion of precolonial indigenous traditions and Spanish elements, studied in the Quechua, Aymara and Guarani pottery, with the country's clay deposits named department by department.

## Sub lesson 2 — Preparing the clay

The potter's earth prepared step by step: grind, sift, soak to a milky consistency, rest until it holds like plasticine; and clay itself — kaolin, chamote, barbotina, engobe, and phasa, the edible clay (teacher compendium — evidence).

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson holds, part by part, in your own words. Companion checks: did every part get named, with its Bolivian name kept?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Bolivia's own way of teaching its arts?
