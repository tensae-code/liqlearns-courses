# Lesson plan — Bolivian Ceramics

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Lesson:** Bolivian Ceramics
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bolivia's ceramics — preparing the clay and making pottery as an expression of Bolivian cultural identity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ceramics as Bolivia's cultural identity** — Bolivian ceramics rooted in the origin arts — a fusion of precolonial indigenous traditions and Spanish elements, studied in the Quechua, Aymara and Guarani pottery, with the country's clay deposits named department by department.
2. **Preparing the clay** — The potter's earth prepared step by step: grind, sift, soak to a milky consistency, rest until it holds like plasticine; and clay itself — kaolin, chamote, barbotina, engobe, and phasa, the edible clay (teacher compendium — evidence).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
