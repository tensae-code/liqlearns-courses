# Lesson plan — Contemporary Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Contemporary Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in the twentieth century artists took up new ways of working — and the idea of speed especially interested them, because people's machines had grown fast: the Futurist painters Russolo and Balla made art of the moving modern world.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A century of faster machines** — People made machines that move on land, on water and through the air — first carts drawn by horses and sleds by dogs, then the bicycle, and in the 20th century much faster machines: the car, the train, the aeroplane and the rocket.
2. **Futurism — the art of speed** — That is why the idea of speed especially interested artists during the 20th century — as in the pictured Futurist paintings of Russolo and Balla.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
