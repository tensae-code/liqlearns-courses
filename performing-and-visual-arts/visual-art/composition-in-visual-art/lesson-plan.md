# Lesson plan — Composition in Visual Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Composition in Visual Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** composition: what it is in visual art, why it matters, and how composition and design arrange a work in two dimensions and in three. Teacher-guide evidence throughout, labelled.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Elements of Visual Art and Elements and Principles of Design: composition is where the elements and principles meet in one arranged work. This lesson carries the Grade 10 guide's own composition lessons as evidence.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **What composition is (teacher guide — evidence)** — the concept of composition in visual art.
2. **Why composition matters (teacher guide — evidence)** — the importance and purposes of composition.
3. **Composition and design — in two and three dimensions (teacher guide — evidence)** — arranging a work in 2D and in 3D.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Elements and Principles of Design (Visual Art) — the principles composition puts to work. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, from the guide's evidence, what the concept of composition in visual art is.
2. Give the importance and purposes of composition, as the guide teaches them.
3. Explain how composition and design arrange a work in two and in three dimensions.

## What you need
Paper, pencil, colours — and a handful of objects to arrange, badly and then well.

## If the learner is stuck
- A work feels 'off' but you cannot say why → it is usually composition: move one element, restore the arrangement, and look again. Naming the fault is the fix's first half.
