# Colours on Clothes


## Do it first

Look down. What colours are YOU wearing today?

Now look at two friends. Their colours?

Clothes are the colours we carry with us — chosen, worn, seen by everyone. This lesson reads clothes the way earlier lessons read flowers and skies.

## Sub lesson 1 — The colours of clothes

The book photographs three garments and asks their colours: a **red T-shirt**, a **green-and-yellow sports shirt**, and a **blue jacket**.

[Picture 9170 — colours on clothes: red T-shirt, green-and-yellow sports shirt, blue jacket]

Clothes come in colours — plain colours, and colours in partnership, like the sports shirt's green WITH yellow. Name what you see precisely: not just "a shirt" but "a green-and-yellow shirt".

Your turn: name the colours of everything you are wearing, garment by garment.

## Sub lesson 2 — The colours of our family's clothes

Grade 2 sends the colour hunt home, with Exercise 3.1.4's own questions:

**What colours of clothes do your father and mother wear? What colours are your classmates' clothes?**

[Picture 9171 — a family in their everyday clothes and their colours]

Everyday colours and festival colours are often different — the same person, two colour wardrobes. Ask at home. Look in class. Report the colours truly, person by person.

## Sub lesson 3 — Traditional dress colours

The section closes with full-page colour pictures of **traditional dress**, captioned with their teaching: **expressing cultural values in the colours of dress**.

[Picture 9172 — traditional dress and its colours]

Traditional dress is not coloured by accident. Its colours are part of what the dress SAYS — who we are, where we belong, what we honour. When you named the flag's colours you met the same idea; here it is worn.

Look at the pictured dress: which colours carry it? Which borders and bands hold the colour?

## Sub lesson 4 — The book's own closing words on colours and clothes

The Grade 1 book closes its culture chapter with a summary that gathers this whole lesson into one line: **colours distinguish our clothes and beautify the surroundings — plants green, flowers red and yellow**.

Distinguish: by colour you can tell one garment, one person, one group from another. Beautify: colour is also the world's decoration, worn and grown.

The review that follows asks you to name and justify a favourite colour. Choose yours now — and be ready to justify it: where it lives, what it means, what it is worn on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Clothes carry colours — a red T-shirt, a green-and-yellow sports shirt, a blue jacket. My family's clothes are … in colour. Traditional dress expresses our values in its colours." Then name the colours you are both wearing.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name the colours of every garment you are wearing, precisely — partnership colours named in full.
2. Answer Exercise 3.1.4 in writing: your father's colours, your mother's colours, your classmates' colours.
3. Draw traditional dress and colour it as the pictures show; write one sentence: what its colours express.
