# Lesson plan — Colours on Clothes

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Colours on Clothes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** clothes carry colours — a red T-shirt, a green-and-yellow sports shirt, a blue jacket — and the colours of our family's and classmates' clothes, and of traditional dress, express our cultural values.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Colours Around Us — the learner names colours in the world; now the colours worn on people's backs get their own study, from Grade 1 garment photographs to Grade 2's values-in-dress teaching, closing with the Grade 1 chapter summary folded in.

## Sub lessons
1. **The colours of clothes** — the Grade 1 garment photographs — a red T-shirt, a green-and-yellow sports shirt and a blue jacket — naming the colours clothes come in.
2. **The colours of our family's clothes** — Grade 2's Exercise 3.1.4 — what colours of clothes do your father and mother wear? What colours are your classmates' clothes?
3. **Traditional dress colours** — Grade 2's full-page dress-colour pictures closing §3.1.4 — traditional dress shown for its colours, captioned: expressing cultural values in the colours of dress.
4. **The book's own closing words on colours and clothes** — the Grade 1 Chapter 3 summary folded in — colours distinguish our clothes and beautify the surroundings (plants green, flowers red and yellow).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Colours Around Us in this unit. Recommended, never forced.
- Its neighbour: Traditional Dress in Culture and Values, where the dress itself — not only its colours — is the subject.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the colours of the pictured garments: a red T-shirt, a green-and-yellow sports shirt, a blue jacket.
2. Say what colours of clothes your father and mother wear, and what colours your classmates' clothes are (Exercise 3.1.4).
3. Say how traditional dress shows cultural values in its colours.

## What you need
Your own clothes and your family's — the lesson is worn, not bought. Paper and colours for the dress drawings.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says only "colourful" → ask garment by garment, colour by colour; precise naming is the skill, and partnership colours (green-and-yellow) count double.
- No traditional dress at home to look at → work from the pictured dress and from festival memories; the lesson needs eyes and memory, not a wardrobe.
