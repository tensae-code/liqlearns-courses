This lesson is Vietnamese Craft Villages.

Vietnamese Craft Villages — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: The kim hoàn craft is the craft of crafting exquisite jewellery items such as rings, necklaces and earrings from the precious metals gold and silver combined with gemstones; it is a craft of long standing with many handmade jewellery-making techniques, so it needs creativity and demands cleverness from the craftsman, with many stages such as cutting, sharpening (gọt) and touching to process and craft the precious metals into goldsmith details, and weighing (cân), grinding (mài) and polishing gemstones to attach and complete them on the jewellery; in our country the kim hoàn craft has existed for thousands of years and has become a traditional craft in many localities — the kim hoàn craft at Định Công (Hanoi) with its long history, the village's products being gold and silver jewellery items with high aesthetic character; the kim hoàn craft at Châu Khê (Hải Dương), where from the silver-casting craft the goldsmiths developed the craft of making gold and silver jewellery, meeting the modern market's needs with products such as necklaces, earrings, rings, vòng and bracelets; the kim hoàn craft at Đồng Xâm (Thái Bình), famous everywhere for its exquisiteness, with unique silver-touch (chạm bạc) jewellery items whose shape-mass stands out thanks to the silver material's reflective characteristic, and its exquisiteness in crafting decorative patterns; and the kim hoàn craft at Kế Môn (Thừa Thiên Huế), whose jewellery products — vòng, rings, bracelets, necklaces and earrings in gold or silver — have quality, with exquisite hand technique and meticulous touch-carving (chạm khắc) made by craftsmen of experience, clever hands and aesthetic, creative character.
