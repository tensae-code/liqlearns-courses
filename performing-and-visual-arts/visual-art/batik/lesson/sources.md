| Kazakhstan — 2.1. Искусство художественной росписи — what it teaches: the art of artistic painting — decorative-applied art is one of fine art's large sections: products people use in everyday life — carpets, fabrics, furniture, tableware, storage boxes, jewellery, toys — which must possess aesthetic quality, their form, function and decor inseparably joined; artistic painting is the art of decorating any surface with paints, the external decoration of a product by artistic means, its technique and means depending on the material decorated; the kinds pictured — жостово, гжель, мезенская, федоскино and хохлома painting; in the folk art of Kazakhstan a large place was held by wall and rock painting, reflecting events, knowledge and the people's accumulated experience and passing information to following generations — examples are the paintings of the Khoja Akhmet Yassawi mausoleum and the rock drawings done in red ochre, and the most famous rock images are the petroglyphs of the Tamgaly archaeological complex, a special place among them held by the sun-headed anthropomorphic beings; in gzhel painting cobalt paints are used, black in nature, which only after firing could achieve up to 20 shades of blue, from light blue to deep blue; research the paintings of the cultures of China, Indonesia, Greece, Russia and Kazakhstan, and sketch in the research album the characteristic painting elements of the cultures studied (student book; original target `visual-art/batik-fabric-painting`) | Book 8, printed p. 32 |
| Kazakhstan — 2.2. Батик и способы его выполнения — what it teaches: batik and the ways of doing it — hand painting of fabric is called batik: paint is laid on the fabric — silk, cotton, synthetic; to get clear borders of the drawing at the junction of paints a special fixer is used — the резерв (resist), chosen by the kind of batik; cold batik — painting on fabric with a cold resist: the fabric is stretched on a frame, the drawing laid on and its contour gone over with the resist, a closed form resulting which does not let the paint flow beyond the contour; the paint is laid with a brush and left to dry, and the product is ironed from the reverse side with a hot iron and washed by hand with soap; hot batik — painting on fabric with molten wax: the drawing is laid on and the drawing's contour gone over with sufficiently hot wax, and after the paint is laid and dries the wax is cleaned off, the product laid out with ordinary newspapers or paper towels and ironed with a hot iron; knotted batik (узелковый батик) — dyeing fabric by tying separate parts of it into knots and laying paint on them: before dyeing, the fabric is folded in a defined way by a thought-out scheme, tied with strong threads and lowered into the paint, and after dyeing and washing the knots are untied; the knotted-batik technique is compared with the shibori technique, which arose in Japan — in the shibori technique the fabric is folded and fixed in a special way, reminding one of the origami technique, and different ways of tying can be used, a definite drawing got by tying knots in a defined order and putting coins, stones or buttons into them; study the sequence of cold-batik painting from the pictures and write the order and the tools' names in the research album (student book; original target `visual-art/batik-fabric-painting`) | Book 8, printed p. 35 |
| Kazakhstan — 2.3. Начало творческой работы. Рисование эскиза для батика — what it teaches: the beginning of the creative work — drawing the sketch for a batik; compare works in the batik technique — the Kazakhstani artist Gulnar Umirbek and Gabriel Daniel Jean-Baptiste, an artist from the Caribbean islands; batik's homeland is considered Indonesia — in translation from Indonesian the word «ba» means «cotton fabric» and «tik» a «dot» or «drop»; ambatik — to draw, to cover with drops, to hatch; the free-painting technique (свободная роспись) — a quick way of creating an interesting picturesque work, differing from classical hot and cold batik in being more like painting than batik: a composition is created on primed fabric, thanks to the ground the paints spread less and keep the stroke's form, and a special device in free painting is the salt-mask form; for the ground a solution of table salt, starch, PVA glue or gelatin can be used (a cosmetic bag and a bright scarf in free painting pictured); study the works of the artist Kamila Zhapalova — what idea is expressed in these pictures, in what technique they are done, what artistic devices the artist used to convey the idea, and what you would name these pictures; in the modern world batik's sphere of application is boundless — kerchiefs, shawls, scarves, ties, coupons for dresses from hand-painted fabric are well known, popular and prestigious beyond fashion's dictates, since each such product exists in a single copy; prepare the work plan, define the needed materials and the sequence of actions, carry out the sketch of your creative work and colour it with watercolour or gouache paints, observing compositional balance in the sketch, not forgetting the composition's centre, and joining colours harmoniously when colouring (student book; original target `visual-art/batik-fabric-painting`) | Book 8, printed p. 38 |
| Kazakhstan — 2.4. Подготовка к выполнению батика — what it teaches: preparing to carry out the batik — for painting fabric one must prepare the fabric, a frame of the needed sizes, transfer the drawing to the fabric and settle on the resist; 1. carrying out the drawing on tracing paper — on millimetre paper with pencil or marker draw the drawing's scheme, the tracing paper's size depending on the product's size; 2. choosing and preparing the fabric — for the work choose natural fabric, better silk (crepe de chine, chiffon), which fixes the paint most qualitatively; rinse the fabric beforehand in water with shampoo — this is needed for the paint to flow better over the fabric; 3. stretching the fabric on the подрамник (stretcher) — take a collapsible frame or make it from wooden planks to the product's size; the fabric is stretched on the stretcher clockwise with drawing pins; stretch the fabric taut, like a drum, keeping the threads' perpendicularity — if the stretching is wrong the drawing will be distorted; 4. transferring the drawing with tracing paper to the fabric — the tracing paper with the drawing must be pinned to the stretched fabric's reverse side, and the showing-through drawing transferred to the fabric in a thin pencil line; 5. laying the resist on the fabric — when the drawing is transferred, one can start laying the resist; lay it carefully so there are no breaks, or the paint will spread; the reserving compound is best laid from top down, so the hand does not smudge the contour already laid below; when the whole drawing is gone over with the resist, leave it to dry; the contour line must be unbroken so the paint does not flow from one space into another — to check the laid contour, look at the fabric against the light (a break in the contour line will be clearly visible) or draw over the closed contour element with a brush and water without admixtures: if the water seeps beyond the contour, the contour line is broken here (student book;
