## Sub lesson 1 — Art and power

Andorra teaches art history **not chronologically but through questions**. **Art and power**: **Baroque painting** served to consolidate **political and religious power** — **Caravaggio, Rubens, Vermeer and Rembrandt, Velázquez**. **Romanesque and Gothic architecture** stood as **symbols of power** — monasteries and churches, Gothic cathedrals, civil building, **including the Romanesque of Andorra**. And **classical Greek and Roman sculpture** and the **Xi'an warriors** show sculpture and power in other civilisations.

## Sub lesson 2 — Art and mentality

**Art and mentality**: from a work, deduce what its epoch thought. **Renaissance painting** shows the **humanist mentality** — Quattrocento: **Masaccio, Uccello, Piero della Francesca, Fra Angelico, Botticelli**; Cinquecento: **Leonardo, Raphael, Michelangelo**. **Catalan modernisme / art nouveau architecture** shows the mentality of the **Catalan bourgeoisie** — **Horta; Domènech i Montaner, Puig i Cadafalch, Gaudí**. **Late-20th-century sculpture** — **Moore, Chillida, Oldenburg, Murakami, Ekpuk, Caro** — shows the mentality of the students' own time.

## Sub lesson 3 — Art and conflicts

**Art and conflicts**: art transmits war. **Goya and Picasso** stand as **witnesses of their epochs** — **Guernica** as symbol of the Spanish Civil War, and Goya's **Executions of the Second of May** and the Peninsular War. **Banksy's graffiti** criticises today's conflicts. **Gargallo and Julio González** carry it in early-20th-century sculpture, and **Le Corbusier's functional** and **Wright's organicist** architecture stand around the Second World War.
