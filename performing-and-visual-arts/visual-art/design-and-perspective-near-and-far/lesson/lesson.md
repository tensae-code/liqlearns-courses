# Design and Perspective: Near and Far

## Do it first

Stand on a road. The trees beside you are huge; the same trees far away are small, and the road itself narrows to a point. Your eye knows perspective before your hand does. The teacher guide behind this lesson teaches design for painting and printing through exactly that — near and far. *Everything here is teacher-guide evidence, labelled as such.*

## Sub lesson 1 — Near and far — perspective (teacher guide — evidence)

*Teacher-guide evidence — the Grade 6 teacher's guide carries a design section **for painting and printing**, and its subject is **near and far: perspective** — in Amharic, የርቀትና ቅርበት, and ዕርዮተ አድማስ, **the view of distance**.*

Perspective is how a flat design holds depth: how things that stand near the viewer and things that stand far from the viewer are each given their true look. The guide's own picture slot fixes the image to learn from: **a road narrowing to a vanishing point, with nearer trees darker and larger**.

Read that picture slowly. The road's edges, wide at your feet, run together until they meet at **one point** in the distance. The trees along it teach the same lesson in a row: **near — large; far — small**. A design that holds these truths stops being a row of things and becomes a place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Near and far in light and shadow (teacher guide — evidence)

*Teacher-guide evidence — the same design section carries a second teaching: **near-and-far in light and shadow — how distance changes light and shade**.*

Distance is not told by size alone. The near tree in the guide's picture is not only larger — it is **darker**. As things go back from the viewer, their shade lightens with them; light and shadow themselves carry the far-ness of a thing.

So the design rule the evidence gives is a pair: **shrink it, and lighten it**, as it goes away; **grow it, and darken it**, as it comes near. Size says how far; shade agrees. When both speak together, the viewer believes the distance.

## Say it to your companion

Describe a road or a row of trees to your companion without drawing: near to far. They must say back the three changes they heard — size, darkness, and the narrowing to a point. If one is missing, tell it again.

## Do It Yourself

1. **The road drawing** — Draw a straight road going away from you: two edges narrowing to one vanishing point. Place three trees along it — the nearest large and dark, the farthest small and light.
2. **Shrink and lighten** — Draw the same cup three times in a row, as if walking away from you: each time smaller, each time lighter in shade. Label them near, middle, far.
