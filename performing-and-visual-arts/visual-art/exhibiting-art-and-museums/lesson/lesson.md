## Sub lesson 1 — Виртуални музеи (Virtual Museums) — part one

with the development of information technologies after the 1970s the last kind of art appears — the digital arts, working in categories such as computer graphics, digital painting, photograph processing, video installations and web design; the Mori museum in Tokyo — the first entirely digital museum in the world (teamLab's "Borderless" exposition, opened 5 July 2018): the building uniting an art gallery and an amusement park, the arts leaving the halls freely, forming relations with people, communicating with other works and sometimes mixing among themselves — in a 10,000 m² complex, 520 computers and 470 projectors creating an unseen world in which each visitor individually communicates with the environment, activating over 50 kaleidoscopic installations through specially made sensors that track the body's movements; the virtual museum defined — a set of information in a definite electronic space where digital exhibits are synthesised (texts, pictures, 3D images, diagrams, photographs, films, animations and others) — and the virtual exposition, the web version of a gallery's or museum's exhibitions; the technologies of presenting and building them (infrared reflectography, the presentation of images through X-rays, 3D laser scanning, IBMR — image-based modelling and rendering), the EU-supported VIHAP3D system for the high-quality 3D acquisition and presentation of virtual heritage, and the Virtual Museum of Digital Art (founded 1993, a non-profit organisation run by Don Archer, presenting and classifying digital works — photography, illustration, design, animation — among them works by Bulgarian artists); the first "digital gallery" (art.net, 1994) and Google's Art Project (Google Arts & Culture), built on Street View technology, gathering information and links to the official online pages of world museums so every user can walk virtually through famous museums in different countries;
