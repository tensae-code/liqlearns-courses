# Lesson plan — Ancient Egyptian Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Ancient Egyptian Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the art of ancient Egypt — its architecture, the sphinx and its paintings, examined and evaluated by their elements and principles, and read for the social life and technology they reflect.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Egypt's buildings: pyramids and the sphinx** — Egyptian architecture — the pyramids — and the sphinx, examined as art forms of ancient Egypt.
2. **Egyptian paintings** — Egyptian paintings, examined and evaluated by their elements and principles.
3. **What the art shows: social life and the Nile** — The social life and technological advancement the art reflects — boats on the Nile among them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Egyptian art is only pyramids → the chapter examines paintings too, and evaluates all of it by elements and principles.
- Reads the pictures as decoration only → the book reads them for what they show: social life and technological advancement, such as boats on the Nile.
