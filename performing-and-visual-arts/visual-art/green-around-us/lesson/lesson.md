# Green Around Us


## Do it first

Stand where you can see outside. Name every GREEN thing you can see — count them on your fingers.

Grass? Leaves? What else is wearing green today?

Green is not one picture in a book. It is the colour of the living world around you — and in this lesson it also becomes a game your whole body plays.

## Sub lesson 1 — Green in grass, leaves and maize

The book teaches green by pointing at the world: first a photograph of **green grass**, then **leaves**, then a whole **maize field** — every one captioned with the same words: green colour.

[Picture 9167 — green around us: grass, leaves and a maize field]

Green is the colour plants wear while they are alive and growing. Small green (a leaf in your hand), field green (maize as far as you can see), deep green and light green — one colour, many dresses.

Go and stand in it: find three greens near you that are NOT the same green, and say how they differ.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Green-Yellow-Red game

Now green becomes a command. The game is called **"አረንጓዴ አረንጓዴ" — Green, Green** — a listening-and-moving game.

The rules are the traffic light's rules, played with your body:
- At **"green"** — **run** in a circle.
- At **"yellow"** — **stop**.
- At **"red"** — **sit**.

Whoever does it wrong is **out**. The one who never errs, all the way to the end, **wins**.

Your ears must be faster than your feet: hear the colour, remember its order, move. Play it now — slowly first, then at real game speed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Green is the colour of grass, leaves and the maize field. And it is a game: አረንጓዴ አረንጓዴ — at green we run, at yellow we stop, at red we sit." Then call the game for them once.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw your three found greens: the thing, its name, and its green — light, deep or bright.
2. Teach the Green-Yellow-Red game to someone at home and play three rounds.
3. Draw a maize field and grass with every green you have — how many greens can one picture hold?
