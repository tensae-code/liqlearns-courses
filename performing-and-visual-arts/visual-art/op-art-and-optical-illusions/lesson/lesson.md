# Op Art and Optical Illusions

## Do it first

Quick start: say in one sentence what you think this lesson is about — an optical illusion is a picture of lines or forms on a flat ground that looks as if it is moving. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pictures that seem to move

**Although we know and see that they are only lines or forms on paper, they can look to us as if they are moving.** When we look at **densely arranged wavy lines** it seems they stir, though they are drawn on a flat ground, and with **forms arranged in a special way** an impression of movement can likewise be created. **Such optical illusions — optical tricks — artists use in their works**, and today they can be achieved especially easily with a computer.

## Sub lesson 2 — Testing the tricks

The book sets tests: **test the dotted lines and the arrowed lines for sameness of length**, and **look at the blue and red circles — do they seem to rest or to move?** It also asks you to **try to spot optical tricks in nature and around you**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Riley, Vasarely — and your own illusion

The book pictures **Bridget Riley's Fragment 5** and **four works by Victor Vasarely**. The making task: **choose one of Vasarely's pictures and make a work following it — first draw it, then colour the work.**
