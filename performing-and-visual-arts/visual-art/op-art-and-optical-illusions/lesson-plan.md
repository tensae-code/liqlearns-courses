# Lesson plan — Op Art and Optical Illusions

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Op Art and Optical Illusions
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an optical illusion is a picture of lines or forms on a flat ground that looks as if it is moving — densely arranged wavy lines seem to stir, and artists such as Bridget Riley and Victor Vasarely use these optical tricks in their works.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pictures that seem to move** — Although we know and see that they are only lines or forms on paper, they can look to us as if they are moving.
2. **Testing the tricks** — The book sets tests: test the dotted lines and the arrowed lines for sameness of length, and look at the blue and red circles — do they seem to rest or to move? It also asks you to try to spot optical tricks in nature and around you..
3. **Riley, Vasarely — and your own illusion** — The book pictures Bridget Riley's Fragment 5 and four works by Victor Vasarely.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks if a picture looks as if it moves, it must really move → the trick is in the arranging — lines and forms set in a special way on a flat ground make the eye see movement that is not there.
