# Video: Moving Pictures

## Do it first

Hold your hands up as a frame, like a camera's view. Look through them at the room. Now walk slowly forward without dropping your hands: the picture moved, and it stayed inside your frame. You have just shot your first video in your mind.

## Sub lesson 1 — What video is

**Video** is a **moving image with its own duration**, prepared on the basis of events, **with or without sound**. It is shot with a video camera and watched on **mobile, computer or television** — music, films, dramas, ball games. The Grade 5 book adds video to the arts as a fourth, new art beside the three strands: still pictures stand still; video's pictures move, and time itself becomes one of its materials.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shooting your first video: six things to mind

Shooting video has **six things to mind**: **(1)** gather and prepare the equipment properly — a mobile phone will do; **(2)** think and plan **what to shoot and where it starts and ends** — a wedding cannot all be shot, so pick the **important moments**; **(3)** control the **amount of light** for quality; **(4)** think ahead about what is **behind the subjects** — its colour too; **(5)** grow **composition** skill — what must enter the camera's view and how it interacts; **(6)** make sure the wanted thing is **truly in frame** — nearness and distance need attention. The exercise: shoot something enjoyable, watch it with friends, and judge it together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Shot sizes: close, medium, far (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide names the **shot sizes** with their Amharic names: the **close-up view** (የቅርብ እይታ ቀረጻ) — near, filling the frame with a face or a thing; the **medium view** (የመካከለኛ እይታ) — the middle distance; and the **far or long view** (የሩቅ እይታ ቀረጻ) — the whole place. The same face in close, medium and long view tells three different amounts of story: feelings, actions, and the world around. Choose the size the moment needs.

## Sub lesson 4 — Creative photography: giving a photo a twist (teacher guide — evidence)

**Creative photography** is a photo **intentionally given a twist** to carry meaning, mood and theme — thinking out of the box. The guide's techniques: **bokeh** — glowing unfocused light orbs; **painting onto a printed photo**; **double exposure** — projecting an image onto a textured surface and re-photographing; **motion blur** — long exposure or panning; **macro photography** — the very small made large. Why it matters: uniqueness, emotion, and inspiring others.

## Sub lesson 5 — Film form: shot, scene, sequence, whole film (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 8 guide reads film at two levels. At **micro level**: the **shot** — fragments of picture and sound — and how a single shot is composed: camera placement, lighting, lenses, frame, duration. At **macro level**: shots arranged into **scenes**, scenes into **sequences**, sequences into the **whole film**. And meaning is the film's soul — what is shown directly and what is hinted beneath (subtext) — carried by **plot, theme, conflict, characters and their motivation**. Your hand-frame video from the start of this lesson already worked at micro level; a film is that, built up three storeys high.

## Say it to your companion

Frame your companion three ways with your hands — close, medium, long — and let them name each shot as you change. Then describe a video you would shoot, using the six things in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. Plan a one-minute video on paper: what, where it starts, where it ends, the important moments.
2. Shoot something enjoyable, watch it with friends, and judge it by the six things (Grade 5's exercise).
3. Take one scene five ways in stills: plain, and as near to bokeh, double exposure, motion blur and macro as your camera allows.
