# Lesson plan — Video: Moving Pictures

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Video: Moving Pictures
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the youngest art in the course — what a video is, the six things to mind when shooting one, the three shot sizes, and the creative ways a camera can be used: creative photography's twists and film's building blocks of shot, scene and sequence.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Photography — the learner can already take and frame a still photograph; this lesson sets the picture moving.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What video is** — a moving image with its own duration, with or without sound, watched on mobile, computer or television (Grade 5, printed p. 36).
2. **Shooting your first video: six things to mind** — prepare equipment; plan what to shoot and its start and end; control light; mind the background; grow composition; keep the wanted thing truly in frame (Grade 5, printed p. 37).
3. **Shot sizes: close, medium, far (teacher guide — evidence)** — the three views — close-up (የቅርብ እይታ ቀረጻ), medium (የመካከለኛ እይታ) and far/long (የሩቅ እይታ ቀረጻ) (Grade 5 teacher guide, §1.3, printed pp. 15–20).
4. **Creative photography: giving a photo a twist (teacher guide — evidence)** — a photo intentionally given a twist to carry meaning, mood and theme — bokeh, painting onto a print, double exposure, motion blur, macro (Grade 7 teacher guide, printed pp. 59–64).
5. **Film form: shot, scene, sequence, whole film (teacher guide — evidence)** — film structure at micro level — shots of picture and sound — and macro level: scenes, sequences, the whole film; meaning, plot, theme, conflict, characters (Grade 8 teacher guide, printed p. 4).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Photography and Photographs. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a video is and where videos are watched.
2. Recite the six things to mind when shooting.
3. Name the three shot sizes: close-up, medium, far/long.
4. Name creative camera twists: bokeh, double exposure, motion blur, macro.
5. Tell a shot from a scene, a scene from a sequence, and a sequence from the whole film.

## What you need
A phone or camera if one is available; without one, the lesson is learned by planning and framing with the hands.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Hold your hands up as a frame, like a camera's view. Look through them at the room. Now walk slowly forward without dropping your hands: the picture moved, and it stayed inside your frame. You have just shot your first video in your mind. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md), with these add-on slots at their exact places:

## If the learner is stuck
- A shaky, unclear first video → return to the six things: prepare the equipment, plan the start and end, control the light, mind the background, compose, and check the wanted thing is truly in frame.
- Confuses scene and sequence → one scene is one place-and-time piece; a sequence is scenes joined into a bigger piece of the story.
