# Lesson plan — The Elements of Visual Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** The Elements of Visual Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the building blocks every picture is made of — line, shape, form, space, texture, value and colour — and what each one does, including the feelings lines carry and the steps from light to dark.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Lines in Every Direction and Shapes You Can Draw — the learner can already draw lines and shapes and name the primary colours; this lesson names the whole element set the older books build with.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Visual art is a language** — visual arts express thought, feeling and desire, understood by seeing; the seven elements named (Grade 5, printed pp. 2–3).
2. **Lines and their feelings** — a line has width, length, direction and feeling; vertical, horizontal, diagonal, zigzag and curved lines each carry a feeling; shapes are areas enclosed by lines (Grade 5, printed pp. 3–4).
3. **Form, and positive and negative space** — form has depth — real form and imaginary form; space is positive where the things are and negative everywhere outside them (Grade 5, printed pp. 5–6).
4. **Value: light and dark steps** — value is the degree of darkness and lightness; tint adds white, shade adds black; make a nine-step value scale (Grade 5, printed pp. 7–8).
5. **The principles of design** — the elements at work under the principles — pattern, contrast, emphasis, balance, scale, unity/harmony, rhythm and repetition, variety (Grade 7 student, printed pp. 8–12; Grade 7 teacher guide, printed pp. 26–41).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Lines in Every Direction and The Primary Colours. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the seven elements of visual art.
2. Say what feeling each kind of line carries.
3. Tell positive space from negative space in a picture.
4. Make a nine-step value scale from light to dark.
5. Name the principles of design: pattern, contrast, emphasis, balance, unity and rhythm.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, coloured pencils or paints, and one picture from home to look at slowly.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Look at one picture near you. Do not say what it shows yet. Say only what it is made of: what lines do you see? What shapes? Where is it light, and where is it dark? (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot name the elements → go tile by tile through the picture: line, shape, form, space, texture, value, colour — find one of each before naming the set.
- Mixes shape and form → hold a flat paper circle, then a ball: the circle is a shape, the ball is a form with depth.
