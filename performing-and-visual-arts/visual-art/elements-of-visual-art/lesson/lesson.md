# The Elements of Visual Art

## Do it first

Look at one picture near you. Do not say what it shows yet. Say only what it is made of: what lines do you see? What shapes? Where is it light, and where is it dark?

## Sub lesson 1 — Visual art is a language

Visual art is a language you read with your eyes. Drawing, colour painting, sculpture, photography, printing and handicraft express thought, feeling and desire; they tell stories, record information and pass history from one generation to the next. The backbone that carries a work's message is its **elements**: line, shape, form, texture, space, value (ፍካት) and colour. Learn these seven names first — everything else in this lesson hangs on them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lines and their feelings

A line is the mark a thing makes moving from a point. It has width, length, direction — and a **feeling**. A **vertical** line feels dignified, alive, strong and firm. A **horizontal** line feels rest, peace and quiet. A **diagonal** line feels speed, movement and falling. A **zigzag**, built from diagonals, feels shattering, cracking, anger and roughness. A **curved** line feels slow movement — ducks on water, a ship's wake. When lines join and close, they make a **shape**: an area with length and width but no depth. Measured, tool-made shapes are geometric; unmeasured shapes from people, animals and plants are natural.

## Sub lesson 3 — Form, and positive and negative space

A **form** has length, width **and depth** — it is three-dimensional. Real form can be moved and placed. Imaginary form is made on a flat board with line, colour, space and value, the way a drawn circle shades into a sphere. The basic forms are the sphere, the cube, the cylinder and the cone. **Space** is the area around, between and inside the things in a work. **Positive space** is what the things themselves occupy; **negative space** is everything outside them. In the book's picture of seats, the white part is positive and the dark part is negative.

## Sub lesson 4 — Value: light and dark steps

**Value** (ፍካት) is the degree of darkness and lightness in a work. Add white to a colour and you make a **tint** (ማንጣት). Add black and you make a **shade** (ማጥቆር). Between the lightest tint and the darkest shade runs the **value scale**. The book's exercise is plain: make a nine-step value scale, first in pencil, then in colour — nine even steps from light to dark, no jumps.

## Sub lesson 5 — The principles of design

Elements are the bricks; **principles** are how the bricks are laid. The Grade 7 book names them: **pattern** (a shape or colour repeated), **contrast** (opposites set against each other), **emphasis** (one part made to catch the eye first), **balance** (the work feeling steady, not tipping), **scale** (sizes in right relation), **unity and harmony** (ውህደት — the parts belonging together), **rhythm and repetition**, **oneness** (አንድነት) and **variety**. The teacher guide adds the elements again one by one — space and point, line and its strokes, shape, form, texture, value and colour — the same seven, now used to build with.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the seven elements. Then point into a real picture and show one of each, saying its name out loud.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw one small picture and label its seven elements in the margin.
2. Make a nine-step value scale in pencil, lightest first.
3. Draw the five line kinds and write the feeling of each under it.
