# The Elements of Visual Art — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** How is visual art understood — by hearing, or by seeing?
**Answer: By seeing — visual arts are understood by seeing.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** What is a shape?
**Answer: An area enclosed by lines — it has length and width, but no depth.**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What is the difference between a shape and a form?
**Answer: A shape has length and width only; a form also has depth.**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** How many steps does the book ask for in the value-scale exercise?
**Answer: Nine steps, in pencil and then in colour.**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Name two principles of design.
**Answer: Any two of: pattern, contrast, emphasis, balance, scale, unity/harmony, rhythm and repetition, oneness, variety.**

**6. (Sub lesson 15)** How do you mark the Grade 5 self-check, in the book's own signs?
**Answer:** ✓ beside a can-do statement I achieved; ✗ beside one I did not — then re-study the crossed ones.

**7. (Sub lesson 16)** Name Grade 7 Chapter 1's four aim-systems.
**Answer:** The elements and principles of design; Ethiopian traditional music and dances; the elements and types of theatre; the elements and types of film.

**8. (Sub lesson 17)** In the book's closing order, what three things end a Grade 7 chapter?
**Answer:** A ✓/✗ self-check table; a summary; a closing exercise.

**9. (Sub lesson 17)** How many items does Chapter 1's self-check table carry, and how is the closing exercise divided?
**Answer:** 7 self-check items; the exercise is 5 true/false and 5 short answers.

**10. (Sub lesson 17)** Name one element and one principle of design from this lesson, in one sentence each.
**Answer:** Any element this lesson teaches (e.g. line — the path a point takes) and any principle (e.g. balance — how the parts are weighted against each other), as this lesson defines them.

**11. (Sub lesson 18)** Name four of Grade 8 Chapter 1's aim-topics beyond design.
**Answer:** Any four of: instrument categories; Ethiopian music's contextual categories; theatre and film preparation stages; folk-dance categories; the basic qenet in varied keys; appreciation of music art.

**12. (Sub lesson 19)** Through what two makings does Ethiopian music express philosophy, culture, education, politics and religion, per the summary?
**Answer:** Melody-and-poem composition, and folk dances.

**13. (Sub lesson 19)** Whose four contexts does the summary name for Ethiopian music?
**Answer:** Zenebe Bekele's four contexts.

**14. (Sub lesson 19)** Do it and show it: name the two practical tasks of the Chapter 1 closing exercises.
**Answer:** Make a picture composition using the elements and principles; make a locally used traditional instrument.
