# Mixing Colours


## Do it first

Three guesses, out loud, before any paint moves.

Blue and yellow, stirred together — what colour will be born?
Red and yellow? Red and blue?

Say your guesses. Remember them. This lesson lets your hands grade your guesses — with real paint, mixed in equal measure.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mixing primaries makes green, orange and purple

The rule is exact: when the primaries are mixed with each other **in equal measure**, they create **second-level colours — the secondary colours**.

[Picture 9166 — three overlapping circles: the primaries mixing into green, orange, purple]

The three births:
- **Blue + yellow = green**
- **Red + yellow = orange**
- **Red + blue = purple (ወይንጠጅ)**

Watch one birth happen:

[GIF 9186 — red and yellow paint stirred together into orange]

Now Activity 12 with your own hands: **mix primaries to create different colours**. Equal measure — the same amount of each parent — or the stronger parent wins and the child colour leans. Then Activity 13, the celebration: **mix colours and draw and colour flowers** with the colours you made.

## Sub lesson 2 — Primary, secondary and neutral colours (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide sets the mixing work inside its full colour framework, taught in its colour-in-culture chapter: **first-level (primary) colours, second-level (secondary) colours, and neutral colours**.

You now own two of the three levels by hand: the primaries you can name and find, and the secondaries you can MAKE. The neutral colours complete the framework in the guide's teaching — the quiet colours that are neither parent nor child of the mixing circle.

The guide pictures the same act this lesson does by hand: **primary paints mixing into secondary colours** — red and yellow swirling together into orange.

*(This part stands on teacher-guide evidence — it shows the framework the mixing sits in, and it is named as evidence here and in the sources.)*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Mix the primaries in equal measure and you make the secondary colours: blue and yellow make green, red and yellow make orange, red and blue make purple — ወይንጠጅ." Show them your three mixed colours as proof.

## Do It Yourself

1. Mix all three secondary colours. Paint one stripe of each and label it with its parents.
2. Draw and colour flowers using only colours you mixed yourself (Activity 13).
3. The book's own trap, answered by hand: mix red and yellow — did you get green? Say what you really got, and why the trap answer is wrong.
