# Lesson plan — Mixing Colours

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Mixing Colours
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** mixing the primary colours in equal measure makes the secondary colours: blue + yellow = green, red + yellow = orange, red + blue = purple (ወይንጠጅ).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Primary Colours — the learner knows the three pure colours; now their hands prove what the song promised: the primaries are parents. The teacher guide's primary/secondary/neutral framework is carried as evidence.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Primary Colours. Recommended, never forced — but mixing without the primaries is guessing.
- Next door: Green Around Us, where the first mixed colour is met in the wild.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Mix blue and yellow in equal measure and make green.
2. Mix red and yellow and make orange; mix red and blue and make purple (ወይንጠጅ).
3. Say what secondary (second-level) colours are, and name all three.
4. Mix colours and draw and colour flowers with them, as Activity 13 asks.

## What you need
Red, yellow and blue paints (or crayons to layer), a palette or plate for mixing, water and a brush if paints are used, and paper.

## If the learner is stuck
- The mix turns muddy → too much of one parent, or a dirty brush. Equal measure, clean brush, fresh puddles — the child colour needs fair parents.
- Cannot remember which pair makes green → chant it by picture: blue sky over yellow sun makes the green grass grow. The world is the memory aid.
