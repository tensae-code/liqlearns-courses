# Lesson plan — Bolivian Origin Arts

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Lesson:** Bolivian Origin Arts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bolivia's origin arts — Tiwanaku sculpture and origin architecture, and the aguayo weaving of the first peoples.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Origin architecture and Tiwanaku sculpture** — Bolivia's first building and its stone sculpture: the Ponce and Bennett monoliths, the Puerta del Sol, the Kalasasaya temple; Tiwanaku, flourishing between the 5th and 11th centuries; Andean architecture today; and adobe, the building material of the west (teacher compendium — evidence).
2. **The aguayo, featherwork and basketry** — The aguayo, one of the most used textiles of the culture, drawn and painted figure by figure; featherwork (plumarios), a ceremonial art bound to sacred activities; and palm-leaf basketry (cesteria) of the lake, river, rice and coffee communities.
3. **Pigments, rock art and the first cultures** — Pigments obtained from nature — eucalyptus, walnut, cochineal, coloured clays; rock art in Bolivia, more than 400 registered SIARB sites; the pre-Columbian cultures Wancarani, Chiripa, Tiwanaku and Uru Chipaya; and appreciating the art of the origin peoples.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
