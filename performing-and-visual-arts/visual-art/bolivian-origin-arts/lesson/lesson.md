# Bolivian Origin Arts

## Do it first

Name one thing from Bolivia that belongs in this lesson — a song, a dance, a pot, a carving, a cloth. Say where it comes from and who makes it. If you can name one, you are already inside the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Origin architecture and Tiwanaku sculpture

Bolivia's first building and its stone sculpture: the Ponce and Bennett monoliths, the Puerta del Sol, the Kalasasaya temple; Tiwanaku, flourishing between the 5th and 11th centuries; Andean architecture today; and adobe, the building material of the west (teacher compendium — evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — The aguayo, featherwork and basketry

The aguayo, one of the most used textiles of the culture, drawn and painted figure by figure; featherwork (plumarios), a ceremonial art bound to sacred activities; and palm-leaf basketry (cesteria) of the lake, river, rice and coffee communities.

## Sub lesson 3 — Pigments, rock art and the first cultures

Pigments obtained from nature — eucalyptus, walnut, cochineal, coloured clays; rock art in Bolivia, more than 400 registered SIARB sites; the pre-Columbian cultures Wancarani, Chiripa, Tiwanaku and Uru Chipaya; and appreciating the art of the origin peoples.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson holds, part by part, in your own words. Companion checks: did every part get named, with its Bolivian name kept?

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one named part of this lesson and find out one more true thing about it — from a person who knows it, or from the book your part came from.
2. Make or perform a small piece in the way of that part: a rhythm, a step, a drawing, a shaped piece of clay.
3. Write one sentence: what does this part show about Bolivia's own way of teaching its arts?
