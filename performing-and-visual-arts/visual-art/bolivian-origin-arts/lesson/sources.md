| Bolivia — Pre-Columbian art in Bolivia — the cultures in turn: Wancarani (from 1200 BC, La Paz and Oruro north of Lake Poopó, a village culture); Chiripa, among the oldest, along Lake Titicaca (its artificial mound of 50–60 metres, rectangular adobe rooms in a semicircle around a semi-subterranean temple, their double walls storing food, and a simple clay-and-sand ceramics among the first); Tiwanaku; and the Uru Chipaya of Oruro near Lake Coipasa, whose circular stone-and-mud huts with straw roofs, cone-shaped, answer the extreme climate, and whose ceramics work the clay (student book; original target `visual-art/bolivian-origin-arts`) | Book 5, printed pp. 31–33 |

| Bolivia — Adobe, the building material of the west — the originary architecture of the valleys and altiplano: adobe prepared by mixing mud with sand (sometimes straw added), emptied into rectangular moulds and dried in the sun to a solid consistency — the material par excellence for dwellings in western Bolivia (teacher compendium — evidence; original target `visual-art/bolivian-origin-arts`) | Book 6, PDF p. 73 |
| Bolivia — What the masks mean — in Bolivia's originary expressions the mask is the link with the ancestors, expressing potency and equilibrium between man and nature, an intermediary between the world of the gods and the world of men; and among the Chané, who use them only in the Arete ceremony — a feast attended by the living and the dead — the masks protect the living: no deceased relative, out of longing, can harm them or snatch a loved one's soul, and no participant is recognised (teacher compendium — evidence; original target `visual-art/bolivian-origin-arts`) | Book 6, PDF pp. 75, 79 |
| Bolivia — Featherwork (arte plumario) — the research establishes that feather art in Bolivia is bound to sacred activities, not only aesthetic ones: the feather is a symbol of prestige and social status, destined only for warriors, nobles or priests, its meaning authority and greatness (teacher compendium — evidence; original target `visual-art/bolivian-origin-arts`) | Book 6, PDF p. 75 |
