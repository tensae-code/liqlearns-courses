# Lesson plan — Lalibela: Churches Carved from One Rock

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Lalibela: Churches Carved from One Rock
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela: how they were carved — downward, from one whole rock — why they were carved into the ground, their eleven names, and the rock-cut works of the wider world.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the appreciation lessons (Sculpture and Historic Places, Culture and Heritage): the learner arrives knowing Ethiopia keeps ancient artworks. This lesson studies the greatest of them closely, in the Grade 7 book's own order.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The study opens — what we ask of Lalibela** — the task: the churches' artistic history and benefit, their building way, and whether the world holds their like.
2. **The wonder — a second Jerusalem** — the town; the visitor's two feelings; the Gedle Lalibela; Alvarez's confession.
3. **Carved downwards, not built upwards** — one whole rock; roof downward; sculpture's method; the iron tools; the suitable stone.
4. **Why carve churches into the ground?** — the book's three answers: eternity, honour to God, and possibly hiding.
5. **The eleven churches, named** — the full roll, in the book's order, ending with Bete Giyorgis.
6. **Rock-cut works of the world** — Egypt, India, Jordan — and what indigenous works everywhere express.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

- Recommended first: Sculpture and Historic Places (Visual Art) — Ethiopia's historic works, appreciated. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Cave Art and Church Painting (Visual Art) — the older rock-and-wall painting tradition. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell what the Lalibela study asks: the churches' history, benefit, building way and technique.
2. Tell the wonder account: the second Jerusalem, the Gedle Lalibela, and Alvarez's confession.
3. Explain how the churches were carved — from the roof downward, from one whole rock — and what made Lalibela's works stand apart.
4. Give the book's three reasons for carving into the ground.
5. Name the eleven churches in order.
6. Name the world's rock-cut comparisons the book gives: Egypt, India, Jordan.

## What you need
Paper, pencil, colours — and, for the Do It Yourself, a lump of clay or soft soap to carve downward.

## If the learner is stuck
- The names blur together → learn them in their places on the plate: five, then four, then two — and Bete Giyorgis, the cross, last and alone.
- Downward carving confuses → think of sculpture, not building: the church is freed from the stone, not stacked up from a foundation.
