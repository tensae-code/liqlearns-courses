# Lalibela: Churches Carved from One Rock — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number, as before. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — The study opens — what we ask of Lalibela
Pass mark: state the study's task: knowing the artistic history and benefit of the Lalibela rock-hewn churches, explain your understanding and appreciation of the story, the ancients' thinking and civilisation history, the churches' building way/process/inputs/technique — and whether similar works exist elsewhere in the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — The wonder — a second Jerusalem
Pass mark: place Lalibela (a town in North Wollo Zone, Amhara Region); tell the visitor's two feelings — regret and fresh wonder; name the Gedle Lalibela as the first written information and the king's aim (a second Jerusalem in Lasta); tell who Francisco Alvarez was and what he confessed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Carved downwards, not built upwards
Pass mark: explain that Lalibela's churches are carved from one whole great rock, cut from the roof downward — not raised from a foundation; compare the method to sculpture; name the tool kinds (hewing, prising, cutting); say what sets Lalibela apart (more than ten buildings in one area at nearly one time, quality, size, decoration) and that the local stone suited the work.

## Sub lesson 4 — Why carve churches into the ground?
Pass mark: give the book's three reasons — to make the buildings eternal; to do an astonishing thing showing honour and greatness to their God; possibly to hide the churches from an enemy's sight — and say that older rock-hewn churches in Lasta and northern Ethiopia show the craft is indigenous and centuries older than Lalibela.

## Sub lesson 5 — The eleven churches, named
Pass mark: name the eleven churches in the book's order, from Bete Medhanialem to Bete Giyorgis.

## Sub lesson 6 — Rock-cut works of the world
Pass mark: name the comparisons: Egypt — the tomb cut from one rock for Queen Hatshepsut; India — the Kailasa rock temple, among the world's largest; Jordan — the Khazneh temple; and say that indigenous artistic works express the community, culture, belief and civilisation of their place.
