| Sub lesson 1 — The Lalibela study, opener: knowing the artistic history and benefit of the Lalibela rock-hewn churches, explain how you understood and appreciate the story, the ancients' thinking and civilisation history, the churches' building way/process/inputs/technique, and whether similar works exist elsewhere in the world | Book 1, §4.3, printed pp. 135–136 |
| Sub lesson 2 — The wonder and the questions: Lalibela, a town in North Wollo Zone, Amhara Region; visitors feel regret or fresh wonder; among the world's astonishing events; the questions — how made, when, by whom, why; Ethiopian writers since at least the 15th century (the Gedle Lalibela, the king's life-book, first written information; the king intended a second Jerusalem in Lasta); foreign writers since the 16th; the Portuguese priest Francisco Alvarez (1520s, six years) described each building and confessed his wonder (paraphrased in the book); Figs. 5–6 church exterior and interior | Book 1, §4.3, printed pp. 136–137 |
| Sub lesson 3 — How the rock churches were carved: some rock buildings elsewhere began from natural caves; Lalibela's kind is carved from one whole great rock, below the ground or from a mountain's side; the work does NOT rise from a foundation — it is cut from the roof DOWNWARD; the method compares to sculpture (Michelangelo, paraphrased in the book: the form inside the block, freed by the sculptor); Lalibela made varied iron tools — for hewing, for prising, for cutting; what sets Lalibela apart — more than ten cut buildings in one area at nearly one time, with special craft: quality, size, cleverness of decoration; the local stone suitable — softening and hardening in the right measure | Book 1, §4.3, printed pp. 138–139 |
| Sub lesson 4 — Why carve churches into the ground?: (1) to make the buildings eternal; (2) to do an astonishing thing, showing honour and greatness to their God; (3) possibly to hide the churches from an enemy's sight in an era of frequent wars; rock-hewn churches believed older than Lalibela in Lasta and other parts of northern Ethiopia — the craft indigenous, practised for many centuries before Lalibela; the northern rock churches, obelisks, writing and carving crafts interwoven | Book 1, §4.3, printed pp. 139–141 |
| Sub lesson 5 — The eleven churches named: 1. Bete Medhanialem, 2. Bete Mariam, 3. Bete Denagel, 4. Bete Meskel, 5. Bete Debre Sina, 6. Bete Golgotha, 7. Bete Amanuel, 8. Bete Aba Libanos, 9. Bete Merqorios, 10. Bete Gebriel we Rufael, 11. Bete Giyorgis | Book 1, §4.3, printed p. 141 |
| Sub lesson 6 — Rock-cut works of the world: in Egypt, the tomb cut from one rock for Queen Hatshepsut; in India, the Kailasa rock temple, among the world's largest; in Jordan, the Khazneh temple; indigenous artistic works express the community, culture, belief and civilisation of their place, differing in purpose, input, method and era | Book 1, §4.3, printed p. 142 |

Honesty notes: The Alvarez and Michelangelo passages are taught as the book's own paraphrases, exactly as the placement records them — no quotation is invented. The Kailasa temple is named as the placement names it ("the Kailasa rock Buddhist temple"); the lesson repeats the placement's wording without adding a tradition of its own. The book's Lalibela photographs (Chapter 4 Figs. 4–12) are described, never copied; Picture 14202 is the builders' own schematic plate — a cross plan in a rock block, not a photograph or a survey map.
