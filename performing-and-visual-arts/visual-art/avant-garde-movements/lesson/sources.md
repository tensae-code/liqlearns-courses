| Venezuela — Expresionismo: Expressionism — reaction against naturalism, realism and impressionism: dramatic, gestural expression in free strokes and strong colour contrasts, on themes of society's decadence and the artists' emotional conflicts (Munch's Scream, Nolde) (student book) | Book 1, printed pp. 218–219 |
| Venezuela — Fauvismo: Fauvism — brilliant, intense, complementary colours and impulsive deformation; the critics named its painters fauves, wild beasts (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 219 |
| Venezuela — Cubismo: Cubism — objects fragmented into geometric surfaces and shown whole on one plane, with collage (Picasso's Guernica, Braque, Juan Gris) (student book) | Book 1, printed pp. 219–220 |
| Venezuela — Futurismo: Futurism — representing movement and speed, fragmenting form and colour (Boccioni's Unique Forms of Continuity in Space) (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 221 |
| Venezuela — Dadaísmo y el objeto encontrado: Dada — the radical movement questioning Western art and its museums, proposing chance and improvisation; and Duchamp's ready-made: an everyday industrial object (the Fountain urinal) placed in a new context as a work of art (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 222 |
| Venezuela — Surrealismo: Surrealism — expressing the world of the unconscious, dreams and imagination (Dalí's Premonition of Civil War) (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 222 |
| Venezuela — Abstracción lírica y abstracción geométrica: the two abstractions — lyrical abstraction, where the object is secondary to chromatic richness and simplification (Kandinsky), and geometric abstraction, simple geometric forms combined, reacting against the purely emotional (Mondrian) (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 223 |
| Venezuela — Pop art: Pop art — appropriating advertising images, fashion, comics and design into mass-produced art, against the consumer society it depicts: Andy Warhol's serigraphed Mona Lisa; Venezuela's Marisol Escobar (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 224 |
