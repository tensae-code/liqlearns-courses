# Avant-Garde Movements

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about this lesson's subject — avant-garde movements. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Expresionismo

Expressionism — reaction against naturalism, realism and impressionism: dramatic, gestural expression in free strokes and strong colour contrasts, on themes of society's decadence and the artists' emotional conflicts (Munch's Scream, Nolde).

## Sub lesson 2 — Fauvismo

Fauvism — brilliant, intense, complementary colours and impulsive deformation; the critics named its painters fauves, wild beasts.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cubismo

Cubism — objects fragmented into geometric surfaces and shown whole on one plane, with collage (Picasso's Guernica, Braque, Juan Gris).

## Sub lesson 4 — Futurismo

Futurism — representing movement and speed, fragmenting form and colour (Boccioni's Unique Forms of Continuity in Space).

## Sub lesson 5 — Dadaísmo y el objeto encontrado

Dada — the radical movement questioning Western art and its museums, proposing chance and improvisation; and Duchamp's ready-made: an everyday industrial object (the Fountain urinal) placed in a new context as a work of art.

## Sub lesson 6 — Surrealismo

Surrealism — expressing the world of the unconscious, dreams and imagination (Dalí's Premonition of Civil War).

## Sub lesson 7 — Abstracción lírica y abstracción geométrica

The two abstractions — lyrical abstraction, where the object is secondary to chromatic richness and simplification (Kandinsky), and geometric abstraction, simple geometric forms combined, reacting against the purely emotional (Mondrian).

## Sub lesson 8 — Pop art

Pop art — appropriating advertising images, fashion, comics and design into mass-produced art, against the consumer society it depicts: Andy Warhol's serigraphed Mona Lisa; Venezuela's Marisol Escobar.
