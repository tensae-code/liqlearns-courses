# Video script — Avant-Garde Movements

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Expresionismo.** Expressionism — reaction against naturalism, realism and impressionism: dramatic, gestural expression in free strokes and strong colour contrasts, on themes of society's decadence and the artists' emotional conflicts (Munch's Scream, Nolde).

**Fauvismo.** Fauvism — brilliant, intense, complementary colours and impulsive deformation; the critics named its painters fauves, wild beasts.

**Cubismo.** Cubism — objects fragmented into geometric surfaces and shown whole on one plane, with collage (Picasso's Guernica, Braque, Juan Gris).

**Futurismo.** Futurism — representing movement and speed, fragmenting form and colour (Boccioni's Unique Forms of Continuity in Space).

**Dadaísmo y el objeto encontrado.** Dada — the radical movement questioning Western art and its museums, proposing chance and improvisation; and Duchamp's ready-made: an everyday industrial object (the Fountain urinal) placed in a new context as a work of art.

**Surrealismo.** Surrealism — expressing the world of the unconscious, dreams and imagination (Dalí's Premonition of Civil War).

**Abstracción lírica y abstracción geométrica.** The two abstractions — lyrical abstraction, where the object is secondary to chromatic richness and simplification (Kandinsky), and geometric abstraction, simple geometric forms combined, reacting against the purely emotional (Mondrian).

**Pop art.** Pop art — appropriating advertising images, fashion, comics and design into mass-produced art, against the consumer society it depicts: Andy Warhol's serigraphed Mona Lisa; Venezuela's Marisol Escobar.

That is Avant-Garde Movements — Venezuela's own books teach it this way.
