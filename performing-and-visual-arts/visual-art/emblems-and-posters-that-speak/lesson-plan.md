# Lesson plan — Emblems and Posters That Speak

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Emblems and Posters That Speak
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** design that speaks at a distance — what an emblem (አርማ) is and its three kinds, the rules for designing one, and how posters are designed, used and made so a whole town can read them from far away.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Beautiful Writing and Photographs — the learner can already make beautiful letters and read pictures; this lesson joins letters and pictures into marks and posters that carry a message to strangers.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Emblems: marks that stand for something** — an emblem (አርማ) identifies an organisation, product or brand immediately; protected by trademark law; survey local marks first (Grade 5, printed pp. 130–131).
2. **The three emblem kinds, and the rules and steps** — text-only, picture-only and combined emblems; the rules — easy to identify, clear shrunk or enlarged, colours and letters clear and limited; the design steps (Grade 5, printed pp. 131–133).
3. **Posters that speak from far away** — poster design rules, uses and making steps; the three kinds — commercial, health, campaign (Grade 5, printed pp. 134–136).
4. **Reading signs and posters (teacher guide — evidence)** — reading the locality's direction signs and symbols and its posted notices with an artist's eye (Grade 5 teacher guide, Ch. 5 §5.2, syllabus tables).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Beautiful Writing and Photographs. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an emblem is and name its three kinds: text-only, picture-only, combined.
2. Give the emblem rules and follow the design steps.
3. Give the poster rules: few eye-catching colours, letters few, big and readable from afar, layout not fighting the picture.
4. Name poster uses and kinds, and follow the making steps.

## What you need
Paper, pencils, colours, and a walk past real posters and shop signs if one is possible.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Think of a mark you know at once, without reading a word — a shop's sign, a team's badge, a mark on a bottle. You recognised it in one second. Who taught you? Nobody: the mark did its work alone. That is the power this lesson teaches you to build. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- An emblem that fails at small size → simplify: the message must stay clear shrunk or enlarged; remove every detail that disappears.
- A poster nobody can read → count its words. Posters speak in few words, big letters, few colours.
