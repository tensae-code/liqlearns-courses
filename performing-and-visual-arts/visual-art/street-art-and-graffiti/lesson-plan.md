# Lesson plan — Street Art and Graffiti

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Street Art and Graffiti
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** graffiti is urban painting — lettering and wall work made as a public art form, art on the street that everyone passing can see.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of its title — recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Graffiti as urban painting** — lettering and wall work: the graffito made on the city's walls.
2. **A public art form** — street work is public — it stands where everyone can see it, and speaks to the whole street.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of the Visual Art title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what graffiti is: urban painting — lettering and wall work.
2. Say why graffiti is a public art form: it is made on walls in public space, for everyone passing to see.

## What you need
Paper and pencils or colours for your own lettering design.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks graffiti is only scribble → the book teaches it as urban painting: lettering and wall work composed as art.
- Thinks a wall picture is private like a sketchbook page → street work is public: its audience is everyone who passes.
