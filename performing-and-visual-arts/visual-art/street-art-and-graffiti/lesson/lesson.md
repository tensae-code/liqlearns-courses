# Street Art and Graffiti

## Do it first

Think of a wall in a town you know, covered in big coloured letters. Who made it? Who is it for — one person, or everyone who walks past? Hold that answer; this lesson names the art.

## Sub lesson 1 — Graffiti as urban painting

Albania's Grade 9 visual-art guide teaches **urban graffiti** as its own topic: graffiti is **urban painting** — **lettering and wall work**. The letters are not scribbled; they are composed — sized, shaped and coloured to work on the wall that carries them.

## Sub lesson 2 — A public art form

Graffiti's wall is not a private page. The guide places it as a **public art form**: the work stands in the city's shared space, and its viewers are all the people of the street. That public place is part of what the art is.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what is graffiti, and why is it public art? Companion checks: urban painting — lettering and wall work? On walls where everyone can see it?

## Do It Yourself

1. Design one word as graffiti lettering on paper, composed to fill the page.
2. Write one sentence: how is a wall painting different from a picture in a sketchbook?
3. Name a place near you where a public wall work would be seen by the most people, and say why.
