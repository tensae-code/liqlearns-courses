# Video script — Art Movements of the 19th Century

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Neoclasicismo.** Neoclassicism — serenity, balance and Greco-Roman canons revived after the Pompeii and Herculaneum excavations; line and proportion ruling painting (David's Coronation of Napoleon, Ingres); marble sculpture (Canova, Thorvaldsen); and academicism, the academies' rules imposed as the measure of taste.

**Romanticismo.** Romanticism — free, individual expression of deep feeling, liberty and equality against the neoclassical rules; dynamic, colourful painting of history, nature and tragedy (Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People, Géricault, Friedrich, Constable); heroic sculpture in violent movement (Rude); architecture borrowing the Gothic.

**Realismo.** Realism — painting everyday life as it is, against classic and romantic attitudes: Courbet's wheat sifters; and Goya before it, whose Fusilamientos del 3 de mayo and war engravings show the horrors of war with detailed crudeness.

**Naturalismo.** Naturalism, Realism's variant — open-air painting of social themes with free colour against academicism: Millet's peasants at field work, and Daumier's paintings, drawings and incisive social caricatures in oil, watercolour and lithography.

**Impresionismo.** Impressionism — landscapes painted outdoors, watching how changing sunlight alters colour; pure pigments juxtaposed so the eye mixes them at a distance (Monet; Pissarro, who learned landscape in Venezuela); and Seurat's pointillism, colour fractioned into small points.

**Postimpresionismo.** Postimpressionism — painters taking Impressionism's themes and techniques into freer, individual searches: Cézanne's synthesis of forms in great strokes, Van Gogh's passionate few-colour strokes, Gauguin's self-taught symbolism.

That is Art Movements of the 19th Century — Venezuela's own books teach it this way.
