# Hands-on — Art Movements of the 19th Century

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Neoclasicismo
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Neoclassicism — serenity, balance and Greco-Roman canons revived after the Pompeii and Herculaneum excavations; line and proportion ruling painting (David's Coronation of Napoleon, Ingres); marble sculpture (Canova, Thorvaldsen); and academicism, the academies' rules imposed as the measure of taste. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Romanticismo
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Romanticism — free, individual expression of deep feeling, liberty and equality against the neoclassical rules; dynamic, colourful painting of history, nature and tragedy (Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People, Géricault, Friedrich, Constable); heroic sculpture in violent movement (Rude); architecture borrowing the Gothic. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Realismo
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Realism — painting everyday life as it is, against classic and romantic attitudes: Courbet's wheat sifters; and Goya before it, whose Fusilamientos del 3 de mayo and war engravings show the horrors of war with detailed crudeness. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Naturalismo
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Naturalism, Realism's variant — open-air painting of social themes with free colour against academicism: Millet's peasants at field work, and Daumier's paintings, drawings and incisive social caricatures in oil, watercolour and lithography. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Impresionismo
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Impressionism — landscapes painted outdoors, watching how changing sunlight alters colour; pure pigments juxtaposed so the eye mixes them at a distance (Monet; Pissarro, who learned landscape in Venezuela); and Seurat's pointillism, colour fractioned into small points. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Postimpresionismo
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: Postimpressionism — painters taking Impressionism's themes and techniques into freer, individual searches: Cézanne's synthesis of forms in great strokes, Van Gogh's passionate few-colour strokes, Gauguin's self-taught symbolism. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.
