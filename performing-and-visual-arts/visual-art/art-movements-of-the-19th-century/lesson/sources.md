| Lesson part | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Venezuela — Neoclasicismo: Neoclassicism — serenity, balance and Greco-Roman canons revived after the Pompeii and Herculaneum excavations; line and proportion ruling painting (David's Coronation of Napoleon, Ingres); marble sculpture (Canova, Thorvaldsen); and academicism, the academies' rules imposed as the measure of taste (student book) | Book 1, printed pp. 176–179 |
| Venezuela — Romanticismo: Romanticism — free, individual expression of deep feeling, liberty and equality against the neoclassical rules; dynamic, colourful painting of history, nature and tragedy (Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People, Géricault, Friedrich, Constable); heroic sculpture in violent movement (Rude); architecture borrowing the Gothic (student book) | Book 1, printed pp. 180–182 |
| Venezuela — Realismo: Realism — painting everyday life as it is, against classic and romantic attitudes: Courbet's wheat sifters; and Goya before it, whose Fusilamientos del 3 de mayo and war engravings show the horrors of war with detailed crudeness (student book) | Book 1, printed pp. 182–183 |
| Venezuela — Naturalismo: Naturalism, Realism's variant — open-air painting of social themes with free colour against academicism: Millet's peasants at field work, and Daumier's paintings, drawings and incisive social caricatures in oil, watercolour and lithography (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 184 |
| Venezuela — Impresionismo: Impressionism — landscapes painted outdoors, watching how changing sunlight alters colour; pure pigments juxtaposed so the eye mixes them at a distance (Monet; Pissarro, who learned landscape in Venezuela); and Seurat's pointillism, colour fractioned into small points (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 185 |
| Venezuela — Postimpresionismo: Postimpressionism — painters taking Impressionism's themes and techniques into freer, individual searches: Cézanne's synthesis of forms in great strokes, Van Gogh's passionate few-colour strokes, Gauguin's self-taught symbolism (student book) | Book 1, printed p. 186 |
