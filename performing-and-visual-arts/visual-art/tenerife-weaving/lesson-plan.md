# Lesson plan — Tenerife Weaving

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Tenerife Weaving
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Tenerife weaving — the lace-like woven technique used for carpets and cloth medallions..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 9 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — what it teaches: this method was invented by the Spanish in the XVI century (бұл тәсілді XVI ғасырда испандықтар ойлап тапқан), and in South America it is called «бразилиялық шілтер» (Brazilian lace); a hexagonal motif is woven on a hexagonal wooden frame in eighteen photographed steps: the design diagram (a hexagonal snowflake-like motif of dots) and its coloured version (red and green) are shown; green threads are stretched across the frame's pegs in three directions, making a triangular grid over the whole hexagon; a red thread is then woven round and round the centre, building a star medallion, the crossings are secured, the finished hexagon is cut from the frame and its edges finished — a red-and-green hexagonal woven medallion
