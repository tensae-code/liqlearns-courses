# Beautiful Writing

## Do it first

Write your name as fast as you can. Now write it again — slowly, carefully, as beautifully as you can. Same letters, two writings. The second one is the beginning of this lesson: **calligraphy** — writing letters so beautifully that the writing itself is a picture.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beautiful letter forms

Calligraphy (ኪነ-ፅህፈት) is the study of improving the shape and look of letters — putting the letters' shape down beautifully, decorated and adorned, on parchment, paper or other material. It works by methods you will meet one by one: **single** writing, **double** (outlined) writing, **shade and light**, and **ornament and pattern**. To study it, one must know line types and directions — letters are built from lines. Beautiful writing holds a high place wherever writing must stand out: in graphics, commercial drawing, advertising and posters, display and title writing, where letters are combined with drawing and ornaments.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lines first

Before letters, lines. To write fidel one must practise drawing lines freely. The line types have names:

- **vertical** (ቋሚ) — standing straight up and down
- **horizontal** (አግድም) — lying flat across
- **diagonal** (ሠያፍ) — slanting
- **curved** (ጎባጣ) — bending round
- **zigzag / uneven** (ወጣ ገባ) — up-down, up-down
- **long** and **short**

[Picture 9314 — the line kinds: vertical, horizontal, diagonal, curved, zigzag, long and short — and fidel built from lines]

Practise the line kinds repeatedly — freehand, and with a ruler — until length and size come out true. Every letter you will ever beautify is hiding inside these lines.

## Sub lesson 3 — Amharic fidel, single and double

Amharic letters have their own shape, size and writing order — practise them repeatedly from the model. In **single** writing (ነጠላ), the letter is drawn with one line, clean and plain. In **double** writing (ድርብ), the letter is outlined — drawn as a shape with an inside and an outside, ready to be filled or coloured.

Practise writing Amharic fidel in pencil from the model, in single writing first. Fidel can also be written in varied line styles — one chosen fidel, drawn with different lines, is a whole page of practice. Show your teacher your best one.

## Sub lesson 4 — English letters, single and double

English letters obey the same two writings. Practise writing them in **single** and **double** from the model — then write the double writings and colour them in an enjoyable colour. Capitals sit evenly on the line, with equal side-thickness. Small letters have rising heads and descending feet, each by its shape. Even the numerals have their own writing order — practise them from the example too.

## Sub lesson 5 — Shade, ornament and your name

Now the decorations. Letters can be written as **form or block** letters, in varied styles and classes. They can carry **light and shade** — one side bright, one side dark, so the letter stands up off the page. They can be decorated **with leaves** and ornaments. The artist **Yigezu Bisrat** prepared varied decorated letters beyond normal writing, used in harag works — proof that a letter can be a whole artwork.

Your practice set: write letters in single composition in your notebook; write letters by tying or doubling two writing tools together; make light and shade on letters; write **your name** in varied decorated letters; and write ሰላም (peace) **LARGE** in the notebook.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the five line kinds by their Amharic names — ቋሚ, አግድም, ሠያፍ, ጎባጣ, ወጣ ገባ — drawing each in the air as you say it. Then ask them: single or double — which writing would you choose for a poster title, and why?

## Do It Yourself

1. Practise one page: the line kinds, freehand first, then with a ruler, until they come out true.
2. Write three Amharic fidel in single writing, then the same three in double writing, and colour the double ones.
3. Write your name in decorated letters — shade, ornament, leaves, anything — and write ሰላም large beneath it.
