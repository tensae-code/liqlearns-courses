# Artists and Art of Saudi Arabia

## Sub lesson 1 — Artists from my country

Saudi Arabia's books introduce the country's artists to its youngest pupils: Salah Bashrahil of Jeddah, whose fish is formed from doughs and pastes; Abdul Halim Radwi, Munira Mosly and Saad Al-Mis'ari; Mohammed Al-Salim, who painted the desert's golden dunes; and the fine artists who showed the Kingdom's paintings in world exhibitions. A child who meets them is told: you too can become an artist.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nature and heritage in Saudi paintings

Saudi painters draw their works from the country's own nature — the Nafud desert, Tabuk's mountains, the Red Sea, Abha's farm terraces — using the formal elements of line, colour and texture. They paint in every kind of colour: pastel, oil, and the watercolours, and their subjects are the society, the heritage and the land around them, from Al-Qatt folk houses to the palm tree.

## Sub lesson 3 — The homeland's story in paintings

Saudi art tells the homeland's story: King Abdulaziz's unification of the Kingdom, painted by Fahd Al-Rubaiq and others; the development of the Kingdom toward Vision 2030 in paintings and in Diya Aziz's sculptures; the National Day each 23 September, expressed in paintings and in digital art; and the national competition "The Homeland in Our Children's Eyes", where pupils' own paintings carry the story on.
