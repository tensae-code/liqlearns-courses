This lesson is Product Design.

Product design — designing useful things: function and beauty, improving and innovating products, furniture design and production. — as China's school books teach it: Product design as function wedded to beauty — the drinking cup analysed: handles for grip and against heat, lids for warmth and dust (a flip-cap for carrying, the gaiwan's lid that strains tea leaves against the coffee set's saucer sized for sugar and spoon); beauty of form, of material (bamboo plain, ceramic kind, glass clear), of craft (hammered silver against machine work); how products evolve — the door handle's life: the Chinese pull-ring that also knocks, handle-and-lock sets, then fingerprint, sensor and voice locks that may abolish the handle; new materials and processes (CNC machining, 3D printing); styles cycling — minimalism, retro, functionalism, New Chinese; designing for differing, changing needs — the wardrobe that always lacks one piece; a family of lamps for work, bedside, ceiling; a "growth stool" that serves a child across ages; and age-friendly design for the elderly — walkers, stool-canes, accessible bathrooms, big-button phones; the design process through fashion — research, inspiration, brainstorm, concept board, sketches, pattern-making (flat or draped on the stand), sample, revision; remaking fabric (pleating — Issey Miyake's permanent pleats pressed between sheets of Xuan paper; tie-dye, embroidery, cutwork); and one-piece cutting with zero waste — the wrapped sari and toga, the holed poncho, Vionnet's bias cut.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
