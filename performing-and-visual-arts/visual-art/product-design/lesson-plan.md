# Lesson plan — Product Design

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Product Design
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Product design — designing useful things: function and beauty, improving and innovating products, furniture design and production.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China HS Design** — Product design as function wedded to beauty — the drinking cup analysed: handles for grip and against heat, lids for warmth and dust (a flip-cap for carrying, the gaiwan's lid that strains tea leaves against the coffee set's saucer sized for sugar and spoon); beauty of form, of material (bamboo plain, ceramic kind, glass clear), of craft (hammered silver against machine work); how products evolve — the door handle's life: the Chinese pull-ring that also knocks, handle-and-lock sets, then fingerprint, sensor and voice locks that may abolish the handle; new materials and processes (CNC machining, 3D printing); styles cycling — minimalism, retro, functionalism, New Chinese; designing for differing, changing needs — the wardrobe that always lacks one piece; a family of lamps for work, bedside, ceiling; a "growth stool" that serves a child across ages; and age-friendly design for the elderly — walkers, stool-canes, accessible bathrooms, big-button phones; the design process through fashion — research, inspiration, brainstorm, concept board, sketches, pattern-making (flat or draped on the stand), sample, revision; remaking fabric (pleating — Issey Miyake's permanent pleats pressed between sheets of Xuan paper; tie-dye, embroidery, cutwork); and one-piece cutting with zero waste — the wrapped sari and toga, the holed poncho, Vionnet's bias cut
2. **China HS Crafts** — Making a splayed-leg stool — cut the stock with allowance, true two reference faces and mark them, set the 5:1 splay with an adjustable bevel, gauge the mortise lines to the chisel's width, chop the angled mortises, cut and trim the tenons, and assemble dry — no glue — then plane and sand; why tenoned furniture outlasts, travels and repairs better than nailed
3. **China HS Modern Media Art** — Makers and prototypes — Xu Bing's Phoenix built from construction debris with pliers and drills; a prototype turns an abstract idea into a thing users can touch and judge: sculpt the mouse in clay to fit your hand, paper-prototype a phone interface and wire its jumps, test, and iterate at the lowest cost and fastest speed
