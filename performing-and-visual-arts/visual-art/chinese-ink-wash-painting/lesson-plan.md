# Lesson plan — Chinese Ink Wash Painting

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Chinese Ink Wash Painting
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Chinese ink wash painting — playing and working in ink and coloured ink, the water-and-ink art of China.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China Grade 3 Art Vol.2** — Ink-wash play — the tools (ink stick, xuan paper, brush, inkstone, seal) and ink's tones from burnt to clear; painting with dark/light and wet/dry ink changes, after Cui Zifan and Zhang Daqian's splashed-ink landscape
2. **China Grade 4 Art Vol.1** — Colour-and-ink on xuan paper — ink's density and wetness, colours blending and crossing, brush speed and pressure making rich effects; painting a lively colour-ink picture (Huang Yongyu, Chu Teh-Chun shown)
3. **China Grade 5 Art Vol.1** — Appreciating Qi Baishi's flower-and-bird painting — the modern master whose bold, moist brushwork, vivid colour and simple lively forms made a new style in Chinese painting; Chinese flower-and-bird painting — brush loading in steps (tip in water, point in thick ink, sweep on xuan paper) and painting birds and flowers with brush, ink and Chinese colours, after Lin Fengmian, Wang Xuetao and Pan Tianshou
4. **China Grade 5 Art Vol.2** — Freehand (xieyi) vegetables and fruit — between likeness and unlikeness: ink first, colour second, colour laid by the form's kind; ink's five tones, breaking ink while wet, the one-wave-three-folds stroke (Xugu's vegetable album shown); freehand animals — "strength through the paper's back": centred and side brush, following and reverse strokes, pauses and turns; Xu Beihong's galloping horse, Qi Baishi's squirrel and grapes, and a squirrel built up from ink shapes step by step; painting the pine — trunk begun at the "neck", branches spreading four ways with front-back covering, bark in fish-scale texture strokes; needles in fan, wheel or horsetail clusters grouped like the character 品， dry trunk first, then needles light before dark, layered
5. **China Grade 6 Art Vol.1** — A colour-ink homeland — painting your home scenery in ink and colour: trees by the deer-antler method (branches reaching up) or the crab-claw method (branches pressing down), leaves dotted three ways, accent figures placed small and half-hidden; ink dark and light for near and far
6. **China HS Painting** — Form the Chinese way — Qiu Ying's Red Cliff leaves most of the silk blank yet the river and mist feel full (reserved white, liubai); Liang Kai's Splashed-Ink Immortal lets an arm vanish into pale ink and still reads; in Chinese painting the idea (xieyi, "writing the idea") outranks the copy
7. **China HS Chinese Painting & Calligraphy** — The Four Treasures both arts share — brushes hard (wolf hair, for outline), soft (goat hair, for washes) and mixed, long/medium/short tips; ink in pine-soot and oil-soot, its "five colours" (burnt, dense, heavy, pale, clear) and dry/wet; Xuan paper raw (thirsty, for xieyi) and sized (for gongbi), a UNESCO-listed craft; the inkstone's grinding and ink-releasing; ink methods — breaking ink into wet ink, piling ink layer on layer, splashing ink, overnight ink; trees branching four ways, leaves dotted or double-outlined; water drawn in line alone (Ma Yuan's twelve studies of water) or left blank for stillness; freehand flower-and-bird painting — Xu Wei's ten-metre Assorted Flowers scroll, painted with calligraphic flying-white at one go; Qi Baishi's rule that painting is wonderful "between likeness and unlikeness — too like is vulgar, too unlike is cheating"; Zhu Da's perilous rock and lone water-bird read against his life; freehand figure painting in steps — begin at the brows and eyes, nose, face outline in pale ink; collar and sleeves with square "broken-reed" or round "wandering-silk" folds; set the figure in its scene (the scholar by the stream with staff); light colour on face and hands; finish with inscription and seal — Liang Kai, Qi Baishi's Fisherman and Li Keran's Viewing a Painting as models
