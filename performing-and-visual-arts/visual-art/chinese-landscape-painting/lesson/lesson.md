# Chinese Landscape Painting

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about chinese landscape painting. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — China Grade 1 Art Vol.1

Chinese ink landscape painting (山水画) of the homeland's mountains and rivers by Qian Songyan, Bai Xueshi and Liu Haisu

## Sub lesson 2 — China Grade 6 Art Vol.1

Mountains and waters — Chinese landscape painting (mature by Sui-Tang, the chief genre after Song-Yuan) and its texture strokes （皴法）: hemp-fibre, axe-cut, rolling-cloud and rain-dot strokes; painting a stone in four steps — outline, texture by its structure, rubbing, dyeing — and water with a dragged brush

## Sub lesson 3 — China HS Chinese Painting & Calligraphy

Painting rocks and mountains — a rock has three faces; the texture strokes (cun) invented for different geologies — hemp-fibre, axe-cut, unravelled-rope, folded-ribbon, raindrop, lotus-leaf; and the four stages of a mountain: outline, texture, wash, dot the moss; landscape space the Chinese way — Guo Xi's Three Distances (high distance looking up, deep distance looking through, level distance looking across), each with its own colour and mood, and "viewing the large as small": the painter holds the whole in the heart and paints what is known and imagined, not only what is seen; inheriting and renewing — Shitao's "brush and ink must follow the times"; Li Keran's Myriad Red Hills built by accumulated ink until red and black answer each other; Pan Tianshou painting lotus with splashed ink, finger-dyeing and fingertip lines; the lotus painted step by step

## Sub lesson 4 — China Senior Art Appreciation

Chinese traditional landscape painting — the realm beyond the image
