# Lesson plan — Chinese Landscape Painting

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Chinese Landscape Painting
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Chinese landscape painting — the shanshui tradition of mountains and water.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China Grade 1 Art Vol.1** — Chinese ink landscape painting (山水画) of the homeland's mountains and rivers by Qian Songyan, Bai Xueshi and Liu Haisu
2. **China Grade 6 Art Vol.1** — Mountains and waters — Chinese landscape painting (mature by Sui-Tang, the chief genre after Song-Yuan) and its texture strokes （皴法）: hemp-fibre, axe-cut, rolling-cloud and rain-dot strokes; painting a stone in four steps — outline, texture by its structure, rubbing, dyeing — and water with a dragged brush
3. **China HS Chinese Painting & Calligraphy** — Painting rocks and mountains — a rock has three faces; the texture strokes (cun) invented for different geologies — hemp-fibre, axe-cut, unravelled-rope, folded-ribbon, raindrop, lotus-leaf; and the four stages of a mountain: outline, texture, wash, dot the moss; landscape space the Chinese way — Guo Xi's Three Distances (high distance looking up, deep distance looking through, level distance looking across), each with its own colour and mood, and "viewing the large as small": the painter holds the whole in the heart and paints what is known and imagined, not only what is seen; inheriting and renewing — Shitao's "brush and ink must follow the times"; Li Keran's Myriad Red Hills built by accumulated ink until red and black answer each other; Pan Tianshou painting lotus with splashed ink, finger-dyeing and fingertip lines; the lotus painted step by step
4. **China Senior Art Appreciation** — Chinese traditional landscape painting — the realm beyond the image
