# Lesson plan — Chinese Calligraphy

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Chinese Calligraphy
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Chinese calligraphy — the brush art of characters, its rules and methods, born from the same root as painting.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China HS Chinese Painting & Calligraphy** — Calligraphy and painting are one root — Zhang Yan-yuan's "calligraphy and painting were one body, not yet divided"; Zhao Mengfu's verse (paint a rock like flying-white script, bamboo with the eight calligraphy strokes); the two arts share origin, tools, brush method and taste — China's unique paired art; the method of regular script — the five-finger grip with hollow palm ("fingers solid, palm empty, wrist level"); the three wrist positions (rested for small script, raised, suspended for large); centre-tip movement, lift and press; a stroke's three stages (entry, travel, finish); practising the basic strokes — dot, horizontal, vertical, sweep, press, turn, hook, pick; building characters and composing a work — single characters balanced yet varied, never mechanically even; composite characters by structure (top-bottom, left-right, enclosing, triple-layered), each with its fitting rule (the frame fitted to what it encloses, horizontals lengthening downward for stability); then the layout (zhangfa) of the whole piece
