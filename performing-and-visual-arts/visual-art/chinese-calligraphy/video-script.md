This lesson is Chinese Calligraphy.

Chinese calligraphy — the brush art of characters, its rules and methods, born from the same root as painting. — as China's school books teach it: Calligraphy and painting are one root — Zhang Yan-yuan's "calligraphy and painting were one body, not yet divided"; Zhao Mengfu's verse (paint a rock like flying-white script, bamboo with the eight calligraphy strokes); the two arts share origin, tools, brush method and taste — China's unique paired art; the method of regular script — the five-finger grip with hollow palm ("fingers solid, palm empty, wrist level"); the three wrist positions (rested for small script, raised, suspended for large); centre-tip movement, lift and press; a stroke's three stages (entry, travel, finish); practising the basic strokes — dot, horizontal, vertical, sweep, press, turn, hook, pick; building characters and composing a work — single characters balanced yet varied, never mechanically even; composite characters by structure (top-bottom, left-right, enclosing, triple-layered), each with its fitting rule (the frame fitted to what it encloses, horizontals lengthening downward for stability); then the layout (zhangfa) of the whole piece.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
