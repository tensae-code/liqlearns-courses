## Sub lesson 1 — Palm-frond weaving and heritage textiles

The palm is a symbol of the people of Saudi Arabia, and its fronds were a craft: the people of Madinah wove mats (الحصر) and baskets (القفاف) from palm fronds. Beside it stand the old folk textiles — the tent, the shamla floor covering, bags for dates and grains, camel gear — woven from the environment's natural wool after dyeing, usually by women, in simple geometric units and the colours red, black, green and yellowish-white.

## Sub lesson 2 — The pottery craft and its cities

Pottery is one of the Kingdom's very old inherited crafts, with no fixed beginning, and every region has its own stamp. The potter Sayyid Ali Al-Gharash tells the clay's journey — lumps brought from Al-Hofuf, crushed, soaked, kneaded with the feet with white sand, kept under burlap — and the famous pottery cities are named region by region: Tabuk, Tayma, Al-Jouf, Al-Ula and Haql in the north; Najran, Jazan, Asir and Al-Baha in the south; Jubail, Khobar, Qatif, Al-Ahsa and Dammam in the east; Makkah, Madinah, Jeddah and Al-Qunfudhah in the west; Riyadh, Diriyah, Qassim, Kharj and Al-Aflaj in the centre.

## Sub lesson 3 — Metals of the Kingdom

Saudi Arabia is endowed with gold, silver, copper, iron, lead and tin. The Muslim artist excelled in metal arts — jewellery, swords, the gilding of Qur'an copies — and the most prominent creation in gold is the Door of the Holy Kaaba, made by skilled Saudi technicians from pure gold. The noblest weaving stands beside it: the Kiswa of the Kaaba, pure silk dyed black, its verses embroidered with gold threads.
