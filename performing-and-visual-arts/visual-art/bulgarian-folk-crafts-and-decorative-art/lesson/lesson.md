# Bulgarian Folk Crafts and Decorative Art

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Технологии в декоративно-приложните изкуства (Technologies in the Decorative-Applied Arts) — part one

decorative-applied art creates objects of artistic, utilitarian and aesthetic purpose, from the decoration of buildings, streets, parks and squares to the artistic working of household objects; its foundations lie in the traditional crafts and Bulgarian folk creativity — the artistic crafts (woodwork, weaving, embroidery, coppersmithing, pottery, braid-making, pack-saddle making, knife-making and others), characterised by handmade production by old technology, some practised for centuries, others revived or newly popularised in limited regions, learned through the apprenticeship system in which youths perfected their skills with a master for years before opening their own activity;

## Sub lesson 2 — Технологии в декоративно-приложните изкуства (Technologies in the Decorative-Applied Arts) — part two

the folk applied arts' specific feature — the unity of the utilitarian and the artistic, of use and beauty, function and decoration, the object's function determining its construction; decoration determined by the material (cloth, wood, stone, clay, glass, metal) and realised as sculptural-relief, painted, engraved, woven work carrying ornaments and motifs (trees, birds, animals, plants, points, lines, dashes, geometric figures), the form itself sometimes a pure, refined shape stressing the material's structure and colour; the making of a decorative work in two stages — design (проектиране) and execution in material — with traditions kept today in carving, carpet-making, embroidery and textiles (the carved wooden ceiling of the Gyokova House in Koprivshtitsa, Bulgarian embroidery and embroidery on contemporary clothing, the Chiprovtsi carpet, Troyan ceramics, and today's Bulgarian ceramics, textile, glass and Tryavna-school woodcarving by pupils)
