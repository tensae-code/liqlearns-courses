| Kazakhstan — 1.11 Граттаж. Техника және материалдар (1.11 Grattage. Technique and materials) — what it teaches: grattage (граттаж) — from the French word gratter — «қыру, тырнау» (to scrape, to scratch) — is a method of drawing a picture on paper or cardboard covered with ink (тушь) or black paint by scratching with a sharp instrument; the technique's other name — воскография (waxography); sometimes it is also called «тырнама» (scratch); pictures drawn in the grattage technique are distinguished by the contrast of white lines and black background and resemble engravings (гравюраларға ұқсайды); and if the paper sheet is painted in varied colours beforehand, the picture comes out very attractive and unusual; grattage can be made positive or negative — in the first case a light background is drawn (the most spread technique), in the second the picture is scratched, and the background is light coloured; read, interesting!: pictures drawn by scratching with a stone's piece on a cliff can be considered the grattage technique's first samples; archaeologists also found pictures drawn with fingers on cave walls' clay surface; monumental sgraffito (сграффито) samples mastered the Greek masters' decorative finishing technique several centuries ago; sgraffito (сграффито) is one of the types of scratching a picture or pattern on plaster of varied colours laid in layers; what is needed for grattage: for drawing a picture in the grattage technique these materials are needed: thick paper or a sheet of cardboard; black ink or black gouache; a paraffin candle, oil pastel or wax crayon; a wide brush with soft bristles; a toothpick, a paper-knife (қағаз қырғыш) or any sharp instrument; try working with grattage: the order of carrying out the work in the grattage technique in steps: 1. stick the sheet on the desk (фанерге) with sticky tape; 2. cover the sheet's surface with oil pastel of varied colours; the colours must be bright and saturated: for example green, yellow, red and blue; alongside, light and dark colours must be chosen; 3. cover the coloured layer fully with black gouache or ink for colour; to mix the gouache, a piece of soap or a drop of PVA glue is added; so the gouache layer lies evenly; but in some places the ink can gather in drops; therefore protect the picture from dust falling on the ink; 4. the paper's surface must fully dry from the ink or gouache; before starting work the surface must not stick to the hands or crumble; if the oil layer gives off pigment, the picture can be covered with soap — so it will be easy to draw the picture; 5. draw the picture's contours with a pencil on the prepared basis; only the main contours and visible parts are drawn; 6. start the most interesting work!; scratch the drawn contours with a sharp instrument; if the paint layer is thick, the picture comes out easy and light; at drawing in the grattage technique, show how varied colours appear from under the paint; the picture's most beautiful colours are shown by the lines' thickness (student book; original target `visual-art/grattage-scratch-drawing`) | Book 9, printed p. 22 |
