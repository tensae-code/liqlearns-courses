# Lesson plan — Master Painters of Pakistan

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Master Painters of Pakistan
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pakistan's master painters, honoured by name in the country's own Class 9 book: Haji Muhammad Sharif, Abdul Rahman Chughtai, Allah Baksh, Shakir Ali, Sadequain, Zahoor-Ul-Akhlaq and Anna Molka Ahmad.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The earlier masters** — Haji Muhammad Sharif; Abdul Rahman Chughtai; Allah Baksh
2. **Shakir Ali and Sadequain** — two masters whose names also stand among Pakistan's master calligraphers
3. **Zahoor-Ul-Akhlaq and Anna Molka Ahmad** — the list completed — seven masters in all

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Visual Art lessons of this course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name all seven master painters of Pakistan from the book's list.
2. Tell where the list stands in the book: in the History of Painting in South Asia chapter, as its Pakistan section.
3. Name the two masters who also appear among the master calligraphers.
