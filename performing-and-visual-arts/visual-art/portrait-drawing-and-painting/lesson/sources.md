and the large-mass characteristic (đặc điểm khối lớn) is the overall structural characteristic of a mass form — in a portrait statue, the large mass is the outstanding shape of the head mass, neck mass, lower body or the statue plinth, the large mass containing the smaller secondary masses and lying in the whole large mass (for example, the head mass includes the skull mass and the face mass); in portrait statues, the large masses have the characteristics usually attributed to the following identifying tendencies — masses with a square tendency (khối có xu hướng vuông) are the masses with a box-shaped structure, the mass's area created by the square edges and corners, masses with this characteristic appearing much on male portrait statues, because by the gender characteristic male portrait statues usually have more edges and corners than female ones, so the large masses such as the skull mass, neck or shoulders and the smaller part masses such as chin, nose or mouth usually have the tendency to identify into more square edges and corners (Alberto Giacometti, "Head" (Cái đầu), 1934, plaster statue); and masses with a round tendency (khối có xu hướng tròn) are the masses with a spherical structure, appearing little or without clear square edges and corners, masses with this tendency easily recognised on female portrait statues because on the original model there are usually more curved lines, and on the face, neck, shoulders or the smaller details such as eyes, nose and mouth there are also usually these characteristics (Nguyễn Thị Kim, "Chân dung cháu gái" (Portrait of a Granddaughter), 1958, bronze statue); and in some cases both identifying characteristics above appear in one portrait statue — then the square-tendency mass combines harmoniously and delicately with the round-tendency mass, this combination seen clearly at the portrait statues of characters at adolescent age, because in the physical development stage the face usually has these characteristics (student book;
