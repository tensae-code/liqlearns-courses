# Lesson plan — Micro Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Micro Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Micro art — the microscope in the artist's and photographer's hands, and nature's own micro-drawings made visible..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

9. **Kazakhstan Grade 7 NIS Girls Artistic Labour 2025 teacher guide** — what it teaches: the guide's factual material — microorganisms (microbes) are organisms of animal and plant origin visible only under strong optical magnification; Robert Hooke (1635–1703), physicist, botanist, inventor and astronomer, who in 1665 published the treatise «Микрография» describing in detail the ways of using the microscope for varied scientific research, its pages full of illustrations and engravings depicting parts of plants, insects and animals; the Dutch naturalist Antoni van Leeuwenhoek (1632–1723), one of the founders of scientific microscopy and the microscope's constructor, whose reading of Hooke's treatise pushed him to study the surrounding nature; Ernst Haeckel (1834–1919), the German naturalist and philosopher who studied and described organisms, made sketches and watercolour drafts of the objects studied, his book «Красота форм в природе», in which nature's perfection is depicted, greatly influencing the art and architecture of the early 20th century; Zachary Kopfer, the contemporary microbiologist who also creates drawings with the help of living microorganisms — he created portraits of many famous people: Leonardo da Vinci, Charles Darwin, Pablo Picasso and others; what it teaches: the guide's factual material — microphotography is photography got with the help of microscope photo-equipment; a bright representative of this kind of creativity is the British photographer Professor Rob Kesseler, who imprints seemingly insignificant exotic microflora in the smallest details, science and art tightly interwoven in his works; inspired by medieval Dutch flower still lifes, he works in close cooperation with scientists-microbiologists, and for studying the creative potential of microphotography a wide collection of images of plant parts appeared in a high-technology laboratory at the junction of science and art — pollen, seeds, fruits and leaves; Kesseler reveals to us the captivating mystery of the smallest parts of the organic world, a mystery of nature whose wealth, complexity and unusually pure beauty cannot be discerned by the naked eye; the British artist Anna Dumitriu chose bacteria as the centre of her creativity — her works attract attention to the global problem of the growth and mutation of bacteria resistant to antibiotics; from fabric on a special technology the artist creates women's dresses, cushions, curtains and other interior objects, using dangerous bacteria in her work while applying standard protection measures and then thoroughly disinfecting the finished products; also study the microphotographs of bacteria made by young scientists of Nazarbayev University
