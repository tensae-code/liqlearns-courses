# Lesson plan — Elements of Design in 3D Arts

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Elements of Design in 3D Arts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what the elements of design do in three-dimensional art: dimension itself, form and function, ergonomics, mass and space, time and motion — and line and shape carried into 3D. Teacher-guide evidence throughout, labelled. A slim lesson, as its evidence is slim.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Elements and Principles of Design. This lesson is that system's 3D step, carried from the Grade 8 teacher guide as labelled evidence — short, because the guide's surviving text for it is short.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **What 3D means — form and function, ergonomics (teacher guide — evidence)** — dimension itself; a made thing's form serving its function; the fit to the human hand.
2. **Mass and space, time and motion (teacher guide — evidence)** — the clay and the emptiness it carries; the viewer who moves.
3. **Line, shape, and the rest — in three dimensions (teacher guide — evidence)** — line made of points, dynamic, activating space; shape, geometric or organic; the remaining elements applied to 3D work.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Elements and Principles of Design (Visual Art) — the elements in their 2D home. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, from the guide's evidence, what dimension/3D means and name the topics the guide joins to it.
2. Explain form and function, and ergonomics, as the guide names them.
3. Say how line and shape work when the art is three-dimensional.

## What you need
A clay vessel or any solid made thing to hold, turn, and look into.
