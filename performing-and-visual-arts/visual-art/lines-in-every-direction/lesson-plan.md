# Lesson plan — Lines in Every Direction

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Lines in Every Direction
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a line can go in every direction — across, down, slanting and curved — and repeated lines become patterns: zigzag, wave and rows of loops.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Marks and Scribbles — the hand is loose; now it learns to steer. The Grade 1 teacher guide's line lesson (warming up the hand, drawing lines and colouring) is carried as teacher-guide evidence.

## Sub lessons
1. **Lines that go across, down and slanting** — the Grade 1 book's full-page line models in colour — horizontal lines, diagonal lines and vertical strokes — to copy.
2. **Curved lines and U shapes** — the curved U-shaped stroke models of the same Grade 1 pages — the line that bends.
3. **Patterns: zigzag, wave and loops** — zigzag and wavy overlapping lines and rows of overlapping oval loops (Grade 1, printed pp. 25–28), with Exercise 5 — move the pencil in different directions and draw many different lines again and again.
4. **The teacher's line lesson (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 1 teacher guide's line lesson — warming up the hand, drawing lines and colouring, opened by its own question: "What is a line?" Cited as teacher-guide evidence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Marks and Scribbles — a loose hand steers better. Recommended, never forced.
- Next door: Shapes You Can Draw, where lines join up and close into shapes.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw horizontal, vertical and diagonal lines, and curved U-shaped strokes, following the book's full-page colour models.
2. Draw zigzag lines, wavy lines and rows of overlapping oval loops.
3. Keep the pencil on the paper through a zigzag and then a wave without lifting.
4. Say, with the teacher guide's question, what a line is — and warm the hand up before drawing lines.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil or crayon. A ruler is NOT needed — these lines are drawn free, by the hand the scribble loosened.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lines wobble badly → slow down and draw BIG; a big line is easier to steer than a small one. The wobble fades with pages, not with worry.
- Lifts the pencil inside a pattern → make it a game: the pencil is a boat and the paper is water — the boat may not fly. Start with a short zigzag of four teeth.
- Mixes up the direction names → point with the whole arm as you say each word: across (arm flat), down (arm drops), slanting (arm like a hill), curved (arm bends like a smile).
