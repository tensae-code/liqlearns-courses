# Lines in Every Direction


## Do it first

Look at the four model lines in this lesson. Copy each one on your paper — the flat one, the standing one, the slanting one, the curved one.

Do not worry if yours wobbles. The wobble is your hand learning.

Now — which way did each line go? This lesson names every direction a line can travel, and then turns lines into patterns.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lines that go across, down and slanting

The book teaches lines as full pages of colour models, one direction at a time: **horizontal** lines running across, **vertical** strokes standing up and down, and **diagonal** lines slanting like a hillside.

[Picture 9151 — line models: across, down, slanting and curved]

Copy each model under itself. Go slowly. A line is one journey of the pencil from its start to its end.

Watch the journeys happen one by one:

[GIF 9181 — a pencil drawing lines stroke by stroke]

Your turn: one page, four journeys — across, down, slanting, and across again, bigger.

## Sub lesson 2 — Curved lines and U shapes

Not every line is straight. The same model pages carry **curved** strokes — lines that bend like a smile, like the letter U, like a bowl that could hold water.

Look at the curved models in Picture 9151 again — the purple curves at the bottom. A curved line never snaps; it flows. Draw it in one flowing journey, the way water pours.

Practise rows of U shapes: down, round the bottom, up — down, round, up. Each U is a small cup. Fill a row with cups.

## Sub lesson 3 — Patterns: zigzag, wave and loops

Repeat a line and it becomes a **pattern**. The book's pattern pages show three: the **zigzag** — sharp teeth up and down; the **wave** — a smooth line rising and falling like water; and **rows of overlapping oval loops**, loop leaning on loop like a row of eggs.

[Picture 9152 — zigzag, wave and loop patterns]

The hard, proud skill here is keeping the pencil **on the paper** — one journey, no lifting, through the whole pattern:

[GIF 9182 — a pencil tracing a zigzag then a wave without lifting]

Exercise 5, in the book's own words of work: move the pencil in different directions and draw many different lines, again and again.

## Sub lesson 4 — The teacher's line lesson (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide teaches this same work from the teacher's side, and it opens with a question worth answering out loud: **"What is a line?"**

Its order is the order of this whole unit: first **warm up the hand** — the scribble you already know — then **draw lines**, then **colour**. Warm hand, line, colour.

Answer the question your way now: a line is the path your pencil travels. You have drawn paths in every direction — across, down, slanting, curved, zigzag, wavy, looping. That is the whole answer, and you can prove it with your own pages.

*(This part stands on teacher-guide evidence — it shows how the lesson is taught, and it is named as evidence here and in the sources.)*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "A line is the path my pencil travels. It can go across, down, slanting, or curved — and repeated, it makes patterns: zigzag, wave and loops." Draw one of each as you say it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw one page with four journeys: across, down, slanting, curved.
2. Draw a zigzag and a wave without lifting your pencil — count how many teeth and waves you made.
3. Fill a row with overlapping oval loops, then a second row under it.
