# Painting with Natural Pigments

## Do it first

Squeeze a red cabbage leaf — purple juice! That juice is paint. And here is its magic: touch it with lemon and it changes colour before your eyes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Painting with red-cabbage juice

**Tape watercolour paper down** and **wash it all over with red-cabbage juice**. Let it **dry**. Then **paint patterns** on it with **lemon juice, vinegar and baking-soda solution — each with its own tool** — and **watch the colours change where each substance lands**. The paint is a vegetable: the cabbage juice is both the painting ground and a colour-changing medium, so the picture's colours are made by the painting substances themselves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making the natural paint

The teacher guide teaches the making: **chop and boil red cabbage** for a **strong juice paint**; keep it **about a week refrigerated**, or **frozen in ice trays**. Other natural substances work too — **blueberry juice** is also a colour-changing paint.

## Sub lesson 3 — Blueberry magic

**Mustikkamagiaa — Blueberry Magic** (from *Maagiset mustikat*, Anniina Jokitalo 2022): make art **with blueberries through chemical reaction** — **nature's own materials as the art medium**, observing **with all senses**. The book prints the activity's goals and framing; its full step-by-step sheet is held on the LUMA Lapland site, so this part stands at that level, honestly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson taught, part by part. Companion checks each part against the sub lessons above.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make your own piece using this lesson's way, from start to finish.
2. Show it to the class and say what you made, how, and what you learnt.
3. Write one sentence: what will you do differently next time?
