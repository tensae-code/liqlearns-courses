# Lesson plan — Knitting

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Knitting
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** knitting from the first stitches — needles and their kinds, knitting from a chart, and designing and making knitted products such as scarves..
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 6 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — what it teaches: a knitted product begins with a sketch in which every detail, stitch and element is drawn — the sketch fixes proportions and pattern placement before a loop is cast; yarns — wool, linen, cotton, synthetic — chosen by the product's purpose, judging thickness, elasticity and dye fastness; what it teaches: knitting needles by material (steel, bone, aluminium, plastic, wood, bamboo) and by form (straight, circular, stocking, auxiliary); the needle 1.5–2 times thicker than the yarn; casting on the first row in pictured steps; the two stitches everything is built from — knit and purl — edge stitches, and binding off the last row; what it teaches: composing your own pattern from knit and purl combinations and drawing it as a chart; chart symbols and how a chart is read — bottom up, snaking right-to-left then left-to-right; the makes — a fringed scarf in four pictured steps (fringe cut 20–25 cm, folded, hooked through the edge loops), a kerchief, a hot pad and a phone case, each with its chart
