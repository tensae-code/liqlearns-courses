# Lesson plan — Paints and How They Work

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Paints and How They Work
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the paint kinds one by one — watercolour and oil first, then the full kit: oil, pastel, acrylic, watercolour, gouache, tempera and crayon and charcoal — what thins each one, how fast it dries, and how it is laid down.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Painting Then and Now and The Colour Wheel — the learner can already mix colours and knows painting has a history; this lesson teaches the materials themselves, kind by kind.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Watercolour and oil: the first two** — watercolour thinned with water, worked light to shade, thinly, with soft brushes; oil thinned with oil, worked either direction, thick or thin (Grade 5, printed pp. 15–16).
2. **The paints: oil, pastel, acrylic, watercolour** — the painter's kit and the four kinds defined by thinner and drying (Grade 6, printed pp. 48–49).
3. **The eight paints, part one: oil, watercolour, acrylic, gouache** — oil's slow drying and knife work; watercolour's flow; acrylic on card, wood, metal and ceramics; gouache, water-mixable and fast-drying (Grade 7, printed pp. 36–39).
4. **The eight paints, part two: tempera, crayon, pastel, charcoal** — tempera from ancient Egypt; crayon, pastel and charcoal — the stick paints, applied by rubbing (Grade 7, printed pp. 40–42).

5. **The Grade 7 chapter opens — painting, printing, music and stage (chapter opening — Grade 7)** — the Grade 7 book's Chapter 2 opener and aims: painting and printing techniques, reading music, performing traditional dance, creating theatre/film ideas — and singing in the Bati and Tizta qenet (Grade 7, printed p. 33).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Painting Then and Now and The Colour Wheel. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell watercolour from oil by thinner, direction of work and drying.
2. Name the painter's kit: paint, brush, palette, thinner, board, canvas, easel, paper, water, cloth.
3. Say how acrylic, gouache and tempera each behave.
4. Say how crayon, pastel and charcoal are alike: sticks, laid down by rubbing.

## What you need
Whatever paints you have — watercolour or poster colour is enough to start — a brush, water, paper, and a cloth.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses gouache and watercolour → both mix with water; gouache covers flat and opaque, watercolour flows thin and light-first.
- Thinks charcoal is a paint with a brush → charcoal is a stick, rubbed like crayon and pastel.
