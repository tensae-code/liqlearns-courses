# Paints and How They Work

## Do it first

Look at a painted picture near you — a book cover, a church painting copy, a poster. Guess: was it painted thin like water, or thick like butter? This lesson teaches you to tell paint kinds apart by how they behave.

## Sub lesson 1 — Watercolour and oil: the first two

**Watercolour** is thinned with **water** and worked **from light to shade**, applied thinly, with **soft brushes that can hold water**. **Oil colour** is thinned with **oil** (the book also names gas/kerosene) and can be worked **shade-to-light or light-to-shade, thick or thin**. Learn the pair by their opposites: watercolour is water-thin and light-first; oil is slow, rich and can stand thick on the surface.

## Sub lesson 2 — The paints: oil, pastel, acrylic, watercolour

The Grade 6 kit: **paint, paper or canvas, brush, easel, thinner and a wiping cloth** — the palette is the plate for mixing colours. The kinds: **oil** — pigment made to mix in oil; it does **not dry quickly**. **Pastel** — paint prepared in **pencil/stick form**, powdered colour with binder; its content is no different from other paints except in use. **Acrylic** — a **very fast-drying** paint, prepared to dissolve in water and oil, applied with varied brushes and knives. **Watercolour** — mixed with water, used on paper and other suitable surfaces. Thinner rule: **water for watercolour, oil for oil paint**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The eight paints, part one: oil, watercolour, acrylic, gouache

The Grade 7 book treats the paints one by one. **Oil**: stiff brushes; paint light-to-shade or shade-to-light; it **dries in 3–4 days, sometimes a week**; it can be rubbed on by palm and fingers, or laid thickly **by knife**. **Watercolour**: sketch lightly in pencil first, thin the paint, paint **light to dark slowly** — the technique is called **"flow"**; its brushes have thick, plentiful, soft hair that holds water and paint. **Acrylic**: used on card, wood, metal and ceramics; water-thinned; dries fast; resists moisture; can be layered. **Gouache**: water-mixable, used on papers, in use since the 12th century, fast-drying, covering flat.

## Sub lesson 4 — The eight paints, part two: tempera, crayon, pastel, charcoal

**Tempera**: first used by the **ancient Egyptians**; water-thinned; fast-drying; applied by brush, finger or sponge on paper, card and papier-mâché. Then the three stick paints. **Crayon**: children's favourite; chalk or fluid form; applied **by rubbing**. **Pastel**: solid stick form; applied by rubbing; thin and thick papers. **Charcoal**: grey/black; rubbed like crayon and pastel; first recorded in painting use in England, 1606 AD. And the book's painting subjects to practise on: portraits, still-life arrangements, landscapes of city and country, animals, plants and birds.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Grade 7 chapter opens — painting, printing, music and stage (chapter opening — Grade 7)

The Grade 7 book opens its second chapter, *Expressing Creativity*, with aims across all the strands:

- **painting and printing techniques** — this lesson's ground, the paints one by one;
- **reading music**;
- **performing traditional dance**;
- **creating theatre and film ideas**;
- and **singing in the Bati and Tizta qenet** — Ethiopia's own scale systems, named in the aims themselves.

This lesson keeps the first aim. The qenet lesson keeps the last; the reading, dance and stage aims live in their own lessons.

## Sub lesson 6 — The Grade 7 chapter closes — paints, printing and qenet (chapter closing — Grade 7)

The Grade 7 book closes its creativity chapter with a **self-check table**, a **summary** and a **closing exercise** — and the self-check tells the chapter's shape: on one side, **painting and printing — their nature, materials and processes**; on the other, **applying written notes, rests and beats, and reading music in Tizta and Bati**. Mark each can-do ✓ or ✗ and re-study your crosses in the lesson that owns them — paints and printing here, qenet in the qenet lesson.

The exercise's heart is six **matching items**, the book's own pairs, and they are worth learning as pairs: **carving ↔ cutting and chipping away; charcoal, pastel and crayon ↔ rubbing; watercolour ↔ worked light to dark only; oil ↔ its long drying; printing ↔ pressing; relief ↔ seen from the front only.** Two fill-ins follow, on **characters and theme** in a work, and six short answers close the chapter — among them a building task: **build G-major in Tizta and D-major in Bati on the staff**. That building is a doing: do it in the qenet lesson's work, where the two scales live; its questions sit there too.

## Say it to your companion

Quiz your companion: name a paint kind, they answer its thinner and one way it behaves. Five kinds each, then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Paint the same small scene twice: once thin and watery, once thick — say which paint kind each try imitates.
2. Make a paint-kind chart: kind, thinner, drying, how it is laid down.
3. Rub a crayon or charcoal shading from light to dark, the way the rubbing paints build tone.
