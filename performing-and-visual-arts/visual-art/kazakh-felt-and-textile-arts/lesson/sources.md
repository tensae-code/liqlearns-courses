Alasha) — Тоқымашылық (Ткачество / Weaving) — what it teaches: applied art always came as folk culture's inseparable part — through it the nation's intellect, being, character and life-custom appear, and it is passed as heritage from generation to generation; carpet weaving's history is several thousand years: humanity, from when it began to use livestock wool purposefully, occupied itself with weaving, and in ancient Egypt's monuments weaving looms' pictures are preserved, belonging to the XIV century BC; carpets were woven mostly in Turkey, Persia (present Iran), the Caucasus, Turkistan, Afghanistan, Pakistan, Nepal, India and China, and hand-woven carpets were also produced in Spain; if we rely on the legend, the Persian carpet's advanced sample counted King Khosrow I's «Көктемгі кілем» (Spring Carpet) was very big — its length 122 metres, its width reaching 30 metres — and the carpet the Persians wove in the VI century in honour of defeating the Romans and capturing the Arab ruler was decorated with gold and silver threads, bordered with precious stones, and a blooming garden was depicted in it in exact volume; in 1949, in the place called Pazyryk (Пазырық) in Altai, among the Saka kurgans a pile carpet was found — today the V–IV centuries BC Pazyryk carpet is counted the oldest among hand-woven carpets preserved until our time; carpet kinds are very many, so their number cannot be defined; the classification scheme (КІЛЕМ ТОҚУ — carpet weaving) divides carpets: by preparation technology — қолмен (by hand) and мәшинемен (by machine); by the base's kind — тоқылған (woven), өрілген (braided) and инемен шаншылған (needle-punched); by the pile's relation — түкті (piled) and түксіз (pileless); by materials — табиғи (natural), жасанды (artificial), синтетикалық (synthetic) and аралас (mixed); carpets developed suiting social, economic and cultural processes — artificial threads and dyes were invented, and high-productivity machine making brought the carpet to the mass consumption market, but despite society's speed and the scientific-technical process's advance, contemporary carpets preserved their own peculiarity, and hand-woven carpets taking the eye's light are today counted true art works; the pictured works: М. Мұқанов «Екеу» (M. Mukanov "Two", a gobelin fragment) and the Pazyryk pile carpet (Пазырық түкті кілемі, V–IV centuries BC) (student book; original target `culture-and-values/kazakh-carpet-weaving`) | Book 7, printed p. 8 |
| Kazakhstan — § 1 (continued). Қазақ кілемдерінің түрлері (The kinds of Kazakh carpets) — what it teaches: carpet weaving is one of the Kazakh people's own-recognised art kinds from the beauty side, and in this art the nation's history and culture's peculiarities found appearance — a child took its first steps on a carpet (Бүлдіршін кілемде қаз тұрып, түйетай басып, алғашқы қадамдарын жасаған); in Kazakhstan a girl's dowry carpet (қыздың жасауы кілемі) was not sold — the carpet was presented as a gift to an honoured guest, and weaving secrets were preserved and passed from generation to generation; mainly women occupied themselves with preparing carpet items, and girls in childhood learned this craft kind from their father, mother and elder sister; in old times varied rites and own customs were made in connection with weaving art; before beginning to weave, the house's mistress (үй бәйбішесі) made the carpet-beginning ceremony (кілем бастау рәсімі); the weaving process began first with preparing the wool: it was washed, dried, then the wool was sorted and cleaned by hand; masters chose the wool densely — dense wool was used for the base, fine wool for the pile — and in preparing a carpet the sheep's spring-shorn soft wool (жабағы жүн) was used, and for the thread's strength a little autumn-shorn wool (күзем жүн) was added in autumn; the thread was prepared through spinning with a spindle (жіпті ұршықпен иіру арқылы); for weaving, easily assembled horizontal and vertical looms called өрмек are used; in weaving an item, relatives, neighbours and friends helped at the rite — the weaving's finishing passed as a small holiday, with the кілем той (carpet feast) and кілем құру (carpet-setting) rite; carpet weaving spread widely: masters working together exchanged mutual experience, helped each other and established creative relations, and from masters' hands came түкті кілем (pile carpet), тақыр кілем (flatweave carpet), алаша, басқұр, бау, дөдеге, тұырлыққас, таңғыш and other varied carpet items; Kazakh carpets pressed (басылған), patterned (өрме) and woven from wool are varied, and they divide into several groups by their ою-өрнек (ornament), colour solution and preparation method's own peculiarity: wool-pressed (felt) carpets (жүннен басылған (киіз) кілемдер) — текемет, сырмақ, түскиіз — are prepared from sheep wool; woven carpet items (тоқылған кілем бұйымдары) are prepared from wool, cotton, silk or synthetic materials — in preparing, the base warp thread (негізгі желі жіп) and the horizontal weft thread (көлденең арқау жіп) were braided at one time, and weaving is carried out on a special weaving loom; Тақыр кілемдер (pileless/flat carpets) are woven on vertical and horizontal weaving looms — in the work process two threads are used, the vertical (желі — warp) and the horizontal (арқау — weft); Түкті кілемдер (pile carpets) are mostly woven by the knot-weaving method (түйінді тоқу тәсілімен) on a horizontal weaving loom, therefore they are also called weaving carpets: the base (негізі (желі)), the knot (түйін (түк)) it makes, and the weft (арқау) fixing the knot — three threads are used, and the pattern arises from the piles fixed to the warp thread according to the pattern picture; Бескесте кілем және алаша (The beskeste carpet and alasha) — what it teaches: carpets woven in the Бескесте техникасы (beskeste technique) have a compositional structure (a big central field (үлкен орталық өріс) and a border (жақтау)) resembling a pile carpet, and it consists of straight lines, triangles, lines and small geometric patterns in the form of shoots resembling horns (мүйізге ұқсас өскін түріндегі ұсақ, геометриялық өрнектерден); the master, looking at the кереге (lattice wall), hangs and stretches the weft and warp and makes the form — therefore items woven with varied-colour wool appear as if embroidered (кестеленген болып көрінеді); from this it is called «бескесте кілем» (beskeste carpet); its colour solution is special: usually a background and two colours close to the pattern's tone are used, and the pattern's decorating is filled with a contrasting colour giving it special expression; in the Kazakh people's life, алаша was mostly used as a floor covering (төсеніш ретінде), while a carpet was mainly hung on the wall; Алаша (alasha) is a woven item made from varied-colour stripes: on the өрмек loom, for laying, stripes of 30–50 cm width (ені 30–50 см-лік жолақтар) are woven; the ready weaving is cut into several parts and sewn joining along the length — thus an item to lay on the floor, алаша, is made; alasha was woven in all Kazakhstan's regions; алаша's volume is taken freely and made depending on the stripes' number; alasha is patternless (өрнексіз) or patterned (өрнекті), and the stripes can be repeated alternately or be varied-colour squares; masters, skilfully harmonising varied stripes, prepared eye-catching items by creating a beautiful pattern through artistic sight (көркемдік арқылы көз тартар бұйымдар дайындаған) (student book; original target `culture-and-values/kazakh-carpet-weaving`) | Book 7, printed p. 13 |
