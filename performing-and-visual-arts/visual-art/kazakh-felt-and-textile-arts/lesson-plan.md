## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Artistic Labour** — Kazakh felt floor coverings: tekemet (pattern inlaid while felting) and syrmak (two-colour appliqué felt), and the patterned shy reed mat
2. **Kazakhstan Grade 4 Labour Training (Ormanbekova)** — the yurt interior: hearth under the shanyrak, tosekagash bed, kebezhe, sandyk, zhukayak, adalbakan; felt and ornament rhythm; syrmak and alasha carpets
3. **Kazakhstan Grade 5 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — what it teaches: chiy (ши) — Kazakhstan's ancient reed-mat weaving in three kinds, өре ши plain, шабақ ши with wool-wrapped stems in colour bands, шым ши patterned, hung between the yurt's kerege and tuyrlyk; weaving a patterned chiy in nine pictured steps; what it teaches: textile mosaic — Kazakh kurak, quilt (two fabrics with wadding quilted between) and patchwork; designing with the colour wheel and templates; the named Kazakh patchwork schemes — Ботагөз, Тұмарша, Қайшы, Құдық (well), Диірмен (mill), Жұлдыз (star)
4. **Kazakhstan Grade 5 Artistic Labour (boys, Chukalin)** — what it teaches: chiy (ши) — Kazakhstan's ancient reed-mat weaving in three kinds, өре ши plain, шабақ ши with wool-wrapped stems in colour bands, шым ши patterned, hung between the yurt's kerege and tuyrlyk; weaving a patterned chiy in nine pictured steps
5. **Kazakhstan Grade 8 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — shi weaving, felt making) — what it teaches: Kazakh home life and nomadic economy grew on wool — Kazakhs mostly made felt (киіз басу) and wove reed mats (ши тоқу); ши is woven from reed or ши stems, each stem wrapped separately with unspun wool fibres or coloured threads so a pattern forms when woven together; ши protected the yurt from heat and sun and served in drying dairy products and storing felt; the ши types — ақ ши (plain white), есік ши (door), шым ши (patterned face), орама ши, терме ши, шидек, өре ши; felt goods for the yurt — киіз itself, текемет (pattern pressed into half-finished felt of dyed wool), сырмақ (two-colour mosaic felt sewn on a base), сырдақ (ornament embroidered on felt with coloured wool threads), түскиіз (embroidered felt wall hanging that warms the yurt, its sides and top edged with flat-woven cloth); felt clothing and bags — аяққап, кесеқап, төсеқап, кермеқап, шайкиіз, жабынды, тесеме, сандыққап; what it teaches: the decorative ши panel's large open pattern of spirals, rhombuses, triangles and squares demands a master's precision and special care, and the weaving technology has survived unchanged to this day; making шым ши — choose the reeds carefully (long, thin, of one diameter), clean and cut them evenly, join stems by type and colour, mark the pattern on each stem and wrap it with wool so the picture emerges; the pattern must show clearly, so weave evenly and watch that the gaps between reeds stay even; the six step photographs show the patterned panel woven on a frame; felting a wool panel (киіз басу — валяние — felting) is a complex multi-stage process — first the felt is cleaned and dyed, laid on ..., combed, hot water poured, rolled up with ши and gathered, tied with thread from the ши's outer side, rolled, hot water poured from time to time, untied, rolled again, the small felters roll the felt, when crumpled the felt's edges gather and smooth, the finished felt is dried; dry-felting needles are very sharp — take fingers off carefully, test the needle on a special mat (brush, foam or dense sponge); begin with a large needle and, as the product's density grows, move to thinner ones; what it teaches: a necklace of wet-felted beads — the sketch shows the round beads and flower centre; the six steps show coloured wool rolled into balls by the wet-felting method, laid in rows, finished and threaded into a моншақ necklace; the headdress) — what it teaches: head measurements — the head circumference taken horizontally (БА.к) and the cross measure (КӨ) — with the tape rules in the table; cutting a stylised national headdress from thin felt (жұқа киіз — fibre felt): the pattern construction table with formulas (AB = БА.к : 5 : 2; AC = 30 cm or more; BD = КӨ : 2 – 3; the FGD points joined by a curved line, each petal's top meeting in a triangle); variant 1 in ten step photographs — the ornament drawn and cut through the red felt, the headdress assembled and glued («Момент-Кристалл» glue or a hot-melt glue is used for gluing thin felt and other products; spread the glue thin and straight when joining parts; use quality, smaller scissors for thin felt — it is comfortable to hold and easy to cut the pattern with); variant 2 — the тақия: its drawing (R = 6.6, band 59.5), the sketch, and fourteen steps — the ornament drawn on the blue felt, embroidered, the crown sewn on the machine, the band joined, the lining put in
6. **Kazakhstan Grade 9 NIS Art (Akhmetkalieva)** — what it teaches: қолөнер (craft) is one of the Kazakh people's age-old traditional arts — the word қолөнер means «art come from a master's hand»; кәсіп/кәсіпшілік (a trade) is the word for being constantly busy with a definite work, with the hand labour earned in everyday going — a master craftsman busy with craft constantly, making items to order or for sale, turns into a trade; craft formed as everyday work's trade over the years, and as a result of being passed from generation to generation a tradition (дәстүр) arose; folk art exists today too — it has not lost its link with tradition or its value; folk craft is now considered separately from applied art: «сандық» (chest) is a word from «сәндеу, безендіру» (to decorate) — its basis is ою-өрнек (ornament); the experiments: 1. Ши тоқу (weaving ши) — materials: ши stems, bamboo sticks, mouline threads, wool yarn, scissors, PVA glue; technology: at first wrap the ши's each stem with varied-coloured spun thread, after one stem is wrapped tie the thread and cut, wrap the second stem likewise, lay the wrapped ши stems in picture order side by side, then weave through them crosswise with wool yarn and a beautiful carpet-picture comes out; 2. Киіз басу (felting) — the materials needed for wet felting: unspun wool or synthetic fibre; the technology in six photographs: lay the wool tufts (5 cm) in one direction on tulle or a bamboo mat, one edge beginning (1-photo), lay another layer crosswise on top (2), sprinkle warm soapy water (1 litre of water) on the laid layer and press and level it with the palms (3), after it is ready wrap it on the bamboo ши or tulle and roll it pressing (4), then take it from the wrap, roll it this way and that, pour the excess water out (5), spread the taken item in a place with a draught and dry it 25–30 minutes (6); dry felting with a needle is also described; 3. Ағаш ою (wood carving) — materials and the technology of drawing the pattern sketch on the board and cutting it; 4. Жұқа киізбен (фетрамен) жұмыс (working with thin felt — fetr): жұқа киіз is made by craft and industrial methods, natural and synthetic kinds exist, thickness 1–5 mm; thin felt has weak sides too — after getting wet it shrinks and can flatten
7. **Kazakhstan Grade 9 Artistic Labour (girls, Alimsaeva)** — Тоқымашылық (Ткачество / Weaving) — what it teaches: applied art always came as folk culture's inseparable part — through it the nation's intellect, being, character and life-custom appear, and it is passed as heritage from generation to generation; carpet weaving's history is several thousand years: humanity, from when it began to use livestock wool purposefully, occupied itself with weaving, and in ancient Egypt's monuments weaving looms' pictures are preserved, belonging to the XIV century BC; carpets were woven mostly in Turkey, Persia (present Iran), the Caucasus, Turkistan, Afghanistan, Pakistan, Nepal, India and China, and hand-woven carpets were also produced in Spain; if we rely on the legend, the Persian carpet's advanced sample counted King Khosrow I's «Көктемгі кілем» (Spring Carpet) was very big — its length 122 metres, its width reaching 30 metres — and the carpet the Persians wove in the VI century in honour of defeating the Romans and capturing the Arab ruler was decorated with gold and silver threads, bordered with precious stones, and a blooming garden was depicted in it in exact volume; in 1949, in the place called Pazyryk (Пазырық) in Altai, among the Saka kurgans a pile carpet was found — today the V–IV centuries BC Pazyryk carpet is counted the oldest among hand-woven carpets preserved until our time; carpet kinds are very many, so their number cannot be defined; the classification scheme (КІЛЕМ ТОҚУ — carpet weaving) divides carpets: by preparation technology — қолмен (by hand) and мәшинемен (by machine); by the base's kind — тоқылған (woven), өрілген (braided) and инемен шаншылған (needle-punched); by the pile's relation — түкті (piled) and түксіз (pileless); by materials — табиғи (natural), жасанды (artificial), синтетикалық (synthetic) and аралас (mixed); carpets developed suiting social, economic and cultural processes — artificial threads and dyes were invented, and high-productivity machine making brought the carpet to the mass consumption market, but despite society's speed and the scientific-technical process's advance, contemporary carpets preserved their own peculiarity, and hand-woven carpets taking the eye's light are today counted true art works; the pictured works: М. Мұқанов «Екеу» (M. Mukanov "Two", a gobelin fragment) and the Pazyryk pile carpet (Пазырық түкті кілемі, V–IV centuries BC); what it teaches: carpet weaving is one of the Kazakh people's own-recognised art kinds from the beauty side, and in this art the nation's history and culture's peculiarities found appearance — a child took its first steps on a carpet (Бүлдіршін кілемде қаз тұрып, түйетай басып, алғашқы қадамдарын жасаған); in Kazakhstan a girl's dowry carpet (қыздың жасауы кілемі) was not sold — the carpet was presented as a gift to an honoured guest, and weaving secrets were preserved and passed from generation to generation; mainly women occupied themselves with preparing carpet items, and girls in childhood learned this craft kind from their father, mother and elder sister; in old times varied rites and own customs were made in connection with weaving art; before beginning to weave, the house's mistress (үй бәйбішесі) made the carpet-beginning ceremony (кілем бастау рәсімі); the weaving process began first with preparing the wool: it was washed, dried, then the wool was sorted and cleaned by hand; masters chose the wool densely — dense wool was used for the base, fine wool for the pile — and in preparing a carpet the sheep's spring-shorn soft wool (жабағы жүн) was used, and for the thread's strength a little autumn-shorn wool (күзем жүн) was added in autumn; the thread was prepared through spinning with a spindle (жіпті ұршықпен иіру арқылы); for weaving, easily assembled horizontal and vertical looms called өрмек are used; in weaving an item, relatives, neighbours and friends helped at the rite — the weaving's finishing passed as a small holiday, with the кілем той (carpet feast) and кілем құру (carpet-setting) rite; carpet weaving spread widely: masters working together exchanged mutual experience, helped each other and established creative relations, and from masters' hands came түкті кілем (pile carpet), тақыр кілем (flatweave carpet), алаша, басқұр, бау, дөдеге, тұырлыққас, таңғыш and other varied carpet items; Kazakh carpets pressed (басылған), patterned (өрме) and woven from wool are varied, and they divide into several groups by their ою-өрнек (ornament), colour solution and preparation method's own peculiarity: wool-pressed (felt) carpets (жүннен басылған (киіз) кілемдер) — текемет, сырмақ, түскиіз — are prepared from sheep wool; woven carpet items (тоқылған кілем бұйымдары) are prepared from wool, cotton, silk or synthetic materials — in preparing, the base warp thread (негізгі желі жіп) and the horizontal weft thread (көлденең арқау жіп) were braided at one time, and weaving is carried out on a special weaving loom; Тақыр кілемдер (pileless/flat carpets) are woven on vertical and horizontal weaving looms — in the work process two threads are used, the vertical (желі — warp) and the horizontal (арқау — weft); Түкті кілемдер (pile carpets) are mostly woven by the knot-weaving method (түйінді тоқу тәсілімен) on a horizontal weaving loom, therefore they are also called weaving carpets: the base (негізі (желі)), the knot (түйін (түк)) it makes, and the weft (арқау) fixing the knot — three threads are used, and the pattern arises from the piles fixed to the warp thread according to the pattern picture; the pictured items: Бау, басқұр (woven bands), a Қоржын (saddlebag), vertical and horizontal weaving looms (Тік тоқыма станок, Көлденең тоқыма станок), a Түкті кілем (pile carpet), a Тақыр кілем fragment and a Сырмақ fragment; what it teaches: carpets woven in the Бескесте техникасы (beskeste technique) have a compositional structure (a big central field (үлкен орталық өріс) and a border (жақтау)) resembling a pile carpet, and it consists of straight lines, triangles, lines and small geometric patterns in the form of shoots resembling horns (мүйізге ұқсас өскін түріндегі ұсақ, геометриялық өрнектерден); the master, looking at the кереге (lattice wall), hangs and stretches the weft and warp and makes the form — therefore items woven with varied-colour wool appear as if embroidered (кестеленген болып көрінеді); from this it is called «бескесте кілем» (beskeste carpet); its colour solution is special: usually a background and two colours close to the pattern's tone are used, and the pattern's decorating is filled with a contrasting colour giving it special expression; in the Kazakh people's life, алаша was mostly used as a floor covering (төсеніш ретінде), while a carpet was mainly hung on the wall; Алаша (alasha) is a woven item made from varied-colour stripes: on the өрмек loom, for laying, stripes of 30–50 cm width (ені 30–50 см-лік жолақтар) are woven; the ready weaving is cut into several parts and sewn joining along the length — thus an item to lay on the floor, алаша, is made; alasha was woven in all Kazakhstan's regions; алаша's volume is taken freely and made depending on the stripes' number; alasha is patternless (өрнексіз) or patterned (өрнекті), and the stripes can be repeated alternately or be varied-colour squares; masters, skilfully harmonising varied stripes, prepared eye-catching items by creating a beautiful pattern through artistic sight (көркемдік арқылы көз тартар бұйымдар дайындаған)
