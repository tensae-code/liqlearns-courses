# Hands-on — Angolan Crafts and Sculpture

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Cokwe art and Angolan sculpture
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: angola's own heritage sculpture, pictured and named: the Cokwe "O Pensador" (The Thinker) and Mwana Pwo figure, Benguela dolls, the Palanca Negra carved in wood, a hand-made batuque drum, Mayembe's sculpture and Etona's wood sculptures — set beside Neves e Sousa's painting of the same subject. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.

## Angolan traditional basketry
Work through this part with real materials or your own body, the way the book sets it: angolan traditional basketry (cestaria) — one of humanity's oldest arts, still practised across Angola's regions; artisanal production handed down generation to generation, every object bearing its maker's mark and never exactly alike. Basketry is kin to pottery (clay bowls were once formed over esparto baskets). The making steps: gather junco, palm branches, corn cane or mateba palm, dry four to five days, scrape the material into thread, then build the object's base. Its products run from a simple hat to furniture. Show a companion what you made or did, and say what the part teaches.
