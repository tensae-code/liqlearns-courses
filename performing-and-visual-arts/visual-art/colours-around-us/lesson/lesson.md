# Colours Around Us


## Do it first

Look around you, slowly. Name the colour of five things you can see — a flower, a fruit, the sky, a shirt, the soil.

You already live in colour. This lesson walks through the colours one by one — where each one lives, what each one means, and what each one can be made from — and ends with you naming colours in real pictures like a judge.

## Sub lesson 1 — Yellow and red around us

Grade 1 teaches colour by photograph. For **yellow**: a yellow **adey flower** and a bunch of **bananas**. For **red**: **red roses**.

[Picture 9168 — yellow adey flowers and bananas; red roses]

No paint pots yet — the colours are introduced where they live: on plants and fruits of your own surroundings. Name them out loud, then hunt: what else near you is this yellow? This red?

## Sub lesson 2 — Red: where it lives and what it means

Grade 3 gives red a whole study. **Where red lives**: it is mostly seen at **sunrise and sunset**, when the sky itself turns red.

**What red means**: red is a **sign of martyrs and sacrifice** — that is why it is **often seen on flags**.

**What red is made from**: red colour can be prepared from **berbere** (chilli), from the **tsigereda** rose/flower, from **red soil** and other things.

[Picture 9173 — red: sunrise, a flag, red flowers, red soil]

The activity: **draw a picture you like using red** — a whole picture carried by one strong colour.

## Sub lesson 3 — Yellow: sun, hope and happiness

Yellow's study is a happy one. Seeing yellow mostly improves **brightness, hope and memory** — and yellow is connected with the **sun, heat and happiness**.

**What yellow is made from**: it can be prepared from **turmeric (እርድ)**, from **yellow flowers** and other things.

[Picture 9174 — yellow: the sun, yellow flowers and turmeric]

The activities: **name yellow things in your area** — then **draw different pictures using yellow** and name yellow things around you as you work.

## Sub lesson 4 — Blue: sky, water, calm

**Blue** resembles the **sky and watery bodies** — the two biggest things a child sees.

Painters know its power: they use blue to create **cold and peaceful places** in a picture. A blue room feels calm; a blue lake rests the eyes.

**What blue is made from**: blue colour can be prepared from **blue flowers, from carbon, from ink** and so on.

The activities: **name blue things around you** — then **draw the sky and watery bodies using blue**. Your drawing is the painter's trick in your own hand: sky above, water below, calm everywhere.

## Sub lesson 5 — Blue in Grade 1, and naming colours in pictures

Grade 1's blue is **ሰማያዊ — sky-blue** — named after the sky itself.

Then Exercise 10 makes judges of you: **tell the class what colours the following pictures have**. The pictured scenes include the **Ethiopian flag — green, yellow, red**; a **sunrise over a river landscape**; and a **crowd of people in colourful traditional wraps**.

[Picture 9169 — blue sky over water, and the green-yellow-red flag]

Naming colours in a real picture is harder than naming a paint pot: colours hide in shadows, mix in crowds, shine in skies. Name them all — every colour you can defend.

## Sub lesson 6 — Colours around us in the teacher guides (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guides walk the same road. The Grade 1 guide's lesson is called, simply, **the colours around us**: noticing and naming the colours in the child's environment — the bridge from perception to colour work.

The Grade 2 guide goes further: **colour as culture** — the colour of the flag, and what the colours mean around us. Colour is never only decoration; it carries meaning, as red on flags already showed you.

*(This part stands on teacher-guide evidence. The Grade 2 guide is a scan with no text layer, read visually, so its part is cited at section level only — exactly as its placements state.)*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Yellow lives in the adey flower and bananas, and it means sun, hope and happiness. Red lives at sunrise and sunset and on flags, a sign of sacrifice, and it can be made from berbere and red soil. Blue — ሰማያዊ — is the sky and the water, cold and peaceful." Then name the flag's colours together.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a picture you like using red — one picture, carried by red (the book's Activity 9).
2. Name and draw yellow things in your area; colour them with yellow (Activities 10–11).
3. Draw the sky and watery bodies using blue (Activity 13), and write the colours of the Ethiopian flag under it in order.
