# Lesson plan — Cave Art and Church Painting

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Cave Art and Church Painting
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how painting began — the cave paintings of the first people, Ethiopia's own cave paintings at Porc-Epic and Lega Oda near Dire Dawa, and the Book of Paintings and the church art that grew from that beginning.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Painting Then and Now — the learner already knows Ethiopian painting has an old and a new face; this lesson goes to the very beginning of painting itself.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Cave art: how art began** — art since humans existed, before writing; Lascaux in France and Altamira in Spain, about 40,000 years old, registered as world heritage (Grade 5, printed pp. 89–91).
2. **Ethiopia's cave paintings: Porc-Epic and Lega Oda** — Porc-Epic — about 60 images, the paint-stones found there; Lega Oda — more than 600 signs in red and black, an endangered rare heritage (Grade 5, printed pp. 91–92).
3. **The Book of Paintings and church art** — the መጽሐፈ ስዕላት from Lebna Dengel's reign; church painting's three marks — few colours, no depth, frontal faces (Grade 5, printed pp. 93–95).
4. **Cave art and painting types (teacher guide — evidence)** — cave art as paintings and engravings in caves and shelters from the Ice Age, Dire Dawa the home example; Ethiopian painting in four types (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 1–4, printed pp. 4–8).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Painting Then and Now and Sculpture and Historic Places. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say when and why the first people painted, as far as scholars can tell.
2. Name the Lascaux and Altamira caves and what covers their walls.
3. Name Ethiopia's Porc-Epic and Lega Oda caves and what is painted in each.
4. Say what the Book of Paintings (መጽሐፈ ስዕላት) is and what it teaches.
5. Give the three marks of Ethiopian church painting: few colours, no depth, frontal faces.

## What you need
Paper and dark pencils or charcoal, and — if you can — a rough stone or wall surface to imagine painting on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Close your eyes and go back 40,000 years. No paper. No books. Your wall is rock, your light is fire. If you could paint one thing on that wall for the people of the future, what would it be? Say it out loud. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the two Ethiopian caves → Porc-Epic: about 60 human and animal images, and the coloured stones of the paints found there. Lega Oda: more than 600 signs, red and black, with an elephant and a man riding a horse.
- Thinks church painting shows depth like a photograph → the book's second mark is NO depth and no background: it is two-dimensional only.
