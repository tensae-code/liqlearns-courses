# Video script — Contemporary Syrian Artists

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This is Syria's own lesson.

**Lesson 25 "Contemporary Syrian Visual Artists".** meeting Syria's living generation of artists by name, city and training — Nabil Razzouk, Jumaa al-Nazhan, Mukhles al-Warrar, Marwan Kassab Bashi and Jean Hanna.

**Lesson 8 "Art and Homeland".** Abd al-Mannan Shamma (b. 1937), Syrian painter who studied in Russia and specialised in wall painting — his beehive-house village and old-city paintings; he stayed committed to portraying his local Syrian environment, and the pupil creates an artwork from their own environment.

**Lesson 10 "I Think and I Draw".** Amjad Ghazi (1964-2017), Syrian artist of Damascus's Fine Arts faculty and the most famous illustrator of Usama, Syria's children's magazine (Ministry of Culture, since 1969) — reading his cover's subject and elements, then inventing and executing your own Usama cover idea.

**Lesson 12 (artist).** Sawsan Jalal (b. 1951), Syrian woman painter, daughter of the artist Mahmoud Jalal — her watercolour flowers and roses, children's drawings and decor work.

**"The Syrian Visual Memory".** Syria's painter hall — Mario Moselli (1946-2015, nature in a realistic style), Mohamed Ghannoum (b. 1949, Arabic calligraphy inside the painting), Ghassan al-Siba'i (1939-2015), Michel Kersheh (1900-1973, oil pioneer of old quarters and faces), sculptor Fathi Mohamed (1917-1958), Haidar Yaziji (1946-2014), Nash'at al-Zu'bi (b. 1939), Omar Hamdi (1951-2015) and Nazir Ismail (1948-2016, faces in an expressive style).

**Lesson 4 "A Tale in a Painting".** Mohammed Harb (Abu Subhi al-Tinawi), the Syrian folk painter of tales — talking about what his painting shows, then drawing a scene from a folk tale you read or your grandmother told you.

**Lesson 5 (artist).** Ghassan Jadid (b. 1946), Syrian painter of Damascus's Faculty of Fine Arts.

**Lesson 6 "The Artist Mohammed Naji al-Obaid".** Mohammed Naji al-Obaid (b. 1918), Syrian folk-naive painter shaped by his environment, founder-member of the Syrian artists' union, whose panel hangs in Paris's National Library; his Eastern ornament is among the finest decoration — composing ornaments inside a 15×15 square in his spirit.

**Lesson 11 "The Artist Khalil Akkari".** Khalil Akkari (b. 1945), Syrian painter of the countryside — describing where the women stand, what they do, the shape and colours of their dress, comparing a village woman's dress with your own city's, and drawing a scene from the Syrian countryside with its people's folk dress.

**Lesson 2 "The Artist Sanaa Qouli — a Creator from My Homeland".** Sanaa Qouli, Syrian woman painter born in Damascus who has lived with childhood polio — looking at her paintings and making children's works in the spirit of her panels.

**Lesson 6 "The Artist Abd al-Latif al-Samoudi — a Creator from My Homeland".** Abd al-Latif al-Samoudi (1948-2005), Syrian painter born in Hama who relied on reducing forms; studying his panel.

**Lesson 7 "The Artist Wahid Maghariba — a Creator from My Homeland".** Wahid Maghariba (1941-2018), Syrian painter born in Aleppo who joined in one panel the beauty of composition; a pupil's work influenced by his spirit.

**Lesson 8 "The Artist Aisha Ajam — a Creator from My Homeland".** Aisha Ajam (1916-2006), Syrian woman of Aleppo who began drawing at the age of five — discussing what her paintings show.

**Lesson 2 (artist).** Walid Qarsali, Syrian artist — talking about what you see in his cool-coloured painting, its colours and elements.

**Lesson 4 (artist).** Mamdouh Qashlan, Syrian artist — the colours present in his painting read through their values.

**Lesson 7 "The Artist Louay Kayali".** Louay Kayali (1934-1978), visual artist born in Aleppo, graduate of the Faculty of Fine Arts who excelled in oil painting — reading his work: what do you feel when you see it, and why did the artist choose this subject?.

**Lesson 8 "The Artist Yusuf al-Sabouni".** Yusuf al-Sabouni (1950-2003), born in Aleppo, member of the Fathi Mohamed Centre for Visual Art, also active in writing short stories for children — what colours did he use, and what marks the colours in his panels?.

**Lesson 12 "The Artist George Ashi".** George Ashi (b. 1940), Syrian visual artist born in Tartous, holder of a decor diploma with excellence from the International School in Beirut — reading his work: what are the people in the painting doing, and what forms its centre?.

**Lesson 16 "The Artist Ali al-Sabouni".** Ali al-Sabouni (1940-2018), Syrian visual artist born in Hama, graduate of the Faculty of Fine Arts (painting), who adopted the realistic style — reading his panels: subject, shape and distribution.

**Lesson 17 "The Artist Ahmad Maalla".** Ahmad Maalla (b. 1958), Syrian visual artist, graduate of the Faculty of Fine Arts (visual communications) — observing the light and the figures and their movement in his panels.

**Lesson 22 "Duaa al-Bastati — The Will of Life and Art".** Duaa al-Bastati (b. 1990), visual artist born in Damascus, graduate of the Faculty of Fine Arts, born without arms, who relied on herself — reading her work: the panels' subjects, their colours, shade and light.

**Lesson 23 "The Artist Mahmoud Jalal al-Asha (Profession and Art)".** Mahmoud Jalal al-Asha (1931-2009), spontaneous applied artist born in Damascus whose basic means of expression is plywood (al-khashab al-mu'akis) — reading his work: what kinds of shapes do you see?.

**Lesson 33 "Contemporary Syrian Visual Artists".** meeting some contemporary Syrian artists — Moafak Makhoul (b. 1958, Damascus; expressive and symbolic style), Mustafa Fathi (1942-2009, Daraa; studied engraving and printing at Damascus's Faculty of Fine Arts), Aktham Talea (b. 1963, calligrapher and graphic designer with a distinctive contrasting colour range), Fuad Abu Assaf (b. 1966, Suwayda; sculptor who handles rock smoothly), Khairallah Salim (b. 1953, Homs; Palestinian-Syrian, visual communications, worked in design).

**Lesson 5 "Said Makhlouf, Pioneer of Syrian Art".** the Syrian sculptor Said Makhlouf (1925-2000), who treated the hard tree trunks spontaneously and consciously, supervised the sculpture workshop at the Damascus International Fair; among his most famous works "The Winged Lion" at Damascus International Airport; he won the Syrian Order of Merit and took part in Arab exhibitions — describe what you see in the artwork: what material executed it, and did the artist adopt the realistic style?.

**Lesson 7 "The Contemporary Syrian Artist Saad Yakan".** Saad Yakan (b. 1950), among the most prominent painters of the contemporary and modern Syrian (and Arab) art movement, who adopted drawing the human in his panels in an expressive way, his works spread across the world; contemplating his artwork for movement and composition, balance (Balance) of the panel's elements, their distribution, and the colour harmony (Harmony) between them; executing an artwork inspired by his panels and colouring it in fitting colours.

**Lesson 15 "The Syrian Artist Nizar Ali Badr".** Nizar Ali Badr (b. 1964) invented a distinctive style reflecting varied techniques that imitate reality; the Syrian sculptor gathered from Jabal Safon (al-Jabal al-Aqra') on our coast small and medium stones to form artworks telling the Syrians' pain through the war on their homeland — Safon in the old Syrian language means the god of storms; contemplating his panels: the material used, whether he used colours, the elements in the panel; try making an artwork from the stones and pebbles in your environment, or from a journey you took, inspired by his works.

**Lesson 23 "The Syrian Artist Michel Karsheh".** Michel Karsheh (1900-1973), Syrian artist, graduate of the National Higher School in Paris, counted among the pioneers who drew the natural scenes and old quarters in the impressionist style and drew faces in a realistic style; he won many Arab and world prizes; reading his panels — their subjects, the visual balance, the colours' relation; draw an artwork inspired by Karsheh's works in which perspective appears for some buildings, and colour it.

**Lesson 26 "The Contemporary Syrian Artist Ahmad al-Ahmad".** Ahmad al-Ahmad (b. 1946), Syrian sculptor, graduate of Damascus University, master's in sculpture from the Academy of Fine Arts in Warsaw, Poland, and a doctorate in the philosophy of human sciences in art history — his research subject the memorial monuments — from Warsaw University; he won several Arab prizes and his works are spread in some museums; abstract art is the departure from reality and the imitation of imagination with the artist's thought (mass — void); design in pencil a sculptural composition from the spirit of Ahmad al-Ahmad and try to execute it in the available materials (clay — soap — carton remains).

**Lesson 32 "The Contemporary Syrian Visual Artists".** meeting some contemporary Syrian visual artists and their important role in developing the Syrian art movement, and knowing their works through electronic research — Hisham al-Ghando (b. 1965, sculptor of the realistic style), Ihsan al-Arr (b. 1969, sculptor who excelled in the abstract style), Naamat Badwi (b. 1963, painter of the realistic style), Yasser Hammoud (b. 1963, abstract style; he designed and executed the Panorama panel in Damascus), Mohamed Ali al-Homsi (1939-1996, linked to still-life and flower subjects), Mahmoud Jawabra (b. 1950, impressionist and expressive), Ali al-Kafri (b. 1948, Palestinian-Syrian who cared for Palestinian folk heritage), Jamal al-Abbas (b. 1941, artist, writer and lecturer who represented Syria in international exhibitions), Ahmed Elias (b. 1954, calligrapher-artist departing from the traditional pattern toward symbol and abstraction).

**Lesson 4 "The Artist Nasir Shura".** Nasir Shura — "The Family" (1957): what do I see in the panel? Name the colours in it; describe what the children in the panel are doing; put the fitting title for this panel; what subject would I love to draw about my family members? Create a panel in a different way.

**Lesson 9 "The Artist Mumtaz al-Bahra".** Mumtaz al-Bahra (1938-2017) — a Syrian visual artist and caricature painter; he drew for the school books (the characters Basem and Rabab) and is counted the spiritual father of the magazine "Usama" and the magazine "The Arab Child"; what do I see in the panel, what are the children doing, where are they sitting? Imagine you and your friends playing in a place you love — create an artwork about what you imagined.

**Lesson 12 (the artist).** contemplating the artwork of the artist Mohamed Wahibi (b. 1947 in Palestine, lived in Damascus, d. 2016), a visual artist — what do I see in the artwork, did I love it and why? Let us create an artwork from the spirit of this panel of the artist Mohamed Wahibi.

That is Contemporary Syrian Artists — Syria's way, kept by name.
