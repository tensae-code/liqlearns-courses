# Art History of Afghanistan

## Sub lesson 1 — The art of Afghanistan before Islam

The oldest human tools in Afghanistan, of obsidian and flint from the Nawur plain of Ghazni, are about 100,000 years old. The Middle Stone Age caves of Aq Kupruk and Hazar Sum in Samangan carry interlaced lines that may be the first painting or writing in Afghanistan (50,000–20,000 years ago). Then come Mundigak near Kandahar, with clay figurines, pottery painted in regular geometric designs and architecture; the ivory carving of Begram; and the Gandhara world of Hadda and the Buddha.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Islamic period

The Islamic centuries: the Ghaznavids of Sultan Mahmud and Masud in Ghazna and Herat; the great congregational mosque of Herat founded by Ghiyasuddin Ghori; and the Timurid flowering under Sultan Hussein in Herat and Babur — its architecture and monuments, among them a minaret of Herat's congregational mosque.

## Sub lesson 3 — Contemporary art and the country's art institutions

The contemporary period, met through pictured works by its masters — Abdul Wase Rahro Omarzad, Qamaruddin Chishti, Sher Mohammad's contemporary illumination (تذهیب), Mohammad Sediq Zhakfar and Gholam Mohiuddin. And the institutions that teach art: the Kabul School of Fine Industries (مکتب صنایع نفیسۀ کابل), proposed by Master Gholam Mohammad Maimanagi, whose master graduates include Ghausuddin, Karim Shah Khan and Khair Mohammad Khan Yari.

## Sub lesson 4 — The National Museum of Afghanistan

A museum is where a country's historical, cultural and artistic works are kept. The National Museum was founded in 1297 SH as the 'House of Wonders' (عجایب‌خانه) with manuscripts and Quran commentaries, rare embroidery, weapons and flags; it became the Kabul Museum, was reorganised to scientific standards with a UNESCO team in 1339 SH, and renamed the National Museum of Afghanistan in 1352 SH — then stalled by the civil wars. Its holdings come from every region and era, from the Stone Age on.
