| Vietnam — Theme 2 "Forms of space in Vietnamese folk painting" — three kinds of space — many-layered space, where figures are sized by their importance, not by nearness (Đông Hồ's "Ngô Quyền"; Hàng Trống's "Tam phủ"); conventional space, where one emblematic image stands on the bare paper and the viewer imagines the rest (Kim Hoàng's "The Lone Pig"; Hàng Trống's "Carp Gazing at the Moon", its form shaded by the cản màu resist technique); and simultaneous space, where many places and moments share one picture (Hàng Trống's "Canh nông chi đồ"; Đông Hồ's "Wrestling") (student book; original target `culture-and-values/dong-ho-and-vietnamese-folk-painting`) | Book 1, printed p. 11 |

| Vietnam — Bài 1, Did you know — the development history of relief printing, and its coming to Vietnam — in the history of graphic art, relief printing is one of the popular printing methods to print books, paintings and cultural products; the relief printing method was born quite early, attached to the development of science, technique and technology, and in the first stage the relief printing blocks were made from clay and wood and printed manually; relief printing is a long-standing printing form widely used in Asia, originating from China — the wood-engraving print of the Diamond Sutra (Kinh Kim Cương) was found in China earliest in about the 9th century (the Diamond Sutra is the wood-engraving print discovered earliest in the year 868 of the Tang dynasty at the cave in Dunhuang (Đôn Hoàng), China, the print currently kept at the London Museum); to the 15th century, in Europe, the printing craft was perfected by Johannes Gutenberg (Giô-han-nơ Gu-ten-béc), who applied the metal movable letter printing technique in the field of book publishing, contributing to creating its own values; and in Vietnam, in the 15th century, Thám hoa Lương Nhữ Học is considered the person who brought the printing block carving craft into Vietnam, used in printing books and folk paintings (student book; original target `culture-and-values/dong-ho-and-vietnamese-folk-painting`) | Book 5, printed p. 6 |
