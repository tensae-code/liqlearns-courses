# Lesson plan — Vietnamese Folk Painting

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Vietnamese Folk Painting
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** vietnamese Folk Painting — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Vietnam Grade 4 Mĩ thuật 4** — Three kinds of space — many-layered space, where figures are sized by their importance, not by nearness (Đông Hồ's "Ngô Quyền"; Hàng Trống's "Tam phủ"); conventional space, where one emblematic image stands on the bare paper and the viewer imagines the rest (Kim Hoàng's "The Lone Pig"; Hàng Trống's "Carp Gazing at the Moon", its form shaded by the cản màu resist technique); and simultaneous space, where many places and moments share one picture (Hàng Trống's "Canh nông chi đồ"; Đông Hồ's "Wrestling"); the folk-print schools — Đông Hồ, Hàng Trống, Kim Hoàng and Làng Sình arose around the 16th–18th centuries, printed on dó, điệp and bản papers; Kim Hoàng works by printing and painting on red paper, sometimes finishing with brush lines; Làng Sình prints the black lines first, then colours the shapes in fresh blue, red, orange, yellow and purple
2. **Vietnam Grade 7 Mĩ thuật 7** — The folk (national) element — the unique, characteristic features of a culture, expressed through line and colour and through deep depiction of the images of people, homeland and country, familiar and distinctive in each period of history — and how to bring it into an artwork, step by step, in the class's lion-dance picture
3. **Vietnam Grade 10 Art — Print Graphics** — In Vietnam's art history, woodcut print graphics is one of the art forms with the longest tradition; its development is attached to the names of folk painting lines born in the 16th–18th centuries — Đông Hồ folk paintings (Bắc Ninh), Hàng Trống (Hanoi), Làng Sình village (Huế) and Kim Hoàng (old Hà Tây, now Hanoi); those lines, together with modern Vietnamese print graphics (woodcut printing, metal-engraving printing, rubber-engraving printing, stone printing, screen printing…), developed from the early 20th century into an important art form reflecting Vietnamese society's aesthetic thinking and life
4. **Vietnam Grade 11 Chuyên đề học tập Mĩ thuật 11** — "Đấu vật" (Wrestling, wood engraving, 18 x 22.1 cm), introducing the figure composition painting, and "Bịt mắt bắt dê" (Blindfold Catching the Goat, wood engraving), the example of the circle-form composition
5. **Vietnam Grade 11 Mĩ thuật 11 — Đồ hoạ (tranh in)** — In the history of graphic art, relief printing is one of the popular printing methods to print books, paintings and cultural products; the relief printing method was born quite early, attached to the development of science, technique and technology, and in the first stage the relief printing blocks were made from clay and wood and printed manually; relief printing is a long-standing printing form widely used in Asia, originating from China — the wood-engraving print of the Diamond Sutra (Kinh Kim Cương) was found in China earliest in about the 9th century (the Diamond Sutra is the wood-engraving print discovered earliest in the year 868 of the Tang dynasty at the cave in Dunhuang (Đôn Hoàng), China, the print currently kept at the London Museum); to the 15th century, in Europe, the printing craft was perfected by Johannes Gutenberg (Giô-han-nơ Gu-ten-béc), who applied the metal movable letter printing technique in the field of book publishing, contributing to creating its own values; and in Vietnam, in the 15th century, Thám hoa Lương Nhữ Học is considered the person who brought the printing block carving craft into Vietnam, used in printing books and folk paintings
