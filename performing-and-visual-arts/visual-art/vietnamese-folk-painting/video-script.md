This lesson is Vietnamese Folk Painting.

Vietnamese Folk Painting — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson. — as Vietnam's school books teach it: Three kinds of space — many-layered space, where figures are sized by their importance, not by nearness (Đông Hồ's "Ngô Quyền"; Hàng Trống's "Tam phủ"); conventional space, where one emblematic image stands on the bare paper and the viewer imagines the rest (Kim Hoàng's "The Lone Pig"; Hàng Trống's "Carp Gazing at the Moon", its form shaded by the cản màu resist technique); and simultaneous space, where many places and moments share one picture (Hàng Trống's "Canh nông chi đồ"; Đông Hồ's "Wrestling"); the folk-print schools — Đông Hồ, Hàng Trống, Kim Hoàng and Làng Sình arose around the 16th–18th centuries, printed on dó, điệp and bản papers; Kim Hoàng works by printing and painting on red paper, sometimes finishing with brush lines; Làng Sình prints the black lines first, then colours the shapes in fresh blue, red, orange, yellow and purple.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
