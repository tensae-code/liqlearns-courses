This lesson is Chinese Seal Carving.

Chinese seal carving — designing and cutting a personal seal in a small square of stone. — as China's school books teach it: The seal — 3,700 years from Shang-dynasty seals; "xi" reserved for the emperor after Qin unification, "yin" for everyone else; the Qin-Han seal as the eternal model; a seal's three lives — credential of office, token of trust, and art in a square inch; the 2008 Beijing Olympics emblem built as a Chinese seal around a running figure that is also the character 京; the carver's way — seal script first (centre-tip, hidden head and tail, write the centre line first for symmetry); layout as "dividing red and disposing white" — natural balance, big-small interplay, interpenetration, and a border deliberately broken where it runs parallel to strokes; the knife as brush (rushing-cut and chopping-cut grips); red-character (relief) and white-character (intaglio) seals; side inscriptions (biankuan); Qi Baishi's bold single-knife manner; carve your own name seal.

Watch, listen — then say it back in your own words.
