# Lesson plan — Chinese Seal Carving

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Chinese Seal Carving
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Chinese seal carving — designing and cutting a personal seal in a small square of stone.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **China HS Chinese Painting & Calligraphy** — The seal — 3,700 years from Shang-dynasty seals; "xi" reserved for the emperor after Qin unification, "yin" for everyone else; the Qin-Han seal as the eternal model; a seal's three lives — credential of office, token of trust, and art in a square inch; the 2008 Beijing Olympics emblem built as a Chinese seal around a running figure that is also the character 京; the carver's way — seal script first (centre-tip, hidden head and tail, write the centre line first for symmetry); layout as "dividing red and disposing white" — natural balance, big-small interplay, interpenetration, and a border deliberately broken where it runs parallel to strokes; the knife as brush (rushing-cut and chopping-cut grips); red-character (relief) and white-character (intaglio) seals; side inscriptions (biankuan); Qi Baishi's bold single-knife manner; carve your own name seal
