# Lesson plan — Abstract Expressionism

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Abstract Expressionism
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The abstract-expressionist movement — large, gestural painting driven by action and feeling rather than depiction.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Апстрактен експресионизам** — Апстрактен експресионизам — New York after the Second World War becomes art's new centre: gestural action painting — Pollock dripping and pouring paint over canvases laid on the floor, Kline, de Kooning — against colour-field painting — Rothko's and Newman's great flat fields that work on the viewer's feelings; its sculpture in David Smith, Calder and Noguchi.
2. **Апстрактен експресионизам** — Апстрактен експресионизам — Abstract Expressionism: the huge canvas and the act of painting itself — Pollock's action painting and the colour-field painters.
