# Zellige Mosaic Art

## Do it first

Look at a tiled wall or floor near you. Are the pieces the same colour repeated — or colours taking turns? That taking-turns pattern has a Moroccan name: zellige.

## Sub lesson 1 — The public fountain — the saqaya

Morocco's Grade 3 guide teaches the **saqaya (السقاية)** — the public fountain decorated with **zellige mosaic (فسيفساء)**. Its space is organised by the **rhythm of alternation and opposition**: the mosaic pieces alternate in colour, and a ruler laid down the fountain's middle shows the **two halves mirror each other** in shape. Pupils name other mosaic-decorated places they know.

## Sub lesson 2 — Zellige in the Moroccan house

In Grade 4 the zellige returns inside **the Moroccan house (البيت المغربي)**: zellige mosaic and carved wood decorate a public fountain, and pupils **colour zellige pieces working by repetition, alternation and opposition** — the same three rhythms that organise the fountain.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what is zellige, and what three rhythms organise it? Companion checks: cut-tile mosaic? Repetition, alternation, opposition?

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a fountain front. Lay a line down its middle and make the two halves mirror each other.
2. Colour a row of tile pieces three ways: by repetition, by alternation, by opposition.
3. Write one sentence: where have you seen mosaic decoration?
