# Lesson plan — Pyrography: Wood Burning

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Pyrography: Wood Burning
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pyrography — burning designs into wood: its techniques.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kazakhstan Grade 7 NIS Boys Artistic Labour teacher guide** — pyrography: drawing with fire on wood, leather, paper and fabric (guilloche on fabric), its history from ancient Peru to the electric pyrograph; the techniques and strokes of wood burning with the pyrograph; choosing and preparing plywood/material for burning; designing the sketch (эскиз) for a burned piece; burning the design onto the piece itself, step by step; finishing and completing the burned work; presenting the finished pyrography piece to an audience
2. **Kazakhstan Grade 7 Artistic Labour (boys, Chukalin)** — what it teaches: the three kinds of burning, differing in technique — contour burning (the nib's tip like a pencil, for inscriptions and hatched ornament), silhouette burning (a smooth black surface without pits or scratches — the гладкий штрих — and отжог, a strip burned along the contour's edge; direct, the image dark on light ground, or negative, the image light on a darkened ground) and decorative burning (built on the first two, teaching light and shade — not a line but a volume built in imagined space); dots and lines by nib speed and pressure, tested on different woods in an experiment; what it teaches: the pyrography sketch — pyrography is kin to engraving, so choose a graphic, not a painterly, image; the sketch drawn in pencil at full size on paper, the whole composition fitted with room for every detail (too many details make a drawing heavy), an ornament border framing it; transfers onto the sanded blank — by carbon or graphite paper, by solvent (white spirit brushed over a face-down print), by heat (an iron over the reversed print) and in a graphics editor, where a found picture is scaled to the blank and printed; burned work can be coloured with watercolour or felt pens, and decorated by pyrotype — patterns stamped with heated metal stamps; what it teaches: one complete make — a yurt picture burned in eight pictured steps: sand the blank, lay the carbon paper, tape the template down, trace the drawing in pencil, burn it, sand again and wipe with a damp cloth, varnish for shine and protection; the varnishes compared — spirit (dry in 30–40 minutes but fears damp), oil (one to three days, never sticks) and nitro (15–20 minutes, flammable, thinned with solvents)
3. **Kazakhstan Grade 7 NIS Boys Artistic Labour student book** — what it teaches: the art of artistic burning — pyrography (from Greek pyros, fire, and graphos, I write/draw — «drawing with fire»), a burning technique of decorative-applied art and artistic graphics: a drawing laid on the surface of an organic material with a red-hot needle; burned pattern decorated household things in many peoples' cultures before our era, when a special awl was heated on fire and the burning was laborious; today the electric pyrograph burns wood, leather and fabric — burning on fabric is called гильоширование (guilloche); eight pattern drills to work through with different nibs; what it teaches: choosing and preparing the material — where once wood and leather were the traditional materials, today bone, paper, cardboard, felt, plastic and even vinyl records can be burned (the Australian artist Scott Marr turns old records into art works, burning them like wood); what it teaches: burning the product — prepare the workplace and the material (the apparatus, a tool for cleaning the hot nib, sandpaper, a towel, the stand for the hot instrument); preparing the wood in five steps — sand and clean the surface, sand it with chalk powder mixed with water, cover with light furniture varnish and sand again, glue a sheet of transparent paper with the drawing over the board to guard the work from dirt, and peel the paper away part by part as the burning advances; finish by varnishing — clean the face thoroughly with sandpaper, colour the ornaments or leave the natural colour, tone the drawing by the flame method or smoking (копчение), lighten dark places by scraping the burned surface with a sharp instrument; paint in thin layers, two or three for liquid paint; what it teaches: presenting the product — the выставка-ярмарка (exhibition-fair), a temporarily organised trading ground where different organisations' products are demonstrated and sold, run by set rules: choose the theme, define aims and tasks, choose the products, the place, the exhibition's structure, its design and advertising, and analyse the results; the laid lacquer will fix the drawing well; lacquer-spray can be used; lacquering must be carried out in two or three layers

6. **Kazakhstan Grade 7 Artistic Labour 2025 (boys, Chukalin)** — what it teaches: getting acquainted with the art of pyrography — pyrography is one of the kinds of decorative finishing of wood: the drawing is laid on the surface with the help of a red-hot needle; with pyrography are decorated caskets, wooden tableware, hangers, wooden statuettes, panels, pictures and other interior objects (a cutting board with a Kazakh-ornament medallion, leather bookmarks, a painted vase, owl spoons, a bonsai on a wood slice and a ship relief pictured); pyrography's themes can be varied: signs and symbols, pictograms, ornaments, fonts, inscriptions, sayings, drawings, caricatures, still lifes, portraits, plot compositions and so on; there exist several different ways of burning — pyrography, pyrotypy (пиротипия), burning with acid, by friction, on an open flame or in hot sand, and with a magnifying glass in the sun; people have been busy with burning on wood for not one hundred years — the term «пирография» appeared in England; in translation from Greek it means «рисование огнем» (drawing with fire); initially the drawing of a pattern on wood with a red-hot poker (раскаленная кочерга) was so called; in other sources it is said that the pyrography technique was borrowed from ancient Germanic images — in those times, to which these images relate, burning was produced by a very primitive way: with the help of a heated iron nail; at the end of the 19th – start of the 20th century for pyrography the Paquelin surgical apparatus was used; similar apparatuses and instruments for burning in old times were heated on an alcohol lamp or gas burner; drawings' burning is carried out with an electric burner — the пирограф; it is of two kinds: transformer with temperature regulation and soldering-iron type; the pyrograph consists of the body, the connecting wires and the heating tip (перо) fixed in a plastic handle; the tip's temperature regulation is carried out by the burner's regulator handle; in the burner's set there must be a set of changeable tips for carrying out a wide range of ornaments and drawings (the devices pictured: a) the soldering-iron type, b) the transformer with a pen, and the heating tips (перья) for the transformer burner with variants of their application); burning can be done on any dry wooden surface — for burning, boards from birch, aspen, poplar, linden are suitable, and plywood can be used; before burning the surface is sanded with sandpaper — this lets one remove small burrs or scratches; after sanding the surface is wiped with a damp rag to clean it from dust; the drawing is transferred through carbon paper; the safety technique at work with the electric burner (to avoid burns, the switched-on device must be taken without touching the metal parts; in breaks in work and during the device's cooling use a special stand; a device without a power regulator should be disconnected from the network every 10–15 minutes; the pen's change is carried out only after disconnecting the device from the power source and only with the help of pliers; in breaks in work disconnect the device from the network; do not use a faulty device; avoid strong pressure on the pen); the experiment: take several small boards or bars of wood of different kinds, prepare the surface of each bar or board for burning, and try to lay lines and dots by burning — analyse the got result: which kinds of wood, in your opinion, are better suitable for burning?; the process of burning can be conditionally divided into four stages: preparation of the work place; transfer of the image from the sketch to the wood; the burning itself directly; completion of the work; in the course of work the following rules must be adhered to: for convenience the handle is better held in the hand like a pencil; burning can be begun when the tip is heated to a dark-red colour; an accidentally laid line on the contour can be sanded with fine sandpaper; for skilful regulation of lines' thickness the pen's movement tempo must be changed: for thin lines conduct quickly, for thicker ones — more slowly; the colour of the drawing's elements changes with the help of the power regulator: for getting a dark colour the degree of heat is increased, and for lighter shades — decreased; for filling a large space of the drawing with colour, first the contour must be gone around, and then the middle of the required fragment shaded; the carbon (нагар) forming on the pen's tip is removed with the help of a metal sponge; many famous artists were busy with burning: Rembrandt, Bruegel, Picasso, the writer Hugo — by this fact one can judge pyrography's popularity; and today it has not lost topicality and continues to develop; the rapid growth of digital and computer technologies let one apply new instruments at finishing products by burning — lasers: these devices create a narrow beam of intensive light whose heating force lets one lay a pattern on the wood's surface; what it teaches: the devices and technique of artistic burning — the ways of burning can be divided into three basic kinds: контурное (contour), силуэтное (silhouette) and декоративное (художественное) (decorative/artistic); they differ from each other mainly by the technique of execution; контурное (contour) burning — this kind is used by many beginning masters; it is relatively simple, since it reminds one of work with a pencil at carrying out a drawing's contour on paper; it is carried out with the needle's tip; with contour burning it is convenient to make inscriptions; this device can be used in work with ornament by the hatching method; силуэтное (silhouette) burning — devices: гладкий штрих (smooth stroke) — a smooth black surface without pits and scratches, strong burn-throughs and light spots — and отжог (burning-off) — a strip burnt away from the edge and a clearly marked contour of the drawing; silhouette burning can be прямое (direct) and негативное (negative): direct silhouette burning — the image has a dark colour, the background stays light; for carrying out direct silhouette burning a special tip with a flattened and sanded end must be used; the needle's sting must be conducted always strictly along the wood's fibres so the surface got smooth and homogeneously dark; for getting unusual transitions of shades the tip's heating temperature should be changed, or the degree of pressure and the processing time changed; negative — the image stays light, the background is darkened; for burning large sections of background a pen for retouching must be used, and for getting a more interesting texture other pens can be used; декоративное (художественное) (decorative/artistic) burning — it is based on contour and more often silhouette burning; it is acquainted with such important concepts as light and shadow; the task of artistic burning: to convey not a line but a voluminous form which must be built; for this first the darkest shadow is picked out, then gradually the transition to half-tones is carried out and further to the lightest places; special attention must be given to working out the background, since it plays the role of the space in which the objects in the foreground are, and others in the depth, and free space is conveyed between them; volume is conveyed not only by voluminous form but also by different colour, and also by the objects' texture; to convey colour saturation, some objects must be made darker, others lighter; for conveying the depicted objects' texture, varied kinds of strokes and тушевка (shading) devices are used; besides the basic kinds of burning, products from wood can be decorated with the help of heated stamps — such a technique is called «пиротипия» (pyrotypy), which in translation from Greek means «огненная печать» (fiery print); in the work imprints of red-hot metallic stamps (штемпели) are used; at the rod's end, made of brass, copper or steel, with the help of a file a relief of a geometric figure is carried out, and from the other side the stamp's handle is fixed; the cliché made in such a way is heated to a defined temperature and pressed to the product; so varied wooden and leather products can be decorated (the stamps for a soldering-iron burner pictured); the way of pyrotypy is most often used at making a large quantity of identical products; what it teaches: carrying out the sketch; choosing and preparing the material — with the term «эскиз» (sketch) you are already acquainted: a sketch we call a preliminary draft fixing the conception of an art production, construction, mechanism or its separate part; it can be carried out in varied technique; the order of carrying out the sketch: to carry out a sketch, think up the drawing's theme; it is better if it is black-and-white, with a brightly expressed contour; it can be a plant ornament, a still life, a landscape, a plot scene; the sketch is carried out in pencil, its size must correspond to the future product's size; the whole composition of the product is drawn through; all the details must be placed in the drawing so that there is enough place for them; at this the details must not hinder each other; it is important to take into account that an abundance of details makes the drawing complex for perception and also complicates its transfer to the blank and the further work on burning; to not admit such a mistake, all the drawing's details must be thought through in detail; the work can be mounted with a frame with ornament — for this, трафареты (stencils), ready drawings can be used, but it is best of all to show fantasy; pencil or pastel are more preferable for sketches — a quickly made watercolour draft or a model from clay or soft wax can also be considered a sketch; Renaissance artists made sketches using a silver pen (серебряное перо) on specially prepared paper; pyrography is similar to other kinds of graphics — for burning it is better to choose an engraving, and not a picturesque canvas (a graphic horse's head marked right, a painted one wrong); for equipping the work place one must have a table with an even surface letting one place the pyrographic device on it, a cleaner for the pen, a blade and a set of templates; at insufficient natural lighting a table lamp must be installed — light must fall on the work surface at a sharp angle so that even the smallest shadows of the drawing can be examined; to clearly and rightly carry out burning, one must try to transfer the drawing to the surface maximally exactly and qualitatively; for the image's transfer several ways are used, each of which demands extreme accuracy and observance of a defined sequence: тепловой перенос рисунка (thermal transfer of the drawing) — first the template must be placed face side down on the blank and fixed; then with the help of an iron evenly warm the drawing's whole surface; after cooling carefully remove the template; this way lets one quickly transfer a complex drawing; перенос рисунка с помощью растворителя (transfer of the drawing with the help of a solvent) — the drawing must be placed on the blank face side down, then fixed in this position with masking tape; with the help of a brush lay the solvent on the drawing's reverse side; the drawing should be pressed so as to fully transfer the image; remove the paper and let the blank dry as it should; the sketch can also be prepared on the computer — for this any graphic editor installed on the computer must be launched, for example Paint; in the editor the drawing you conceived can be carried out; as a sketch a ready contour drawing will also suit, which is easy to find on the Internet (a yurt sketch pictured); ready drawings must be fitted to the blank's size — this can be done in any graphic editor: the drawing is opened in the editor and its sizes increased or decreased, then printed on the printer; the surface to which the image will be laid is thoroughly sanded with sandpaper, then wiped with a clean damp rag — this is necessary to remove the wood dust forming at sanding; what it teaches: artistic burning of the product; mounting — оформление (mounting/finishing) is giving something a final, established or necessary form; for the artistic mounting of products after burning, lacquer, vegetable oil, wax, морилка (stain) and varied paints are most often used; covering wood with lacquer not only gives the product a finished look but also protects the drawing from external physical influences; linseed oil well soaks the wood and thereby protects it from moisture; mixing wax with oil and dyeing substances, one can get one or another colouring of the finished product; морилка is laid on the wooden surface for toning (тонирование); for giving the product colour, matte paints will suit — gouache, tempera and acrylic, which will fully cover the wood's colour — or watercolour, creating a light semi-transparent effect; the practical work «Художественное выжигание. Юрта» (Artistic burning. The Yurt) — equipment and materials: the electric burner, sandpaper, a blank from wood, carbon paper, a template with a drawing, a pencil, lacquer, a rag; the order of carrying out in eight pictured steps: 1. sand the blank with sandpaper; 2. lay the carbon paper on the blank; 3. put the template with the drawing on top and fix it with sticky tape; 4. transfer the drawing in pencil; 5. carry out the drawing's burning; 6. sand once more and wipe with a clean damp rag; 7. cover the product with lacquer; 8. the finished work; lacquer well protects wood from dirt and spoiling and decorates the product; lacquers are divided into спиртовые (spirit), масляные (oil) and нитролаки (nitro-lacquers): spirit lacquer dries in the course of 30–40 minutes but possesses low moisture-resistance; oil lacquer dries slowly — from one to three days — but is moisture-resistant; nitro-lacquer also possesses high moisture-resistance and dries in the course of 15–20 minutes; thick lacquer is thinned with special solvents; since lacquer possesses a high speed of evaporation, use it in a well-aired room; to avoid loss and the rise of harmful vapours' concentration in the air, it should not be left open for a long time; the presentation of works and the organisation of an exhibition: 1. mount the creative work for the exhibition; 2. write an annotation to your work; 3. think up and mount the name of the exhibition-sale (выставка-продажа); 4. give an announcement and prepare invitations; 5. think out such a placement of works that they did not close each other; 6. the colour gamma must harmonise with the colouristics of the placement place; remember: all works must be signed (name, author, age, class, price) — signatures are carried out in one font, in one colour, on sheets of one format; the technical demands to the product: 1. correspond to all the demands of the technology of making; 2. have a finished look and corresponding mounting; 3. be carried out aesthetically and accurately
7. **Kazakhstan Grade 9 NIS Art (Akhmetkalieva)** — what it teaches: in the same methods table: Пирография (отпен сурет салу — drawing with fire) is used in decorative-applied art and artistic graphics; this method's peculiarity is that it is used as the main or additional drawing method on a natural material's (wood, bark, stone, paper, cardboard, leather, fabric) face; the material is chosen according to the picture drawn with fire on its face; pyrography is also known by the name ағашқа күйдіріп сурет салу өнері (the art of drawing by burning on wood); Peter Wolford (Питер Уокерд) turned wood-burning picture drawing into a world-level star in the pyrography field — Peter Wolford is an Australian pyrographer famous for drawing pictures by burning on wooden boards; he makes surfboards from wood and works together with other designers (p. 69); Salvador Dalí saw Giuseppe Arcimboldo's (Джузеппе Арчимбольдоның) «Жыл мезгілдері» (The Seasons) and «Стихиялар» (The Elements) picture series and was amazed: the artist depicted the elements in morphological images — the «Жер» (Earth) element as the animals living on earth (goat, ram, camel, elephant, horse, monkey, peacock, boar, duck, etc.), the «Ауа» (Air) element as birds (eagle, owl, parrot, rooster, etc.), the «От» (Fire) element by fire's varied elements (a wax candle, a match, flint, a kerosene lamp, etc.), the «Су» (Water) element as varied sea animals and water's world (a whale, a sea..., a turtle, a shell and ear/nose ornaments of pearls) (p. 69)
