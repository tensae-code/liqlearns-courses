# First Marks and Scribbles


## Do it first

Take your pencil or crayon. Do not draw a house. Do not draw a person. Just move your hand.

Go round and round. Go up and down. Cross over your own lines. Fill the page. Fast, then slow. Your hand is playing.

That playing has a name, and it is the start of all drawing. Keep your page — the whole lesson grows out of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — First free marks

Look at the book's first drawing page: a whole page of free red scribble — ሞነጫጨር — loops and crossings with no shape to copy, and under it, coloured strokes standing straight up.

This is the first drawing act. Before lines that obey, before shapes, the hand moves freely and the paper fills. There is nothing to get wrong: every mark you make is a mark you chose to make.

Scribble again now — bigger, smaller, round and round. This free page is not a warm-up outside drawing. It IS drawing, at its first step.

## Sub lesson 2 — Scribbling loosens the hand

The older book gives scribbling its definition: scribbling is the **first-level drawing method** — moving the hand **left, right, up and down** with a pencil or crayon on paper.

Why do it? Because it **loosens the hand**. A stiff hand makes stiff lines. A loose hand can go anywhere — and drawing needs a hand that can go anywhere.

Then comes the activity: look at pictured lines, and **draw different lines of your own** after them. Not copies that must match — your own lines, following the pictured ones the way a song follows a tune.

Do it now: scribble for ten breaths to loosen your hand — left, right, up, down — then draw three lines of your own under it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The books' own closing words

Both books close their chapters by saying what this work builds.

The Grade 1 summary says it plainly: **scribbling and line practice build handwriting and drawing skill**, and they teach the **shapes and colours** of things. The free page you made is already teaching your hand to write.

The Grade 2 closing exercise adds one proud word: the professional known for drawing is called an **artist**. That is the road this course walks — from a free scribble to an artist's hand.

End the lesson the books' way: draw a line and an oval shape, and colour it — the review task that closes the Grade 1 chapter.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "This is scribbling — ሞነጫጨር. It is the first drawing. I move my hand left, right, up and down, and my hand gets loose and ready." Then show them your freest page and let them fill a page too.

## Do It Yourself

1. Fill one whole page with free scribble — use two colours if you have them.
2. Scribble to loosen your hand, then draw five lines of your own under the scribble.
3. Draw a line and an oval shape, and colour the oval carefully — the books' own review task.
