# Lesson plan — First Marks and Scribbles

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** First Marks and Scribbles
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** free scribbling (ሞነጫጨር) — moving the pencil or crayon freely on paper — is the first drawing act, and it loosens the hand for every line and shape that comes after.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's own hand and a pencil — the first making lesson of the course. The Grade 2 book then names and practises it as the first-level drawing method.

## Sub lessons
1. **First free marks** — the Grade 1 book's full page of free red scribble (ሞነጫጨር) above coloured vertical strokes — free mark-making as the first drawing act.
2. **Scribbling loosens the hand** — the Grade 2 book's definition — scribbling as the first-level drawing method, moving the hand left, right, up and down — and its activity of drawing lines of your own after pictured lines.
3. **The books' own closing words** — the Grade 1 and Grade 2 chapter summaries folded in — scribbling and line practice build handwriting and drawing skill and teach the shapes and colours of things; the professional known for drawing is called an artist.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — this is a first door into drawing. A pencil or crayon and paper are enough.
- Next door in this unit: Lines in Every Direction, where the loosened hand learns to steer.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Make free marks on paper without fear — loops, waves and crossings in any direction.
2. Say what scribbling is: the first-level drawing method — moving the hand left, right, up and down with pencil or crayon on paper.
3. Scribble on purpose to loosen the hand before drawing, the way the Grade 2 book practises it.
4. Draw different lines of your own after pictured lines, as the Grade 2 activity asks.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil or crayon — red if you have it, for the first free page. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Afraid of doing it wrong → there is no wrong mark in this lesson; a page full of marks is a finished page. Praise the filling, not a shape.
- Presses so hard the paper tears → loosen the grip, hold the pencil like a bird — firm enough it cannot fly away, soft enough not to hurt it — and scribble lightly first.
- Only draws tiny marks in one corner → make big arm movements in the air first, then let the pencil be big on the paper too.
