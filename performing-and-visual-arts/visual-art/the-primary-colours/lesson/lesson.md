# The Primary Colours


## Do it first

Name the colours you can see on plants around you. Now name the colours of the animals at home.

Here is a harder question. Some colours are MADE by mixing other colours. Some are not made at all — they are simply found, pure. Which of your colours are the pure, unmixed ones?

This lesson names them: three first colours, the parents of all the rest.

## Sub lesson 1 — What primary colours are

Here is the definition, in the book's own frame: **primary colours (መስራች)** are colours **found pure in nature, without mixing with other colours**.

There are three: **red, yellow and blue**.

[Picture 9163 — three paint pots: red, yellow, blue]

Pure means nobody mixed them. Red is not made of other colours. Yellow is not. Blue is not. They are first — primary — and every other colour in this course will be made FROM them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The primaries in pictures

The book pictures the primaries two ways: as **balloons** — yellow, blue, red — floating up, and as **glasses of coloured liquid** — red, yellow, blue — standing in a row.

[Picture 9164 — the primaries as balloons and as glasses of coloured liquid]

The same page carries the song **"መስራች ቀለማት" (Primary Colours)**, which names yellow, blue and red as the **mother and father of colours** — because all other colours are born from them. (The song itself is sung in the Music lessons; here it tells you WHY the primaries matter.)

And the activity turns outward: **make a drawing you like using a colour found in your area** — the pure colours live around you, not only in pots.

## Sub lesson 3 — Finding primaries around us

Can your eyes sort? The book shows **clothes of different colours** and asks: **separate out the ones that are primary colours**.

[Picture 9165 — clothes of different colours: sort out the primaries]

Work it slowly: red shirt — primary, keep. Green shirt — made by mixing, set aside. Blue — primary. Yellow — primary. Purple and orange — mixtures, aside.

Do the same hunt beyond the picture: clothes at home, flowers, pots, doors. The primaries hide everywhere once your eyes know them.

## Sub lesson 4 — Drawing in the primary colours

Last, the primaries go to work. The book shows **pictures made in blue colours**, and **pictures made in different colours**, and then sets the task — Activity 14: **make different drawings in the primary colours**.

[Picture 9178 — drawings made in the primary colours]

A drawing in ONE primary is a study: a whole page of blue, and everything in it — sky, water, flower — must find its blue. A drawing in all three is a small festival. Make both.

## Sub lesson 5 — The basic colours in the teacher guides (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide teaches the same three colours under their other name — the **basic colours** — naming and explaining them as the first sorting of colour.

It also carries the framework this course grows into: **first-level (primary) colours, second-level (secondary) colours, and neutral colours** — taught inside its colour-in-culture chapter. The next lesson, Mixing Colours, builds the second level with your own hands.

*(This part stands on teacher-guide evidence — it shows how the basic colours are taught and framed, and it is named as evidence here and in the sources.)*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Primary colours — መስራች — are found pure, mixed with nothing: red, yellow and blue. They are the mother and father of colours, because every other colour is made from them." Point to one primary colour near you as you finish.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw three balloons and colour them yellow, blue and red; draw three glasses under them in red, yellow and blue.
2. Sort your own clothes (or draw them): which are primary colours? Make the pile and name it.
3. Make one drawing in blue only, and one drawing in red, yellow and blue together.
