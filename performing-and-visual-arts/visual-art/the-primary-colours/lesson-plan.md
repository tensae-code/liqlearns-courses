# Lesson plan — The Primary Colours

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** The Primary Colours
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the primary colours (መስራች) — red, yellow and blue — colours found pure in nature, mixed with no other colour; the mother and father of all the other colours.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 1 colour lessons (Colours Around Us, Green Around Us, Colours on Clothes) — the learner names colours freely; Grade 3 now sorts them: which colours are first, pure and unmixed. The teacher guides' basic-colours teaching is carried as evidence.

## Sub lessons
1. **What primary colours are** — the Grade 3 definition — primary (መስራች) colours are colours found pure in nature without mixing with other colours: red, yellow and blue.
2. **The primaries in pictures** — the primaries pictured as balloons and glasses of coloured liquid, the song "መስራች ቀለማት" naming them mother and father of colours, and the activity of drawing in a colour found in your area.
3. **Finding primaries around us** — sorting the pictured clothes (Drawing 3) and separating out the ones that are primary colours (Activity 8).
4. **Drawing in the primary colours** — pictures made in blue and in different colours (Drawings 10–11), with Activity 14 — make different drawings in the primary colours.
5. **The basic colours in the teacher guides (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 3 teacher guide's basic-colours lesson (§1.3) and its colour-in-culture framework (§3.4) — primary, secondary and neutral colours — cited as teacher-guide evidence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Colours Around Us and Green Around Us in this unit. Recommended, never forced.
- Next door: Mixing Colours, where the primaries meet and make new colours.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a primary colour is: a colour found pure in nature without mixing with other colours.
2. Name the three primary colours — red, yellow, blue — and pick them out in pictures (balloons, glasses of coloured liquid).
3. Separate out the primary-coloured clothes from a mixed set of clothes.
4. Make drawings using the primary colours.

## What you need
Red, yellow and blue colours — paints, crayons or coloured paper. Pictures of clothes in many colours for the sorting work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Insists green is primary because grass is everywhere → green is made: blue + yellow (next lesson proves it by hand). Pure means unmixed, not common.
- Mixes up the glasses order → the colours are the same three in every picture; only their order changes. Name them by colour, not by place.
