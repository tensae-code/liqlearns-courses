# Lesson plan — Printing

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Printing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** printing — repeating a thing's image by painting it and pressing it — in its ways: direct, palm, leaf, found things, carved block and stencil, up to batik and silk-screen.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own hand — the palm print is the first block every child owns — and on the colours lessons next door. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Direct printing** — what printing is; painting a thing's surface and pressing it, repeatedly.
2. **Palm printing step by step** — choose, paint, press flat, lift straight up; then palm-print designs.
3. **Leaf printing** — fresh leaves, painted, pressed, lifted, veins and all.
4. **Printing with found things** — caps, flowers, shoes: every surface signs its own name.
5. **Block printing: carve it backwards** — raised prints, gouged rests, and the mirror rule; potato and soap blocks.
6. **Stencil printing, and printing grows up** — dab-through designs, three colours, and the grown forms: batik, silk-screen and the printmaker's methods.

7. **Sharp tools: the carving safety rules (Grade 7)** — the book's carving-safety teaching — a printing block is carved with the same sharp tools.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the primary colours and mixing lessons (Visual Art, Group B) — printing begins by choosing colours. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what printing and direct printing mean, and print a found surface.
2. Palm-print in the true step order, and build circle, square and heart designs from prints.
3. Leaf-print so the veins show, and repeat prints into decorations.
4. Print with found things, naming the tools of the work.
5. Carve a potato or soap block — reversed — and stamp with it.
6. Stencil a design, explain three-colour stencilling, and say how batik and silk-screen work.

## What you need
Colours/paints, paper and cloth, leaves, a potato or flat soap, a cutter (with an adult), a sponge, bottle caps and other found things, a flat table.

## If the learner is stuck
- The palm print is faint or smeared → too little paint, or the hand slid: repaint the whole palm, press straight down, hold, lift straight up.
- The leaf print shows no veins → press more evenly across the whole leaf, with the painted side down.
- The block print reads backwards wrongly → the carving was not reversed: the block must be the mirror of the wanted print.
