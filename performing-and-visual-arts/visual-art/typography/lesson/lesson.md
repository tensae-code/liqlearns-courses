# Typography

## Do it first

Start with the first part below — do it or make it before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The named letter styles

Letters are message-carriers, and they come in named styles. Primary 4 names **Gothic, Sans-serif, Italics and Three-dimensional** styles, including styles from a computer. Primary 6 tells the two great families apart: **serif letters carry small tails/lines extended on them; sans-serif letters have none** — written in font styles such as italics, with Britannic Bold among the book's examples.

## Sub lesson 2 — Letters at work in design

Letter styles are used where Rwanda reads every day — **textbooks, newspapers, trade names and cards** — and in the poster's designed lettering. The teacher's guide adds the design-technology step: **learners work in pairs at a computer to examine serifs, sans serifs, italics and other fonts** for themselves (teacher guide evidence).
