# Houses of Our Country

## Do it first

Look at the house you are in, or the nearest house you can see. Look slowly for one full minute: roof, walls, door, the things made by hand. Appreciating begins with slow looking — this lesson practises it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Learning to appreciate art

**Appreciation** can be of natural or man-made things, judged by criteria of better art use and result. We appreciate **sound, dance and movement**, and the **beauties seen in nature**. It happens **in museums and anywhere**, as exhibitions and shows — and movable works can be shown **at school**. Appreciation **builds knowledge and is enjoyable**. The country's presentable works the book lists: clay, dress, weapons and shields, painting, drawing, sculpture, architecture, photography, parchment writings, religious paintings, music, drama and film — every art in this course is something that can be appreciated.

## Sub lesson 2 — Houses of the highlands and the lowlands

Appreciating local houses means reading **how a land builds**. The book sets **traditional house building of the highlands against the lowlands** — two captioned photographs, two answers to two different lands. Then history: **Merto Lemariam (Gojjam)** is pictured, and medieval-period architecture — **Tedbabe Mariam** (the Amhara Saint church) and **mosques built in North Shewa and South Wollo**. A house is never only shelter: it is the land, the materials and the faith of its builders, made visible.

## Sub lesson 3 — Home ornaments, wooden goods, doors — and weapons as craft

Inside the house, art continues: **home decoration ornaments**; **wooden household goods — a chair and a table**; and **door designs**, each door its own composition. Even **weapons are craft**: the book pictures **spears and a shield** among the household treasures. The activity joins it all: **draw a favourite home traditional object in line, colour and shape**, and prepare a poem and drama about traditional household goods.

## Sub lesson 4 — Exhibiting paintings and appreciating the other arts

Appreciating others' visual works **builds knowledge and skill** — so hold a **school exhibition**: gather pupils' works, **make frames** from paper or wood, and **hang them in a suitable hall**. Appreciate each work by **content, form, colour and composition**. The appreciation then walks to the other arts through pictures: traditional **instrument players**, **children dancing together**, a **traditional dance**, and the **traditional instruments** themselves — with the activity of performing a favourite traditional song type: shilela, engurguro, fukera and the like, and making a favourite instrument in drawing, colour and local materials.

## Say it to your companion

Describe one house in this lesson to your companion without naming it. They must say: highland or lowland — and which detail gave it away.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a favourite home traditional object in line, colour and shape.
2. Frame one of your drawings with paper or wood and hang it straight.
3. Write two appreciation sentences about a classmate's work: one about its content, one about its colour.
