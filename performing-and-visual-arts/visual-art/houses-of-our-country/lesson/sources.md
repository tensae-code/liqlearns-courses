| Vietnam — Lesson 1 — the characteristics of architectural works — functionality (tính công năng): an architectural work is built first of all to solve the problems relating to the living environment of humans; each architectural work has its own function, for example: apartment-block and villa works for living in; headquarters/office works for working; factory/workshop works for production; barn works (công trình chuồng trại) for raising animals…; architecture needs to ensure the convenience requirement (yêu cầu tiện nghi), favourable for the users and the activities inside, and based on each kind of work the functional requirement is shown in different forms (the hospital work is the place of medical examination and treatment; the stadium work is the place of sports competition; the factory work is the place of working and production; the school work is the place of studying and research); aesthetics (tính thẩm mĩ): besides the material requirements, architecture also needs to have aesthetics to answer the spiritual needs of humans as well; the expression of architectural aesthetics is first in the form-creating composition (bố cục tạo hình), the way of handling the mass areas, lines, colours and materials, reasonably and harmoniously with the landscape; and the beauty of an architectural work is not only in the outside form but also in the way of organising the space inside (the villa on the waterfall designed by the architect Frank Lloyd Wright (Phranh Loi Rai) is considered a model (hình mẫu) of the thorough exploitation and subtle use of the advantages of the position and the terrain (địa hình): the work has a form like the stone slabs (phiến đá) stacked on each other, letting the stream flow through without having to level the hilly terrain (san bằng địa hình đồi núi) or fill in the water stream (lấp đi dòng nước), creating a special architectural landscape); sustainability (tính bền vững): to ensure safety for the users, the architectural works must have the ability to bear the effects of the outside factors such as sun, wind, rain, storms and earthquakes…, and the inside effects such as the load (tải trọng) of the users, the furniture and the work itself; the level of construction science and technique developing more and more helps a work not only be sustainable in the bearing aspect but also increases the aesthetic value through the diversity of the shape-mass and structure (the high-rise works in Dubai (Đu-bai) — the high-rise or super-high-rise house architecture genre requires advanced construction technique, ensuring safety and sustainability); and the concept of sustainability in architecture is not only in the physical aspect (khía cạnh vật lí) but also the harmony and the reduction of negative effects to the natural environment (the Sydney Opera House (Nhà hát Ô-pê-ra Xít-ni), Australia (Ô-xtrây-li-a): the Opera House has a foundation system (hệ móng) of 580 concrete piles (cột bê tông) driven 25 m deep under the sea-water level; the Opera House is roofed with a tile material with the ability to clean the surface itself (tự làm sạch bề mặt) and is designed so that the sea wind can blow inside, conditioning the temperature naturally (điều hoà nhiệt độ một cách tự nhiên), helping to save energy) (student book) | Book 9, printed p. 7 |
| Vietnam — Lesson 1 — the kinds of architectural works — based on the use function, architectural works are divided into the following kinds: civil works (công trình dân dụng), including two main groups: house works (công trình nhà ở) — for example villas, attached houses (nhà liên kế) and apartment blocks (chung cư)… — and public works (công trình công cộng) — for example schools, hospitals, communal houses (đình) and pagodas (chùa)… (the Bảo Đại Palace (Dinh Bảo Đại), Đà Lạt; the Việt–Pháp Hospital, Hanoi); industrial works (công trình công nghiệp) — for example factories, enterprises (xí nghiệp) and workshops (phân xưởng)…; agricultural works (công trình nông nghiệp) — for example barns (chuồng trại) and irrigation dams (đập thuỷ lợi)… (the Hoà Bình irrigation–hydroelectric work); technical-infrastructure works (công trình hạ tầng kĩ thuật) — for example pumping stations (trạm bơm), water supply and drainage stations and wastewater treatment stations… (the perspective (phối cảnh) of the Yên Nghĩa pumping station); national-defence and security works (công trình quốc phòng an ninh) — for example citadels (thành trì), barracks (doanh trại) and warehouses… (the Sơn Tây ancient citadel, Hanoi); and transport works (công trình giao thông) — for example bridges, roads, airports and ports… (the Long Biên Bridge, Hanoi) (student book) | Book 9, printed p. 9 |
| Vietnam — Lesson 2 "Architectural works" — architectural space (không gian kiến trúc) — if the natural environment is considered the first environment (môi trường thứ nhất), then architecture is the second environment (môi trường thứ hai) created to serve the living needs of humans; in an architectural work there are usually many spaces, and each space again has a function corresponding to the different needs; based on the use need, those spaces have their own shape, size and organisation way (the shape-mass form (hình thức hình khối) of the Vietnamese traditional rural house (nhà ở nông thôn truyền thống Việt Nam); the ground plan organising the space of the Vietnamese traditional rural house: 1. guest-receiving place (nơi tiếp khách); 2. worship place (nơi thờ cúng); 3, 4, 5, 6. sleeping and resting places (nơi ngủ, nghỉ); 7. place to keep the rice (nơi để thóc gạo); 8. place to keep precious objects (nơi để đồ quý); 9. cooking kitchen (bếp nấu); 10. drying yard (sân phơi); 11. water tank (bể nước); 12. vegetable garden (vườn trồng rau); 13. animal barn (chuồng trại); 14. straw stack (đống rơm)); and the classification of architectural space: in form, architectural space can be classified into: inside space (không gian bên trong — interior (nội thất)), usually created thanks to the covering components of the work (ceiling/roof, walls, doors, floors); transition space (không gian chuyển tiếp), the space connecting between the inside space and the outside space, the most common being the veranda form (hình thức hiên nhà) in the work; and outside space (không gian bên ngoài — exterior (ngoại thất)), the space around the work and lying in the construction grounds (khuôn viên xây dựng) such as yard, garden, water lake and swimming pool… (the inside space of the An Hiên house work, Thừa Thiên – Huế — the inside space; the veranda space of the An Hiên house work — the transition space; the yard space (khoảng sân) of the An Hiên house work — the outside space); besides, according to the character of the use activity, architectural space can be classified into still space (không gian tĩnh) and moving space (không gian động); according to the use function, it can be classified into guest-receiving space, eating space, sleeping space, working space… (student book) | Book 9, printed p. 13 |
