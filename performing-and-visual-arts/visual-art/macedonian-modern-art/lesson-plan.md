# Lesson plan — Macedonian Modern Art

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Macedonian Modern Art
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** North Macedonia's modern art — from its beginnings after icon and fresco painting to the generations after the Second World War.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the course's earlier lessons in its title; recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Модерната уметност во РС Македонија, beginnings** — Модерната уметност во РС Македонија, beginnings: art bound to icon and fresco painting until the last zographs — Papradiški, Vangelović, Zografski — begin to paint space, volume and everyday scenes; the first academically trained moderns — Pandilov's poetic landscapes, Martinoski's emotional social scenes, Ličenoski's strong colour; Dimo Todorovski founding modern sculpture, Borka Avramova the first woman sculptor (exhibition 1954).
2. **Macedonian modern art after the Second World War** — Macedonian modern art after the Second World War: art enters public space with architecture (Vladimirski, Kodžoman, Lazeski's murals); the 1950s–60s generation (Kondovski, Korubin, Mazev; sculptors Mitriќeski and Hadži Boškov); the Albanian painter Adem Kastrati's social and ethno-folklore motifs; the groups DENES and MUGRI; abstraction (Mazev, Kondovski) and Macedonian informel — rough matter and the strong gesture (Anastasov, Petlevski); figuration returning in the 1970s as symbol (Lulovski, Frangovski, Temkova, Omer Kaleši's portraits); Perčinkov's geometry tied to architecture; the postmodern new wave (Svetieva, Vangeli, Šumkovski, Pavleski, group Zero); Albanian artists Memeti, Bejtuli-Kica, Beќiri, Ameti.
