# Geometric and Technical Drawing

## Do it first

Quick game: look at a snail shell, a mosquito coil, a plate seen from the side. Spirals and ellipses are hiding in your house — this lesson teaches you to draw them exactly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tracing spirals

A **spiral** is a **plane curve that winds around a point, moving further from it at every turn** — seen in snails, stove elements and mosquito coils.

Spirals of **two, three and four centres** are traced step by step, the **compass point alternating between the centres** so each new arc continues the last. Decorative geometric compositions are built from spirals, for **carpentry, metalwork, textiles and the ornamentation of spaces**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The óvulo and the oval

The **óvulo** is an **egg-shaped closed curve of concordant arcs from four centres**, traced from a given span — a form much used in Renaissance decoration.

The **oval** is a **closed multi-centre curve traced from its given major axis**. Both are traced step by step, and compositions are built from óvulos and ovals.

## Sub lesson 3 — Conic curves and the ellipse

**Conic curves** are the figures made by **cutting a cone with a plane**: the **ellipse** (oblique cut), the **parabola**, the **hyperbola** — and the **circle itself** as a conic.

The **ellipse** has its elements named: **major and minor axes, vertices, foci, focal distance, centre**. An ellipse is what a circle looks like in perspective — as in plates, basins and pots. It is drawn by the **foci method**, step by step (the gardener's, rectangle and circumference methods named), the points joined freehand.

## Sub lesson 4 — The parabola and the hyperbola

The **parabola**: defined by its elements — **directrix, axis, focus, parameter, vertex** — and constructed by **intersecting arcs from the focus onto lines parallel to the directrix**, the points joined freehand. Its everyday form is the **satellite dish**.

The **hyperbola**: an **open two-branch curve**, its elements **axis, vertices, foci**, constructed by the same intersecting-arc method — pots and hourglasses are its everyday forms. Compositions are built from the three conics.
