# Lesson plan — Making with Paper and Pieces

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Making with Paper and Pieces
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** five ways to build a picture or a thing from pieces — papier-mâché by mould and by pulp, collage, mosaic and paper mosaic, tie-dye cloth, and decorating with whatever materials are nearby.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Making with Clay and Paper and Printing — the learner can already shape clay, fold paper and print with found things; this lesson adds the piece-building methods the Grade 4–7 books teach.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Papier-mâché: mould and pulp** — tear paper, soak it, and layer it with glue over a chosen object so the work copies its shape (Grade 4, printed pp. 29–30). mashed soaked paper mixed like clay, modelled over a tied wire-and-wood armature (Grade 4, printed p. 30).
2. **Collage and texture** — a picture glued from natural and man-made things onto one surface, two methods; collage can render roughness and smoothness (Grade 4, printed pp. 30–32).
3. **Mosaic: pictures from small pieces** — glue very small pieces onto a board, sorted and laid grade by grade following light and shade (Grade 5, printed pp. 64–65).
4. **Paper mosaic and potato printing (teacher guide — evidence)** — paper mosaic in five steps; printing with a carved potato stamp (Grade 5 student, printed p. 66; Grade 5 teacher guide, §2.4, printed pp. 62–69).
5. **Tie-dye: tie, boil, reveal** — white cotton folded, twisted and tied tight, dyed, rinsed, untied and dried — the design is decided by the tying (Grade 7, printed pp. 45–46).
6. **Decorating and drawing with materials (teacher guide — evidence)** — decorating and beautifying by using materials, and drawing — the Grade 2 making strand, cited at section level from the scanned guide (§2.4, printed pp. 35–37).

7. **Drawing texture: cross-hatching, dots and smudging (Grade 5)** — the Grade 5 texture techniques — cross-hatching and repeated dots for rough, smudging for smooth.
8. **Mosaic: the ancient way and the heritage practicals (Grade 6)** — mosaic's ancient history, the paper-mosaic method, and the Jegol wall and Gondar palace practicals.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Making with Clay and Paper and Printing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Make papier-mâché two ways: layered over a mould, and modelled as pulp over an armature.
2. Make a collage from natural and man-made things glued onto one surface.
3. Build a mosaic by gluing small pieces grade by grade, light and shade in order.
4. Tie-dye a white cloth: fold, tie, dye, rinse, untie, dry.
5. Decorate and draw with materials found nearby.

## What you need
Old paper and newspapers, glue or flour paste, scissors, coloured papers, a cup or plate to use as a mould, and — for the tie-dye part, with a grown-up — white cotton cloth, string and dye.

## If the learner is stuck
- Papier-mâché sticks to the mould → let every layer dry before adding the next, and lift the shell only when it is fully dry and hard.
- Mosaic pieces drift from the design → draw the design and mark its dark and light grades on the board first, as the book teaches.
