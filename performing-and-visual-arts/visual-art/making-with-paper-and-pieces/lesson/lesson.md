# Making with Paper and Pieces

## Do it first

Tear one page of old paper into small pieces. Lay the pieces in a row, longest to shortest. You have just met the raw material of this whole lesson: small pieces, patiently placed, become big things.

## Sub lesson 1 — Papier-mâché: mould and pulp

**Papier-mâché, method one — the mould method.** Tear paper into pieces, soak them, and layer them with glue **over a chosen object**, so the dried work copies the object's shape. The book's examples: cups, plates, glasses, buckets, waste bins — and its picture shows a **face mask, a cup and a lidded plate** made this way. When the layers are fully dry, lift the shell off the mould: the object lent its shape, and the paper keeps it.

**Method two — the pulp method.** Mash the soaked paper **like clay** and model it over an **armature**: a frame tied together from wire and wood pieces that gives the work its skeleton. The book's picture shows a **hen** made this way — the armature holds the hen's pose, and the pulp becomes its body. Mould method borrows a shape; pulp method builds one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Collage and texture

**Collage** (ኮላዥ) is making a picture by **gluing natural and man-made things onto one surface**. Method one: cut a drawn shape from one paper and glue it onto a thicker surface. Method two: cut photos from newspapers, magazines and books and glue them into composed scenes. Materials: cut papers, grain seeds, plants, barley and wheat grains, cloth pieces. Collage also teaches **texture**: a bird collage can render roughness and smoothness — everything we see and touch has a feel, told apart by looking and by touching, bark and stone against glass and leaf.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mosaic: pictures from small pieces

**Mosaic** makes a big picture by **gluing very small pieces onto a board**. The pieces differ in colour, size and shape; materials include glass shards, metal pieces, various papers, beads (ዶቃ) and marble. Before starting, observe your local things — their colour, smoothness and roughness, shape, fading and standing-out. The process: **draw the design on the board and mark its dark and light degrees; sort the cut pieces by light and shade; glue grade by grade**, following light, shade and texture; finish with varnish if needed.

## Sub lesson 4 — Paper mosaic and potato printing (teacher guide — evidence)

As collage can be made from paper, so can **mosaic**: the **paper mosaic** in five steps — gather coloured papers, magazines, books and newspapers; cut them small in the wanted shapes and colour-value grades; glue the design's surface and place the pieces by their darkness and lightness; back the picture, if you like, with a non-matching paper for beauty; **dry it in the sun**. The teacher guide adds its partner craft: **potato printing** (ህትመት በድንች ማተም) — carve a stamp into a cut potato and print with it, stamp by stamp, into a pattern.

## Sub lesson 5 — Tie-dye: tie, boil, reveal

**Tie-dye** (አስሮ ማቅለም) hides its pattern until the end. The book's way: boil water with **dye, salt and squeezed lemon** in a wide metal pot — a grown-up's job. Take **white cotton cloth**; fold it, twist it, and **tie it tight** with sibago string; tying in pebbles, stone, wood or metal pieces gives richer designs. Boil the bundle **40–50 minutes**, turning it with wood. Rinse in cold water again and again, **untie**, and dry in the sun. The designs vary with the folding, twisting and tying — you designed it before you could see it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Decorating and drawing with materials (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 2 teacher guide's making strand is **decorating and drawing**: decorating and beautifying **by using materials** (§2.4.1) and **drawing** (መሳል, §2.4.2) — models and decoration made from available materials, plus drawing. This guide is a scan with no text layer, so this part is cited at **section level only**, exactly as its placements require — the section's promise is the teaching: what is nearby, decorated well, becomes the work. It is the same promise as the Grade 4 book's crafts from what is nearby — local materials spread out for decorating, shapes and forms.

## Sub lesson 7 — Drawing texture: cross-hatching, dots and smudging (Grade 5)

Texture is the smoothness or roughness you feel by **touching** and by **seeing**: smooth — glass, cotton, soft wool; rough — bark, a brick wall, sacking. In drawing, you make texture with the pencil itself:

- **Roughness** is made by **cross-hatching** — lines crossing lines — or by **repeated dots** (a pattern of points).
- **Smoothness** is made by **smudging** the pencil softly, or by leaving the surface untouched.

[Picture — described only, not made in this fix build: a bark patch built stroke by stroke from cross-hatched lines, beside a smooth smudged patch — the student says which technique made each.]

Try it: draw one small square of bark with crossing lines, one of dots, and one smooth square smudged with a fingertip. Your fingers should be able to guess each one with your eyes shut — that is texture doing its work.

## Sub lesson 8 — Mosaic: the ancient way and the heritage practicals (Grade 6)

Mosaic is among the **ancient** visual methods: temples' and palaces' floors and walls were decorated by assembling and gluing **coloured stones and pottery fragments** — and the practice keeps mosaic beloved in architecture today. Mosaic means making a picture by gluing stones, pottery fragments and glass pieces of varied colours.

**Paper mosaic** is its easy form, worked with thick coloured papers. Glue can be mucilage, cola or local paste. Tools: thick paper, carton, paper glue, pencil, scissors, sharpener, eraser. The steps: draw the wanted thing on thick paper or carton; cut the coloured papers into small sizes; spread glue on the design and, **before it dries**, stick the papers as suited (Fig. 53 shows an example paper-mosaic work).

The heritage practicals: make **Harar's Jegol wall** in paper mosaic; and in groups, make **Gondar's palace** in paper mosaic — and explain how it was first built, and how the fathers preserved it until now.

## Say it to your companion

Hold up your finished piece. Tell your companion which method made it, step by step, without looking at the lesson. They check your steps against the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a papier-mâché cup or mask by the mould method.
2. Make a small paper mosaic of a sun, a house or a flag.
3. Glue a collage of leaves, seeds, cloth and paper that shows roughness and smoothness side by side.
