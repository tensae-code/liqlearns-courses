# Making with Paper and Pieces — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons"). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number, as before. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. The sub lessons climb inside this one lesson, from the youngest books to the oldest.

## Sub lesson 1 — Papier-mâché: mould and pulp
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: make one mould-method piece and say the steps in order: tear, soak, layer, dry, lift. And: say how the pulp method differs from the mould method and what an armature is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Collage and texture
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: make a collage by one method and point to a rough place and a smooth place in it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mosaic: pictures from small pieces
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: lay out a mosaic design with its light and dark grades marked before any gluing begins.

## Sub lesson 4 — Paper mosaic and potato printing (teacher guide — evidence)
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: recite the five paper-mosaic steps and say how a potato stamp prints.

## Sub lesson 5 — Tie-dye: tie, boil, reveal
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: say the tie-dye steps in order and what decides the design.

## Sub lesson 6 — Decorating and drawing with materials (teacher guide — evidence)
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: say what the section teaches: decorating with materials at hand, and drawing (መሳል).

## Sub lesson 7 — Drawing texture: cross-hatching, dots and smudging (Grade 5)
Pass mark: name the smooth and rough examples (glass/cotton/wool; bark/brick/sacking) and make roughness by cross-hatching or repeated dots and smoothness by smudging or leaving untouched.

## Sub lesson 8 — Mosaic: the ancient way and the heritage practicals (Grade 6)
Pass mark: tell what mosaic is (pictures glued from coloured stones, pottery fragments and glass), give the paper-mosaic tools and steps, and make the Jegol wall or Gondar palace practical.
