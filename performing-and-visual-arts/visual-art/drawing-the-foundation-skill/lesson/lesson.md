# Drawing: The Foundation Skill

## Do it first

Take a pencil and draw the cup or pot in front of you — once, quickly, without rubbing out. Keep it. At the end of this lesson you will draw it again and lay the two side by side.

## Sub lesson 1 — Drawing defined: the basis of all visual art

**Drawing is the basis of all visual-art work**: the act of making marks and tone levels with pencil, pen, chalk, charcoal and coloured pencils on paper, card or another board, to express delicate feeling and thought. Drawings also serve as **plans** — a fashion designer draws the clothing design on paper first; an engineer begins a house by drawing its complete design. And drawing grows by **study**: simple-line exercises to understand the main character of a thing. Repeat the study of one thing and the representation comes back corrected and better. Skill grows from the scribble: as scribbling is tamed by study, drawing develops.

## Sub lesson 2 — The drawing tools and the pencil grades

The drawing tools are mainly: **pencil, drawing paper, eraser, sharpener and a drawing support**. The pencil comes in **hard, medium and soft grades** and gives line, light-and-shade, texture and a feeling of distance and depth, with thin and thick points. The **easel** is the fixed support that holds your paper steady. And **how you hold the pencil matters**: the drawing grip gives the hand free movement and lets you rule lines freely — correct your grip before you correct your lines.

## Sub lesson 3 — Shading, the human figure and gesture study

When you draw, render the **light and shade keeping the gradation from light to dark**, and show roughness and smoothness as they are. Drawing is also the basis under painting and sculpture — the sculptor's drawing and the painter's drawing come first. Practise the pictured still lifes, then draw the **human figure above the chest**, female and male. Then go further: **gesture study** — practise the human figure's movement in simple lines in your notebook, and show classmates and teacher. A gesture drawing catches the movement, not the details.

## Sub lesson 4 — Freeing the hand: line drills

The art needs **mind and hand moving together**. First train the hand muscles to obey: practise lines **freely, without a ruler**, holding the pencil or pen long. Repeat **vertical, horizontal, diagonal and curved lines, quarter-circles, half-circles and full circles**, using thickening, thinning, joining and spacing. These drills are not the drawing — they are the hand learning to be trusted, so the drawing can follow.

## Sub lesson 5 — Bigger and smaller on a grid; drawing what you see

To grow a drawing, rule a **grid** over the small picture and a bigger grid with the same number of squares on your new paper; copy **one square at a time** and the picture grows true. The same grids shrink a picture smaller. And the oldest drawing skill of all: **imitation drawing** (አስመስሎ መስራት) — drawing a thing on paper **while looking at it**, at a picture, a poster, a photograph, or the thing itself. Eyes to the thing, eyes to the page, back and forth, until the page agrees with the eyes.

## Sub lesson 6 — The five basic skills of drawing (teacher guide — evidence)

The federal guide names **five basic skills of drawing**: recognising **edges**, understanding **proportion**, **perspective**, **colour schemes**, and **putting the thought together**. It teaches two great sources: drawing **from imagination and memory** — drawing based on verbal and visual memories — and drawing **from what you see**: model drawing, the observational drawing of a model or subject. This part rests on the teacher guide only, and it is labelled that way in the sources.

## Say it to your companion

Show your companion your first drawing and your last one. Tell them two things the second drawing does better, and which sub lesson taught each one.

## Do It Yourself

1. Shade one ball or cup from light to dark, keeping the gradation even.
2. Fill one page with line drills: vertical, horizontal, diagonal, curve, quarter-circle, half-circle, full circle — no ruler.
3. Draw a small picture, grid it, and grow it twice as big on a second grid.
