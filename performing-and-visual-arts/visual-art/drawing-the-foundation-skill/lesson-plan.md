# Lesson plan — Drawing: The Foundation Skill

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Drawing: The Foundation Skill
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** drawing as the basis of all visual art — its tools and pencil grades, shading from light to dark, studying figures and movement, freeing the hand with line drills, growing a drawing on a grid, and drawing what you see.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Marks and Scribbles and Lines in Every Direction — the learner can already make marks freely; this lesson turns mark-making into the studied foundation skill the Grade 6 book teaches.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Drawing defined: the basis of all visual art** — drawing is making marks and tone levels to express thought and feeling; drawings serve as plans and designs; the drawing study (Grade 6, printed pp. 3–6).
2. **The drawing tools and the pencil grades** — pencil, paper, eraser, sharpener and support; hard, medium and soft pencil grades; the easel and the drawing grip (Grade 6, printed pp. 6–8).
3. **Shading, the human figure and gesture study** — render light and shade keeping the gradation; draw the human figure above the chest; study movement in simple gesture lines (Grade 6, printed pp. 9–12).
4. **Freeing the hand: line drills** — mind and hand moving together; practise the seven line kinds freely without a ruler (Grade 5, printed p. 56).
5. **Bigger and smaller on a grid; drawing what you see** — enlarge and reduce a picture cell by cell on a grid; imitation drawing — drawing a thing while looking at it (Grade 4, printed pp. 86 and 97).
6. **The five basic skills of drawing (teacher guide — evidence)** — recognising edges, proportion, perspective, colour schemes, and putting the thought together; drawing from memory and from observation (Grade 9 teacher guide, Lessons 22–25, printed pp. 40–44).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Marks and Scribbles and Lines in Every Direction. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why drawing is the basis of all visual-art work.
2. Name the drawing tools and what hard, medium and soft pencils do.
3. Shade a form keeping the gradation from light to dark.
4. Free the hand with line drills — seven line kinds, freely, without a ruler.
5. Grow or shrink a drawing on a grid, and draw a thing while looking at it.

## What you need
Pencils (soft and hard if you have them), paper, an eraser, a sharpener, and one object to draw from life.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Take a pencil and draw the cup or pot in front of you — once, quickly, without rubbing out. Keep it. At the end of this lesson you will draw it again and lay the two side by side. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Shading jumps from light to black → build the middle greys first; the gradation must walk, not jump.
- The grid copy drifts → copy one square at a time and check its neighbours before moving on.
