# Lesson plan — Drawing What Our Culture Does

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Drawing What Our Culture Does
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** drawing the cultural practices of your own people — threshing with oxen, children dancing, the genna game, a woman roasting coffee — and expressing your feelings in the drawing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the drawing lessons before it — lines, shapes and careful colouring — and turns them onto the richest subject a child has: the life of their own community.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Shapes You Can Draw and Colouring Inside Shapes. Recommended, never forced.
- Its neighbour: Traditional Dress in the Culture and Values unit, where the dress worn in these scenes is studied for itself.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four cultural practices the book pictures for drawing: threshing, dancing, the genna game, coffee roasting.
2. Choose a cultural practice and draw it, showing what the people are doing.
3. Say what feelings your drawing expresses, the way the book asks learners to express their feelings in drawing.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil and colours. Best of all: a real practice you can watch first — or remember clearly.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot choose → draw the practice you have SEEN most recently; memory is freshest there.
- Figures look stiff → draw the ACTION first as big movement lines (the stick swinging, the oxen circling), then dress the lines with bodies.
- Says "my drawing has no feeling" → ask what they feel about the practice itself; that feeling, drawn big or drawn bright, is the answer.
