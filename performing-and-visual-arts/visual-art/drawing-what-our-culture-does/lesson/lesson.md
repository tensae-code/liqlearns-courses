# Drawing What Our Culture Does


## Do it first

Draw one thing your people do together. A work. A game. A celebration. Draw it from your memory, quickly, before any teaching.

Keep it. Now look at what the book chooses to draw — four pictures of a culture at work and at play. Is your drawing a cousin of one of them?

## The four pictured practices

The book's drawing pages show four cultural practices, and learners draw them — and express their feelings in the drawing:

[Picture 9161 — four cultural practices to draw: threshing, dancing, the genna game, coffee roasting]

**Threshing** — oxen walking round and round inside a stone-ringed threshing floor, treading the grain out. **Children dancing** together. **The genna game** — players with curved sticks, chasing the ball. **A woman roasting coffee** — the pan, the rising smoke, the cups waiting.

Beside the pictures sits Exercise 11's question work: when is traditional dress worn, and what is it for? The people in your drawings wear it — dress is part of the practice.

Draw each practice the same way: **who** is there, **what** are they doing, **where** does it happen? Figures in action, in their place.

## Expressing feelings in drawing

The book's ask is bigger than copying: learners **draw cultural performances and express their feelings in drawing**.

A feeling can live in a drawing. Joy can be bigger dancing figures and bright colour. Pride can be a practice drawn large and carefully. Love of home can be the details you bother to include — the ring of stones, the curve of the stick, the smoke over the pan.

Choose the practice that lives in your own home or street. Draw it big. Put your feeling in on purpose — then be ready to say what the feeling is and where it sits in the picture.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Our culture works and plays — threshing, dancing, genna, roasting coffee. I drew this one. The feeling in my drawing is …, and you can see it here." Point to where the feeling sits.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the genna game: two players, curved sticks, the ball — mid-action.
2. Draw coffee roasting at your home: the pan, the smoke, the cups, the person roasting.
3. Draw a cultural practice from YOUR area that is not in the four pictures, and write its name under it.
