# Lesson plan — Painting Then and Now

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Painting Then and Now
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopian painting has its own old style that has grown into today's painting — and a learner can stand before a painting, look slowly, and say what it gives them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Looking at Pictures (First Steps) — the learner already knows how to say what a picture shows; here the pictures are paintings with a history. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Ethiopian painting then and now** — the old colour-painting style, today's painting, and the named modern painter Afework Tekle.
2. **Appreciating paintings** — slow looking at two painting works, and saying what they give you.

3. **Indigenous knowledge in visual art: the heritage roll (Grade 6)** — §4.1 — architecture, jewellery, pottery, cave paintings, Lalibela, Gondar, Jegol, Tiya, church painting since the 4th century.
4. **Indigenous knowledge on stage, film and photograph (Grade 6)** — §4.2 — knowledge that solved the society's problems, and the three house kinds of Fig. 54.
5. **Showing appreciation: documentaries, exhibitions, stamps (Grade 6)** — how appreciation is expressed — and the photo route, with the Konso waka of Fig. 55.
6. **Heritage practicals (Grade 6)** — the §4.2 practicals — theatre circles, the justice system and reconciliation ceremony on stage, tourism-office photographs.
7. **Visual art IS our heritage (Grade 6)** — §5.1.1 — the heritage roll restated, and protecting heritage with posters, stamps, drawing and making.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Looking at Pictures and Appreciating the Arts (First Steps). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say that Ethiopia has its own old colour-painting style, changed through time to today's painting.
2. Name Afework Tekle as the books' modern example.
3. Stand before a painting, look slowly, and say what they see first, which colours carry it, and how it makes them feel.

## What you need
Paper and colours, for the Do It Yourself two-ways painting.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner pictures the oldest painting they can imagine, then a painting of today, and says one difference (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — then-and-now, the named modern painter, and how to appreciate a painting (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9312]: a picture of an old-style painting beside a colourful painting of today — the student names two differences between the two ways of painting.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "I don't like it" and stops → that is a beginning, not an end: ask what they see first, and which colour does the most work. Feelings follow looking.
- Mixes old and new → neither is wrong: the old style carries memory in its own way; today's painting carries today's eyes.
