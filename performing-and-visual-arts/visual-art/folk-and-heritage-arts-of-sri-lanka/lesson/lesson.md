# Folk and Heritage Arts of Sri Lanka

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about folk and heritage arts of sri lanka. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ves masks

Sri Lanka's traditional mask art whole — the mask-making regions (Ambalangoda first), the carving process in stages shown in photographs (kaduru wood prepared, soaked, carved, painted), and the mask families by name and use: royal masks, demon (rakshasa) masks, the sanni disease masks, Naga Raksha, Gurulu Raksha, Gara Yaka and the village-character masks

## Sub lesson 2 — Let's study the art creations of the Hindu kovil

The kovil as an artwork — the gods' vehicle sculptures (Simha the lion, the horse, the peacock and others, photographed), the great wooden festival chariot (Manjam — Jaffna Kandasamy Kovil's 1909–1910 chariot pictured) and its carved tiers, and the kovil tower's (gopuram/vimanam) structure and ornament

## Sub lesson 3 — Let's study Laksha creations

Sri Lanka's lacquer craft — the lacquer industry centred on Matale, its history from the Kandyan kingdom, the two techniques (Niyapothu fingernail work and Pattal lathe work), materials and colouring, and the products (spinning tops, boxes, vases, bobbins) shown in workshop photographs

## Sub lesson 4 — Let's study Sandakada Pahana (moonstone) creations

The moonstone threshold carvings of ancient Sri Lanka — their carved bands and meanings studied from the named examples: Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa and Mahagama (Magul Maha Viharaya) moonstones, photographed

## Sub lesson 5 — Stone Entrance Art Of Ancient Sri Lanka

The carved stone doorway as one art — guardstones (Muragala, with their five named types), balustrades (Korawakgala), the carved stair sides (Siyagal Pela), and the Vahalkada frontispieces and Vatadage circular shrines (Mihintale, Thuparama, Medirigiriya, Polonnaruwa), all photographed and analysed

## Sub lesson 6 — Village arts that express our national heritage

The folk-art map of the island — the book's family tree sorting Sri Lanka's arts into heard arts (folk songs), built arts (weaving, pottery, lacquer, brass, jewellery) and performed arts (Wannam, Kolam, Sokkari drama, folk dance), so a pupil can place any craft they meet

## Sub lesson 7 — the lacquer craft

Arakku (lacquer) work pictured and explained — coloured lacquer built up and patterned on turned wood (fig. 1.3) (joins the Art guide's Laksha placement)
