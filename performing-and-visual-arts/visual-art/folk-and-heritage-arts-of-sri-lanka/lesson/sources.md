| Lesson part | Where it comes from |

|---|---|

| Sri Lanka — Activity 6.1.1 "Ves masks" — Sri Lanka's traditional mask art whole — the mask-making regions (Ambalangoda first), the carving process in stages shown in photographs (kaduru wood prepared, soaked, carved, painted), and the mask families by name and use: royal masks, demon (rakshasa) masks, the sanni disease masks, Naga Raksha, Gurulu Raksha, Gara Yaka and the village-character masks (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/ves-masks-of-sri-lanka`) | Book 1, printed pp. 35–39 |

| Sri Lanka — Activity 6.2.1 "Let's study the art creations of the Hindu kovil" — the kovil as an artwork — the gods' vehicle sculptures (Simha the lion, the horse, the peacock and others, photographed), the great wooden festival chariot (Manjam — Jaffna Kandasamy Kovil's 1909–1910 chariot pictured) and its carved tiers, and the kovil tower's (gopuram/vimanam) structure and ornament (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/hindu-kovil-art-and-chariots`) | Book 1, printed pp. 40–45 |

| Sri Lanka — Activity 6.3.1 "Let's study Laksha creations" — Sri Lanka's lacquer craft — the lacquer industry centred on Matale, its history from the Kandyan kingdom, the two techniques (Niyapothu fingernail work and Pattal lathe work), materials and colouring, and the products (spinning tops, boxes, vases, bobbins) shown in workshop photographs (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/laksha-lacquer-work-of-sri-lanka`) | Book 1, printed pp. 46–49 |

| Sri Lanka — Activity 7.1.1 "Let's study Sandakada Pahana (moonstone) creations" — the moonstone threshold carvings of ancient Sri Lanka — their carved bands and meanings studied from the named examples: Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa and Mahagama (Magul Maha Viharaya) moonstones, photographed (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/sandakada-pahana-moonstone-carvings`) | Book 1, printed pp. 63–65 |

| Sri Lanka — Activities 7.1.2–7.1.3 — the carved stone doorway as one art — guardstones (Muragala, with their five named types), balustrades (Korawakgala), the carved stair sides (Siyagal Pela), and the Vahalkada frontispieces and Vatadage circular shrines (Mihintale, Thuparama, Medirigiriya, Polonnaruwa), all photographed and analysed (teacher guide — evidence; original target `culture-and-values/stone-entrance-art-of-ancient-sri-lanka`) | Book 1, printed pp. 66–74 |
