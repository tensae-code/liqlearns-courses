# Lesson plan — Folk and Heritage Arts of Sri Lanka

**Course:** Performing and Visual Arts · **Title:** Visual Art · **Lesson:** Folk and Heritage Arts of Sri Lanka
**Place in the curriculum:** Visual Art (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** folk and Heritage Arts of Sri Lanka — its forms, history and cultural meaning, taught as one topic lesson.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course in the same title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ves masks** — Sri Lanka's traditional mask art whole — the mask-making regions (Ambalangoda first), the carving process in stages shown in photographs (kaduru wood prepared, soaked, carved, painted), and the mask families by name and use: royal masks, demon (rakshasa) masks, the sanni disease masks, Naga Raksha, Gurulu Raksha, Gara Yaka and the village-character masks
2. **Let's study the art creations of the Hindu kovil** — the kovil as an artwork — the gods' vehicle sculptures (Simha the lion, the horse, the peacock and others, photographed), the great wooden festival chariot (Manjam — Jaffna Kandasamy Kovil's 1909–1910 chariot pictured) and its carved tiers, and the kovil tower's (gopuram/vimanam) structure and ornament
3. **Let's study Laksha creations** — Sri Lanka's lacquer craft — the lacquer industry centred on Matale, its history from the Kandyan kingdom, the two techniques (Niyapothu fingernail work and Pattal lathe work), materials and colouring, and the products (spinning tops, boxes, vases, bobbins) shown in workshop photographs
4. **Let's study Sandakada Pahana (moonstone) creations** — the moonstone threshold carvings of ancient Sri Lanka — their carved bands and meanings studied from the named examples: Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa and Mahagama (Magul Maha Viharaya) moonstones, photographed
5. **Stone Entrance Art Of Ancient Sri Lanka** — the carved stone doorway as one art — guardstones (Muragala, with their five named types), balustrades (Korawakgala), the carved stair sides (Siyagal Pela), and the Vahalkada frontispieces and Vatadage circular shrines (Mihintale, Thuparama, Medirigiriya, Polonnaruwa), all photographed and analysed
6. **Village arts that express our national heritage** — the folk-art map of the island — the book's family tree sorting Sri Lanka's arts into heard arts (folk songs), built arts (weaving, pottery, lacquer, brass, jewellery) and performed arts (Wannam, Kolam, Sokkari drama, folk dance), so a pupil can place any craft they meet
7. **the lacquer craft** — Arakku (lacquer) work pictured and explained — coloured lacquer built up and patterned on turned wood (fig. 1.3) (joins the Art guide's Laksha placement)
