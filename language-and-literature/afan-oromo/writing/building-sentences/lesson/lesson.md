# Building Sentences

## Do it first
Here are three words lying flat: *sareen — foon — nyaatti*. A dog, meat, eats. They are not a sentence yet — they are a pile.

Now watch them stand: **Sareen foon nyaatti** — the dog eats meat. A sentence is a pile of words stood up in the right order, with the verb agreeing with its doer. The Grade 4 guide builds sentences at five benches. This lesson works at all five.

## Sub lesson 1 — From three word columns
The first bench: **three word columns** — who/what in the first, the middle words in the second, the verb in the third. Pick one from each column, in order, and a sentence stands. The guide's key gives:

- **Borsumaan barataa cimaadha** — Borsumaa is a strong student
- **Faayoon uffata miiccite** — Faayoo washed clothes

Notice Faayoo's verb: *miiccite* — the she-form, from He, She and They. The columns do not free you from agreement; every pick must still agree.

## Sub lesson 2 — From word groups
The second bench: **word groups** — cards with words on them, laid out and ordered into sentences. The guide's key:

- **Furtuu balbalaa qabdi** — the key holds the door
- **Sareen foon nyaatti** — the dog eats meat
- **Re'een baala nyaatti** — the goat eats leaves
- **Lukkuun dheedhii guurratti** — the hen gathers grain

Same building, looser timber: no columns this time, just the words and your knowledge of their order — doer, thing, doing.

## Sub lesson 3 — From phrases — gaalee into hima
The third bench starts one step lower: a **phrase** — *gaalee*. A phrase is a word-group with no verb of its own: the guide's key first names favourite dishes as phrases — *ittoo lukkuu* (chicken stew), *ittoo foonii* (meat stew), *ittoo shiroo*, *ittoo misiraa* (lentil stew) — and then builds phrases into whole sentences (*hima*):

- **Maatii kabajuun nurraa eegama** — respecting family is expected of us

A phrase is a sentence's seed. Give it a verb and a doer and it stands.

## Sub lesson 4 — Around a verb
The fourth bench starts from the engine: **the verb**. The guide's key builds sentences around given verbs:

- **Boontuun dhufte** — Boontuu came
- **Boonaa konkolaataa bite** — Boonaa bought a car

Given the doing word, the builder must supply who did it and what it was done to — and the verb's form must fit the doer chosen (dhufte for Boontuu, she). The verb is the bench's fixed point; the sentence is built around it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Completing sentences
The fifth bench works on a sentence already begun: **complete it**. The guide's keys give two shapes of the work:

- Fill the **missing subject word**: the sentence "…nyaatte" (…ate) waits for its doer — "Muuzii nyaatte" is the key's worked example of the completion habit, the eater's food named and the sentence standing whole.
- **Complete started sentences** in the family chapter's exercises, where the verb persons of the answers (isheen dhufte / ani dhufe / nuti dhufne / ati dhufte / isaan dhufan) show that a completion is only right when its verb agrees with the subject you supply.

A completion is a small building job: read what stands, find what is missing, supply exactly that.

## Say it to your companion
Build against each other. One of you writes three word columns, or lays out a word group, or gives a bare verb. The other builds the sentence aloud. Then swap benches. Every build gets the agreement check: point at the verb, name its doer, and say why the form fits.

## Do It Yourself
1. Cut twelve word cards at home: four doers, four things, four verbs. Build every sentence the cards allow and write the best six.
2. Write two phrases of your own — a food, a place — and grow each into a sentence.
3. Take the verb bite (bought). Build three sentences around it with three different doers.
4. Write three started sentences, swap with a friend or family member, and complete each other's.
