# First Written Pieces

## Do it first
Write the best Afaan Oromoo sentence you can. Right now, on paper.

Done? You have just done what this lesson is about — only this lesson does it five ways, the five first pieces the guides set a new writer: greeting words, a tiny story, an introduction, food sentences, and the names of your own relatives, spelled right.

## Sub lesson 1 — First words — writing the greetings
The Grade 3 guide's first chapter ends with the hand: after the greeting dialogues, learners **read and write the greeting words** — the words of ganama, guyyaa and galgala meetings, set down on paper.

These are first words in both senses: the first words of the course, and the first words a new writer writes in Afaan Oromoo. Write them slowly and whole; a greeting written is a greeting kept.

## Sub lesson 2 — A four-sentence story about a friend
The Grade 4 guide sets the first story with a strict frame: a friend's story — **seenaa hiriyyaa** — **in not more than four sentences**.

Four sentences is a discipline, not a poverty: one to place the friend, one or two for what happened, one to close. A writer who can say it in four has understood it; a writer who needs forty may not have found it yet.

## Sub lesson 3 — Introducing yourself in a short text
The introduction you spoke in the Speaking unit, written: name, mother's and father's names, age, and the short telling of experience — as a **short text**, in full sentences, in order.

The guide joins the skills: the text is written, then **read aloud** — the speaking lesson owns the reading; here, own the writing. Names spelled as your family spells them; every fact present; nothing padded.

## Sub lesson 4 — Sentences about eating and cutting food
The food chapter's writing: sentences that describe food actions, from the guide's answer key:

- **buddeena nyaataa jira** — (he/she) is eating injera
- **foon murataa jira** — (he/she) is cutting meat

These are the happening-now sentences of the Sentence unit, written about the table: *nyaataa jira*, *murataa jira* — eating, cutting, caught in the middle of the act. Write about a real meal you have seen: who eats, who cuts, who serves.

## Sub lesson 5 — Spelling names of relatives
The marriage-custom chapter's writing twin: **maqaa firoota** — the names of relatives — **spelled and written**.

A relative's name spelled wrong is a small discourtesy in ink. The work: take your family's names one by one — the wider circle the Speaking lesson told aloud — and write each name exactly as it is spelled, until the hand knows them all.

## Say it to your companion
Exchange pieces. Read your companion's friend story: count its sentences — four or fewer? — and find its placing, happening and close. Read the introduction: are all the facts there? Then dictate each other three relatives' names for spelling, and check them letter by letter.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the greeting words for ganama, guyyaa and galgala.
2. Write your friend's story in not more than four sentences.
3. Write your introduction as a short text.
4. Write two food sentences — one eating, one cutting — about a real meal.
5. Write your relatives' names, spelled exactly — six at least.
Keep all five pieces together: they are your first book.
