# Hands-on — Kinds of Writing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Time-marker line
For narrative: a washing line across the room holds time-marker cards — four years ago, two years later, three months ago, a week later (the model's own markers). Learners hang their narrative's paragraph cards under the markers they use, and the class reads the line as one story.

## Recognise me
Descriptive paragraphs are read aloud with names removed. The class must say who is described — from the features alone. If no one can, the description goes back for truer features, not more words.

## Reasons auction
For persuasion: pairs collect reasons for their position on cards, then "bid" them in order — strongest last before the ask, as the model builds health, money, community, ask. The class judges which ordering persuades.
The class poetry reading (sub lesson 5, G7 supplement): the lesson closes as the book's Exercise 7 closes — each learner stands and reads their walaloo aloud from the front of the class, Picture 12511's scene made real; the teacher's feedback is given kindly and at once, and each poet writes the one change they will make before leaving.
