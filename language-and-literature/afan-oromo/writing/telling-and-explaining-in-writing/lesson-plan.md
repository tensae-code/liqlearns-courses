# Lesson plan — Telling and Explaining in Writing

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Writing · **Lesson:** Telling and Explaining in Writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Writing (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Grade 7 writing strand's first steps: narrating an event in order (taatee seenessuu), the explanatory paragraph (keeyyata ibsaa) that lays out steps, the outline (gumee) you make before writing, and continuing a folk tale from its opening.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building Sentences and First Written Pieces — the learner writes whole short pieces. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Narrating an event in order** — narrating a simple event in order (taatee seenessuu), with the chapter's grammar — present/habitual time forms (hennaa ammeennaa) (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 2 contents).
2. **The explanatory paragraph — steps and explanations** — explanatory paragraph models printed in the guide: (1) how to brew coffee step by step; (2) the benefits of group study (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 2).
3. **Making an outline — gumee — before writing** — outlining before writing (gumee): the worked outline for "Faayidaa Barnootaa" (The Benefits of Education), then a three-paragraph text built from it (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 2).
4. **Continuing a folk tale** — a story-writing exercise opening with a folk tale: a hyena ate someone's cow; two men hunt it; fleeing, it begs a man driving a donkey to hide it on the donkey… (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 3).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Written Pieces — the friend story there is this lesson's narrating in miniature. Recommended, never forced.
- The present forms of Saying What Is Happening Now are the narrating tools of Sub lesson 1.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Narrate a simple event in order (taatee seenessuu), step by step.
2. Write an explanatory paragraph that lays out steps — the coffee-brewing model — or explains — the group-study model.
3. Make an outline (gumee) before writing, on the guide's worked model (Faayidaa Barnootaa), and build a text from it.
4. Continue a folk tale from a given opening, keeping its road.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells a real small event — a spilt cup, a lost goat — aloud, and the class catches where the order broke (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — narrating in order; the two explanatory models the guide prints; the outline (gumee) and its worked example; the folk-tale opening and how to continue it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9945] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of the gumee as a tree: the trunk labelled Faayidaa Barnootaa, three branches — understanding our environment, finding solutions to problems, development — each with its twigs named — the student copies the tree from the picture, then grows the same tree for a new topic of their own.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 2: a voice reading the coffee-brewing steps in order, slowly — the student listens and numbers the steps on paper as they come, then checks the count against the lesson.
3. **Say it to your companion** — swap outlines before writing — build your text from your companion's gumee and let them check it against their plan (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — an outline growing like a tree and a text growing from it, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, ordering, writing, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — outline and write one explanatory paragraph at home: how something in your house is done, step by step (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Narration scrambles order → number the steps in the margin before writing the sentences. The numbers are scaffolding; rub them out at the end.
- Explanatory paragraph lists benefits when it promised steps → the two roads are different: steps can be *done* in order; reasons can only be *weighed*. Ask which road the paragraph is on and hold it there.
- Outline copies the text instead of planning it → a gumee holds heads and points only — Faayidaa Barnootaa's model has no sentences. If it has sentences, it is a draft, not an outline.
