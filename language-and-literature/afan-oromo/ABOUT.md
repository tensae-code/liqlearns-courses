# About this course — Afan Oromo

**How this course is made.** Every lesson in this course is
built from Ethiopia's own government teacher guides for Afan
Oromo, read page by page in the November 2026 Ethiopia reading
wave (148 placements). Each lesson's own `sources.md` carries
the full per-part provenance: which guide, which chapter, which
printed page, and the acceptance line.

**The books — all three are teacher guides.** No Afan Oromo
student textbook exists in any record we hold, so this whole
course stands on guide evidence, and every lesson's acceptance
count is 0 countries — teacher-guide evidence only.

- **Grade 3 teacher guide** — Afan Oromo taught as a second
  language. Addis Ababa City Administration Education Bureau.
  Complete: printed pp. 1–68 (printed page = PDF page − 4).
- **Grade 4 teacher guide** — Afan Oromo taught as a second
  language. Addis Ababa City Administration Education Bureau.
  Complete: printed pp. 1–89 (printed page = PDF page − 3).
- **Grade 7 teacher guide** — Afan Oromo taught as a mother
  tongue. Addis Ababa City Administration Education Bureau.
  Complete: printed pp. 1–170 (printed page = PDF page − 3).

One correction carried through every citation: the library's
book list (books.json) credits these guides to an "Oromia
Education Bureau". That line is wrong — the guides are Addis
Ababa (Finfinnee) bureau books, and the lessons cite them so.

**One supplement, from a completed re-read.** The Grade 7
guide's placements file first reached the builders cut off
inside Chapter 9; a full re-read on 2026-11-12 completed it
(Chapter 9's tail and the whole of Chapter 10 — the
oral-poetry chapter), and a supplement was built from those
eleven placements the same day: six new lessons and five new
sub lessons, listed in CURRICULUM.md. The Chapter 10 reading
(a childhood remembered) is stocked by its answer keys only —
the reading's own text is not printed in the guide, and the
praise song of the plough-ox is carried as a summary, its
text staying in the guide for the teacher to sing from.

**The two framings are kept apart.** The Grade 3/4 guides teach
the language to learners who speak another language first; the
Grade 7 guide teaches children who already speak it at home.
Lessons founded on each framing say which one they stand on.

**How the guides themselves teach — their own shapes
(teacher-guide evidence).** The course above reorganises the
guides' contents by the size of the thing (Letters →
Listening). The guides' own way of organising the year is
different, and it stands here as evidence in its own right —
these are the books' own structures, not the course's.

*Grade 3 guide (second language).* The year is **10 chapters
in two semesters** — Chapters 1–5 in Semester 1, Chapters
6–10 in Semester 2 — totalling **120 periods of 45 minutes**
(*wayitii*): Ch. 1 ×10, Ch. 2 ×10, Ch. 3 ×11, Ch. 4 ×12,
Ch. 5 ×12, Ch. 6 ×13, Ch. 7 ×12, Ch. 8 ×13, Ch. 9 ×13,
Ch. 10 ×14 (Grade 3 TG, chapter heads, printed pp. 1–48;
chapter starts pp. 1, 3, 8, 12, 17, 23, 28, 32, 38, 43). The
book closes with its own **syllabus tables (printed
pp. 49–58)** giving, per chapter, the objectives, the
listening/speaking competencies, the reading/writing
competencies, the teaching strategies and the assessment —
the book's own scope-and-sequence summary, and the prime
evidence for this course's map. (One printed inconsistency
rides with it: Chapter 8's chapter text gives 13 periods
while its syllabus table, printed p. 56, gives 12 — recorded
as printed in each place.) Every chapter follows the same
internal shape: introduction (*seensa*) → objectives
(*kaayyoowwan*) → contents (*qabiyyee*) → teaching method
(*mala baruu barsiisuu*) split into Lesson 1 Listening &
Speaking (*dhaggeeffachuufi dubbachuu*) and Lesson 2 Reading
& Writing (*dubbisuufi barreessuu*), sometimes a Lesson 3 →
**full answer keys** (*deebii gilgaalotaa*) for every
exercise in the student book → assessment method (*tooftaa
madaallii*); the four skills are worked in every chapter,
and this chapter → lesson → exercise-answer shape is the
shape the course's own evidence rests on. The guide's
teaching-method menu, restated per chapter: group
discussion, singing (*faaruu*), demonstration,
role-play/dialogue (*waliin dubbii*), question-and-answer,
picture observation, syllable splitting/joining (*qoqqooduu
/ walitti makuu*), flash cards (*kaardii libsuu*), charts
and the blackboard. Its assessment is continuous (*madaallii
itti fufiinsa qabu*), restated per chapter: oral
questioning, classwork, homework, projects, role-play and
observation of the four skills.

*Grade 4 guide (second language).* The year is **12
chapters, 120 periods of 45 minutes** — the same year-shape
as Grade 3: Ch. 1 ×13, Ch. 2 ×10, Ch. 3 ×10, Ch. 4 ×10,
Ch. 5 ×9, Ch. 6 ×10, Ch. 7 ×10, Ch. 8 ×10, Ch. 9 ×10,
Ch. 10 ×10, Ch. 11 ×9, Ch. 12 ×9 (Grade 4 TG, printed
pp. 1–71; chapter starts pp. 1, 7, 12, 17, 23, 28, 34, 40,
47, 54, 61, 66). (Chapter 5's count differs inside the book:
its chapter header, printed p. 23, says 10 periods; its
syllabus table, printed p. 77, says 9 — recorded both ways.)
The chapters are followed by the book's own per-chapter
**syllabus tables (printed pp. 72–89)**, all twelve chapters
tabled. Every chapter now has **three lessons** — a step up
from Grade 3's two: Lesson 1 Listening & Speaking
(*dhaggeeffachuufi dubbachuu*), Lesson 2 Reading & Writing
(*dubbisuufi barreessuu*), Lesson 3 Vocabulary Use &
Grammar (*fayyadama jechootaafi caasluga/seerluga*) — with
objectives, contents, teaching strategy (*tarsimoo
barsiisuu*), a long assessment menu (*madaallii*) per skill,
and full answer keys (*deebii gilgaalotaa*). The assessment
menus, restated per chapter per skill: observation, oral
questions, flash-card letter checks, started-sentence
completion, peer applause/correction, written tests and
sentence-building checks; flash cards (*kaardii libsuu*) and
the blackboard are the book's standard teaching aids
throughout. The guide's two running spines — numbers (ten
numbers per chapter) and grammar (taught *alkallattiin*,
through examples, not head-on) — already run through the
course as progressive strands inside the number-words and
sentence lessons.

*Grade 7 guide (mother tongue).* The year is **10 thematic
chapters** of shorter, **40-minute** periods: Chapters 1–6
×9 and Chapters 7–10 ×12 — **102 periods in total** (Grade 7
TG, printed pp. 1–170; chapter starts pp. 1, 15, 30, 44, 58,
72, 87, 100, 116, 131). After the body come the book's own
**Semester 1 and Semester 2 syllabus tables**, covering all
ten chapters. The chapters: 1 *Uumama Dinqisiifachuu*
(Admiring Creation/Nature), 2 *Karoora Maatii* (Family
Planning), 3 *Tola Ooltummaa* (Volunteering), 4 *Jireenya
Namoota Beekamoo* (Lives of Famous People), 5 *Bishaaniifi
Faayidaa Isaa* (Water and Its Benefits), 6 *Daddabarsa Dhala
Namaa* (Human Trafficking), 7 *Hariiroo Hawaasaa* (Social
Relations), 8 *Araada* (Addiction), 9 *Jaalala Biyyaa* (Love
of Country), 10 *Afwalaloo* (Oral Poetry). The guide's
five-lesson chapter shape and its three-stage
before/during/after frame, and its writing and grammar
strands, are already carried inside the Grade 7 lessons
themselves.

**Book-level honesty, carried into the lessons that meet it:**
the Grade 4 alphabet chart prints the letter Q twice (taught
once, the doubling reported, not hidden); the Grade 4 chapter 9
decade words clash with the book's own pattern (the
pattern-consistent forms are taught and the mismatch flagged);
the Grade 3 number key for "0–20" prints no word for zero (none
was invented); the Grade 4 seasons table gives no months for
Ganna (left open, not filled in); songs whose wording lives in
the missing student books are sung as the guides' own word rows.

**Pictures and films.** The course's pictures and demonstration
GIFs live in the shared picture library (numbers 9850–9907 for
Units 1–3) and are drawn for this course — the Qubee alphabet is
Latin script, so charts are drawn, keeping every letter exact.
Every lesson also carries a video script and described audio
slots; recording is a later step.
