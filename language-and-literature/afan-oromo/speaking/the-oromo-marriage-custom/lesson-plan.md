# Lesson plan — The Oromo Marriage Custom

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** The Oromo Marriage Custom
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** describing the Oromo marriage custom (sirna fuudhaafi heerumaa aadaa Oromoo) from pictures and role-playing it — acting out the marriage ceremony of your own area, and telling each other your relatives' names.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole Speaking unit — the learner greets, introduces, describes and converses. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Introducing Yourself — relatives' names and family placing are learned there. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the Oromo marriage custom (sirna fuudhaafi heerumaa aadaa Oromoo) from pictures.
2. Role-play the marriage ceremony of your own area with classmates.
3. Tell each other your relatives' names (maqaa firoota) inside the custom's talk.

## What you need
Pictures of a traditional Oromo wedding gathering help — the lesson's own picture, or family photos where families allow. Space to role-play.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells what they have seen at a real wedding in their area, before any teaching (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the custom described from pictures, as the Grade 4 guide works it; then the role-play — learners act out the ceremony of their own area; relatives' names told to each other (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9942] in Sub lesson 1: a picture of a traditional Oromo wedding gathering: families assembled in festive modest dress, elders honoured at the front, the couple at the centre of the gathering — the student describes the gathering from the picture — the people, their order and their dress — before the role-play begins.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 3: a voice naming relatives and their standings one by one, with a pause after each — the student listens and repeats each name with its standing aloud in the pause.
3. **Say it to your companion** — prepare the role-play together in groups, then tell each other your relatives' names and where each stands in the family (lesson/lesson.md).

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — ask at home about a wedding in your family's memory and retell its order aloud (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Describes a wedding from anywhere but their own area → the guide's placement is specific: the ceremony of your own area. Differences between areas are content, not error.
- Role-play dissolves into giggling → walk the order as stations first, narrator telling each step; the play begins only when the walk is right.
- Relatives named without standing → a name at a wedding carries its place: whose, which side, elder or younger. Ask "where does this relative stand?" until the placing is automatic.
