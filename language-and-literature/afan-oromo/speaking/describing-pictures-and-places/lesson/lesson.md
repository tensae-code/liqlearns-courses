# Describing Pictures and Places

## Do it first
Close your eyes while I describe: *flat fields, then a river bending, then hills rising behind, trees along the water.* Open them. You saw a place that isn't in this room — the words built it.

That building is describing. The guides teach it in three widening rings: a picture, your school ground, and the land itself. This lesson walks the three rings.

## Sub lesson 1 — Describing a picture
The Grade 3 book gives a whole chapter to this: **Fakkiiwwan Ibsuu** — Describing Pictures. The method is the lesson: look at the picture, and describe what it shows **using your prior experience** — what you already know of the world the picture shows.

A description is not a list of everything. It goes: what is this place or scene, what stands where, what is happening, what it reminds you of in your own life. The same describing is done **orally and in writing** — the mouth first, the hand after, as everywhere in these guides.

## Sub lesson 2 — Describing my school and surroundings
The Grade 4 chapter is called **Naannoo Ibsuu** — Describing Your Surroundings. Its speaking work: the **things found in the school and its surroundings**, named from pictures and then described in place.

Walk the compound with words: the classrooms, the trees, the flag, the gate, the road beyond, the shops or fields around. Where is each thing against the others — beside, behind, across from? A surroundings description is a small map spoken aloud, and the listener should be able to walk it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Describing the land in a picture
The widest ring: **haala teessuma lafaa** — the shape of the land, its lay. The Grade 4 guide sets learners to describe a landscape from a picture: the hills and how they rise, the river and where it bends, the flat fields and what grows or grazes there, the sky over it all.

Land describing has its own order: near to far, or low to high — pick a road for the eye and walk the listener along it. The land words you need were stocked in the Word lessons; here they stand up and form a view.

## Say it to your companion
The hidden-picture game. One of you holds a picture the other cannot see — a scene, a school ground, a landscape. Describe it until your companion can draw its shape: what is where, what is happening. Then show the picture: what did the words build right, and what did they miss? Swap pictures and play again.

## Do It Yourself
1. Choose a picture — from a book, a wall, anywhere. Describe it aloud in five sentences.
2. Describe your school compound (or your home ground) in five spoken sentences, each thing placed against another.
3. Describe the land you can see from your door: hills, water, flat land, sky — along one road for the eye.
4. Write your best of the three descriptions down, exactly as you spoke it.
