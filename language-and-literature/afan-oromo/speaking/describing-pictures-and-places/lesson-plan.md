# Lesson plan — Describing Pictures and Places

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** Describing Pictures and Places
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** saying what a picture shows and what a place holds — describing a picture from your own experience, naming the things of the school and its surroundings, and describing the land in a picture (haala teessuma lafaa): hills, river, flat fields.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word lessons — the learner holds the vocabulary of animals, house things, school things and land words. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Describing a picture** — describing what a picture shows, orally and in writing, using learners' prior experience (Grade 3 guide, Ch. 4 — Fakkiiwwan Ibsuu, Describing Pictures).
2. **Describing my school and surroundings** — things found in the school and its surroundings named from pictures; the school compound described (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 1).
3. **Describing the land in a picture** — describing a landscape/terrain from a picture (haala teessuma lafaa) (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 9).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is

the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Word lessons — domestic animals, things in the house, school materials. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe what a picture shows, orally, using your own prior experience.
2. Name the things found in the school and its surroundings and describe the compound.
3. Describe the land in a picture (haala teessuma lafaa) — its hills, its river, its flat fields.

## What you need
Pictures to describe — the lesson's own picture, or any picture to hand. Paper and a pencil for the written twin of the task.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner describes the view from the nearest window, right now, before any teaching — and notices what a description must do (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — describing a picture the Grade 3 guide's way, from prior experience; then the school compound's things, Grade 4; then the land in a picture (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9938] in Sub lesson 3: a picture of a landscape to describe: flat fields in front, a river bending through the middle, hills rising behind, trees along the water and a wide sky — the student describes the land along the near-to-far road — fields, river, hills, sky — in order.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 1: a voice describing a busy market scene slowly, sentence by sentence — the student listens and draws the scene from the words alone, then compares with a real market picture.
3. **Say it to your companion** — describe a picture your companion cannot see, until they can draw its shape from your words alone (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a landscape opening like a page — hills, river, fields — as its description is spoken, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — describe your own school or home ground in five spoken sentences, then write them (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists things with no placing → a description puts each thing against another: beside, behind, across from. Without placing, it is a shopping list, not a scene.
- Describes only the middle of the picture → give the eye a road: near to far, or low to high, and walk it without jumping.
- Copies nothing from own experience → the Grade 3 method is explicit: describe using prior experience. Ask "what does this remind you of at home?" and the sentences come.
