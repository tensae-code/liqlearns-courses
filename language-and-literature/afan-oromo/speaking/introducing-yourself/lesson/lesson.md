# Introducing Yourself

## Do it first
Stand up. Pretend the room is full of people who have never met you. Say who you are — out loud, in the language you think in.

What did you say? Probably your name first. The Afaan Oromoo way goes further, and it is a good way: name, then **your mother's and father's names**, then **your age**. You are introduced not as a lone pebble but as a person who comes from people. This lesson teaches that introduction — first for the Grade 3 voice, then for the deeper Grade 4 one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saying who you are
The Grade 3 guide's syllabus names the introduction's contents exactly:

1. **Your own name** — who you are called.
2. **Your mother's name and your father's name** — whose child you are.
3. **Your age** — how many years you carry.

Practise it standing. Name first, spoken clearly. Then mother and father — in Afaan Oromoo families this is not extra information; it *places* you, the way a surname places people in other languages. Then your age, and you are done: a whole person, introduced.

## Sub lesson 2 — Telling your experiences
The Grade 4 guide returns to the introduction and deepens it. Now, after the three facts, you **tell**: something that happened to you — personal experience — and something that happened to your group — group experience.

A fact places you; a small story *shows* you. "I am the one who…" — who carried the water when the pipe broke, who won the race at the school games, whose group planted the trees by the road. Keep it short and true: two or three sentences of telling after the three facts.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading your introduction aloud
The Grade 4 way finishes the act by joining the two skills: you **write** your introduction as a short text, and then you **read it aloud** to the class.

Writing first steadies the speaking: the names are spelled right, the order is set, nothing is forgotten standing up. Then the reading must still be a *telling*, not a mumble into the page — eyes up at the ends of sentences, voice to the back row.

The written text itself is stocked in full in the Writing lesson First Written Pieces; here the same text is your speaking script.

## Say it to your companion
Introduce yourselves to each other, in pairs, the full way: greeting, name, mother's and father's names, age, and one small experience. Your companion listens with a checklist: all three facts present? The telling true and short? Swap, and introduce again — the second time is always smoother.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write your three facts on paper: your name, your mother's name, your father's name, your age.
2. Stand and say your introduction aloud three times — once to the wall, once to a chair, once to a real person.
3. Add one true experience sentence. Stand and give the whole introduction again.
4. Write the introduction as a short text (the Writing lesson shows how) and read it aloud to someone at home.
