# Lesson plan — Introducing Yourself

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** Introducing Yourself
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** standing up and saying who you are in Afaan Oromoo: your own name, your mother's and father's names, and your age — then, a year deeper, telling your experiences and reading your own written introduction aloud.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Greetings lesson next door — the learner can already greet and be greeted. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Saying who you are** — self-introduction: saying your own name, your mother's and father's names, and your age (Grade 3 guide, Ch. 3; syllabus table).
2. **Telling your experiences** — greetings and self-introduction revisited at a higher level: telling personal and group experiences (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 6).
3. **Reading your introduction aloud** — writing a short text introducing yourself and reading it aloud (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 6) — the speaking and writing of the same act, joined.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Greetings — a self-introduction opens with a greeting. Recommended, never forced.
- The Writing lesson First Written Pieces carries the written form of this same introduction.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Introduce yourself aloud: your name, your mother's and father's names, and your age.
2. Tell something of your personal and group experiences, the Grade 4 step.
3. Read your own short written introduction aloud to listeners, standing tall.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil for the written step. Listeners — a class, a family, a companion.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands, right now, and introduces themselves in their own language — then learns the Afaan Oromoo shape of the same act (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the Grade 3 guide's own introduction contents — name, parents' names, age; then the Grade 4 revisit: experiences told, the introduction written and read aloud (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives only a name → the Afaan Oromoo introduction is three facts: name, mother's and father's names, age. The parents' names place the child; they are not decoration.
- Rushes the names → names are the load-bearing words. Slow down exactly there; the rest can flow.
- Reads the written text into the page → eyes up at every sentence end. The text is a script for speaking, not a hiding place.
