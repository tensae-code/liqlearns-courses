# Greetings

## Do it first
It is morning. You meet your teacher at the school gate. What do you say? Now picture the same meeting at midday — and again in the evening, on the road home. The greeting changes, doesn't it? The words bend to the hour.

Afaan Oromoo bends them openly: the day has its divisions — **ganama**, **guyyaa**, **galgala** — and the greeting fits the division. This is the Grade 3 guide's very first chapter, and it is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Greeting the teacher and each other
The first chapter of the Grade 3 guide is called **Nagaa Walgaafachuu** — Greeting Each Other. Its work is simple and whole: learners **greet the teacher** and **greet each other**, aloud, standing, until the greeting is a habit of the body and not a line remembered.

A greeting is the first speaking a child does in a new language, because it is the first speaking a child does at all. Practise it the guide's way: real greetings, to real people, at the real start of the lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Greetings for the times of day
The guide's answer key matches greetings to the **division of the day**:

- **ganama** — the morning
- **guyyaa** — the midday, the day
- **galgala** — the evening

[Picture 9936 — greetings for the times of day]

One day, three greetings. The sun is your clock: low and rising — ganama; high — guyyaa; low and setting — galgala. The greeting you choose tells your listener you have noticed the hour you share.

## Sub lesson 3 — Greeting dialogues
Then the chapter turns the greeting into talk: **shaakala waliin dubbii** — dialogue practice. In pairs, learners greet, answer, and greet back — a small conversation with its turns taken properly: one speaks, one listens, one answers.

After the mouth knows them, the hand learns them: learners **read and write the greeting words**. (The writing of them is stocked in the Writing lesson First Written Pieces — here, keep the pair talk flowing.)

## Say it to your companion
Run the dialogue in pairs, three rounds: a morning meeting at the school gate, a midday meeting on the road, an evening meeting at the door of home. Swap roles each round. Your companion checks: did the greeting fit the hour? Were the turns taken — speak, listen, answer?

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the day's three divisions: ganama, guyyaa, galgala — with the part of the day each names.
2. Greet three real people today, each in their right division, in Afaan Oromoo.
3. At home, greet your family at galgala and teach them the word for the evening division.
4. Write the greeting words from the lesson's dialogue, from memory.
