# Lesson plan — Greetings

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** Greetings
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** greeting people in Afaan Oromoo — the teacher, each other — with the greeting that fits the part of the day: ganama (morning), guyyaa (midday/day), galgala (evening) — and holding a greeting dialogue in pairs.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the learner's own daily life — everyone already greets. It is the first Speaking lesson's ground.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — greetings are the door of the Speaking unit. The Letters and Reading lessons help with reading the greeting words once spoken.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Greet the teacher and greet each other, standing, in Afaan Oromoo.
2. Match the greeting to the day's division: ganama — morning; guyyaa — midday/day; galgala — evening.
3. Hold a pair dialogue (shaakala waliin dubbii): greet, answer, greet back.
4. Read and write the greeting words after speaking them.

## What you need
Nothing but people to greet. A clock or the sky tells you which part of the day it is.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses one greeting all day → point at the sun's place: low-rising, high, low-setting. The day has three divisions and the greeting names the one you are in.
- Answers a greeting with silence → a greeting is a turn-taking: greet, answer, greet back. Practise the three beats on fingers until the body knows them.
- Knows the words but whispers → greetings are gate-work: voice up, eyes on the person. Practise at the real door, not the desk.
