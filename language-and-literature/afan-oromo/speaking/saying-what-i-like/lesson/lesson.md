# Saying What I Like

## Do it first
Point at your nose. What is it *for*? You know the answer in your own language before you think: smelling. Your eyes? Seeing. Your ears? Hearing.

Afaan Oromoo says these in one clean shape — *Funyaan kootiin nan fuunfadha*: with my nose, I smell. This lesson gives you that shape for six body parts — and pairs it with the other great pair-talk of the Grade 4 book: saying what you like, and what you don't.

## Sub lesson 1 — What I like and don't like
The Grade 4 book's house chapter opens its speaking work with pair talk: **waan jaallatan / hin jaallanne** — what you like, and what you don't like.

In pairs, learners trade likes and dislikes — of foods, games, work, weather, school things. A like is only half the talk: the *don't like* is where opinions get interesting, and where a speaker learns to disagree kindly and say why.

Hold the shape: I like… / I don't like… — then a reason. Two likes and two dislikes each makes a real conversation, not a list.

## Sub lesson 2 — What my body parts do
Now the body answers its own questions. The guide's answer key gives six sentences — learn their one shape: **(part) kootiin nan (doing)** — with my (part), I (do):

- **Luka kootiin nan deema** — I walk with my legs
- **Funyaan kootiin nan fuunfadha** — I smell with my nose
- **Ija kootiin nan ilaala** — I see with my eyes
- **Gurra kootiin nan dhaggeeffadha** — I hear with my ears
- **Harka kootiin nan qaqqaba** — I touch/hold with my hands
- **Arraba kootiin nan dhandhama** — I taste with my tongue

*Kootiin* is "with my" — the word that straps the part to its work. Say each sentence touching the part it names; the body remembers what the mouth says.

## Say it to your companion
Interview each other. First the likes: two things you like, two you don't, with reasons — your companion asks one "why?" for each. Then the body round: your companion points at a part — eyes, ears, nose, tongue, hands, legs — and you answer with its full sentence: Ija kootiin nan ilaala. Swap after six pointings.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write three sentences about your likes and dislikes — one food, one game, one school thing — each with a reason.
2. Write the six body sentences from memory. Check them against Sub lesson 2.
3. Draw a simple figure of yourself and label the six parts with their sentences.
4. Ask someone at home what they like and don't like, in Afaan Oromoo, and write their answers.
