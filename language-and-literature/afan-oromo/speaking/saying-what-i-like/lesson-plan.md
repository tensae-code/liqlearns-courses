# Lesson plan — Saying What I Like

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** Saying What I Like
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** talking about what you like and don't like (waan jaallatan / hin jaallanne) in pairs — and saying what your body parts do: Luka kootiin nan deema — I walk with my legs.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word lessons' body-parts vocabulary — the learner can name the fifteen body parts. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the body-parts vocabulary in the Word unit (my-body-and-the-senses) — the sentences here put those names to work. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Talk in pairs about what you like and what you don't like.
2. Say the six body sentences: walking with legs, smelling with the nose, seeing with the eyes, hearing with the ears, touching with the hands, tasting with the tongue.
3. Answer "what do you do with this part?" for any of the parts named.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A partner for the pair talk. A flower or something with a smell helps the nose sentence.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner points at their own nose and tries to say what it is for, in Afaan Oromoo, before the sentence is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — pair talk about likes and dislikes as the Grade 4 guide sets it; then the guide's six sense-and-function sentences from its answer key (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9937] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of a child smelling a flower and listening to a bird, with the six body sentences as a border chart around the scene — legs, nose, eyes, ears, hands, tongue — the student points at the part in the picture, then finds and reads its sentence in the border.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 2: a voice asking "what do you do with this part?" part by part, with a pause for the answer — the student answers each question aloud in the pause with the part's full sentence.
3. **Say it to your companion** — interview each other — two likes, two dislikes, then the body questions: what do you do with your eyes? (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a child smelling a flower and listening to a bird as the sentences land, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write three like/dislike sentences about food, games and schoolwork, and the six body sentences from memory (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives a like with no reason → a list is not talk. Ask "why?" every time until reason-giving is the habit.
- Says the part but not the sentence → the shape is (part) kootiin nan (doing). Drill the frame with one part until it is automatic, then change the part, not the frame.
- Mixes ilaala (see) and dhaggeeffadha (hear) → eyes see, ears hear: point at the part first, then speak. The pointing is the check.
