# Lesson plan — Talking Together

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** Talking Together
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** holding real talk in Afaan Oromoo — a longer conversation that gives and receives information, a sequence told from pictures in before/after order (the coffee-making; a morning routine), talk about traditional games, and a debate held under debate rules.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Greetings and Introducing Yourself — the learner can already take turns in short dialogue. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Longer conversations** — extended dialogue (waliin dubbii dheeraa): holding a longer conversation, giving and receiving information (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 7).
2. **Telling a sequence from pictures** — picture-sequence speaking in before/after order (duraa duuba): the coffee-making sequence (adeemsa bunni itti danfu) and a child's morning hygiene sequence (Grade 3 guide, Ch. 8 answer keys; syllabus table).
3. **Playing and talking about traditional games** — traditional/cultural games (taphoota aadaa) played as a speaking activity (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 3 syllabus table).
4. **Debate** — a debate (falmii) held under debate rules on a chosen question (Grade 7 mother-tongue guide, Ch. 2).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Greetings — dialogue turns start there. Recommended, never forced.
- The coffee sequence here is Ethiopia's own way: the same ceremony returns as writing in Telling and Explaining in Writing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Hold a longer conversation (waliin dubbii dheeraa) — giving and receiving information across many turns.
2. Tell a sequence from pictures in before/after order (duraa duuba): the coffee-making, and a child's morning routine.
3. Talk about traditional games (taphoota aadaa) you play.
4. Take part in a debate (falmii) under debate rules.

## What you need
A partner or a class to talk with. Picture strips help the sequence work — the lesson's own picture, or drawings of your own.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells the teacher their whole morning, from waking to school, in order — and notices where the order breaks (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — longer conversations as the Grade 4 guide holds them; telling sequences from pictures, Grade 3's coffee and morning; traditional games talk; then the Grade 7 debate (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9953] in Sub lesson 2: coffee being roasted in a pan, then ground in a mortar, then poured from a jebena into a small cup — the ceremony in sequence — the student tells each step aloud as it happens on screen — first, then, after that, last — keeping voice and pictures together.
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9939] in Sub lesson 2: a picture strip of the coffee-making steps in order, and beside it a child's morning routine strip: waking, toilet, washing hands, breakfast, uniform, school — the student covers the strips and tells both sequences from memory, uncovering only to check.

3. **Say it to your companion** — run a long conversation in pairs, then narrate each other's morning routine back in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — coffee roasted, ground and poured in sequence as the telling runs, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, ordering, short-answer, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — tell the coffee-making sequence, or your own morning, aloud at home — in order, beginning to end (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Conversation dies after three turns → the turn did not build on the last information. Practise the echo: begin each turn by picking up one fact from your partner's turn.
- Sequence jumps around → stand at the pictures and point-step: finger on the step you are telling, never ahead, never back. Order words are nails: first, then, after that, last.
- Debate becomes shouting → turns by rule or it is not falmii. The rule that saves it: answer their point before yours.
