# Buying and Selling

## Do it first
Mime it: you are at a market stall. You point at the bananas, hold up fingers, hand over money, take the fruit, smile, leave. Everyone understood you — and you said nothing.

Now do it in words. Buying and selling — **Bittaafi Gurgurtaa** — is the Grade 4 guide's market chapter, and its talk is some of the most useful Afaan Oromoo a speaker owns. This lesson builds it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What buying and selling are
The chapter opens with the plain question: what *are* buying and selling?

Buying is taking goods and giving their worth; selling is giving goods and taking their worth. Two sides of one act, and the market is where they meet. The trader of the Sentence lessons — *daldalaa*, many *daldaltoota* — stands on the selling side; every learner has stood on the buying side since childhood.

Talk it through before you act it: who gives, who takes, what passes each way. When both sides are clear in words, the stall conversation has something to stand on.

## Sub lesson 2 — Talking at the market — daily activities
Around the market work, the chapter talks about **daily activities at home and at school** — the day's rounds that buying and selling serve: what is cooked, what is carried, what is needed, what was bought yesterday and what must be bought today.

This is the stall conversation's real body: not prices alone, but the life the goods go home to. Practise it in pairs — buyer and seller — with real goods in front of you where you can.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sequencing pictures after a heard story
The chapter's third strand joins listening to ordering: learners **hear a story**, then **sequence its pictures in time order** — first, then, after that, last — and retell the order aloud.

The ear holds the story; the hands lay it out; the mouth proves it. It is the Talking Together sequence work turned toward a heard text: if the pictures land in the story's order, the listening landed too.

## Say it to your companion
Run the stall in pairs twice: once as buyer, once as seller, with different goods each time — bananas, then grain. Your companion listens for the whole act: greeting, asking about the goods, agreeing, paying, thanking. Then tell each other one daily activity the purchase serves at home.

## Do It Yourself
1. Tell the story of one real purchase — yours or your family's — in Afaan Oromoo: the goods, the seller, the agreeing, the carrying home.
2. Name five things your house buys every week, and who sells them.
3. Draw a four-picture story of a market trip, shuffle the pictures, and sequence them again while retelling aloud.
4. Talk about tomorrow: what must be bought, and for what daily activity?
