# Lesson plan — Buying and Selling

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** Buying and Selling
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** talking the market in Afaan Oromoo: what buying and selling are, the daily activities of home and school talked about around them, and pictures sequenced in time order after a heard story.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Talking Together — the learner already holds a market-stall conversation. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Talking Together — the longer conversation at a market stall is practised there. Recommended, never forced.
- The Sentence lesson One and Many carries the trade-word plurals (daldalaa → daldaltoota) that market talk uses.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what buying and selling are, in Afaan Oromoo talk.
2. Talk about daily activities at home and at school in market conversation.
3. Sequence pictures in time order after hearing a story, and retell the order aloud.

## What you need
A partner for the stall talk. Real or play market things help: fruit, grain, small goods.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner acts a market purchase in mime, then finds the words the mime was missing (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what buying and selling are, as the Grade 4 guide's market chapter opens; daily activities talked about; pictures sequenced in time order after a heard story (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9941] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of a market stall with bananas, grain and cups laid out, a seller behind the stall and a buyer before it, mid-conversation — the student plays both parts aloud against the picture — the seller's lines, then the buyer's — for the goods shown.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 3: a voice telling a short market story, in order, at listening pace — the student listens, then lays out the story's order in drawings or words and retells it aloud.
3. **Say it to your companion** — run a full stall conversation in pairs — buyer and seller — then swap roles and goods (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a market stall with a buyer and a seller talking, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, ordering, short-answer, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — tell someone at home about one real purchase today — what, where, from whom — in Afaan Oromoo (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mimes instead of talking → the mime was the warm-up. Freeze the hands: goods stay on the stall until the words have done the buying.
- Stall talk is prices only → bring the day in: what will the goods cook, who will eat, what happened at home that sent you to market. That is the chapter's own second strand.
- Picture order scrambles → retell the heard story aloud first, slowly; the hands cannot lay an order the mouth cannot say.
