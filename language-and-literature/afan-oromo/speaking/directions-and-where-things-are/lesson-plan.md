# Lesson plan — Directions and Where Things Are

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Speaking · **Lesson:** Directions and Where Things Are
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Speaking (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** using direction words in speech: saying where your house is relative to the school, and where the shop (suuqii) is from home — placing the places of your life against each other, out loud.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Describing Pictures and Places — the learner already places things against each other in a description. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Describing Pictures and Places — placing language starts there. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say where your house is, relative to the school.
2. Say where the shop (suuqii) is, from home.
3. Give a route in words that a listener could walk.

## What you need
Your own real ground — home, school, shop. Paper for drawing the map in Hands-on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells a new visitor how to get from school to their house, in their own language, and notices the turns and landmarks the telling needs (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — direction words used in speech as the Grade 4 guide's directions chapter works them: your house against the school, the shop from home, then the whole route (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9940] in Sub lesson 3: a picture map of a school, a house and a shop joined by roads, with direction arrows and one big tree as landmark — the student traces the route school → house → shop with a finger while giving the directions aloud.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 1: a voice giving directions from a school to a house, slowly, leg by leg — the student listens and draws the route on paper from the words alone, then compares with the lesson's map.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give each other directions between real places and check them by walking the route in your heads together (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a child walking a route on a map as the directions are given, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — give the way from home to school aloud to someone at home, and let them repeat it back (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Places a thing "there" with pointing only → pointing is not speaking. The listener's eyes may be closed; the words must carry the whole way: direction, distance feel, landmark.
- Gives absolute places with no anchor → every direction starts from what the listener knows. Ask: from where? Then tell again from that anchor.
- Route legs out of order → walk it in your head as you tell it, one leg at a time; the telling should arrive when the walking would.
