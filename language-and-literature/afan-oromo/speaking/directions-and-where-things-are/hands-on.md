# Hands-on — Directions and Where Things Are

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The chalk map
Draw a big map on the ground or a board: school, a house, a shop, roads and one landmark. A learner stands at the school, gives directions aloud, and a second learner walks the map with a finger — or with feet, if the map is chalked on the ground — following only the words.

## Blind guide
In pairs: one closes their eyes; the other guides them three or four steps across a safe space with direction words only — no touching, no pointing. Swap. Direction words stop being vocabulary and become trust.

## Re-anchor round
Call a place — the shop! — and the class must place it twice: once from school, once from home. Quick rounds, changing the called place and the anchor each time.
