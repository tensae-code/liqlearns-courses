# Directions and Where Things Are

## Do it first
A visitor stands at your school gate and asks: "Where is your house?" Pointing helps — but pointing is not speaking. To answer in words you need direction words, and a way of placing one place against another: my house against the school, the shop against my home.

The Grade 4 guide gives a whole chapter to exactly this — **Kallattiiwwan**, Directions. This lesson is that chapter's speaking heart.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where my house is
The guide's speech work begins with the two anchors of a child's life: **home and school**. Saying where your house is *relative to the school* — this side or that side, near or far, along which road, past which landmark.

Relative is the key word. No place is anywhere by itself in speech; it is always somewhere *from* somewhere. The school is the shared anchor — everyone in the conversation knows it — and your house is placed against it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where the shop is
Then the anchor moves: now **home** is the known place, and the **suuqii** — the shop — is placed from it.

Same skill, new anchor. A good direction-teller can re-anchor at will: from school, my house; from home, the shop; from the shop, the market. Each telling starts from what the listener already knows and walks outward from there.

## Sub lesson 3 — A route in words
Put the two tellings together and you have a **route**: school → home → shop, each leg placed against the last.

Walk it in words slowly: where you start, which way you turn, what you pass, where you arrive. A route in words is a description that moves — the Describing lesson's placing, set walking. If your listener could follow it with their eyes closed, the directions are complete.

## Say it to your companion
Trade routes. One of you gives directions from school to your house; the other repeats the route back, leg by leg, and names anything unclear. Then the shop leg, from home. Finish with the whole route in one telling, both of you walking it in your heads together and checking the turns against the real ground you both know.

## Do It Yourself
1. Say where your house is relative to your school, aloud, three times — smoother each time.
2. Say where the shop is from home.
3. Give the whole route — school, home, shop — to someone at home and let them repeat it back to you.
4. Draw the map of the three places from your own telling.
