# Figures of Speech — short answer

Write a short answer for each. The number shows which sub lesson each question belongs to.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What is the difference between *iddeessa* and *akkasaa*?
**Answer: iddeessa (metaphor) says the thing IS the other — "the Oromo are bees"; akkasaa (simile) says it is LIKE the other, and shows it with words like akka, amma, fakkaata.**
*Why it matters: telling the two apart is the first skill of the whole toolbox.*

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Name the two directions of *arbeessa* and what each does.
**Answer: arbeessuu stretches a thing upward — bigger than it is; kineessuu squeezes it downward — smaller than it is.**
*Why it matters: exaggeration has a direction, and the direction is the meaning.*

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Sort these two: (a) "The snake crossed the poem, and everyone felt the warning of cunning." (b) "O moon, watch over us tonight."
**Answer: (a) fakkoomii — the snake is the book's symbol of cunning; (b) hinjirree dubbisuu — speaking to the absent moon as if it stood near.**
*Why it matters: this sorting is the guide's own Exercise 8 work.*

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Take a song or saying you know and find one figure of speech working inside it. Name the figure and your evidence.
**Answer: the learner's own finding, with the tell-tale word or shape named (an akka for a simile, a thing named as another for a metaphor, and so on).**
*Why it matters: the figures are only learned when they can be caught in the wild.*
