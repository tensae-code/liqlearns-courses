# Figures of Speech — the video script

**Format note (standing rule):** video is produced from these scripts only — never generated. This script is written to be filmed or read aloud. Follow the lesson plan's order; the learner does each thing first, then hears the explanation.

**Narrator (or teacher on camera):**

**Opening.** [Picture 12514 on screen.] "A boy standing beside a lion. A hyena, speaking. A great tree. Three panels — three tricks of talk. Poets call them *malleen dubbii* — figures of speech. Today we learn the whole toolbox, one tool at a time, the way your book teaches them."

**Sub lesson 1 — Comparing.** "First, the comparisons. *Akkasaa* — the simile — says a thing is LIKE another: it carries its tell-tale words — *akka*, *amma*, *fakkaata* — like, as, it seems. Brave *akka* a lion: brave like a lion. But *iddeessa* — the metaphor — goes one step bolder: it says the thing IS the other. Your book's example: *Oromoon kaannisa* — the Oromo are a swarm of bees: one body, many wings, working as one. No 'like'. It simply names them bees."

**Sub lesson 2 — Giving life, stretching size.** "*Nameessa* — personification: a thing or an animal stands up and acts with a person's intentions — the hyena speaks, the river decides. And *arbeessa* — exaggeration: stretching a thing past its true size to make the feeling land. Stretch UP — *arbeessuu* — he carried a hundred sacks! Or squeeze DOWN — *kineessuu* — the great man made small as an ant."

**Sub lesson 3 — Opposite, standing-for, calling out.** "*Habalaka* — irony: saying the opposite of the meaning, so the hearer feels the truth bite. *Eemitii*, also called *fuggisoo* — antithesis: opposites set face to face in one saying. *Fakkoomii* — the symbol: one thing standing for a greater thing — the **odaa** tree for Oromo identity, the **dove** for peace, the **snake** for cunning, the **scales** for justice, **red** for struggle. And *hinjirree dubbisuu* — apostrophe: speaking straight to what is absent, as if it stood before you — 'O rain, why do you delay?' Last, the *dubbiilee qoolaa* — the witty sayings, the play of a quick tongue."

**Sub lesson 4 — Name that figure.** "Now catch them. I say a saying; you name its figure. That is your book's own exercise — and when you can sort them all, the toolbox is yours."

**Close.** "Every walaloo you will ever hear is built from these. Now you can watch the builder's hands."
