# Figures of Speech — hands-on

Served by this build: the lesson's picture strip (12514) — the boy beside the lion (simile), the speaking hyena (personification), the odaa tree (symbol) — opens the lesson and anchors the sorting game. Do them in the lesson's order.

- **Like or is? (sub lesson 1).** The teacher fires comparisons; learners hold up LIKE (akkasaa) or IS (iddeessa) cards. Any saying with akka/amma/fakkaata in it is a LIKE; a saying that simply names the thing as the other is an IS.
- **Stretch it (sub lesson 2).** A plain sentence — "the man is strong" — is stretched up (arbeessuu) and squeezed down (kineessuu) by the class in turns, then given life (nameessa): what would the strong man's river do, if the river were a person?
- **Symbol walk (sub lesson 3).** The guide's symbol stock is walked as a memory chain: odaa — identity; dove — peace; snake — cunning; scales — justice; red — struggle. Each learner adds a gesture for one symbol and the chain is re-run from the top.
- **The sorting summit (sub lesson 4).** Sayings are read aloud one by one (the lesson's six, then sayings the learners bring from home); the class sorts each by its figure and must give the reason — the tell-tale word or shape — before the next is read.
