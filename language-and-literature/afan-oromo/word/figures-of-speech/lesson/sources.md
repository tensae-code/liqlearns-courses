# Figures of Speech — sources

**Course:** Afan Oromo — as named in README.md, Unit 3 (Word).

This course is built ONLY from the Afan Oromo teacher guides in hand — no pupil book was in hand for this course. Everything in this lesson traces to the book below: what the guide itself teaches is stocked; nothing is invented, and no other book or outside material is used. Placements were written during the 2026-11-12 Ethiopia Wave 2 read (the Grade 7 file completed by re-read the same day), never reconstructed afterwards.

**Book 1:** *Barnoota Afaan Oromoo, Qajeelcha Barsiisaa Kutaa 7ffaa* (teacher guide — evidence; Afaan Oromoo as the learners' mother tongue) — Ethiopia, Addis Ababa (Finfinnee) City Administration Education Bureau, 2014 E.C. / 2022, printed pp. 1–170. Page rule: printed page = PDF page − 3. Read in full, page by page, in the 2026-11-12 Ethiopia Wave 2 read. Placements at liqlearns-courses-work/ethiopia-2026-11-11/afan-oromo/g07-teacher-placements.md.

**How the lesson maps to the book.** The Grade 7 guide teaches the figures of speech (*malleen dubbii*) one by one across its oral-poetry chapter — the same chapter whose poem-writing exercise (Exercise 7) asks learners to use them — and sorts worked examples among them in Exercise 8. This lesson carries the figures whole, in the guide's own order of teaching, with the guide's own named examples (the bee-swarm metaphor; the symbol stock):

| Lesson part | Source |
|---|---|
| Sub lesson 1 — Comparing | Printed pp. 139–145 (the figures taught one by one) — *iddeessa* (metaphor), with the guide's example "Oromoon kaannisa"; *akkasaa* (simile), with its marker words (akka, amma, fakkaata, caala, gaha) |
| Sub lesson 2 — Giving life, and stretching size | Printed pp. 139–145 — *nameessa* (personification); *arbeessa* (exaggeration) in its two directions, *arbeessuu* upward and *kineessuu* downward |
| Sub lesson 3 — Opposite, standing-for, and calling out | Printed pp. 139–145 — *habalaka* (irony); *eemitii/fuggisoo* (antithesis); *fakkoomii* (symbol), with the guide's symbol stock (odaa tree = Oromo identity, dove = peace, snake = cunning, scales = justice, red = struggle); *hinjirree dubbisuu* (apostrophe); *dubbiilee qoolaa* (witty sayings) |
| Sub lesson 4 — Name that figure | Printed pp. 135–136 — Exercise 8's sorting task: worked examples sorted among the figures (its named kinds: habal aka, arbeessuu, akkasaa, nameessa, bak-buusa-iddeessa); the six sayings sorted in this sub lesson are built on the figures' own definitions and the guide's named examples, in the exercise's form |
| Picture 12514 | Drawn for LiqLearns for this lesson — the figures in a picture strip: a boy beside a lion (simile), a speaking hyena (personification), an odaa tree (symbol) |
