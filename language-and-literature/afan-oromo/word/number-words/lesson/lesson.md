# Number Words

## Do it first

Count on your fingers in Afaan Oromoo: **tokko, lama, sadii, afur, shan** — one to five. Now: what is thirteen? What is seventy-seven? What is one hundred? This lesson climbs the whole stair, decade by decade, until every number to **dhibba** — 100 — has its word.

## Sub lesson 1 — Numbers 1–20

Numbers begin as words to be read, matched and written — figure to word, word to figure:

**tokko** 1 · **lama** 2 · **sadii** 3 · **afur** 4 · **shan** 5 · **jaha** 6 · **torba** 7 · **saddeet** 8 · **sagal** 9

Then the teens climb by **kudha** (ten) plus the unit:

**kudhan** 10 · **kudha tokko** 11 · **kudha lama** 12 · **kudha sadii** 13 · **kudha afur** 14 · **kudha shan** 15 · **kudha jaha** 16 · **kudha torba** 17 · **kudha saddeet** 18 · **kudha sagal** 19 · **digdama** 20

The top step is a word of its own: **dhibba** — one hundred. The ladder is complete: units, kudha-teens, and decade stairs from digdama to dhibba. In the same chapter the book pairs the numbers with plurals of trade words (daldalaa → daldaltoota, a trader → traders) and pronoun sentences — the numbers stand beside the grammar of the *Sentence* lessons, where those parts are taught in full.

## Sub lesson 10 — The grammar companions of the number road

The number road never travels alone — each decade chapter carries a grammar companion. Meet them here briefly; the *Sentence* lessons teach each in full.

**Plurals** — one becomes many: **nama → namoota** (person → people), **kitaaba → kitaabota** (book → books), **hoolaa → hoolota**, **mana → manneen** (house → houses), **barataa → barattoota** (student → students), **gamoo → gamoowwan**, **konkolaataa → konkolaattota** (car → cars).

**The verb changes with the person** — one verb, five runners: **isheen dhufte** (she came), **ani dhufe** (I came), **nuti dhufne** (we came), **ati dhufte** (you came), **isaan dhufan** (they came).

**And back again** — plural to singular: **firoota → fira**, **manneen → mana**, **haadholii → haadha**, **barattoota → barataa**, **barsiistota → barsiisaa**.

Numbers count the world; these companions name it one-and-many and set its verbs running — that is why the book walks them together.

## Say it to your companion

Count together from tokko to digdama, one number each in turn — miss one and start the decade again. Then quiz: one calls a figure ('forty-six!'), the other answers with its word ('afurtamii jaha!'). Ten each, across all the decades.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the numbers 1–20 in words in two columns: figures left, words right.
2. Pick the decade of your own age and the decade of a parent's age; write both decades out in words.
3. Write the round tens in words: digdama, soddoma, afurtama, shantama, jahatama, torbaatama, saddeettama, sagaltama, dhibba.
