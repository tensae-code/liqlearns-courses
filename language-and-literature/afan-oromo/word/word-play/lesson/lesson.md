# Word Play

## Do it first

Three quick games. One: the opposite of **dheeraa** (tall)? Two: a word that means nearly the same as another word is called its…? Three: can you read **mammaaksa** whole, without splitting it? This lesson is word play — the serious kind, where every game makes words yours.

## Sub lesson 1 — Opposites

Some words stand back to back. **Opposites** — *hiika faallaa*, meanings facing each other:

**furdaa ↔ qallaa** (fat ↔ thin) · **dheeraa ↔ gabaabaa** (long/tall ↔ short) · **adii ↔ gurraacha** (white ↔ black)

[Picture 9883 — three opposite pairs]

The work reading of Grade 4 plays the same game inside a text: its **synonyms** (*hiika walfakkii* — words of like meaning) and its **antonyms** are matched from the reading itself. A word is known twice when you know its opposite: say furdaa, and qallaa stands up across from it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Idioms and synonyms

The mother-tongue book plays deeper games. **Synonyms** — *jechoota hiika walfakkii*, words of like meaning — are matched in pairs: two different words walking to the same meaning.

[Picture 9884 — two word cards of like meaning joining]

And **idioms** — *ibsoota jechamoota* — are read **in use**: phrases whose meaning is bigger than their words, learned where they live, inside real reading, never as bare lists. Word play at this level is reading work: you meet the idiom in its sentence, and the sentence teaches it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Meanings in context

A word's meaning is settled by the company it keeps — its **context**. The Grade 7 book works meanings from its reading in sets like these:

**nyaachuu / qopheessuu** (eating / preparing) · **uffachuu / miiccuu / bitachuu** (wearing / washing / buying) · **dhuguu / dhaabuu** (drinking / planting, standing) · **dubbisuu / barreessuu** (reading / writing)

The method: meet the word inside the reading, hold the sentence around it, and let the sentence say what the word is doing there. Never tear a word out of its sentence to guess it — the context is the dictionary you always carry.

## Sub lesson 4 — Building whole words

Pieces you already own — letters, syllables — build **whole words**, and the book's word-building answers stand as the proof set:

**bineensa** (an animal) · **qalama** (a pen, a pencil) · **mallattoo** (a sign, a symbol) · **mammaaksa** (a proverb) · **alaabaa** (a flag) — and **gaara** (a mountain), **naacha** (a crocodile), **raacha** (a frog), **saree** (a dog).

[Picture 9884 — two word cards of like meaning joining]

Read them whole, then split one slowly — **bi-neen-sa, ma-lla-ttoo** — and feel the parts you built in the Reading lessons carrying the whole word. That is word-building finished: the pieces disappear into the word.

## Sub lesson 5 — The school word-web and everyday actions

Words gather in **webs**. The school's web hangs on one centre word — **meeshaalee barnootaa**, learning materials:

**qubeessaa** (a pencil) · **dabtara** (an exercise book) · **maarkarii** (a marker) · **haqxuu** (an eraser) · **gabatee gurraacha** (the blackboard — the black board) · **kobbee** · **sarartuu** (a ruler)

[Picture 9886 — the school materials word-web]

And the day has its **action words**, learned from pictures — the hygiene set: **qaama dhiqachuu** (washing the body), **qeensa qorachuu** (cutting nails), **rifeensa murachuu** (cutting hair), **kophee miiccuu** (washing shoes), **kubbaa taphachuu** (playing ball), **huccuu miccachuu** (washing clothes).

[Picture 9887 — everyday actions and hygiene]

A web for things, a set for actions — carry both, and the Word title is complete.

## Sub lesson 6 — Vocabulary in use

The Grade 7 book's Chapter 9 ends by putting its whole word-hoard to **work** — its answer keys are a lesson in themselves, in three kinds of work. [Picture 12502 — "Bishaan ol hinyaa'u…" and "…jette harreen": two halves of a saying joining into one.]

**Finish the idiom.** Saying-phrases wait for their missing ends; the book's key completes them: **…hinfuudhani**; **ilma afaan hinurgaahu**; **halkan beekkachiise**; **kan barana jedhe hinoolu**; **ollaan birmata**; and the donkey's famous word — **"bishaan ol hinyaa'u," jette harreen** — *"water doesn't flow upward," said the donkey.*

**Verbs in their present dress.** The chapter's verbs, in the present-tense forms the key uses: **nicaraana, ni aada, niyuusa, niharqa, nibukumsa, nikorrisa, niqasaati / kofalti, niwacci, ni iyya, nikaakkisti** — hear how each one carries the *ni-* of the present.

**Synonym sets, sorted.** The chapter's same-meaning words: **ciibsata; gaana; goromtii; maseena / dhabduu; daa'ima; qubeellaa; Amartii; lootii** — each standing beside the word it matches in the chapter's talk.

**Meanings from the sentence — hiika galumsaa.** And the chapter review's last skill: a word's meaning is settled by the sentence it stands in. The review sets sixteen sentences asking exactly that — what does this word mean HERE? The context decides, exactly as it did in sub lesson 3.

### Now you try

Complete it, and say who "said" it: "Bishaan ol hinyaa'u…" *(Answer: "…jette harreen" — the donkey said it.)*

## Sub lesson 7 — More words of the same meaning

Chapter 10 — the oral-poetry chapter — keeps a synonym hoard of its own (its Exercise 8). Learn the set: **weedduu** (the song of longing itself); **sakaaluu = takaaluu** — one word in two dresses, the pair this sub lesson's picture is built on [Picture 12503 — sakaaluu = takaaluu, two word cards of the same meaning]; **gorora; gosa; danqaraa; gatii; kaarruu; gufuun; magaala; maraachaa**.

You will meet *weedduu* again as a kind of afwalaloo in the listening lessons. And the same exercise asks you to sort sayings by their **figures of speech** — that sorting lives whole in the Figures of Speech lesson (sub lesson 4), where the toolbox is taught one figure at a time.

### Now you try

Which two words in the set are one word in two dresses? Then use **gatii** in a sentence of your own. *(Answer: sakaaluu = takaaluu.)*

## Say it to your companion

Opposites duel: one fires a word, the other must answer its opposite within three heartbeats — furdaa! qallaa! Then the web game: one names the centre (meeshaalee barnootaa), the other must hang three materials on it. Last, mime a hygiene action; its Afaan Oromoo name is the prize.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three opposite pairs, both words each.
2. Write the school web: centre word, then its seven materials around it.
3. Write three hygiene actions and do one of them today — qaama dhiqachuu counts.
