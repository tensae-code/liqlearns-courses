# Colours

## Do it first

Look around you right now. Find something red, something white, something green. For each, you are about to own the Afaan Oromoo word. And soon you will say them joined: not just 'yellow', but **daraaraa keelloo** — a yellow flower.

## Sub lesson 1 — The six colours

Six colours open the colour family:

**diimaa** — red · **gurraacha** — black · **magariisa** — green · **cuquliisa** — blue · **keelloo** — yellow · **adii** — white

[Picture 9867 — the six colour cards]

[GIF 9905 — a white shape filling with each colour as it is named]

The book learns them with a **song** — the colours song, *faaruu halluuwwanii*. Sing the six words in their order until the order sings itself: diimaa, gurraacha, magariisa, cuquliisa, keelloo, adii. A colour word is known when your eye sees the colour and your mouth has the word before you think.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colour and thing together

Colours earn their keep standing beside things. The book builds **colour phrases** — *gaalee*:

- **daraaraa keelloo** — a yellow flower
- **sangaa gurraacha** — a black ox
- **xaafii adii** — white teff
- **samii cuquliisa** — a blue sky
- **konkolaataa diimaa** — a red car

[Picture 9885 — colour and thing phrases]

Notice the order: the **thing first, the colour after** — daraaraa (flower), then keelloo (yellow). Build phrases the same way with things around you: the colour word rides behind its thing, always.

## Say it to your companion

One calls a colour word; the other must touch something of that colour in the room and answer with its phrase — thing plus colour. Six colours, six phrases, then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six colour words in a row and read them until the order is easy.
2. Write the five book phrases: daraaraa keelloo, sangaa gurraacha, xaafii adii, samii cuquliisa, konkolaataa diimaa.
3. Find three things at home and write each as a phrase: the thing's word, then its colour word.
