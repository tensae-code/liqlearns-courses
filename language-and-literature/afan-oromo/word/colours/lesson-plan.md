# Lesson plan — Colours

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Colours
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the six colour words — diimaa, gurraacha, magariisa, cuquliisa, keelloo, adii — known by sight, sung, and joined to things: daraaraa keelloo, samii cuquliisa.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Reading lessons; colours are the first describing words, learned with a song as the book learns them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The six colours** — colours identified, read and written, with the colours song (faaruu halluuwwanii): diimaa, gurraacha, magariisa, cuquliisa, keelloo, adii (G3 Ch. 10).
2. **Colour and thing together** — colour phrases built (gaalee): daraaraa keelloo, sangaa gurraacha, xaafii adii, samii cuquliisa, konkolaataa diimaa (G4 Ch. 5).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Words by Their Sounds. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six colours: diimaa, gurraacha, magariisa, cuquliisa, keelloo, adii.
2. Sing the colour row in order, the colours song's own way.
3. Build colour phrases — thing first, colour after: daraaraa keelloo, samii cuquliisa, konkolaataa diimaa.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Real coloured things from the room.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner finds a red, a white and a green thing in the room before learning their words.
2. **The explanation** — the six colours named and sung; colours joined to things in phrases, thing first and colour after (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9867]: a picture of six colour cards, each card one colour with a thing of that colour on it: a red car-shape, a black ox-shape, green leaves, a blue sky strip, a yellow flower, a white sheet — the student points to each card, says its colour word, then finds the same colour in the room.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9905]: a picture of a plain white flower outline that fills with yellow, then a white sky strip that fills with blue, then a white car that fills with red, each as its colour word appears — the student says each colour word as its fill spreads.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9885]: a picture of a strip of three phrase pictures: a yellow flower, a black ox and a red car, each labelled with its phrase — the student reads each phrase aloud — thing first, colour after.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice singing the six colour words in order, the colours song's own list — the student sings along, then sings the row alone.
3. **Say it to your companion** — one names a colour; the other finds that colour in the room and names the thing with its phrase.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, true or false, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — name the six colours, write the five phrases, and build two phrases from things at home.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
