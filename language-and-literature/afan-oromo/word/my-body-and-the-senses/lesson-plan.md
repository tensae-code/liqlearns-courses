# Lesson plan — My Body and the Senses

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** My Body and the Senses
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the body's words — the fifteen parts from the book's numbered picture, and the five senses with their organs.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word lessons before it; the learner's own body is the picture this lesson is read on.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Parts of the body — the fifteen** — fifteen body parts named from a numbered picture (G4 Ch. 6): mataa, gurra, ija, funyaan, afaan, morma, laphee, harma, irree, garaa, quba harkaa, sarbaa, jilba, koronyoo, quba miillaa.
2. **Body parts and the senses** — body parts with the five senses from pictures (G3 Ch. 6 answer keys): gurra, ija, arraba, funyaan, harka.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Family and People — the body is named among its people. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the fifteen body parts from the book's picture, on your own body.
2. Name the five sense organs: gurra, ija, arraba, funyaan, harka.
3. Match each sense — hearing, seeing, tasting, smelling, touching — to its organ.

## What you need
Only your own body. Paper and pencil for the writing work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner touches ear, eye, nose, tongue and hand and hears the word arraba.
2. **The explanation** — the fifteen parts named from the numbered picture and touched on the learner's own body; the five sense organs with their senses (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9881]: a picture of a standing child drawn full-body, fifteen numbered dots on the parts — head, ear, eye, nose, mouth, neck, chest, breast, arm, belly, finger, thigh, knee, ankle, toe — each number tied to its word — the student touches each part on their own body as its word is read, in the picture's order.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9882]: a picture of five small pictures in a row: an ear with sound waves, an eye looking at a flower, a tongue, a nose over a flower, a hand touching cloth — the student names each organ and its sense — hearing, seeing, tasting, smelling, touching.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the fifteen parts slowly, top to toe — the student touches each part a breath after the voice, then leads the voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — one touches a part; the other names it. Then the senses: one names a sense, the other its organ and its sentence.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — name the fifteen on your own body, write the five sense organs, and match each sense to its organ.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
