# Lesson plan — Food and Plants

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Food and Plants
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the food words and the plant words — the seven foods matched picture to word, the further food set, and the vegetables of the garden.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Reading lessons; food words were first read in sentences, now they are owned as a family.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Foods** — food types named from pictures and matched picture↔word (G3 Ch. 9): keekii, buddeena, marqa, daabboo, caccabsaa, foon, akaayii; and the further set paastaa, damma, piizaa, ruuzii.
2. **Vegetables and plants** — vegetable words heard, read and used in own sentences (G4 Ch. 3): salaaxaa, kaarota, timaatima, raafuu, dinnicha, raafuu maraa, qullubbii diimaa.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Colours — its phrase rule (thing first, colour after) returns in qullubbii diimaa. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the seven foods: keekii, buddeena, marqa, daabboo, caccabsaa, foon, akaayii.
2. Name the further foods: paastaa, damma, piizaa, ruuzii.
3. Name the vegetables: salaaxaa, kaarota, timaatima, raafuu, dinnicha, raafuu maraa, qullubbii diimaa — and use them in sentences.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A real meal or market stall teaches best.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names three foods from their last meal, ready for their Afaan Oromoo words.
2. **The explanation** — the seven pictured foods matched to their words with the further set; the vegetables named and used in own sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9877]: a picture of seven foods drawn on a table and a mesob: a cake, injera rolled, a porridge bowl, a loaf, roasted barley in a small heap, meat pieces, roasted grain — the student points to each food and says its word before reading the labels.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9878]: a picture of a garden row of vegetables: lettuce leaves, carrots with tops, tomatoes on a vine, a cabbage, potatoes, a red onion — the student names each vegetable down the row, then picks the two most eaten at home.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the seven food words, pausing after each — the student says each word in the pause, picturing the food.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name foods to each other from memory; build sentences with the vegetables for each other.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write the seven foods, the four further words, and the vegetable set.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
