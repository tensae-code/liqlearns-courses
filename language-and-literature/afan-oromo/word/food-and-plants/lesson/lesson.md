# Food and Plants

## Do it first

Look at your last meal in your mind. Injera — **buddeena**. Meat — **foon**. Honey — **damma**. This lesson names the table and the garden: foods first, then the vegetables they grow from.

## Sub lesson 1 — Foods

Food is named from pictures and matched, picture to word:

**keekii** (cake) · **buddeena** (injera, the flatbread) · **marqa** (porridge) · **daabboo** (bread) · **caccabsaa** (a roasted barley snack) · **foon** (meat) · **akaayii** (roasted grain)

[Picture 9877 — the seven foods on a table]

And the further food words the book reads and builds: **paastaa** (pasta), **damma** (honey), **piizaa** (pizza), **ruuzii** (rice). Eleven food words: say them at your own table and they will never leave you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vegetables and plants

The garden gives its own set — vegetables heard, read, and then **used in sentences of your own**:

**salaaxaa** (lettuce) · **kaarota** (carrot) · **timaatima** (tomato) · **raafuu** (cabbage) · **dinnicha** (potato) · **raafuu maraa** · **qullubbii diimaa** (red onion)

[Picture 9878 — the vegetables in a garden row]

Notice **qullubbii diimaa** — the colour word rides behind its thing, exactly as the Colours lesson built phrases: onion, then red. A vegetable is learned whole when you can name it in the garden, at the market, and in a sentence at the table.

## Say it to your companion

Play the table game: one names a food in English, the other answers in Afaan Oromoo — then a sentence must follow using it ('Damma…' — honey is good for health, if you remember the reading sentence). Vegetables score double.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eleven food words in two groups: the seven pictured, the four further.
2. Write the seven vegetables and draw one of them.
3. Write two sentences of your own, each using one vegetable word.
