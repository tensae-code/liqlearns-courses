# Things in the House

## Do it first

Stand in the doorway of your home and look in. Everything you see has an Afaan Oromoo name in this lesson's family — and the house itself, whether it is mana citaa or mana qorqorroo, has its kind named too. Start naming.

## Sub lesson 1 — Things in the house

The house has its own word-family: **meeshaalee manaa** — the things of the house.

[Picture 9872 — a house interior with its things]

The book names the household things from pictures, and then does reading work with their words — splitting picture words into syllables: **u-lee** (a stick), **sa-ree**, **barattoota**, **a-laa-baa** (a flag), **xiy-yaa-ra** (an aeroplane) among the pictured words. Name the things in your own house the same way: point, name, and if the word is long, split it and let the syllables carry you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Things in the house and what they are for

The Grade 4 book goes one step further: household things — *meeshaalee mana keessaa* — are named **with their uses**, *tajaajila isaanii*: what each thing is *for*.

[Picture 9872 — a house interior with its things]

Learn a thing as a pair: the name, and the work it does. A thing's use is part of its meaning — when you can say what a household thing is for, you do not merely know its word, you know the thing. Walk your own house with this lesson: name each thing, then say its work.

## Sub lesson 3 — Kinds of houses

Houses themselves have names by their kind:

- **mana citaa** — a thatched hut
- **mana qorqorroo** — a corrugated-iron house
- **mana viillaa** — a villa
- **gamoo dheeraa** — a tall building
- **mana guddaa** — a big house
- **mana xiqqaa** — a small house
- **mana waliinii** — a shared, communal house

[Picture 9873 — the seven kinds of houses]

Read the list as a street: from the thatched hut to the tall building, every roof has its word. Which kinds stand on your own street? Name them as you pass them.

## Say it to your companion

Stand in one room together (or picture one). One names a thing — the other must answer with its use: what it is for. Five things each. Then the house game: one describes a house kind in English, the other names it in Afaan Oromoo.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five household things you can see now, each with its use in a few words.
2. Write the seven house kinds in the lesson's order.
3. Split these words into syllables in writing: ulee, alaabaa, xiyyaara.
