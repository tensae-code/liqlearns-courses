# Lesson plan — Things in the House

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Things in the House
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words for the house and its things — meeshaalee manaa — what household things are called, what they are for, and the kinds of houses people live in.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Reading lessons; the learner reads whole words and sentences, and now names the nearest world of all — home.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Things in the house** — household items named (meeshaalee manaa) and picture words split into syllables (G3 Ch. 8).
2. **Things in the house and what they are for** — household things named and their uses learned (meeshaalee mana keessaa fi tajaajila isaanii), G4 Ch. 11.
3. **Kinds of houses** — house types listed, read and written: mana citaa, mana qorqorroo, mana viillaa, gamoo dheeraa, mana guddaa, mana xiqqaa, mana waliinii (G3 Ch. 10).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Words by Their Sounds — the syllable-splitting of this lesson stands on it. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name household things (meeshaalee manaa) and split their words into syllables.
2. Pair a household thing with what it is for (tajaajila isaanii).
3. Name the seven kinds of houses, mana citaa to mana waliinii.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Your own house is the classroom.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names three things in the room and guesses the kind of their own house.
2. **The explanation** — household things named and split into syllables; things paired with their uses; the seven kinds of houses (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9872]: a picture of the inside of a house: a bed, a table, a jebena on a stove, a cup, a chair and a lamp, each drawn clear and separate — the student walks the picture with a finger, naming each thing and saying what it is for.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9873]: a picture of a strip of seven houses in a row: a thatched hut, a corrugated-iron house, a villa, a tall building, a big house, a small house and a shared house — the student reads along the strip and names the kind of house they live in.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the seven house kinds slowly — the student repeats each kind and points — in the picture or out of the window — to its nearest match.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name a household thing; your companion answers with what it is for. Then walk the house-kind strip together.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — name five things at home with their uses, and write the seven house kinds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
