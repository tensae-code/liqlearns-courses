# Days, Months and Seasons

## Do it first

Answer quickly: what day is it today? What month? What season? You know them in your first language — now own them in Afaan Oromoo: the day row, the month song, the four seasons, and the parts of the day itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Days of the week

The week has seven days, and the book reads them from a reading called *Guyyoota Torbanii* — the Days of the Week:

**Wiixata** (Monday) · **Kibxata** (Tuesday) · **Roobii** (Wednesday) · **Kamisa** (Thursday) · **Jimaata** (Friday) · **Sanbata** (Saturday) · **Dilbata** (Sunday)

[Picture 9868 — the weekly calendar]

[GIF 9906 — the days lighting up in order across a calendar week]

Say the row until it walks by itself: Wiixata, Kibxata, Roobii, Kamisa, Jimaata, Sanbata, Dilbata. Then find today in the row — the days before it are behind you, the days after it are coming.

## Sub lesson 2 — Months of the year — the song

The year has twelve months, and the book learns them by **singing** — the months song, *faaruu ji'oota waggaa*:

**Fulbaana · Onkoloolessa · Sadaasa · Muddee · Amajjii · Guraandhala · Bitootessa · Eebla · Caamsa · Waxabajjii · Adoolessa · Hagayya**

[Picture 9869 — the twelve-month year wheel]

Sing the row slowly first, pointing at each month on the wheel; the song carries the order so your memory does not have to. These are the months of the Ethiopian year — the year your school and your holidays live by.

## Sub lesson 3 — The seasons

The year also turns through four seasons — *waqtiilee*:

**Birraa · Bona · Arfaasaa · Ganna**

[Picture 9870 — the same farm in the four seasons]

Learn them as the year's four faces: the land dries, the rains come, the harvest stands, the cold or the green returns — each season changes what farmers do, what children wear, and what the sky promises. Say the four in order, then say which season you are standing in now.

## Sub lesson 4 — Seasons, months and weather together

The Grade 4 book sets the **months inside the seasons** — learn its table as it prints it:

- **Birraa:** Fulbaana, Muddee, Bitootessa, Waxabajjii
- **Bona:** Onkoloolessa, Amajjii, Eebla, Adoolessa
- **Arfaasaa:** Sadaasa, Guraandhala, Caamsa, Hagayya
- And **Ganna** stands in the season row with them.

Around this table the book talks about **weather** itself — what weather is, and how the day's sky is named: cold (*qorraa*), hot (*ho'aa*), cloudy (*duumessa*). Put the three word-families together — day, month, season, weather — and you can place any day of your life in words.

## Sub lesson 5 — Times of the day

The day itself has names for its parts. You already met three with the greetings: **ganama** (morning), **guyyaa** (midday, the day), **galgala** (evening) — and the night is **halkan**.

[Picture 9871 — the day in four pictures: morning, midday, evening, night]

The book matches the **hours to their work**: lessons run through the school hours, and the play period (9:00–12:00 in the book's clock) is for playing; the evening is for home and study, the night — halkan — for sleeping. Name the part of the day whenever you say what you are doing, and your sentences carry their time with them.

## Say it to your companion

Run the rows against each other: one sings the months, the other must stop them at any month and name the next. Then the day-parts game — one says an activity ('sleeping!'), the other answers its time ('halkan!').

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven days in order and circle today.
2. Write the twelve months in the song's order, around a circle like the wheel.
3. Write the four seasons and, under each, one thing that happens in it where you live.
