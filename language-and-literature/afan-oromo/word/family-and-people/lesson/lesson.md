# Family and People

## Do it first

Point around your own family in words you may not yet own: father, mother, brother, sister, grandmother, grandfather. And two harder questions: what is the word for a person who helps others — and for one who lies? This lesson hands you the people words, all three kinds.

## Sub lesson 1 — Family members

The family — **maatii** — in its own words:

**abbaa** (father) · **haadha** (mother) · **obboleessa** (brother) · **obboleettii** (sister) · **adaadaa** (aunt) · **akkoo** (grandmother) · **akaakayyuu** (grandfather) · **daa'ima** (a baby, a child)

[Picture 9874 — a three-generation family]

And the words that sort people: **dhiira** (a male, a man), **dhalaa** (a female, a woman), **jaarsa** (an old man; a husband), **jaartii** (an old woman; a wife).

Say the family row standing in a circle in your mind — grandparents at the top, parents, brothers and sisters, the daa'ima in arms. Name your own people into the words.

## Sub lesson 2 — Jobs people do

People are also named by their work — *gosoota hojii*, the kinds of jobs:

**barsiisaa** (a teacher — a woman teacher is **barsiistuu**) · **doktoora** (a doctor) · **daldalaa** (a trader) · **konkolaachisaa** (a driver)

[Picture 9875 — a teacher, a doctor, a trader and a driver at work]

Notice **barsiisaa / barsiistuu**: the teacher word changes its ending for a man and a woman — the first taste of a pattern the Sentence lessons teach in full. Name the jobs you see on your own street.

## Sub lesson 3 — Words for good and bad character

The Family reading ends by sorting **character** — and its word sets are worth learning whole.

**The good set:** **ajajamuu** (obeying) · **walgargaaruu** (helping each other) · **hangafa kabajuu** (respecting elders) · **barsiisota kabajuu** (respecting teachers) · **maatii kabajuu** (respecting family) · **obsaan dubbachuu** (speaking patiently) · **waan gaarii raawwachuu** (doing good things)

**The bad set:** **arrabsuu** (insulting) · **hanna** (stealing) · **soba / kijiba** (lying) · **dogoggorsuu** (misleading) · **meeshaalee balleessuu** (breaking things) · **walreebuu** (fighting) · **jeequu** (disturbing)

[Picture 9876 — the good set and the bad set as two word columns]

Learn them as two roads: the reading asks friends to be of good character — now you own the words that say what that means.

## Say it to your companion

Take turns naming your own family in the words: 'Abbaa — my father…' Then the character game: one reads a character word, the other answers 'good' or 'bad' and glosses it. All seven good words should pass between you.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw your family tree and label each person with their word.
2. Write the four jobs and beside each, a person you know who does it.
3. Write the good set from memory — seven phrases — then check against the lesson.
