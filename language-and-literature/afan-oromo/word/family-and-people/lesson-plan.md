# Lesson plan — Family and People

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Family and People
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the people words — the family (abbaa, haadha, obboleessa, obboleettii and their elders), the jobs people do, and the words for good and bad character.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Reading lessons, above all the Maatii reading: the family is met first in a text, now in its full word-family.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Family members** — family members named, read and written: abbaa, haadha, obboleessa, obboleettii, adaadaa, akkoo, akaakayyuu, daa'ima; also dhiira, dhalaa, jaarsa, jaartii (G3 Ch. 8).
2. **Jobs people do** — jobs (gosoota hojii) named: barsiisaa/tuu (teacher), doktoora (doctor), daldalaa (trader), konkolaachisaa (driver) (G3 syllabus table).
3. **Words for good and bad character** — the good set and the bad set built from the Maatii reading (G4 Ch. 9): ajajamuu, walgargaaruu, hangafa kabajuu… against arrabsuu, hanna, soba…

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Everyday Sentences — its Maatii reading is where this family first appears. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the family: abbaa, haadha, obboleessa, obboleettii, adaadaa, akkoo, akaakayyuu, daa'ima.
2. Name the jobs: barsiisaa/barsiistuu, doktoora, daldalaa, konkolaachisaa.
3. Sort character words into the good set and the bad set, and gloss them.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A family photo helps, if there is one.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names their family members in English and wonders at the words for helping and lying.
2. **The explanation** — the family words placed in a three-generation circle; the four jobs; the good and bad character sets built from the Family reading (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9874]: a picture of a three-generation family drawn together: grandfather and grandmother, father and mother, brother, sister and a baby, each labelled with their word — the student points down the generations, naming each person's word.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9875]: a picture of four figures at work: a teacher at a board, a doctor with a patient, a trader at a stall, a driver at a wheel — the student names each job and one real person who does it.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9876]: a picture of two word columns on one card: the good set under a sun, the bad set under a dark cloud — the student reads the good column aloud, then sorts three given words into their column.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the family row — abbaa, haadha, obboleessa, obboleettii, adaadaa, akkoo, akaakayyuu, daa'ima — the student says each word after the voice, picturing their own family.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name your families to each other in the words; then sort character words into their sets for each other.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your family in the words, name the four jobs, and sort six character words.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
