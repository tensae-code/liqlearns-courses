# Lesson plan — Animals

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Animals
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the animal words in their two families — the domestic animals of the farmyard and the wild animals of the bush.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Reading lessons; animals were met inside syllable counts and sentences, now they stand as their own word-family.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Words by Their Sounds. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the domestic animals: saree, adurree, harree, hoolaa, sa'a.
2. Say what bineensota bosonaa means and how the wild family differs from the farmyard family.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names the animals they have seen this week and guesses which are never wild.
2. **The explanation** — the five domestic animals named from pictures; the wild animals as a family — bineensota bosonaa — listed and talked about (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9879]: a picture of five animals standing in a farmyard: a dog, a cat, a donkey, a sheep and a cow, each drawn clear and apart — the student names each animal before reading its label, then reads the row.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9880]: a picture of wild animals at a distance in bushland — shapes among trees and grass, none of them tame — the student says what makes these bineensota bosonaa — wild — against the farmyard family.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the five farmyard words slowly — the student says each word and one thing that animal does at home.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name the farmyard five to each other from memory; argue kindly over which family a named animal belongs to.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write the five domestic animals and the wild family's name, with what it means.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
