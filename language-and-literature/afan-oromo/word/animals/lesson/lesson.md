# Animals

## Do it first

Name every animal you have seen this week. A dog, surely. A cat, perhaps. A donkey or a cow if you passed them. Which of your animals would never be found wild in the bush? This lesson sorts the animal words into their two families.

## Sub lesson 1 — Domestic animals

The animals of home and farmyard, named from their pictures:

**saree** (a dog) · **adurree** (a cat) · **harree** (a donkey) · **hoolaa** (a sheep) · **sa'a** (a cow)

[Picture 9879 — the five domestic animals in a farmyard]

Five animals, five words — the farmyard set. You have already met **saree** and **hoolaa** inside other lessons' word work; here they stand together with their companions. Say the row, then shut the book in your mind and name the animals of your own compound.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wild animals

Beyond the farmyard stands the second family: **bineensota bosonaa** — the wild animals, the animals of the bush.

[Picture 9880 — wild animals in bushland]

The book's work with them is **listing and talking**: the wild animals are listed, and learners talk about them — where they live, how they differ from the animals of the compound. The line between the families is the fence: **domestic** animals live with people and work with people; **wild** animals live their own lives in the bush. Hold the two family names — and the farmyard five — and you hold this lesson.

## Say it to your companion

One names an animal from the lesson; the other answers its family — farmyard or bush — and its word. Then talk, as the book talks: where does the wild animal live, and why is it not in anyone's compound?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five domestic animals with their English beside them.
2. Write bineensota bosonaa and its meaning.
3. Draw one farmyard animal and one wild scene; label both.
