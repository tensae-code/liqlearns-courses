# Listening to the World

## Do it first
Listen: "Tomorrow will be cold." One heard sentence — and your hands reach for the warm clothes, not the light ones. Listening changes what you *do*.

This lesson trains the ear on the spoken world: a weather report you must report back, clothes sorted by what you heard, and instructions about time that you must carry out — with a clock that will show whether your ear was true.

## Sub lesson 1 — The weather report
The work chapter of the Grade 4 guide opens its listening with the **oduu haala qilleensaa** — the weather report, the news of the sky's state. Learners **listen to the report and report the day's weather** themselves:

- **qorraa** — cold
- **ho'aa** — hot
- **duumessa** — cloudy

Listen once for the whole sky; listen again for the exact word. Reporting back is the proof: if you can say the day's weather in your own sentence, your ear caught it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Clothes for the weather
The report's knowledge is worn. The guide sorts **clothes for cold weather against clothes for hot weather**: the heavy and the covering against the light and the loose.

[Picture 9927 — the weather report and its clothes]

Sort them from the heard word, not from the window: when the report says *qorraa*, the warm pile grows; when it says *ho'aa*, the light pile. Listening that cannot dress a person has not finished its work.

## Sub lesson 3 — Following instructions about time
The time chapter's listening is active: learners **listen and carry out instructions** — do what the heard words say, at the time they say — and then talk about what they have watched and seen.

[GIF 9926 — clock hands moving to a named time]

The clock is the strictest listener's check there is: an instruction about time, heard wrongly, puts the hands in the wrong place for everyone to see. Listen for the hour word, move the hands, and let the clockface judge.

## Say it to your companion
Give each other the world to hear. One reads a weather report for an invented day; the other reports it back and dresses a drawn figure for it. Then time: one gives an instruction — "at 9:00, play; at 2:00, lessons" — and the other sets a drawn clock's hands and repeats the instruction before acting it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Report today's real weather in one Afaan Oromoo sentence: qorraa, ho'aa or duumessa in it.
2. Lay out tomorrow's clothes from your own report, and say why each piece fits it.
3. Ask someone at home for a time instruction ("at … we eat") and carry it out exactly.
4. Draw a clock showing 9:00 — the play time of the guide's time work — and write what happens then.
