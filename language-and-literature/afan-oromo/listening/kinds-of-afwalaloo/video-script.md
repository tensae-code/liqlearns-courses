# The Kinds of Afwalaloo — video script

**Format note (standing rule):** video is produced from these scripts only — never generated. This script is written to be filmed or read aloud, as a walk past the picture strip: one panel at a time.

**Narrator:**

**Opening.** [Picture 12513 on screen.] "Oral poetry is not one thing. It is a family — and each member has its own place and its own hour. Walk the strip with me."

**Panel 1 — weedduu.** "A singer, alone with a song of longing. *Weedduu* — the song the heart makes when it reaches for someone far away."

**Panel 2 — sirba.** "Now feet are moving. *Sirba* is the kind you do not only sing — you dance it. Song and dance together."

**Panel 3 — eebba.** "An elder's hand over a child's head. *Eebba* — the blessing spoken over a person: may your road be good. But this kind has another face — *abaarsa*, the curse, turned against wrong. Blessing and curse live in the same mouth, and the gathering knows both."

**Panel 4 — faaruu gootaa.** "A shield, a spear, a name carried high. *Faaruu gootaa* — praise sung for a hero, naming his deeds — and its brother *geerarsa*, the heroic chant that stirs men before and after great deeds. You have met praise already in this course: the farruu sung for the plough-ox. Praise is the family's loudest voice."

**Panel 5 — hurursa.** "And the quietest voice of all: a mother, a baby, a song soft as breath. *Hurursa daa'immanii* — the lullaby. Beside it, one more quiet kind with no panel of its own: *gumgummii* — the complaint a wronged person murmurs to themselves, low, where only the heart hears it."

**Close.** "Longing, dance, blessing, heroes, complaint, cradle — seven kinds, one family: afwalaloo."
