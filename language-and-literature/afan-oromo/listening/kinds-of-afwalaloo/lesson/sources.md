**How the lesson maps to the book.** The Grade 7 guide's oral-poetry chapter inventories the kinds of afwalaloo in its teaching note; this lesson carries that inventory whole, grouped by occasion:

| Lesson part | Source |
|---|---|
| Sub lesson 1 — Songs of the heart | Printed pp. 142–143 — the kinds inventory: *weedduu* (song of longing) and *sirba* (song-and-dance) |
| Sub lesson 2 — Blessing and heroes | Printed pp. 142–143 — *eebba* (blessing), with *abaarsa* (cursing) named as its other face; *geerarsa* (heroic chant); *faaruu gootaa* (praise of a hero) |
| Sub lesson 3 — The quiet kinds | Printed pp. 142–143 — *gumgummii* (the murmured complaint) and *hurursa daa'immanii* (the lullaby); the *farruu* cross-reference is to this chapter's own listening song, printed p. 133 |
| Picture 12513 | Drawn for LiqLearns for this lesson — the kinds in a picture strip: a singer (weedduu), dancers (sirba), an elder blessing a child (eebba), a praised hero (faaruu gootaa), a mother soothing a baby (hurursa) |

No picture from the book is reproduced in this lesson, and no book text is copied beyond the guide's own kind names and exercise material named in the table, which are the teaching content itself.
