# Listening to Rivers

## Do it first
Before you hear one word about a river, answer this: what do you already know about the Abbayyaa — the Blue Nile? Where does its water go? What does it do for people?

Hold those thoughts. The Grade 7 guide never lets a listening start cold: **before** the text, you predict from what you know; **during** it, you work — filling gaps, marking true and false, taking notes while the teacher reads, up to three times; **after** it, you respond with your own thought. This lesson hears two rivers inside that frame.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Benefits of the Abbayyaa River
**Before** — predict: a text called *Faayidaa Laga Abbayyaa*, "The Benefits of the Abbayyaa River". What benefits will it name?

**During** — the text, as the guide prints it (summary only — the lesson never copies the text out): the Abbayyaa gives great service — **generating electricity, irrigation farming, recreation and transport**. It is one of Ethiopia's natural resources, and a country has the internationally recognised right to use its soil, water and minerals to improve its people's lives. Its scenery is admirable — **water plunging from the heights, trees around it, the gorge it flows through** — and this natural wealth must be developed and cared for.

The gap-fill answers name its tributaries, from the companion reading: **Daabbus, Dhidheessaa, Fincaa'aa, Gudar, Mogor, Jamaa**.

**After** — respond: which benefit touches your own family's life most nearly? Say why.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Awash River
**Before** — predict from the title: *Laga Hawaas*, the Awash River. What do you know of it?

**During** — the text, as the guide prints it (summary only): the Awash is one of the country's resources, **rising at Ginchi, 90 km west of Finfinnee**; about **1,200 km long** — the second longest of the country's rivers — and it **never leaves its own country**, spending its soil and water on its own people. It generates **electricity**, irrigates **vegetables and fruits**, and feeds **tourist places** on its banks — the Fincaa'aa falls at Malkaa Garbaa and Malkaa Qunxurree, and wildlife parks. In the dry season it plays the great part in **dry-season (bonee) farming** and **sugarcane production**. Citizens together must protect, admire and use this resource, and teach those who do not understand it.

**After** — respond: the Abbayyaa runs out toward the sea; the Awash stays home. What difference does that make to how the two texts praise them?

## Say it to your companion
Retell against each other. One takes the Abbayyaa, the other the Awash: three minutes each, from notes only. The listener's job is the after-stage: one question the text answers, and one thing the retelling missed. Then the tributary test: six names, called in any order — Daabbus, Dhidheessaa, Fincaa'aa, Gudar, Mogor, Jamaa — until both of you hold them all.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the Abbayyaa's four benefits and its six tributaries from memory.
2. Write the Awash's story in five sentences: rise, length, staying home, uses, duty.
3. Draw the two rivers' difference: one flowing out of the country, one ending inside it — from what the texts say.
4. Tell one river's story aloud at home, three-stage style: first ask what they know (before), tell (during), ask their thought (after).
