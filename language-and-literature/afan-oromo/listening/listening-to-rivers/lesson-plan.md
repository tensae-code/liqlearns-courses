# Lesson plan — Listening to Rivers

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Listening · **Lesson:** Listening to Rivers
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Listening (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two great rivers heard in full listening texts, the Grade 7 guide's three-stage way — before, during, after: the Abbayyaa and its benefits, and the Awash, the river that never leaves its country.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Reading lessons' water work — sources, uses and lakes. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Benefits of the Abbayyaa River** — listening text Faayidaa Laga Abbayyaa — full text printed in the guide, taught here as a summary: electricity, irrigation, recreation, transport; its scenery; a country's right to its resources; tributaries named from the companion reading (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 1).
2. **The Awash River** — listening text Laga Hawaas — full text printed in the guide, taught here as a summary: rising at Ginchi, about 1,200 km, the second longest, never leaving its country; electricity, irrigation, falls and parks, dry-season farming and sugarcane (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 5).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Reading lesson Reading Our World — water's sources, uses and lakes are read there. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Listen the three-stage way: predict before, work during, respond after.
2. Retell the Abbayyaa's benefits: electricity, irrigation, recreation, transport — and name its tributaries: Daabbus, Dhidheessaa, Fincaa'aa, Gudar, Mogor, Jamaa.
3. Retell the Awash: rising at Ginchi, about 1,200 km, never leaving its country; electricity, irrigation, tourist places; its part in dry-season farming and sugarcane.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil for during-listening notes.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says everything they already know about the great river before hearing a word — the before stage, lived (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three-stage frame itself first, then the Abbayyaa text's summary heard stage by stage, then the Awash's (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9961] in Sub lesson 1: river water plunging over a rocky fall into a gorge pool, trees on the banks, the water running white down the rock face in a loop — the student watches the fall while saying the scenery sentence — water plunging from the heights, trees around it, the gorge it flows through.
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9960] in Sub lesson 1: a picture of a great river falling through a gorge with trees on its banks, and a second, wider river running through sugarcane fields, the two panels headed Abbayyaa and Hawaas — the student points to each panel and retells that river's benefits from the picture before checking the text summary.
   - ADD AUDIO throughout the lesson: the teacher's voice reading each listening text up to three times, the guide's during-stage way — the student works the during-stage tasks — gap-fills, true/false, notes — on the second and third hearings.
3. **Say it to your companion** — retell the two rivers to each other from notes — one river each — and question each other on tributaries and benefits (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — river water plunging over a fall as the Abbayyaa's benefits are named, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, short-answer, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — tell a family member the Awash's story from your notes, and write the Abbayyaa's six tributaries from memory (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Predicts nothing in the before stage → prior knowledge is never empty: a river's name, a bridge crossed, a story heard. Ask narrower: "have you seen it? heard of it? what does its name tell you?"
- Notes everything during listening and hears nothing → the guide's during-work is shaped: gap-fills, true/false, short notes. Give the shape before the reading, not freedom during it.
- Mixes the two rivers → anchor each to its one strange fact: six named tributaries (Abbayyaa); never leaves its country (Awash). Retell from the anchors.
