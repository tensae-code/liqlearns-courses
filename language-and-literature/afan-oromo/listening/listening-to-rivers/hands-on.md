# Hands-on — Listening to Rivers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Three-stage wall
Three sheets on the wall: BEFORE, DURING, AFTER. For each river, learners pin their predictions on the first sheet before hearing, their gap-fills and notes on the second during, and their response sentences on the third after. The wall keeps the frame visible until it is habit.

## Tributary river map
Draw the Abbayyaa as a big river line on a shared sheet; learners add its six tributaries as joining streams, each labelled — Daabbus, Dhidheessaa, Fincaa'aa, Gudar, Mogor, Jamaa — from memory, checked against the lesson. The Awash gets a second line that stops inside the border: "it never leaves home."

## Falls in a bottle
Pour water from a jug held high into a basin: the plunge of the falls. While it pours, a learner narrates the Abbayyaa's scenery sentence — water plunging from the heights, trees around, the gorge below — so the heard words meet a seen thing.
