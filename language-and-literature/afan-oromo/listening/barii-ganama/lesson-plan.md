# Lesson plan — Barii Ganama

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Listening · **Lesson:** Barii Ganama
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Listening (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the song Barii Ganama ("Early Morning") — sung to its tune, its beat (rukuttaa) identified, and its words explained: a listening lesson carried by a song, the Grade 4 guide's way.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Greetings lesson's morning world — ganama is already the learner's word for the morning division. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Greetings — ganama (morning) is learned there. Recommended, never forced.
- The Listening lesson Listening to Stories and Talk closes with another neighbourhood song; the song-lesson shape is the same.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sing Barii Ganama to its tune, with the class, unaided.
2. Identify the song's beat (rukuttaa) — clap or tap it while singing.
3. Explain the song's words — say what the morning song is about and what its key words mean.

## What you need
Voices. Hands for the beat. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sings the tune but cannot find the beat → strip the words: hum only, and walk the beat with feet. The rukuttaa is a walk, not a pattern to memorise.
- Sings without meaning → stop at one word and explain it, the guide's own third step. A song is understood line by line or it is only noise, however sweet.
- Follows a neighbour instead of carrying the part → sing in shrinking groups: class, half, pairs, solo line — until each voice has stood alone once.
