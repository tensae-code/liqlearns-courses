# Barii Ganama

## Do it first
Hum a song you know — any song. Now clap along to it. Feel that? Under the words there is a **beat**, walking steadily, carrying them.

*Barii Ganama* means "Early Morning" — *barii*, the dawn; *ganama*, morning. The Grade 4 guide teaches it as a listening-and-song lesson: sung to its tune, its beat found, its words understood. Not sung *at* you — sung *by* you, until you can carry it alone.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meeting the song
The plants chapter of the Grade 4 guide carries the song **Barii Ganama** — "Early Morning".

[Picture 9949 — barii ganama, early morning]

First the song is *met*: heard whole, then sung with the teacher, then sung to its tune by the class. A song is listening you can hold: the tune carries the words into memory in a way a read page cannot. The song's full wording lives in the student book that is not in hand — this lesson teaches the song as the guide evidences it: its name, its tune-work, its beat and its word-meanings, and the class sings it as the guide directs, from the teacher's voice.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its beat
Under the words walks the **rukuttaa** — the beat. The guide has learners **identify the beat** of the song: where the steps fall, steady as walking.

Clap it. Tap it on the desk. The beat is the song's floor: words may run fast or slow over it, long sounds lengthening and short sounds shortening — the ear work of this whole course — but the beat does not stumble. When the class can keep the beat with the words gone, the listening has landed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its words explained
Then the guide **explains the song's words**: a song you sing without understanding is a birdcall; a song you understand is speech with wings.

The dawn words — *barii*, *ganama* — and the morning world the song names are opened one by one, the way the guide works a song's words after its tune is held. Sing it again when the words are explained: the same song, now carrying meaning in every line.

## Say it to your companion
Sing in pairs: one carries the tune, the other keeps only the beat — claps, no words. Swap. Then sing together and stop at your companion's signal: each of you explains one word of the song before singing on.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sing Barii Ganama at home tomorrow at real barii — dawn — and notice what the morning around you is doing.
2. Teach the beat to someone at home: clap it until they can keep it alone.
3. Write the title and the two dawn words — barii, ganama — and what each means.
4. Sing it once more at galgala (evening) — and feel how a morning song changes out of its hour.
