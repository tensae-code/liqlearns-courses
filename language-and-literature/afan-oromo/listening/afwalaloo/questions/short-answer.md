# Afwalaloo — short answer

Write a short answer for each. The number shows which sub lesson each question belongs to.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** How does afwalaloo stay alive if it is not written in books?
**Answer: it is presented by mouth and passed on by hearing — one generation's voices carry it into the next generation's memory.**
*Why it matters: that is exactly what "a branch of afoola" means.*

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Name the six features of afwalaloo.
**Answer: qusannaa jechootaa (economy of words), filannoo jechootaa (choice of words), walqixxummaa (equal measure), rukuttaa manaa (end-rhyme), yeedaloo (melody), suur-sammuu uumuu (imagery).**
*Why it matters: the six features are the guide's whole test for recognising the poetry.*

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Name two freedoms an oral poem has that ordinary writing does not.
**Answer: any two — it may break the rules of grammar; it may borrow a word from another language; it may coin a new word of its own.**
*Why it matters: the poem's first law is that it must say the thing.*
