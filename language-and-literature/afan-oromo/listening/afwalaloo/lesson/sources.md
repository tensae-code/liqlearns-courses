**How the lesson maps to the book.** Chapter 10 of Afaan Oromoo's Grade 7 guide — the oral-poetry chapter — carries a full teaching note on afwalaloo between the praise-song listening lesson and the figures-of-speech lessons. This lesson stocks that note whole:

| Lesson part | Source |
|---|---|
| Sub lesson 1 — Poetry by mouth | Printed pp. 137–139 — the teaching note: afwalaloo is poetry presented by mouth (*afaan*), a branch of *afoola*; carried by *yeedaloo* (melody) |
| Sub lesson 2 — The six features | Printed pp. 137–139 — the note's six features (*amaloota*): *qusannaa jechootaa* (economy of words), *filannoo jechootaa* (choice of words), *walqixxummaa* (equal measure), *rukuttaa manaa* (end-rhyme), *yeedaloo* (melody), *suur-sammuu uumuu* (imagery) |
| Sub lesson 3 — The shape of a poem | Printed pp. 137–139 and the note's form passage (to printed p. 142) — the form (*unkaa*): line (*bo'oo*), stanza (*buufata*), the capital start, long or short; the poem's freedoms (breaking grammar, borrowing, coining) |
| Picture 12512 | Drawn for LiqLearns for this lesson — an elder reciting poetry to a circle of seated listeners at dusk |

No picture from the book is reproduced in this lesson, and no book text is copied beyond the guide's own feature names, definitions and exercise material named in the table, which are the teaching content itself.
