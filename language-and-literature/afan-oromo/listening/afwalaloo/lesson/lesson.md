# Afwalaloo — Oral Poetry

*Afwalaloo*

## You do it, we guide you

### Before you start

In the last listening lesson, a farmer sang the praise of his ox. Here is a question about that song: what KIND of thing is it? It was never written in a book for farmers to read — it lives in mouths and memories, and it is sung. Your book has a name for poetry like that, and a whole teaching note about it. This lesson learns the name, the marks, and the shape. [Picture 12512 — an elder reciting poetry to a circle of seated listeners at dusk.]

## Sub lesson 1 — Poetry by mouth

Everything a people passes **from mouth to mouth** — stories, proverbs, sayings, songs — is called **afoola**: the heritage carried in voices. **Afwalaloo** is its poetry: *poetry presented by mouth* — the word itself says it: **afaan**, the mouth, and **walaloo**, poetry.

An afwalaloo poem is not read off a page. It is **performed**: an elder or a singer presents it at a gathering, carried by its melody — its **yeedaloo** — and the listeners carry it away in their memory. It belongs to everyone who hears it. The praise song of the plough-ox is one: a *farruu*, an afwalaloo sung for the ox.

### Now you try

Name three things that belong to afoola. Then say, in one sentence, what makes afwalaloo different from a poem printed in a book. *(Answer in your own words: it is presented by mouth and carried in memory, not printed and read.)*

## Sub lesson 2 — The six features

Your book names six features — *amaloota* — that mark afwalaloo. Learn them one by one:

1. **Qusannaa jechootaa — economy of words.** Few words, each carrying heavy meaning. The oral poem does not waste a syllable: it must be carried in memory, so it travels light.
2. **Filannoo jechootaa — choice of words.** The words are picked, not poured: the right word, in the right place, for sound and sense together.
3. **Walqixxummaa — equal measure.** Line is balanced against line — the beats and lengths match, so the poem walks evenly.
4. **Rukuttaa manaa — end-rhyme.** The lines close with the same sound: the end of one line answers the end of the next, and the ear knows where each line lands.
5. **Yeedaloo — melody.** The poem is sung or chanted, not spoken flat: the tune is one of its two handles (remember the riddle from the praise-song lesson — the words and the tune).
6. **Suur-sammuu uumuu — imagery.** The words make pictures in the listener's mind — *suur-sammuu*, a picture for the mind. The ox you never saw stands in front of you when the farruu is sung well.

Think back to the praise song: the short chosen lines, the returning refrain, the ox standing before your eyes — the features were all at work.

### Now you try

Which feature is at work when a poem's lines all end with the same sound? And which one is at work when you can SEE the thing the poem sings about? *(Answers: rukuttaa manaa; suur-sammuu uumuu.)*

## Sub lesson 3 — The shape of a poem

The poem's form — its *unkaa* — has named parts:

- A **line** is a **bo'oo** — one row of the poem.
- Lines gathered together make a **stanza** — a **buufata**.
- Each new line **begins with a capital letter**.
- A poem may be **long or short** — the saying decides, not a rule.

And an oral poem is **free** in ways ordinary writing is not: it may **break the rules of grammar** when the saying demands it; it may **borrow** a word from another language, or **coin** a new word of its own. The poem's first law is that it must say the thing — the rules of ordinary talk come second.

### Now you try

Take any short poem or song you know by heart. Count its lines and its stanzas, and point to where each line begins. Did it bend any rule? Say which one.

## Do It Yourself

Choose a praise, a lullaby or a song you know by mouth. Write down its shape: how many lines, how many stanzas. Then list which of the six features you can find in it — with the line or moment where each one shows. Finish by saying it aloud to a companion, the way it was made to be carried.

## Say it to your companion

*"Afwalaloo — poetry by mouth: few words, chosen words, even measure, rhyming ends, a melody, and pictures in the mind."*
