# Afwalaloo — Oral Poetry

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Listening · **Lesson:** Afwalaloo — Oral Poetry
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Listening (CURRICULUM.md).

**What this lesson teaches:** the Grade 7 guide's own teaching note on oral poetry, whole: what *afwalaloo* is (poetry presented by mouth, a branch of *afoola*), its six features (*amaloota*) named in the guide's own words — economy of words, choice of words, equal measure, end-rhyme, melody, imagery — and its form (*unkaa*): the line (*bo'oo*), the stanza (*buufata*), the capital start, and the poem's freedom to break grammar and coin words. This lesson names the kind that the praise song of the last lesson (Farruu Qotiyyoo) belongs to, and it stands before The Kinds of Afwalaloo, where the family is walked kind by kind.

**What the learner will be able to do:**
- Say what afwalaloo is and where it sits inside afoola.
- Name the six features and say what each one does.
- Name a poem's parts — line and stanza — and say how an oral poem's form is free.

## Sub lessons
1. Poetry by mouth — one list, in this order, in `lesson/lesson.md`; each opens by proving the one before, by the 2026-11-08 rule stated there.
2. The six features
3. The shape of a poem

**Gate pool:** Sub lesson 2's prove-it check is drawn from sub lesson 1's whole question set (mixed with earlier parts of this lesson), and sub lesson 3's check from the whole set before it — every check at most 5 questions, picked from the whole set, never a fixed list.

**How the lesson runs:** The picture first (the elder and the circle — what oral poetry looks like), then the definition, then the six features one by one with a listening ear on the praise song the class already knows, then the poem's shape and freedoms. The video script is spoken by two voices: a narrator and an elder.

**Level of the language:** Full Afaan Oromoo sentences, with the poetry words glossed in English as they come.
