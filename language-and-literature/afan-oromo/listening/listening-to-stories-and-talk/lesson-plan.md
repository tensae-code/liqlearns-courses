# Lesson plan — Listening to Stories and Talk

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Listening · **Lesson:** Listening to Stories and Talk
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Listening (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the ear's full range: a listening story with a hidden watcher (Kara Adeemaa Beela'e), two dialogues that teach while they talk, two texts about living together and planning a family, a self-questioning poem, and a neighbourhood song — heard, matched to pictures, and answered.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the Listening lessons before it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

2. **Two dialogues — becoming, and a warning** — listening dialogue (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 11, summary only): Beektuu and Beekaa on what they want to become; listening dialogue (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 6, summary only): Taliilee and her teacher Bilisee on irregular migration.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Listening to Rivers and Listening to History and Heroes — the three-stage frame and the dialogue-listening are used at full stretch here. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Hear the traveller's story, match it to pictures and answer questions on it.
2. Follow two teaching dialogues: Beektuu and Beekaa on what they want to become; Taliilee and her teacher Bilisee on the danger of irregular migration.
3. Retell Hawaasummaa's values — a person is medicine for a person — and the benefits of family planning.
4. Hear the poem Maafan Tamboo Xuuxaa? as a smoker's self-questioning, and sing the neighbourhood song's burden: houses as shelter from cold and heat.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Pictures for the traveller matching.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells the class about a time they were very hungry on a journey — the story's ground, lived first (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the story heard and matched to pictures; the two dialogues, Grade 4 then Grade 7; the two living-together texts; the poem and the song to close (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9952] in Sub lesson 1: a picture of a traveller eating beans in a field while a woman watches hidden behind the field's edge, the bean stages shown in three small panels — pod, shelled beans, inner part — the student points to each panel in the story's order and retells that stage before hearing the story again.
   - ADD AUDIO throughout the lesson: the teacher's voice reading the story, the dialogues and the poem aloud, the guides' listening way, with the neighbourhood song sung to its beat — the student matches pictures to the story, answers questions on the dialogues, and joins the song when the beat is held.
3. **Say it to your companion** — retell the traveller's stages to each other and answer as Taliilee's teacher would — advice in a caring voice (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a traveller eating beans in a field while a woman watches hidden, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, ordering, short-answer, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — tell the traveller's story at home, in order, and write the three things the hidden woman saw him eat (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Traveller's stages blur → the stages are physical: pod ON, pod OFF, inside ONLY. Mime the hands while retelling; the body sequences what the ear heard.
- Dialogues heard as one voice → two chairs, two bodies. A dialogue is learned by standing in both chairs, not by summarising both parts flatly.
- Poem heard as advice, missing its form → it is a man questioning *himself*: every line is a question he cannot escape. Read the questions as questions.
- Family-planning benefits listed without the harms → the text pairs them: spacing is hope *because* closeness harms. Retell benefit and harm together, as the answers do.
