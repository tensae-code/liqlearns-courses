# Listening to History and Heroes

## Do it first
A hero is a life that carries more than itself. In this lesson your ear carries three: a whole country's victory, a runner from the highlands of Arsi, and a woman who picked up famine's orphans and would not put them down.

All three texts are printed in the Grade 7 guide — the teacher reads them aloud — and all three are taught here as summaries only, in the guide's three stages: before, during, after.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Victory of Adwa
**Before** — the title: *Injifannoo Adwaa*, the Victory of Adwa. What do you know of Adwa?

**During** — the text (summary only): Ethiopia is a country of many nations, nationalities and peoples, and her freedom has been preserved by their great sacrifice. Knowing a people's history and culture strengthens patriotic feeling, and a generation filled with it guards the country's sovereignty. In **1896** (as the guide prints the year) Italy invaded to colonise and enslave, and at Adwa was defeated by the country's heroes, with no way back. The victory stopped Italy's colonial expansion and changed world history — the first victory of Black people over white colonisers in modern history. By it Ethiopia kept her sovereignty and remained the one African country that never fell under European colonial rule. Adwa created national unity: the people, without dividing by nation or faith, together broke an enemy armed with modern weapons and bequeathed us freedom.

The post-listening answers name Oromo heroes: **Koloneel Abdiisaa Aagaa, Jeneraal Taaddasaa Birruu, Jeneraal Waaqoo Guutuu, Koloneel Aliyyii Cirrii, Gabayyoo Gurmuu** — and the Oromo cultural hall (*galma aadaa Oromoo*).

**After** — respond: the text says the new generation must build, in brotherhood, a country for all its citizens. What is your part in that building?

## Sub lesson 2 — Derartu Tulu — the runner
**Before** — a biography of an athlete. What does a runner's life hold? Predict from the title: *Atileetii Daraartuu Tulluu*.

**During** — the life (summary only): born 21 Bitootessa 1972 (E.C.) at **Bekoji in Arsi**, raised herding cattle in the highlands. She became the **first African woman to win Olympic gold (1992)**. Then loss: 1993–94 went to a knee injury — and she returned to win the **1995 IAAF World Cross-Country title**, and in **2000 her second Olympic 10,000 m gold** — the first woman ever to win two — plus a third world cross-country title, the 2001 World 10,000 m, and the London and Tokyo marathons. Fourth at the 2005 Worlds in a personal best 2:23:30. At **37**, she won the **2009 New York City Marathon**. Six Olympic and World gold medals in all.

**After** — respond: her life turns on the injury years. What carried her through them, as the text tells it?

## Sub lesson 3 — Abbabach Goobanaa — mother to orphans
**Before** — the chapter is volunteering (Tola Ooltummaa). The biography: *Abbabach Goobanaa*. What might her good be?

**During** — the life (summary only): born 1936 (Ethiopian calendar) in North Shewa near **Fiche**, in the year Italy invaded Ethiopia; her father died fighting the Italians. Given in marriage as a child against her will, she fled to **Finfinnee** and was sheltered. She took three months' handcraft training, studied at evening school to grade 4, and worked for a grain-export company, where she met her second husband Kabbadaa and raised his five children as her own. In **1982**, returning from Gishe Mariam through famine-stricken Wollo, she found infants whose mothers had died and carried them home — choosing the children when her husband made her choose between them and the marriage. The children multiplied: first the **"Biruh Tesfa"** organisation, and in **1984** a foundation in her name, which rescues the poor, raises orphans, educates them and gives mothers health services — about **seven million people** benefited. She received many awards, an honorary doctorate from Jimma University, and is called **"the Mother Teresa of Africa"**.

**After** — respond: the choice of 1982 — children or marriage. What does the text honour in it?

## Say it to your companion
Retell the lives in rotation: one of you takes Adwa, one Derartu, one Abbabach — or take turns across all three. The listener checks from notes: dates in order? Deeds complete? Then the hard questions: Derartu's listener must ask about the injury years; Abbabach's about the 1982 choice; Adwa's must name the heroes list back.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write Derartu Tulu's life as a date line: 1972, 1992, 1993–94, 1995, 2000, 2009.
2. Write Abbabach Goobanaa's life in five sentences.
3. Write the Oromo heroes of Adwa from memory: five names.
4. Tell one whole life at home, in order, without notes.
