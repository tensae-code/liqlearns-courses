# Lesson plan — Listening to History and Heroes

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Listening · **Lesson:** Listening to History and Heroes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Listening (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** three lives and one victory, heard: the Victory of Adwa (Injifannoo Adwaa), the runner Derartu Tulu breaking the finish tape, and Abbabach Goobanaa, mother to orphans — listening biographies in the guide's three stages.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Listening to Rivers — the three-stage ear is trained. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Listening to Rivers — the before/during/after frame is learned there. Recommended, never forced.
- The Writing lesson Kinds of Writing teaches biography (seenaa jireenyaa), this lesson's written kind.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Retell the Victory of Adwa: Italy's 1896 invasion, the united defeat of the coloniser, Ethiopia's unbroken sovereignty — and name Oromo heroes of the post-listening answers.
2. Retell Derartu Tulu's life: Bekoji, herding cattle, first African woman to win Olympic gold (1992), the injury years, the second gold (2000), the 2009 New York City Marathon at 37.
3. Retell Abbabach Goobanaa's life: Fiche, the flight to Finfinnee, the famine road of 1982, the children, Biruh Tesfa and the 1984 foundation, "Mother Teresa of Africa".

## What you need
Paper and a pencil for during-listening notes. A finish-line ribbon for the Derartu moment in Hands-on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names one hero they already know and says what a hero is, before the texts begin (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three hearings in the guide's order through the year: Abbabach's biography is placed in its chapter sequence here with Adwa and Derartu, each heard in the three stages and retold from notes (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9946] in Sub lesson 2: a woman runner in Ethiopian colours breaking through a finish-line tape, arms rising as the tape parts — the student retells Derartu's road to 2000 while watching, and raises both arms with the runner as the tape breaks.
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9951] in Sub lesson 1: a picture of Ethiopian patriots of many peoples marching together under the flag, a runner crossing a finish line, and a woman carrying two infants through a dry landscape — the three hearings in three panels — the student points to each panel and names its text before the listening begins — the before-stage predictions made visible.
   - ADD AUDIO throughout the lesson: the teacher's voice reading each biography up to three times, the guide's during-stage way — the student takes notes on the second hearing — dates and deeds — and answers the after-stage question on the third.
3. **Say it to your companion** — retell the lives against each other — dates and deeds checked from notes, not from memory's drift (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a runner breaking the finish tape as Derartu's second gold is told, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, ordering, short-answer, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — tell one of the three lives at home, fully, and write its five key dates or deeds (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Dates drift in retelling → anchor each life to its turning years only (Derartu: 1992, 2000; Abbabach: 1982, 1984; Adwa: 1896 as printed) and hang the rest of the deeds on them.
- Adwa retold as one people's victory → the text's own point is unity: many nations and faiths, together. Ask who is named in it, and restore the togetherness.
- Derartu's injury years skipped → they are the life's hinge: without the loss, the return means nothing. A retelling that skips them fails the after-stage question.
