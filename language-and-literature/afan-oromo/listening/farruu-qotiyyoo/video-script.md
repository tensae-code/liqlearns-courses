# Farruu Qotiyyoo — video script (a performance script)

**Format note (standing rule):** video is produced from these scripts only — never generated. This is a PERFORMANCE script: the song in this lesson is carried as a summary (its text stays in the teacher's guide, printed p. 133, for the teacher to sing from), so this script performs the praise aloud in the lesson's own words, and marks where the class joins in. Read it with a singer's voice — slow, warm, proud.

**Performer:**

**Opening.** "Before a song, we sharpen our ears. Two riddles first — the way your book opens this lesson. *What is tied together, and works upon the ground?* … The plough and its harness — the gear of the ox. And the second riddle calls the poem itself *a branch of afoola with two handles* — hold that thought: the words, and the tune."

**The praise, spoken.** "Now hear the shape of the song — *Walaloo Farruu Qotiyyoo*, the praise of the plough-ox. The singer looks at his ox and sees beauty: fat and shining, strong in the shoulders, the tips of its horns held high. He sings its CLOTHES — *uffata* — and the ox's clothes are not cloth: they are the gear of its work, the harness it wears into the field. He sings the farmer — *qonnaan bulaa* — walking behind, and the straight furrow opening in the earth. And through it all, one cry comes back and back—" [the class joins:] "**Ushururuu!** — the cry of wonder."

**During — ears open.** "When the teacher sings the song from the book, listen for the missing words — the fat one, the washed-clean one, the bone — and hold them ready. And every time the refrain comes, it is yours: *Ushururuu!*"

**Close.** "A song that praises the one who feeds you. That is *farruu* — and the ox has earned every word of it."
