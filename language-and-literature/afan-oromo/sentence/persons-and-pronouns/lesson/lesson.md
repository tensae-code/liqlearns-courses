# Persons and Pronouns

## Do it first
Point at yourself and say "I" in your own language. Point at a friend: "you". Point at a boy across the room: "he". At a girl: "she". At your whole group, yourself inside it: "we". At a group over there: "they".

Six pointings, six little words. Afaan Oromoo has its own six, and this lesson gives them to you — and shows you the secret they carry: in Afaan Oromoo the verb at the end of the sentence changes its shape to match the person, so the sentence itself tells you who did it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The pronouns
Here are the pronouns (*bamaqaalee*) — the little words that stand in for people's names:

- **ana / ani** — I
- **ati** — you (one person)
- **isa** — he / him
- **ishee** — she / her
- **nuyi** — we
- **isaan** — they

Say them with the pointings from Do it first. *Ana* — point at yourself. *Ati* — point at your friend. *Isa* — the boy. *Ishee* — the girl. *Nuyi* — your whole group, with you inside it. *Isaan* — the group over there.

Two of them you will meet in two shapes: *ana* and *ani* are both "I", and "we" appears as *nuyi* in the Grade 3 book and as *nuti* in the Grade 4 book. Both shapes are real; the books use them in different places, and this lesson keeps each where its book puts it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The persons — first, second, third
The Grade 4 guide sorts the pronouns into **persons** (*ramaddii*):

- **First person** — the one who is speaking: **ani** (I, one), **nuti** (we, many).
- **Second person** — the one spoken to: **ati** (you).
- **Third person** — the one spoken about: **isa** (he), **ishee** (she), **isaan** (they).

The guide's table crosses the persons with two more sorts: **Dhiira / Dubara** — masculine and feminine — and **Baay'ina**, number: **qeenxee** is one, **danuu** is many. So *ani* is first person, one; *nuti* is first person, many; *isaan* is third person, many.

Hold the three persons like three chairs: the speaker sits in the first, the listener in the second, and everyone talked about in the third.

## Sub lesson 3 — Pronouns in sentences — the verb agrees
Now watch the pronouns work. The Grade 4 guide's answer key gives the verb "came" with every person:

- **isheen dhufte** — she came
- **ani dhufe** — I came
- **nuti dhufne** — we came
- **ati dhufte** — you came
- **isaan dhufan** — they came

Look at the endings: **-e, -ne, -te, -an**. The verb changes its ending to agree with its person — that is the Afaan Oromoo way, and the guide teaches it *alkallattiin*, through examples, not head-on. *Isheen* and *ati* both take **dhufte** here; the pronoun in front keeps them apart.

The same agreement runs through the guide's market chapter, where pronoun sentences are built with trade words — the trader word itself goes plural, *daldalaa → daldaltoota* — and every sentence still begins from its person.

## Say it to your companion
Point and name, one by one: yourself (ana/ani), your companion (ati), a boy (isa), a girl (ishee), you both together (nuyi/nuti), a group across the room (isaan). Then say the five "came" sentences — isheen dhufte, ani dhufe, nuti dhufne, ati dhufte, isaan dhufan — and let your companion name the person of each one from the verb's ending alone.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the six pronouns in a row, with the pointing for each: self, friend, boy, girl, my group, that group.
2. Write the persons table from memory: first — ani, nuti; second — ati; third — isa, ishee, isaan.
3. Write one true sentence for each pronoun — who really came, went, ate or played today — with the verb ending that agrees.
4. Cover the pronouns and read only the verb endings of your sentences. Can you still tell who each sentence is about?
