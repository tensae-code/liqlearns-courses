# Lesson plan — One and Many

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** One and Many
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** nouns come in one (qeenxee) and many (danuu) — and Afaan Oromoo builds the many from the one with endings like -oota, -toota, -neen and -wwan, and can strip them back to the one again.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word lessons and on Persons and Pronouns next door — the learner already meets qeenxee and danuu as the number words of the pronoun table. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **One and many — qeenxee and danuu** — singular and plural nouns (maqaa qeenxee fi danuu) and tishoo words, as the Grade 7 mother-tongue guide's chapter names them in its contents and objectives.
2. **Making plurals** — the Grade 4 guide's singular → plural answer key: nama→namoota, kitaaba→kitaabota, hoolaa→hoolota, mana→manneen, barataa→barattoota, gamoo→gamoowwan, konkolaataa→konkolaattota.
3. **Back to one — plural to singular** — the same street walked backwards, from the Grade 4 guide's plural → singular answer key: firoota→fira, manneen→mana, haadholii→haadha, barattoota→barataa, barsiistota→barsiisaa.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Persons and Pronouns — qeenxee (one) and danuu (many) are first met there, in the persons table. Recommended, never forced.
- The number-words lessons in Word carry the counting words; this lesson is about the noun's own shape changing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what qeenxee (singular — one) and danuu (plural — many) mean.
2. Make plurals the books' way: nama→namoota, kitaaba→kitaabota, hoolaa→hoolota, mana→manneen, barataa→barattoota, gamoo→gamoowwan, konkolaataa→konkolaattota.
3. Run the street backwards: firoota→fira, manneen→mana, haadholii→haadha, barattoota→barataa, barsiistota→barsiisaa.
4. Say that different nouns take different plural endings — there is no single ending for all.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A book and a pile of books for the GIF moment, if you have them.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner holds up one book, then a pile of books, and feels the word itself must change between the two handfuls (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — singular and plural nouns as the Grade 7 guide's chapter names them; the Grade 4 guide's plural-making answer key; then the same street walked backwards, plural to singular (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9931] in Sub lesson 2: one book on a table multiplying into a stack of many books, the words kitaaba → kitaabota under them — the student says the one word, watches the books multiply, and says the many word as it lands.
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9928] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of the one-and-many chart: the seven forwards pairs in two columns, one (qeenxee) on the left and many (danuu) on the right, each tail picked out in a second colour — the student covers the right column and says each many-shape before uncovering it.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 3: a voice reading the five backwards pairs — firoota → fira, manneen → mana, haadholii → haadha, barattoota → barataa, barsiistota → barsiisaa — the student listens and writes the one-shape of each pair before checking.
3. **Say it to your companion** — deal the plural pairs like cards — one names the one, the other answers with the many, then swap (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — one book multiplying into many, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — find five things at home, write each in its one shape and its many shape (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Looks for one tail for every noun → the books themselves give four different tails in one answer key. The noun carries its tail with it; learn them together, word and tail.
- Chops haadholii to a stump → the return trip is not always a clean cut; haadholii settles back into haadha. Read the key pairs whole, both shapes as whole words.
- Mixes qeenxee/danuu with the counting words → those live in the Word lessons; here qeenxee and danuu name the noun's shape, one or many.
