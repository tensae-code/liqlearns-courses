# One and Many

## Do it first
Hold one book in your hand. Now hold a pile of books. In English the word grows a small tail: book — book**s**. Afaan Oromoo grows tails too, but it has several different tails, and each noun knows its own.

This lesson teaches the one and the many — *qeenxee* and *danuu* — the way the guides build them: forwards, from one to many, and backwards, from many to one.

## Sub lesson 1 — One and many — qeenxee and danuu
A noun that names **one** is **qeenxee** — singular. A noun that names **many** is **danuu** — plural.

The Grade 7 guide gives a whole grammar lesson to *maqaa qeenxee fi danuu* — singular and plural nouns — and to *tishoo* words beside them: words identified and explained in that same lesson. You met the two number words already in the pronoun table of Persons and Pronouns: there they counted people; here they count nouns.

Every noun you know lives in both houses. *Kitaaba* — one book — lives in the qeenxee house. Its many-shape lives in the danuu house. The next sub lesson shows you the doors between the houses.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making plurals
Here is the Grade 4 guide's own answer key — one on the left, many on the right:

- **nama → namoota** (person → people)
- **kitaaba → kitaabota** (book → books)
- **hoolaa → hoolota** (sheep → sheep, many)
- **mana → manneen** (house → houses)
- **barataa → barattoota** (learner → learners)
- **gamoo → gamoowwan** (building → buildings)
- **konkolaataa → konkolaattota** (car → cars)

Watch for the tails: **-oota, -toota, -neen, -wwan**. There is no one tail for every noun — *mana* takes -neen, *gamoo* takes -wwan, *barataa* takes -toota. Each noun knows its own tail, and you learn them noun by noun, the way the guide's grammar spine does it all year: *alkallattiin*, through examples.

## Sub lesson 3 — Back to one — plural to singular
A good reader runs the street both ways. The guide's key for the return trip:

- **firoota → fira** (relatives → a relative)
- **manneen → mana** (houses → a house)
- **haadholii → haadha** (mothers → a mother)
- **barattoota → barataa** (learners → a learner)
- **barsiistota → barsiisaa** (teachers → a teacher)

Read each pair right to left and the many melts back into the one: the tail comes off, and sometimes the inside of the word settles back too — *haadholii* gives back *haadha*, not a chopped stump. When a long danuu word stops you in reading, strip its tail off first and read the one hiding inside.

## Say it to your companion
Deal the pairs like cards. Say a one — *kitaaba* — and your companion answers with its many — *kitaabota*. Then turn the deck over: you say a many — *manneen* — and they answer with the one — *mana*. Seven pairs forwards, five pairs back.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find five things at home. Write each word twice: its qeenxee shape and its danuu shape.
2. Write the seven forwards pairs from Sub lesson 2 from memory. Check them against the lesson.
3. Write the five backwards pairs from Sub lesson 3 from memory. Check them.
4. Pick the pair whose tail surprises you most — mana → manneen, or gamoo → gamoowwan — and write it three times, saying it aloud each time.
