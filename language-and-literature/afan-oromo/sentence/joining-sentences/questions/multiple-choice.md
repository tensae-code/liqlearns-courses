# Joining Sentences — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Sirna tuqaalee is —
a) the alphabet  b) the punctuation system  c) a kind of story
**Answer: b) the punctuation system.**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A sentence that asks must end with —
a) a comma  b) a full stop  c) a question mark
**Answer: c) a question mark.**

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** The items of a written list are separated by —
a) speech marks  b) commas  c) nothing
**Answer: b) commas.**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Himoota dachaa are —
a) compound sentences  b) single words  c) questions only
**Answer: a) compound sentences** — two whole sentences joined as equals.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** In a compound sentence, each half —
a) cannot stand alone  b) has no verb  c) could stand alone
**Answer: c) could stand alone.**

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** In a joined sentence (himoota ceelaa), one part —
a) leans on the main sentence  b) is always a question  c) has no doer
**Answer: a) leans on the main sentence** — it cannot stand by itself.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** "Bring it here" is a sentence that —
a) tells  b) asks  c) orders
**Answer: c) orders.**

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** Hiika dhokataa is —
a) a hidden or contextual meaning of a word  b) a spelling rule  c) a plural ending
**Answer: a) a hidden or contextual meaning of a word.**
