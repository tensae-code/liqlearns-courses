# Lesson plan — Joining Sentences

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Joining Sentences
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sentences are finished with punctuation marks (sirna tuqaalee), joined into compound sentences (himoota dachaa), linked into joined sentences (himoota ceelaa) — and every sentence does a job: it tells, asks, orders or exclaims.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole Sentence unit so far — the learner builds plain sentences with agreeing verbs. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the

## Sub lessons

1. **Punctuation marks — sirna tuqaalee** — punctuation (sirna tuqaalee) used correctly in writing (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 8 objectives; exercise answers punctuate questions, lists and quoted speech naming the novel Godaannisa and the film Eelaa).
2. **Compound sentences — himoota dachaa** — compound sentences (himoota dachaa) built (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 8 objectives) — two whole sentences standing side by side as equals, joined.

4. **Kinds of sentences by what they do** — sentence types by their function (gosoota himaa tajaajila isaaniin) and the hidden/contextual meanings of words (hiika dhokataa) (Grade 7 guide, Ch. 6 contents/objectives).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other Sentence lessons — plain sentences with persons, plurals and present forms. Recommended, never forced.
- The Writing lessons use this lesson's punctuation and joining from their first page.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Use punctuation marks (sirna tuqaalee) correctly in writing, including in questions, lists and quoted speech.
2. Build a compound sentence (himoota dachaa) — two whole sentences joined as equals.
3. Meet the joined sentence (himoota ceelaa) of the Grade 7 guide's water chapter.
4. Sort sentences by what they do: telling, asking, ordering, exclaiming.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

1. **Do it first** — the learner reads one long breathless string of words and feels where the stops, joins and question marks must fall (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — punctuation first, from the Grade 7 guide's exercise answers; then compound sentences; then joined sentences; then the jobs sentences do (lesson/lesson.md).

5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Joins everything with commas → a comma cannot marry two whole sentences; that is the join's work, and the full stop's. Each half of a compound must first be finishable alone.
- Cannot tell dachaa from ceelaa → cover one half. If the other still makes a whole sense, it was compound (dachaa). If it leans and waits, it is joined (ceelaa).
- Forgets the question mark → an asking sentence ends asking; if your voice rises at the end when you read it, the paper must rise too.
