# Complex Sentences — video script

**Format note (standing rule):** video is produced from these scripts only — never generated. This script is written to be filmed or read aloud. Follow the lesson plan's order; the learner does each thing first, then hears the explanation.

**Narrator (or teacher on camera):**

**Opening.** "One sentence is standing in the road all by itself. Another one is leaning — it cannot stand; it needs the first one to lean on. Put them together and you have a complex sentence — *himoota xaxamoo*. Today we build them."

**Sub lesson 1 — Standing alone and leaning.** [GIF 12505 plays: the two clause cards slide together.] "Every clause has two parts: a subject — who or what — and a predicate — what is said about it. *Caalaan barataa cimaadha* — Caalaan is a strong student. It stands alone: it is a whole sentence. That is a *ciroo of danda'aa*. But hear this: *sababa roobni roobeef* — because it rained. Is that a sentence? No — it leans. It is waiting for the other half: *mana homaa hin dhaqne* — I did not go anywhere. The leaning clause is a *ciroo hirkataa*, and it begins with a joining word: *yoo, waan, osoo, yemmuu, erga, akka*."

**Sub lesson 2 — Joining two halves.** [Picture 12504 on screen.] "Now the halves game, from your book. I give you a beginning that leans; you find the half that lets it stand. *Yeroon mana oolu* — when I am at home — joins *summuugaa cufeera*: I hang the mosquito net. Six halves, six joins."

**Sub lesson 3 — Counting and writing.** "Last step: count the clauses. One standing clause and one leaning clause — that is two. Some sentences carry three. And then — the book's own finishing task — you write ten complex sentences of your own, and share them with your group."

**Close.** "A clause that stands, a clause that leans, one gold joining word — that is a complex sentence."
