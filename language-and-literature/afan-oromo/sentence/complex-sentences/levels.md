# Complex Sentences — levels

This lesson is a step: doing it fully is what "knowing it" means. Depth, in learning order — the sub lessons of this lesson, which are its levels:

- **Sub lesson 1 (the base level) — Standing alone and leaning.** A clause has a subject and a predicate. The *ciroo of danda'aa* stands alone; the *ciroo hirkataa* leans, starting with a joining word (*hirkisiistota*: akka, waan, osoo, yoo, yemmuu, erga).
- **Sub lesson 2 — Joining two halves into one sentence.** The book's Exercise 9 halves matched and joined into six whole complex sentences.
- **Sub lesson 3 (the top level) — Counting clauses, and writing your own.** Clauses counted in whole sentences (1, 2, 3), and the book's review task done: ten complex sentences written and shared.

A learner "has" this lesson when all three are proved: they can take any sentence, find its clauses, name each kind, and build complex sentences of their own.
