# Connectives — levels

This lesson is a step: doing it fully is what "knowing it" means. Depth, in learning order — the sub lessons of this lesson, which are its levels:

- **Sub lesson 1 (the base level) — The join-words at work.** A connective joins a word, a phrase, a clause or a sentence; the book's nine rainy-season sentences completed with their printed connectives.
- **Sub lesson 2 — Joining equals.** The coordinating kind (*qofgaleessaa*) and its four works: adding (*gichisiistuu*), opposing (*faallessituu*), choosing (*filachiistuu*), reason-giving (*sababaati*) — each with the guide's own word-lists.
- **Sub lesson 3 (the top level) — Joining a dependent clause.** The subordinating kind (*hirkatoo*) and its words — the same words that open the leaning clauses of Complex Sentences — and sentences built with a connective of every kind.

A learner "has" this lesson when all three are proved: they can pick up any connective, say its kind and its work, and use it to join talk of their own.
