# Connectives — video script

**Format note (standing rule):** video is produced from these scripts only — never generated. This script is written to be filmed or read aloud. Follow the lesson plan's order; the learner does each thing first, then hears the explanation.

**Narrator (or teacher on camera):**

**Opening.** "Two sentences are standing apart. Watch what one small gold word can do." [GIF 12507 plays: the two cards slide together, the connective clicks in.] "That word is a connective — *walqabsiistuu*. Today we learn the join-words: what they join, and how your book sorts them."

**Sub lesson 1 — The join-words at work.** "A connective joins a word to a word, a phrase to a phrase, a clause to a clause, a whole sentence to a whole sentence. Your book practises them inside a story about the rainy season. *Barataa cimaan ni moo'a moo ni…?* — will the strong student win, *moo* — or — lose? The little word *moo* holds the two choices up side by side. Nine sentences, nine joins — we do them all."

**Sub lesson 2 — Joining equals.** [Picture 12506 on screen.] "Some connectives join equals — side by side, neither one leaning. Your book calls them *qofgaleessaa*, and it sorts them by their work. The **adders** — *gichisiistuu*: *fi*, *akkasumas*, *dabalees* — they pile things up. The **adversatives** — *faallessituu*: *garuu*, *haata'u malee* — they turn the talk the other way. The **choosers** — *filachiistuu*: *yookiin*, *moo* — they hold up choices. And the **reason-givers** — *sababaati*: *kanaaf*, *malee* — they give the because."

**Sub lesson 3 — Joining a dependent clause.** "The other great kind ties a clause underneath another one — *hirkatoo*: *akkuma*, *erga*, *yoo*, *osoo*, *yemmuu*. You have met these words before: they are the words that make a clause lean, in Complex Sentences. One family of words, two lessons — now you know their name and their work."

**Close.** "Pick the right join-word, and your talk holds together."
