# Saying What Is Happening Now

## Do it first
Look out of the window, or around the room. Things are happening *now*: someone is walking, a dog is barking somewhere, children are playing.

Afaan Oromoo has a special verb shape for "happening now", and another for "happens as a habit". This lesson teaches both — first from pictures, the way the Grade 4 guide does it, then with the Grade 7 guide's own grammar name for the present time.

## Sub lesson 1 — Happening now — sentences from pictures
The Grade 4 guide shows pictures and asks: what is happening? Its answer key gives five sentences — learn them whole:

- **Sareen dutaa jira** — the dog is barking
- **Namichi deemaa jira** — the man is walking
- **Lukkuun iyyaa jira** — the hen is crowing
- **Re'een ni mar'atti** — the goat is bleating
- **Ijoolleen taphachaa jiru** — the children are playing

[Picture 9930 — happening now]

Say each sentence while you picture its scene. The dog, the man, the hen, the goat, the children — each one caught in the middle of its action, not finished, *happening now*.

## Sub lesson 2 — The shapes inside the sentences
Now look inside the sentences. The happening-now shape is built in two parts: the verb stretched with **-aa**, and **jira** — "is" — standing after it:

- dut**aa jira** — is barking (the dog, one)
- deem**aa jira** — is walking (the man, one)
- iyy**aa jira** — is crowing (the hen, one)
- taphach**aa jiru** — are playing (the children, many)

One doer: **jira**. Many doers: **jiru** — the agreement you already know, alive inside the present too.

The goat's sentence wears the other present shape: **Re'een ni mar'atti** — **ni** before the verb, **-atti** at its end. That is the shape for what a creature does as its way — the goat bleats; it is her present and her habit in one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Present and habitual time — hennaa ammeennaa
The Grade 7 guide gives this ground a name: **hennaa ammeennaa** — the present/habitual time. It is the verb's time for what is happening now *and* for what happens again and again as a habit: the sun rises, the goat bleats, we learn.

In the same chapter the guide pairs the grammar with **narrating a simple event in order** (*taatee seenessuu*) — telling a happening step by step. Told in the present time, a narration runs like a film in front of the listener: first this happens, then this, then this — each step alive, not finished.

That is where this lesson's road leads: into the Writing lesson Telling and Explaining in Writing, where narrating an event in order is the first sub lesson. The present forms you hold now are the tools that lesson writes with.

## Say it to your companion
Watch the room, the yard or the street together for one quiet minute. Then trade sentences: one happening-now sentence each — Sareen dutaa jira is your model — until you reach ten between you. Your companion checks each one: is the doer one or many, and does jira or jiru match?

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the five sentences from Sub lesson 1 from memory. Check them against the lesson.
2. Stand at a door or window for one minute. Write three new happening-now sentences about what you really see, with jira or jiru agreeing with the doer.
3. Write two habit sentences about your own day — what you do every morning — in the present time.
4. Narrate a simple event in three steps, in the present: first…, then…, then….
