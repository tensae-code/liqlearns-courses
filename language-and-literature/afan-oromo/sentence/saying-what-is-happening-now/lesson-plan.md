# Lesson plan — Saying What Is Happening Now

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Saying What Is Happening Now
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the present shapes of Afaan Oromoo verbs — the -aa jira / ni -atti forms that say what is happening now (Sareen dutaa jira — the dog is barking) and the present/habitual forms (hennaa ammeennaa) that say what happens as a habit.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Persons and Pronouns and He, She and They — the learner already knows the verb changes its ending for its person. It needs nothing else.

1. **Happening now — sentences from pictures** — present-continuous sentences from pictures, from the Grade 4 guide's answer key: "Sareen dutaa jira", "Namichi deemaa jira", "Lukkuun iyyaa jira", "Re'een ni mar'atti", "Ijoolleen taphachaa jiru".
2. **The shapes inside the sentences** — the present shapes picked out of the same key: -aa jira (one doer), -aa jiru (many doers), ni -atti — the verb agreeing with its doer even in the present.
3. **Present and habitual time — hennaa ammeennaa** — the Grade 7 mother-tongue guide's grammar for this chapter: present/habitual time forms (hennaa ammeennaa), taught beside narrating a simple event in order (chapter contents).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Persons and Pronouns and He, She and They — verb agreement and the person endings start there. Recommended, never forced.
- The Writing lesson Telling and Explaining in Writing uses these same present forms to narrate an event in order.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read and say the five happening-now sentences from the guide's key: the dog barking, the man walking, the hen crowing, the goat bleating, the children playing.
2. Spot the present shapes: -aa jira for one doer, -aa jiru for many, ni -atti for the goat's habit-like present.
3. Say what hennaa ammeennaa is: the present/habitual time of the verb, the Grade 7 guide's grammar name for it.
4. Narrate a short happening in order using present forms.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Eyes for watching what is happening around you right now.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks out of the window and tries to say, in Afaan Oromoo, one thing that is happening at this moment (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the five present-continuous sentences from the Grade 4 guide's picture key; the shapes inside them; then the Grade 7 guide's present/habitual time (hennaa ammeennaa) and narrating in it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9930] in Sub lesson 1: a picture strip of the five happening-now scenes — a barking dog, a walking man, a crowing hen, a bleating goat, children playing — each with its sentence under it — the student covers the sentences and says each one from its picture before uncovering.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 1: a voice reading the five happening-now sentences slowly, twice — the student listens, repeats each sentence while picturing its scene, then says which doer is one and which many.
3. **Say it to your companion** — watch the room together and trade happening-now sentences, one each, until you reach ten (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the five animals and children doing their actions as their sentences are said, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — stand at a door or window for one minute and write three sentences about what is happening now (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reaches for the past forms (deeme, dhufte) → those tell what is finished. The present stretches the verb with -aa and stands jira/jiru after it; point at the action still moving and say the sentence while it moves.
- Uses jira for many doers → many takes jiru: Ijoolleen taphachaa jiru. One — jira; many — jiru, the same one/many agreement as everywhere else.
- Mixes the goat's ni -atti shape with -aa jira → both are present shapes in the guide's own key; the ni shape is the creature's way-of-doing shape. Keep both, each where the key puts it.
