# Hands-on — Saying What Is Happening Now

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Window watching
Stand at a window or door with paper ready. For one quiet minute, watch. Write every happening you can catch in a happening-now sentence — a bird flying, a person carrying, rain falling. Read your list aloud; the class counts how many happenings one minute holds.

## Mime the present
One learner mimes an action — walking, playing, eating, drinking are easy. The class says the sentence while the action is still happening: Namichi deemaa jira! The sentence must land while the mime is moving — that is the present.

## Habit or now?
The teacher says happenings: "the sun rises", "the dog is barking at this moment", "we eat at midday", "the hen is crowing now". Learners hold up one finger for a habit, two for happening-now, and say which present shape each would wear.
