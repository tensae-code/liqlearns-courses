# Lesson plan — Cause and Effect

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Cause and Effect
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sentences can carry a reason and its result — sababaafi bu'aa, cause and effect — linked by cause-and-effect words, carried in noun phrases, and sometimes packed inside an idiom.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Joining Sentences — the learner already builds joined sentences where one part leans on the other, which is the cause sentence's own shape. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture

shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Joining Sentences — the leaning join (himoota ceelaa) is the shape cause sentences take. Recommended, never forced.
- Idioms and synonyms are also stocked as vocabulary in the Word lessons; here they are met as grammar, the Grade 7 guide's way.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name sababaa (cause) and bu'aa (effect) in a sentence.
2. Read the guide's four cause-and-effect workings: unclean → disease; enough water and air → a country fit for life; not working hard → problems multiply; enough rain → a good harvest.
3. Use cause-and-effect words and transition words (jechoota ce'umsaa) to link a reason to its result.
4. Say what an idiom (jechamaa) is: a saying whose meaning is its plain meaning as the guide explains it, not the sum of its words.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the effect first and calls it the cause → ask the arrow question: which one makes the other happen? Rain makes harvest; harvest makes no rain.
- Misses the not-causes → two of the guide's four causes are absences: cleanliness NOT kept, work NOT done. An effect follows a missing cause just as surely.
- Reads an idiom word by word → stop and ask for the whole saying's plain meaning, the guide's own way with jechamoota.
