# He, She and They

## Do it first
Watch a boy run across the yard. Now watch a girl run. In English the sentence stays the same: "he ran — she ran". Only the little word changes.

Afaan Oromoo does more. The **verb itself** changes its ending between the boy and the girl — and changes again when the runners are many. This lesson teaches those endings: he did, she did, they did.

## Sub lesson 1 — Masculine and feminine — the first meeting
The Grade 4 guide does not open with a rule. It opens with a **picture of a man and a woman**, and teaches **koornayaa** — the masculine and feminine sort — *alkallattiin*, indirectly, through what the picture shows.

Its model sentences are about the body in the same picture:

- **Ilkaan adiidha** — the teeth are white
- **arrabni diimaadha** — the tongue is red

Afaan Oromoo words and verbs sort into masculine and feminine, and a speaker keeps the sort straight without thinking — the way you keep *isa* (he) and *ishee* (she) straight. The next sub lessons show where the sort bites hardest: in the verb's ending.

## Sub lesson 2 — He did / she did — the verb pairs
Here is the guide's answer key, two columns. Read down the left — the masculine forms, what *he* did. Then down the right — the feminine forms, what *she* did:

- **deeme / deemte** — went
- **nyaate / nyaatte** — ate
- **rafe / rafte** — slept
- **fiige / fiigde** — ran
- **bite / bitte** — bought
- **dhufe / dhufte** — came

See the pattern with your eyes: the feminine form carries an extra **-t-** beat inside it — deem**t**e, nyaa**tt**e, dhuf**t**e. The masculine form ends clean: deem**e**, nyaat**e**, dhuf**e**. When you hear that extra beat, a *she* did it.

## Sub lesson 3 — She, he and they — the three-way sets
Now add the third runner: **they**. The guide's key gives whole three-way sets — she, he, they:

- **baratte / barate / baratan** — learned
- **taphatte / taphate / taphatan** — played
- **dhugde / dhuge / dhugan** — drank
- **qo'atte / qu'ate / qu'atan** — studied
- **cimte / cime / ciman** — was strong

The shapes to hold: **she** ends in **-tte** (or -de, -te with the t-beat inside: dhug**d**e keeps her own shape), **he** ends in **-te / -e**, and **they** ends in **-tan / -an** — the many-ending you met in *isaan dhufan*.

Read the sets across, she–he–they, until the three endings feel like three different footsteps.

## Say it to your companion
Mime and answer. One of you mimes an action — running, eating, drinking, sleeping. The other says the sentence three ways: he did it, she did it, they did it — fiige, fiigde, and the they-form from the sets. Swap after five actions.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the six he/she pairs from Sub lesson 2 in two columns. Cover the right column and say each she-form before you look.
2. Write one three-way set — baratte, barate, baratan — and say what each form means.
3. Write three true sentences: one about a man or boy you know (he), one about a woman or girl (she), one about a group (they), each with its right verb form.
4. Read your three sentences aloud. Can a listener tell he, she and they apart from the verb endings alone?
