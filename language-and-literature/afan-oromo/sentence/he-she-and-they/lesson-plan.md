# Lesson plan — He, She and They

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** He, She and They
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Afaan Oromoo verbs wear the doer's kind: masculine forms end one way (deeme, nyaate), feminine forms carry the extra -t- shape (deemte, nyaatte), and they takes -tan/-an — he did, she did, they did.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Persons and Pronouns — the learner already knows isa, ishee, isaan and that the verb agrees with its person. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Masculine and feminine — the first meeting** — gender (koornayaa) taught indirectly (alkallattiin) from a picture of a man and a woman, with the model sentences "Ilkaan adiidha, arrabni diimaadha" (Grade 4 guide, Ch. 1).
2. **He did / she did — the verb pairs** — gender in verb forms from the Grade 4 guide's answer key: masculine deeme/nyaate/rafe/fiige/bite/dhufe against feminine deemte/nyaatte/rafte/fiigde/bitte/dhufte.
3. **She, he and they — the three-way sets** — third-person verb forms from the Grade 4 guide's answer key: feminine -tte / masculine -te / plural -tan in baratte/barate/baratan, taphatte/taphate/taphatan, dhugde/dhuge/dhugan, qo'atte/qu'ate/qu'atan, cimte/cime/ciman.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Persons and Pronouns — isa (he), ishee (she), isaan (they) and verb agreement start there. Recommended, never forced.
- Masculine and feminine (koornayaa) were first met in the Word lessons through family words (dhiira, dhalaa).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what koornayaa means: the masculine/feminine sort of words and verb forms.
2. Read the six masculine/feminine verb pairs: deeme/deemte, nyaate/nyaatte, rafe/rafte, fiige/fiigde, bite/bitte, dhufe/dhufte.
3. Tell she (-tte), he (-te) and they (-tan) apart in the third-person sets: baratte/barate/baratan, taphatte/taphate/taphatan, dhugde/dhuge/dhugan, qo'atte/qu'ate/qu'atan, cimte/cime/ciman.
4. Build a sentence for he, one for she and one for they with the form that fits.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A boy and a girl volunteer help for the acting in Hands-on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner watches a boy run and a girl run and feels that the Afaan Oromoo sentence about each cannot end the same way (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — gender (koornayaa) met first the guide's indirect way, from a picture; then the he did / she did verb pairs; then the three-way third-person sets — she, he, they (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9954] in Sub lesson 2: a boy running across the frame, then a girl running the same path, the words fiige and fiigde appearing under each runner — the student says fiige as the boy runs and fiigde as the girl runs, hearing the extra beat land with the change of runner.
   - ADD IMAGE [IMAGE 9929] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of the he/she/they chart: the six verb pairs in two columns, then one three-way set — baratte, barate, baratan — in three — the student covers the she column and says each form before uncovering it.
   - ADD AUDIO in Sub lesson 3: a voice reading the five three-way sets across — she, he, they — the student listens and writes which form of each set is the they-form.
3. **Say it to your companion** — act and answer in turns — one runs, eats or drinks in mime, the other says the sentence with the form that fits (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a boy and a girl doing the same actions in turn, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write three sentences about real people — a he, a she, and a they — with the verb forms that fit each (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Hears no difference between deeme and deemte → slow the pair down: deem-e, deem-te. The feminine carries the extra t-beat; clap it: one clap for his, two for hers.
- Gives they the he-form → the many-ending is -tan/-an, the same -an as isaan dhufan in Persons and Pronouns. Point back to that sentence.
- Stumbles on dhugde/dhuge/dhugan → this set's she-form is dhugde, not a -tte shape; the guide's key prints it so. Learn the set whole, as printed.
