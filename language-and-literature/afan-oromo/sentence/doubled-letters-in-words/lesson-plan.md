# Lesson plan — Doubled Letters in Words

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Doubled Letters in Words
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Afaan Oromoo words carry doubled letters (-dd-, -ss-, -qq-, -rr-, -tt-, -bb-, -ff-, -nn-, -yy-, -gg-) and tight letter clusters (-mn-, -lm-, -lb-, -ms-, -lch-, -rg-, -bd-, -rb-, -bs-, -rq-) — and the doubled letter is a sound you must hold, not a spelling decoration.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Letters and Reading lessons — the learner reads Qubee words and knows short and long sounds. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Letters lessons — the Qubee alphabet, digraphs, and short and long vowels. Recommended, never forced.
- The plural work in One and Many meets some of these words again (waggaa, biyya).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read the ten doubled-letter sets with their words: baddaa/adda, keessa, buqqee, jaarraa, bittaa, tabba, barreeffama, hanna, biyya, waggaa.
2. Read the ten cluster sets: gamna, silmii, jilba, barumsa, ilaalcha, marga, abdii, gurbaa, cabse, marqaa.
3. Say why the doubled letter matters: it is held longer in the sound — short, long, soft and strong sounds are practised on these very words.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads doubles short → slow the word down and clap the twin beats. The spelling is the sound's map: two letters written, a held sound spoken.
- Inserts a vowel inside clusters (gamana for gamna) → step the cluster on the floor stones: no stone, no vowel. The tongue steps straight across.
- Thinks doubling is a spelling mistake → biyya with one y is not the word for country. The double is the word's own body.
