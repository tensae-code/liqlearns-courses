# Doubled Letters in Words

## Do it first
Say this word: **biyya** — country. Now say it again, but hold the middle: bi-**yy**-a. Hear the difference? The held sound is not decoration. In Afaan Oromoo a doubled letter is a longer sound, and a longer sound can be the whole difference between one word and another.

The Grade 7 guide drills exactly this: doubled letters inside words, and tight clusters of letters with no vowel between them. This lesson works through both of its answer sets, word by word.

## Sub lesson 1 — Doubled letters
Here is the guide's answer set — the doubled letter first, then its words:

- **-dd-** — baddaa, adda
- **-ss-** — keessa
- **-qq-** — buqqee
- **-rr-** — jaarraa
- **-tt-** — bittaa
- **-bb-** — tabba
- **-ff-** — barreeffama
- **-nn-** — hanna
- **-yy-** — biyya
- **-gg-** — waggaa

[GIF 9955 — the doubled letter held]

Read each word slowly and **hold** the doubled letter: kee-**ss**-a, bi-**yy**-a, wa-**gg**-aa. The double is one sound held twice as long — your tongue or lips stay on it. Write the word without its twin and it is a different word, or no word at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Letter clusters
The second half of the set is the **clusters** — two consonants standing together with no vowel between them:

- **-mn-** — gamna
- **-lm-** — silmii
- **-lb-** — jilba
- **-ms-** — barumsa
- **-lch-** — ilaalcha
- **-rg-** — marga
- **-bd-** — abdii
- **-rb-** — gurbaa
- **-bs-** — cabse
- **-rq-** — marqaa

A cluster is a tight corner in a word: no vowel to rest on, so the tongue must step straight from one consonant to the next — ga-**mn**-a, ji-**lb**-a, ma-**rq**-aa. Read them slowly first, feeling each step, then at full speed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Short, long, soft and strong sounds
The guide practises these words as **sound** work: short and long sounds, soft and strong sounds. That is the point of the whole sub lesson pair:

- A **short** sound is clipped — the single letter.
- A **long** sound is held — the doubled letter, and the long vowels of the Letters lessons.
- **Soft and strong** are the mouth's two pressures on the same letters, and Afaan Oromoo spelling writes what the mouth does.

So when you meet a doubled letter or a cluster in the wild, do not guess: read what is written, hold what is doubled, step through what is clustered. The spelling is the sound's map.

## Say it to your companion
Call and answer. One of you calls a mark — "-qq-!" — and the other answers with its word: "buqqee!" Ten doubled marks, ten cluster marks. Then swap. Any word that comes out with its double unheld gets called again until it lands.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the ten doubled-letter words. Ring each doubled letter.
2. Write the ten cluster words. Underline each cluster.
3. Read your twenty words aloud, holding every double and stepping through every cluster.
4. Write biyya and waggaa three times each, saying them with the hold, every time.
