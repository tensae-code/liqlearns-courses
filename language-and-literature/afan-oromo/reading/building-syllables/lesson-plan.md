# Lesson plan — Building Syllables

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Reading · **Lesson:** Building Syllables
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Reading (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** building a syllable from a consonant and a vowel — and growing up into syllable charts, splitting words into syllables, joining them back, and counting them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Letters lessons: the learner reads letters and knows short from long; now letters join into the pieces words are built from.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does

while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,

shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Joining a consonant and a vowel** — consonant + vowel blocks joining into a syllable, short then long (G3 Ch. 3 answer-key tables).
2. **The syllable charts — short and long** — the book's charts: every consonant with the five vowels, short (Ta Te Ti To Tu…) and long (Taa Tee Tii Too Tuu…), for T, Ts, V, W, X, Y, Z.
3. **Splitting words and joining them** — words split into syllables (mala → ma-la) and syllables joined into words (Na+ma=nama and its set).
4. **Counting syllables** — words counted by their beats: Gamoo 2, Bulbulaa 3, Adurree 3, Baabura 3 — and words sorted by their count.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Vowels — Short and Long; Double Letters. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Build a syllable: consonant + vowel, short and long.
2. Read the book's syllable charts for T, Ts, V, W, X, Y and Z, short row and long row.
3. Split words into syllables: mala → ma-la, see-naa, waa-da, qu-ba, la-ga.
4. Join syllables into words: Na+ma = nama, Ma+na = Mana, Qu+ba = Quba.
5. Count a word's syllables and sort words by their count.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Two hands for clapping the beats.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says ma-la in two beats, then joins it into mala.
2. **The explanation** — syllables built from consonant + vowel; the book's short and long charts; splitting and joining in both directions; counting a word's beats (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9855]: a picture of two blocks — a consonant block C and a vowel block a — with an arrow joining them into one block, Ca — the student builds the syllable aloud as the blocks meet: C… a… Ca.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9900]: a picture of two syllable blocks sliding together across the screen until they touch and read as one syllable, Ca — the student slides two fingers together on the table in time with the blocks.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9856]: a picture of a syllable chart grid: the consonant T with its short row Ta Te Ti To Tu and its long row Taa Tee Tii Too Tuu — the student reads the short row across, then the long row, holding the long sounds.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9901]: a picture of the word mala as two blocks, ma and la, that pull apart and then slide back together into mala — the student claps on each block as it separates, then says the whole word as it joins.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9857]: a picture of the word mala drawn as two joined blocks, ma + la, with the split line between them — the student points to each block and says its beat, then reads the whole word.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9860]: a picture of syllable blocks Na + ma joining into the word nama, with Gamoo (2), Bulbulaa (3), Adurree (3) counted beneath in blocks — the student counts the blocks of each word aloud with claps.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the T chart row, short then long — the student reads along, one beat behind the voice, then alone.
3. **Say it to your companion** — one splits a word into beats; the other joins it back and counts the beats.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, true or false, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — build two chart rows, split three words, join three pairs, and count Gamoo and Bulbulaa.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
