# Building Syllables

## Do it first

Say this word slowly: **ma-la**. Two beats — hear them? Now push the beats together: **mala**. You have just split a word and joined it again. Every hard word in this language opens the same way; this lesson gives you the key.

## Sub lesson 1 — Joining a consonant and a vowel

A **syllable** is one beat of a word — a consonant joined to a vowel, said in one breath-push.

**C + a → Ca.** Two blocks, one sound-piece:

[Picture 9855 — consonant and vowel blocks joining into a syllable]

[GIF 9900 — syllable blocks sliding together: C + a → Ca]

Build them in rows, as the first lessons did: **Ta Te Ti To Tu**, then hold the vowels and the row grows long: **Taa Tee Tii Too Tuu**. Every word in Afaan Oromoo is a row of these pieces standing together — learn to build the pieces and no word can lock you out.

## Sub lesson 2 — The syllable charts — short and long

The book drills syllables in **charts** — a consonant across the top, the five vowels across, short first and long beneath. Its chart consonants are **T, Ts, V, W, X, Y, Z**:

[Picture 9856 — the syllable chart]

Short row for T: **Ta Te Ti To Tu**. Long row: **Taa Tee Tii Too Tuu**.
For Ts: **Tsa Tse Tsi Tso Tsu** — **Tsaa Tsee Tsii Tsoo Tsuu**.
For W: **Wa We Wi Wo Wu** — **Waa Wee Wii Woo Wuu**.

Read a row left to right, each syllable lighting up as you say it. The chart is a ladder: climb it short, climb it long, and your tongue knows every step a word can stand on.

## Sub lesson 3 — Splitting words and joining them

Reading runs both ways. **Split** a word into its beats:

**mala → ma-la** · **na-ma** · **see-naa** · **waa-da** · **qu-ba** · **la-ga**

[GIF 9901 — the word mala splitting into ma + la and joining again]

And **join** beats into a word: **Na+ma = nama** (a person) · **Ha+ra = Hara** · **Ma+na = Mana** (a house) · **La+fa = Lafa** (land) · **Qu+ba = Quba** (a finger) · **Ba+ra = Bara**.

[Picture 9857 — the word mala in two syllable blocks]

Say the pieces, then say them faster, then say the word. Splitting is how you attack a word you have never seen; joining is how it becomes yours.

## Sub lesson 4 — Counting syllables

Clap once for every beat and you can **count a word's syllables**:

- **Gamoo — 2** (Ga-moo)
- **Bulbulaa — 3** (Bul-bu-laa)
- **Adurree — 3** (A-dur-ree)
- **Baabura — 3** (Baa-bu-ra)

[Picture 9860 — syllable blocks joining into nama, with the counting words]

The book also sorts words by their size: three-syllable words like **Finfinnee, Birbirsa, kitaaba**, and four-syllable words like **Konkolaataa, bilisummaa, walabummaa**. Count first, then sort — a long word is only a row of small beats, and now you can prove it.

## Say it to your companion

One of you writes a word from this lesson — mala, nama, Gamoo, Adurree. The other claps its beats, says the count, then splits it in writing. Three words each, then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the short and long chart rows for W.
2. Split these words into syllables: na-ma is done for you — do see-naa, waa-da, qu-ba, la-ga in the same way. Write each split with dashes.
3. Join and write: Ha+ra, La+fa, Ba+ra. Then count the syllables in Finfinnee and Konkolaataa.
