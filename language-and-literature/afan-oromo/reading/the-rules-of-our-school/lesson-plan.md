# Lesson plan — The Rules of Our School

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Reading · **Lesson:** The Rules of Our School
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Reading (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the school rules (Dambii fi Qajeelfamoota Mana Barumsaa) read as a whole text — the one reading the guide prints in full — and understood rule by rule.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Reading Everyday Sentences: a real, whole text, read slowly and answered from itself.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does

while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Everyday Sentences. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read the school-rules text rule by rule.
2. Tell all nine rules in your own words.
3. Say what each rule protects — time, learning, safety, the school's things, other people.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names two school rules they already live by before reading the text.
2. **The explanation** — the nine rules of the printed text, read rule by rule and gathered under what each protects (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9864]: a picture of pupils in uniform arriving on time at a school gate, each carrying a school bag with books — the student points to the on-time arrival and the full bags, and names the two rules they show.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the nine rules slowly, one by one — the student follows each rule with a finger and repeats it quietly.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read the rules aloud in turn; quiz each other: 'what does rule four protect?'
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, true or false, fill in the blank, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write the nine rules in your own English words and tick the ones your school shares.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
