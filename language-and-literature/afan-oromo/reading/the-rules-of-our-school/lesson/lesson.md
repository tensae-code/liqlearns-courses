# The Rules of Our School

## Do it first

Your school has rules you already live by: you know what happens to a learner who is late, and what happens to school things that are broken. Here is a school's rule book, written in Afaan Oromoo — the whole text, printed in the book itself. Read it as law: slowly, rule by rule.

## The rules, one by one

The text is called **Dambii fi Qajeelfamoota Mana Barumsaa** — the Rules and Directions of the School. Here is what it lays down:

1. **Do not be late.**
2. **Wear the uniform, and bring all learning materials** at lesson time.
3. **Do classwork and homework.**
4. **Do not leave the school compound before lessons end.**
5. **Care for school property.**
6. **Keep free of disturbance.**
7. **Do not fight or throw stones.**
8. **Obey teachers and school workers.**
9. **Follow the school's other rules.**

[Picture 9864 — pupils in uniform arriving on time with their school bags]

Read the list slowly. Every rule protects something: the first protects **time**, the second and third protect **learning**, the fourth and seventh protect **safety**, the fifth protects the **school's things**, and the sixth and eighth protect **other people's** learning and work.

## Living the rules

A rule is read when you can say what it asks of *you*, today:

'Do not be late' asks your feet to arrive before the lesson. 'Bring all learning materials' asks your bag to be packed the night before. 'Care for school property' asks your hands to treat the school's books and benches as borrowed treasures.

Read the nine again and beside each one say, quietly: 'this asks me to…' That is the reading finished — a rule understood is a rule half kept.

## Say it to your companion

Read the rules aloud between you, one rule each. Then quiz: one names what is protected — 'safety!' — the other must find the rules that protect it (staying inside the compound; no fighting, no stones).

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the nine rules in your own words, one line each.
2. Write beside each rule what it protects: time, learning, safety, things, or other people.
3. Name one rule your own school has that this list does not — and one rule on this list your school does not have.
