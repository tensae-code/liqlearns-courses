# Words by Their Sounds

## Do it first

Read this pair aloud: **lafa … laafaa**. Same letters, almost — but the second word holds its aa longer, and it means something else (soft, not land). And here is a broken word: **A_a**. What sound is missing? This lesson is about reading words by exactly these things: their sounds, their lengths, their missing pieces.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words with short and long vowels

Read these words whole — no splitting now; your eyes take them in one sweep:

**lama** (two) · **muka** (wood/tree) · **lafa** (land) · **fala** · **jala** · **bofa** (a snake) · **mana** (a house) · **buna** (coffee) · **kuma**

[Picture 9858 — a coffee cup labelled buna and a house labelled mana]

And the longer-built set: **eelee, lukaa, hoolaa, gaala**.

Now the pairs where length alone changes the word — read each pair and lean on the long one:

**laga / laagaa** · **lafa / laafaa** (land / soft) · **mala / maala** · **bona / boonaa** · **gara / gaara**

Say them until your ear files them apart by itself: short word, one beat per vowel; long word, the doubled vowel held.

## Sub lesson 2 — Words by their letter count

Words can also be sorted by **how many letters they carry** — count the letter-shapes, remembering that a double letter like *sh* counts as its two shapes here, as the book's own sort counts them:

**4 letters:** lama · mana · nama · saree
**5 letters:** baruu · banaa · tolaa · gamoo
**6 letters:** sangaa · furdaa · boonaa · tulluu · loomii · birraa

[Picture 9861 — words sorted into letter-count columns]

Counting letters is not a game only: it trains the eye to take a word's true length before reading it, so a six-letter word is never rushed as if it were four.

## Sub lesson 3 — Completing words with the missing sound

A word with a sound missing is a word with a hole. The book's work: look at the hole, say the word, and give it its sound back.

**A_a → Ala** — the missing sound is **l**.
**Baal_ → Baala** — the missing sound is **a**.

[GIF 9902 — the missing letter filling the gap in A_a]

[Picture 9859 — a hand labelled harka and a finger labelled quba]

The answer set stands whole when mended: **Aduu** (the sun) · **Buna** (coffee) · **Harka** (a hand) · **Quba** (a finger) · **Lama** (two). Read each mended word aloud — a word you have mended is a word you own.

## Say it to your companion

Read the five pairs aloud to your companion — they must say 'short, long' after each pair in the right order. Then write a word with a sound blanked out; your companion mends it and reads it whole. Three each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Copy the short word set and read it aloud twice, faster the second time.
2. Sort these into letter-count columns: lama, gamoo, tulluu, banaa, sangaa, nama.
3. Mend and write: A_a, Baal_, and the whole answer set — Aduu, Buna, Harka, Quba, Lama.
