# Reading Our World

## Do it first

Think of everything you know about water — where it comes from, what your home uses it for. Hold those thoughts. Now think: what does a reader do with a page about water? Guess first, gather facts while reading, answer after. That three-part way — before, during, after — is how this lesson reads the whole world.

## Sub lesson 1 — Water — sources, uses and lakes

**Before** you read: say everything you know about water. Nothing is called wrong yet.

**During** the reading, gather its facts. Water is found in **rain**, and in **springs rising from the ground**. It washes clothes, houses, tools and the body; it serves for drinking, recreation, electricity, transport and fish farming.

The great lakes it names: **Hara Danbal** (Lake Ziway), **Xaanaa** (Lake Tana) and **Abijaataa**. Its water creatures and plants: **qurxummii** (fish), **naacha** (crocodile), **roobii** (hippo), **fattee**. And the boats built to travel lakes and great rivers: **bidiruu, doonii, yabala**.

[Picture 9888 — a lake with a boat and a hippo]

**After**: which of water's uses does your own home depend on most? Say it, and say why.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rainbow and its colours

After rain, when the sun comes back, a band of colours can stand in the sky: the rainbow — **sabbata waaqaa**.

[GIF 9903 — the rainbow's colour bands appearing one by one]

[Picture 9862 — a rainbow over fields, its bands in the course's colour words]

Its colours are the colours you are learning in this course — **diimaa** (red), **keelloo** (yellow), **magariisa** (green), **cuquliisa** (blue) among them. The book's work is to read about the rainbow, list its colours, and **draw it yourself** — a reading you answer with a pencil in your hand.

## Sub lesson 3 — What volunteering is

**Tola ooltummaa** — volunteering — is doing good freely: giving your work and your time to help others, with no pay asked.

[Picture 9889 — volunteers helping at a community event]

The reading names **Henerii Duunaa** — Henry Dunant — with this work: a name the whole world ties to helping strangers in their worst days. Before the reading, learners say what they think volunteering is; during it, they check their idea against the text; after it, they name one free good they could do this week — the reading is answered with a life, not only with words.

## Sub lesson 4 — A patriot hero's life — scanning for facts

Some reading is **scanning**: your eyes run over a life story hunting single facts — a place, a year, an age — the way you hunt a name in a list.

[Picture 9890 — a young patriot, with fact cards from his story]

The facts this hero's answer key holds: places in **Wallagga** and **Najjoo**; **Hoolataa Gannatii**; the year **1912** as the book prints it; a **Captain Juuliyoo**; the year **1970**; a British citizen; Italy's invasion of Ethiopia; and a young man **fighting at 18**.

The key never names the hero, and this lesson does not guess his name. Scanning is the skill: find the fact, leave your finger on it, and read only the lines around it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Addiction and its harms

**Araada** — addiction — is the reading's hard subject, and it is read plainly.

[Picture 9891 — a no-smoking, no-drinking sign]

Its words are learned from their context, the book's own way: **amaleeffannaa** — habit-forming, the quality that makes a thing hard to stop; **dhugaatii nama macheessu** — an intoxicating drink, a drink that takes a person's clear mind.

The reading's work: hear it, then **report its main idea aloud** in your own words. A hard reading is mastered the same way as an easy one — before, during, after — and its lesson is protection: what addiction takes is health, money, family trust and years.

## Sub lesson 6 — Reading — work

The last reading of this lesson is about **work** — *hojii* — the reading the Grade 4 year calls *dubbisa hojii*, the work reading.

[Picture 9895 — people at different kinds of work]

Its way is the day's work in words: the reading is read, **its words are named** — each hard word pulled out and made plain — and then **questions are answered** from it. Work in the reading is many-handed: farming, building, teaching, trading, driving — the same jobs this course names in its word lessons. After reading, say which work in the text your own family knows best, and one thing that work gives the people around it.

## Say it to your companion

Take the water reading. Before: each of you guesses two facts it will hold. During: read it aloud between you and tick the guesses that came true. After: each reports one fact the other did not guess. Then swap roles on the hero's story — one scans, one asks for facts.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write water's uses from the reading — at least five — from memory, then check against the lesson.
2. Draw the rainbow and label its bands with the colour words you know.
3. Scan the hero's fact cards: write the two places, the two years, and the age at which he fought.
