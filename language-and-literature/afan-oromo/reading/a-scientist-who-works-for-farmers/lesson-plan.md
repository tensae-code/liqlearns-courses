# Lesson plan — A Scientist Who Works for Farmers

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Reading · **Lesson:** A Scientist Who Works for Farmers
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Reading (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading a biography for its facts — the life of an agricultural scientist, gathered from its answer key: West Shewa and Oolankomii, an agriculture college, a research centre, sorghum, and a Nobel reference — the scientist unnamed, and unnamed here.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the scanning work of Reading Our World: a life read through its answers, fact by fact, with nothing guessed.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Our World — scanning for facts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Gather a biography's facts from its answer key without inventing what the key does not hold.
2. Say what the facts prove: a life joined to farming science — study, research, and sorghum (mishingaa).
3. Answer true/false and multiple-choice questions in the book's own formats, from the facts.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner lists what can truly be known of the scientist from the given facts, and what cannot.
2. **The explanation** — the biography's facts as its answer key holds them; the book's question formats — true/false and multiple choice — answered from the facts (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9892]: a picture of a scientist in a sorghum field holding a sorghum head up to the light, a research building behind — the student names mishingaa (sorghum) and says what a research centre is for.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the fact list slowly — Oolankomii, Jimma, research centre, mishingaa — the student repeats each fact and says what it tells about the life.
3. **Say it to your companion** — one reads a fact; the other says what it proves about the scientist's life.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, true or false, fill in the blank, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write the fact list, then write two true/false statements the facts can judge.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
