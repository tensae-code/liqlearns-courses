# A Scientist Who Works for Farmers

## Do it first

Here is a life with its name missing. The facts: a place called **Oolankomii** in West Shewa; a college of agriculture and technology at **Jimma**; a **research centre**; a crop — **mishingaa**, sorghum; and the word **Nobel**. Whose life is it? The book's answer key never says — so neither do we. What we can do is read a life the way this lesson teaches: facts first, guesses never.

## The facts the key holds

The biography is read at Grade 7 through questions, and its answer key keeps these facts:

- The life is connected with **West Shewa** and **Oolankomii**.
- The scientist studied at **Jimma College of Agriculture and Technology**.
- The work stands at an **agricultural research centre**.
- The crop named is **mishingaa** — sorghum.
- The key carries a **Nobel** reference.

[Picture 9892 — a scientist examining sorghum in a field]

Read the facts as a life in outline: a child of the farming country, schooled in agriculture, given to research — a scientist whose laboratory is a field and whose work is food. The person's name is not stated in the answer key, and it is not guessed here. A fact-only life still teaches: this is what an agricultural scientist's path looks like.

## The book's own questions

The biography is answered with **true/false** and **multiple choice** — the book's own formats for a life read for facts. Both formats run on one rule: the answer must stand on a fact you can point to.

True or false: 'The scientist's college is joined to agriculture and technology.' The fact stands — Jimma College of Agriculture and Technology: **true**.

Multiple choice: 'The crop named in the key is — (a) coffee (b) sorghum (c) teff (d) wheat.' The fact says **mishingaa**: answer (b).

That is the whole craft of this lesson: point to the fact, then answer.

## Say it to your companion

Read the five facts aloud. Your companion must build one sentence of the life from each fact — 'He studied at…' 'His crop was…' — and stop dead at anything the facts do not hold. Then swap: who can resist guessing the name longest?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the fact list — places, college, centre, crop, Nobel — from memory, then check it.
2. Write two true/false statements about the scientist that the facts can judge, and answer them.
3. Write one sentence: what does an agricultural scientist do for farmers?
