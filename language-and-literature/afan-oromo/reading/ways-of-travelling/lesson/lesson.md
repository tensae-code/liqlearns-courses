# Ways of Travelling

## Do it first

Three travellers are waiting: a **boat**, a **train** and an **aeroplane**. Three roads are waiting too: **water**, **land** and **sky**. Match them in your head before you read — then check yourself against the book's own sentences.

## Three travellers, three roads

The book's transport reading holds three sentences. Learn them as three pictures:

- **Dooniin bishaan irra deema.** — The boat travels on water.
- **Baaburri dachii irra deema.** — The train travels on land.
- **Xiyyaarri samii irra balali'a.** — The aeroplane flies in the sky.

Say each sentence with its picture in your mind: **doonii — bishaan** (boat — water), **baabura — dachii** (train — land), **xiyyaara — samii** (aeroplane — sky). The aeroplane's verb is its own: it does not travel, it **flies** — *balali'a*.

## True or false on the road

The reading is answered true or false (*dhugaa / soba*), from the sentences themselves:

'Dooniin samii irra balali'a' — the boat flies in the sky? The sentences give the sky to the aeroplane and the water to the boat: **soba, false**.

'Baaburri dachii irra deema' — the train travels on land: **dhugaa, true**.

Judge every statement by going back to the three sentences. They are short, and they hold everything this reading asks.

## Say it to your companion

One names a traveller — 'xiyyaara!' The other must answer with its whole sentence: 'Xiyyaarri samii irra balali'a.' Then make one false sentence for your companion to catch — swap the roads and see if they notice.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three travelling sentences from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Draw the three travellers, each on its own road, and label them: doonii, baabura, xiyyaara.
3. Judge these: 'Dooniin bishaan irra deema.' 'Xiyyaarri dachii irra deema.' Write dhugaa or soba.
