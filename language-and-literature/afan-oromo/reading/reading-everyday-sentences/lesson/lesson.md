# Reading Everyday Sentences

## Do it first

Read this sentence: **Muuzii ni mi'aawa.** Three words — and it tells you something true about a banana. Sentences are where reading starts to pay you back. In this lesson you read sentences, then a whole reading about family, then answer for what you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sentences about everyday things

Words grow up into sentences. Read these four — every one is about an everyday thing:

- **Muuzii ni mi'aawa.** — A banana is sweet.
- **Konkolaataan saffisaan deema.** — The car goes fast.
- **Dammi fayyaa namaaf gaariidha.** — Honey is good for a person's health.
- **Bishaan dhugaatiif oola.** — Water is used for drinking.

[Picture 9893 — bananas, a car, a honey pot and water]

Read each sentence slowly first, word by word; then read it again as one smooth line, the way you would say it. A sentence is read when its meaning stands up in your mind — banana, sweetness; car, speed; honey, health; water, drinking.

## Sub lesson 2 — Family and friends — the reading

The book prints a reading called **Maatii** — Family. This is what it teaches:

People who come together and live in one house are a **family** — mother, father, brother and sister. Father and mother **share the responsibility** of leading the family. Children **respect** their family and **help** with what they can, outside their study time. And where there is love and caring, the family has happiness.

[Picture 9894 — a family together at home]

Then the reading turns to **friends**: neighbours or schoolmates you talk with, play with, think things over with and share secrets with. A friend, it says, must be of **good character** — choose friends the way you would choose a path.

## Sub lesson 3 — True or false — answering from the reading

The book tests reading with **true or false** — *dhugaa / soba*. The rule: never guess from the air; go back to the reading and let it judge.

[Picture 9896 — a tick card and a cross card]

Statement: 'Father alone leads the family.' The reading says father and mother **share** the leading — so the statement is **soba, false**, and the true sentence is: father and mother share the responsibility.

Statement: 'Children help with what they can outside study time.' The reading says exactly this — **dhugaa, true**.

Read, judge, correct. That is the whole method, and it works on every reading you will ever meet.

## Say it to your companion

Read the four sentences aloud to each other, each saying the meaning after reading. Then invent one true and one false statement about the Family reading; your companion judges each and corrects the false one from the reading.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four everyday sentences and read them aloud at speaking speed.
2. Write two sentences of your own about your family, using abbaa, haadha, obboleessa or obboleettii.
3. Judge these from the reading: 'A friend must be of good character.' 'Children lead the family.' Write dhugaa or soba beside each.
