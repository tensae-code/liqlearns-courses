# Lesson plan — Reading Everyday Sentences

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Reading · **Lesson:** Reading Everyday Sentences
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Reading (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading whole sentences about everyday things — and the Family reading, finished with true-or-false questions answered from the text.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Words by Their Sounds: the learner reads words whole; now words stand in sentences and a whole reading is understood.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Sentences about everyday things** — the answer key's four sentences read and understood word by word (G4 Ch. 10).
2. **Family and friends — the reading** — the Maatii reading printed in the guide, taught as its own summary: what a family is, who leads it, how children stand in it, and what a friend must be.
3. **True or false — answering from the reading** — the book's true/false (dhugaa/soba) work: statements judged against the reading, the false ones corrected from it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Words by Their Sounds. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read the four everyday sentences and say what each tells.
2. Retell the Maatii reading: what a family is, who leads it, what children do, what a friend must be.
3. Answer true-or-false questions from a reading — and correct a false statement from the text.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads 'Muuzii ni mi'aawa' and says what it tells.
2. **The explanation** — four everyday sentences read and understood; the Maatii reading; true/false answered from the text (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9893]: a picture of a strip of four everyday things: bananas, a fast car, a honey pot and a cup of water — the student reads each sentence and points to its picture.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9894]: a picture of a family — mother, father, brother and sister — together at home, a mesob between them — the student names each family member in Afaan Oromoo and says who shares the leading.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9896]: a picture of two cards side by side: a big tick marked dhugaa (true) and a big cross marked soba (false) — the student holds up the right card — in the air with a finger — for each statement read.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the four everyday sentences slowly, then at speaking speed — the student reads along the second time at speaking speed.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read the sentences to each other; make true/false statements about the Family reading for each other.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, true or false, fill in the blank, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — read the four sentences, retell the reading in three sentences, and judge two statements.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
