# Vowels — Short and Long

## Do it first

Say these two syllables out loud: **Ca** … **Caa**. Did the second one take longer in your mouth? Say them again, and clap once for the short one, twice — a longer clap — for the long one. That difference, short voice against long voice, is what this lesson teaches.

## Sub lesson 1 — The five vowels

Letters are of two families. The **consonants** (*dubbifamaa*) are the body letters — b, c, d, f and the rest. The **vowels** (*dubbachiiftuu*) are the voice letters, and there are only five:

**a — e — i — o — u**

No syllable and no word in Afaan Oromoo stands without a vowel inside it. The consonant gives the word its shape; the vowel gives it its voice. Read the five aloud: **a, e, i, o, u**. Short, clear sounds — hold nothing back and stretch nothing yet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Short syllables

Join a consonant to the five vowels, one at a time, and you get five short syllables. With **b**:

**Ba Be Bi Bo Bu**

With **t**: **Ta Te Ti To Tu**. With **m**: **Ma Me Mi Mo Mu**.

[Picture 9851 — the short and long vowel chart]

Short means short: say each syllable once, cleanly, and let it end. **Ba** — done. **Be** — done. A short vowel is a single beat of the voice.

## Sub lesson 3 — Long syllables — the vowel stretches

Now write the vowel twice, and hold the sound twice as long:

**Baa Bee Bii Boo Buu** — **Taa Tee Tii Too Tuu** — **Maa Mee Mii Moo Muu**

Short and long are not decoration in Afaan Oromoo — they change words. **Laga** (a river, with short a) is not **laagaa**; **lafa** (land) is not **laafaa** (soft). Your ear must catch the length, and your pencil must write it: one vowel letter for the short sound, two for the long sound.

## Say it to your companion

One of you says a syllable — short or long, you choose in secret. The other says 'short' or 'long' and writes it. Five each, then swap. Disagree kindly: say the pair Ca / Caa together and listen again.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five vowels in a row, then under each write it doubled: aa ee ii oo uu.
2. Copy the pairs lafa / laafaa and laga / laagaa. Say each pair aloud, holding the long one.
3. Pick three consonants and write their full short row and long row: like Ta Te Ti To Tu, Taa Tee Tii Too Tuu.
