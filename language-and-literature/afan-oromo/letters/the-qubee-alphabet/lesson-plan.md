# Lesson plan — The Qubee Alphabet

**Course:** Afan Oromo · **Title:** Letters · **Lesson:** The Qubee Alphabet
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Letters (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the whole Qubee alphabet — every capital letter and its small letter, in the book's own order.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the learner's own eyes and voice — the first lesson of the course.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The capital letters** — the full alphabet in capitals, in the book's order, read from the answer key's own list.
2. **The small letters** — each capital matched to its small letter; the pairs that change shape most are practised most.
3. **Writing the letters** — letters written stroke by stroke from basic strokes and lines, capitals then small.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — this is the first door of the course. Everything else in Afan Oromo stands on these letters.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read the whole Qubee alphabet in the book's order, capitals and small letters.
2. Match every capital to its small letter.
3. Count the double letters — CH, DH, NY, PH, SH, TS, ZY — and treat each as one letter, one sound.
4. Write letters stroke by stroke so they come out clean.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A wall or table to lean the alphabet chart on, if you print it.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads the alphabet row aloud and points to letters they have seen in the world.
2. **The explanation** — the alphabet in the book's order; capitals matched to small letters; letters written stroke by stroke from basic strokes (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 9850]: a picture of the complete Qubee alphabet chart, each capital beside its small letter — the student reads the row across, then down each pair, saying capital and small together.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 9897]: a picture of the letter B being written stroke by stroke, then the small b — the student traces the letter in the air with a finger while it is written, then writes it on paper.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the alphabet slowly in the book's order, capitals then small letters — the student says each letter just after the voice, then once more alone.
3. **Say it to your companion** — chant the alphabet in order, then quiz each other with single letters.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank, matching, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — read the whole alphabet, write five letters capital and small, and write your own name in Qubee.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread mid-lesson → go back to the Do it first at the top of lesson/lesson.md and do it again with real things in hand; the explanation lands the second time.
- Reads the Afaan Oromoo words but cannot say what they mean → the English gloss beside each word set is the bridge; say word, gloss, word again, in that order, three times.
