# The Qubee Alphabet

## Do it first

Look at this row of signs: **A B C CH D DH E F G H I J K L M N NY O P PH Q R S SH T TS U V W X Y Z ZY**. Say out loud which of them you have seen before — on a shop sign, a bus, a book cover. Then say the whole row slowly, pointing at each sign. You have just read the Qubee alphabet; this lesson makes it yours.

## Sub lesson 1 — The capital letters

Afaan Oromoo is written in **Qubee** — letters built from the Latin letters, with a few two-letter signs that count as letters of their own.

Here is the whole alphabet, in the order the book gives it:

**A B C CH D DH E F G H I J K L M N NY O P PH Q R S SH T TS U V W X Y Z ZY**

Say it slowly, letter by letter. Count them as you go: there are **thirty-five** signs in the row — twenty-eight single letters and seven double letters (CH, DH, NY, PH, SH, TS, ZY) that each make ONE sound and stand as ONE letter.

[Picture 9850 — the Qubee alphabet chart]

Notice the shape of the row: it runs A to Z like the Latin alphabet, but the double letters sit inside it in their own places — CH after C, DH after D, NY after N, PH after P, and SH, TS and ZY near the end. Qubee is not the English alphabet borrowed; it is Afaan Oromoo's own alphabet, fitted to Afaan Oromoo's own sounds.

## Sub lesson 2 — The small letters

Every capital has a small partner, and reading is mostly done in the small letters:

**a b c ch d dh e f g h i j k l m n ny o p ph q r s sh t ts u v w x y z zy**

Most pairs are the same shape, big and small: **B–b, D–d, K–k, S–s**. A few change their look as they shrink — **A–a, G–g, Q–q, R–r** — so give those extra looks.

[Picture 9850 — the Qubee alphabet chart]

A word is written in small letters, and its first letter stands up as a capital when the word starts a sentence or names one special person or place. Read the small row now in the same order as the capitals: a … zy.

## Sub lesson 3 — Writing the letters

Letters are built from basic strokes and lines — a straight line down, a curve, a cross. Watch one letter being made:

[GIF 9897 — the letter B written stroke by stroke]

Take your pencil. For **B**: pull a straight line down, then two round bellies against it, top first. Every letter has its own small path like this: start in the right place, move the way the letter is always moved, and the letter comes out clean and readable every time.

Write the row you know best — your own name's letters — once in capitals and once in small letters. Slow is right: a letter written slowly and truly beats a fast letter nobody can read.

## Say it to your companion

Chant the alphabet together in the book's order. Then point at any letter — your companion says its name and writes its partner (small for capital, capital for small). Ten letters each, then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the alphabet in capitals on one line and in small letters under it, pair by pair.
2. Write your own name twice: all capitals, then the way it is written in a sentence.
3. Find five Qubee letters on things around your home or street and say each one's name.
