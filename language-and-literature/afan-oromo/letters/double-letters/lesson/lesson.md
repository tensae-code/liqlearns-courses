# Double Letters

## Do it first

Read this word out loud: **Shan**. Now read it as if its first sound were two separate letters, s-then-h. It falls apart, doesn't it? **Sh** is one sound in one piece. This lesson teaches all seven of those one-piece sounds.

## Sub lesson 1 — The seven double letters

Some Qubee letters are written with two shapes but sound as ONE letter. They are the **double letters** — *qubee dachaa*:

**ch — dh — ny — ph — sh — ts — zy**

[Picture 9852 — the seven double-letter cards]

Watch two shapes become one sound:

[GIF 9899 — c and h sliding together to make ch]

Never split them when you read. In a word, **ch** is not c-then-h; it is one sound wearing two shapes, exactly as its card shows. Count them again: seven double letters. With the single letters they complete the alphabet you learned first.

## Sub lesson 2 — Double letters in words

A double letter earns its place inside real words. Here is the book's own matching set — digraph to word:

- **Ch — Chaappaa**
- **Dh — Dhadhaa** — butter
- **Ny — Nyaata** — food
- **Ph — Buuphaa**
- **Sh — Shan** — five
- **Ts — Tsahaay**
- **Zy — Televizyiinii**

[Picture 9852 — the seven double-letter cards]

Read each word slowly and feel the double letter working at its front: **Dha-dhaa. Nyaa-ta. Shan.** When a word is missing its double letter, the word breaks — a missing-letter exercise is finished by giving the word its digraph back, whole.

## Say it to your companion

One says a digraph — 'dh!' The other answers with its word — 'Dhadhaa!' Then swap the game: one says the word, the other names the double letter hiding at its front. All seven, both ways.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven double letters in a row, and under each its word.
2. Copy Dhadhaa, Nyaata and Shan. Circle the double letter in each word.
3. Say Shan, then count on your fingers: how many letter-shapes, how many sounds at its start? (Two shapes, one sound.)
