# Lesson plan — Fidel for New Speakers

**Course:** Amharic as a Second Language (አማርኛ እንደ ሁለተኛ ቋንቋ) · **Title:** Fidel · **Lesson:** Fidel for New Speakers
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Fidel for New Speakers (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the fidel — Amharic's letters — learned the way this guide teaches them to new speakers: family by family, in confusable pairs, sung, matched to pictures, and written on the line.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Lesson 1 (the learner can stand, listen and speak in the new language) and on nothing written — the learner may read another Ethiopian script or none; the fidel start here from the voice and the eye.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The first pairs** — በ (bä) with ሰ (sä), then ሸ (shä) with ከ (kä): the Chapter 1 families. Each family is heard, sung (the መዝሙር song), repeated chorally, matched to a picture whose word begins with it (ከ → ከበሮ, the drum), and written keeping the line.
2. **The middle pairs** — ለ (lä) with አ (a); ዘ (zä) with ዠ (zhä); ደ (dä) with ጀ (jä); ጸ (ts'ä) with ጰ (p'ä); ጠ (t'ä) with ጨ (ch'ä) — the families of Chapters 2–6, same cycle.
3. **The last families and the double sounds** — ገ ነ ኘ, ተ ቸ, ቀ ኀ, ፐ የ, ዐ ፀ ወ መ, ሀ ሠ ረ ፈ (Chapters 7–10), and the double-sound labialised fidels (ሏ ሷ ሿ ኳ-type, Chapter 10).
4. **All the fidels in order** — the whole set read and written in its order (Chapter 11's consolidation), the chart the learner now owns end to end.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Greetings and Introductions (Second Language) — the learner has stood and spoken in the new language. Recommended, never forced.
- A pencil and a notebook with lines: this lesson writes from its first part.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read and say each fidel family with its seven orders (ዝርያ/እርባታ), family by family, in the guide's order.
2. Tell apart the confusable pairs the guide pairs up: በ/ሰ, ሸ/ከ, ለ/አ, ዘ/ዠ, ደ/ጀ, ጸ/ጰ, ጠ/ጨ, ተ/ቸ, ቀ/ኀ.
3. Match a fidel to a picture whose word begins with it — ከ to ከበሮ (the drum).
4. Write a family in the notebook keeping the line (መስመር ጠብቀው), with a corrected pencil grip (የእርሳስ አያያዝ).
5. Sing the family song (መዝሙር) that fixes a family's orders.
6. Read and write the double-sound (labialised) fidels, and read the whole fidel set in order from the full chart.

## What you need
A pencil, a notebook with lines, and your voice. Picture cards help for the matching.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up a confusable pair (በ/ሰ, ደ/ጀ…) → that is why the guide teaches them as pairs: put the two families side by side, sing both rows, and match each to its picture. The pair is the medicine, not the problem.
- Writes off the line → back to the guide's rule: write the family keeping the line (መስመር ጠብቀው), slowly; check the pencil grip (የእርሳስ አያያዝ) — fix the grip first, then the letter.
- Reads the orders but cannot say which family a fidel belongs to → cover the row, show one fidel, and let the learner sing the song until the row comes back.
- This lesson's next door: Two-Fidel Words — putting two of these fidels together to make words.
