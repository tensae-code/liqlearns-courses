# Greetings and Introductions (Second Language)

## Do it first

Stand up. Right where you are — stand up tall.

Now say your name out loud, so the whole room can hear it. Just your name. That is all.

You just did the hardest part of this lesson. A new language begins exactly there: one person, standing, saying who they are. Sit down — now we learn how the whole meeting goes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meeting and introducing yourself

This lesson is ሰላምታ መለዋወጥ (selamta melewaweṭ) — exchanging greetings. Amharic is a new language for you. Your first language is another language of Ethiopia, and it is a good language — this lesson adds a new one beside it; it takes nothing away.

Here is how the first meeting goes, in class:

**The teacher goes first.** On the first day, the teacher stands and introduces himself or herself — says who he or she is, so the class can hear how it is done. Nobody asks you to do a thing you have not first heard done.

**Then the learners stand, in turn.** One by one, each learner stands where they are, greets the teacher and the class, and says who they are. Then they sit, and the next one stands.

**And here is a true and important thing: you may feel shy.** Standing up and speaking a new language in front of everyone can make your heart beat fast. The guide for this lesson says it plainly: learners may be afraid when they introduce themselves in the new language — so the teacher's job is to encourage, and the class's job is to listen kindly. Shy is not wrong. Shy is where everybody starts. You speak anyway, a little at a time, and it gets easier.

They are doing what you are learning to do: meeting, greeting, saying who they are. Wave back at them, in your mind. That wave is a greeting too.

When you introduce yourself, two things are enough: your greeting, and your name. Say them standing, say them clearly, and you have done it. The rest — where you learn, where you live — grows lesson by lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Standing and saying who you are

Now the talk goes two ways. This part is ራስን መግለጽ (rasin megilets) — expressing oneself.

Two learners stand at the front: a boy and a girl. They hold a dialogue — a ጭውውት (chiwiwit, a conversation of question and answer). He asks; she answers. She asks; he answers. The class does not talk over them. The class **listens** — listening is your job while others speak, and it is real work: you are learning their answers and getting ready for your turn.

Then every learner in turn stands and expresses himself or herself — says who they are, in front of everyone.

Why standing? Why in front of everyone? Because the aim of this part is ሎታ (lota) — confidence: the spoken courage to present yourself in the new language. Confidence is not something you wait to feel. It is something that grows each time you stand and speak and the world does not end. The class helps it grow: they listen, they are kind, and when you finish, you sit down knowing you did it.

Practise it like this: stand. Picture the boy and the girl. Take the asking side first — ask your partner who they are. Listen to the answer. Then swap: now you answer. Both sides, standing, out loud.

## Say it to your companion

Stand up with your companion. Greet each other. Introduce yourself — your name, clearly. Then run the dialogue: one asks, one answers, three questions, then swap sides. While your companion speaks, do the class's job: listen, quietly and kindly. Then let your companion listen for you.

## Do It Yourself

1. Stand in front of a mirror, or a window, or a tree — greet it, and introduce yourself out loud, three times. Each time, stand a little taller.
2. Teach someone at home how the meeting goes: you go first, like the teacher; then they stand and introduce themselves. Encourage them the way this lesson encourages you.
3. Today, greet three real people in Amharic — and when one of them asks who you are, stand (or sit) tall and tell them.
