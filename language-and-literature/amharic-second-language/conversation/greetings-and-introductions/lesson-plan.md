# Lesson plan — Greetings and Introductions (Second Language)

**Course:** Amharic as a Second Language (አማርኛ እንደ ሁለተኛ ቋንቋ) · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Greetings and Introductions (Second Language)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Greetings and Introductions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** meeting people in Amharic — greeting them, standing up, and saying who you are, in a language that is new to you.
**This lesson's own level:** it is the first lesson of the second-language course. It stands on nothing but the learner's own world: their name, their class, and the people in front of them. The learner's first language is another Ethiopian language; Amharic is the new one, and this lesson is its front door.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Meeting and introducing yourself** — the teacher goes first; then each learner stands in turn and introduces himself or herself to the teacher and the class. Being shy is normal here, and encouragement is part of the method, not an extra.
2. **Standing and saying who you are** — the dialogue (ጭውውት): a boy and a girl stand, ask and answer each other while the class listens; then every learner in turn stands and expresses himself or herself — building the spoken confidence (ሎታ) to present oneself in the new language.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Nothing comes before this lesson — it is the first lesson of the course. Recommended, never forced: just bring your name and your voice.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Greet a teacher and a classmate in Amharic (ሰላምታ መለዋወጥ — exchanging greetings).
2. Stand up in front of others and introduce himself or herself — say who he or she is.
3. Take both sides of a short question-and-answer dialogue (ጭውውት) with a partner.
4. Listen while a classmate speaks, and answer when it is his or her turn.
5. Say why feeling shy in a new language is normal — and speak anyway, the way the class does it together.

## What you need
Nothing but yourself. A partner or a class helps for the dialogue.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands up, right where they are, and says their name out loud to the room — before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — how a meeting goes in the new language: the teacher introduces himself or herself first, then the learners stand in turn; the encouragement of shy learners; then the dialogue — two learners standing, asking and answering, and every learner taking a turn (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Too shy to stand and speak → that is exactly what this lesson is built for: the teacher (or the voice) goes first, the whole class speaks together, and only then does one learner stand. Never skip the standing — make it smaller instead: stand, say just the name, sit.
- Listens but will not answer in the dialogue → let the learner take the asking side first; answering comes second, with a partner who speaks slowly.
