# Lesson plan — Describing My World (Second Language)

**Course:** Amharic as a Second Language (አማርኛ እንደ ሁለተኛ ቋንቋ) · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Describing My World (Second Language)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Describing My World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** naming the learner's own world in the new language — body, family and household things, foods, houses and their rooms, the world outside — and putting heard stories in picture order.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all four lessons before it: the learner greets and introduces (Lesson 1), reads the fidels (Lesson 2), builds and changes words (Lesson 3), and counts and builds pronoun sentences (Lesson 4). The naming itself starts from the learner's own body — the nearest world there is.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order, by theme — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **My body** — naming the parts of the body (የሰውነት ክፍሎች), point and say (Chapter 8; spoken again inside Chapter 11).
2. **My family and the things in our house** — family words (ቤተሰብ): listen and list; household things (የቤት ቁሳቁሶች) named from pictures (Chapter 9).
3. **Foods we eat** — the kinds of food (የምግብ አይነቶች), and the learning materials (የመማሪያ ቁሳቁሶች) named alongside (Chapter 10).
4. **Houses and their rooms** — the types of houses and the parts/rooms of a house; right naming praised, wrong naming corrected with the right term (Chapter 14).
5. **The world around me, and stories in order** — describing the environment (አካባቢን መግለጽ): tree, house, river, animals (Chapter 11); and sequencing pictures to a heard story — arranging them in the order of what was heard (Chapter 9).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Numbers to 100 (Second Language) and the lessons before it. Recommended, never forced — a learner who only needs the naming can start here and point.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Point at the parts of his or her own body and name them in Amharic.
2. Name the people in a family picture and the household things beside them.
3. Name the foods on a table — injera, bread, banana, milk and more.
4. Name the house types (tukul, corrugated-roof house, apartment) and a house's rooms.
5. Describe a scene — tree, house, river, animals, people — in his or her own Amharic sentences.
6. Put four pictures in the order of a story he or she has heard.

## What you need
Your own body; your own home and its things; a table with food if the hour allows. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner touches his or her own head, hand and foot in turn and tries each name in Amharic before any teaching (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — theme by theme: body, family and household, foods, houses, the outside world — each theme picture-first, the learner pointing and naming, praise for right naming and kind correction for wrong, the guide's manner throughout; the lesson closes with the story-order game (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8585]: a flat figure with pointer dots on head, eye, ear, nose, mouth, hand, body, leg and foot — the student points at each dot and says the part's name; then points at the same parts on his or her own body.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice naming the body parts in sequence — the student touches each part on himself or herself as it is named.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8575]: a family standing at home — grandmother, mother, father, girl, boy — with a clay pot, a wooden chair and a lamp on a mat beside them — the student names each person and each household thing.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice naming the family words and the household things — the student listens after answering and repeats the corrected ones.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8576]: a food table — injera rolls in their basket, bread, a glass of milk, eggs, bananas, greens — the student names each food.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8577]: three houses in a row — a round tukul with a thatched roof, a corrugated-roof house, a two-storey apartment — the student names each house type and says which rooms each might hold.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8579]: a village scene — big tree, small house, river, cow, goat, two children on a path — the student describes the scene, thing by thing, then in whole sentences.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8589]: a four-picture story strip — morning at the house, the road, the school, the open book — shown shuffled in practice; the student puts the pictures in the order of the story he or she hears.
   - ADD AUDIO: a short story read aloud, four beats long — the student listens, then orders the strip (Picture 8589) to match it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name your body parts for your companion to point at; describe the scene picture (8579) while your companion points at each thing you name; tell the strip's story (8589) in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the scripts in video-script.md, filmed — the guide names video needs for these chapters: "Parts of my body", "My family", "Foods we eat", "Houses and their rooms" (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, ordering, matching, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md). These are oral/picture questions: the learner points, names and describes.
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — name your own home room by room; describe your own street; retell the strip's story to someone at home (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot find a name → point smaller: not "the body" but one dot, one part. One true name beats five guessed ones.
- Names the thing in his or her first language → that is a bridge, not a failure: say the Amharic name together, kindly, and let the learner say it again — the guide's correct-and-repeat manner.
- Story strip won't settle → retell the story once more, beat by beat, and let the learner move one picture per beat as it is heard.
- Describing the scene stalls after two things → use the pronoun sentences from Lesson 4: point and build — "there is a tree; the cow is eating" — one true sentence per thing.
