# Describing My World (Second Language)

## Do it first

Touch your head. Now your hand. Now your foot.

Three parts of you, touched in three seconds. You know them better than anything in the world — they are yours. This lesson gives them, and everything around them, their Amharic names: your body, your family, your food, your house, and the world outside your door.

## Sub lesson 1 — My body

This part is የሰውነት ክፍሎች (yesewinet kifloch) — the parts of the body.

[Picture 8585 — a figure with pointer dots: head, eye, ear, nose, mouth, hand, body, leg, foot]

The orange dots mark the parts to name. Point at a dot and say the part's name in Amharic — the names ride on the voice, and your job is to say each one before you hear it, then check. Then do the real work: point at the same part **on your own body** and say it again. Head. Eye. Ear. Nose. Mouth. Hand. Body. Leg. Foot.

Point — say — touch — say. Slow first, then faster, until your hand and your mouth move together. These names come back again later in the guide's year, spoken inside the describing chapter — the body is never finished business; it is the first dictionary you carry everywhere.

## Sub lesson 2 — My family and the things in our house

This part is ቤተሰብ (beteseb) — family.

The way of learning it: **listen and list.** You listen to family words, and you say them back and list them. And the things of the house — የቤት ቁሳቁሶች (yebet kusakusoch, household things) — are named from pictures.

[Picture 8575 — a family at home, with a pot, a chair and a lamp on a mat]

Look at the family. Five people: a grandmother, a mother, a father, a girl, a boy. Name each one as you point. Who is the oldest? Who is the youngest? The family words name them all.

Now the mat beside them: a clay **pot**, a wooden **chair**, a **lamp**. Household things, each with its name. Point — say — listen — say again. Your own house holds the same things; tonight you will name the real ones.

## Sub lesson 3 — Foods we eat

This part names የምግብ አይነቶች (yemigib ayinetoch) — the kinds of food — and, beside them in the guide's chapter, የመማሪያ ቁሳቁሶች, the materials of learning: the things on your desk have names too.

[Picture 8576 — a food table: injera, bread, milk, eggs, bananas and greens]

The table is set: **injera** rolls standing in their basket, a loaf of **bread**, a glass of **milk**, **eggs** in a bowl, **bananas**, and green leaves to eat. Point at each food and say its Amharic name. These are the foods the guide's own chapter pictures name — the food of your own table, in the new language.

## Sub lesson 4 — Houses and their rooms

This part is የቤት አይነቶች (yebet ayinetoch) — the types of houses — and the parts of a house: its rooms.

[Picture 8577 — three houses: a tukul, a corrugated-roof house, an apartment]

Three houses stand in a row. A round **tukul** with its thatched roof. A house with a **corrugated iron roof**. A tall **apartment** house, two floors up. Name each one.

Then go inside, in your mind: a house has rooms — a room for sleeping, a room for eating, a door, windows. Name them. And hold the guide's manner from this chapter in both hands: **right naming is praised; wrong naming is corrected with the right term** — you hear the right word, and you say it again, right. That is not failure. That is the lesson working.

## Sub lesson 5 — The world around me, and stories in order

This part is አካባቢን መግለጽ (akababin megilets) — describing the environment, what is around you.

[Picture 8579 — a village scene: tree, house, river, cow, goat, children on a path]

Describe it, thing by thing: a big **tree**. A small **house**. A blue **river**. A **cow** eating. A **goat** grazing. Two **children** on the path. Then lift your eyes and describe it whole, in sentences: the tree stands by the house; the river runs past; the children are walking. One true sentence per thing, then the whole picture in your own words — that is describing.

And the last skill of this lesson is about **order**. Sometimes you hear a story, and afterwards you are given its pictures — shuffled. Your job: **arrange the pictures in the order of what you heard** (ስዕሎችን በቅደም ተከተል ይደረድራሉ). No writing. Your ears lead, your hands follow.

[Picture 8589 — a four-picture story strip: the house in the morning, the road, the school, the open book]

Listen to the story's four beats: in the morning, the child is at home. Then — on the road. Then — at school. Then — the book is open, and reading begins. Now shuffle the strip in your mind and set it right: house, road, school, book. A story you can put in order is a story you understood.

## Sub lesson 6 — Reading for the gist — Chapter 14's reading (teacher guide — evidence · SECOND-LANGUAGE)

Chapter 14 — Types of Houses — carries a reading part beyond its house words, stocked here as labelled evidence in the guide's Chapter 14 lesson.

**Read for the gist.** Learners read a text and state its **main idea (ፍሬ ሀሳብ)** — not every word, the fruit of the text: what it is *about*, in one breath.

**Pictures that answer.** On printed p.155 the check is visual: learners answer **true/false from the pictures** — what the picture shows against what the text said.

**Sentences, formed and finished.** The same part has learners **form sentences and complete sentences** — reading that turns back into making: gist taken in, sentences put out.

## Sub lesson 7 — Time-indicating suffixes — Chapter 14's word work (teacher guide — evidence · SECOND-LANGUAGE)

Chapter 14's second word-level part, stocked here as labelled evidence: **time-indicating suffixes (ጊዜ አመልካች ቅጥያዎች)**.

Learners **add the time-indicating suffixes to words, following the model**, and **write them** — a suffix that tells *when*, attached the model's way, practised in writing. It is this course's first suffix family beyond the -ች gender change and the singular/plural work of the grammar block: suffixes that carry time, as those carried person and number.

## Say it to your companion

Name your body parts while your companion points at them on the figure, then on you. Take Picture 8579: describe the scene while your companion points at each thing as you name it — if you name a thing that is not there, they catch you kindly. Finish by telling the strip's story (Picture 8589) aloud, picture by picture, in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. Stand at your own door. Name five things you can see, in Amharic, touching or pointing at each.
2. Name your own home, room by room, and three things in each room.
3. At the next meal, name every food before you eat.
4. Draw the strip's four pictures from memory — house, road, school, book — in order, and tell the story to someone at home.
