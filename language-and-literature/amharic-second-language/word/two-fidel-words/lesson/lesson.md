# Two-Fidel Words (Second Language)

## Do it first

Here is a word: **መጣ**.

Point at it. How many fidels live in this word? Count them with your finger: መ … ጣ. **Two.** One word, two fidels — and you already know both of them.

This lesson is about words like this: ባለሁለት ፊደል ቃላት — words of two fidels. You will read them, split them, join them, obey them, and change them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two-fidel words

A two-fidel word is built of exactly two fidels from the families you have learned. Reading one is a small journey: first fidel, second fidel, then the whole word in one breath.

መ … ጣ … **መጣ** — "he came."

The way of learning them is the guide's way: **identify** the words built of two fidels; **match** them to the pictures formed from the learned fidels; **read** them aloud; **write** them; and **form** new ones yourself from the fidels you own. A word you have formed yourself is a word you understand from the inside.

Watch the two fidels meet:

[GIF 8596 — መ and ጣ slide together to make መጣ, then split apart]

Apart, they are two sounds. Together, they are a word with a meaning. That meeting — that is reading.

## Sub lesson 2 — Splitting and joining fidels

Now the same motion, done by your own hands. This is ፊደላትን መነጠልና ማጣመር (fidelatin meneṭelina maṭamer) — **splitting fidels apart and joining them together**.

[Picture 8586 — መጣ whole, split into መ + ጣ, and joined again]

Panel one: the whole word, መጣ. Panel two: the word split — መ standing alone, ጣ standing alone. Panel three: joined again, መጣ.

Why learn to split what you just learned to join? Because every longer word you will ever read is fidels joined — and when a new word stops you, splitting it is how you get through it: apart, sound by sound; together, the word. Split and join the word three times now, out loud: መጣ — መ, ጣ — መጣ.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hear it, do it: carrying out commands

Some words are not for reading quietly. They are for **doing**. This part is ትዕዛዝ (ti'izaz) — commands — and ትዕዛዝ መፈጸም: carrying them out.

The rule of this part: **you show you understood by doing.** The teacher says a command — stand. You stand. The teacher says sit — you sit. Open the book — your hands open the book. Nobody asks you to translate the command or explain it. The action itself is the answer — the guide even checks listening this way, by watching the body's response (አካላዊ ምላሽ).

So practise it as a game of quick feet: hear the command, do the command. Stand. Sit. Open the book. Close it. The faster and righter the doing, the deeper the understanding.

## Sub lesson 4 — The word changes: -ች, and one-and-many

Words can change their shape — and when the shape changes, the meaning changes with it.

**The -ች change.** The guide shows it on the blackboard like this:

[Picture 8587 — መጣ becomes መጣች]

**መጣ** — "he came." Now add one fidel at the tail: **-ች** (ch). **መጣች** — "she came." One small tail, and the word turns from a man or boy to a woman or girl — ወደ ሴት, toward the female. Read the pair aloud: መጣ … መጣች. The teacher reads first; a volunteer leads; everyone follows — the guide's own road for this too.

[GIF 8597 — the -ች joins መጣ to make መጣች, then leaves]

**One and many.** Words also change from one to more-than-one — singular and plural. The guide puts singular and plural words on the blackboard and on flash cards; you read after the teacher, and then you change given singular words into their plurals in your notebook.

[Picture 8588 — one book, and many books]

Point: one book. Many books. One — many. In your notebook the same change happens with words: read the singular, write the plural. What exactly grows on the word is something your eye learns word by word, the way it learned the fidel rows: by seeing true pairs, again and again.

## Say it to your companion

Take the word መጣ. Split it aloud for your companion — መ, ጣ — then join it: መጣ. Now give your companion a command and watch them do it; then take a command from them and do it. Finish with the pair: say መጣ, then add the tail and say መጣች, and tell your companion what changed.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write three two-fidel words you can build from the learned fidels. Split each one in writing (fidel — fidel), then write it whole again.
2. Write መጣ. Under it, write መጣች. Read the pair aloud five times, covering and uncovering the ች with your thumb.
3. In your notebook, write two singular words, then turn each into its plural next to it.
4. Play hear-it-do-it with someone at home: five commands each way.
