# Lesson plan — Two-Fidel Words (Second Language)

**Course:** Amharic as a Second Language (አማርኛ እንደ ሁለተኛ ቋንቋ) · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Two-Fidel Words (Second Language)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Two-Fidel Words (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** words built of two fidels — reading them, splitting them apart and joining them back, doing what a word tells you to do, and the first ways a word changes: መጣ → መጣች, and one → many.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Fidel for New Speakers — the learner can read the families and their orders. It needs nothing else; every word here is built from fidels the learner already owns.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Two-fidel words** — words built of exactly two fidels: spot them, match them to their pictures, read them, write them, and form new ones from the learned fidels (Chapter 2).
2. **Splitting and joining fidels** — መነጠልና ማጣመር: take a two-fidel word apart into its two fidels and join it back together (Chapter 6) — the operation that prepares all word-building.
3. **Hear it, do it — carrying out commands (ትዕዛዝ)** — a command is understood by doing it: hear it (or read it), then do it; the action itself is the comprehension check (Chapter 4).
4. **The word changes: -ች and one-and-many** — adding -ች to መጣ ("he came") makes መጣች ("she came"); and singular words become plural (the guide's unheaded grammar block).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Fidel for New Speakers — the families and their orders. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Spot a two-fidel word and read it, fidel by fidel, then whole.
2. Split a two-fidel word into its two fidels and join them back (መነጠልና ማጣመር).
3. Form a new two-fidel word from fidels he or she has learned.
4. Carry out a spoken command — stand, sit, open the book — showing he or she understood by doing (ትዕዛዝ መፈጸም).
5. Change መጣ to መጣች with -ች and say what changed; change a singular word to its plural in the notebook.

## What you need
A pencil and notebook; a book to open for the commands; room to stand and sit.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the two fidels but not the word → slow the join down: say the first fidel longer, slide into the second without a gap — the GIF does exactly this; imitate it.
- Splits a word in the wrong place → two-fidel words split only in one place: between the two fidels. Count the fidels on fingers first.
- Hears a command but freezes → the check is the action, not a translation: demonstrate once, then let the learner copy the action while hearing the command again (the guide's physical-response check).
- Confuses መጣ and መጣች → the -ች is the whole difference: cover it and uncover it; the word grows by one fidel and the meaning turns from "he" to "she".
