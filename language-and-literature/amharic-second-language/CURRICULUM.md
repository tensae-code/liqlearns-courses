# Amharic as a Second Language — CURRICULUM (the path)

**This is the second-language course**: Amharic taught to
children whose first language is another Ethiopian language. It
stands inside the Language and Literature folder as its own
course, beside — and separate from — the first-language Amharic
course (`language-and-literature/amharic/`). Its lessons are
never merged into the first-language course's counts.

**Its one source** is a teacher guide: the Addis Ababa City
Administration Education Bureau's *Amharic as a Second Language,
Teacher Guide, Grade 3* (አማርኛ እንደ ሁለተኛ ቋንቋ, የመምህር መምሪያ,
ሦስተኛ ክፍል), read in full (163 PDF pages; printed page = PDF
page) in the 2026-11-11 Ethiopia Wave 1 read; placements at
`liqlearns-courses-work/ethiopia-2026-11-11/amharic/g03-second-language-teacher-placements.md`.
Because the book is a teacher guide, **every lesson here stands
on teacher-guide evidence only** — its acceptance line reads
"taught in 0 countries … for SECOND-LANGUAGE learners, named
here, counted nowhere", exactly as the placements label it
(TG-EVIDENCE · SECOND-LANGUAGE).

**Levels, not grades:** a learner climbs when they are ready,
not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book
comes from (Grade 3) appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The guide's own shape, kept as method:** chapters of 8–9
periods working a small set of fidel families through one cycle —
listen & speak (ማዳመጥና መናገር) → read & write (ማንበብና መጻፍ) →
vocabulary (ቃላት) — with picture-matching at every step, a
family song (መዝሙር), writing that keeps the line, corrected
pencil grip, and one repeating assessment bank (listening by
physical response, speaking by role-play and retelling, reading
aloud and by flash cards, writing by practice, vocabulary by
picture matching). The course teaches the way the guide teaches.

**Book inconsistency, recorded, not hidden:** the guide's table
of contents lists 12 chapters, but the body also contains an
unheaded grammar/assessment block (printed pp.145–151) and a
Chapter 14 (printed pp.152–161) — no Chapter 13 heading appears
anywhere. The numbering jump is the book's own; lessons cite
chapters and printed pages exactly as they stand.

## The ladder — a short path for a new speaker

1. **Greetings and Introductions (Second Language)**
   (`conversation/greetings-and-introductions`) — meeting,
   introducing yourself, standing and saying who you are
   (Ch.1 + Ch.3's self-expression dialogue); the encouragement
   of shy learners is part of the method.
2. **Fidel for New Speakers** (`fidel/fidel-for-new-speakers`)
   — the families in confusable pairs, sung and matched and
   written on the line; the double-sound fidels; all the
   fidels in order (Ch.1–Ch.11's fidel work).
3. **Two-Fidel Words (Second Language)**
   (`word/two-fidel-words`) — two-fidel words; splitting and
   joining fidels (Ch.6); hear it, do it — carrying out
   commands (Ch.4); the -ች change (መጣ → መጣች) and singular
   and plural (grammar block, pp.145–148).
4. **Numbers to 100 (Second Language)**
   (`word/numbers-to-100-second-language`) — reading and
   writing 0–100 (Ch.7); colours (Ch.12); pronouns
   (ተውላጠ ስሞች) and sentences built with them (Ch.12).
5. **Describing My World (Second Language)**
   (`conversation/describing-my-world`) — body parts (Ch.8);
   family words and household things (Ch.9); foods (Ch.10);
   houses and their rooms (Ch.14); describing the
   environment (Ch.11); sequencing pictures to a heard story
   (Ch.9).

Units as the ladder's steps: Unit 1 — Greetings and
Introductions · Unit 2 — Fidel for New Speakers · Unit 3 —
Two-Fidel Words · Unit 4 — Numbers to 100 · Unit 5 —
Describing My World.
