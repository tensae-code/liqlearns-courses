# Amharic as a Second Language — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

**This is the SECOND-LANGUAGE course** — Amharic for children whose
first language is another Ethiopian language. It is a separate
course from the first-language Amharic course
(`language-and-literature/amharic/`), with its own ladder, its own
lessons and its own counts. Nothing here is merged into the
first-language course's numbers.

It is built from one book: the Addis Ababa City Administration
Education Bureau's *Amharic as a Second Language, Teacher Guide,
Grade 3* (አማርኛ እንደ ሁለተኛ ቋንቋ, የመምህር መምሪያ, ሦስተኛ ክፍል) —
a **teacher guide**, so every lesson stands on teacher-guide
evidence, labelled as such in its sources, and counted nowhere as
pupil-taught.

Built from the bottom, the same way as the other courses: a new
speaker does not start at stories — they start with their own
voice, the letters, and the world they can point at. The path
climbs by the size of the thing:

**Conversation (greetings) → Fidel → Word → Word (numbers) → Conversation (my world)**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead" path points
down this ladder to the bottom).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **Conversation** — speaking first: greetings, introductions
   and standing to say who you are (the guide's Chapters 1 and 3);
   and, at the ladder's top, describing your own world — body,
   family, food, houses, the world outside (Chapters 8–14).
   Conversation brackets the course: a new speaker speaks first
   and describes last.
2. **Fidel** (ፊደል) — the Amharic letters for new speakers:
   the families taught in confusable pairs (በ/ሰ, ሸ/ከ, ለ/አ,
   ዘ/ዠ, ደ/ጀ, ጸ/ጰ, ጠ/ጨ, ተ/ቸ, ቀ/ኀ…), each family sung
   (መዝሙር), matched to a picture whose word begins with it,
   written keeping the line — then the double-sound (labialised)
   fidels, then all the fidels in order.
3. **Word** — two-fidel words, splitting and joining fidels
   (መነጠልና ማጣመር), carrying out commands (ትዕዛዝ), the -ች
   gender change and singular/plural; then numbers to 100,
   colours, and the pronouns (ተውላጠ ስሞች) that start real
   sentences.
