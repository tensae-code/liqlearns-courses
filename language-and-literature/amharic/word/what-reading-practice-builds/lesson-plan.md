# Lesson plan — What Reading Practice Builds

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** What Reading Practice Builds
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what reading teaching is for and what it builds — the Grade 10 guide's three purposes of teaching reading, and the twelve abilities that receptive work grows.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner already reading whole texts; it is a method lesson, built wholly on teacher-guide evidence, and it explains the reading lessons the learner has been doing all through the course.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Why we teach reading — three purposes** — the three purposes of reading teaching: access to information; a foundation for listening, speaking and writing; self-confidence.
2. **The twelve abilities** — the 12 abilities receptive work builds, from predicting to understanding the context of presentation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Study Skills (word/study-skills) — its three stages of reading are the classroom shape this lesson explains the purpose of. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three purposes of teaching reading.
2. List the twelve abilities receptive work builds.
3. Choose a reading purpose and a target ability before reading a real text.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A text you are reading this week, for the practice parts.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tries the lesson's first task before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md), with the picture/GIF slots at their places.
3. **Say it to your companion** — retell each sub lesson's teaching in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

- ADD AUDIO: the lesson read aloud once, slowly — the student follows and marks each sub lesson as it is read (described slot only; no audio file is made in this build).
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student works along with it.

## If the learner is stuck
- The method sounds like rules about teachers, not tools for me → turn each point into a move you make on your next real text; the lesson's hands-on parts do exactly that.
- The lists will not stay in memory → say them in the guide's own order, in chunks, aloud; then write them once from memory and repair from the lesson.
