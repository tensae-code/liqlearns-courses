## Sub lesson 1 — Why we teach reading — three purposes

The Grade 10 guide stops, before its Hospitality chapter's reading work, and says why reading is taught at all. Three purposes, stocked here as labelled evidence.

**First: access to information.** Reading opens the storehouse — a reader can reach what no one is present to tell them.

**Second: a foundation for the other skills.** Reading underlies **listening, speaking and writing**: the words, shapes and ideas a learner meets in texts become the material of everything they later say and write.

**Third: self-confidence.** A learner who can read stands surer in every other part of learning.

## Sub lesson 2 — The twelve abilities

Then the guide lists what the work builds — **twelve abilities** that receptive practice (reading, with listening) grows in a learner. Learn them as a set; they are the reading lesson's true syllabus:

1. **Predicting** what a text will say.
2. **Extracting specific information** — finding the one fact you came for.
3. **Gaining a general understanding** of the whole.
4. **Guessing unfamiliar words from context.**
5. **Understanding text organisation** — how the text is built.
6. **Understanding the author's feeling.**
7. **Grasping the main idea.**
8. **Linking prior knowledge to new knowledge.**
9. **Leaving aside irrelevant detail.**
10. **Understanding indirectly stated messages** — what is meant, not only what is said.
11. **Understanding detailed information.**
12. **Understanding the context of presentation** — where and why this text stands as it does.

Twelve abilities: a reader who grows them all is not practising reading — they *have* reading.
