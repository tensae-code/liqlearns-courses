# Study Skills

## Do it first

You are about to read a text about the civet (ጥርኝ), an Ethiopian
animal. Before you read anything: write down two things you
already KNOW about civets — and two things you WANT to know.
Keep the paper. Sub lesson 1 shows you the chart the books build
from exactly this work.

## Sub lesson 1 — KWL charts

The KWL chart has three columns:

[Picture 8527 — the KWL chart]

- **K — What I know.** Filled BEFORE reading, from what you
  truly already know.
- **W — What I want to know.** Your questions, before reading.
- **L — What I learned.** Filled AFTER reading — and it should
  answer your W questions.

The books fill this chart again and again: for the listening
story of the wolf and the sheep; for the boy who spends his time
on films; for the Awramba community's work culture; for road
traffic; and in full for the civet reading — where what you want
to know (how many cubs? what is the ዝባድ it secretes?) is
answered by the text itself. One book asks the same work in two
columns — "what I want to know / what I learned" — the W and L
of the same chart. A KWL chart turns reading into a hunt: you
read to answer your own questions.

## Sub lesson 2 — Concept webs

A concept web — the books call it a **ቢጋር** (bigar) — holds an
idea and everything that joins it:

[Picture 8528 — webs that hold ideas]

One word or idea sits at the centre. Its related words hang
around it on branches. The books build webs for:

- **ጎረቤት** (neighbour) — and then write five sentences from the web;
- good behaviour against bad behaviour;
- cotton growing into clothes;
- the foods, sorted into their groups;
- the words of the forest and the farm year.

A web is not a list: every branch must JOIN the centre. Building
the web before writing is how the books gather a paragraph's
sentences before a word of the paragraph is written — you met
this in First Paragraphs; here it is named as the tool it is.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cause-and-effect webs

Some webs show not joining, but **causing**. The books' own chain
is the shortest:

**ዝናብ ዘነበ → ጎርፍ ተከሰተ** — the rain fell → the flood came.

The full form is the **cause → problem → solution** web, worked
in the books on a polluted neighbourhood and on a story about a
wasted lake: what caused it, what the problem is, what would
solve it. And the reading on polluted water in Grade 3 is worked
with a cause–effect concept web from the start.

Test every arrow with one question: **did this MAKE that
happen?** If yes, it is a cause link. If it only happened after,
it is a sequence — the order tool from Writing About Stories —
and naming the difference is itself a study skill.

## Sub lesson 4 — The fidel crossword: a game

The Grade 4 guide prints a word game: the **fidel crossword**.
Its rules are printed with it:

1. Only **one fidel per box**.
2. Answers are placed only in the prepared direction — **across
   (ወደ ቀኝ) or down (ወደ ታች)**.
3. **Shaded boxes take no fidel.**
4. Use the **letters already placed as starting points**.

[GIF 8530 — the crossword filling fidel by fidel]

Here is the book's own grid, filled with its printed answers:

[Picture 8529 — the fidel crossword]

**Across (ወደ ቀኝ):** 1. ጀግና · 3. ንብ · 4. አክሱም · 7. ሰር · 8. ሳሙና · 9. ትራስ
**Down (ወደ ታች):** 2. ግንብኛ · 4. አክስት · 5. ኬራር · 6. መቀጨ · 10. ሱሪ

Play it the book's way: cover the filled grid, work from the
empty one, and let the crossings help you — every down answer
lends its fidel to the across answers it crosses. (The grid in
Picture 8529 is drawn for this lesson to hold the book's printed
answers; the book's own printed grid layout is its own — see
sources.md.)

## Say it to your companion

Show your companion the web and the chain you built in this
lesson. Their job: add ONE true branch to your web and ONE true
link to your chain — or show you why an arrow fails the "did
this make that happen?" test. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take the next text you read. Fill a KWL chart for it — K and W before, L after. Did L answer your W?
2. Build a concept web around ጎረቤት (neighbour) or around your own street, and write three sentences from its branches.
3. Build a cause → problem → solution web for one problem near home (a dirty water point, a dangerous crossing).
4. Copy the crossword grid empty, and fill it from the across/down answer lists without looking at the filled picture. Check yourself against Picture 8529.
