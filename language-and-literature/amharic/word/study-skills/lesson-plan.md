# Lesson plan — Study Skills

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Study Skills
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the study tools Ethiopia's books teach inside the language lessons: the KWL chart (what I know / want to know / learned), concept webs (ቢጋር), cause-and-effect webs — and the fidel crossword, the books' own word game.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word and Fidel units — the learner knows words and the fidel well enough to organise them; these tools organise READING itself, and every later unit uses them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture

shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **KWL charts** — before reading: what I know (K) and what I want to know (W); after reading: what I learned (L) — the chart the books fill for wolves, films, civets and work parties.
2. **Concept webs** — the ቢጋር web: one idea at the centre, its words and branches around it — neighbour, behaviour, cotton-to-clothes, foods — a web for holding what a text teaches.
3. **Cause-and-effect webs** — chains and webs that show what causes what: ዝናብ ዘነበ → ጎርፍ ተከሰተ; cause → problem → solution — the books' way of thinking through a text.
4. **The fidel crossword — a game** — the Grade 4 guide's printed game (teacher guide — evidence): one fidel per box, answers across or down, shaded boxes take no fidel, placed letters are starting points — with the book's own grid answers.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Fidel lesson (fidel/the-fidel) for the crossword, and any reading lesson for the charts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Fill a KWL chart for a new text: K and W before reading, L after.
2. Draw a concept web (ቢጋር) around a centre word and use its branches in sentences.
3. Build a cause-and-effect chain of at least three links from a text.
4. Play the fidel crossword by its four rules, and place the book's answers across and down.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A reading text to work on.

## If the learner is stuck
- The K column is filled with guesses presented as knowledge → K holds what you truly already know; guesses and hopes belong in W as questions.
- The web becomes a list → a web must JOIN: every branch touches the centre idea; if a word touches nothing, it belongs to another web.
- The chain has links that don't cause the next → test each arrow by asking "did this MAKE that happen?" If not, it is a sequence, not a cause — still useful, but name it rightly.
- The crossword stalls → use the placed letters as starting points (rule 4) and the across/down crossing: a down answer lends its fidel to the across one.
