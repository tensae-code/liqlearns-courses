# Reading-Lesson Cautions

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Do not spoil the reading

The Grade 10 guide ends its Migration chapter with a list of cautions for the reading lesson — the ways a reading lesson is accidentally ruined. The first three guard the reading itself, and they are stocked here as labelled evidence.

**Do not read aloud in the classroom, and do not make learners read aloud.** A reading lesson where voices perform the text is a listening lesson wearing reading's name — the learners' own eyes must do the work.

**Do not let the reading lesson turn into a vocabulary lesson.** Trying to teach every word eats the reading. Words are worked elsewhere in the method; here, the text is read.

**Do not rely only on a few question types.** True/false and multiple choice alone cannot carry comprehension — include **varied question types** that build the targeted sub-skill.

## Sub lesson 2 — Let the answer live

The remaining cautions guard the answering — how a learner's response is received while comprehension is the point.

**Encourage learners to guess word meanings.** Guessing grows vocabulary knowledge; a guess is work, not failure.

**Do not stop a learner mid-answer to point out grammar faults** when the focus is comprehension. The thought finishes first; grammar has its own lessons.

**Accept even short answers given in speech form.** A true, short answer is an answer.

**Do not force a complete sentence where it is not required.** The sentence shape must never be priced higher than the understanding it carries.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.
