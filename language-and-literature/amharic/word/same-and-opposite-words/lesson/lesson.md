# Words That Go Together

## Do it first

Think of eating. You can say it more than one way, can't you?
Think of going. Think of looking at something. Our language keeps
two ways of saying many things — and it also keeps words that say
the exact opposite of each other. Before anyone explains, try it:
say "big" in two ways. Say the opposite of "black". You already
own these words in your mouth. This lesson puts them into pairs
on the page, so your eyes know them too.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words that mean the same

Some words are partners. They mean the same thing — you can swap
one for the other and the story stays the same.

Read these pairs aloud. The second word of each pair says what
the first word says:

- **በላ** (bela) — **ተመገበ** (temegebe) · both tell about eating: he ate.
- **ሄደ** (hede) — **ተራመደ** (teramede) · both tell about going: he went.
- **አወራ** (awera) — **ተናገረ** (tenagere) · both tell about talking: he spoke.
- **ቆመ** (qome) — **ተነሳ** (tenesa) · he stood — he stood up.
- **መስማት** (mesmat) — **ማዳመጥ** (madamet') · hearing — listening.
- **ማየት** (mayet) — **መመልከት** (memelket') · seeing — looking.
- **ሳቅ** (saq) — **ደስታ** (desta) · laughter — joy.
- **ትልቅ** (tilq) — **ግዙፍ** (gizuf) · big — huge.

Now cover the second word of each pair with your finger. Read the
first word and say its partner from memory. Slide your finger
away and check. Then write the partner word for a word you are
given — that is the real work: a word comes alone, and you supply
the word that means the same.

Why keep two words for one thing? Because a story sounds better
when it can change its clothes. በላ is the everyday word; ተመገበ
is its dressed-up partner. A reader who knows both is never
stopped by either.

## Sub lesson 2 — Words that mean the opposite

Other words face away from each other. One says a thing; the
other says its opposite.

Read these pairs aloud:

- **መጣ** (met'a) — **ሄደ** (hede) · came — went.
- **ጎበዝ** (gobez) — **ሰነፍ** (senef) · clever — lazy.
- **ጥቁር** (t'iqur) — **ነጭ** (nech') · black — white.

[Picture 8211 — black and white circles]

- **ጠባብ** (t'ebab) — **ሰፊ** (sefi) · narrow — wide.
- **ፈጣን** (fet'an) — **ቀርፋፋ** (qerfafa) · fast — slow.

[Picture 8212 — fast and slow runners]

And more pairs that face away:

- **ጣፍጭ** (t'afch') — **መራራ** (merara) · sweet — bitter.
- **ትልቅ** (tilq) — **ትንሽ** (tinsh) · big — small.

[Picture 8210 — big and small trees]

- **ወንድ** (wend) — **ሴት** (set) · man — woman.
- **ቅርብ** (qirb) — **ሩቅ** (ruq) · near — far.
- **ወፍራም** (wefram) — **ቀጭን** (qech'in) · thick — thin.

Did you see it? **ትልቅ** (big) is in both sub lessons. Its
same-word is ግዙፍ (huge) — they shake hands. Its opposite is
ትንሽ (small) — they face away. One word can have both kinds of
partner. When someone asks for ትልቅ's partner, first ask: the
same kind, or the opposite kind?

The work here is matching: a word is given, and you find the word
that faces away from it. Play it until the pairs answer at once.

## Sub lesson 5 — Grade 7: synonyms — the nature chapter's set

Grade 7's nature chapter matches synonyms: ላሂ, ትዕይንት, ግዙፍ, አምባ, ባልጩት, ሀሴት, መቅረዝ, ኩልል, ሰጠ, ፍንትው — each word paired with the word that means the same. አምባ you already know from the amba reading: the flat-topped mountain itself stands in the list, and its partner is the word that names the same high flat land. Match from meaning, not from look — partners rarely resemble each other.

## Sub lesson 6 — Grade 7: antonyms — the Tis Abay chapter's set

The antonyms set follows the Tis Abay reading: ሰውሠራሽ, መስህብ, ግንዛቤ, ጉጉት, መርሐግብር, ቀስተ ደመና — each word given its opposite, then both used in sentences. ቀስተ ደመና (the rainbow, 'bow of the cloud') stood over the falls in the reading; its place in this set is as a word to know deeply — its parts, its picture, its opposite-work — the way the book deepens every reading word.

## Sub lesson 8 — Grade 8: the bat cloze bank, Maru matching and word halves

Grade 8 mixes the forms. The bat chapter (pp.44–45) pairs a cloze word bank with antonym matching: ጦርነት, ዳር, ድል, ሰማይ, መጥፋት, ማብረድ, ቆመ, ፈራ, እርቅ, አገኘ, ምስጋና — war and reconciliation, defeat and victory, standing face to face as the bat and the hen did. Maru Forsa Part 2 (p.54) matches related words (ላባቸው, ወገባቸው, ተስፋ, ብድር, አውራ, ሀሞቱ) and closes a cloze from its bank. The child-labour chapter (pp.95–96) adds word halves to join (ለሥራ→ክፍያ, ጉልበታቸው→ሳይጠነክር, ተመጣጣኝ→ዋጋ), antonyms (መንፈሳቸው, ባዕድ, ማታለል, አቀርቅረው, ብርሃን, ማደር, ጠባብ, ግዳጅ) and its own cloze — three forms in one lesson, as the course's cloze home in Study Skills also records.

## Say it to your companion

Sit facing your companion. One of you says a word from the
lesson. The other answers fast: first with the word that means
the same (if it has one), then with the word that means the
opposite (if it has one). Try: ሄደ — its same-partner is ተራመደ;
its opposite is መጣ. Now swap. Five words each. If your companion
gives the opposite when you asked for the same, stop and fix it
together — that mix-up is the lesson's one trap, and naming it
out loud is how it dies.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight same-meaning pairs from memory. Check them
   against Sub lesson 1 and fix any that slipped.
2. Write the ten opposite pairs from memory. Check them against
   Sub lesson 2.
3. Say one sentence with በላ, then say the same sentence with
   ተመገበ. The story did not change — only its clothes did.
4. Look at three things near you. For each one, say a word and
   its opposite about it: the cup is ትንሽ, the pot is ትልቅ; the
   road is ሰፊ, the path is ጠባብ.
