# Lesson plan — Reading Habits and Speed

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Reading Habits and Speed
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how reading itself works — the eyes travelling along the line and fixing on words, reading speed counted in words per minute, and the habits that make a reader — all from a reading text that teaches reading.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner already reading Amharic text fluently enough to watch their own reading happen; it is a method lesson, built wholly on teacher-guide evidence, and it feeds every reading lesson in the course.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **How reading works — the travelling eye** — the reader's eyes travel along the line of words from left to right, and vision fixes at the moment words are taken in.
2. **Reading speed — words per minute** — reading speed differs from reader to reader, counted in words per minute.
3. **Good reading habits — the note** — the guide's note (ማስታወሻ) on developing good reading habits: people gain the great bulk of their knowledge from reading.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Study Skills (word/study-skills) — its three stages of reading stand near this lesson. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe how the eyes cross a line of text (left to right) and what fixation is.
2. Count a reading speed in words per minute and say why it differs between readers.
3. State the guide's reason for building good reading habits, and keep a daily reading practice.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A text you are reading this week, for the practice parts.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tries the lesson's first task before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md), with the picture/GIF slots at their places.
3. **Say it to your companion** — retell each sub lesson's teaching in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- The method sounds like rules about teachers, not tools for me → turn each point into a move you make on your next real text; the lesson's hands-on parts do exactly that.
- The lists will not stay in memory → say them in the guide's own order, in chunks, aloud; then write them once from memory and repair from the lesson.
