## Sub lesson 1 — How reading works — the travelling eye

The Grade 7 guide does something rare: its Chapter 1 reading text is a text *about reading itself*. What it teaches is stocked here as labelled evidence.

As you read, **your eyes travel along the line of words from left to right**. They do not glide smoothly like a finger on glass — they move, and they **stop**. At the moment your vision **fixes** — settles on the words — the words are taken in. Reading is a chain of these fixations: move, fix and take in, move, fix and take in, from the line's start to its end.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading speed — words per minute

The same text teaches the second fact: **reading speed differs from reader to reader** — and it can be counted, in **words per minute**. One reader crosses a page in many quick fixations and takes in much; another moves slowly and takes in little at a step. Neither reader is broken; they read at different speeds.

Knowing your speed is not a race. It is a measure — the same measure the course's fluency lessons use — and it tells a reader whether the taking-in is growing. Speed is counted so it can be grown, with the understanding growing beside it, never instead of it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Good reading habits — the note

The guide closes the part with a note (ማስታወሻ) **on developing good reading habits**, and its reason is stated plainly: **people gain the great bulk of their knowledge from reading**. Not from being told — from reading.

A habit is a thing done until it does itself. The reader this note wants is one who reads daily, who fixes on words and takes them in, who knows their own speed and grows it — because the knowledge of a whole life is waiting in texts, and reading is the door. This lesson is that note, kept whole: read, and keep reading, on purpose.
