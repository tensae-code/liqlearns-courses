# Dictionary Meaning vs Meaning in the Text

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two layers of one word

Grade 8's first vocabulary lesson works in two layers. Layer one — dictionary definitions: ነጮች, ባህል, ወግ, ሥርዓት, አንጓለለ, ትውፊት, each given its dictionary meaning. Layer two — the SAME kind of words inside the reading: ነጮች, ጀግና, በደምሳሳው, እድላችን, እምብርት, ሳይደክም ማረፍ, each meaning what the text makes it mean. The two layers often agree — and often do not. The dictionary gives the word's home meaning; the text gives its working meaning, here, in this sentence.

## Sub lesson 2 — Heritage words — both layers

The heritage chapter repeats the two-layer work on its own words. Dictionary definitions first: ጥበብ, ፈጠራ, ውቅር, እርካታ, ሀሴት, ስልጣኔ, አለት, ግርምት, ድንቅ, ባልደራስ. Then the contextual layer: underlined words inside sentences from the heritage reading, meaning what those sentences make them mean. Practise the order the book uses: dictionary first (what does the word mean at home?), text second (what is it doing here?). When the two disagree, the text wins for that reading — and the dictionary explains why the writer chose the word.

## Sub lesson 3 — Play words — in the text and out of it

The play chapter sharpens the same skill: some phrases are explained as they stand IN the text — መንቀሳቀሻዬን ቀምተው…, ሽበት የወረረው, ሌላ ውል ፈርሙ, እንደገና ሽማግሌ ላከ — and words are then built into sentences in their similar and opposite meanings (model: ደልደል ያለ → similar ወፈር ያለ, opposite ከሳ ያለ; words: ማጋዝ, ቁልል, ተሸፈነ, ተበጥሯል, አጣደፈኝ, እንዳቀረቀሩ). A phrase's in-text meaning is often a whole situation compressed; outside the text, the same words return to their dictionary homes. Keep the two apart and you will never mistake a play's idiom for a dictionary entry.
