# Hands-on — Word Families and Webs

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## A web on the ground
With chalk on the ground or a stick in dust, draw a big circle
and write ቤተሰብ inside it. Around it, draw eight small
circles joined to the centre by lines. Family members call out
the eight family words — አባት, እናት, ልጅ, ወንድም, እህት, አያት,
አክስት, ሰራተኛ — and a child writes each one in its circle and
says a sentence about their own family with it.

## Handshake or face-away?
Write word pairs on slips of paper — some synonyms, some
antonyms (ቅርብ–ሩቅ, መተማመን–አለመጠራጠር, ዝግ–ክፍት, ወደቀ–ተነሳ,
ወፍራም–ቀጭን…). Two players draw a slip: if the pair means the
same, they shake hands; if opposite, they turn back to back.
Wrong move? The other players say the right family.

## The swap relay
One person writes a short sentence with one word underlined.
The next person swaps the underlined word for its synonym and
reads the whole sentence aloud, standing. Then they write a
new sentence for the next player. Five rounds — the sentence
must stand every time.

## Web of my street
On paper, put one word from your own street or market at the
centre — a shop, a food, a vehicle. Hang at least four words
off it: things, people, actions that belong with it. Read your
web aloud from the centre out.
