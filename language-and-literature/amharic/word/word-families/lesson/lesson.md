# Word Families and Webs

## Do it first

Here is one word: **መጉዳት** — to harm, to hurt.

Your job, before anything is explained: find TWO words for it.

1. One word that means nearly the SAME as መጉዳት.
2. One word that means the OPPOSITE of መጉዳት.

Say both out loud. If you found both, you have already done
the heart of this lesson: one word can have a same-family and
an opposite-family at the same time. (The books' answers are
መበደል for the same, and መጥቀም — to benefit — for the opposite.
You will meet them again below.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Synonyms and antonyms as systems

You already know pairs. Now meet the families whole.

**ተመሳሳይ ፍቺ** — synonyms — are **words carrying the same
idea**: ወረፋ and ተራ travel together; መዘር and መሽከርከር travel
together. Two words, one idea, said two ways.

**ተቃራኒ ፍቺ** — antonyms — are **words carrying mutually
cancelling ideas**: መባ and ሄዶ cancel each other; ወፍራም
(fat, thick) and ቀጭን (thin) cancel each other. Two words
facing away.

Across your school years these pairs grow into sets. In Grade
2 you match opposites like these:

- **ቅርብ ↔ ሩቅ** — near ↔ far
- **ክረምት ↔ በጋ** — the rainy season ↔ the dry season
- **ዝግ ↔ ክፍት** — closed ↔ open

In Grade 3 the sets grow longer — **ወደቀ → ተነሳ** (fell →
rose), **ጩኸት → ፀጥታ** (noise → silence) — and the same-meaning
sets grow too: **መተማመን → አለመጠራጠር** (to trust → not to
doubt). And in Grade 4 you write synonyms inside real stories —
**ማጥለቅለቅ, ስህተት, መዝነብ** and their same-meaning partners,
found in the reading itself.

And the deepest step of the system: **one word can hold both
families at once.** መጉዳት (to harm) has the synonym **መበደል**
and the antonym **መጥቀም** (to benefit). አስተዋይ (wise) has the
synonym **ብልህ** (clever) and the antonym **ሞኝ** (foolish).
When you truly own a word, you own its whole family — the
words that shake its hand and the words that face away from
it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Word webs

A family too big for a list gets drawn as a **web**: the main
word sits in the centre, and its family hangs off it on lines,
like fruit on branches.

The first web you ever meet is your own family. Around
**ቤተሰብ** (family) hang:

**ሰራተኛ** (worker) · **አያት** (grandmother) · **አክስት** (aunt) ·
**አባት** (father) · **እናት** (mother) · **ልጅ** (child) ·
**ወንድም** (brother) · **እህት** (sister)

[Picture 8454 — the ቤተሰብ family word web]

Say each word, then use it in a sentence about your own
family. That is what a web is FOR: not to look at, but to talk
from.

At school the webs keep growing. Your classroom itself is a
web of words — **መጽሐፍ** (book), **ዲስታር** (duster), **ጥቁር
ሰሌዳ** (blackboard), **ቼክ** (chalk), **ተማሪ ወንበር** (pupil's
desk) — the school words that hang around ትምህርት ቤት (school).

And in Grade 4 a web can hold qualities, not just things.
Around **እርዝ** (length, long) hang **ታታሪ** (diligent),
**ትንህ**, **ጠንካራ** (strong), **ብርቱ** — words that feed into
length. You build the web on the board, then use each hanging
word in a sentence of your own — in the guide's lesson, in
sentences about road distances.

[Picture 8453 — the እርዝ word web]

To read a web: start at the centre. Say the centre word. Then
walk out along one line, say the word hanging there, and come
back to the centre before you walk the next line.

## Sub lesson 3 — Swap the word, keep the sentence

The last skill turns the families into a tool. Here is the
method, exactly as the Grade 4 guide runs it:

1. A sentence is written with ONE word underlined.
2. You replace the underlined word with its **synonym** — its
   same-meaning partner.
3. You re-say the WHOLE sentence, standing, with the new word
   inside it.

Watch it happen:

- First: **ቁርሌን በላሁ።** — "I ate qurle (a food)."
- Swap በላሁ (I ate) for its partner ተመገብኩ (I dined, I took
  food).
- Standing: **ቁርሌን ተመገብኩ።**

Nothing else moved. The sentence stands, and it means the
same — said the other way. The drills run in pairs like
ስለሳ–ሹራሪ and ጥቁር–ነጭ: one word out, its partner in, the
sentence re-said whole.

This is why the families matter. A reader who owns the
same-meaning family can say everything twice — and a writer
who can swap never gets stuck on one word.

## Say it to your companion

Grow a web in talk: one of you names a centre word — ቤተሰብ,
ትምህርት ቤት, or እርዝ — and the other hangs words off it, one by
one, until the web has at least five words. Then swap: say a
short sentence, let your companion swap one word for its
synonym and re-say the whole sentence standing.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the ቤተሰብ web on paper from memory: centre word,
   eight hanging words. Check it against Picture 8454.
2. Write መጉዳት in the middle of a page. On the left, write
   its synonym (መበደል); on the right, its antonym (መጥቀም).
   Do the same for አስተዋይ (ብልህ / ሞኝ).
3. Take three sentences you said today at home. In each one,
   swap one word for its synonym and write the sentence again,
   standing.
4. Build one new web of your own: a word from your street or
   your kitchen at the centre, at least four words hanging
   off it.
