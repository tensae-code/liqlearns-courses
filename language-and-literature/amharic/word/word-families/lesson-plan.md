# Lesson plan — Word Families and Webs

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Word Families and Webs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** words live in families —
words that mean the same (ተመሳሳይ ፍቺ, synonyms) and words that
mean the opposite (ተቃራኒ ፍቺ, antonyms) form systems you can
map on a web (ቤተሰብ family web; እርዝ length web), and inside a
sentence you can swap a word for its same-meaning partner and
keep the sentence standing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Words That Go
Together, the first Word lesson, which pairs same and opposite
words one pair at a time; this lesson grows those pairs into
whole systems and webs, following the Grade 2–4 books' own
sets, webs and swap method.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Synonyms and antonyms as systems** — the two families
   defined (ተመሳሳይ ፍቺ / ተቃራኒ ፍቺ) and worked as sets across
   the grades: the antonym sets (ቅርብ↔ሩቅ, ክረምት↔በጋ, ዝግ↔ክፍት;
   ወደቀ→ተነሳ, ጩኸት→ፀጥታ), the synonym sets (መተማመን → አለመጠራጠር;
   ማጥለቅለቅ, ስህተት, መዝነብ), and one word holding BOTH a synonym
   and an antonym (መጉዳት → መበደል / መጥቀም; አስተዋይ: ብልህ / ሞኝ).
2. **Word webs** — a word at the centre, its family hanging off
   it: the ቤተሰብ (family) web of Grade 1 (ሰራተኛ, አያት, አክስት,
   አባት, እናት, ልጅ, ወንድም, እህት), the school-words set of Grade 1,
   and the እርዝ (length) web of Grade 4 (ታታሪ, ትንህ, ጠንካራ, ብርቱ).
3. **Swap the word, keep the sentence** — the Grade 4 guide's
   method: the teacher writes a sentence with one word
   underlined; you replace it with its synonym and re-say the
   sentence, standing: ቁርሌን በላሁ → ቁርሌን ተመገብኩ.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define ተመሳሳይ ፍቺ (synonyms) and ተቃራኒ ፍቺ (antonyms) in
   the books' own words and give a pair of each.
2. Match the placed sets: antonyms (ቅርብ↔ሩቅ, ክረምት↔በጋ, ዝግ↔ክፍት,
   ወደቀ→ተነሳ, ጩኸት→ፀጥታ) and synonyms (መተማመን → አለመጠራጠር).
3. Give one word BOTH a synonym and an antonym (መጉዳት →
   መበደል / መጥቀም).
4. Read a word web: name the centre word and every word hanging
   off it (ቤተሰብ web; እርዝ web), and use a web word in a
   sentence.
5. Swap an underlined word for its synonym inside a sentence
   and re-say the whole sentence standing (በላሁ → ተመገብኩ).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A big sheet or a cleared patch of ground
helps for drawing webs.

2. **The explanation** — the two families as systems with the
   grades' own sets; word webs (family, school words, እርዝ);
   the swap method (lesson/lesson.md).

     — family at the centre, አባት, እናት, ልጅ, ወንድም, እህት, አያት,
     አክስት, ሰራተኛ hanging off it — the student names every

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes synonym and antonym → use the handshake test from Words
  That Go Together: same-meaning words shake hands; opposite
  words face away. Then place the pair in the system.
- Reads a web as a list → a web is not read top to bottom; start
  at the centre, say the centre word, then walk out along each
  line to a hanging word.
- Swaps the word but breaks the sentence → the swap changes ONE
  word only. Everything else in the sentence stays exactly
  where it was, and the sentence must still stand and mean the
  same.
