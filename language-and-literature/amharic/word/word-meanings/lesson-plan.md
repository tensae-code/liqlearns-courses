# Lesson plan — What a Word Means

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** What a Word Means
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a word's meaning is not
fixed inside the word alone — the sentence around it tells you
which meaning is alive (አውዳዊ ፍቺ, meaning from context), one
word can carry several meanings, some words carry an extra
figurative meaning (ፍካሬያዊ ፍቺ), and a word is a word at all
only when its sounds stand in order and give meaning (ቃል) —
and, at the top, the grown pair of names for a word's two
coats: denotative meaning (እማሬያዊ ፍች), which does not depend
on context, and connotative meaning (ፍካሬያዊ ፍች), which does.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word lessons before
it — the learner can read whole words and knows same-meaning and
opposite-meaning pairs (Words That Go Together). The worked
example, ደረሰ, and the figurative table come from Ethiopia's
Grade 4 teacher guide; the meaning-from-context key comes from
the Grade 3 teacher guide; the several-meanings work is placed
in the Grade 3 and Grade 4 student books.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Meaning from the sentence around it** — አውዳዊ ፍቺ: a word's
   exact meaning is known when the word stands with other words;
   the worked example is ደረሰ — "አበል ትምህርትቤት በሰዓቱ ደረሰ"
   (he arrived) against "ደራሲው መጽሐፍ ደረሰ" (the author wrote).
2. **One word, many meanings** — the same word alive in several
   senses: ድምጽ as "voice" and as "vote", and the student books'
   two-sentence words (መሪ, ወጥቼ, አዕምሮ, ማረኩ, ጥማት).
3. **When a word means more — figurative meanings** — ፍካሬያዊ
   ፍቺ: the extra meaning beyond the direct one — ኤርግብ (dove)
   → an innocent person, አንበሳ (lion) → a brave person, and the
   rest of the Grade 4 guide's table.
4. **What makes a word a word** — ቃል: an ordered set of sounds
   that gives meaning — መስጠል IS a word; ስለጠል is NOT, because
   its sounds stand out of order and give no meaning.
5. **Two kinds of meaning: denotative and connotative**
   (teacher guide — evidence) — እማሬያዊ ፍች: the direct,
   dictionary meaning, not depending on context (መሬት = earth /
   land; መብረቅ = flash and thunder); ፍካሬያዊ ፍች: the layered
   meaning, depending on the linguistic and non-linguistic
   context (መብረቅ = powerfully shocking) — and a poem's words
   sorted the same way.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

  the lesson stands on words the learner already speaks.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what አውዳዊ ፍቺ (contextual meaning) is and find a word's
   meaning from the sentence around it.
2. Explain both meanings of ደረሰ and say which sentence carries
   which meaning.
3. Give a second meaning for ድምጽ, መሪ and one more word from
   the student books' sets.
4. Say what ፍካሬያዊ ፍቺ (figurative meaning) is and read the
   figurative table: dove → innocent, lion → brave, and the rest.
5. Say what makes a word a word (ቃል) and judge መስጠል (a word)
   against ስለጠል (not a word).
6. Sort a word's meanings into denotative (እማሬያዊ ፍች) and
   connotative (ፍካሬያዊ ፍች), and say which of the two depends
   on the context — with መሬት and መብረቅ as the worked pair.

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives only one meaning for ደረሰ → read the two sentences
  again slowly and ask: "Who is doing it — a boy going to
  school, or an author with a book?" The people in the sentence
  point at the meaning.
- Thinks a figurative meaning is a mistake → it is not a wrong
  meaning; it is an EXTRA meaning the word carries. A person
  called አንበሳ is not an animal — he is brave like one.
- Calls ስለጠል a word because it "looks like letters" → letters
  alone are not enough: the sounds must stand in order AND give
  meaning. ስለጠል fails both tests; መስጠል passes both.
- Mixes denotative and connotative up → ask one question: does
  this meaning need the sentence and the world around the word?
  If it stands alone in the dictionary, it is denotative
  (እማሬያዊ ፍች); if it leans on context, it is connotative
  (ፍካሬያዊ ፍች). መብረቅ carries both — sort them.
