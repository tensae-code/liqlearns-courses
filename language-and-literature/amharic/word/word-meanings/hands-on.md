# Hands-on — What a Word Means

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The two-picture game
Draw two small pictures on two cards: a boy at a school door,
and a person writing in a big book. A partner reads one of the
ደረሰ sentences aloud. You hold up the card the sentence
belongs to — and say the meaning out loud: "arrived!" or
"wrote!" Five rounds, then swap.

## Meaning detectives
Take a story book or a page someone reads to you. Hunt for one
word you have seen mean two things — ድምጽ, መሪ, ደረሰ or one of
your own. Write the sentence you found it in, and under it,
the meaning it has THERE. Say which words around it gave the
clue.

## Praise cards
Make two praise cards for two people you know. On one, draw a
dove and write ኤርግብ — for someone gentle and innocent. On the
other, draw a lion and write አንበሳ — for someone brave. Give
the cards away and explain the figurative meaning as you hand
each one over.

## Word or not a word?
Write six strings of letters on slips of paper: four real
words, and two shuffled non-words in the style of ስለጠል.
Spread the slips out. A partner judges each slip with the two
tests — sounds in order? gives a meaning? — and sorts them
into WORD and NOT A WORD piles.
