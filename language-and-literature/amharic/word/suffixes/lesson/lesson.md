# Little Pieces on Words — ቅጥያ

## Do it first

Look at this word: **ክፍያውን**.

Somewhere inside it hides a smaller word, and a little piece is riding on its back. The piece is **ውን**. Peel it off, and the word inside stands clear: ክፍያ + ውን.

Pieces like this are called **ቅጥያ**. This lesson teaches you to see them, peel them, and put them on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Suffixes on verbs

A suffix is a little piece joined to the **end** of a word. The books teach you to separate the suffix from its word — here are their own examples:

- **ክፍያውን → ውን** — the suffix is ውን; the word inside is ክፍያ.
- **ቤትህ** — house + ህ: the piece ህ rides on ቤት.
- **ልጅህ** — child + ህ.
- **ጠላች** — the piece ች rides at the end.
- **ዝንጀሮን** — the piece ን rides at the end.

More from the books' review pages: **ይብሉኝ**, **ሰጠችው**, **ሰፈርህ** — each one is a whole verb carrying a little piece that tells you more: who did it, or for whom.

And the same peeling works in the other direction of word-building: strip the pieces off በሬዎች, አህያዎች, አህያቸው and the plain words stand clear; and a verb form can be changed back to its base — ይመነቅራል → መመንቀር, ትበላለች → መብላት. The piece is never the word. Peel first, then read.

## Sub lesson 2 — Prefixes and suffixes

A word can carry pieces at **both** ends. Picture the word cut into three blocks:

**prefix + stem + suffix**

- Pieces **before** the stem: **እንደ-** and **ስለ-**.
- Pieces **after** the stem: **-አች** and **-ምች**.

Now watch one stem collect its family. The stem is **በላ** (ate):

- **እንደበላ**
- **ስለበላ**
- **እስበላ**
- **ሊበላ**
- **ቢበላ**

[GIF 8393 — pieces snapping onto በላ]

See the pieces snap onto በላ one by one. The stem never changes — በላ stays በላ. Each rider in front changes what the whole word says. When you meet a long word, do not fear it: find the stem in the middle, then read the riders.

## Sub lesson 3 — Whose house?

The word **ቤት** (house) takes **person suffixes** — little pieces that say whose house it is:

[Picture 8394 — whose house? the ቤት chart]

- **ቤቴ** — my house. The piece -ኤ says "mine".
- **ቤትህ** — your house, said to a boy or a man. The piece -ህ.
- **ቤትሽ** — your house, said to a girl or a woman. The piece -ሽ.
- **ቤታችን** — our house. The piece -አችን says "ours".

One word, four owners — and the only thing that changes is the little piece at the end. That is the whole lesson of ቅጥያ in one word: the stem carries the thing; the piece carries the person.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: a ቅጥያ is a little piece on a word — before the stem or after it. Peel ውን off ክፍያውን for them. Then say the four house words — ቤቴ, ቤትህ, ቤትሽ, ቤታችን — and let your companion guess whose house each one is.

## Do It Yourself

1. Peel the suffix off each word and write the two pieces: ክፍያውን, ቤትህ, ልጅህ, ዝንጀሮን.
2. Build the በላ family yourself: write እንደበላ, ስለበላ, እስበላ, ሊበላ, ቢበላ from the stem በላ.
3. A friend who is a girl shows you her house. Which word do you say — ቤትህ or ቤትሽ? Write it and say why.
4. Write "our house" and "my house" — ቤታችን, ቤቴ — and underline the piece in each.
5. Take ሰጠችው and ሰፈርህ: find the riding piece in each and write what is left when you peel it off.
