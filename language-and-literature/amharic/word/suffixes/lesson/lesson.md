# Little Pieces on Words — ቅጥያ

## Do it first

Look at this word: **ክፍያውን**.

Somewhere inside it hides a smaller word, and a little piece is riding on its back. The piece is **ውን**. Peel it off, and the word inside stands clear: ክፍያ + ውን.

Pieces like this are called **ቅጥያ**. This lesson teaches you to see them, peel them, and put them on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Suffixes on verbs

A suffix is a little piece joined to the **end** of a word. The books teach you to separate the suffix from its word — here are their own examples:

- **ክፍያውን → ውን** — the suffix is ውን; the word inside is ክፍያ.
- **ቤትህ** — house + ህ: the piece ህ rides on ቤት.
- **ልጅህ** — child + ህ.
- **ጠላች** — the piece ች rides at the end.
- **ዝንጀሮን** — the piece ን rides at the end.

More from the books' review pages: **ይብሉኝ**, **ሰጠችው**, **ሰፈርህ** — each one is a whole verb carrying a little piece that tells you more: who did it, or for whom.

And the same peeling works in the other direction of word-building: strip the pieces off በሬዎች, አህያዎች, አህያቸው and the plain words stand clear; and a verb form can be changed back to its base — ይመነቅራል → መመንቀር, ትበላለች → መብላት. The piece is never the word. Peel first, then read.

## Sub lesson 2 — Prefixes and suffixes

A word can carry pieces at **both** ends. Picture the word cut into three blocks:

**prefix + stem + suffix**

- Pieces **before** the stem: **እንደ-** and **ስለ-**.
- Pieces **after** the stem: **-አች** and **-ምች**.

Now watch one stem collect its family. The stem is **በላ** (ate):

- **እንደበላ**
- **ስለበላ**
- **እስበላ**
- **ሊበላ**
- **ቢበላ**

[GIF 8393 — pieces snapping onto በላ]

See the pieces snap onto በላ one by one. The stem never changes — በላ stays በላ. Each rider in front changes what the whole word says. When you meet a long word, do not fear it: find the stem in the middle, then read the riders.

## Sub lesson 3 — Whose house?

The word **ቤት** (house) takes **person suffixes** — little pieces that say whose house it is:

[Picture 8394 — whose house? the ቤት chart]

- **ቤቴ** — my house. The piece -ኤ says "mine".
- **ቤትህ** — your house, said to a boy or a man. The piece -ህ.
- **ቤትሽ** — your house, said to a girl or a woman. The piece -ሽ.
- **ቤታችን** — our house. The piece -አችን says "ours".

One word, four owners — and the only thing that changes is the little piece at the end. That is the whole lesson of ቅጥያ in one word: the stem carries the thing; the piece carries the person.

## Sub lesson 4 — Suffixes that make adjectives

Some suffixes do a bigger job: they turn a plain word into a **describing word — an adjective (ቅጽል)**. Each family of suffixes has its own flavour.

[Picture 8680 — suffixes that make adjectives]

**-አዊ — of a place, belonging to it.** The teacher's chart of suffix jobs calls this family ወገን (origin):

- **ኢትዮጵያ + -አዊ → ኢትዮጵያዊ**
- **ኤንያ + -አዊ → ኤንያዊ**

You meet the family inside sentences too: **ታሪካዊ ቀን** — a historic day: ታሪካዊ carries -አዊ on ታሪክ.

**-አማ / -ኣም — full of the thing.** The same chart calls this family የኤይነት ገላጭ (showing a quality):

- **ድንጋይ + -አማ → ድንጋይአማ** (full of stones)
- **እሸዋ + -አማ → እሸዋማ**
- **ሆድ + -ኣም → ሆዳም** — built in the word web from ልብ, ፍሬ, ሜዳ, መርዝ, ጤና, ሆድ, with the suffixes -ኧኛ, -ኣም, -ኣማ.
- And chained further: **ዝናብ + -ኣማ → ዝናባማ**, the first step inside ዝናባማነት (ዝናብ + -ኣማ + -ነት) — the same building that gives ዉኃማነት, በረሃማነት, ምርታማነት.

**The -ኛ family — the one with the thing.**

- **ሕመም + -ተኛ → ሕመምተኛ** — the patient: the one who has the illness.
- In sentences: **አመለኛ በቅሎ** — and the web's own suffix **-ኧኛ**.

[GIF 8682 — the suffix chip joins the stem]

Watch the chip land: stem, suffix, whole adjective. The stem says what the thing is; the suffix turns it into a describing word.

## Sub lesson 5 — Verb families from one root — ሥርወቃል

A verb root is not alone. From one root, a whole **family** of verbs grows — this growing is called **ሥርወቃል** (derived verb forms).

Here is the model family, grown on the root **ሰፋ**:

**ሰፋ → አሰፋ, ተሰፋ, ተስፋፋ, አስፋፋ**

The same growing is then worked on ጀመረ, ነገረ, ደረሰ, ለመነ. And the review table grows the full family of **ቆረጠ**:

[Picture 8681 — the ሥርወቃል verb-family tree]

- **ተቆረጠ** — the **ተ-** family: the action done to itself / received (the passive family).
- **አሰቆረጠ** — the **አስ-/አ-** family: the action made to happen (the causative family).
- **አቆራረጠ** and **ተቆራረጠ** — the doubled-middle forms: the action done again and again, or back and forth (the frequentative family).

The table is completed the same way for ዘመረ, ነገረ, ደረበ, ሰረቀ, ጠረገ, ቀመሰ. The rule for reading: however big the grown verb looks, the root stands inside it — find ቆረጠ inside ተቆራረጠ, and you have found the word's home.

## Sub lesson 6 — Prefixes: the pieces that enter before

The riders at the FRONT of a word have had less of the light
than the riders at the back. The higher classes name the whole
system at once: **ቅድመ ገብና ድህረ ገብ ቅጥያዎች** — prefixes and
suffixes.

- A **prefix (ቅድመ ገብ ቅጥያ)** enters **before** the word.
- A **suffix (ድህረ ገብ ቅጥያ)** enters **after** the word.

[Picture 9056 — prefixes enter before, suffixes enter after]

The guide's own keyed answers sort the pieces like this:

- **-አዊ** — a suffix (you built with it in Sub lesson 4:
  ኢትዮጵያ + -አዊ → ኢትዮጵያዊ).
- **-ነት** — a suffix (it stands in the key twice).
- **ኢ-** — a prefix; and in the keyed word it stands
  **together with -አዊ**: one word carrying a piece at each
  end.
- **መ-** — a prefix.
- **-ኦሽ** — a suffix.

The wider system agrees. A word's bound pieces (ጥገኛ ምዕላድ)
are prefixes such as **ስለ-, ከ-, በ-, መ-** and suffixes such as
**-ኦች** — the plural piece you peeled in Sub lesson 1 —
**-ኤ, -ኣቸው, -ዋ**.

[GIF 9057 — ከ- and -ኦች joining ሴት from both sides]

Watch both ends at once: ከ- walks in from the front, -ኦች
from the back, and ሴት stands in the middle: **ከሴቶች**. So
the reading rule of this whole lesson grows to its full
size: **a long word may carry pieces at BOTH ends — peel from
the front AND the back, and the word stands in the middle.**
The work that goes with it is the builder's work: form words
with affixes — take a word, add a prefix, add a suffix, and
read what you have built.


## Sub lesson 7 — Grade 5 and 6: the አድናቂ chain, stems and ending suffixes, and ዘመናቸው split


### Root + suffix chains — the አድናቂ chain, and a paragraph on clean surroundings

A root word plus suffix chains (ቅጥያዎች: -ዎች, -ኡ, -ን, -ነት) builds new words in a table: አድናቂ → አድናቂዎቹን; with ተቆርቋሪ, ዘፈን, ቀጣይ, ኃላፊ worked the same way. Suffix chains stand in sub lessons 1 and 4 of this lesson — the -ኣማ chains there are a different placement of the same book — the አድናቂ chain itself is stocked here. The writing that rides with it: a short paragraph on keeping one's own surroundings clean.

### Stem + ending suffix — እንቅልፋችሁ

Separate a word into ዋና ቃል (root/stem word) and መድረሻ ቅጥያ (ending suffix): እንቅልፍ + -ኣችሁ → እንቅልፋችሁ ("your sleep"); then work አጭሩ, ሕጻናት, ቀናት, ሰነፎች the same way. The person/possessive paradigm stands in sub lesson 3 from guide evidence; this part stocks the Grade 5 student book's own grammar lesson at its own page.

### Word and suffix — ዘመናቸው split

Split words into root and suffix as the Grade 6 book sets it: ዘመናቸው = ዘመን + -ኣቸው; then አካባቢያችን, ልጃቸው, ሰዎች, አገራት. Stem-and-suffix splitting stands in sub lessons 1 and 4 from other books and pages; this part stocks the Grade 6 book's own set at its own page.


## Sub lesson 8 — Split the word: root and suffix — and ten matches (teacher guide — evidence)

This part is teacher-guide evidence, in two halves. First, ten matches: words whose meanings are close (ተመሳሳይ ፍቺ) are matched — printed key, Task 1: 1. መ 2. ረ 3. በ 4. ሐ 5. ቀ 6. ሀ 7. ሸ 8. ሠ 9. ሰ 10. ለ. Then the splitting: words are split into ዋና ቃል (the root word) + ቅጥያ (suffix) — worked answers: ተወዳዳሪ-ምች-ኡ (shown in the guide's splitting as ከ-ተወዳዳሪ-ምች-ኡ), መረጥ-ኡችሁ, ንጽጽና-ኡች-ኡችው-ን, ወጤት-ኡማ-ገተ-ኡች ን (as printed). Split slowly: find the root that can stand alone, then name each piece hung on it and what that piece adds. Suffix splitting is stocked in this lesson from the Grade 4 guide and student books in earlier parts; this part is the Grade 6 guide's own matches and splits, as evidence.
## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: a ቅጥያ is a little piece on a word — before the stem or after it. Peel ውን off ክፍያውን for them. Then say the four house words — ቤቴ, ቤትህ, ቤትሽ, ቤታችን — and let your companion guess whose house each one is. Then build: give your companion the stem ኢትዮጵያ and let them add -አዊ; give them ሆድ and let them add -ኣም. Last, grow a family together: say the root ቆረጠ aloud, then its family — ተቆረጠ, አሰቆረጠ, አቆራረጠ, ተቆራረጠ — one form each, in turn. Then sort the pieces: say ኢ-, -አዊ, መ-, -ነት, -ኦሽ one by one, and let your companion call "prefix — before!" or "suffix — after!" for each one.

## Do It Yourself

1. Peel the suffix off each word and write the two pieces: ክፍያውን, ቤትህ, ልጅህ, ዝንጀሮን.
2. Build the በላ family yourself: write እንደበላ, ስለበላ, እስበላ, ሊበላ, ቢበላ from the stem በላ.
3. A friend who is a girl shows you her house. Which word do you say — ቤትህ or ቤትሽ? Write it and say why.
4. Write "our house" and "my house" — ቤታችን, ቤቴ — and underline the piece in each.
5. Take ሰጠችው and ሰፈርህ: find the riding piece in each and write what is left when you peel it off.
6. Build the adjective: ድንጋይ + -አማ, ኢትዮጵያ + -አዊ, ሆድ + -ኣም, ሕመም + -ተኛ. Write each whole word and name its suffix family.
7. Split ዝናባማነት into its three pieces, on the model ዝናብ + -ኣማ + -ነት.
8. Grow the family of ቆረጠ from memory — ተ-, አስ-/አ-, and the two doubled-middle forms — then check against Picture 8681.
9. Sort these pieces into prefixes and suffixes: ኢ-, -አዊ, መ-, -ነት, -ኦሽ, ስለ-, -ኦች. Then build one word with a piece at each end, on the model ከ- + ሴት + -ኦች → ከሴቶች, and peel it back into its three pieces.
