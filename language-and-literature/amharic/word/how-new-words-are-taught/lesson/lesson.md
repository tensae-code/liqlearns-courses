# How New Words Are Taught

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — First collect, then work five ways

The Grade 10 guide's vocabulary method begins before any teaching: **learners first collect the new words from the chapter's texts** — the words are found in real reading, not handed down in a list. Then the guide works them **five ways**, stocked here as labelled evidence:

1. **Dictionaries** — look the word up; take its stated meaning.
2. **Context with examples** — explain the word in its context, with examples around it.
3. **Networks** — set the word among its relations (ዝምድና) — the next sub lesson opens this out.
4. **Active use (ማውጣጣት)** — the word is *used*, spoken and written by the learner, until it is theirs.
5. **Guessing from context** — work the meaning out from the passage it stands in.

No single way is the method. The five together are.

## Sub lesson 2 — Words in networks — ዝምድና

The third way deserves its own space, because it changes what a word *is* to a learner: not a lone item, but a knot in a net.

The guide has learners look at words **spreading in networks (ዝምድና)** — relations of two kinds:

- **Meaning relations** — words linked by the message they carry: words that belong to one idea lean on each other, and knowing one holds the others up.
- **Context relations** — words that **line up together in a passage about one thing**: a text on farming gathers its own company of words, and meeting them together teaches them together.

[Picture 31503 — a word in its network: meaning relations and context relations]

A word learned in its network is learned with its neighbours' hands holding it — which is why it stays.
