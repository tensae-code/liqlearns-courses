# Using the Dictionary

## Do it first

Here are four words: **ሰገነት · መደዳ · ደርብ · ክፍለዘመን**.

Say honestly, word by word: which of these can you explain to
a friend right now, and which can you not?

Do not guess. A word you cannot explain is not a failure — it
is a signpost. It points at one tool, a book whose whole job
is word meanings. This lesson hands you that tool.

## Sub lesson 1 — The dictionary as a tool

The tool is the **መዝገበቃላት** — the dictionary: the book where
words stand with their meanings, waiting for you to visit
them.

In Grade 4, dictionary work is one of the NEW skills of the
year — the book names it among the things this grade adds:
multiple-choice questions, true-and-false checks, summary
writing, news writing, debates — and መዝገበቃላት, dictionary
work. From this year on, when a word stops you, you are not
expected to sit stuck. You are expected to look it up.

That is what makes the dictionary different from every other
book you own. A story book you read from its first page to
its last. The dictionary you VISIT: you come for one word,
you take its meaning, and you go back to what you were
reading. It is a tool, like a spade or a key — it is judged
by the work it does in your hand.

## Sub lesson 2 — Finding a word and writing its meaning

The Grade 4 book's own task is short and exact: **look up the
word, and WRITE its definition.**

Here is the book's own visiting set — the words of the
Ayiteyef hall reading, the great hall where rich and poor
shared equally:

- **ሰገነት** — balcony
- **መደዳ** — row
- **ደርብ** — storey (a floor of a building)
- **ክፍለዘመን** — century (a hundred years)

The finding runs the way the dictionary itself is built: its
words stand in fidel order, the order you learned with the
ፊደል — ሀ, ለ, ሐ, መ… — so you walk the pages in that order,
family by family, until your word stands in front of you.
Then the second half of the task, the half learners skip: do
not only read the meaning. **Write it.** A meaning you have
written is a meaning you keep; a meaning you only glanced at
belongs to the dictionary, not to you.

Try the four now: cover the meanings above, say each word,
write what you think it means — then uncover and check. That
cover–write–check round IS the dictionary habit.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dictionary words inside real readings

The book never sends you to the dictionary for nothing. Every
look-up serves a reading.

In the listening about the **Abune Aron monastery** — its
treasures and its miraculous window — the hard words are
handed to the dictionary first, so the listening can flow:

- **ገዳም** — monastery
- **ብራና** — parchment (the old writing skin)
- **አምድ** — pillar
- **ተዓምር** — miracle
- **ማህደር** — (the book's fifth word of the set, looked up the
  same way)

And inside the river reading — the village that learned to
lift its river's water and now harvests twice and three times
a year — the dictionary words are **ስጦታ** (a gift), **ትጋት**
(diligence), **ማሳመን** and **መዘከር**: words the story itself
turns on. The expert's ትጋት is why the village honours her;
the ስጦታ of ten thousand birr is the story's thank-you. Look
them up, and the reading opens.

So the habit of this lesson, whole: **read; stop at the word
you do not know; visit the dictionary; write the meaning;
read on.** Five steps, and no word can bar your road for
long.

## Say it to your companion

Name the tool and its job: what is መዝገበቃላት, and when do
you visit it? Then quiz each other on the lesson's words —
ገዳም, ብራና, አምድ, ተዓምር, ሰገነት, መደዳ, ደርብ, ክፍለዘመን, ስጦታ,
ትጋት. Every word a companion cannot explain goes on a
look-up list you both keep.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four visiting words (ሰገነት, መደዳ, ደርብ,
   ክፍለዘመን) with their meanings from memory. Check yourself
   against Sub lesson 2.
2. In the next reading you do — any book, any page — mark
   every word you cannot explain. Look each one up and write
   its meaning in a small word book of your own.
3. Teach the habit: show a younger child or a friend the five
   steps — read, stop, visit, write, read on — using one real
   word from a page you open together.
