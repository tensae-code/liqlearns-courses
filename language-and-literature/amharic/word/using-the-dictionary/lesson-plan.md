# Lesson plan — Using the Dictionary

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Using the Dictionary
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Word (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the dictionary
(መዝገበቃላት) is the Word lessons' own tool — when a word's
meaning is not known, you look it up and write its definition,
the way the Grade 4 book sends you to it again and again: for
the monastery words (ገዳም, ብራና, አምድ, ተዓምር), for the visiting
words (ሰገነት, መደዳ, ደርብ, ክፍለዘመን), and inside the river
reading (ስጦታ, ትጋት, ማሳመን, መዘከር).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word lessons
before it — the learner reads whole words and knows that a
word's meaning can change inside a sentence (What a Word
Means); the dictionary gives the word's standing meaning, the
one you check the sentence against. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The dictionary as a tool** — መዝገበቃላት: the book of word
   meanings; Grade 4 is where dictionary work arrives in the
   course — the book names it among the new skills of the year
   and sends learners to it inside real readings.
2. **Finding a word and writing its meaning** — the book's own
   task shape: look up and WRITE the definitions — ሰገነት
   (balcony), መደዳ (row), ደርብ (storey), ክፍለዘመን (century).
3. **Dictionary words inside real readings** — the monastery
   listening's five words (ገዳም monastery, ብራና parchment, አምድ
   pillar, ተዓምር miracle, ማህደር) and the river reading's four
   (ስጦታ, ትጋት, ማሳመን, መዘከር): the dictionary serves the
   reading, not the other way round.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What a Word Means — the dictionary gives
  a word's standing meaning; that lesson teaches the meaning
  the sentence gives it. Recommended, never forced.
- The Fidel lessons: a learner who knows the fidel order can
  walk through the dictionary's pages in that same order.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what መዝገበቃላት (the dictionary) is and what it is for.
2. Look up a word and write its definition, on the book's own
   task: ሰገነት, መደዳ, ደርብ, ክፍለዘመን.
3. Give the dictionary meanings of the monastery words: ገዳም,
   ብራና, አምድ, ተዓምር.
4. Use the dictionary inside a reading: when a word in a text
   is not known (ስጦታ, ትጋት, ማሳመን, መዘከር), look it up and
   read on with the meaning in hand.

## What you need
A dictionary (መዝገበቃላት) if one is at hand — school, library
or home copy. Paper and a pencil.

   which ones they cannot yet explain; the lesson then hands
   them the tool that answers (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what the dictionary is and when the
   book sends you to it; the look-up-and-write task; the

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the dictionary is a story book → it is a TOOL book:
  you do not read it front to back; you visit it for one word
  and leave with that word's meaning.
- Finds the word but copies without understanding → the book's
  task is look up AND write — then close the dictionary and
  say the meaning in your own words before you read on.
- No dictionary at hand → the habit still stands: mark the
  unknown word, ask a person or check at the library later —
  the Grade 3 books teach tree names "by dictionary or asking
  a person"; both roads are honourable, the dictionary is the
  one that is always awake.
