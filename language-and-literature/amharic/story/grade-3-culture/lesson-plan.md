# Lesson plan — Grade 3 Culture

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Grade 3 Culture
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Culture is what a people hands down: games in a circle, riddles at dusk, the Ashendye grass at dancing girls' waists, and the eskista's shoulders telling a story no words can.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 3 learner's fluent reading and listening: the texts are read twice the fluent way, listened to from the teacher's voice, and answered from what was read and heard.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order:

1. **Listening — the cultural show, and bamboo work** — Listen to **የባህል መታያ** — The Cultural Show.
2. **Reading culture — children's games, Ashendye, the Queen of Eskista** — Read **የልጆች ጨዋታ** — Children's Games — as oral heritage, ቃላዊ ሀብት: games passed mouth to mouth across generations, each with its rules.
3. **Writing games and culture** — Write your favourite children's game — then demonstrate how it is played.
4. **Playing and speaking culture** — Play **እንካስላንትያ** in small groups, taking turns — everyone plays, everyone gets a turn; a game is a culture lesson your feet learn.
5. **Culture games, word-building and grammar in the reading** — Play the vocabulary BINGO tournament on a mixed word grid — the chapter's words all shuffled together, groups competing to the final.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Stories and Listening Stories — this lesson's Grade 3 texts are read and heard the same way, on the chapter theme of culture. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Retell this chapter's listening and reading texts in sequence, in their own words.
2. Use this chapter's word sets in sentences and in role play.
3. Do this chapter's writing tasks — sentences, paragraphs, webs and tables — with correct punctuation.
4. Do this chapter's grammar review work (suffixes, plurals and word-kinds as the chapter names them).

## If the learner is stuck
- A story will not retell → go back to its sequence: first, then, last — three steps carry every tale in this lesson.
- A word set will not stick → say it in a pair, use it in a sentence, then write it: ear, mouth, hand.
- A writing task feels big → the web or table first, sentences after: the book's own order in every chapter.
