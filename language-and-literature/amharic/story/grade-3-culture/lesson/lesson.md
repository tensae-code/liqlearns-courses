# Grade 3 Culture

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ባህል** — culture. Culture is what a people hands down: games in a circle, riddles at dusk, the Ashendye grass at dancing girls' waists, and the eskista's shoulders telling a story no words can. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about culture in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — the cultural show, and bamboo work

Listen to **የባህል መታያ** — The Cultural Show. Its sentence stays with you: forgetting culture is forgetting identity — who you are is carried in what your people do and sing and make.

Listen to **የቀርቀሃ ሥራ** — Bamboo Work. Bamboo work is linked with culture. Ask as you listen: should women take part? And why is bamboo not used on the very day it is cut?

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading culture — children's games, Ashendye, the Queen of Eskista

Read **የልጆች ጨዋታ** — Children's Games — as oral heritage, ቃላዊ ሀብት: games passed mouth to mouth across generations, each with its rules. The worked example is the call-and-response circle game **መሐረቤን ያያችሁ** — have you seen my handkerchief?

Read **አሸንድዬ** — the girls' festival of the Amhara region, also called ሻደይ and ሶለል, in Lasta, Raya, Qobo and Waghimra. Girls go to the fields, cut አሸንዳ/እንግጫ grass, tie it round their waists, and dance house to house singing, receiving hospitality as they go.

Read **የእስክስታዋ ንግሥት** — Eniye Takele, Queen of Eskista. Born in Koladba, Gondar, she danced from childhood at every kebero drum. She danced with the Fasiledes troupe, then the Hizb le-Hizb kinet; she had a cultural restaurant in America; now she runs a cultural evening house in Gondar, training young dancers.

## Sub lesson 3 — Writing games and culture

Write your favourite children's game — then demonstrate how it is played. The writing must be clear enough that a reader could play from your words.

Describe a well-known local game completely: who plays, when, where, and how it ends. Four questions, and the description is complete.

Gather information and write a paragraph on traditional house building — the materials, the order of the work, the people who help.

## Sub lesson 4 — Playing and speaking culture

Play **እንካስላንትያ** in small groups, taking turns — everyone plays, everyone gets a turn; a game is a culture lesson your feet learn.

Ask your parents, write five እንካስላንትያ, and compete in pairs with them. Home's games come to school in your notebook.

Put the Ashendye celebration steps in their right order — field, grass, waist, houses, song. Sequence is reading with your feet in it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Culture games, word-building and grammar in the reading

Play the vocabulary BINGO tournament on a mixed word grid — the chapter's words all shuffled together, groups competing to the final.

Build doer-nouns — the person who does the work: **ውዝዋዜ → ተወዛዋዥ**, and the pattern in **ሸማ ሠሪ** weaver, **ወርቅ ሠሪ** goldsmith, **ጥልፍ ሠሪ** embroiderer. The doer wears the doing in their name.

In the eskista reading, pick out the pronouns — መስተዋድድ — and the adjectives. Grammar lives inside real reading, not only in lists.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **ባህል** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
