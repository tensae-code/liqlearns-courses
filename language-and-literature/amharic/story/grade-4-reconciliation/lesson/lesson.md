# Grade 4 Making Peace

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ድርድር** — negotiation — making peace. The reconciliation chapter: elders who sit until peace is concluded, a compound that feuds for four years and forgives in a day, and the honoured father in whose presence no quarrel survives. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — the Abagar reconciliation order

Listen to **የአባጋር የእርቅ ሥርዓት** — the Abagar reconciliation order. Once the elders sit for peace, they do not rise until it is concluded. Punishment is not imprisonment. Offender and victim alike submit to the order. Judge the statements true or false from what you heard — the order's firmness is its kindness.

Expand the concept web into a full paragraph: the Abagar peace order — the offender's family, the victim's family, removing the harm, people of other areas. The web holds the parties; the paragraph sets them talking.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading — bringing down reconciliation

Read **እርቅ ማውረድ** — bringing down reconciliation. Neighbours in one compound feud for four years: the shared yard, the parking place, the guard's pay. They run to the kebele and even the police. The guard quits in disgust, and thieves begin stripping cars and laundry. At last neutral residents choose three women and three men as elders. The elders hear both sides, examine the cause, name the faults, and persuade them to forgive — the compound has peace again.

Choose the correct word form in brackets to complete the sentences — **ጠርቶ** against **መጥራት**, **አሳውቆ** against **አውቆ**: the gerund form and the infinitive form do different work, and the sentence tells you which it needs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Listening — if the sky is torn, an elder sews it

Listen to **ሰማይ ቢቀደድ ሽማግሌ ይሰፋዋል** — if the sky is torn, an elder sews it. Why is **ሽምግልና** — elder mediation — a traditional peace system? What is its main purpose, and why do Ethiopians honour it greatly? The saying itself is the first answer.

Match the definitions to the words: **ይቅርታ** — standing before the wronged, repenting; **እርቅ** — agreement between feuding sides; **ቁርሾ** — a wrong kept in the heart awaiting its time; **ባህል** — culture; **ክብር** — honour. Peace has a vocabulary; learn it before you need it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Reading — my memory, and the honoured father

Read **ትዝታዬ** — my memory. Twenty years a teacher, the narrator remembers being posted to a rural school with Felegush, a geography teacher. They rented a two-room house. She had never tasted coffee; Felegush disliked goat meat — and only goat was sold locally. By talking it through they agreed: he would drink coffee, she would eat goat. The goat cost sixty birr. Both were glad, and they still phone each other today. Small reconciliations are rehearsals for big ones.

Read **የተከበሩት አባት** — the honoured father. Ato Mgbaru of Debre Markos is eighty-five: thirty-eight years a faithful government worker, covered with certificates, his children raised in his image. Where he stands, no quarrel goes unforgiven. Hearing his late neighbour's children have fallen into a bitter inheritance quarrel, he goes, hears the cause, names the fault, makes them forgive each other and divides the property equally — peace in one hour.

## Sub lesson 5 — Speaking and writing — drama, essays, and money for children

In groups, make and show a short drama: some of you are elders, some the reconciled parties. Peace you have acted is peace you understand.

Write a three-paragraph essay from the **ሽምግልና** web: what it is / its benefit / its process / the elders. The web's branches become the essay's paragraphs.

Discuss in a group: what should you — and should you not — do when a disagreement arises with a friend? Bring one real rule from your own life.

Write a three-paragraph essay: **የክረምት ትዝታዬ** — my rainy-season memory. Memory writing stands on true detail: the rain, the road, the people.

Listen to **ገንዘብ ለሕጻናት** — money for children. A husband and wife debate giving children money: children cannot yet tell bad from good, says one side. Whose side do you take? Listening ends in your judgement, with reasons.

Present in groups: the reconciliation system's positive contribution to making peace — what it gives a community that courts alone do not.

Complete the starter: the reconciliation process in our own area, with examples of past conflicts and how elders settled them. Your area is the evidence.
