# Lesson plan — Grade 4 Making Peace

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Grade 4 Making Peace
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The reconciliation chapter: elders who sit until peace is concluded, a compound that feuds for four years and forgives in a day, and the honoured father in whose presence no quarrel survives.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 4 learner's fluent reading and listening: the texts are read twice the fluent way, listened to from the teacher's voice, and answered from what was read and heard.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order:

1. **Listening — the Abagar reconciliation order** — Elders who do not rise until peace is made, and a web grown into a paragraph.
2. **Reading — bringing down reconciliation** — Four years of feud in one compound — and the elders who end it.
3. **Listening — if the sky is torn, an elder sews it** — ሽምግልና, elder mediation, and the chapter's peace words defined.
4. **Reading — my memory, and the honoured father** — Coffee and goat meat talked through; an 85-year-old who settles a quarrel in an hour.
5. **Speaking and writing — drama, essays, and money for children** — Play the reconciliation, write from the ሽምግልና web, and debate pocket money.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Stories and Listening Stories — this lesson's Grade 4 texts are read and heard the same way, on this chapter's theme. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Retell this chapter's listening and reading texts in sequence, in their own words.
2. Use this chapter's word sets in sentences and in role play.
3. Do this chapter's writing tasks — sentences, paragraphs, webs and tables — with correct punctuation.
4. Do this chapter's grammar review work (suffixes, plurals and word-kinds as the chapter names them).

## If the learner is stuck
- A story will not retell → go back to its sequence: first, then, last — three steps carry every tale in this lesson.
- A word set will not stick → say it in a pair, use it in a sentence, then write it: ear, mouth, hand.
- A writing task feels big → the web or table first, sentences after: the book's own order in every chapter.
