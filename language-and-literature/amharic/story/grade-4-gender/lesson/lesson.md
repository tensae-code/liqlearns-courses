# Grade 4 Women and Men Together

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ሥርዓተፆታ** — gender — women and men together. The gender chapter: equal participation, equal share — mothers' work seen clearly, old sayings that belittle women rejected, and fathers and children sharing the work of the house. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — equal participation, equal share

Listen to **እኩል ተሳትፎ እኩል ድርሻ** — equal participation, equal share. Which natural conditions can reduce women's labour power? Discuss it as a class. Then sort the tasks: **ማርገዝ** (carrying a child) is nature's; **እንጀራ መጋገር** (baking injera), **ማረስ** (ploughing), **ማጥባት** (breastfeeding) — which are woman's, which man's, which shared? Sorting honestly is the lesson.

Judge true or false: the division of work between men and women comes from custom and culture, not from nature — true. What custom made, people can re-make.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading — honour to our mothers, and women in public

Read **ክብር ለእናቶቻችን** — Honour to our mothers — twice, the fluent way. Dr Aweke, a children's doctor, and Dr Birke, a birth doctor, talk. Mothers carry children to hospital and nurse them. Pregnancy, birth and breastfeeding are nature's — but why don't fathers help with carrying, washing and feeding? Women are the house's pillar at home and equal workers in farm and office. Laws on equality exist, but they are not properly applied.

Read **ሴቶች ባደባባይ** — Women in public. The old sayings that belittle women are quoted — and rejected as unacceptable in this age. Today women excel as ministers, judges, presidents, country leaders and border guards. New sayings replace the old: no national development without women's participation; educating a woman is educating society.

## Sub lesson 3 — Listening and reading — equality at home

Listen to **እኩልነት** — equality. Think of the saying: clapping with one hand makes no sound. Women's participation is the second hand. Hear how equality shows in hospitals and universities.

Sort the phrases: examples of women's equality — **ስኬታማነት** (success), **ጉልህ ተሳትፎ** (full participation), **ሀገር መሪ** (country leader); non-examples — **ወደማጀት**, **በወንድ ያልቅ**, **ልማዳዊ አባባሎች** (customary sayings). The sort itself is the argument.

Read **እኔና ልጆቼ** — I and my children — twice, the fluent way. A father overhears his two children reading a story about house-work division. His daughter says: there is no man's work and no woman's work — we all work helping each other. His little son asks: then why does mother work alone in our house? The father slips away, reproaching himself for never helping his wife.

Read **እማዬና አባዬ** — my mother and my father. Mother, an office manager, is buried in office work even on rest days. This week father does the house: he makes breakfast, wakes and dresses the children, serves mother her breakfast, cooks lunch, washes the clothes — then goes to his trade. The house stands, carried by two.

## Sub lesson 4 — Reading — harmful customs, and the woman mayor

Read **ሴቶችና ጎጂ ልማዶች** — women and harmful customs — twice, the fluent way. Selamnesh, who works on gender, asks her mother about the old customs. Her mother was married to a man she had never seen — our parents loved each other and married us off. The husband commands the money and walks in front: what has money to do with a woman? Selamnesh concludes: deep-rooted customs are not easily removed.

Read **የከተማችን ከንቲባ** — our city's mayor. She is a woman under forty: wise, tireless, in love with work. She cut unemployment, set up equal service for all, corrected the negative view of women, and keeps the peace — and she won first place competing with other mayors.

## Sub lesson 5 — Writing for mothers, and review

Write a short story about rural mothers, from the concept web: caring for children, rising before dawn, baking injera, fetching water, splitting firewood, sleeping last. The web holds her day; your story walks through it.

Write a full paragraph from the starter: **ሴቶችን ማስተማር ማኅበረሰቡን ማስተማር ነው** — educating women is educating society. The starter is the seed; the paragraph is the plant.

Split the suffixes off: **ዘመናት → ዘመን - ኣት**; do the same with **ጥንታዊ, መሪነት, እኩልነት, የበታችነት**. Peel the tail, name the stem.

Complete the starter **የወንዶች ቤት**: in our house mother is the only female among four males, yet she cares for all, walking far to the spring at dawn. Continue truly.

Write two paragraphs: list mother's work; describe her burden and her lack of time; say how you can help. The third part — your help — is the point of the first two.

Judge the stereotypes true or false: better-paying jobs are men's jobs — false. Women can be nurses but not doctors — false. There is no field of work women do not enter — true.

Complete the starter **ችግሩን ቀመስነው**: the family never noticed mother's burden until she fell ill. The doctor found heart strain from overwork and ordered a week's rest. What does the family learn? Your sentences answer.

Write **የገጠር እናቶች ድካም** — the labour of rural mothers — in two paragraphs: what rural mothers do from morning to night.

Listen to **ሴቶች በገጠርና በከተማ** — women in countryside and town. Compare their work in a two-branch concept web. Whose burden is heavier? The web first; your judgement after.

Join verb and suffix: **ሳቀ -ች, ጠራ -ን, መጣ -ሁ, ጻፈ -ኣችሁ, አየ -ኣት**. Verbs take person tails — the tail says who did it.

Complete the starter: oppression of women may seem lessened, but many still live under the yoke of wrong. List kinds of abuse — naming them is the first step to ending them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **ሥርዓተፆታ** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
