# Lesson plan — First Stories

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** First Stories
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a story can be heard,
retold in your own words, and acted — and a reader answers
questions about what happened in it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Sentence unit —
the learner reads whole sentences; a story is sentences walking
together after one person. Every story here is told as a summary
in the lesson's own words — never the book's text.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Butiye and the flower** — a girl, a flower, and the water that made it grow; the lesson's first story questions (fill the gaps, true or false).
2. **Ali and Hawa** — a family story known through its questions: father rode to town on a horse, Hawa's car is red, and a donkey walks in it.
3. **Mamo and his brother Gobeze** — the first real reading passage: Mamo, six years old, who sings to his little brother, plays with him, teaches him to eat and helps him dress.
4. **Gobez Meseret, who washes her hands** — a supporting story from the teachers' training: a ten-year-old girl whose clean habits her whole village holds up as the example (evidence-labelled SUPPORTING).

Every story is run the same way — the three passes: hear it,
say it, act it (the teachers' own method; SUPPORTING, labelled
in lesson/sources.md).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: My First Sentences — stories are sentences
  walking together. Recommended, never forced.
- Telling about yourself (name, age) helps the Mamo story land:
  Mamo is six, like the learner.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Hear a story once and retell it in their own words — who,
   where, what happened.
2. Answer true/false and gap questions about Butiye's flower:
   it grew because she watered it.
3. Say what the questions tell us about Ali and Hawa's family:
   father went to town by horse; Hawa's car is red.
4. Read the Mamo passage (or hear it read) and answer: Mamo is
   six, not seven; Gobeze is his little brother; Mamo helps him.
5. Name Meseret's clean habits and say which one they will copy
   this week.
6. Act a story's best moment with a partner — the third pass.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A cup of water for the acting passes. Room
to stand and move.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner hears the shortest story
   (Butiye) once, eyes closed, then retells it before any
   question is asked (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the four stories, each run hear it →
   say it → act it, with the story questions after each
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8215]: a picture of a young girl in a
     modest long dress watering a small pink flower in a pot
     outside a round thatched home — the student points and says
     who this is and what the water will do.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8216]: a picture of an older boy singing
     happily to his little brother inside a home, music notes in
     the air — the student says who the boys are and what the
     older one is doing.
   - ADD AUDIO for every story: the story read aloud at child
     pace — the student listens with eyes on the picture for the
     first pass, and retells after it (the Mamo passage's
     read-aloud is the book's own audio need).
3. **Say it to your companion** — retell one story to your
   companion; the companion asks one question about it, and you
   answer from the story (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     three-pass treatment of the Mamo story: heard, retold, and
     acted (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: true-or-false,
   multiple-choice, short-answer — served at the learner's level
   and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — choose one story and draw its best
   moment; tell the story to someone at home (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot retell → do not re-read the whole story; ask the three
  handles: Who is in it? What did they do? How does it end? The
  handles carry the story.
- Mixes Mamo and Gobeze → names on two hands: the big hand is
  Mamo, six; the small hand is Gobeze, the little brother. Help
  flows from the big hand to the small one.
- Answers from outside the story → come back to the page: "Show
  me where the story says that." Every question here is
  answerable from its story.
