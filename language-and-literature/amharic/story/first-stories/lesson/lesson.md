# First Stories

## Do it first

Close your eyes and listen to a whole story — the first one in
this lesson, about a girl and her flower. One hearing only. Then
open your eyes and tell it back: who was in it, what did she do,
how did it end? You just did the thing this lesson teaches. A
story is not a page — it is something you hear, then say in your
own words, then act with your body. Every story in this lesson
runs that way: **hear it → say it → act it.**

## Sub lesson 1 — Butiye and the flower

**Hear it.** Butiye (ቡጥዬ) has a flower. A flower is a living
thing, and living things need water. Butiye gives her flower
water — she waters it, and she keeps watering it. And the
flower grows. It grows because she watered it. That is the
whole story, and its lesson walks home with you: plant flowers
at home, too — and water them, like Butiye.

[Picture 8215 — Butiye watering her flower]

**Say it.** Tell it back in your own words: a girl, a flower,
water, growing. Four handles — hold the story by them.

**Act it.** Mime it: the watering can tips, the water falls,
the flower stands taller. Hands up slowly — that is the flower
growing.

Then the story's own questions, the way the book asks them —
fill the gaps: Butiye's flower grew because she ______ it. And:
we should plant ______ at home, too. (Watered; flowers.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Ali and Hawa

**Hear it.** This story lives in its questions — the book asks
about it, and the questions themselves tell us the story. Ali
and Hawa are children in a family. Their father went to town —
and he did not walk, and he did not drive: he went by horse.
Hawa has a car, and her car is red. And there is a donkey in
this family's story, too.

**Say it.** Tell back the three things the questions hand you:
father — town — horse. Hawa — car — red. And the donkey, who
belongs to the tale.

**Act it.** Ride the horse to town, hands holding reins. Now be
Hawa, proud beside a red car. Two small scenes — you have acted
the story's heart.

Then answer the story's questions, true or false: Their father
went to town by horse. Hawa's car is red. If anyone tells you
Hawa's car is blue, or that father walked — the story says
otherwise, and now you know it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Mamo and his brother Gobeze

**Hear it** — this is your first real reading passage; read it,
or hear it read at a child's pace. Mamo is six years old. This
year Mamo is the graduating pre-primary pupil — he is finishing
his pre-primary year. Mamo has a little brother, Gobeze, and
Mamo loves him. Mamo sings mezmur to his little brother. Mamo
plays with him. Mamo teaches him how to eat. And Mamo helps him
get dressed. A big brother who sings, plays, teaches and helps
— that is Mamo.

[Picture 8216 — Mamo singing to his little brother]

**Say it.** Retell it by its handles: Mamo — six — graduating.
Gobeze — little brother. Sing — play — teach — help.

**Act it.** Be Mamo: sing softly to your partner, play a small
game together, show them how to eat properly, help them into a
jacket. You have done everything the passage says.

Then the passage's questions: Gobeze's brother is Mamo — true.
Mamo is seven — false! He is six; catch it. And: who helps whom?
Mamo helps Gobeze.

## Sub lesson 4 — Gobez Meseret, who washes her hands

This story comes from the teachers' own training book — it is
the story the teachers themselves tell, and it is marked as
supporting material, not a workbook page.

**Hear it.** Meseret is a girl of ten, in a village. Meseret
never eats without washing her hands first. After the toilet
she washes with soap and water. She keeps her nails cut. She
brushes her teeth. When she sneezes or coughs, she covers her
nose and mouth. And the whole community points to her and says:
be like Meseret. Her name itself says it — Gobez Meseret,
Clever Meseret.

**Say it.** Her habits are the handles: hands before eating,
soap and water after the toilet, nails cut, teeth brushed,
cover the sneeze.

**Act it.** At real water, with real soap: wash before eating,
the Meseret way, and say her list as you go.


## Sub lesson 5 — Your first reading: care of your book

Before the stories, the Grade 1 book speaks to you itself
(printed p.ii). Read this first text slowly — it is a reading
with a purpose:

Your book is your friend. Cover it, so its outside stays
strong. Keep it dry and clean — water and dirt spoil its
pages. Do not fold its pages down; use a bookmark (a marker)
to keep your place, so you can find tomorrow's page at once.
Carry it carefully, and it will teach you all year.

Say the four cares back: cover it, keep it dry and clean, use
a bookmark, carry it carefully.

## Say it to your companion

Choose the story you loved most. Retell it to your companion —
beginning, middle, end, in your own words. Your companion then
asks you one question about it, and you answer from the story,
not from outside it. Then swap: a new story, a new teller. A
story you can retell is a story you own.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the best moment of one story. Say one sentence about
   your drawing.
2. Tell the Butiye story to someone at home tonight — and then
   really water a plant, or plant a flower, like she does.
3. Answer from the Mamo passage, in whole sentences: How old is
   Mamo? Who is Gobeze? Name two things Mamo does for him.
4. Pick one of Meseret's habits and start it today. Tell your
   companion which one you picked.
5. Act the horse ride from Ali and Hawa for your family — and
   tell them the colour of Hawa's car.
