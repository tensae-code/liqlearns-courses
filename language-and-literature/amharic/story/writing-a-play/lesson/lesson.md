# Writing a Play

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first part — set the stage

The book's writing task begins the play: choose a play title, then write the play's first part. Open with stage directions — the place, the furniture, who is on stage and how they look — because a play is seen before it is heard. Bring your main characters on and let the problem appear, as Belayhun's debt appears in his calculator and the creditors' calls. Then list the elements present in your first part under the table's 'beginning' column: characters, setting, action so far, problem.

## Sub lesson 2 — The middle part — let it grow and complicate

Next, write the middle part of your play and enter its elements in the table's 'middle' column. In the middle, the action grows and complicates (የድርጊት ማደግ መወሳሰብ): the conflict sharpens, new demands arrive, and somebody names the pressure underneath — as Demena names custom in Act 2. The book pairs this writing with performance and grammar: perform Act 2 in class, each learner in a character's part, imitating their speech; and join sentences with conjunctions (አያያዦች) as the play's own dialogue does — 'Ato Belayhun is very worried about the return matter, ይሁን እንጂ (however) he hasn't yet discussed it with his wife.'

## Sub lesson 3 — Finishing the play — the final part

Finish the play by writing its final part, entering its elements in the table's last column, and summarising all the elements — beginning, middle, end — in one organiser (ቢጋር). A good ending answers the problem the beginning set: in የሠርጉ መዘዝ the debt is answered not by money but by a decision — cancel the return, live within your means. Check your own ending the same way: does the climax (ጡዘት) land? Does the last speech carry the play's message? Then perform the whole play in class.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

2. Do the last sub lesson's task once more on your own, without looking, then check against the lesson.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, in your own words.
