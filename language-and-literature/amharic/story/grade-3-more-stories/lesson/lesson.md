# Grade 3 More Stories

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ታሪኮች** — stories. More tales: cows who stand together, a clever man and a hyena, a lion asking a dangerous question — and the games readers play with story words. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about stories in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fluent tales — standing together, and the bent tree

Read **አራት ላሞች** — Four Cows — twice, fluently. Together, the four cows drive off the tiger. Then they quarrel and separate — and the tiger eats them one by one. The tale's shape is its lesson: together, safe; apart, eaten.

Read **ጎባጣው ዛፍ** — The Bent Tree. The trees meet to talk about the axe. The wisest tree says: our enemy is not the axe — it is the bent trees among us who become the axe's handle. Read for that wise sentence.

Read **የአይጥና የድመት ጋብቻ** — The Wedding of the Mice and the Cats. Mice and cats agree to intermarry. But the mice suspect a trick, so they dig an escape hole by the wedding tree — and when the moment comes, they use it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tale comprehensions — a rule quoted back, a clever man, a lion's question

Read **እኔን አይመለከትም** — It Doesn't Apply to Me. A father forbids his son to pick up anything that has fallen. Then the father himself falls — and the son quotes the rule back to him. The father answers: that doesn't apply to me. Judge the answer fairly as you read.

Read **ብልሁ ሰው** — The Clever Man. A man falls into a pit. A hyena pulls him out — then means to eat him. The man stalls him: call all your kin to share me. While the hyena gathers them, the man escapes up a tree and mocks him from the branches.

Read **የአንበሳ ጥያቄ** — The Lion's Question. The lion asks the goat, the dik-dik and the monkey how his breath smells. The honest goat is eaten; the flattering dik-dik is eaten too. The monkey escapes by pleading a cold that blocks her nose — she cannot smell at all, so she cannot answer.

## Sub lesson 3 — Listening tales — the hare, and the bird and the man

Listen to **የጥንቸል ፍርሃት** — The Hare's Fear. Listen to what frightens the hare, step by step — then judge: were her fears reasonable? A fear can be real and still be bigger than its cause.

Listen to **ወፍና ሰው** — The Bird and the Man. The bird has an escape trick, and advice to give. Listen for the trick first, then for the advice — a tale often carries both.

## Sub lesson 4 — Writing tales — charts and new tales

For ብልሁ ሰው, fill the chart: problem + problem → solution. The man's problems grow — the pit, then the hyena's hunger — and his solution answers them. A chart shows a story's bones.

Compose a short tale of your own: imagine yourself the lion, questioning four animals. Each animal must answer in its own way — and you must decide, as the lion, what happens.

## Sub lesson 5 — Story games and grammar review

Play with a grid of fidel letters: build at least ten words of 3–4 letters from the letters in the grid. Every word must be a real word — the grid is the limit and the fun.

Play word BINGO: two boards of story vocabulary, and groups compete. When a word is called, find it on your board — the fastest reader's eye wins.

Complete sentences by choosing the right word — and finish each with the correct punctuation. The right word and the right mark, both.

Join suffixes to words: **-ኦች, -ዎች, -ኣት**, and **እነ-** in front. Joining is the mirror of splitting — now the parts come together.

Put the bracketed word into its correct plural form inside the sentence — the sentence itself tells you one or many.

Review whole: hunt the -ኣት words; sort words into singular and plural; and write a short tale about two animals who are natural enemies.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **ታሪኮች** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
