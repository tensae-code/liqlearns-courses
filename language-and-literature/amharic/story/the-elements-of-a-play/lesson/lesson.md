# The Elements of a Play

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The drama primer — five elements

Before the Grade 8 play begins, the book names what a play is made of. ገፀባሕርያት are the characters — the people who speak and act. መቼት is the setting — where and when it happens. ድርጊት is the action — what is done on stage. ግጭት is the conflict — the clash that drives it. Together these are the አላባውያን, the elements. A reader who can name the elements can take any play apart and put it back together.

## Sub lesson 2 — The elements table — beginning

After Act 1 of የሠርጉ መዘዝ, the book sets an analysis table: characters / the action's growth and complication / setting / problem / climax (ጡዘት) — filled first for the story's beginning. For the beginning you ask: who is here? Where are we? What problem has appeared? In the play's opening, Ato Belayhun sits alone among the wedding's rented goods, working a calculator, while creditors phone — the problem (debt) is on stage before any quarrel is spoken. Learning to fill the beginning column teaches you how to find a play's beginning, middle and end — and why knowing them helps: each part has its own job.

## Sub lesson 3 — The elements table — middle and end

The book returns to the same table for the middle (after Act 2) and the end (after Act 3). The middle asks how the action grows and complicates: the conflict between Belayhun and Ayelech sharpens, the video man's bill arrives, and custom (ወግ) itself is named as the pressure that made the debt. The end asks where the climax lands and how the problem resolves: the family decides to cancel the return feast and live within its means. One table, three fillings — beginning, middle, end — is the book's whole method for analysing a play.
