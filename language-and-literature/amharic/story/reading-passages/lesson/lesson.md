# Reading Passages

## Do it first

Read the school-compound passage in Sub lesson 2 once. Then
answer, from the passage alone: which trees stand in the
compound? You have just done what this lesson teaches — a
passage is read **for understanding**: first the pre-reading
questions, then the reading, then the passage's own tasks, and
at the end its main idea in one sentence of your own.

Passages come in kinds, and this lesson walks them kind by
kind: comprehension, descriptive, expository, procedural, and
history.

## Sub lesson 1 — Comprehension passages: people near us

**A Near Neighbour Beats a Far Relative (ከሩቅ ዘመድ የቅርብ
ጎረቤት).** The guide opens this reading from its picture:
look at the picture and say what the proverb shows before you
read. The passage carries a proverb in its title — a neighbour
who is near is better than a relative who is far, because the
near one is beside you when you need help. Its tasks are the
comprehension kind: some answers in full sentences, some
short, some chosen by letter, and the meanings of underlined
words and phrases taken from the passage itself.

[Picture 8320 — a near house and a far house]

The same shelf holds the student books' neighbour passages:
three neighbour families so close their children eat at each
other's houses (ሦስቱ ጎረቤቶች), and the lion and the mouse —
the mouse he once spared gnaws the trap rope and frees him.

## Sub lesson 2 — Descriptive passages: places and tables

A descriptive passage shows a place or a thing part by part,
so you can see it without going there.

**The School Compound (የትምህርትቤቱ ግቢ).** Flowers stand at
the gate. Inside are coffee, mango and olive trees, and the
warka, weyra and tid trees; grass and shrubs cover the ground.
Read it slowly — the passage is a walk through the compound,
and its order is the walk's order.

**Serving the Food (የምግብ አቀራረብ).** On Sunday the lunch is
laid out in order: bread, injera, meat wat, tibs and minchet
abish; dairy — ergo and ayib; vegetables; fruit; and shiro,
misir and kik wats in deep bowls. The description is the
order of the table itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — Expository passages: how things are

An expository passage explains — it tells how something works
or why it matters.

**Keeping Ourselves and Our Surroundings Clean (የአካባቢና የግል
ንፅህና).** The guide runs this reading with pre-reading
questions, then its tasks: write the reading's main idea in
three sentences, and explain in writing the difference between
environmental hygiene (የአካባቢ ንፅህና) and personal hygiene
(የግል ንፅህና). Its completion task gives a word box; the
guide's own key fills the sentences: 1. በጠና 2. መድኃኒት 3. ሰዎች
4. የግል ንፅህና 5. የአካባቢውን ንፅህና.

[Picture 8322 — clean ground and dirty ground]

**Preventing Disease (በሽታን መከላከል).** Washing hair, body,
hands and clothes prevents skin disease; collecting and
burning waste and using latrines prevents diarrhoea, eye
disease and flu; draining stagnant pools stops malaria
mosquitoes breeding.

**Growing a Backyard Garden Wisely (የጓሮ አትክልትን ማልማት
በብልሃት).** No big land or tools are needed: a small hoe, a
spade, a bucket and a watering can; soil in sacks and
containers; fast crops — onion, lettuce, kale, cabbage,
tomato and pepper. The book asks for its whole teaching in one
sentence — try it.

**Animal passages.** The night bird (የሌሊት ወፍ) borrowed food
by day and, unable to repay, took to the night; it suckles its
young and feeds them sixty days. Dolphins bear and suckle live
young, one at a time, and are kind to people; crocodiles lay
up to fifteen eggs in the sand (ዶልፊንና ዐዞ). The giraffe
(ቀጭኔ) is the tallest land animal, and its long tongue can
even clean its ears. The civet (ጥርኝ) gives ዝባድ, the musk
people mix into perfume.

## Sub lesson 4 — Procedural passages: steps in order

A procedural passage gives steps, and the steps only work in
their order.

**How a Kebero Drum Is Made (የከበሮ አዘገጃጀት).** Soak the
hide and scrape it. Hollow the wanza-wood body — wide at the
top, narrow at the bottom. Lace the hide over both openings
with straps. Rub it with embway fruit and sun it, for a good
sound.

**How to Prepare Salad (የሰላጣ አዘገጃጀት).** The ingredients:
lettuce, lemon or vinegar, onion, tomato, pepper, salt and
vegetable oil. The steps in order: wash; chop; mix in a deep
bowl; oil and salt; lemon juice. Serve with bread or injera.
Retell the steps from the step board — first to last, none
skipped.

## Sub lesson 5 — History passages: Finote Selam

**The Naming of Finote Selam (የፍኖተሰላም ስያሜ).** A history
passage: it tells what happened, in time order. Italy invaded
in 1928 E.C. The patriots resisted for five years from the
forests and mountains. Haile Selassie returned by way of
Sudan. Resting at the town called ወጀት, he renamed it
ፍኖተሰላም — "path of peace". The passage's own name-word is a
compound: ፍኖተ-ሰላም joined into ፍኖተሰላም.

[Picture 8321 — the path-of-peace sign]

## Say it to your companion

Take one passage. Say its main idea in ONE sentence. Your
companion opens the passage and checks: is your sentence the
passage's true heart, with nothing added from outside? Then
swap — a new passage, a new one-sentence teller.

## Do It Yourself

1. From the school-compound passage, list its trees in the
   passage's order.
2. Write the difference between personal hygiene and
   environmental hygiene, in two sentences of your own.
3. Put the salad steps in order from memory — then check
   against the passage.
4. Retell the Finote Selam passage in four sentences, in time
   order.
5. Summarise Growing a Backyard Garden Wisely in one
   sentence, as the book asks.
