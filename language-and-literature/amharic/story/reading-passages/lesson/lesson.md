# Reading Passages

## Do it first

Read the school-compound passage in Sub lesson 2 once. Then
answer, from the passage alone: which trees stand in the
compound? You have just done what this lesson teaches — a
passage is read **for understanding**: first the pre-reading
questions, then the reading, then the passage's own tasks, and
at the end its main idea in one sentence of your own.

Passages come in kinds, and this lesson walks them kind by
kind: comprehension, descriptive, expository, procedural, and
history.

## Sub lesson 1 — Comprehension passages: people near us

**A Near Neighbour Beats a Far Relative (ከሩቅ ዘመድ የቅርብ
ጎረቤት).** The guide opens this reading from its picture:
look at the picture and say what the proverb shows before you
read. The passage carries a proverb in its title — a neighbour
who is near is better than a relative who is far, because the
near one is beside you when you need help. Its tasks are the
comprehension kind: some answers in full sentences, some
short, some chosen by letter, and the meanings of underlined
words and phrases taken from the passage itself.

[Picture 8320 — a near house and a far house]

The same shelf holds the student books' neighbour passages:
three neighbour families so close their children eat at each
other's houses (ሦስቱ ጎረቤቶች), and the lion and the mouse —
the mouse he once spared gnaws the trap rope and frees him.

## Sub lesson 2 — Descriptive passages: places and tables

A descriptive passage shows a place or a thing part by part,
so you can see it without going there.

**The School Compound (የትምህርትቤቱ ግቢ).** Flowers stand at
the gate. Inside are coffee, mango and olive trees, and the
warka, weyra and tid trees; grass and shrubs cover the ground.
Read it slowly — the passage is a walk through the compound,
and its order is the walk's order.

**Serving the Food (የምግብ አቀራረብ).** On Sunday the lunch is
laid out in order: bread, injera, meat wat, tibs and minchet
abish; dairy — ergo and ayib; vegetables; fruit; and shiro,
misir and kik wats in deep bowls. The description is the
order of the table itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — Expository passages: how things are

An expository passage explains — it tells how something works
or why it matters.

**Keeping Ourselves and Our Surroundings Clean (የአካባቢና የግል
ንፅህና).** The guide runs this reading with pre-reading
questions, then its tasks: write the reading's main idea in
three sentences, and explain in writing the difference between
environmental hygiene (የአካባቢ ንፅህና) and personal hygiene
(የግል ንፅህና). Its completion task gives a word box; the
guide's own key fills the sentences: 1. በጠና 2. መድኃኒት 3. ሰዎች
4. የግል ንፅህና 5. የአካባቢውን ንፅህና.

[Picture 8322 — clean ground and dirty ground]

**Preventing Disease (በሽታን መከላከል).** Washing hair, body,
hands and clothes prevents skin disease; collecting and
burning waste and using latrines prevents diarrhoea, eye
disease and flu; draining stagnant pools stops malaria
mosquitoes breeding.

**Growing a Backyard Garden Wisely (የጓሮ አትክልትን ማልማት
በብልሃት).** No big land or tools are needed: a small hoe, a
spade, a bucket and a watering can; soil in sacks and
containers; fast crops — onion, lettuce, kale, cabbage,
tomato and pepper. The book asks for its whole teaching in one
sentence — try it.

**Animal passages.** The night bird (የሌሊት ወፍ) borrowed food
by day and, unable to repay, took to the night; it suckles its
young and feeds them sixty days. Dolphins bear and suckle live
young, one at a time, and are kind to people; crocodiles lay
up to fifteen eggs in the sand (ዶልፊንና ዐዞ). The giraffe
(ቀጭኔ) is the tallest land animal, and its long tongue can
even clean its ears. The civet (ጥርኝ) gives ዝባድ, the musk
people mix into perfume.

## Sub lesson 4 — Procedural passages: steps in order

A procedural passage gives steps, and the steps only work in
their order.

**How a Kebero Drum Is Made (የከበሮ አዘገጃጀት).** Soak the
hide and scrape it. Hollow the wanza-wood body — wide at the
top, narrow at the bottom. Lace the hide over both openings
with straps. Rub it with embway fruit and sun it, for a good
sound.

**How to Prepare Salad (የሰላጣ አዘገጃጀት).** The ingredients:
lettuce, lemon or vinegar, onion, tomato, pepper, salt and
vegetable oil. The steps in order: wash; chop; mix in a deep
bowl; oil and salt; lemon juice. Serve with bread or injera.
Retell the steps from the step board — first to last, none
skipped.

[Picture 8323 — the step board: wash, cut, mix, serve]

## Sub lesson 5 — History passages: Finote Selam

**The Naming of Finote Selam (የፍኖተሰላም ስያሜ).** A history
passage: it tells what happened, in time order. Italy invaded
in 1928 E.C. The patriots resisted for five years from the
forests and mountains. Haile Selassie returned by way of
Sudan. Resting at the town called ወጀት, he renamed it
ፍኖተሰላም — "path of peace". The passage's own name-word is a
compound: ፍኖተ-ሰላም joined into ፍኖተሰላም.

[Picture 8321 — the path-of-peace sign]

## Sub lesson 6 — Descriptive passages: mountains and waterfalls

**Wehni Amba (ወህኒ አምባ).** Read the description slowly:
a bare, steep amba, its top flat, its sides falling away.
The passage paints with comparisons — note each simile
as you read, and say what it compares the amba to. A
descriptive passage succeeds when the reader can see the
place standing. Close the passage and describe Wehni
Amba to your companion from the words alone.

[Picture 8620 — Wehni Amba, the bare steep flat-topped mountain]

**Life on an Amba (የአምባ ላይ ኑሮ).** Read how people live
on top of an amba: the climbing, the water, the fields,
the wind. Pull the passage's main idea out in one
sentence — then list three details the passage gives
that prove it.

**As I Saw Tis Abay (ጢስ ዓባይን እንዳየሁት).** Read the
visit to the great falls: the Fasil Bridge, the hanging
footbridge, and the spray that rises like smoke — ጢስ —
which is the name's own origin: Smoke of the Nile. Read
the name's story inside the passage, and retell the
visit in its order: the road, the bridge, the falls,
the spray.

**A Visit (ጉብኝት).** The "Know Your Country" club visits
the culture and research institute named after Dr Haddis
Alemayehu. Read what the club goes to learn, and what it
finds. A visit passage is a descriptive passage with
feet: it moves from place to place. Name the places in
the order the visit names them.

## Sub lesson 7 — Passages with tables: read the numbers

**Headache (የራስምታት), with its data table.** Read the
passage on headaches — migraine among them, and its
triggers. Then turn to its table. This passage's rule:
every question is answered FROM the table, not from
memory. Practise: ask a partner a question whose answer
stands in a table cell, and let them point to the cell
before they speak.

**Climate of the Amhara Region (የአማራ ክልል አየር ንብረት),
with its three tables.** Read the region's climate by
its zones and their shares: ቆላ 6%, ወይና ደጋ 60%, ደጋ
31%, ውርጭ 3% — and its temperatures, from the lowest,
12.4 °C, to the highest, 27.8 °C. Read the three tables
the way you read sentences: across, then down. Say which
zone covers most of the region, and prove it from the
table.

[Picture 8621 — the climate zones and the shares the passage gives]

**Improved Seed (ምርጥ ዘር).** Read about improved seed,
then work its teff variety table: ማኛ, ቁንጮ, ዱከም, ኬና,
እፁብ, ዲማ, ኮራ, ዳግም, ንጉሥ. The passage's questions are
answered from the table. A table is a passage folded
small — unfold it row by row, and say one full sentence
each row teaches.

## Sub lesson 8 — Research-style readings: how a report is made

**The First Driver (የመጀመሪያው ሾፌር).** Eskinder must
answer a hard question: who was Ethiopia's first driver?
Read how he researches it in the library — newspapers,
magazines, books — and how he notes each finding with
its author's name and its publication year, then writes
the whole up as an essay. His finding: Menelik was
Ethiopia's first driver. The reading's lesson for you is
the method inside it: a claim, its sources named, its
years kept.

[Picture 8622 — researching in the library: newspapers, books, note cards]

**Student Mulunesh's Report.** Read the report Mulunesh
compiled — from television, newspapers, magazines, books
and websites — on energy: which sources renew themselves
and which do not. Read the report as a reader, not a
copier: name her sources in order, then sort her energy
kinds into the passage's two families — renewable and
non-renewable.

**Emperor Fasilides' Palace (የአፄ ፋሲለደስ ቤተመንግሥት).**
Read this palace passage the KWL way: what you Know,
what you Want to know, what you Learned. Its numbers
stand ready: 32 metres high, three floors, 132 steps,
20 rooms. After reading, close the passage and give its
four numbers back. A passage with numbers is understood
when its numbers can be carried away.

## Sub lesson 9 — Lives in prose: biography readings

Read each life the same way: first for the life, then
for its dates and deeds — a biography passage is
understood when its facts can be retold in order.

**Artist Shileshi Demissie (አርቲስት ስለሺ ደምሴ).** Read
the artist's life and work, and say what kind of artist
the passage shows, from its own evidence.

**The Award Winner — Wangari Maathai (ተሸላሚዋ).** Read
how Wangari Maathai's Green Belt movement planted trees
— over 30 million of them — and how she received the
Nobel Prize in 1996 E.C. Say the passage's chain: trees,
movement, prize.

[Picture 8623 — women planting tree seedlings in a row]

**Success (ስኬት) — Tirunesh Dibaba.** Read the runner's
life: at Beijing in 2008 she won double gold — the first
woman ever to do it. Retell her passage by its races.

**Dr Woldemeskel Kostre.** Read the coach's life — the
man behind the runners. Say what a coach's work is, from
the passage's own account.

**President Sahle-Work Zewde (ፕሬዝዳንት ሣህለወርቅ ዘውዴ).**
Read the president's life as the passage lays it out,
and give its main steps in order.

**Berhanu Zerihun (ብርሃኑ ዘርይሁን).** Read the writer's
life: eleven novels and three plays. A writer's
biography is counted in works — name the count, and one
thing more the passage tells.

**Emahoy Tsegue-Maryam Guebrou (እማሆይ ፅጌማርያም ገብሩ).**
Read the composer's life: over one hundred piano
compositions, gathered as ኢትዮጲክስ. Say what her music
carries, from the passage.

**Afewerk Tekle (አፈወርቅ ተክሌ).** Read the artist's
life: more than 97 international awards. Say what the
passage counts, and what it praises beyond counting.

**Empress Taytu Betul (እቴጌ ጣይቱ ብጡል).** Read the
empress's life: the Wuchale Treaty, Adwa, and the
founding of Addis Ababa. Retell it in time order — a
history life is a history passage wearing one name.

*The writing of a life — its method — is taught in the
Writing lessons; here each life is met as reading only.*

## Sub lesson 10 — Argue and advise: passages that take a side

**Let's Not Be a Rubbish Dump (ቆሻሻ መጣያ አንሁን).** Read
the experts' counsel on guarding the mind — five
counsels the passage gives. Read them as a list, then
argue with them fairly: which counsel is hardest to
keep, and why? An argue-passage is understood when you
can state its side — and the strongest point against it.

[Picture 8624 — a 'garbage' word bouncing off a shield of calm]

**"Littering Is Forbidden!" (ቆሻሻ መጣል ክልክል ነው!).**
Read how this passage widens the word rubbish: hate
speech is rubbish thoughts thrown at people. It carries
the proverb ለነገረኛ ሰው ጀርባህን ስጠው — to a quarrelsome
person, give your back. Say what the passage forbids
throwing, in both its senses.

**Social Media (ማኅበራዊ ሚዲያ).** Read the passage's
rule for the flood of posts: separate the true and the
useful before believing. Practise on three claims from
the passage: mark each — true, useful, both, neither —
as the passage teaches.

**Let Hate Stop (ጥላቻ ይቁም).** Read this passage's case
against hatred, in its own order of reasons. Pull its
main idea out in one sentence, then name the reason you
find strongest, and say why — from the passage.

## Sub lesson 11 — Explain the world: causes in prose

**The Shrinking of the Natural Forest (የተፈጥሮ ደን
መመናመን).** Read the Wof Washa protected forest's
numbers: 92,200 hectares in 1994 E.C.; 8,290 in 2002
E.C.; 8,222 in 2005 E.C. An explain-passage is
understood when its cause is named: say what shrank the
forest, from the passage — and carry its three numbers
away in order.

[Picture 8625 — the forest shrinking: the passage's own figures]

**Climate Change (የአየር ንብረት ለውጥ).** Read the
explanation whole: greenhouse gases, the ozone layer
19–48 kilometres up, and the carbon cycle turning.
Draw the passage as a circle of arrows from its own
account — what rises, what traps, what warms.

[GIF 8626 — planting a seedling: dig, place, cover, water]

**Causes of Child Labour Exploitation (የሕፃናት ጉልበት
ብዝበዛ መንስኤዎች).** Read the passage's care first: it
separates children's work from exploitation. Then its
three causes — ድህነት, poverty; parents' distorted
attitude; and illegal people-movers. Name all three
from the passage, in its order.

**Illegal Human Trafficking (ሕገወጥ የሰዎች ዝውውር).** Read
the definition the passage takes from the Palermo
Protocol, and its warning: refuse brokers' promises.
Say the definition in your own words, and one promise
the passage teaches you to refuse.

## Sub lesson 12 — Work culture: the Awramba reading

**Awramba (አውራምባ) (teacher guide — evidence).** Read about
a community known for one thing above all: its **strong
work culture**. Begin the way this reading begins — with
pre-reading questions on the Awramba community: what do you
expect a place to look like where work is honoured by
everyone? Then read silently, on your own, and answer from
the passage. Pull its main idea out in one sentence: what
does this community prove about work?

## Sub lesson 13 — Unity in diversity: two readings

**Tebechisa (ተበቺሳ) (teacher guide — evidence).** Read this
passage — a cultural practice of one of the peoples of this
land, taken as the case study of diversity itself. Its
comprehension work is done aloud, answers given in detail.
Read it, then say: what does this one people's practice
teach about how different peoples hold one country?

**Unity and Diversity: Nature's Twin Faces (አንድነትና
ብዝሃነት፦ የተፈጥሮ መንታ ገጽታዎች) (teacher guide — evidence).**
Read this passage for its themes. Its twin points stand in
its title: **unity** and **diversity** — two seemingly
opposite ideas that belong together, the way nature itself
holds many kinds in one world. Name the passage's themes
when you finish, and say in one sentence how the two ideas
hold each other up.

## Sub lesson 14 — Hospitality and the reckoning of time

**Ethiopian Hospitality (ኢትዮጵያዊ እንግዳ ተቀባይነት) (teacher
guide — evidence).** Read this passage — the same titled
text that is also heard in its chapter — and read further
texts on hospitality beside it. Its work is the reader's
work at full stretch: state the gist (ጭምቅ ሀሳብ) of what
you read; write a summary of it; and compare it with your
own experience in writing. A passage is understood when
its gist fits in one breath and its summary in one page.

**The Reckoning of Time (የዘመን አቆጣጠር) (teacher guide —
evidence).** Read — as this chapter sets it, after hearing —
how time itself was reckoned. Recurring natural signs mark
a period called **ዓውድ** (awd) — a cycle, a circle — in
Ge'ez. The scholars of the **ባሕረ ሐሳብ** (Bahre Hasab)
recorded the history of the kings in their books, taking
as their models foreign historians — above all Abu Shakir
and George ibn al-Amid, of the thirteenth century. And
they linked the change of eras to the movement of the
stars and planets. List the passage's general information
and its detailed information apart: the cycle itself, then
who kept it and how.

[Picture 8953 — a Bahre Hasab manuscript page, its star-points joined in their cycle]

[GIF 8998 — the ዓውድ: twelve star-points circling a still centre, round and round]

**The Knowledge of Traditional Medicine (ሀገረሰባዊ የህክምና
ጥበብ) (teacher guide — evidence).** Read what traditional
medicine is, the process of preparing it (ቅመማ ሂደት), and
what it focuses on. As you read, list two columns: the
general information — what it is — and the detailed
information — how a preparation is made, step by step.

## Sub lesson 15 — Inclusive education: two readings

**Tsehay and Her Challenges (ፀሐይ እና ተግዳሮቶቿ) (teacher
guide — evidence).** Read the story of a learner with
special needs — a reading that teaches attitude as much as
information. Hold its keyed answers whole: help for
disabled learners begins with **attitude change**; exams
can be reached through **Braille and tape-recording**; and
inclusive education (የአካቶ ትምህርት) exists to meet disabled
learners' differing needs in a special way. Its challenges
are named plainly: people's attitudes; some children's
difficulties never being identified; and the assumption
that disabled people cannot reach high levels. Inside its
answers, two growing sequences are taught: speech grows
**sound → words → sentences**, and movement grows
**roll → crawl → creep → walk**. Say both sequences in
order when you finish.

**A Symbol of Perseverance (የአይበገሬነት ተምሳሌት) (teacher
guide — evidence).** Read this passage and list its
detailed information — not its gist this time, but the
details, one by one, as the reading asks. A perseverance
passage is understood when its details can be carried
away in order: what was faced, what was done, what it
proved.

## Sub lesson 16 — Migration: the reading

**Migration (ስደት) (teacher guide — evidence).** Read this
passage on migration. When you finish, do its two works:
explain what you have grasped, in your own words; and
write the themes the passage presents. A migration passage
is understood when you can say both — what it told, and
what it is about — without opening it again.

## Say it to your companion

Take one passage. Say its main idea in ONE sentence. Your
companion opens the passage and checks: is your sentence the
passage's true heart, with nothing added from outside? Then
swap — a new passage, a new one-sentence teller.
