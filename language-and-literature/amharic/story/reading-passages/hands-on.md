# Hands-on — Reading Passages

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Walk the compound, describe it
Walk your school or home compound slowly, the descriptive
passage's way: what is at the gate? Which trees stand there?
What grows low? Come back and say it part by part, in the
passage's order — gate first, then trees, then the ground.

## Make the salad by the passage
With an adult, follow the salad procedure with real things:
wash, chop, mix in a deep bowl, oil and salt, lemon juice —
saying each step aloud before you do it, in the passage's
order. Eat it with bread or injera, as the passage ends.

## Clean-side, dirty-side
Draw your own Picture 8322: your street or yard in two halves —
as it is when waste is left, and as the cleanliness passage
wants it. Under each half, one sentence from the passage's
teaching in your own words.

## One-sentence summaries
Take any passage from this lesson. On a strip of paper write
its main idea in ONE sentence. Read it to a partner: can they
name the passage from your sentence alone?

## If it is hard
Read the passage in pairs, a paragraph each; keep the word box
beside you for the completion task, as the guide allows; answer
orally first, then write. The guide's own order: pre-reading
questions, reading, then the tasks — never the tasks cold.
