# Lesson plan — Reading Passages

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Reading Passages
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a passage can be read for understanding — its kind named (comprehension, descriptive, expository, procedural, history) and its main idea pulled out in one sentence.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Reading Stories: the learner already reads stories fluently; here the texts are passages — they describe, explain, order steps and tell history — and the task is understanding: pre-reading questions, the passage's own questions, and the main idea in one sentence.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Comprehension passages — people near us** — A Near Neighbour Beats a Far Relative, and the books' neighbour and cleanliness comprehension texts, answered in full sentences and short answers.
2. **Descriptive passages — places and tables** — the school compound described flower by flower and tree by tree; and a Sunday lunch table laid out in order.
3. **Expository passages — how things are** — Keeping Ourselves and Our Surroundings Clean; Preventing Disease; Growing a Backyard Garden Wisely; and the animal passages — the night bird, the dolphin and the crocodile, the giraffe, the civet.
4. **Procedural passages — steps in order** — how a kebero drum is made, and how a salad is prepared — read as sequences and retold in order.
5. **History passages — Finote Selam** — the naming of Finote Selam: invasion, five years of resistance in forest and mountain, and a resting town renamed Path of Peace.
6. **Descriptive passages — mountains and waterfalls** — Wehni Amba described simile by simile; life on an amba; Tis Abay seen and named; and a club's visit to the Haddis Alemayehu institute.
7. **Passages with tables — read the numbers** — Headache with its data table; the Amhara climate in three tables; and Improved Seed's teff varieties — every question answered from the table.
8. **Research-style readings — how a report is made** — Eskinder researches the first driver in the library; Mulunesh compiles her energy report from five kinds of source; and Fasilides' Palace read the KWL way.
9. **Lives in prose — biography readings** — nine lives read for their facts in order: Shileshi Demissie, Wangari Maathai, Tirunesh Dibaba, Woldemeskel Kostre, Sahle-Work Zewde, Berhanu Zerihun, Emahoy Tsegue-Maryam, Afewerk Tekle and Empress Taytu Betul.
10. **Argue and advise — passages that take a side** — Let's Not Be a Rubbish Dump and its five counsels; "Littering Is Forbidden!" and hate speech as rubbish; Social Media's true-and-useful rule; and Let Hate Stop.
11. **Explain the world — causes in prose** — the Wof Washa forest's shrinking numbers; climate change explained; the three causes of child labour exploitation; and the Palermo definition of trafficking.
12. **Work culture — the Awramba reading** (teacher guide — evidence) — a community known for its strong work culture, read from pre-reading questions to its one-sentence main idea.
13. **Unity in diversity — two readings** (teacher guide — evidence) — Tebechisa, one people's practice as the diversity case study; and Nature's Twin Faces, unity and diversity as themes that belong together.
14. **Hospitality and the reckoning of time** (teacher guide — evidence) — Ethiopian Hospitality (gist, summary, comparison with your own experience); the Bahre Hasab reckoning of time (ዓውድ, the scholars, the stars); and the traditional-medicine reading (what it is, its preparation process, its focus).
15. **Inclusive education — two readings** (teacher guide — evidence) — Tsehay and Her Challenges (attitude change, Braille and tape-recording at exams, the named challenges, the two growing sequences); and A Symbol of Perseverance, read for its detailed information.
16. **Migration — the reading** (teacher guide — evidence) — explain what was grasped, and write the passage's themes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Stories — passages are read the
  same fluent way, then questioned harder. Recommended, never
  forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name a passage's kind: comprehension, descriptive, expository, procedural or history.
2. Answer A Near Neighbour Beats a Far Relative from the passage, and give underlined words' meanings from the passage.
3. List the school compound's planting from the descriptive passage (flowers at the gate; coffee, mango and olive trees; grass and shrubs).
4. Complete the cleanliness passage's sentences with its word-box words, and say the difference between personal and environmental hygiene.
5. Retell a procedure — kebero-making or salad-making — in its right order.
6. Retell the Finote Selam passage in time order, and say what its name means.
7. Describe Wehni Amba from the passage's words alone, similes named.
8. Answer a question from a passage's table by pointing to its cell first.
9. Say the climate zones' shares — ቆላ 6, ወይና ደጋ 60, ደጋ 31, ውርጭ 3 — and the lowest and highest temperatures.
10. Name Eskinder's research method: sources searched, authors and years noted, essay written.
11. Give Fasilides' Palace's four numbers: 32 metres, three floors, 132 steps, 20 rooms.
12. Retell one biography reading by its dates and deeds, in order.
13. State an argue-passage's side fairly, and the strongest point against it.
14. Carry the Wof Washa numbers in order — 92,200; 8,290; 8,222 hectares — and name the cause.
15. Give the Awramba reading's main idea in one sentence.
16. Say how unity and diversity belong together, from Nature's Twin Faces, and what Tebechisa is a case study of.
17. State the hospitality reading's gist (ጭምቅ ሀሳብ), and explain ዓውድ and who the Bahre Hasab scholars were.
18. Name Tsehay's three challenges to inclusive education, and say both growing sequences in order.
19. Explain the migration reading's grasped content and write its themes, with the passage closed.

## What you need
The lesson page. Paper and a pencil. For the Do It Yourself procedure: a bowl, and salad things or a drum-like pot at home.

## If the learner is stuck
- Answers come from outside the passage → the guide's rule:
  look at the passage's own picture and words first; the answer
  stands in the text.
- Main idea comes out as a list → shrink it: "This passage
  tells that…" and one sentence only.
- Steps jumble → lay them on the step board (Picture 8323) one
  by one, in the order the passage gives.
