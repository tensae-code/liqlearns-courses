# Grade 4 Visiting Our Heritage

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ጉብኝት** — visiting. The visiting chapter: monasteries and mosques, a falls like smoke, palaces and old capitals — what Ethiopia's heritage places are, and why visiting them teaches citizens. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — the Goze mosque and Ysma the King

Listen to **የጎዜ መስጊድ** — the Goze mosque. Where does it stand? The Argoba communities lived below it. Why does it attract tourists, and what are its walls and roof made of? A listening about a place is a visit by ear.

Listen to **ይስማ ንጉሥ** — Ysma the King, the place named for a king. Who signed the Treaty of Wuchale with Italy? Why is Adwa Day celebrated every year — and who is the pioneer of black people's freedom the listening names? Names of places carry history; this listening unpacks one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fluent reading — Captain Silenat, and Ayiteyef hall

Read **ካፒቴን ስለናት** — Captain Silenat — twice, the fluent way. Captain Silenat is a famous woman pilot, now flying international routes. She tells foreign tourists about the Amhara region's attractions, and through her connections tourist numbers and the region's income grow. A pilot can also be a guide.

Read **አይጠየፍ** — twice, the fluent way. A class makes its first school visit to the Ayiteyef hall in Dessie: a huge hall of 2,130 square metres, built in 1907 E.C. by King Mikael for assemblies and feasts where poor and rich shared equally. That equality is why it is called አይጠየፍ — he is not asked, he is not wanting.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading — Tis Abay falls, and Tana at a glance

Read **ጢስ አባይ ፏፏቴ** — the Tis Abay falls. The falls lie 30 kilometres east of Bahir Dar and spread about 400 metres wide. The water plunges into a gorge 40 to 50 metres deep, and its spray covers the area like smoke — ጢስ — with rainbows forming in it. The visitor's heart fills with joy.

Build the concept web on the falls: where it is / the natural phenomena / its width and depth / the visitor's feeling. Four branches carry the whole reading.

Read **ጣናን በጨረፍታ** — Tana at a glance. With only half a day, the writer cannot tour Lake Tana by boat, and admires it from a Bahir Dar hotel balcony. Tana — read here as some 1,500 square miles — has no rival in Ethiopia for size or for history. A glance can still be a true visit, if the eyes are honest.

## Sub lesson 4 — Reading — Guzara palace and Ankober

Read **ጉዛራ ቤተመንግስት** — the Guzara palace — twice, the fluent way. The palace rests on a hill across the Garno river near Gondar: a stone wall, three gates, built in the mid-16th century by Atse Serse Dingil. One storey, ten rooms, four windows — and its western part has fallen. Ruins still teach, if you read them closely.

Read **አንኮበር** — Ankober, 42 kilometres east of Debre Berhan. It was founded in 1733 E.C. by Negasi Amha Iyesus, and it is a resting place of Ethiopian kings since Yikuno Amlak — seat of six Shewa kings. Sahle Selassie made it a permanent court where over a thousand carpenters, masons, weapon-makers and metalworkers worked. Complete the table: king / reign / deeds.

## Sub lesson 5 — Writing and adjective review

Write two paragraphs listing the benefits tourist attractions bring citizens — work, income, pride, and care for the places themselves.

Review adjectives — ቅጽል: adjectives describe a noun by kind (**ነጩ በግ** — the white sheep), by quality (**ብልሁ ተማሪ** — the clever student), by size (**ትልቅ ዳቦ** — the big bread). Pick the adjectives out of the chapter's sentences.

Build one full paragraph from the starter notes on Lake Zengena: in the Awi zone; green-looking; ringed by forest; used for recreation. Notes are bones; the paragraph is the body.

Write a paragraph on the benefits of visiting historical places — what a visitor carries home that no photograph holds.

Pick the adjectives out of the Ayiteyef sentences: **ግዙፍ** (huge), **ብዙ** (many), **ጥሩ** (good), **ውድ** (dear).

Complete sentences by adding adjectives and verbs around given nouns: **ጓደኞች** (friends), **ሸሚዝ** (shirt), **መጽሐፍ** (book), **ቤት** (house), **ወንድም** (brother), **ቦርሳ** (bag). The noun is the seed; adjective and verb grow it into a sentence.

Complete the paragraph: students should visit their region's tourist sites — firstly…, secondly…, thirdly… Order words carry a paragraph the way signposts carry a traveller.

Match suffixes to words — **መንግሥት -ኣት = መንግሥታት** — and build full sentences with given adjectives: **ሰነፍ, ብልህ, ደግ, ቸር**.
