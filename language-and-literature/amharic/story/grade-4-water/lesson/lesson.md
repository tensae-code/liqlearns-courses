# Grade 4 Water

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ዉኃ** — water. The water chapter: water as medicine, water wasted and water saved, the river that feeds a village twice a year, and the rain cycle that carries water round the world. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — water as medicine

Listen to **የዉኃ ህክምና** — water as medicine. Match the water kinds to the treatments: ice, cold water, hot water, or either — for migraine, for colds and flu, for fall and collision injuries. Water heals as well as quenches; the matching is the lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading — the gold in your hand

Read **በእጅ የያዙት ወርቅ** — the gold in your hand is counted as copper. A boy visits his uncle. His elder brother pours hand-washing water on the ground, and the uncle leaps up in rage — less than a litre! The boy learns the secret: the uncle strains that water and pours it under his yard plants — that is why they are green. One stone, two birds: nothing wasted, everything fed.

Build a full paragraph from the given points: a big lake; people who did not value it; farming its shores carelessly; silt washing in; waste joining; the lake drying; the greenness gone; the people's deep regret. Points are a story folded small — your paragraph unfolds it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Listening and reading — water's benefit, and our river

Listen to **ዉኃና ጥቅሙ** — water and its benefit. Build the concept web of water's many services, with three supporting details on its branches. Water works everywhere at once; the web keeps count.

Read **ወንዛችን** — our river. Five years ago a diligent agricultural expert — a plant-science graduate — was posted to the kebele. She taught the village to lift the river's water for irrigation, and now they harvest twice and three times a year. The village gathers to honour her with ten thousand birr — though, the child narrator says, even this is too little for that diligence.

Expand the starter paragraph: below our house there is a year-round river; the neighbourhood children made vegetable beds and planted backyard vegetables… Your sentences water the beds the starter dug.

## Sub lesson 4 — Listening and reading — the water cycle, and the black stranger

Listen to **የቤት ሥራ** — the homework story, set inside a reading on the water cycle. Build the story web: beginning / middle / end. Learn the cycle words: plants and water bodies give off vapour — **ትነት**; the atmosphere and the ocean and lake vocabulary ride along.

Read **ጥቁር እንግዳ** — the black stranger. A couple are tormented by water shortage — a long walk to a spring that sometimes fails. They host a black stranger kindly, though they have no water even to offer him. He turns out to be a well-digger: together, in ten days, they dig an eight-cubit well and strike water. He refuses their gift: it is the return — አፀፋ — for your good deed.

Trace the rain-formation cycle web once more: vapour rises from plants and water bodies in the sun → clouds gather → rain falls → the water runs and soaks → vapour rises again. The loop never stops; learn to walk it in order.

## Sub lesson 5 — Speaking and writing — honouring water

Speak with evidence: support the claim — the honour we give water is small compared to the benefit it gives us. Evidence, not volume: name the benefits, name the carelessness, and let the two lists argue.

Compare and contrast water and soft drinks in two paragraphs, from the given table: sugar, colour chemicals, natural against man-made, expiry. One paragraph for what is the same and different at once — compare honestly, both ways.

Write a paragraph from the starter: a spring on a hillside that father fenced with wood, so cattle would not spoil it. A fence is a sentence about care; your paragraph says why.

Discuss in pairs: our water-use habit should encourage saving. Bring one habit from your own house as your evidence.
