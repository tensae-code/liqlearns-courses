# Measuring Your Reading Speed

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The timed passage — የዓለም የሙቀት መጨመር ተፅዕኖ

The Grade 8 fluency lesson prints a passage — 'The Impact of Global Warming' — with cumulative word counts running down its margin (6, 11, 17 … 136) and six attempt columns. The passage itself teaches while you time: the world's temperature rises on land and sea; evaporation increases and soil moisture is depleted, worsening drought; Arctic sea ice retreats and glaciers melt; sea level rises; species flee towards the poles; coastal people face floods, and low islands great danger. You read aloud for one timed minute and put a slash (/) at the word where the minute ends. The margin count at that line is your raw count.

## Sub lesson 2 — The scoring — speed, correct speed, comprehension

Now score it, exactly as the book's worked example does. The example student reached the line counted 102, but 4 words after her slash were unread — so her speed is 102 − 4 = 98 words per minute. She made 6 error marks, so her CORRECT reading is 98 − 6 = 92 words per minute. Her comprehension was 4 out of 5 questions — 80%. Three numbers, three different things: speed (how much you covered), correct speed (how much you covered cleanly), comprehension (how much you understood). A blank record form follows in the book for your own three numbers, attempt by attempt. Improving reading means lifting correct speed and comprehension together — never raw speed alone.

## Say it to your companion
