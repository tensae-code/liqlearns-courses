# Grade 4 Honest Trade

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ንግድ** — trade. The trade chapter: success through effort and honest scales, the licence that makes trade legal, the fraud that ruins a family, and a graduate who grows rich on chickens — plus the book's own list of the books behind it. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — the caravan trader

Listen to **ሲራራ ነጋዴ** — the caravan trader of former times. Grandfather returned home only after long absences, bringing goods from Addis Ababa. The listening asks: where did all that love and respect go? Trade once walked slowly and was honoured; hear what changed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading — success through effort, and the market words

Read **ስኬት በጥረት** — success through effort. A Debre Tabor couple have three children and nothing. A friend lends them money. At the **ጉልት** market he sells backyard vegetables — tomato, carrot, potato, onion, cabbage — and she retails spices. Their goods are fresh and of quality; they never cheat the scale — instead they are known for adding a blessing-measure, መመረቅ. She freely advises customers how to prepare shiro and berbere. Their daily sales and profit top the others', and now they plan to open a company.

Classify the market words in the concept web: backyard vegetables on one branch — **ካሮት, ድንች, ሽንኩርት, ጥቅል ጎመን** — spices on the other — **አብሽ, ድንብላል, ኮረሪማ, ነጭ አዝሙድ, ጥቁር አዝሙድ**. Sorting stock is a trader's first arithmetic.

## Sub lesson 3 — Law and trade, the ruined profit-seeker, and the idioms

Listen to **ሕግና ንግድ** — law and trade. Why a trade licence? Where does the money for schools, clinics and roads come from — taxes. Build the web: illegal trade — what it is, its harms — against legal trade — what it is, its benefits.

Read **አተርፍ ባይ አጉዳይ** — the profit-seeker is ruined. A wealthy, tax-paying, development-leading trader of fifty-five is befriended by a bad man who mocks his tax-paying as foolishness and tempts him with air trade — አየር ባየር. He sells all his stock, mortgages house and warehouse, hands over money without a receipt, then all the rest. The friend vanishes like the smell of water. The trader falls gravely ill, lies bedridden over a year; the mortgaged house and warehouse are sold; the family scatters; he ends in an unknown town as a street beggar. Greed's whole road, walked to its end.

Explain the idioms — ፈሊጣዊ አባባሎች: **የጧት ጤዛ** — morning dew: wealth that vanishes; **አየርባየር** — air trade: fraud; **ያልጋቁራኛ** — bedridden; **የዉኃሽታ** — the smell of water: vanished without trace; **ባዶእጅ** — empty-handed. An idiom is a story folded into two words.

## Sub lesson 4 — Listening and reading — the traders of two worlds, and the chicken trader

Listen to **የኹለት ዓለም ነጋዴዎች** — the traders of two worlds. Why do people prefer to queue at Ato Mesfin's shop? Is unfair profit useful? Why did the neighbours bear him a grudge? Two traders, two worlds — hear which world the queue chooses.

Read **ዶሮ ነጋዴው** — the chicken trader. A first-degree graduate in entrepreneurship never wasted time seeking a government job. He took out a trade licence in Mekane Iyesus town, buys chicks from a Bahir Dar hatchery, feeds them over a month and sells them for meat — a striking profit each round, seven rounds done. Now he has bought an incubator and is preparing to hatch his own. His friends, who scorned working with chickens, still sit idle.

## Sub lesson 5 — Writing and speaking — essays of trade, and the book's own sources

Write a complete three-paragraph essay: **ነጋዴ ብሆን** — if I were a trader. What would you sell, how would you treat customers, what would you never do? The chapter has shown you both roads; choose in writing.

Expand the starter notes into a three-paragraph essay: a country girl finishes school, rents a container in town, starts a jebena-coffee business, joins an **ዕቁብ** savings circle, buys a plot and builds a house. Notes are steps; the essay climbs them.

In pairs, write a two-paragraph composition on the things that make a trader legal — licence, taxes, honest scales, receipts.

Discuss: if someone said let's trade together and asked you for money, what would you do? The ruined trader's story is your evidence — receipts, patience, and questions first.

Write a two-paragraph essay on measures to control illegal traders.

Expand the final paragraph of ዶሮ ነጋዴው in your own words into two paragraphs — the graduate's next rounds, in your telling.

Finally, meet **ዋቢዎች** — this book's own references, the books and writers behind its lessons: Baye Yimam's የአማርኛ ሰዋስው (1987), Getahun Amare (1989), Kebede Mikael (1999), Zerihun Asfaw (1992), Marew Alemu (2005), the earlier Amharic-as-mother-tongue textbooks of 2006 and 2007, a 2019 writing on water as medicine, writings on the አዕምሮ newspaper, and TUBA Worldwide. Every lesson in this course stands on books like these — a book that names its sources teaches you to name yours.
