# Figures of Speech (ዘይቤ)

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — ዘይቤ — saying it stronger

The Grade 7 book defines ዘይቤ: deliberately departing from words' ordinary meaning, or from the ordinary order of arrangement, to give strength to expression. A figure presents a condition by making the meaning and combination of words out-of-the-ordinary and elevated, so the idea carries more — more information, more height. Plain speech informs; ዘይቤ makes the same thing strike. At this grade three kinds are taught, and the book then asks you to name the kind in given verses — in work poems, in mourning verses and in love poems alike.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three kinds — ተነፃፃሪ, ተለዋጭ, ሰውኛ

Kind 1 — ተነፃፃሪ ዘይቤ (comparison): formed by comparing two things, ideas, characters or actions: 'ልጁ እንደተቆረጠ ዛፍ ዥው ብሎ ወደቀ' — the boy fell wham, like a cut tree. Kind 2 — ተለዋጭ ዘይቤ (substitution): formed by giving one thing's character to another: 'ልጅቷ ፀሐይ ናት' — the girl is a sun; she does not resemble the sun, she IS it, in the verse. Kind 3 — ሰውኛ ዘይቤ (personification): formed by giving human characters, actions and abilities to non-human things: the idle worker's bed 'wept', squeaking all day and night. The test questions are always the same: what two things meet here? Is it 'like' (comparison), 'is' (substitution), or a thing acting human (personification)?

## Sub lesson 3 — Naming figures in real poems

The book practises figure-naming on real verses. In the mourning poems you classify the figure in six given verses, finding the ተነፃፃሪ and ሰውኛ verses among them. In the love poems you fill an analysis table on the model: ተረከዝ (heel) → 'ተረከዘ ሎሚ' — her heel is a lemon — built as ዘይቤ, kind ተለዋጭ, intended idea: praising the heel. Then you write a poem's message in prose, in order, and rebuild a scrambled poem by correcting its lines' order and punctuation — the set poem is on envy (ቅናት), the disease whose pain is worst. Naming the figure is not the end: always finish by saying what the figure DOES — what it praises, blames or makes felt.
