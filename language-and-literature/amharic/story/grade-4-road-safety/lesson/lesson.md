# Grade 4 Road Safety

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **የመንገድ ደህንነት** — road safety. The road safety chapter: walk on the left facing the cars, cross only at crossings, read the traffic lights, and never hurry — hurrying leads to death. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — the guest at our school, and opposite words

Listen to **የትምህርትቤታችን እንግዳ** — the guest at our school. The guest is a traffic police officer. Hear why we walk on the left side of the road, facing the cars: when you face the cars, you see them coming, and they see you.

Match the antonyms — words that stand opposite: **ብልህ** (clever) answers **ሞኝ** (foolish), **ጠርዝ** (edge) answers **መሀል** (middle), **መመረቅ** answers **መረገም**. An opposite is the word at the other end of the same line.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fluent reading — Mirtinesh, and my clever little sister

Read **ምርትነሽ** — Mirtinesh — twice, the fluent way. Mirtinesh is a Grade 4 student who fears car accidents, so she keeps every rule: she uses only the pedestrian way, crosses only at crossings, looks left and right, and never hurries — hurrying leads to death.

Read **ብልኋ እህቴ** — my clever little sister. Two boys build goals from stones on the road and play with a cloth ball. Little Smret, without a word, carries the ball away. The boys understand her silent teaching, and move their game into the compound, off the road.

Complete the starter: a blind man has waited long at the edge of a busy road, unable to cross. Your writing continues his story — who notices, who helps, and how a crossing is made safely.

## Sub lesson 3 — The traffic policeman, the lights, and Gizish

Build the concept web of the traffic laws — the rules of the road gathered around their centre, each rule on its own branch.

Attach the fitting suffix: **ቸኮለ + -ች = ቸኮለች**; **እግር** takes **-ኧኛ**; **መሻገር** takes **-ኢያ**. The suffix must fit the word's end — say the joined word aloud to check it.

Build the two-part web: obeying traffic rules on one side, breaking traffic rules on the other — what follows from each.

Listen to **የትራፊክ ፖሊስ** — the traffic policeman. Pedestrians cross when their light is green. Zebra lines mark where crossing belongs. Hear which groups are most at risk on the road — the young, the old, and those who cannot see well.

Build the traffic-light concept web: three lights, and what each one is for — red, stop; yellow, beware; green, go.

Read **ጊዜሽና አባቷ** — Gizish and her father — twice, the fluent way. Gizish walks to town with her father, the traffic policeman. At the junction he shows her the black-and-white zebra crossing and teaches: red for cars means green for pedestrians — when one side stops, the other side goes.

Judge true or false: cars may stop on zebra lines — false. A drunk driver who causes no accident has no problem — false. Drunk driving is a problem before any accident, not only after one.

## Sub lesson 4 — Reading — speed's outcome, the traffic light text, and the village children

Listen to **ፍጥነትና መዘዙ** — speed and its outcome. A family travels to Addis Ababa for a celebration. The father, drunk with joy, drives like a storm and ignores the family's pleas to slow down. Hold the title in mind: speed always has an outcome.

Read **የትራፊክ መብራት** — the traffic lights — twice, the fluent way. Three colours: red — stop! yellow — beware! green — go! When vehicles have green, pedestrians have red, and the other way round. The two sides take turns; that taking-turns is what keeps everyone alive.

Read **የሰፈሬ ልጆች** — the village children. Hearing festival music from a passing bus, the children dance eskista in the middle of the road. The driver stops, gathers them, warns and teaches them. They apologise, and he drives on. A road is for travelling, not for dancing — however sweet the music.

## Sub lesson 5 — Writing and word-building review

Write a short story: the rules you follow, in order, when you cross a car road — first, then, then, last. Order is the story's skeleton.

Split the suffixes, including double suffixes: **መንገደኞች** splits into **መንገድ - ኧኛ - ኦች** — one stem wearing two tails. Peel one tail at a time.

Complete the paragraph by inserting conjunctions: Tirngo, a country girl crossing a road in Dessie for the first time, falls — and is saved by an alert driver. The conjunctions carry the story from one moment to the next.

Complete the paragraph: the careful driver checks the road before driving and while driving. Care is a habit, not a moment.

Build the colour web: what red, yellow and green mean on the traffic light — and what the same colours mean on Ethiopia's flag. One colour, two homes.

Build new words by matching stems to suffixes — **-ኣማ, -ነት, -ኣዊ, -ኦሽ, -ኣም, -ኧኛ**: **ደርቅ + ኦሽ = ድርቆሽ**. Each suffix changes the word's work; say the new word in a sentence to prove it.
