# Writing About Stories — short answer

Answer in a few words, or one sentence.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What is ጭብጥ (chibt), the theme?
**Answer: the message the story carries (conveys).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** When you retell a story in writing, whose words must the retelling use?
**Answer: your own words — from a starter sentence, not copied from the book.**

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** What are the three order questions for any story?
**Answer: what is the beginning, what is the middle, what is the end?**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** How is the story's outcome named in the higher books' wording, and what does the name say?
**Answer: ልቀት/ውጤት — the resolution/outcome: ልቀት (the release) stands beside ውጤት, and the part comes when the problem is solved.**

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** What is ትልም/ሴራ, the plot?
**Answer: the cause-and-effect connection in the fiction or drama — each event happening because of the one before it.**

**6. (Sub lesson 6)** In the short-fiction chapter, what two things must a learner be able to do with short fiction itself, before the story work begins?
**Answer: state what short fiction is, and list its components.**

**7. (Sub lesson 7)** Fiction's characters are made up — so in what way is fiction not cut off from truth?
**Answer:** Its characters wear the behaviour of real people — they act as people truly act — inside a made world with its own place and time; and its theme can be true though its events are made.

**8. (Sub lesson 8)** Name the five marks that set short fiction and long fiction apart.
**Answer:** The number of stories/narrative lines (one vs main-and-sub lines), the number of characters (few vs many), the setting (narrow vs wide), the plots (single line vs main plot with sub-plots), and the conflicts (one line's trouble vs possibly more than two).

**9. (Sub lesson 9)** Say the four beats of reading እናቲቱ ("Enatitu") at full stretch, in order.
**Answer:** (1) Pre-reading pair discussion and prediction; (2) timed silent reading; (3) post-reading answers recorded in a table, in groups; (4) the groups report in turn.

**10. (Sub lesson 12)** What proverb does the accused contradict, and how?
**Answer:** 'For a fox there is no threshing-floor' — he answers that the fox enters a forty-cubit lunch, so the proverb fails.

**11. (Sub lesson 13)** Name two doors into addiction the set text names.
**Answer:** Any two of: imitating others; taking smoking/chat as signs of maturity and freedom; fear of losing addicted friends' love; not knowing the harm.
