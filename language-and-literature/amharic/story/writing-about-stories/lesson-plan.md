# Lesson plan — Writing About Stories

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Writing About Stories
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** writing about stories the Ethiopian way: name a story's shape (setting, characters, problem, actions, outcome — with plot and theme at the higher level), work inside a story frame, complete and retell stories in writing, and put events in their order — grown in Band 3 into the formal study of stories: the five parts re-named in the higher books' wording (ችግሮች, ልቀት/ውጤት), the literary elements (የሥነጽሑፍ አላባውያን) defined formally — characters, setting, conflict (ግጭት), theme, plot (ትልም/ሴራ) — and short fiction (አጭር ልቦለድ), what makes a short story.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Stories and Writing Sentences — the learner can hear and retell a story and write whole sentences; this lesson gives the shape their writing follows.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The story's shape** — መቼት setting, ገፀባሕርያት characters, ችግር problem, ድርጊት actions, ውጤት outcome — and at the Grade 4 level ሴራ plot and ጭብጥ theme join them.
2. **Frames, completion and retelling in writing** — the story frame (who / where / when / why / how), completing a started story, predicting and writing an ending, and retelling a story in your own words.
3. **Putting events in order** — sequencing: jumbled story sentences and events put back into the story's own order — beginning, middle, end.
4. **The five parts, named again higher up** — the Grade 5 naming: መቼት setting (time and place), ገፀባሕርያት characters, ችግሮች problems (the problem that pushes the characters to act), ድርጊት the action taken, ልቀት/ውጤት resolution/outcome (when the problem is solved); a story (ሚዛን, "The Scale") mapped onto the frame in an organiser.
5. **The literary elements, grown formal** — the Grade 7 note (የሥነጽሑፍ አላባውያን): characters (the persons in a fiction or drama text), setting (place and time), conflict ግጭት (the clash characters create with other characters or natural conditions), theme ጭብጥ (the essential matter/message), plot ትልም/ሴራ (the cause-and-effect connection); an elements organiser filled for a read text.
6. **Short fiction: what makes a short story (አጭር ልቦለድ)** (teacher guide — evidence for the chapter's aims and its read-aloud excerpt) — a complete fiction in a small compass carrying the same elements; the set work: state what short fiction is, list its components, listen and tell a similar story, list a short fiction's characters with their roles, summarise it in at most one page, join parts with transitional words (መሸጋገሪያ ቃላት).
7. **Fiction itself — what ልቦለድ is** (teacher guide — evidence) — the federal guides' definitions (Zerihun, 1996; Amare, 1976): a creative work from the author's own mind/heart, not cut off from truth; characters wearing real people's behaviour in a made world with its own place and time.
8. **Short fiction and long fiction** (teacher guide — evidence) — short: one story, few characters, narrow setting, a single narrative line. Long: many life-faces, many characters, wide setting, main and sub-plots, possibly more than two conflicts; learners write short texts and organise paragraphs into an essay.
9. **Fiction met whole — heard, read, and one whole book** (teacher guide — evidence) — ጠቃሚ ምክር heard (from Kebede Mikael's የእውቀት ብልጽታ); እናቲቱ "Enatitu" read at full stretch (predict, timed reading, answers in a group table); ሰመመን and ሸክም named for the shelf; and extensive reading (መጠነ ሰፊ ንባብ) — one whole short-fiction book over two weeks or more, tested at 10 or 15 marks.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Stories (story/first-stories) and Writing Sentences (sentence/writing-sentences). Recommended, never forced.
- First Paragraphs helps the retelling part: a written retelling is paragraphs around the story's shape.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five parts of a story's shape in Amharic, and the two that join at the higher level (ሴራ, ጭብጥ).
2. Answer a story frame in writing: who, where, when, why, how.
3. Complete a started story and write an ending of your own for a story that stops.
4. Retell a story in your own words from a starter sentence.
5. Put six jumbled story sentences back into the story's order.
6. Name the five parts in the higher books' wording — ችግሮች and ልቀት/ውጤት included — and map a story onto the frame.
7. Define the five literary elements formally — ግጭት and ትልም/ሴራ included — and fill an elements organiser for a read text.
8. Say what short fiction (አጭር ልቦለድ) is, list a short fiction's characters with their roles, and summarise a short fiction in at most one page.
9. Define fiction (ልቦለድ) in the guides' wording: a creative work from the author's mind/heart, not cut off from truth, its characters behaving like real people in a made world.
10. Tell short fiction from long fiction by the five marks — story lines, characters, setting, plots, conflicts — and prove a known fiction's size from them.
11. Run a fiction at full stretch: predict before reading, read timed and silent, record answers in a group table; and read one whole short-fiction book extensively, over two weeks or more.

## If the learner is stuck
- The retelling is a copy of the book's words → the books' rule is your own words from a starter; cover the book and tell it the way YOU say it.
- The shape feels like five hard words → each name asks one easy question: መቼት — where and when? ገፀባሕርያት — who? ችግር — what went wrong? ድርጊት — what did they do? ውጤት — how did it end?
- Ordering is guessed → look for the order words and the sense: what must happen before the ending can happen?
- ችግር and ግጭት get mixed up → the problem (ችግር/ችግሮች) is the trouble; the conflict (ግጭት) is the trouble pushing against someone — a character against a character, or characters against nature.
- Plot is retold as a list of events → rebuild it as a chain of *because*: cross out any event that nothing caused and nothing follows from, then re-join the rest with transitional words.
- The short-story summary runs long → the story's own size is the measure: at most one page; if it overflows, the retelling crept back in — cut to the elements.
