# Lesson plan — Writing About Stories

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Writing About Stories
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** writing about stories the Ethiopian way: name a story's shape (setting, characters, problem, actions, outcome — with plot and theme at the higher level), work inside a story frame, complete and retell stories in writing, and put events in their order.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Stories and Writing Sentences — the learner can hear and retell a story and write whole sentences; this lesson gives the shape their writing follows.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The story's shape** — መቼት setting, ገፀባሕርያት characters, ችግር problem, ድርጊት actions, ውጤት outcome — and at the Grade 4 level ሴራ plot and ጭብጥ theme join them.
2. **Frames, completion and retelling in writing** — the story frame (who / where / when / why / how), completing a started story, predicting and writing an ending, and retelling a story in your own words.
3. **Putting events in order** — sequencing: jumbled story sentences and events put back into the story's own order — beginning, middle, end.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Stories (story/first-stories) and Writing Sentences (sentence/writing-sentences). Recommended, never forced.
- First Paragraphs helps the retelling part: a written retelling is paragraphs around the story's shape.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five parts of a story's shape in Amharic, and the two that join at the higher level (ሴራ, ጭብጥ).
2. Answer a story frame in writing: who, where, when, why, how.
3. Complete a started story and write an ending of your own for a story that stops.
4. Retell a story in your own words from a starter sentence.
5. Put six jumbled story sentences back into the story's order.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A favourite story in mind.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner hears a tiny story told in the wrong order and must say what is wrong before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the shape, the frame work, and the ordering (lesson/lesson.md).

   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8517]: a picture of the farming year in its six ordered steps (እርሻ ይታረሳል → … → አዝመራ ይሰበሰባል) — the student reads the steps in order and says what comes after step 3.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice telling a short story and stopping before the end — the student says what they will write to finish it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — retell a story by its shape: setting, characters, problem, actions, outcome (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, matching, writing, ordering — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — frame, complete and order stories of your own (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- The retelling is a copy of the book's words → the books' rule is your own words from a starter; cover the book and tell it the way YOU say it.
- The shape feels like five hard words → each name asks one easy question: መቼት — where and when? ገፀባሕርያት — who? ችግር — what went wrong? ድርጊት — what did they do? ውጤት — how did it end?
- Ordering is guessed → look for the order words and the sense: what must happen before the ending can happen?
