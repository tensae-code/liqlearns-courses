# Poems and Songs — true or false

Say if the sentence is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In the flower poem's performance, the group has one leader and three receivers.
**Answer: True.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** The song says children should cross without looking, to be quick.
**Answer: False** — look ግራ ቀኝ ግራ, and cross when the vehicles stop.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** The poem ሮዛ is about Rosa, a river and a crocodile.
**Answer: True.**

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Hoya Hoye is sung house to house at Buhe, and the singers are rewarded with mulmul bread or gifts.
**Answer: True.**

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** ጠንቃቃ teaches that a careful pupil may play in the road.
**Answer: False** — never plays in the road; crosses only at the zebra.
**6. (Sub lesson 5)** Oral heritage can safely stay unwritten, since memories never fail.
**Answer: False** — the books warn it must be written down, lest it be lost.

**7. (Sub lesson 6)** The harvest work-poem in the guide is performed by the teacher first, and its refrain is "ሆ ይላለ ገበሬ".
**Answer: True** — the class joins after the teacher's performance.

**8. (Sub lesson 7)** ቀረርቶ is sung to please and comfort the enemy.
**Answer: False** — it is the heroism-boast that enrages and provokes the enemy.

**9. (Sub lesson 8)** Love poems in this lesson are transcribed in full so learners can memorise them.
**Answer: False** — they are known by their images only; no poem in this lesson is transcribed whole.

**10. (Sub lesson 9)** A ስንኝ is a verse — it takes a full pause.
**Answer: True** — and a ሐረግ is the part read in one breath.

**11. (Sub lesson 10)** While hearing ሀገሬ ዋርካ ናት, the learners take notes.
**Answer: True** — the poem is read by the teacher and heard with note-taking.

**12. (Sub lesson 11)** A poem's theme is called ጭብጥ, and analysing poems means naming their themes.
**Answer: True.**

**13. (Sub lesson 12)** The guide says the difference between poetry's words and prose words is best seen by explaining it, never by writing.
**Answer: False** — teachers are told to make learners write a poem, so the difference is seen in practice.

**14. (Sub lesson 12)** Learners write short poems of their own in this chapter, and organise and analyse a poem's idea.
**Answer: True.**

**15. (Sub lesson 17)** In the poem the Abay is reconciled with itself and its people.
**Answer: True**

**16. (Sub lesson 18)** Correcting a poem guards its rhyme and breath-lines as well as its spelling.
**Answer: True**

**17. (Sub lesson 19)** The refrain 'ዓባይ ዓባይ' repeats the river's name to press its greatness home.
**Answer: True**

**18. (Sub lesson 20)** መዘዝ in the song's refrain is its tribute — what the great river is owed and gives.
**Answer: True**

**19. (Sub lesson 21)** After judgement, offender and offended embraced and held a reconciliation feast (እርቅ).
**Answer: True**

**20. (Sub lesson 22)** ነጭለባሽ — 'the white-clad' — names the police in the poem's world.
**Answer: True**

**21. (Sub lesson 23)** Oral heritage must be written down lest it be distorted or lost — the reading's own conclusion.
**Answer: True**

**22. (Sub lesson 24)** The poem warns the country could melt like fat into desert if the forest is destroyed.
**Answer: True**

**23. (Sub lesson 25)** The grandmothers' long age is presented as the fruit of a childhood of service in uprightness.
**Answer: True**

**24. (Sub lesson 26)** The Grade 9 guide explains why sayings matter before learners explain the sayings' meanings.
**Answer: True**
