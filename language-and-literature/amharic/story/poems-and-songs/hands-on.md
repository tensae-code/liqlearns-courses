# Hands-on — Poems and Songs

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Perform the flower poem in fours
Form the guide's group: one leader (መሪ) and three receivers.
The leader gives the verse; the receivers answer. Perform
አበባ አየሁሽ for another group; then change leaders and go
again, so everyone leads once.

## Cross on the line
Mark a zebra crossing on the ground with chalk or a rope.
Sing ልጆች ተጠንቅቁ while you act it: walk on the left, stop at
the line, look ግራ ቀኝ ግራ with the whole head, and cross only
when your "traffic light" partner holds up the red card for
the cars.

## Clap the rhyme
Chant መናጩ ዋናጩ ቂናጩ with claps on the beat, faster and
slower, the way the book chants rhymes like music. Invent one
more rhyming line of your own in the same beat.

## Hoya Hoye at the door
In a small group, sing a Hoya Hoye visit the books' way: go to
a classroom door, sing, dance a little, receive the "house's"
thanks (a clap stands in for the mulmul bread), and end with a
blessing verse of your own making.

## Under the warka
Sit under the biggest tree near home or school. Each person
says one thing the tree gives — shade, rest, a meeting
place, fruit, a home for birds. That list is the wide idea;
now squeeze it into two lines of verse, the chapter's way:
much meaning, few words.

## Present and correct kindly
In pairs, present your poem የመማሪያ ክፍላችን aloud. Your
partner corrects one fault they truly heard — a stumbled
word, a dropped ending — the way the chapter corrects:
while it is presented, kindly, and once only. Then swap.

## If it is hard
Join only the refrain first (ግራ ቀኝ ግራ); clap where you
cannot yet chant; perform in the group, never alone, until the
piece is yours. The books build performance in groups for
exactly this reason.

## Added 2026-11-12 — sing and show
Sing ሆያ ሆዬ in tune with others; then show qererto (finger to ear, swaying) and fukera (staff raised and lowered) without words, and let others name the kind.
