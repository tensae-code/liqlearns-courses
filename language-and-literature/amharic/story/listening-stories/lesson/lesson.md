# Listening Stories

## Do it first

Close your eyes and only listen. Your teacher (or the speaker)
will read a story aloud — once. There is nothing to read: these
stories live in the ear. When it ends, tell it back — who was in
it, what happened, how it ended. That is the whole skill of this
lesson, and every story below runs the same way: **talk about
the picture first → hear the story → stop and guess at the
prediction question → hear the rest → answer the story's
questions → retell it in your own words.**

The prediction questions are printed in the guides like this:
***…?*** — when you hear one, the story stops. You guess. Then
the story goes on and you find out.

## Sub lesson 1 — Family at home

**Ato Nuradin and His Family.** Hear about Ato Nuradin
(አቶ ኑራዲን) and the family he lives with. The story's own
questions ask: what does Ato Nuradin look like, and what does
he do? Listen for the answers as the story is read — they are
in the hearing, not on a page.

[Picture 8310 — an Ethiopian family in their house]

**Our Family (የእኛ ቤተሰብ).** A child tells this one. The child
lives with their parents in one house, and in the evening —
ማታ ማታ — the parents tell stories, and the family is together.
Listen, then tell about your own family the same way: who lives
in your house, and what you do together in the evening.

## Sub lesson 2 — The house riddle and the carpenter

**Who Am I? (እኔ ማን ነኝ?)** This listening text is a riddle.
The speaker describes itself in parts: I have a place where
people sleep; a place where people eat; a place where guests
are received — and people live inside me. Guess as each part
is read. The answer is **ቤት** — a house.

**The Carpenter (አናጢው).** Samuel the carpenter makes the
furniture of a house — the story names his work and his tools
(መድረግ, መጋዝ, ማኅተም). On his way home he meets a friend who
needs help. Stop here — the story's prediction question:
***ሳሙኤል ምን የሚያደርግ ይመስላቸዋል?*** (What do you think Samuel
will do?) Guess — then hear it: Samuel stops and helps his
friend instead of hurrying past.

## Sub lesson 3 — School and friends

**The Late Student (አርፋጅ ተማሪ).** Selam and Abel are
neighbours who walk to school together. One day Abel is late:
the school gate is closed, he misses the first lesson, and he
quarrels with Selam. The prediction question: ***መምህራቸው ምን
የሚል ይመስላቸዋል?*** (What do you think their teacher will say?)
At the story's end, Abel apologises — ይቅርታ ጠይቃት.

**Two Friends (ሁለት ጓደኝሞች).** ንዱማ and ካሌብ play at break
time, and they make a contest: who can jump most? Two
prediction questions stop this story: ***ካሌብ ምን የሚሆን
ይመስላቸዋል?*** and ***ንዱማስ ምን የሚያደርግ ይመስላቸዋል?*** Hear
the end: Kaleab falls, the school health club gives first aid,
and the teacher's closing word is — take care of yourselves
while you play.

## Sub lesson 4 — Clothes, seasons and a park diary

**Clothes and What They Are Made From (ልብስና መስሪያዎች).**
Clothes are a basic human need — they protect the body from
cold and sun. First the prediction question: ***what do you
think clothes are made from?*** Then the answer, in three
families: cotton (ጥጥ) — ጋቢ, ነጠላ and shirts; leather (ቆዳ) —
ኮት (coat), jackets and ሱራ; wool (ሱፍ) — sweaters, coats and
jackets.

[Picture 8318 — cotton, leather and wool]

**Ethiopia and Her Seasons (ኢትዮጵያና ወቅቶቿ).** Ethiopia has
four seasons, and each holds its own months: ክረምት (the rains)
— ሰኔ, ሐምሌ, ነሐሴ, ጳጉሜ; መኸር (autumn) — መስከረም, ጥቅምት, ኅዳር;
በጋ (the dry season) — ታኅሣሥ, ጥር, የካቲት; በልግ (spring) —
መጋቢት, ሚያዝያ, ግንቦት. The story itself asks which months በጋ
and በልግ hold — listen, then answer from the picture.

[Picture 8313 — the four seasons and their months]

**Marta's Diary (የማርታ ማስታወሻ).** Marta tells her friend
Kamilat how she spent her break. For her good result, her
mother bought her clothes, shoes and a story book (የተረተ
መጽሐፍ). Her father took her to a special green place, and a
man there explained it: Hamle 19 Park (ሓምሌ 19 መናፈሻ) — made
in 1975 E.C., twenty hectares of local and foreign trees,
flowers and grass by the Qebena river, where people hold
weddings and birthdays. Its nickname is አረንጓዴው ገነት — the
Green Paradise. Retell the diary in order: the gifts, the trip,
the park, the nickname.

## Sub lesson 5 — Teeth, neighbours and a mother

**Abel's Teeth.** Abel and Aster are pupils in Addis Ababa.
Aster keeps her teeth clean; Abel does not. Abel falls ill
with tooth trouble and goes with his parents to the health
station (ጤና ጣቢያ). Mid-story, the question: ***የአቤል ሀመም
መንስኤ ምን ሊሆን ይችላል?*** (What could be the cause of Abel's
illness?) The doctor explains: the cause is not keeping his
teeth clean. The order: brush your teeth morning and evening
— ጠዋትና ማታ ጥርስህን መብረሽ አለብህ — or the tooth will be lost.
Abel recovers, and from then on he brushes properly.

[Picture 8314 — brush in the morning and in the evening]

**Good Neighbours (ጉርብትናና ጠቀሜታው).** Living together
strengthens people's social life when it rests on respect,
tolerance, helping and trust. Ato Amha and Woizero Hait,
neighbours in Lideta, Addis Ababa, are the models: he visits
the sick, helps the needy and reconciles quarrels; she shares
feasts and welcomes guests. When his daughter married and her
son graduated, the neighbours organised and celebrated
together.

**The Martyr Mother (ሰማዕታ እናት).** Hear this one gently; it
is a summary only. A mother once pulled her child out of a
burning house, wrapped in a wet cloth, and her own face was
marked by the fire. She raised the child alone, feeding him
from her work. Grown, the boy was ashamed of her marked face,
and when friends visited he called her "my servant". Later she
told him the truth: my face was spoiled because I pulled you
out of the fire — I was burnt instead of you. The guide asks:
***የልጁን እናት ስታቀላት በሚን ምክንያት ይሆን?*** (For what reason
might the son have introduced his mother so?) The son repents,
gathers his friends, and says: look — this is my martyr
mother; her scar was taken for me.

## Sub lesson 6 — Green areas and work shared at home

**What Green Areas Are (የአረንጓዴ ቦታዎች ምንነት እና ጠቀሜታ).** A
green area is land not covered by asphalt or stone: it lets
water into the soil and gives it back to the air. In a city,
green areas are public parks, roadside trees, riverbanks,
forests and grass-covered ground. They clean the air — taking
in the carbon from cars and factories — they cut noise, and
they give shade and places to rest. The listening questions:
***አረንጓዴ ሰፍራዎች የሚባሉት የተኞች ናቸው?*** (Which places are
called green areas?) and ***እጽዋት ከዚህ በተጨማሪ ምን ጠቀሜታ
አላቸው?*** (What more do plants do for us?)

[Picture 8315 — a green area]

**Siblings Who Share the Work (እህትማማችች).** Two siblings
whose parents have gone visiting relatives share the housework
between them — without quarrelling. They finish it together,
and when guests arrive they serve them properly. Helping each
other inside the home is this story's model.

**Whose Work Is Housework? (የስራ ክፍፋል).** A brother refuses
housework, calling it women's work. A neighbour woman teaches
him — through the school's gender club — the rule this story
ends on: work is shared by ability, not by gender.

## Sub lesson 7 — Friends true and false

**The Loyal Friend (ታማኝ ጓደኛ).** Ayalew's words travel
until they reach the king — and the king sentences him to
death. Hear the story once. Then hear what Ayalew asks for,
and what his friend does. The story's own questions ask what
we understand without being told, and whether the judgement
was just. Answer in your own words: what makes a friend
loyal — in good days only, or in the worst day too?

[Picture 8600 — a man pleading before the king]

**The Goat and the Leopard (ፍየልና ነብር).** Two unlike
animals travel together, and between them stands an oath —
ቃለመሐላ, a sworn promise. Hear what each one swears, and
listen for what the story says befalls the one who breaks
an oath. The plot is short; the oath is the story.

[Picture 8601 — the goat and the leopard travelling together]

**A Friend Is Like a Mirror (ጓደኛ እንደመስታወት).** Abdu
hates washing. His friend Seada — nicknamed የዉኃ እናት, the
mother of water — advises him, the way a mirror shows you
your own face. Hear how Abdu changes. Say the title's
meaning in your own sentence: what does a true friend show
you?

[Picture 8602 — washing at the basin, a friend smiling]

**The Town (ከተማዋ).** Hear about a town that nobody
cleans. The story's printed questions are the lesson:
who dirties it, who should clean it, and what a town
becomes when everyone waits for someone else.

## Sub lesson 8 — Ceremony, medicine and shared work

**The Qoti Betrothal Ceremony (የቆቲ ሥነሥርዓት).** Hear the
ceremony in its order: the Sultan, the elders (ሽማግሌዎች),
the gifts carried in, the አደስ incense burning, and the
foods of the ceremony. Retell it step by step — a ceremony
is a story whose steps must not change places.

[Audio — the ceremony account read aloud; retell the steps in order]

**Traditional Medicine (ባህላዊ ሕክምና).** Hear a discussion,
not a tale: the teacher Seyid and the student Siraj talk
about traditional medicine. Listen for the two sides —
where it helps, and where it can harm. The listening
teaches you to hold both sides of a debate in your head
at once. Say one point from each side.

**Who Does Not Work Shall Not Eat (ያልሠራ አይብላ).** A
listening about eating together and working together.
Hear the proverbs it carries — among them ድር ቢያብር አንበሳ
ያሥር: when many threads join, they can bind a lion. Say
what the title's rule means for a house, a class, a village.

**The Lion and the Hyenas (አንበሳና ጅብ).** Nine hyenas
together take one buffalo. One lion alone takes nine.
Hear the story of the division — who hunted, who claimed,
and how the shares were counted. Count with the story:
how many hunters, how many buffaloes, who ends with what?

[GIF 8606 — the division of the kill: one to the many, nine to the one]

## Sub lesson 9 — A wedding, a champion, love of country

**The Bride and Groom (ሙሽሮቹ).** This listening is a
description, not a tale: hear how the wedding dress is
described, word by word — the ጫጉላ ቤት, the wedding house,
and the ጥለት, the pattern on the bride's ነጠላ. Listen
twice: once for the picture, once for who organises what —
the groom's side or the bride's side. Draw the dress from
the words alone, then compare with a partner's drawing.

**He Was Honoured Because He Honoured (ተከብሮ ያስከበረ).**
Hear the life of the runner Abebe Bikila. The guide keeps
his whole biography: born at Jato on 30 Nehase 1925 E.C.;
at Rome he ran the marathon barefoot and won in 2:15:16.2;
at Tokyo in 1964 he won again, 2:12:11.2; he died in
1966 E.C. Listen for the dates and the times — a biography
is a story whose facts must stay exact. Say them back in
order.

[Picture 8603 — the barefoot runner crossing the finish line]

**Loving One's Country (ሀገር መውደድ).** Hear a story from
the war years, and hear how a ፉከራ — a hero-poem — is
performed inside it: the voice rises, the boast names the
deed. Listen for what the hero is praised for, and say
what loving one's country asks of a person in this story.

## Sub lesson 10 — Mountains, a writer, hard lives

**The Wonderful Mountains (ድንቅዬዎቹ ተራራዎች).** The heard
text itself is not printed for you — only its questions
are. Hear about high mountains where the walia ibex
lives, and the cry the listeners remember: "ፍሬን ያዝ! ፍሬን
ያዝ!" — Hold the fruit! Hold the fruit! Answer from what
you heard: what makes these mountains wonderful, and who
calls out, and why?

[Picture 8604 — a walia ibex on the cliff]

**Listening to a Writer's Life — Haddis Alemayehu.** Hear
the writer's life and works. While you listen, build a
timeline: his works set in their periods, one line for
each. A timeline is a story laid straight — when the
listening ends, read your line back in order.

**The Unsuspecting One Got Ensnared (ያልጠረጠረ ተመነጠረ).**
A hard story, told plainly: children were moved from
place to place and made to beg. The story's own words for
it: their childhood was stolen. Listen with care. Say
what was taken from the children, and what the story asks
us to watch for and refuse.

**Two Friends from School (teacher guide — evidence).**
The guide tells of two who were friends at school. One
comes home drunk. From there the friends' paths divide —
Hibist's path and Zemzem's path do not end at the same
place. Hear both paths to the end before you judge either.

**A Life Told by Its Owner (teacher guide — evidence).**
The guide reads a first-person life aloud: the narrator
won a school speaking competition, became vice-president
of the student association, and writes poems — one poem
of his begins «የማታ እንጀራ ስጠኝ». Only its first line is
given; the life is the listening. Say what the narrator
is proudest of, and how you know.

## Sub lesson 11 — Advice, a bird, and lives to learn from

**The Expert's Advice (የባለሙያ ምክር).** The farmer
Molalign hears an agricultural expert. While you listen,
fill a cause-and-effect organiser: the problem on one
side, its cause in the middle, the expert's counsel at
the end. Advice is only advice when the cause is named —
say the chain back whole.

**The Migrant Wane (ስደተኛዋ ዋኔ).** Hear the wane bird
talk with a herdsman. The wane travels far and returns;
the herdsman stays with his herd. Listen for what each
one knows that the other does not. Say one thing the
bird teaches, and one thing the herdsman teaches.

**Man Is for Work (ሰው ለስራ) (teacher guide — evidence).**
The guide reads a reflective piece aloud: a person is
for work — but the heart of it is አውቆ መስራት, knowing
how to work, not merely working. Listen for the
difference the piece makes between busy hands and a
knowing worker. Say the difference in one sentence.

**The Mother of Ethiopia's Mothers (teacher guide —
evidence).** Hear the biography of Dr Catherine Hamlin:
the fistula hospitals she built for injured mothers, the
"Right Livelihood" award, and the name she was given —
የኢትዮጵያ እናቶች እናት, the mother of Ethiopia's mothers.
Listen for what she gave, and for how long. Say why the
name fits her.

[Picture 8608 — a hospital ward where mothers are cared for]

**A Room, a Bed, a Thought (teacher guide — evidence).**
The guide reads the opening of a short story aloud: a
narrator lying on his bed in a one-room rented house,
asking himself «ረሃቡ ነው ወይስ ሀሳብ ያደክማል» — is it
hunger that tires me, or thought? Hear only the opening;
the rest is yours to wonder about. Say what the question
tells you about the narrator before anything happens.

## Sub lesson 12 — Stories the guides keep whole

**The Reading Children (የንባብ ልጆች) (teacher guide —
evidence).** A folktale of four kings. The Western king
raises a poor woman's son beside his own, the two boys
the same age. One day the boys slip away to hunt — and
are lost in the deep forest. The guide stops mid-tale
and asks: what happens next? Guess, and say why — then
hear the rest. (The tale is Alemayehu Wali Eshte's,
2012, adapted in the guide.)

[Picture 8605 — two boys lost in the deep forest]

**Environmental Cleanliness (የአካባቢ ንጽህና) (teacher
guide — evidence).** Hear why the ground around a home,
a school, a town must be kept clean — and what dirt
brings with it. Say the listening's chain: dirt, then
what, then what?

**Yoqa Be'e — the Dawro New Year (ዮክ ቤኤ) (teacher guide
— evidence).** Hear a New Year kept the Dawro way: on
New Year's Eve the household and the animals eat their
fill, and after, the youth go singing from house to
house. Listen for who eats, who sings, and in what order
the night runs.

[Picture 8607 — the New Year feast: everyone eats their fill]

**The Taxi and the Crossing (teacher guide — evidence).**
Menen, Saada and Asmayehu ride in a taxi. The taxi
swerves — a pedestrian is crossing at a place with no
crossing. Hear how the friends end the day: with a pledge
to carry traffic-safety awareness from their family, to
their school, to their community. Say the pledge's three
steps in order.

**The Cultural Dress (የሀገር ባህል ቀሚስ) (teacher guide —
evidence).** Hear three dresses told apart by their own
names: the ሽብሽባ ቀሚስ, the ጉርድ ቀሚስ and the ጉንፋ ቀሚስ.
Listen twice — once for the names, once for what makes
each one itself. Say which is which, from the words alone.

**City Farming (teacher guide — evidence).** Hear about
young people in Addis Ababa who began as day labourers
and took up የከተማ ግብርና — city farming. They save their
surplus in a bank, and they teach their neighbours. Say
the story's steps: work, grow, save, teach.

## Sub lesson 13 — Traditional mediation: the Afar law and the five steps

**The Afar Traditional Legal System (teacher guide —
evidence).** Hear how the Afar people order their law. The
law's own name is **መድዓ** (med'a). Two ideas hold it up:
**ድግ** (dig) — mutual tolerance and support — and **አድሊ**
(adli) — justice and balance. And it stands in levels: the
sultanate, the clan, and the youth. Before you listen, talk
about the traditional law of your own area; while you listen,
take notes; if a part is not understood, hear it again.

[Picture 8950 — elders seated between two parties under a tree]

**Traditional Mediation — the reading heard as listening
(teacher guide — evidence).** Mediation has its own aim and
its own principles: equality, fairness, balance,
truthfulness, humanity. And it runs in **five steps**, in
this order:

1. a preliminary inquiry — finding out what happened;
2. obtaining the two sides' consent to reconcile;
3. choosing mediators for both sides;
4. choosing the mediation judge;
5. conducting the mediation itself.

At the end, the parties swear an oath confirming the
decision to reconcile. Say the five steps back in order —
a mediation out of order is no mediation. Then act it: the
way this is learned is by enacting it — choose a mediation
custom you know, cast the parts, and perform it.

[GIF 8997 — the five steps of mediation lighting in order, the two sides drawing closer]

[Audio — the Afar law account and the mediation reading read aloud; retell the law's name, its two ideas, and the five steps]

## Sub lesson 14 — A captain, and tools that let every learner take part

**The First Ethiopian Woman Captain (የመጀመሪያዋ ኢትዮጵያዊት ሴት
ካፒቴን) (teacher guide — evidence).** The title is written up
before you hear a word. First, questions aloud: what do you
already know? Then hear the life of the first Ethiopian
woman to captain an aeroplane — and hold its keyed number:
**4,475 flying hours** in her career. After listening,
answer first in your group, then to the class. Say why her
story stands in the Gender chapter: what it proves about
who may do great work.

**Women's Participation in Education (የሴቶች የትምህርት ተሳትፎ)
(teacher guide — evidence).** Hear a passage that works
through the world's commitments on girls' and women's
education, up to the year 2015. Take notes as you listen.
Say the listening's road in one sentence: what was promised
for girls' schooling, step by step, up to 2015?

**The Assistive Devices (teacher guide — evidence).** Now
hear a table — a device, who uses it, and what it does.
Learn it whole; it is a complete teaching in itself:

- **ብሬል** (Braille) — raised dots read by touch, for blind learners;
- **ኬን** (white cane) — felt ahead on the ground, for blind walkers;
- Braille paper and **ስታይለስ** (stylus) — the sheet and the point that writes the dots;
- **ተሸከርካሪ ወንበር** (wheelchair) — for learners whose legs cannot carry them;
- **ክራንች** (crutches) — support under the arms for walking;
- **የእይታ መነፅር** (eyeglasses) — for eyes that see partly;
- **ማዳመጫ መሳሪያ** (hearing aid) — for ears that hear partly.

[Picture 8952 — the assistive devices board: Braille, cane, stylus, wheelchair, crutches, glasses, hearing aid, Braille paper]

Say each device with its user and its work — device, who,
what it does — until the table is yours.

## Sub lesson 15 — A satellite, a sickness, and the dry land

**Ethiopia's First Satellite (teacher guide — evidence).**
Hear the text twice. The satellite is **ETRSS-1** — 70
kilograms, built with Chinese support, and its ground
station stands at **Entoto**. Listen for what it serves:
agriculture, mining, environmental protection, climate
monitoring and mapping. If pictures of it can be shown,
look — then listen again and check the five services
against what you heard.

[Picture 8951 — ETRSS-1 above the land, its signals reaching the Entoto ground station]

**Malaria (የወባ በሽታ) (teacher guide — evidence).** Before
listening, talk: what do you know of malaria — or of another
sickness that passes from person to person where you live?
Then hear the text and take notes. Hold its facts in order:
two parasites carry it — *Plasmodium falciparum*, the
dangerous one, and *P. vivax*, which hides in the body and
returns. The female Anopheles mosquito carries them; the
sickness runs through the liver and the red blood cells.
The text's counted figures (the World Health Organization,
2013) are 241 million cases and 627,000 deaths — and
four-fifths of the deaths were children under five. And
hear the prevention whole: sleep under an insecticide-
treated net with no holes, tucked in over the bed; spray
inside the house; screen windows and doors; and clear

**Let Us Prevent Desertification (teacher guide —

forests has brought climate change home — floods, the soil

questions are set on the talk; answer them from what you
heard. Say the talk's chain back: the trees fall — then

## Sub lesson 16 — Tax, and a patriot

**Tax: Willingness and Duty (የግብር ውዴታና ግዴታ) (teacher
guide — evidence).** Take notes while you listen. The
text's argument, as its answers key it: many citizens see
tax only as an obligation laid on them — not as building
their own country. That way of seeing, the text says, must
change. Say the argument in one sentence, and one thing
built with public money that you yourself use.

**Colonel Abdisa Aga (ኮሎኔል አብዲሳ አጋ) (teacher guide —
evidence).** Hear a patriot's biography read aloud. Your
work is to draw detailed information out of the hearing:
who he was, what he did, in what order. Then two exercises
the biography carries: define words from the text and build
sentences with them, and match causes to their effects.
A biography heard well is a life you can retell in order —
retell his.

## Sub lesson 17 — Listening well: the seven strategies

Hear this chapter's teaching on listening itself — the
seven strategies of effective listening (ውጤታማ የማዳመጥ
ብልሃቶች) (teacher guide — evidence):

1. **Decide to listen** — listening is a choice; make it.
2. **Drop your own agenda and prejudice** — set them down before the speaker starts.
3. **Be curious** about how others see the world — ask open questions.
4. **Keep eye contact**, and read the speaker's visual cues.
5. **Hear the whole message before answering** — never interrupt.
6. **Be patient** while a speaker finds their words.
7. **Respect the right** to express a differing idea.

Practise them on the very next story you hear in this
lesson: choose one strategy before it starts, and say
afterwards what it changed.

## Sub lesson 18 — Hard roads: addiction and migration

**The Consequences of Narcotic Drugs (የአደንዛዥ ዕጾች መዘዝ)
(teacher guide — evidence).** Before listening, predict
from the title alone: what will this text say? Then hear it
— read two, up to three times, because hearers come to it
with different strengths in the language. Hold what its
answers key: the health-care costs of use and the work
users lose; and, when use spreads, pressure on safety and
order — crime growing where drugs grow. Say the text's
costs in order: to the body, to the purse, to the town.

**Migration and Its Suffering (ስደትና መከራው) (teacher guide
— evidence).** Take notes while you listen to an account of
migration and the suffering inside it. When it ends, give
its main points and its detailed points apart — what the
account is about, and what exactly it says. Say one
suffering it names, and what caused it, from the hearing
alone.
