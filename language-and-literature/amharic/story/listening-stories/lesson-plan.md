# Lesson plan — Listening Stories

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Listening Stories
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a story can be understood by listening alone — heard once, guessed at the prediction question, retold in order, and answered about.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Stories (hear it, say it, act it) and on the Sentence unit — the learner already retells short stories; here the stories are longer, are never printed for the learner, and carry the guides' own prediction questions (***…?***). Every plot here comes from a teacher guide's printed listening text — the student books print only the title, a picture and questions for these stories.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Family at home** — Ato Nuradin and his family, and a child who tells about their own family in the evening-story house.
2. **The house riddle and the carpenter** — the riddle-story እኔ ማን ነኝ? (Who Am I?) whose answer is ቤት, a house; and Samuel the carpenter, who stops to help a friend on his way home.
3. **School and friends** — Selam and Abel, neighbours who walk to school together, and the day Abel is late; and ንዱማ and ካሌብ, whose jumping contest ends with first aid.
4. **Clothes, seasons and a park diary** — what clothes are made from; Ethiopia's four seasons and their months; and Marta's diary of her trip to Hamle 19 Park, the Green Paradise.
5. **Teeth, neighbours and a mother** — Abel, whose teeth hurt because he did not brush; the model neighbours of Lideta; and the Martyr Mother — told gently, as a summary only.
6. **Green areas and work shared at home** — what a green area is and why it matters; siblings who share the housework without quarrelling; and a brother who learns that housework is not women's work.
7. **Friends true and false** — the loyal friend before the king; the goat and the leopard's oath; Abdu and Seada, the mirror friend; and the town nobody cleans.
8. **Ceremony, medicine and shared work** — the Qoti betrothal ceremony in its steps; Seyid and Siraj debate traditional medicine; who does not work shall not eat; and the lion and the hyenas divide the kill.
9. **A wedding, a champion, love of country** — the bride and groom described word by word; Abebe Bikila's biography with its dates and times exact; and a war-years story with its hero-poem performed.
10. **Mountains, a writer, hard lives** — the wonderful mountains and the walia ibex; Haddis Alemayehu's life laid on a timeline; children made to beg; and two guide stories — school friends divided, and a life told by its owner.
11. **Advice, a bird, and lives to learn from** — Molalign and the expert's cause-and-effect counsel; the wane bird and the herdsman; and three guide listenings: Man Is for Work, Dr Catherine Hamlin, and a short story's opening question.
12. **Stories the guides keep whole** — the folktale of the four kings and the boys lost hunting; environmental cleanliness; the Dawro New Year; Saada's taxi and the crossing pledge; the three cultural dresses; and the city farmers of Addis Ababa.
13. **Traditional mediation — the Afar law and the five steps** (teacher guide — evidence) — the Afar law መድዓ with its ideas ድግ and አድሊ and its levels; and mediation's principles and five steps, ending in the parties' oath.
14. **A captain, and tools that let every learner take part** (teacher guide — evidence) — the first Ethiopian woman captain and her 4,475 flying hours; women's participation in education up to 2015; and the assistive-devices table — device, who uses it, what it does.
15. **A satellite, a sickness, and the dry land** (teacher guide — evidence) — ETRSS-1 (70 kg, Entoto) and its five services; malaria's two parasites, its carrier, its counted figures and its prevention; and the talk against desertification.
16. **Tax, and a patriot** (teacher guide — evidence) — tax as willingness and duty, not obligation only; and the patriot biography of Colonel Abdisa Aga, heard for its details and its cause-and-effect pairs.
17. **Listening well — the seven strategies** (teacher guide — evidence) — decide to listen; drop agenda and prejudice; be curious; keep eye contact; hear the whole message; be patient; respect a differing idea.
18. **Hard roads — addiction and migration** (teacher guide — evidence) — the consequences of narcotic drugs (health-care costs, lost work, pressure on safety); and migration and its suffering, noted while listening.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Stories — a listening story is the
  same hear-it pass, longer. Recommended, never forced.
- The Fidel and Word units help with the stories' own words
  (ቤተሰብ, ቤት, ወቅት), but no earlier lesson gates this one.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Hear a story once and retell it in order — who, where, what happened, how it ends.
2. Guess at a story's prediction question before hearing the rest, and say why they guessed it.
3. Answer Ato Nuradin's questions: what he looks like and what he does.
4. Solve the house riddle እኔ ማን ነኝ? from its clues — the answer is ቤት, a house.
5. Name what clothes are made from: cotton, leather and wool — and one thing made from each.
6. Match Ethiopia's four seasons to their months (ክረምት, መኸር, በጋ, በልግ).
7. Say why Abel's teeth hurt and what the doctor ordered: brush morning and evening.
8. Say what good neighbours do — visit the sick, help the needy, celebrate together.
9. Say who shares housework: everyone in the house, by ability — not girls only.
10. Retell The Loyal Friend and say what its questions ask about justice.
11. Say what an oath (ቃለመሐላ) binds, from The Goat and the Leopard.
12. Retell the Qoti betrothal ceremony in its steps, in order.
13. Hold both sides of the traditional-medicine debate — one point each side.
14. Count the lion and the hyenas' division: nine hunters, ten buffaloes, who ends with what.
15. Say Abebe Bikila's biography facts in order — born at Jato, Rome barefoot, Tokyo, with the times.
16. Build a timeline of a writer's works by period while listening.
17. Fill a cause-and-effect organiser from The Expert's Advice.
18. Stop mid-tale at the folktale's prediction point, guess, and say why.
19. Name the Afar law (መድዓ), its two holding ideas (ድግ, አድሊ), and say mediation's five steps in order.
20. Say the first woman captain's keyed career number — 4,475 flying hours — and work the assistive-devices table: device, who uses it, what it does.
21. Name ETRSS-1's five services, malaria's two parasites and carrier, and the four preventions.
22. State the tax text's argument in one sentence, and retell Colonel Abdisa Aga's biography in order.
23. List the seven strategies of effective listening and say what one of them changed in your own hearing.
24. Say the drug text's costs in order — body, purse, town — and give one main and one detailed point from the migration account.

## What you need
Ears and a quiet corner. Paper and a pencil for the Do It Yourself retelling notes. The pictures above.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lost the thread of a story → do not re-play the whole story;
  ask the three handles: Who is in it? What happened? How does
  it end?
- Cannot guess at the prediction pause → give two choices from
  the story's own world ("Will Samuel hurry home, or stop to
  help?") and let them pick and say why.
- The Martyr Mother upsets a learner → stay with its ending:
  the son learns the truth and honours his mother. Never press
  a learner to retell it aloud; a written retelling counts.
