# Grade 4 Helping Each Other

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **መረዳዳት** — helping each other. The helping chapter: thread joins thread and becomes strong — harvesting together, caring for a grandmother, a needle and a thread, and help that asks for nothing back. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — when thread joins thread, and words that partner

Listen to **ደር ቢያብር** — when thread joins thread. One thread breaks alone; many threads together hold. People without money can still help: hands, time and kindness are also wealth. Build the concept web: who needs support — the weak, the elderly, and others — and the kinds of support there are.

Join word partnerships — words that mean something together: **የገንዘብ ድጋፍ** — money support; **የቤት እድሳት** — house repair. Some words only do their full work holding another word's hand.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fluent reading — the harvest together, and Tesfaye

Read **አዝመራን በህብረት** — harvesting our crop together — twice, the fluent way. The whole neighbourhood brings sickles at dawn. Mother and child carry food and water and reap alongside. Both fields are finished in a day — alone, the work would take a week.

Read **ህጻኑ ተስፋዬ** — the child Tesfaye. Tesfaye is thirteen, clever and well-behaved. He sweeps, washes his own plate, and cares for his blind grandmother of eighty-eight: he washes her feet and her light clothes and guides her wherever she goes. She blesses him from her heart.

Choose the fitting meanings: **እጦት** means shortage; **ይሻሉ** means they need. A word's fitting meaning is the one its sentence asks for.

## Sub lesson 3 — Stories — the needle and the thread, the dove and the kite, being an example

Read **መርፌና ክር** — the needle and the thread — twice, the fluent way. The needle boasts to the thread: you are always the follower. The thread answers back. Then Ato Libs — Mr Garment — reconciles them: only together do you mend my tears. Neither tool works alone; that is the tale's heart.

Read **ርግብና ጭልፊት** — the dove and the kite. Wind blows the dove's nest down. Her neighbour the kite takes her into her cave, and sharing the housework, they live happily. Follow the story-flow web: beginning action → next action → final outcome.

Read **አርኣያነት** — being an example — twice, the fluent way. Two boys nicknamed the rowdies, failing and unhelpful, are befriended by well-behaved peers. They copy the example, and little by little they reform: their grades rise, and they begin helping parents and neighbours. Example teaches where scolding fails.

## Sub lesson 4 — Writing — helping paragraphs and helping stories

Complete the starter: today my uncle's house is being built. Two men are on the roof; others saw, carry mud-plaster, and nail the four corners; by nine o'clock the house wears its corrugated roof. Your sentences carry the building to its end.

Write a paragraph: how could you help a child who has lost his home? Real help, in small true steps — not wishes.

Write a paragraph on how the students of one class can help each other — in lessons, in play, and when one is absent or ill.

Compose a two-paragraph story on the given title: **ወንዝ የሞላበት ነጋዴና ዋናተኛው** — the merchant when the river was full, and the swimmer. The title gives you the people and the problem; the story is yours.

Complete the starter tale: the poet who had no voice and the azmari who could not compose lived by lending each other their gifts — until an envious man… Your ending decides what envy does, and what the two friends do back.

## Sub lesson 5 — Review — plurals, suffix words, and migration

Make plurals with **-ኦች** and **-ዎች**: ዶሮ, በግ, ተማሪ, ወፍ, ሰው — each word takes its fitting tail. Then give the contextual meanings of **በረከት** (blessing), **ምርቃት**, **አያት** (grandmother) and **አዋቂ** as this chapter uses them.

Find words that continue with the suffixes **-ኢያ, -ኧኛ, -ኣዊ, -ኣቸው, -ኣም** — hunt real words wearing each tail, and read them whole.

Match stems to suffixes and write the joined words: **ሀብት -ኣማ**, **ጤና -ኧት**, **ደርቅ -ኣም**. The joined word must be a word you can use in a sentence.

In pairs, complete a paragraph on **ስደት** — migration: leaving family and area for a foreign country. Write truly: what is left, what is hoped for, what it costs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **መረዳዳት** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
