# Hands-on — Reading Stories

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Read it twice, out loud
Take the shortest story and read it aloud to a partner. Then
read it again — smoother, without stopping at every word. Your
partner marks where the second reading flowed better. That is
the books' fluent-reading practice, done with real voices.

## Act the climax
With a partner, act the Rooster and the Fox's last moment: one
is the fox, holding (not grabbing) the other; the fox opens
their mouth wide to shout — and the rooster hops free and runs
to the "shepherds" (two more friends). Swap roles.

## Plant like the champions
The seedling story's class planted and cared for seedlings. If
you can, plant one seed in a tin or a corner of ground, water
it morning and evening like the environment club's rota, and
tell its story in three sentences after a week.

## Story handles on paper
For Pumpkin and Potato or the vine and the trunk, draw the two
characters facing each other and write one sentence each says,
in your own words — what each one boasts or complains about.

## If it is hard
Hear the story read aloud once before reading it yourself;
read in pairs, taking turns by paragraph; keep the pictures in
front of you while you retell. Small steps, in the books' own
order — hear it, read it together, read it alone.
