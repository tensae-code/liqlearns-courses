# Reading Stories

## Do it first

Read the first story below — or hear it read at a child's pace
— then close the lesson and retell it: who, what happened, how
it ends. In this lesson the story is **on the page**: you read
it, you read it again until it flows, and you answer from the
page. Read it the books' way: do not count letters one by one,
do not turn back, pause only where a phrase or a sentence ends.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kirubel and Fikirte

Kirubel (ኪሩቤል) and Fikirte (ፍቅርተ) are brother and sister in
one family. Fikirte is blind (አይነስወር). Kirubel plays with
her. Kirubel walks to school with her. Kirubel goes shopping
with her. A brother who plays, walks and shops beside his
sister — that is the whole story, and it is a story about
kindness you can see.

[Picture 8316 — Kirubel walking with Fikirte to school]

Retell it by its handles: Kirubel — brother. Fikirte — sister,
blind. Play — walk to school — shop.

## Sub lesson 2 — Our School Environment Club

Grade 1 pupils — the reading names them — run their school's
environmental care club (የትምህርት ቤታችን የአካባቢ እንክብካቤ ክበብ).
The club waters the school's plants morning and evening, by
rota, and keeps the compound clean. The school director takes
the list of the members' names. The reading's own marked
question stops here: ***ርዕሰ መምህሩ የክበቡን አባላት ስም ዝርዝር
የወሰዱት ስለምን ይመስላቸዋል?*** (Why do you think the director
took the club members' name list?) Answer it in your own words
— then finish the reading.

## Sub lesson 4 — Protect Our Environment

A fluent-reading text (አካባቢያችን እንጠብቅ) — read it repeatedly
until it flows, the way the guide runs it: individuals stand
and read aloud to their classmates. Its main idea is in its
title: let us protect our environment — the place we live,
learn and play. Retell that main idea in your own words when
your reading is done.

## Sub lesson 6 — Fluency stories and fables

The student books' own fluent-reading stories, told here as
summaries — read each one in its book, twice, the fluency way.

**Seedling Planting (ችግኝ ተከላ).** On seedling-planting day,
teachers and pupils meet at school. The narrator's group —
champions at football — are sure they will also win the
planting competition.

**Success (ስኬት).** The class consults their farming teacher,
prepares seedlings — mango, mandarin, orange and olive —
plants them and cares for them. The land is covered with
plants, and the class is nominated for a prize.

**The Vine and the Trunk (የወይንና የግንድ ጠብ).** The vine scorns
the trunk that holds it up and demands its freedom. The trunk
answers: you cannot stand alone. Then it leaves the vine in
peace. A fable about scorning the one who supports you.

**Pumpkin and Potato (ዱባና ድንች).** Pumpkin and Potato meet
at market and tease each other about their looks — with the
proverb of the frog that burst trying to match the ox. In the
continuation, the two friends fall out, each despising how the
other grows — and Potato is sold first.

**The Rooster and the Fox (አውራ ዶሮና ቀበሮ).** The fox
flatters the rooster — "sing for me!" — seizes him and runs.
Shepherds chase. The rooster tells the fox to shout "this
rooster is not yours!" — the fox opens his mouth, and the
rooster escapes to the shepherds. The fable's own moral: the
rooster saved himself by using the fox's own trick. Its
prediction question stands mid-story: ***will the rooster
escape the fox?***

[GIF 8330 — the fox opens his mouth and the rooster escapes]

**More fables on the same shelf.** The Clever Fox (ብልጡ ቀበሮ)
judges a stolen cheese and nibbles both shares away "to make
them equal". The fox flatters the crow (ቀበሮና ቁራ) until the
crow opens its beak to sing and the meat falls. The monkey
rides the crocodile's back (ዝንጀሮና የአፕል ፍሬ) and escapes by
claiming her heart is still on the apple tree. Read each one
twice, and tell what the trick was.

## Sub lesson 7 — Wisdom, fear and measure

**The Wise Girl (ብልኋ ልጅ).** Read the story twice — first
for the story, then for fluency. A father tells his two
children to "build houses". One child builds with stone;
the other builds friends. At the end, the friend-maker is
honoured above the stone-builder. Say the story's meaning
in one sentence: what house lasts longest?

[Picture 8610 — one child building, one greeting friends on the road]

**Fear (ፍርሃት).** Fasikaw meets a lion — and plays dead.
Read how fear thinks fast, and what the story teaches at
its end: do not befriend a person who betrays you in a
day of trouble. Retell the meeting in order: what Fasikaw
saw, what he did, what the lion did.

**A Dream — Or Not? (ሕልም እልም).** Tirngo dreams of
migrating far away. She wakes — relieved — and vows to
stay in school. Read the dream and the waking apart:
which parts belong to the dream, and which to the morning
after? Say the vow in your own words.

**The Scale (ሚዛን).** Senait sells butter. Zebiba pays
for it with bread — and Senait weighs the butter against
Zebiba's own bread on the scale, measure for measure.
Read what happens to their friendship, and how an apology
mends it. Say the story's rule: how you measure others
is how you will be measured.

[Picture 8611 — a balance scale: butter against bread]

## Sub lesson 8 — Sickness, salt and enough

**Kedir (ከድር).** Kedir secretly takes his brother's
medicines, meaning no harm. He falls ill — and only at
the hospital is the cause found out. Read in order: the
taking, the sickness, the discovery. Say what the story
warns every reader about medicines that are not theirs.

**Salt (ጨው).** A father asks his daughters how much
they love him. The youngest answers: "You are salt." The
father is angry — until the cook is told to serve his
food without salt. Read the meal that teaches him. Say
why salt was the truest answer of all.

**Enough — Let It Stop With Me (በእኔ ይብቃ).** At Fasika,
Amsal eats and eats. The sickness that follows teaches
what the doctor orders: break a fast gradually, a little
at a time. Read Amsal's feast and his lesson. Say the
doctor's rule as the story gives it.

**A Father's Trust (የአባት አደራ).** Sisay returns and
finds his village emptied by drought. He does not leave
again: he becomes an environmental-protection
professional in his own home village. Read what he
found, and what he built his life on. Say what the
father's trust asked of him.

**The Blind Champion Rider (ልበብርሃኑ ሽምጥጋላቢ).** Addis
Sewnet cannot see — and wins the 600-metre Agew
horse-gugs race at Injibara, on his horse Shasho, who
knows his voice. Read the race twice: once for the
winning, once for how a rider and a horse trust each
other.

[Picture 8614 — the blind champion on his horse]

## Sub lesson 9 — Clothes, blessing and dreams

**"Feed My Clothes!" (ልብሴን ኖር በሉት).** A guest comes in
rags, and nobody honours him. He returns in fine clothes,
and the feast is his. So he feeds the food to his
clothes: "It was for them you made it." Read the two
visits side by side. Say what the story mocks — and what
it honours instead.

[Picture 8612 — the guest feeding his fine clothes at the feast]

**Blessing and Curse (ምርቃንና ርግማን).** King Solomon
tests a bee and a fly. Ordered to kill her queen, the
bee refuses — and is blessed. The fly obeys — and is
cursed. Read the two answers carefully. Say why refusal
was blessed and obedience cursed, in this story's world.

**When a Dream Is Answered (ህልም ሲፈታ).** Three students
walk 12 kilometres to school, day after day. Years on,
Asefa and Tirngo stand as international athletes, and
Andarge as a language teacher. Read the walking and the
arriving together. Say what the long road built in them.

**The Lion and the Three Oxen.** This fable hides inside
a reading about the intangible attractions — it opens
the old way: "ተረት ተረት? — የመሠረት!" Three oxen plough
and live together, and no lion dares touch them. Divided,
they fall one by one. Read the fable, then say its rule
in one sentence.

[Picture 8617 — three oxen together, the lion watching]

## Sub lesson 10 — Family, hatred and refusal

**Family and Addiction (ቤተሰብና ሱስ).** Ato Altaye
drinks, and his family carries the cost. One day his
wife and children walk into his own tavern. Read what he
sees, and what he understands at last. Retell it gently
and in order — the story is about a fault seen clearly,
and a man who owns it.

**One Does Not Choose One's Lineage (ዘር አይመረጥም).**
Mohammed and Zeyneb's family lives where hatred is
preached against their kind. Read how the village
council ends it: the village resolves to stand together
against hate. Say the title's meaning, and the council's
answer, in your own words.

**I Will Not Give In (እጅ አልሰጥም).** Be'emnet is promised
in early marriage. She flees, studies law, and founds a
free legal-aid centre for women. Read her road in order:
the flight, the study, the centre. Say what she refused,
and what she built instead of refusing only.

## Sub lesson 11 — Birds, regret and blighted hope

**The Bird's Song (የወፏ ዝማሬ).** A bird builds her nest
by the river. Three times it is destroyed. Read what
the birds decide at last: to live together,
cooperatively. Say why the fourth building stands in
the story's teaching, when the first three fell.

[Picture 8613 — the bird building her nest beside the river]

**What Regret Bore (ፀፀት የወለደው).** A man who emigrated
returns — and founds an elderly-care centre in his own
parents' compound. Read what he regrets, and what his
regret builds. Say the title backwards: regret is the
parent; what is the child?

**Friendship and Volunteering (ጓደኛነትና በጎፈቃደኛነት).**
Mansur and Admasu's volunteers clean two holy grounds:
the Meskel Demera site, and the Eid prayer place. Read
who cleans, where, and why both places. Say what the
story teaches about friendship that works with its hands.

**Blighted Hope (የመከነ ተስፋ).** A young woman goes to
Jeddah as a domestic worker, full of hope. She returns
home empty-handed. Read her going and her returning
together. Say what was blighted — and what the story
asks readers to weigh before they go.

**The Desert Journey (የበረሃው ጉዞ).** Travellers follow
brokers across the desert. Read to the day the news of
the deaths arrives — and the whole town falls silent.
Read this story slowly. Say what its silence teaches
that words could not.

## Sub lesson 12 — Trust, debt and rough hands

**Trustworthiness Is for Oneself (መታመን ለራስ ነው).**
Cleaning, Asrebeb finds dropped money. For two hours he
guards it, until its owner comes — then he returns it.
Read the finding, the guarding and the returning. Say
the title's meaning: who profits from being trustworthy?

**You Shall Not Go Mad Without Paying Me (ሳትከፍለኝ
አታብዳትም).** A man who has eaten public money feigns
madness to escape judgement. The examination finds him
sane — and the judgement falls twofold. Read the trick
and its unmasking. Say why the title is spoken like a
promise.

**Maru Forsa (ማሩ ፎርሳ), Parts 1 and 2.** Maru loads
sacks at a factory. When his child falls ill, the
workers' association loan pays for the treatment. Later,
the workers elect Maru chairman. Read both parts in
order. Say what the association was for Maru before it
was anything else — and what his chairmanship repays.

[Picture 8615 — Maru carrying a wheat sack into the factory store]

**Rough Hands (ሸካራ እጆች).** A graduate washes his
mother's hands — the rough hands of a washerwoman who
washed clothes for others all her life. In the washing
he learns three lessons; afterwards he is hired as a
manager. Read the lessons as the story gives them. Say
what rough hands can teach that smooth ones cannot.

**The Secret of the Bat's Hiding (የሌሊትወፍ የመደበቅ
ምስጢር).** In the war of the birds and the beasts, the
bat joins whichever side is winning. Both sides drive
him off at last — and the hen counsels peace. Read the
bat's two choices. Say why neither side keeps him.

**May I Be One Who Repays a Debt (ብድር መላሽ ያርገኝ).**
Elephants are caught in a hunters' net. The rats come
and gnaw the ropes through, strand by strand, until the
elephants stand free. Read who owed whom, and how the
debt was paid. Say the title's wish in your own words.

[GIF 8616 — the rats gnawing the net until the elephants stand free]


## Sub lesson 13 — Grade 1: pots on the road, and good advice

Read these aloud, slowly and well. Stop at each full stop.

- ሸክላድስትና ብረትድስት — "The clay pot and the iron pot" (Grade 1,
  pp.80–81). The two pots travel together and are frightened by the
  cars on the road. A rooster advises them: keep to the left edge,
  and cross the road only at the zebra crossing.

[Picture 21202 — cross at the zebra crossing]

- ጩኸት ሰሙ — "They heard a shout" (p.84). The pots cross at the
  zebra, then walk on the right side by mistake. A car comes. The
  crowd shouts; the iron pot runs, but the clay pot falls and
  shatters. The right side is the dangerous side — that is the
  story's own lesson.
- የትራፊክ ፖሊሱ ምክር — "The traffic policeman's advice"
  (pp.87–88). Never play on the road: a car's brake can fail all of
  a sudden. Play only on the field.

## Sub lesson 14 — Grade 1: the children of our neighbourhood (a story in four parts)

This story comes in four parts, one after the other, the way a
long story is read across days. At the end of each part, stop and
guess what comes next.

[Picture 21201 — the children of our neighbourhood]

- Part 1, የሰፈራችን ልጆች — "The children of our neighbourhood"
  (p.91). Ayele loves climbing trees. The children go to eat shola
  fruit; Ayele climbs — then screams in fright. What did he see?
  Stop here and guess.
- Part 2, ልጆችዬ እናምልጥ! — "Children, let's flee!" (p.92). Ayele
  slides down, stammering, and runs. The others run after him
  without knowing why.
- Part 3, ፈራን — "We were afraid" (pp.94–95). They reach Ayele's
  house and find him fallen on the bed. Now they can ask him what
  he saw.
- Part 4, እንኳን አላያችሁት! — "Good thing you didn't see it!"
  (pp.95–96). Ayele saw a huge python coiled on the shola tree,
  and escaped without being seen. The children decide: never go
  to the bush without a grown-up. Match the book's words as you
  read: ጫካ (the bush), መገረም, ተጠመጠመ.

## Sub lesson 15 — Grade 1: more fluent stories and fables

- የጦጢት ዳኝነት — "The monkey's judgement" (p.99). Two foxes quarrel
  over a loaf of bread. The monkey "judge" nibbles the bigger side
  to make the scales balance — again and again, until she has
  eaten the whole loaf. The foxes go home hungry.
- የምግቦች ስብሰባ — "The foods' meeting" (p.103). The foods that
  protect us, the foods that build the body and the foods that
  give energy live in different neighbourhoods, and each holds its
  Sunday meeting about how it helps people.
- ምርጫው — "The election" (p.107). The three food groups elect
  their members: protecting foods — pepper, lemon, orange,
  mandarin, olive; building foods — meat, egg, milk, beans, peas;
  energy foods — maize, banana, potato, sugarcane, wheat. Rivalry
  breaks out, quarrelling follows — and the lemon mediates.
- ስስታሙ ውሻ — "The greedy dog" (pp.107–108). A dog carrying a bone
  sees its reflection in the river, barks to snatch the "other"
  bone — and drops its own. It is sorry. Tell the ending in brief,
  in your own words.
- ብልጡ ዔሊ — "The clever tortoise" (p.110). A hungry crocodile and
  a tortoise meet. The tortoise proposes drawing lots for any food
  they find — claiming the lots always fall to him.
- ምንጫችን — "Our spring" (pp.114–115). A clear spring comes down
  from the mountain. It keeps the village children clean, and they
  swim in it; all week they long for Saturday and Sunday.
- የቡሄ ዕለት — "Buhe day" (p.118). On the Buhe festival day the
  children receive much bread. They go to the swollen river and
  its waterfall, wash their hands and eat — and agree together:
  going alone to the river is wrong.
- ጎሹ! — "Goshu!" (p.122). The buffalo Goshu hates washing and
  rolls in the dirt. A sharp stone wounds his dirty back and the
  wound festers. The doctor says the filth caused it, gives a
  little medicine, and orders him washed.
- የአህያና የበሬ ጓደኝነት — "The donkey and the ox's friendship"
  (p.126). Each animal starves while the other eats. They meet,
  admit their quarrelling harms them both, and plan to take turns.
- ጥጋበኞቹ — "The greedy ones" (p.131). Ato Degu's donkey and ox
  grow greedy and idle. Their owner sees through them, rages, and
  sells the ox. The donkey grieves.
- በሚናቸው ተከራከሩ — "They argued about their roles" (p.134).
  The pack animals — horse, mule, donkey, camel — each claims to
  serve people best. The ox, the goat and the sheep are made
  judges. Their judgement is read in Reading Passages.

## Sub lesson 16 — Grade 2: washed shirts, sly mice and rainy nights

- የአክሊሉ ነጭ ሸሚዝ — "Aklilu's white shirt" (Grade 2, p.2). His
  sister washes the birthday clothes, and the new black coat dyes
  the white shirt in the basin. His mother and sister soak it
  again, with bleach (በረኪና) and Omo, until it is white again.
- ህልም እልም — "Pipe dream" (pp.2–3). A farmer lets his daughter sell
  a pot of milk and buy shoes, a shirt and a dress. Daydreaming of
  tomorrow's finery, she tosses her head — the pot falls, breaks,
  and the milk spills. Afterwards, match the clothing words to
  their pictures, the book's way.
- ተንኮለኛዋ አይጥ — "The sly mouse" (p.7). A mouse hides in the
  wardrobe and nibbles towel, trousers, gabi, shirt, coat and
  dresses. The owner sets a trap — and as he is about to kill her,
  the cat snatches her.
- ታጥቦ ጭቃ — "Washed — mud!" (pp.11–12). A washerman takes carpets,
  blankets, curtains and clothes to the river, and loads the wet
  wash on his donkey. At midnight a thief gathers the hung
  clothes; the donkey brays, the thief flees — and the dog slept
  through it all. The owner mutters the saying: washed mud —
  work wasted.
- ዙሪያክብ ጨዋታ — "The circle game" (p.17). Neighbourhood children
  play a circle game: the referee calls a weekday name — "ሰኞ"
  stand, "ማክሰኞ" sit, "ረቡዕ" embrace. Zewditu wins. Say the days
  of the week in order before you read, and listen for them as
  you read.
- በውድቅት ሌሊት — "In the dead of night" (p.21). Heavy rain.
  Bezabih's wife wakes him: flood water has filled the house.
  Saying the proverb "ሠርገኛ መጣ በርበሬ ቀንጥሱ", they go out into the
  rain and dig a drainage channel.
- ወይ አለማስተዋል! — "Oh, not noticing!" (p.27). A traveller rests
  under a shola tree. He sees its tiny fruit high above, and a
  huge pumpkin on a low vine below, and questions how things were
  made. A ripe shola fruit falls on his nose — and he blesses the
  making: the pumpkin does not hang overhead.

## Sub lesson 17 — Grade 2: judges, patience and balanced food

- መንገደኛውና ተኩላ — "The traveller and the jackal" (pp.31–32).
  The traveller frees a jackal tied to a tree. "I will eat you,"
  says the jackal. The monkey judge orders the scene played
  again, from the start — and the jackal is tied again:
  "ዱላ ከእጅህ! ተኩላ ከፊትህ!"
- ብልኋ ሴት — "The clever woman" (p.36). A wife, troubled by her
  husband, is told by her grandmother to bring a lion's gofer
  (ጎፈር — the tuft of hair). Day by day she feeds the lion — from
  far, then nearer — until it is tame enough to follow her home.
  Patience is the cure.
- የተዛባ ፍርድ — "The crooked judgement" (pp.39–40). Lion, leopard,
  hyena and donkey agree to confess their sins and be judged.
  Each meat-eater's prey "had no one to let it out" — acquitted.
  But the donkey only nibbled grass while loaded, listening to
  its master talk — "you brought the calamity!" They fall on her
  and eat her. (The book names its source: Fekade Azaze.)
- ወይ አለማወቅ! — "Oh, ignorance!" (p.49). Parents bring their
  two-year-old to the doctor: thin hair, a swollen belly, bleeding
  gums. The doctor finds the cause — no balanced food — and
  orders milk, minced meat, egg, honey, grains, vegetables and
  fruit. The mother repents.
- የሐና አመጋገብ — "Hana's eating" (pp.53–54). The science teacher
  teaches balanced eating. Hana goes home resolved: "from today I
  won't say I like this and not that — and I won't trouble you."

## Sub lesson 18 — Grade 2: dialogues — read them in two voices

Some of these readings are dialogues. Read them with a partner,
one voice each.

- በቁማችን ሞተናል! — "We died standing!" (p.59). Ato Agmasie suffers;
  his neighbour Ato Ayalew sees the green water and smells the
  waste around them: "we have killed ourselves with our own
  hands!" Tomorrow he will call the community and declare a
  cleaning campaign.
- ታሞ ከመማቀቅ… — "Before sickness… take care" (p.63). A cat and a
  dog talk. The cat buries its waste in a dug hole; the dog fouls
  anywhere. The proverb speaks: ታሞ ከመማቀቅ አስቀድሞ መጠንቀቅ — take
  care before falling sick and groaning. The dog promises to
  change.
- መተባበር ለመልካም ተግባር — "Cooperation for a good deed"
  (pp.63–64). A phone conversation: Yeshwas stayed from work to
  organise the neighbours; they cleaned the drainage channels
  and burned the dry waste in a dug pit. After reading, list the
  things that pollute, the book's way.
- ሁሉም ቢተጋገዝ — "If everyone helped each other" (pp.67–68). In
  the rains, people throw their stored waste into the drainage
  tubes. The flood, blocked, covers the road with floating bags,
  old clothes and papers. Ashamed, the people take spades and
  clean the channels and the road.
- እረኛውና ዋሽንቱ — "The shepherd and his washint flute" (p.73).
  The shepherd plays his flute over the herd, singing. An ox
  strays into the maize; he throws the flute at it — and the ox
  steps on it and breaks it. His friend comforts him: "there is
  shenbeko (ሸንበቆ reed) in our yard — let's go and make one."
  Use the book's instrument words: ዋሽንት ይነፋል (the washint is
  blown); ክራር, ማሲንቆ, አታሞ, ደወል each do their own work.
- ይቅርታ ጠይቂ! — "Apologise!" (p.88). Elsa takes her sister
  Elhana's shoes without asking; they fight over them. Their
  father orders: "Elsa, apologise to your sister! Don't touch
  what isn't yours without permission." She apologises; they
  embrace and kiss.
- መስጠትና መቀበል — "Giving and receiving" (pp.88–89). Zebiba asks,
  "does a hen lay eggs?" Natnael laughs, full of himself. Eden
  answers — a hen may lay, or may not — and Zebiba teaches him:
  learn not only to give knowledge, but to receive it.
- ታላላቆቻችሁን አክብሩ — "Respect your elders" (p.93). An old man
  rests again and again under his load. Nejim and Hana run to
  him, take his load and walk with him — remembering their
  Amharic teacher's words.
- ውሸታሙ እረኛ — "The lying shepherd" (p.99). The hired shepherd
  twice cries "Fox! Fox!" as a joke; the villagers run for
  nothing. The third time, nobody comes — and the foxes eat many
  sheep. The owner sends him away for his bad behaviour.

## Sub lesson 19 — Grade 2: competitions, telephones and kind animals

- የምግብ ዝግጅት ውድድር — "The food-preparation competition"
  (p.110). Alemnesh cooks meat wat in a clay pot and vegetables
  in an iron pot, but has no second clay pot for her shiro. Her
  rival teases her — then: "trouble creates cleverness." She
  makes the shiro in a frying pan, laughing.
- የፈሰሰ ዉኃ… — "The spilt water…" (pp.110–111). A feast table is
  loaded: doro wat, lamb tibs, kitfo with butter, ayib, ergo,
  tela and tej. The hostess waits for late guests. A cat darts
  past; her son Seyfu runs in with a stick — and the ergo is
  overturned on the table.
- አሸናፊ ማነው? — "Who is the winner?" (p.114). Cow, sheep, goat
  and cabbage argue their usefulness before the ox judge: the
  cow's meat, milk and butter; the sheep's meat for tibs and
  qiqil; the goat's healthy meat from browsing shrubs; the
  cabbage: "all of you rest on vegetables and fruit as your
  base." The judge rules: "you are all right — there is no
  winner and no loser."
- የወንድሜ ስልክ — "My brother's telephone" (p.120). The brother
  passes the grade-12 leaving exam and is assigned to Gondar
  University. The family in the country buys two phones: the
  phone takes photos, receives and sends news, and plays music
  and films. The family is photographed with it before he leaves.
- የመጀመሪያዋ ቀን — "Her first day" (pp.120–121). A country girl
  comes to the city at night with her uncle. The city shines with
  electric lights, and her uncle names the electric things:
  phone, television, radio, washing machine, water filter,
  cooker, fridge, kettle, camera.
- ዓይነሥውሩ ተማሪና ስልኩ — "The blind student and his phone"
  (p.125). A boarding-school pupil who cannot see is given a
  tape recorder and lesson cassettes, and is trained; he notes
  in Braille (ብሬል). Later he has a phone with the JAWS
  application (ጃውስ): he dials by touch, and the phone speaks
  aloud the names or numbers of callers it has saved.
- ሲሮጡ የታጠቁት ሲሮጡ ይፈታል — "What is buckled running comes undone
  running" (pp.125–126). Abdi's haste has broken his watch, his
  calculator and his phone. Today, rushing to the football
  field, he drops his mother's phone: screen broken, battery
  holder ruined — and the repair shops are shut. "Come
  tomorrow."
- የተሳሳተ የስልክ ጥሪ — "The wrong-number call" (pp.129–130). A
  comic dialogue: the caller scolds; the receiver apologises;
  then the service messages speak — "the subscriber cannot be
  reached… out of service…" Checking the number he dialled, the
  caller falls silent.
- ነብርና ድኩላ — "The leopard and the dik-dik" (pp.135–136). The
  two swear to live together. The dik-dik grows fat and glossy;
  the leopard longs to break the oath, and asks, "what if an
  oath is broken?" — "It falls on the child." As the leopard
  settles on its back, the dik-dik's horn pierces its belly.
  Dying, the leopard protests; the dik-dik answers: "who knows
  if your father broke an oath before you?" (The book names its
  source: Kebede Michael, ታሪክና ምሳሌ book 2.)
- ጉንዳንና ዋኔ — "The ant and the wane bird" (p.140). The ant,
  swept away in a stream, is saved when the bird drops it a
  leaf to float on. Later the bird is caught in a boy's trap;
  the ant bites the boy's foot hard — startled, he drops the
  rope, and the bird escapes.

## Say it to your companion

Pick one story. Retell it in order — beginning, middle, end.
Your companion listens for the story structure and names the
three parts back to you. Then your companion retells a
different story, and you listen for its three parts.
