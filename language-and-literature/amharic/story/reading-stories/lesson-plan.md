# Lesson plan — Reading Stories

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Reading Stories
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a printed story can be read fluently — read twice, understood, and retold from the page.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Listening Stories and First Stories: the learner already hears and retells; here the story is on the page, read and re-read the books' fluent-reading way (አቅላጥፎ ማንበብ), and answered from the text.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Kirubel and Fikirte** — two siblings in one family; Fikirte is blind, and Kirubel plays with her, walks to school with her and shops with her.
2. **Our School Environment Club** — Grade 1 pupils run the school's environment club — watering plants morning and evening by rota and keeping the compound clean.
3. **Holiday Clothes** — the fluent-reading text of the clothes unit, read and re-read until it flows, with weaker readers timed on the chart.
4. **Protect Our Environment** — the fluent-reading text learners stand and read aloud to classmates — caring for the surroundings we live in.
5. **Eyoel and Amira** — two children who care for their surroundings; read in turns, its main idea then written in the reader's own sentences.
6. **Fluency stories and fables** — the student books' own fluent-reading stories and fables: seedling planting, success, the vine and the trunk, Pumpkin and Potato, and the Rooster and the Fox.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Listening Stories — the same stories'
  shape, heard before they are read. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read a story twice — first for the story, then for fluency — without counting letters one by one.
2. Retell Kirubel and Fikirte: who they are, Fikirte's blindness, and Kirubel's three helps.
3. Say what the school environment club does and why the director took the members' name list.
4. Retell the Rooster and the Fox, including its climax: the fox opens his mouth and the rooster escapes.
5. Tell the vine and the trunk's quarrel and what the trunk answers.
6. Write a story's main idea in one sentence of your own.

## What you need
The lesson page. A partner to read in turns with. Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reading stalls word by word → the books' own fluency note:
  do not count letters one by one, do not turn back, pause only
  at phrase and sentence ends; read the story again whole.
- A retelling invents new events → come back to the page:
  "Show me where the story says that."
- The fable's trick is missed → act the climax with GIF 8330:
  the mouth opens, the bird is gone. The trick is the story.
