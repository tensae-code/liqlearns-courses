# Grade 3 Neighbours

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ጉርብትና** — neighbourliness. Neighbours are the people whose fields touch yours and whose children eat at your table. This chapter teaches patience between neighbours, help given and returned, and what a bad neighbour — in the animal tales — does to everyone. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about neighbourliness in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening to neighbours

Listen to **ከመቸኮል ማስተዋል** — learning from hurrying. It is a fire-and-water fable: patience beats haste. In the tale, the flood filling the pit is what saves the ox. Listen for the moment hurrying fails and patience works.

Listen to **ዶሮ ለምን ትጭራለች?** — Why does the hen cackle? It is a neighbours' dispute tale. Retell it in sequence afterwards — first, then, then — and judge fairly: whose fault was the quarrel?

Listen to **የሰፈር ልጆች** — the neighbourhood children. One child questions and one answers, and the answerer's famous answer is: the wind took me. Listen for how the question-and-answer game is played.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fluent reading — neighbour love, and a peace broken

Read **የጎረቤት ፍቅር** — Neighbour Love — twice, fluently. Ato Aynalem and Ato Anbaye are farmer neighbours whose fields adjoin. Their friendship is so close that they promise to marry their children to each other, to seal it for the next generation.

Read **ከስህተት አለመማር** — Not Learning from a Mistake. The cat and the mouse agree to live in peace. The cat breaks the peace — then imitates a dog's bark to lure the mouse out of her hole. Read it smoothly; feel where the peace breaks.

## Sub lesson 3 — Story readings — the fox, and the proud elephant

Read **ያልጠረጠረ ተመነጠረ** — the one who did not suspect was wiped out. The fox, envious of the hyena's many cattle, smears blood on the sleeping hyena's mouth while stolen meat is the evidence. The lion believes the false sign and kills the hyena — and the fox takes all the cattle. Read for the trick, step by step.

Read **ትዕቢተኛው ዝሆን** — The Proud Elephant. The elephant smashes the weaver-bird's eggs. Her neighbours combine against him: the tsetse fly buzzes, the woodpecker blinds, the frog croaks from a deep pit — the elephant leaps in after the sound and is badly hurt. Small neighbours together can answer a proud giant.

## Sub lesson 4 — Neighbour words and speaking

Practise the antonym pattern: **የዋህ** (gentle/innocent) answers to **ተንኮለኛ** (sly/harmful). Say the pair aloud; opposites are learned as pairs, hand in hand.

Group words by their meaning relations — families like **መፋቀር / መዋደድ / አይለያዩም**: loving one another, being loved, never separating. Words that live together in meaning are sorted together.

Build a concept web with three kinds of branches: words that mean the same, words that mean the opposite, and unexpected words that surprise you in the set. The web holds all three kinds at once.

Speak in groups with concept webs: one group calls words, the other group answers with the opposites. The web is in front of you; the speaking is the game.

Work the role table with chicken words — **ጭልፊት** kite (the bird), **ጫጩት** chick, **ክንፍ** wing. In your group one is the word-caller, one the sentence-maker, one the corrector. Each role has its own job.

## Sub lesson 5 — Writing and grammar review — neighbours

Write four sentences on helping and respecting each other — what neighbours do when they live well together.

Write three sentences criticising the fox of ያልጠረጠረ ተመነጠረ. Say what he did, why it was wrong, and what it cost the hyena.

Retell the elephant story on a concept web: the bird and her neighbours in the middle branches, their plot against the elephant growing out of it. The web first — then the retelling follows the branches.

Write a short paragraph criticising the elephant: his pride, the smashed eggs, and the end his pride brought him.

Find words that grow with the **-ኣት** suffix: **አባል → አባላት** (member → members). Hunt the set for every word that takes -ኣት.

Split a word into smaller real words: inside **ብርቅ** you can find **ቅርብ, ብር, ቅር, ቅብ**. A big word is a house with small words living in it.

Write the plural forms of the given words — one becomes many, and the ending does the work.

Review the plurals whole: split the **-ኦች** suffix off words; make plurals with **-ኣት**; make plurals with **እነ-** in front of names. Three tools, three kinds of plural.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **ጉርብትና** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
