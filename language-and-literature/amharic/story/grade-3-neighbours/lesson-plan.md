# Lesson plan — Grade 3 Neighbours

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Grade 3 Neighbours
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Neighbours are the people whose fields touch yours and whose children eat at your table. This chapter teaches patience between neighbours, help given and returned, and what a bad neighbour — in the animal tales — does to everyone.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 3 learner's fluent reading and listening: the texts are read twice the fluent way, listened to from the teacher's voice, and answered from what was read and heard.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order:

1. **Listening to neighbours** — Listen to **ከመቸኮል ማስተዋል** — learning from hurrying.
2. **Fluent reading — neighbour love, and a peace broken** — Read **የጎረቤት ፍቅር** — Neighbour Love — twice, fluently.
3. **Story readings — the fox, and the proud elephant** — Read **ያልጠረጠረ ተመነጠረ** — the one who did not suspect was wiped out.
4. **Neighbour words and speaking** — Practise the antonym pattern: **የዋህ** (gentle/innocent) answers to **ተንኮለኛ** (sly/harmful).
5. **Writing and grammar review — neighbours** — Write four sentences on helping and respecting each other — what neighbours do when they live well together.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- A story will not retell → go back to its sequence: first, then, last — three steps carry every tale in this lesson.
- A word set will not stick → say it in a pair, use it in a sentence, then write it: ear, mouth, hand.
- A writing task feels big → the web or table first, sentences after: the book's own order in every chapter.
