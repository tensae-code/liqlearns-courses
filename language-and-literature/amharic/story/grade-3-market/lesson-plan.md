# Lesson plan — Grade 3 Market

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Grade 3 Market
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Market day has its own name, its own smells and its own manners. This chapter walks the gult market and the modern hall, learns the spice row by name, and practises buying and selling politely.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 3 learner's fluent reading and listening: the texts are read twice the fluent way, listened to from the teacher's voice, and answered from what was read and heard.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order:

1. **Listening at the market** — Listen to **የእማማ ኩሪ የጉልት ገበያ** — Emama Kuri's vegetable (ጉልት) market.
2. **Reading the market — the hall, a picture story, the spice row** — Read **ገበያ** — The Market.
3. **Market words — goods, doers, double meanings** — Build the merchandise concept web: which goods count as **ሸቀጣሸቀጥ** — merchandise.
4. **Market writing and speaking** — Write a reasoned paragraph: which do you prefer — traditional or modern clothing? A reasoned paragraph gives its reasons, not only its choice.
5. **Market grammar review — and where this book's stories came from** — Split word and suffix in the review set: **ገበየች → ገበየ - ች**, and እነአለሚቱ, ጆኒያው, ትህትናዋ with theirs.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Stories and Listening Stories — this lesson's Grade 3 texts are read and heard the same way, on the chapter theme of the market. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Retell this chapter's listening and reading texts in sequence, in their own words.
2. Use this chapter's word sets in sentences and in role play.
3. Do this chapter's writing tasks — sentences, paragraphs, webs and tables — with correct punctuation.
4. Do this chapter's grammar review work (suffixes, plurals and word-kinds as the chapter names them).

## If the learner is stuck
- A story will not retell → go back to its sequence: first, then, last — three steps carry every tale in this lesson.
- A word set will not stick → say it in a pair, use it in a sentence, then write it: ear, mouth, hand.
- A writing task feels big → the web or table first, sentences after: the book's own order in every chapter.
