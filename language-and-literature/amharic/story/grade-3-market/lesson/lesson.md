# Grade 3 Market

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ገበያ** — the market. Market day has its own name, its own smells and its own manners. This chapter walks the gult market and the modern hall, learns the spice row by name, and practises buying and selling politely. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about the market in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening at the market

Listen to **የእማማ ኩሪ የጉልት ገበያ** — Emama Kuri's vegetable (ጉልት) market. As you listen, learn why markets are named by their weekday: **ሰኞ ገበያ** Monday market, **ረቡዕ ገበያ** Wednesday market, **ቅዳሜ ገበያ** Saturday market. The name is the calendar.

Listen to **የገበያዎች ወግ** — Market Customs: traditional markets against modern markets, and the saying **ሰው ወርቅ አያደምቅ** carried in the talk.

Listen to **የገበያ ጭውውት** — Market Chat. Halima wants a dress sewn. An appointment — ቀጠሮ — is made… and not kept. Listen for how the chat handles a broken appointment.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the market — the hall, a picture story, the spice row

Read **ገበያ** — The Market. A market is where goods are bought and sold. Open-field markets meet on one weekday and are named by that day. Modern market halls — አዳራሽ — hold clothes, electronics, grain and spice shops and juice houses, and trade all week long.

Read **ግብይት** — Shopping — as a picture story: six buyer–seller exchanges in a shop. Match each dialogue to its picture. At the end the buyer leaves without the school bag — because the seller is rude, distracted, and finally closing. Manners are part of trade.

Read **ቅመም ተራ** — The Spice Row — fluently. A mother points out each spice in its sack: **ጥቁር አዝሙድ, ቁንዶ በርበሬ, ዝንጅብል, ኮረሪማ, ቅርንፉድ** — cloves, for tea. The berbere's price has 'reached the sky', so they buy 2 kilos instead of 5.

## Sub lesson 3 — Market words — goods, doers, double meanings

Build the merchandise concept web: which goods count as **ሸቀጣሸቀጥ** — merchandise. ሳሙና soap is the yes-example; ጤፍ is the sorting task — decide, and say why.

Build doer-nouns from verbs, on the ሻጭ seller set: **ገዢ** buyer, **ጸሐፊ** writer, **ዳኛ** judge, **መጋቢ, ሟች, ሯጭ**. The market is full of doers; each name tells the doing.

Make sentences showing both meanings of words that carry more than one: **አይገኝም** can mean 'is not found' and 'doesn't keep time'. One word, two sentences, two meanings shining.

## Sub lesson 4 — Market writing and speaking

Write a reasoned paragraph: which do you prefer — traditional or modern clothing? A reasoned paragraph gives its reasons, not only its choice.

Write a review paragraph on the importance of communication in trade — buyer and seller understanding each other is the market's engine.

Collect oral poems about market and trade, and family sayings that mention grains. Ask at home, write what you collect, and bring the market's own poetry into the classroom.

In pairs, imagine you are at the market: role-play seller and buyer. Greet, ask, bargain politely, agree, thank.

Debate in groups, from prepared notes: modern market against traditional market. Notes first — a debate is speaking built on reading.

Talk as a class: the preparations you will make to succeed in next year's learning. The book's last speaking looks forward.

## Sub lesson 5 — Market grammar review — and where this book's stories came from

Split word and suffix in the review set: **ገበየች → ገበየ - ች**, and እነአለሚቱ, ጆኒያው, ትህትናዋ with theirs. Splitting is the year's grammar habit, one last time.

Read the book's own references page — **ዋቢዎች**. The year's readings came from somewhere: the 1989 and 2006 Amhara Bureau textbooks, Kebede Mikael (1999), Zerihun Asfaw (1998), the ILO (2018), Deutsche Welle (2009) and the Reporter newspaper (2013). Every book stands on earlier books — and says so.
