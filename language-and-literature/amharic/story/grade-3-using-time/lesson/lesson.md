# Grade 3 Using Time

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **የጊዜ አጠቃቀም** — using time. Time is the one thing everyone has the same amount of. This chapter teaches keeping time, planning the day, and the book's golden saying: ጊዜ ወርቅ ነው — time is gold. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about using time in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — not knowing, and human desire

Listen to **አለማወቅ** — Not Knowing. A student is misjudged. When the truth comes out, judge with the class: whose mistake was biggest — the teacher's, the student's, or the friends'?

Listen to **የሰው ፍላጎት** — Human Desire. Human desire is deep as the ocean. The listening compares leading your time by a plan to gems and gold — a planned life is the treasure.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading — time used well

Read **ጎበዙ ተማሪ** — The Capable Student. A student who walks on crutches tells Sofiya: being disabled does not stop excellence. The main thing is using time properly. Read it with respect — it is about what a person can do, not what they cannot.

Read **የሐሴት መልካም ልምድ** — Haset's Good Habit. Haset folds her clothes, does her homework, helps and plays — everything by plan, ዕቅድ. Ikram, who has no plan, runs home in regret. The two children are the two ways.

Read **ትርፍ ጊዜ የለም** — There Is No Spare Time. A boy envies his friend's happiness. The friend explains: every activity has its appointed time — that is the whole secret.

Read **በጊዜያችሁ ተጠቀሙ** — Use Your Time. Go to the ants: they store food in harvest season with no overseer over them. Do not waste your golden time in gossip and idleness.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading — time is gold, and a marriage cancelled

Read the dialogue **ጊዜ ወርቅ ነው** — Time Is Gold. Huluagersh and Ato Usman agree: the saying only means something when time is divided among works and actually used. Gold in a box does nothing; time in a plan does everything.

Read **ጋብቻው ተሰረዘ** — The Marriage Was Cancelled. Alemnesh never wastes time. Her parents tell her she will marry and leave school. Her teacher persuades the parents — the marriage is cancelled, and Alemnesh continues learning.

## Sub lesson 4 — Time words and sayings

Match synonyms among the time-keeping words: **ሰዓት ማክበር** — keeping time; **ማርፈድ** — being late. A synonym match is a meaning match.

Build new words by changing the first letter: **ገመተ → ሸመተ**. One letter changes, and a new real word stands there.

Ask people at home and write down five sayings about time. Sayings are a people's library, carried in memory.

Learn **ጊዜ ወርቅ ነው** as a ምሣሌያዊ አባባል — a proverb-like figurative saying: it does not mean time is made of gold; it means time is precious like gold. Then fill sentences with **ውጤት, ብልህ, አባባል** and their set.

## Sub lesson 5 — Writing and reviewing time

Draw up your own daily timetable — hour by hour — and have friends evaluate it: is anything missing? Is play there as well as study?

List your usual Saturday and Sunday activities in sequence — in the order you really do them.

Write on a concept web: I divide my time by work — with branches for reading, play and study growing from the centre.

Write a plan paragraph: how you will spend the coming **ክረምት** — the rainy season — when the days change their shape.

Research by asking: what are the differences between traditional and modern time-keeping? Write your findings as a paragraph.

Choose your writing: (1) a paragraph on the benefits of working versus not working, or (2) write a dialogue — ጭውውት — about using time, and perform it.

Match words to the suffix each one takes: **-ኦች, -ዎች, -ኣም, እነ-, -ኣት, -ነት**. Every word has its own ending that fits.

Write six words carrying -ኦች / -ዎች plurals; then copy sentences and underline the nouns and the verbs — the two word-kinds you can now name.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **የጊዜ አጠቃቀም** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
