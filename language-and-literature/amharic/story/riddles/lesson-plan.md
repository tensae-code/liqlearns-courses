# Lesson plan — Riddles — እንቆቅልሽ

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Riddles — እንቆቅልሽ
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a riddle can be asked and guessed the Ethiopian game's way — clues in order, the answer held back, played in groups of four.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Listening Stories (the house riddle was heard there) and on the speaking work of the Story title: the learner already hears and retells; here the story shrinks to a riddle — a description that hides its answer — and the skill is asking, guessing and sorting, played as the guides teach it.

- Recommended first: Listening Stories, Sub lesson 2 — the

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play the riddle game in a group of four, taking turns asking and answering, keeping the riddle procedure.
2. Give the five printed answers: ባህር (sea), እሳት (fire), ጀበናና ስኒ (coffee pot and cup), ሰዓት (clock), ወፍጮ (mill).
3. Read a printed riddle and write its answer — the tongue riddle's answer is ምላስ.
4. Sort riddle answers into things that sit (ተቀማጭ) and things that move (ተንቀጥቃጭ).
5. Give the orange riddle-passage's answer — the fruit is the orange.
6. Make a Who-Am-I riddle of your own: clues first, answer last.

## What you need
A group of four to play with. Paper and a pencil for written answers. The riddle cards (Picture 8327).

## If the learner is stuck
- A guesser is stuck → give the riddle's clues again, one at
  a time, slower; a riddle is solved clue by clue, not all at
  once.
- The answer is shouted out of turn → the game's procedure:
  the answer belongs to whose turn it is; the group keeps the
  turns, as the guide directs.
- A learner's own riddle gives its answer away → clues must
  describe, not name: keep the name for the very end.
