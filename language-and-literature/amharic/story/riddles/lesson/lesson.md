# Riddles — እንቆቅልሽ

## Do it first

Hear one riddle. Do not shout — riddles are guessed in turn.

*I have a place where people sleep. I have a place where
people eat. People live inside me. Who am I?*

You know this one — it is the house riddle from Listening
Stories, and its answer is ቤት, a house. That is the whole
game of this lesson: **clues first, in order — the answer held
back to the end.** እንቆቅልሽ: the questioner asks (እንቆቅልሽ —
ምን አውቅልሽ?), the guesser guesses, and riddles sharpen the
mind.

## Sub lesson 1 — The riddle game: ask and guess in fours

The game as it is taught: learners sit in **groups of four**,
with the group's mix kept as the guide directs, and take
**turns asking and answering riddles**, following the riddle
procedure. The extra talk around the game builds the habit of
speaking politely to each other.

These are the game's five printed riddles — their answers are
printed in the guide, and the game is to reach them by the
clues:

1. The answer is **ባህር** — the sea.
2. The answer is **እሳት** — fire.
3. The answer is **ጀበናና ስኒ** — the coffee pot and its cup.
4. The answer is **ሰዓት** — a clock.
5. The answer is **ወፍጮ** — a mill.

Play it properly: the asker gives the riddle whole; the
guessers guess in turn; the answer is confirmed only at the
end. The answer cards wait face down until then.

## Sub lesson 2 — The student books' riddle lessons

**The riddle lesson (የእንቆቅልሽ ጨዋታ).** The student book's
own riddle lesson teaches how riddles are played — and that a
wrong answer can cost a forfeit — then prints riddles **as
reading**, with answers to write. Two of its printed riddles:
"ቀይ ዘንዶ በዋሻ ውስጥ ተጋድሞ" — a red snake lying in a cave —
whose answer is **ምላስ**, the tongue; and "ስሄድ አገኘኋት
ስመለስ አጣኋት" — going I found it, returning I lost it — to
solve in the lesson's own work. Its review sorts riddles into
**ተቀማጭ** (things that sit) and **ተንቀጥቃጭ** (things that
move), and sets more riddles to answer.

**Who Am I? — the orange (እኔ ማን ነኝ?).** A fluent-reading
riddle-passage: "I am a fruit, round, yellow when ripe,
peeled to eat, with eight to ten segments and seeds…" Read it
as a riddle — the answer is the orange, and the passage is
the proof that a whole reading text can be one riddle long.

## Sub lesson 3 — The house riddle and the Who-Am-I shelf

**The house riddle, cross-referred.** You met it in Listening
Stories (Sub lesson 2): the speaker with rooms for sleeping,
eating and receiving guests, and people living inside — the
answer ቤት. Give its clues yourself now, from the picture,
one room at a time.

[Picture 8311 — a house cut open, its rooms showing]

**The lemon riddle-story.** The student book's riddle-story
asks እኔ ማን ነኝ? of the lemon (ሎሚ): the speaker tells why it
is honoured, with the saying "ሎሚ ለሽታ", and the class answers
with prediction and oral questions — a story-length riddle.

**The traffic policeman's Who Am I?** A reading text (እኔ ማን
ነኝ?) in which a traffic policeman tells his own day as a
riddle: he teaches pedestrians to keep left, fines speeding
and overloading drivers, and stands in frost and heat at the
roadside. Guess who speaks before the text tells you — then
write your own Who-Am-I for a worker you know: teacher,
farmer or soldier, as the book sets it.


## Sub lesson 4 — Grade 5: the riddle session whole — formula, evenings, and classification by source


### Riddles (እንቆቅልሽ) — the Grade 5 book's session

The riddle game whole, as the Grade 5 book teaches it: who takes part; the order and formula with which the asker opens; why riddles are played in the evenings and on Sundays; how a riddle game differs from other verbal games; the rule for loser and winner; and why riddle-asking is called investigative (አመራማሪ) — worked through the book's example: "a small razor that shaves a country", answered "fire" (እሳት) by reasoning. Five riddles are set to solve (among them: "when I go I find her, when I return I lose her"). The riddle game stands in sub lessons 1 and 2 of this lesson from other books, and its classification there from the Grade 2 book; this part stocks the Grade 5 book's own session at its own pages.

### Classifying riddles by source

The review task that closes the session: classify riddle questions by their source — from plants, from animals, from objects, from the human body — in a table. A riddle is filed by where its answer lives.


## Sub lesson 5 — Grade 7: the asker and the answerer — እንቆቅልህ!

Grade 7 writes riddles in asker/answerer arrangement, on this model dialogue. ጠያቂ (asker): 'እንቆቅልህ!' — a riddle for you! መላሽ (answerer): 'ምን አውቅልህ!' — what do I solve for you? Asker: 'ዞሮ ዞሮ እራቱ ጥፊ እወቅልኝ' — going round and round, its supper is a slap — solve it for me! Answerer: 'ወንፊት' — a sieve. Asker: 'አውቀሀል። ተራው ያንተ ነው' — you solved it; the turn is yours. The dialogue IS the game: challenge, acceptance, riddle, answer, and the turn passing. Write the riddles you know in exactly this arrangement, as its companion work — naming the figure (ዘይቤ) in the mourning verses of the same pages — stands in Figures of Speech.

## Say it to your companion

Ask your companion one riddle from this lesson — clues whole,
answer held back. They guess in turn; confirm only at the
end. Then swap: their riddle, your guesses. If you finish,
ask your own Who-Am-I from Sub lesson 3.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five printed answers from memory: sea, fire,
   coffee pot and cup, clock, mill — in Amharic.
2. Write the tongue riddle's answer and say why it fits: red,
   lying in a cave.
3. Sort these into sits / moves: ሰዓት (clock), ባህር (sea),
   ወፍጮ (mill), እሳት (fire), ጀበና (coffee pot).
4. Make a Who-Am-I riddle for one thing in your home — three
   clues, answer last — and ask it at home tonight.
5. Retell the house riddle to your companion, clue by clue,
   and let them stop you when they know.
