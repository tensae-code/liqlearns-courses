# Grade 3 Transport

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **መጓጓዣ** — transport. From a donkey's back to an aeroplane: this chapter rides every way Ethiopians travel — pack animals and the tankwa reed boat, the train, the car, and the plane overhead — and teaches the road's safety with them. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about transport in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening on the move

Listen to **የባሕርና የየብስ መጓጓዣዎች** — Sea and Land Transport. First guess the meanings of the listening's words; then check your guesses against the story itself.

Listen again and build the concept web of vehicle accidents: the causes on one side — untrained drivers among them — and the solutions on the other: train and test drivers. Cause, then cure.

Listen to **ባቡር** — The Train. The listening calls the train precious to us. Ask as you listen: who does 'us' mean? A small word, a big answer.

Listen to **ሽርሽር በጣና ሐይቅ** — An Excursion on Lake Tana. Who is narrating? And as you listen, compare travelling on water with travelling by air.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fluent reading — old ways and new wings

Read the dialogue **ሀናና አየለ** — Hana and Ayele — twice, fluently. Hana explains traditional transport: pack animals on land — mule, donkey, horse, camel — and the **ታንኳ**, the reed boat, on water.

[GIF 21611 — a tankwa being poled across water]

Read **ነጎድጓዳማ ድምፅ** — A Thundering Sound. A thunder-like sound shakes the classroom. The pupils rush out — and identify the aeroplane overhead. The sound comes first in this reading; the naming comes after looking.

Read **ጉዞ በአውሮኘላን** — A Journey by Aeroplane. A mother flies to Addis Ababa for the first time — her brother sent the ticket for his wedding. One hour, and she phones home. A journey that took days now takes an hour.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading — the mule and the car, trouble and cleverness, road rules

Read **የበቅሎና የመኪና ሙግት** — The Argument of the Mule and the Car. On the road they argue. The mule boasts that the car's wealth came from her ancient service. Listen inside the reading for the sound-words: **እህህ** — laughter; **ሩር** — the engine's roar.

Read **ችግር ብልሃትን ይፈጥራል** — Trouble Creates Cleverness. The ambulance sticks in the mud, carrying a woman in labour. So they tie her bed to a wooden frame and carry her on a **ቃሬዛ** — a stretcher — the old way saving the day when the new way sticks.

Read the dialogue **የእነጀሚላ ውይይት** — The Jemila Household's Discussion. Jemila and Tilaye see a boy hurt in a car accident, and resolve together: learn the road rules, teach family and neighbours, ask for crossings, and serve as student traffic police.

## Sub lesson 4 — Transport words — comparing old and new, and the words of the air

Build the comparison concept web: **በቅሎ** the mule — ጥንታዊ, traditional — against **መኪና** the car — ዘመናዊ, modern. The car's words grow on its side: **ጪስ** smoke/exhaust, **ሞተር** motor, **ጎማ** tyre, **ጋማ** and their kin.

Build a concept web of words and phrases belonging to air travel, with words like **አሰተናጋጅ** among them. A web for the airport: the people, the places, the actions.

## Sub lesson 5 — Transport writing and grammar review

Write a paragraph on the service of one traditional means of transport — choose one and honour its work.

Research by asking people: why is the mule sterile — why can it not have young? Write what you learn as a paragraph.

Write a paragraph from a chosen starter: cycling has many benefits, or the donkey gives many services. One starter, one whole paragraph.

Write a paragraph: the precautions pedestrians must take against car accidents — the walker's own safety rules.

Write a paragraph on precautions for riding a bicycle — the rider's safety rules, sister to the walker's.

Review whole: build the transport concept web (ባህላዊ/ዘመናዊ, የየብስ/የባሕር and their uses); split suffixes; pick out the nouns and build sentences from them.

Do the table work: pull the noun and the verb out of given sentences, each into its column.

Make the singular plural with እነ-: **ጀሚላ → እነጀሚላ**. Then sort words into noun and verb, and build sentences from given word sets.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **መጓጓዣ** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
