# Lesson plan — Grade 3 Farming

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Grade 3 Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The farm year turns: plough, sow, weed, harvest. This chapter learns the tools, the seasons, the forest that holds the soil, and the garden behind the house.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 3 learner's fluent reading and listening: the texts are read twice the fluent way, listened to from the teacher's voice, and answered from what was read and heard.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order:

1. **Listening on the farm** — Listen to **በብልሀት ያረሱ እንደልብ ጎረሱ** — Those Who Plough Cleverly Harvest as They Wish.
2. **Reading — the forest, and the backyard garden** — Read the dialogue **የደን ጥቅም** — The Benefit of the Forest.
3. **Farming words — tools, seasons and the parts we eat** — Match the traditional farm tools to their parts and uses: **ሞፈር, ቀንበር** the yoke, **እርፍ, ድግር, ማረሻ** the plough, **ማነቆ, መርገጥ, ምራን, ወገል**.
4. **Farming writing and research** — Write a paragraph from the starter: caring for the forest is useful — with correct punctuation from its first capital to its final ።.
5. **Reading aloud — long words and farm poems from home** — Pick out five complex long words from the forest dialogue — **ካልተንከባከብን** is one — and read them repeatedly: in parts, then whole, until they run smooth.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reading Stories and Listening Stories — this lesson's Grade 3 texts are read and heard the same way, on the chapter theme of agriculture. Recommended, never forced.

2. Use this chapter's word sets in sentences and in role play.
3. Do this chapter's writing tasks — sentences, paragraphs, webs and tables — with correct punctuation.
4. Do this chapter's grammar review work (suffixes, plurals and word-kinds as the chapter names them).

## If the learner is stuck
- A story will not retell → go back to its sequence: first, then, last — three steps carry every tale in this lesson.
- A word set will not stick → say it in a pair, use it in a sentence, then write it: ear, mouth, hand.
- A writing task feels big → the web or table first, sentences after: the book's own order in every chapter.
