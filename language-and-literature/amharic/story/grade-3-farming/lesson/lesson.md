# Grade 3 Farming

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ግብርና** — agriculture. The farm year turns: plough, sow, weed, harvest. This chapter learns the tools, the seasons, the forest that holds the soil, and the garden behind the house. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about agriculture in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening on the farm

Listen to **በብልሀት ያረሱ እንደልብ ጎረሱ** — Those Who Plough Cleverly Harvest as They Wish. As you listen, learn the farm tools and what each one is for.

Listen to **ትጉህ አርሶአደሮች** — Diligent Farmers. Is farm work heavier in **ክረምት** the rains or **በጋ** the dry season? Listen for how the rainy season binds a farmer's whole life.

Listen to **ዶሮ ርባታ** — Chicken Raising. Which season suits it? How do chicks hatch, and how are hens kept healthy? The listening answers all three.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading — the forest, and the backyard garden

Read the dialogue **የደን ጥቅም** — The Benefit of the Forest. Mustefa and Meklit talk it through: forests hold the soil and stop the land turning to desert. People cut trees for fuel and building — so plant a new seedling for every tree cut, and be an example first.

Read the dialogue **የጓሮ አትክልት** — The Backyard Garden. Teferi and Rediet list the vegetables — ጎመን, ጥቅል ጎመን, ሰላጣ, ቆስጣ, ቲማቲም, ቃሪያ, ቀይስር — and how they protect us from disease. The steps to start: learn, want, prepare a spare plot, ask experienced people, buy seed or seedlings, plant.

## Sub lesson 3 — Farming words — tools, seasons and the parts we eat

Match the traditional farm tools to their parts and uses: **ሞፈር, ቀንበር** the yoke, **እርፍ, ድግር, ማረሻ** the plough, **ማነቆ, መርገጥ, ምራን, ወገል**. A tool is known by its work.

Build the seasons concept web: **ክረምት** the rains, **በጋ** the dry season, **መፀው/መኸር** autumn, **ፀደይ/በልግ** spring — then write four full sentences from the web, one for each season.

Classify vegetables by the part we eat — root, leaf or fruit: ካሮት, ቲማቲም, ዝንጅብል, ድንች, ሽንኩርት and their kin each go in their column.

## Sub lesson 4 — Farming writing and research

Write a paragraph from the starter: caring for the forest is useful — with correct punctuation from its first capital to its final ።.

Gather information and write a paragraph on the harms of deforestation — then, in groups, speak about how to prevent it. Writing first, speaking from the writing.

Join pairs of sentences into one with conjunctions — **እና, ግን, ስለዚህ** — the grammar review of this chapter: two short sentences go in, one joined sentence comes out, and the joining word carries the meaning between them.

## Sub lesson 5 — Reading aloud — long words and farm poems from home

Pick out five complex long words from the forest dialogue — **ካልተንከባከብን** is one — and read them repeatedly: in parts, then whole, until they run smooth.

Ask your parents and write down oral farm poems — ቃላዊ ግጥም — the work chants sung over ploughing. Home is a library too; bring its poems to school in writing.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **ግብርና** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
