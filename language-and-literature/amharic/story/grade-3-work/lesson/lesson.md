# Grade 3 Work

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ሥራ** — work. Work divided is work done: the carpenter's two branches, the bees' three kinds, a healer's old knowledge and a lawyer who cannot see — this chapter honours the people who do a country's work. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about work in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading — the carpenter, the bees, and a life

Read **አናጺ** — The Carpenter. Carpentry has two branches: big construction, and house work. Read what carpenters make and install, the physical and mental skills the work needs, and how plans and drawings are read before wood is cut.

Read **ንቦችና ሥራቸው** — The Bees and Their Work. The bees narrate themselves: one queen, many workers, and drones who guard. All work by division of labour. And read how people take their honey — with smoke, and with covered hands.

Read **የትነበርሽ ንጉሤ** — Yetnebersh Nigussie. Blinded by meningitis at five, she was educated at a school for the blind, became a law graduate of Addis Ababa University, and works as a human-rights and disability-rights advocate, with national and international awards. Her life is the reading — facts in order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Listening — the honour of teaching, and a healer

Listen to **የመምህርነት ክብር** — The Honour of Teaching. A teacher shapes a whole generation. Listen for how one person's work becomes a hundred people's future.

Listen to **ሃኪም አበበች ሽፈራው** — Hakim Abebech Shifera, a traditional healer and a student of **ሥነፈለግ** — astronomy. Her listening joins indigenous knowledge with caring for the environment.

## Sub lesson 3 — Work words — the carpenter's web, animal similes, sorting

Build the carpenter concept web in four branches: tools, products, actions, skills. Every carpenter word you know goes on its branch.

Build animal similes — 'like…' phrases: **እንደንብ ታታሪ** — as diligent as a bee. Now build them for the lion, the dove, the sheep and the fox, each from what that animal is known for.

Sort words into adjective and adverb: ትናንት, መልካም, ዛሬ, ቀይ, ጀግና, ሁልጊዜ and their set. Which kind describes a thing, and which tells when or how?

## Sub lesson 4 — Grammar at work — agreement and possessives

Correct the underlined word-forms so the sentence's parts agree: **ወደቤት ሂጂ → ወደቤትሽ ሂጂ**. The ending must fit the person it belongs to.

Learn the possessive suffixes on nouns — **-ኤ** mine, **-ህ, -ኡ, -ኣችን, -ኣችሁ, -ኣቸው** — drilled on ወንድም, ግድብ, ባህል, አባት. Whose is it? The ending answers.

## Sub lesson 5 — Writing and speaking about work

Gather information and write a paragraph about the job you want to do in the future — what it does, and why you choose it.

Write a paragraph about two Ethiopians you admire — who they are, and what in their work or life you admire.

Gather information and write a paragraph on indigenous knowledge — **ሀገርበቀል እውቀት** — the knowledge grown in this country, like the healer's.

Speak to the class on the motion: house work should be done by girls, not boys. You may argue either side — but argue it in order, with reasons.

Role-play the bees: one is the queen, one a worker, one a drone. Each speaks its own work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chapter word — **ሥራ** — and the one part of this lesson you liked most, in Amharic where you can. Your companion retells one story or rule from the lesson back to you, in their own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one story or text from this lesson and retell it in sequence — first, then, last.
2. Write five sentences using this lesson's own words.
3. Draw the concept web or table this lesson built, from memory, and check it against the lesson.
4. Teach one saying, rule or word set from this lesson to someone at home.
