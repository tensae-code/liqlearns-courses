# Grade 4 Our Environment

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **አካባቢ ጥበቃ** — protecting the environment. Our environment chapter: how air and water are spoiled, how forests hold the land together, and what people do together — terraces, seedlings, clean rivers — to protect the place they live in. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — How this book works, and listening for our environment

This book is school property, kept for next year's children too. Its care message — የጥንቃቄ መልዕክት — gives ten rules for keeping it clean and whole: clean hands before you open it, no food or drink near it, no writing inside it, carry it dry and safe, and hand it on in good heart. A book cared for teaches many children; a spoiled book teaches no one.

At the front, parents and guardians get their own guide. This book follows the 2013 E.C. curriculum reform: learning is activity-based — you do things, you do not only listen. Every chapter trains the four skills — listening, speaking, reading, writing — plus grammar, and it carries indigenous knowledge, the wisdom of our own people. Parents help at home: tell stories, read aloud, ask the child to retell, and let the child write real messages.

Listen to **አካባቢያችን** — our environment. It tells how toxic gases pollute the air we breathe, how waste can be turned into useful products instead of poison, and where renewable energy comes from — energy that does not run out. Listen for the three threads: spoiling, turning waste into good, and clean energy.

Learn this chapter's first words in their places: **የአካባቢ ሚዛን** — ecological balance, the way living things hold each other steady; **መበከል** — pollution, the spoiling of air, water or land; **ዉኃወለድ** — water-borne, carried by water; **ቀዬ** — your home area, the place you belong to. A word learned inside its own story stays learned.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fluent reading — the hare, and Regret

Read **ጥንቸሏ** — the hare — twice, the fluent way. The hare lives in a dense, kind forest. Then people cut the forest down: the springs dry, the shade vanishes, the food vanishes. The hare curses the cutters and migrates — መሰደድ — leaving the ruined home behind. Read it smoothly, the way you would tell it.

Read **ቁጭት** — Regret. A man returns to the village of his childhood and weeps at its river. The native trees are felled; plastic bottles and cloth float in the water; latrine waste flows into it; floods come; the soil will not yield without fertiliser. An elder despairs that no one listens. The narrator, who knows natural resources, resolves to gather the village and teach. ቁጭት means regret — ፀፀት.

Match the synonyms — words that mean the same: **መዳረግ** matches **መጋለጥ** (to be exposed), **ጠረን** matches **ሽታ** (smell), **ቁጭት** matches **ፀፀት** (regret). Synonyms let a writer say the same thing a second way, fresh.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading — diligent farmers and a day for our environment

Listen again, and go deeper: how do we protect the ecosystem? Reduce the gases we burn. And waste — is it better buried or burned? Hear the arguments and hold your own answer ready.

Write sentences using the story words in their story meanings: **መሰደድ** — to migrate, to leave a ruined place; **መቋቋም** — to endure, to hold out; **ምድረበዳ** — wilderness, empty wild land. The sentence must show the meaning, not only carry the word.

Practise the affixes — the small parts that join words: **እየ-** and **ተ-** go before a word; **-ኦች**, **-ኣት** and **-ኧት** come after it. Attach them to words and read the new words whole.

Go back to ጥንቸሏ and complete the sentences with the missing words from the tale itself — a cloze. The story gives you the words; your memory of the story finds them.

Listen to **አስቴርና ሶፊያ** — Aster and Sofiya. The two friends agree: deforestation is the root of the rising heat and the drought. They resolve to be examples themselves and to mobilise their friends to plant many seedlings. Listening ends in a promise — hold it.

Read **ውሏችን ለአካባቢያችን** — our day for our environment — twice, the fluent way. The neighbours meet at the appointed place carrying spade and shovel. All day they build terraces — ርከን — on the flood-damaged land, and they part promising to plant seedlings next week. One day of many hands changes a hillside.

Read **ትጉህ አርሶአደሮች** — the diligent farmers. They plough on the contour, terrace against the floods, guard the forest from charcoal-makers, plant trees and tend them. Answer the comprehension questions in multiple-choice form: read every choice before you pick.

Write the chart paragraph: diligent farmers on one side, lazy farmers on the other — their habits and their results. The chart holds the thinking; the paragraph carries it.

Listen once more and think: can planting seedlings alone restore a destroyed forest? What else would you do? There is no single right answer — there is your answer, with reasons.

Choose the closest meanings: **ተግባቡ** is closest to **ተስማሙ** (they agreed), **ቀዬ** to **አካባቢ** (area), **መሸርሸር** to **መደለል**. Closest-meaning questions test whether a word lives in your mind with its true neighbours.

Match words to their suffixes — **-ዬ, -ኝ, -ኤ, -ዎች**: each word in the review takes the tail that fits its end, and the joined word is read whole. A suffix is not decoration; it changes who owns, who acts, or how many there are.

## Sub lesson 4 — Reading — Sindu's mango trees, saving Tana, and the forest text

Write a short paragraph on communal development work done together in your area or at your school — the work many hands did that one pair of hands could not.

Listen to **ጣናን እንታደግ** — let's save Tana. Lake Tana is Ethiopia's largest lake. Farmers live and work around it, and a dangerous invasive weed — **እንቦጭ**, water hyacinth — spreads over its water. Tana feeds the Abbay river. Answer the true/false questions from what you heard: true means the listening said it; false means it did not.

Read **ስንዱና ማንጎዎቹ** — Sindu and her mango trees — twice, the fluent way. Sindu studies under her mango trees: they give fruit every day, shade that cools the air, clean air to breathe, and a windbreak for the house. She wishes everywhere were trees.

Read **ሳይቃጠል በቅጠል** — the forest text that explains, not tells a story. Forests steady the climate and the rain, stop soil erosion, and shelter wildlife. Irresponsible cutting and burning brings desertification, food and water shortage, death and migration. Stopping the destruction today is every citizen's duty.

Build the concept web: the benefits of forest on one head, the harms of deforestation on the other. Sort the given points under the two heads — sorting is the thinking; the web shows it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Words and writing review — punctuation, suffixes and fruit trees

Punctuate: take unpunctuated sentences and insert **፣** **፤** **!** and **?** where each belongs. The comma pauses, the ፤ pauses longer, ! carries strong feeling, ? asks. Read the sentence aloud first — your voice finds the marks.

Split the suffixes off: **ማንጎዎች** splits into **ማንጎ - ዎች** — the stem and its plural tail. Then give the contextual meanings of **ሀሴት**, **ንጹህ** (clean, pure) and **ነጻነት** (freedom) as this chapter uses them.

Write a paragraph naming fruit-bearing plants and their uses — the mango that feeds and shades, and the others you know. Name the plant, then its use; that is the paragraph's spine.
