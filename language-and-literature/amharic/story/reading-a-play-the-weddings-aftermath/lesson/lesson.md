# Reading a Play — The Wedding's Aftermath

## Do it first

Look at the Act 1 stage picture in words: a salon, a sofa, a calculator. Guess what trouble this man is in — then read Act 1 and check.

## Sub lesson 1 — Act 1 — I and my debt alone remained

The curtain opens on a wide salon — sofa, commode, a curtained window. Ato Belayhun, fully dressed with a tie, grey hair combed back, face knotted with worry, works a calculator with pen and paper before him: after the wedding feast, 'I and my debt alone remained.' Creditors phone demanding rented goods back or a new contract. His wife Ayelech enters smiling in traditional dress; the guard Demena reports Bela's demand for the stored chairs and the shawl-seller's call; the worker Meseret brings more demands. Belayhun cries 'Woe, no place to flee!' and storms out as the curtain closes. Read a play by its stage directions first — they tell you where you are and how each person feels before a word is spoken.

## Sub lesson 2 — Act 2 — It's custom, no?

Act 2: Mhret, the son, answers the phone — 'In the wedding's aftermath, the debt pile…' Belayhun dictates: tell the creditor the return feast is not cancelled; the chairs will stay. The video cameraman wants 600 birr for filming, up to 1,600 with editing and the return — Belayhun refuses: first his debt must be audited. Then Demena, given areqe, speaks the act's heart: it is custom (ወግ) — 'the people won't accept or recognise you if you don't feast and spend'; skip the feasts and your son is called ጋለሞታ, your wedding ቦጭቧጫ (shabby), you ቆጥቋጣ (stingy) — even for funerals, the forty-day memorial and a monument, or be scorned. His own father never missed an iddir payment yet died untreated for want of money: 'we the eaters found nothing.' Ayelech and Belayhun re-enter quarrelling as the curtain closes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Act 3 — Knowing our capacity is not shame

Act 3: Belayhun, restless, signs 'I am not here!' to each creditor's call; in despair he climbs on the table pulling his tie towards the cornice, and Demena holds his legs, crying out, until he descends. He resolves to absent himself from the return feast. Then Mhret reveals he never sent the return invitations — fenced in by senseless custom (አጉል ልማድ) and people-pleasing (ይሉኝታ), he stopped to think. The daughter has phoned too: cancel the return, excuse father — 'the one who made a wedding is not my world but my death.' Ayelech admits her brave talk was only to keep hope alive. Demena pleads: let a person eat what he has; let our house not be destroyed. Mhret closes the play: not feasting a return is knowing our capacity, not shame — the family will be an example to a society that wastes wealth on tottering feasts. The curtain closes.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.
