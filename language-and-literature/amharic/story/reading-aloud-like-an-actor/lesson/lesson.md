# Reading Aloud Like an Actor — the Four Levels

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The expressive-reading rubric — የአገላለጽ ንባብ መመዘኛ

When the Grade 8 class reads Act 2 of the play aloud, the book marks them with an expressive-reading rubric. Level 4 (excellent): fully following the punctuation, fully imitating the characters' speech, reading the words correctly without stopping. Level 3 (very good): mostly so, with a few lapses. Level 2 (good — needs improvement): partly so, with some stopping and mispronunciation. Level 1 (beginner — much improvement needed): mostly not following punctuation, not imitating the characters, much stopping. The rubric is a ladder, not a judgement: find your level honestly, then practise the one thing that lifts you to the next.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading fluently and accurately — the book's criteria

The Grade 8 book's fluency note names accuracy plainly. Reading WITHOUT error (አቀላጥፎ ማንበብ at its best) means: exact pronunciation without stumbling, self-correcting when you slip, no omission, no substitution, no skipping and nothing left out — and proper delivery. Reading WITH error is the reverse of each of these. Use the two lists as a mirror: read a paragraph aloud, mark which list you matched, and read it again aiming at the clean list. Fluency is not speed alone — it is smooth, complete, accurate reading.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.
