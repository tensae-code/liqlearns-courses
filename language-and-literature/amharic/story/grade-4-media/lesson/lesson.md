# Grade 4 Newspapers Radio and Television

## Do it first

Say the chapter word aloud: **ብዙኃን መገናኛ** — mass communication — media for everyone. The media chapter: the first newspapers, a journalist who crossed ages, the hawker and the woman journalist, and how radio and television carry news to everyone — rich and poor alike. Before any teaching, try this: name one thing you already know about this chapter's theme in Amharic, and one word you want this lesson to teach you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening — the first newspaper

Listen to **የመጀመሪያው ጋዜጣ** — the first newspaper — and match the events to their years: the paper that distributed 200 copies; the paper that focused on world affairs; the founding of the **አዕምሮ** newspaper; the 1929 format of three pages of news and two pages of adverts. A newspaper's history is a country's memory learning to print itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading — the journalist who crossed ages

Read **ዘመን ተሻጋሪው ጋዜጠኛ** — Pawlos Gnogno, the journalist who crossed ages. He was born in 1926 E.C. near Kulbi and raised in Dire Dawa; his schooling stopped after Grade 4 when the Italian invasion came. As a child he drew Ethiopian patriots killing and capturing Italians. He edited የድምጽ ጋዜጣ, became famous for his አንድ ጥያቄ አለኝ — I have one question — column in Addis Zemen, and wrote nineteen published books: histories of Tewodros and Menilek and the Ethio-Italian war, story collections, and Menilek's letters in two volumes, published twenty years after his death. He gave permission for others to reprint his work without removing his name. A short schooling, a long pen.

## Sub lesson 3 — Listening — the questioning student, and the media word map

Listen to **ጠያቂዋ ተማሪ** — the questioning student. How does information travel from one group to the whole society? Her father names the mass media. Compare print media and voice media: what each carries, and whom each reaches.

Build the word map of **ብዙኃን መገናኛ** — mass media: **ሬዲዮ** radio, **ቴሌቪዥን** television, **ጋዜጣ** newspaper, and their kin. A word map is a family portrait — every member in its place.

## Sub lesson 4 — Reading — the newspaper hawker, and prepositions at work

Read **ጋዜጣ አዟሪው** — the newspaper hawker of Bahir Dar. He is twenty-three, finished Grade 12, and sells papers and magazines while waiting for university entrance results. On a rainy-season day he sells fast, enters a café — his bundle slips: papers scatter, he hits his head on a table, tea glasses smash. The waiter demands payment in a rage. The café manager cleans up, counsels the hawker, and lets him go with forgiveness. News is carried by people; people need carrying too.

Pick out the prepositions in the hawker's text: **በ, ወደ, ከ, ለ, ስለ** — small words standing before nouns, showing place, direction, source, purpose. Find each one where it works.

## Sub lesson 5 — Radio against television, and the woman journalist

Write three paragraphs from the radio-versus-television comparison web. Radio: voice only, battery, cheap, not for the deaf. Television: voice and picture, dear, good for the deaf. Shared: both are mass media, both inform society, both have journalists. The web compares; the paragraphs conclude.

Write a three-paragraph composition describing your school's layout, for a friend who lives in another area — write so the friend can walk it in the mind.

Listen to **ሀያትና ፀዳለ** — Hayat and Tsedale debating: which do you follow more, radio or television? Hear why radio is the one that serves even the poor of the town — cheap, battery-run, everywhere.

Read **ጋዜጠኛዋ** — the woman journalist. Sisaynesh, at a radio station, draws experience from the veteran journalists and above all from her supervisor Weyzero Tihitna, thirty-five years in the profession. She learns to gather true information and prepare fact-based reports. Her programme wins many listeners — and at the annual festival she is named exemplary and diligent journalist of the year.

Speak: tell the class the subject of a news item you heard on television or radio. One subject, clearly told — that is a news report in miniature.
