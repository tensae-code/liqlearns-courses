# Lesson plan — First Paragraphs

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** First Paragraphs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Paragraph (CURRICULUM.md) — this lesson OPENS the Paragraph unit.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a paragraph is sentences together around one idea — and how a paragraph is completed and continued from a starter.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Writing Sentences — the learner can already build, dictate and order single sentences; this lesson joins sentences into the next bigger thing.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Sentences together around one idea** — what makes sentences a paragraph and not a list: they all carry the same one idea, like the books' short paragraph about the wild animals you like.
2. **Completing a paragraph** — a paragraph that stops in the middle: read what is given, keep its idea and its flow, and write the part that finishes it.
3. **Continuing and growing a paragraph** — grow a paragraph from a starter sentence — at least five lines on the books' own model — and write paragraphs of your own from points and starters.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Writing Sentences (sentence/writing-sentences). Recommended, never forced.
- Story reading helps: a paragraph is the size of one scene or one thought inside a story.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what holds a paragraph together: its sentences all carry one idea.
2. Write a short paragraph about the wild animals you like, and read it aloud.
3. Complete a starter paragraph, keeping the given idea and flow.
4. Grow a paragraph of at least five lines from one starter sentence.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads three sentences — two about one idea, one about another — and picks the stranger before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the paragraph as sentences around one idea; completing; continuing (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8514]: a picture of one idea in a circle — "the animals I like" — with three sentence strips under it, all about the same idea — the student says the idea each sentence carries.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 8513]: a GIF of sentence strips stacking one by one inside a frame until they stand as one paragraph — the student counts the sentences and names the one idea.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading a starter paragraph aloud, stopping where the given part ends — the student says what the next sentence must stay about.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read your grown paragraph aloud; your companion names its one idea (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — complete and grow paragraphs of your own (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- The paragraph wanders to a new idea → underline each sentence's idea in the margin; a sentence about a different idea belongs to the NEXT paragraph.
- Completing feels like starting again → it is not: read the given part twice, say its idea in your own words, and only then write the next sentence.
- Five lines feels long → grow line by line: starter, one more sentence about it, one more — the books grow paragraphs the same way.
