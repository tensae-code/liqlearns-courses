# How-To Writing

## Do it first

Here are the steps of making a salad, all mixed up:

- serve it with bread or injera
- chop everything small
- wash the lettuce, tomato and onion
- mix in a deep bowl, with oil and salt, then lemon juice

Put them in the right order and say them aloud. How did you know
which step had to come first? Hold that thought.

## Sub lesson 1 — Instructions in order

A how-to text tells how a thing is done. Its whole strength is
**order**: step 1, step 2, step 3 — each step done before the
next can start.

[Picture 8518 — how-to writing: the salad steps in order]

The books teach how-to reading and writing everywhere:

- **The salad** (ሰላጣ): wash the ingredients (lettuce, lemon or
  vinegar, onion, tomato, pepper, salt, oil); chop; mix in a deep
  bowl; oil and salt; lemon juice; serve with bread or injera —
  and then **retell the steps in sequence**. Retelling in order
  is the test that you hold the how-to.
- **The kebero drum**: soak the hide, scrape it, hollow the
  wanza-wood body (wide top, narrow bottom), lace the hide over
  both openings, rub and sun it for a good sound.

[GIF 8519 — the kebero steps appearing in order]

- **Doro wat's steps in order**: ይገዛል (it is bought) → ይታረዳል
  (slaughtered) → ይለበለባል → ይገሸራል → ይዘፈዘፋል → ይበለታል → ይወጣል —
  the book sets you to put them in order, and the order is the
  lesson.
- **Preparing ጥቅል ጎመን (cabbage) for eating**: ask, then write
  the steps in sequence.
- **Describing how one craft is done** — shema weaving, pottery,
  coffee brewing (የቡና አፈላል), doro-wat making, tella brewing:
  the Grade 3 book's writing choice, and every choice is a
  step-by-step doing.

The writing rule is the same for all of them: **name each step,
keep the steps in their true order, skip nothing.** A reader who
follows your writing must arrive at the finished thing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plan before you write: the outline (ዝርዝር)

Before the writing comes the plan. The Grade 4 guide's writing
part teaches it plainly: learners **draft an outline (ዝርዝር)
before composing**.

[Picture 8520 — the outline: plan before you write]

An outline is short. It holds:

1. The **topic** — the doing you will write about.
2. The **points in order** — the steps or parts, one short line
   each, in the order the writing will take them.

Then the full writing grows from the outline, point by point.
The guide works the outline through everyday examples drawn from
television, radio and the world around the learners — your own
outline works the same way: pick a doing you truly know (how
coffee is brewed at home, how a garden bed is prepared), list
its steps in order, and only then write.

Plan first, and the writing has a spine. Skip the plan, and the
steps arrive in the order you happen to remember them — which
Sub lesson 1 has already shown you is not good enough.

## Sub lesson 3 — A real process in five steps: traditional mediation

A process text can carry a whole piece of real life. Here is
one the higher classes work with: **ባህላዊ ሽምግልና** —
traditional mediation, the settling of a dispute by elders.
It has its own aim, and its principles: **equality, fairness,
balance, truthfulness, humanity**. And it runs as a process
in **five steps**:

[Picture 9052 — traditional mediation: a process in five steps]

1. **Ask first** — the preliminary inquiry: find out what
   happened, and what each side says.
2. **Get consent** — both sides agree to reconcile; nobody is
   mediated against their will.
3. **Choose mediators for both sides** — each side has its
   own mediators.
4. **Choose the mediation judge** — the one who will hold
   the sitting.
5. **Hold the mediation** — the sitting itself, where the
   matter is heard and settled. The parties swear an oath
   confirming the decision to reconcile.

[GIF 9053 — the five steps appearing in order]

Now test it with Sub lesson 1's rule: could the steps change
places? Consent cannot come after the sitting — nobody may
be reconciled against their will. The judge cannot be chosen
after the mediation is held. Each step prepares the next;
that is what makes this a **process**, and that is why it is
written as numbered steps and not as a story about elders.

Write yours the same way: take a real process you know —
settling, building, harvesting, cooking — and ask of each
step the process question: what must happen FIRST, so the
rest can happen at all?

## Say it to your companion

Choose a doing you know well. Tell it to your companion as a
how-to, steps in order, without writing. Your companion listens
with one job: stop you the moment a step is skipped or swapped.
Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the salad how-to in full sentences, steps in order. Read it to someone and ask: could you make it from my writing alone?
2. Make an outline (ዝርዝር) for "how coffee is brewed" (የቡና አፈላል): topic line, then the steps in short lines.
3. Write the full how-to from your outline. Check it against the outline, line by line.
4. Write the steps of preparing one food or craft in your home, in sequence, and number them.
5. Write the five steps of traditional mediation (ባህላዊ ሽምግልና) from memory, numbered. Then write one real process from your own home or work in five numbered steps — and check: does each step prepare the next?
