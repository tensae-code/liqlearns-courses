# Hands-on — How-To Writing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do it, then write it
Make something simple together — a cup of tea, a salad, a paper
boat. One person does each step slowly; everyone says the step
aloud as it is done. Then write the steps in order from memory
and check them against a second doing.

## Step cards
Write the steps of making a kebero (soak the hide, scrape it,
hollow the wood, lace the hide, sun it) on five cards. Mix them.
A partner orders them and reads the how-to aloud — then defends
the order: why must this step come before that one?

## Outline on the wall
For a how-to you will write this week (how coffee is brewed,
how doro wat is made), write the outline first on one shared
sheet: the topic, then three to five short point-lines. Pin it
up. Write from it. At the end, check: does the writing follow
the outline, line by line?
