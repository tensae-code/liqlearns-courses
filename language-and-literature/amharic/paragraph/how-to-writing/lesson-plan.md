# Lesson plan — How-To Writing

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** How-To Writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Paragraph (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** procedural writing — writing how a thing is done, its steps in their order — and the plan you make before you write: the outline (ዝርዝር).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Paragraphs — the learner can already write a paragraph around one idea; a how-to paragraph's one idea is a doing, and its sentences are steps.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Paragraphs (paragraph/first-paragraphs). Recommended, never forced.
- Putting events in order (story/writing-about-stories, Sub lesson 3) is the same order muscle, used on stories there and on doings here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write the steps of making a salad in their order, as the book sets them.
2. Say why a skipped or swapped step breaks a how-to text.
3. Make an outline (ዝርዝር) for a how-to piece before writing it: topic first, points in short lines.
4. Write a full how-to piece from your outline — how one craft or food is done.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. If possible, the real things for one recipe or craft to look at while writing.

## If the learner is stuck
- Steps come out in the order remembered, not the order done → do the doing (or mime it) while saying the steps; the body remembers order better than the eye.
- The writing starts before the plan → stop after the topic line: the outline is three short lines, two minutes — then the writing is easy.
- A step is missing → ask at each step: "and then what does my hand do?" A how-to step is a thing a hand (or eye) actually does.
