# Lesson plan — How-To Writing

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** How-To Writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Paragraph (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** procedural writing — writing how a thing is done, its steps in their order — and the plan you make before you write: the outline (ዝርዝር) — and, at the top, one real process written whole: traditional mediation (ባህላዊ ሽምግልና) in its five steps.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Paragraphs — the learner can already write a paragraph around one idea; a how-to paragraph's one idea is a doing, and its sentences are steps.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Instructions in order** — how-to texts the books teach by: making a salad, making a kebero drum, the steps of doro wat, preparing cabbage — steps named, numbered by their order, nothing skipped.
2. **Plan before you write — the outline (ዝርዝር)** — the Grade 4 guide's writing rule: draft an outline (ዝርዝር) before composing — the topic and its points in short lines first, then the full writing grows from it (teacher guide — evidence).
3. **A real process in five steps — traditional mediation** (teacher guide — evidence) — ባህላዊ ሽምግልና written as a process text: preliminary inquiry; obtaining consent to reconcile; choosing mediators for both sides; choosing the mediation judge; conducting the mediation — with its principles (equality, fairness, balance, truthfulness, humanity) and the parties' oath.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Paragraphs (paragraph/first-paragraphs). Recommended, never forced.
- Putting events in order (story/writing-about-stories, Sub lesson 3) is the same order muscle, used on stories there and on doings here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write the steps of making a salad in their order, as the book sets them.
2. Say why a skipped or swapped step breaks a how-to text.
3. Make an outline (ዝርዝር) for a how-to piece before writing it: topic first, points in short lines.
4. Write a full how-to piece from your outline — how one craft or food is done.
5. Write traditional mediation as a process text: its five steps numbered, in order, each step preparing the next.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. If possible, the real things for one recipe or craft to look at while writing.

## If the learner is stuck
- Steps come out in the order remembered, not the order done → do the doing (or mime it) while saying the steps; the body remembers order better than the eye.
- The writing starts before the plan → stop after the topic line: the outline is three short lines, two minutes — then the writing is easy.
- A step is missing → ask at each step: "and then what does my hand do?" A how-to step is a thing a hand (or eye) actually does.
- The mediation steps blur into a story about elders → put the five step-names back as headings; a process text answers "what is done next?" at every line, and consent must stand before the sitting.
