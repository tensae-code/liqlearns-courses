# Descriptive Paragraphs

## Do it first

Read this short paragraph:

"Culture is what a people has built together over a long time.
It is in the clothes they wear and the food they eat. It is in
their songs, their work and their ways of greeting. A people's
culture shows who they are."

One of these sentences carries the whole paragraph — the others
all hold it up. Which one? And here are two openings: "First he
rose, then he saddled the horse, then he rode out" — and "A
saddle is the seat a rider straps on a horse's back." One tells
what happened; one explains what a thing is. Which is which?
Hold your answers; the sub lessons give the names and the rules.

## Sub lesson 1 — The power sentence: ኃይል ቃል and መንደርደሪያ

You already know a paragraph's sentences all carry one idea.
Now meet the sentence that carries it most: the **topic
sentence**.

In the Grade 5 teaching it is called the **ኃይል ቃል** — the
"power word", the sentence with the power: the one that carries
the paragraph's main idea. The sentences around it are the
**መዘርዝሪያ ዓረፍተነገሮች** — the supporting sentences. They spread
out and hold up what the power sentence says.

[Picture 8780 — the power sentence, and the sentences that hold it up]

The Grade 7 book names the same sentence **መንደርደሪያ ዓረፍተነገር**
— the topic sentence that carries the paragraph's whole idea.
Two names, one job: find that sentence and you hold the whole
paragraph in one line.

The work is always the same three steps:

1. Read the whole paragraph once.
2. Ask: what is the one idea?
3. Find the sentence that says that idea whole — the others
   will be explaining it, proving it, or giving its parts.

In the paragraph you read first, the power sentence is the
first one: "Culture is what a people has built together over a
long time." Every other sentence is a supporting sentence,
showing that idea in clothes, food, songs and greetings.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plan first: the outline (ቢጋር)

Grown-up paragraph writing is process writing: **before**
writing the paragraph, you prepare an outline — a **ቢጋር**. A
ቢጋር is a plan drawn before writing that keeps the paragraph
on its main idea and in order.

[Picture 8781 — the outline in its two formats]

The ቢጋር is written in **two formats**:

- **(ሀ) ብርዕስ መልክ — the topic outline.** Short points, not
  sentences. The worked model is "የአየር ብክለትና መንስኤዎቹ" (Air
  Pollution and Its Causes): 1. የአንዱስትሪዎች ጭስ (with 1.1 ቀላል
  and 1.2 ከባድ under it) 2. ጠረንች 3. የተሽከርካሪዎች፣
  የአውሮፕላኖችና የርኬቶች ጭስ 4. የተፈጥሮ አይጋዎች ጭስ.
- **(ለ) በዓረፍተ ነገር መልክ — the sentence outline.** The points
  are full sentences. The worked model is "ከራምት ሲጠባ" (When
  Harvest Time Comes): 1. the sky darkens… 2. people busy
  themselves… 3. farm inputs grow precious… 4. feasts and
  weddings pause…

[GIF 8784 — an outline growing into its paragraph]

The outline also runs backwards: take a finished paragraph and
unpack it into a ቢጋር — its **ዋና ሀሳብ** (main idea) at the
top, then its supporting points numbered 1.–4., with sub-points
under them. If a paragraph will not unpack, it has no skeleton
— and the writer finds that out before a reader does.

The same planning tool sorts ideas before writing, too: for
ገበያ (market), an idea organiser — a **ሐሳብ ማደራጃ ቢጋር** —
holds rows (ተራ) for vegetables, fruit and grain, and the
paragraph is then expanded from it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Two ways to build a paragraph: ገላጭ and ተራኪ

A paragraph is a unit of sentences carrying one main idea —
and there are two ways to build it out:

[Picture 8782 — explaining and recounting, side by side]

- **ገላጭ ስልት — the descriptive/expository way.** It explains
  what a thing is: its use, its working, its handling. The
  worked model is a paragraph defining **ባህል** (culture) —
  exactly the paragraph you met in Do it first.
- **ተራኪ ስልት — the narrative way.** It recounts an event in
  order of **time, place and circumstance** — what happened,
  then what happened next. The worked model is a short
  narrative about an old man, ባሻ ኤራምዶ.

To classify a paragraph, ask what it does. Explaining what a
thing is → ገላጭ. Walking you through events in their order →
ተራኪ.

Writing practice uses the ተራኪ way directly: choose a topic —
የቡና አፈላል (brewing coffee), ባለፈው ሳምንት (last week), የልብስ
አስተጣጠብ (washing clothes), ጉዟችን (our journey) — and write a
ተራኪ አንቀጽ, a narrative paragraph, keeping the events in the
order they happened.

## Sub lesson 4 — The descriptive paragraph: ሥዕላዊ አንቀጽ

The finest form of the ገላጭ way is the **ሥዕላዊ አንቀጽ** — also
named ምስልከሳች አንቀጽ, the picture-painting paragraph. It is
written with **ምስልከሳች ቃላት** — imagery words — so that the
reader **sees the scene in the mind**.

[Picture 8783 — a misty rocky mountain]

The worked example describes the **Tarmaber mountain**: the
words are chosen so the mountain stands in front of the
reader's eyes — its rock, its mist, its height. The task that
goes with it: write a two-paragraph descriptive text titled
**ብርሃን** (Light).

A second model stands in the descriptive prose about **Wehni
Amba**: a bare, steep mountain, almost no vegetation, rock
like bone, ringed by sharp chain mountains, its nose in the
clouds — whoever slips from its edge is dashed like a
millstone. No sentence says only "the mountain is big and
dangerous"; every sentence shows a thing the eye can meet.
That text's own writing work is this lesson in one line:
expand two outline points into a short paragraph — Sub lesson
2's ቢጋር feeding Sub lesson 4's picture.

The descriptive rule, then: **do not tell the reader the scene
— show it, word by word, until they see it.**

## Say it to your companion

Read your companion a paragraph you wrote. Your companion must
do two things: point to its power sentence (ኃይል ቃል /
መንደርደሪያ), and say whether the paragraph is ገላጭ or ተራኪ —
and why. Then swap, and listen for the same two things in
theirs.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take any paragraph from a book you are reading. Write out its ኃይል ቃል (topic sentence) and list its መዘርዝሪያ ዓረፍተነገሮች (supporting sentences) under it.
2. Choose a topic of your own. Prepare its ቢጋር twice: once in ብርዕስ መልክ (topic outline), once in በዓረፍተ ነገር መልክ (sentence outline).
3. Unpack a finished paragraph into a ቢጋር: ዋና ሀሳብ at the top, supporting points 1.–4. under it.
4. Write a ተራኪ አንቀጽ (narrative paragraph) on ጉዟችን (our journey) or ባለፈው ሳምንት (last week), events in their true order.
5. Write a ሥዕላዊ አንቀጽ about a place you know, with ምስልከሳች ቃላት — then write the ብርሃን (Light) task in two paragraphs.
