# Lesson plan — Nature and Man-Made

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Nature and Man-Made
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how to sort the world twice: natural or man-made — did people make it, or not? — and living or not living.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's own world — the trees, rocks, houses and cars the child passes every day. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before

1. **Made by people, or not?** — sorting the world into natural things — not made by people — and man-made things, with Ethiopia's own places.
2. **Living or not living** — the second sort: living things grow and breathe; non-living things do not.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing. This is a First Steps lesson — a child can start here. The animals and plants lessons next door help, because living things are the animals and plants. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort things into natural (tree, sea, soil, rocks) and man-made (house, car, chair, bed, aeroplane, jumper).
2. Name Ethiopia's own natural places: the Simien Mountains ሰሜን ተራራ and the Abay river የአባይ ወንዝ.
3. Sort things into living (animals, plants, people) and non-living (car, balloon), and name three of each.
4. Say the two test questions: "Did people make it?" and "Does it grow and breathe?"

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls a tree man-made because it grows in a garden → people planted it, but people did not make the tree; the tree grows by itself. Natural.
- Calls a wooden chair natural because it is wood → the wood is natural, but the chair is made by people. The question is about the thing itself: did people make this chair? Yes — man-made.
- Says a stone is living because it is natural → natural and living are two different sorts. A stone is natural, but it does not grow or breathe — non-living.
