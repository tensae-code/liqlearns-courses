# Hands-on — Play and Moving

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Push, pull, lift at home
Push a chair gently to its place. Pull a basket towards you. Lift a light box or a book and carry it across the room. Say the word each time: push, pull, lift, carry.

## Run and jump outside
Run a short way, jump over a line drawn on the ground, throw a soft ball. Rest, then do it again.

## Moving together
With a friend or your family: clean up together, build with blocks or stones together, carry one thing with two hands between two people. Moving together is መተባበር — working together.

## If it is hard
Signs a grown-up can watch for while a child moves and plays (SUPPORTING — the gross motor delay indicators): walking that is unsteady; poor balance; running that is uncoordinated; not yet able to jump over small objects; difficulty with stairs; falling when changing direction; needing help to walk. Seeing a sign is never a label on the child — it only says: use the support activities below, in small steps, and praise every try.

Support activities, written as help, never as a label on the child:
- Walk along a rope laid on the ground — the rope on the left, on the right, between the legs. Walk on a bold line.
- Stand on one leg holding a wall, then let go for a second. Swap legs.
- Jump on the spot, then over one line, then over two lines, then over small things that grow a little.
- Run a short way holding a partner's hand, then alone.
- Kick a ball standing still, then from one metre, then running up to it.
Take part in cultural dances — they build balance and moving together.
