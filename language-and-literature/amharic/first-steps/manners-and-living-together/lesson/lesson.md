# Manners and Living Together

## Do it first

An elder is walking towards you, carrying something heavy. What
do you do? Show me — with your body, not your words. That thing
you just did is this whole lesson: መልካም ስነ-ምግባር, good manners —
how we live together kindly.

## Respect and humility

[Picture 8021 — a child greeting and helping an elder]

Look at this child. An elder comes to the door, and the child
greets him and holds the door for him. This is **ትህትና** (tihitina)
— humility, respect. Respect looks like this: greet the elder,
stand for the elder, help the elder, carry what is heavy.

## The kind words

Three sentences carry good manners everywhere:

When we do wrong, we must say **sorry**.
When good is done for us, we must say **thank you**.
**እባክዎ** (ibakwo) — **please** — is a GOOD thing to say when we
ask. Anyone who says "please is a wrong thing to say" is wrong.

## Living together

Living together (አብሮነት) is a picture with many parts: friends
**going together**, **playing together**, **studying together**,
**helping a friend**, **eating together**.

And more: **helping our elders**, **respecting each other**,
**caring for our parents**, **loving our parents**.

Living together also means helping people with disabilities and
supporting the elderly — nobody is left outside the together.

## Say it to your companion

Say the three kind sentences to your companion, each one with a
little scene: "I broke it — I am sorry." "You helped me — thank
you." "Give me the cup — እባክዎ, please." Your companion answers
kindly. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Greet an elder today the respectful way — first, standing,
   kindly.
2. Hold a door, or carry something, for someone today.
3. Use all three kind sentences once each: sorry, thank you,
   እባክዎ.
4. Do one thing together with a friend — play, study, or eat —
   and help them once inside it.
