# Lesson plan — Feelings

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Feelings
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the names of the feelings a
face can show — happy, surprised, sad, angry, and their neighbours.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's
own world — the child has felt every one of these feelings.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short,
single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09). Naming a feeling and
matching two faces with the same feeling are one skill practised
two ways, not two sequential parts.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: My Body — the same face, whose parts this
  lesson now reads. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the feeling on a face: መደሰት (happy), surprised, ማዘን (sad),
   መናደድ (angry), ማኩረፍ (sulking), መከፋት (upset), መፍራት (afraid).
2. Match two faces that show the same feeling.
3. Show a feeling on their own face and say its name.

## What you need
Your own face. A mirror helps, but is not needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner makes faces in a mirror (or for a
   friend) before any names are given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the four big faces one by one, then the
   wider feeling words the older books write (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8013]: a picture of four big faces — happy,
     surprised, sad, angry — the student names each feeling, then
     finds the same feeling on their own face.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 8014]: a drawn face changing from happy to sad to
     angry — the student says the new feeling each time the face
     changes.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice saying the feeling words slowly — መደሰት,
     ማዘን, መናደድ — the student repeats and makes the face.
     Described only in this build; no audio file is made.
3. **Say it to your companion** — "I am feeling …" with the face to
   match (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching,
   true-or-false — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the feelings diary of one day
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes sad and angry → look at the mouth and the eyebrows
  together: sad turns the mouth down softly; angry pulls the
  eyebrows down hard.
- Cannot name the feeling → let the child show it on their own face
  first; the name comes after the face, never before.
- This lesson's next door: Manners and Living Together — what we
  do with our feelings around other people.
