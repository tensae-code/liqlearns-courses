# Feelings

## Do it first

Make the biggest smile you can. Now make a sad face. Now an angry
face. Your face just told three stories without one word. This
lesson teaches the names of those stories — the feelings (ስሜት).

## The feelings on a face

[Picture 8013 — the four big faces]

Look at the four faces.

The first face is smiling, eyes nearly closed with joy. This is
**happy** — መደሰት (medeset). We feel it when something good happens.

The second face has wide eyes and a round, open mouth. This is
**surprised** — something happened that we did not expect.

The third face has tears and a mouth turned down. This is **sad**
— ማዘን (mazen). We feel it when we lose something, or when we are
hurt.

The fourth face has eyebrows pulled down hard and a tight mouth.
This is **angry** — መናደድ (menaded). We feel it when something is
unfair, or when we are stopped from what we want.

There are more feelings. **ማኩረፍ** (makuref) is sulking — the face
turned away, not speaking. **መከፋት** (mekefat) is being upset or
offended. **መፍራት** (mefrat) is being afraid.

Watch this face change, and name each new feeling as it comes.

[GIF 8014 — a face changing happy to sad to angry]

Two faces can show the same feeling even when the children are
different. When you match feelings, look at the mouth and the
eyebrows — they tell the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Today I am feeling …" — and make the face.
Your companion guesses the feeling's name from your face before you
say it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw four faces on paper: happy, surprised, sad, angry. Write
   the name under each one — መደሰት, ማዘን, መናደድ for the ones you know
   in Amharic.
2. Three times today — morning, midday, evening — stop and ask:
   what am I feeling? Say the name out loud.
3. Look at a family member's face. What feeling is it showing?
   Ask them if you read it right.
