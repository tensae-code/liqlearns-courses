# Lesson plan — Farming

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** farm work then and now — the field ploughed with oxen, the tools and grains farmers work with, and how city and countryside farm work differ.
**This lesson's own level:** pre-primary — it stands on nothing but
the child's own world: their body, their home, their street.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **Farm work then and now** — sorting traditional and modern farm work, and city farm work against countryside farm work.
2. **Tools and grains — the discussion** — a discussion activity: the names and uses of traditional farm tools, the grain types compared by size, and ox or donkey or horse ploughing against tractor ploughing (SUPPORTING evidence, labelled).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Basic Needs — farming is where the first need, food, comes from. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort farm-work pictures: traditional or modern.
2. Say how the field is ploughed with oxen — the way most farmers in our country plough.
3. Name traditional farm tools and grain types, and compare grains by size (the discussion).
4. Say one difference between ox ploughing and tractor ploughing.

## What you need
The picture in this lesson; if you can, real grains — wheat, beans, chickpeas, corn, peas — in the hand for the discussion.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes traditional and modern → ask what pulls the plough: oxen — traditional, the way of long ago that still feeds us; a tractor — modern.
- The discussion stalls → put real grains in the hand and sort them by size first; words come easier with things to hold.
