# Lesson plan — Counting

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Counting
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** counting — one number for each
thing, in order — from 1 to 9, then zero and 10, then on to 20, with
the number names in Amharic and Ethiopia's own Ge'ez numerals.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's own
world — fingers, stones, cups and spoons at home. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Counting 1 to 9** — one number at a time, the books' own shape: the big numeral, the matching set of things to count, the dotted numeral to trace — 1 banana, 2 cups, 3 strawberries, 4 pencils, 5 spoons, 6 lions, 7 apples, 8 cars, 9 lemons — then counting sets and matching them to their number.
2. **Zero, and counting to 10** — zero comes FIRST, before 1, the way the Level One book teaches it: zero is empty — an empty jar beside a full jar. Then the numbers 0–10, each with its Ge'ez numeral, and filling the missing number in a line.
3. **Counting to 20 and number names** — the counter and the thing counted; the number names as words (አንድ 1 … አስር 10), digits written as words and words matched to digits; counting sets and writing how many, on to 20.
4. **Ge'ez numerals — Ethiopia's own numbers** — ፩ = 1 … ፲ = 10: reading them, copy-writing them, matching the Western numeral to the Ge'ez numeral, and filling the missing Ge'ez numeral.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — this is a first lesson. The child's
  own fingers are the first counting tool. Recommended, never forced.
- Adding and taking away is next door in this unit — it counts on
  this lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Count a set of things 1–9, touching each thing once, and say the
   whole count.
2. Say and read the number names አንድ (and, 1) … ዘጠኝ (zeṭeñ, 9),
   and trace and write the numerals 1–9.
3. Say what zero means — ዜሮ (zero) is empty, nothing there — and
   count 0–10 in order.
4. Count sets and write how many, up to 20, and fill a missing
   number in a line (0 _ 2 _ 4 …).
5. Read the number names as words and match digits to words
   (2 = ሁለት, 10 = አስር).
6. Read the Ge'ez numerals ፩–፲ and match each one to its Western
   numeral.

## What you need
Things to count — fingers first, then stones, spoons, cups, bottle
tops. Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts a thing twice, or skips one → touch each thing and move it
  aside as you count it; a thing touched is a thing counted once.
- Says the numbers but not in order → walk the 0–20 line on Picture
  8121 forward, then back, until the line is one piece.
- Mixes the digit and the name → the chart on Picture 8120 holds all
  three together: Ge'ez numeral, Western numeral, name.
- Thinks zero is "nothing to learn" → zero is a real number here:
  the empty jar IS the lesson (Picture 8119).
- This lesson's next door: Adding and taking away (same unit) —
  counting put to work.
