# Hands-on — Counting

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Stone counting
Gather ten stones. Count them one by one, moving each stone to a
new pile as you say its number: አንድ, ሁለት, ሶስት… The last number
is how many. Mix them up and count again — the count must not
change.

## The empty jar
Take two jars or cups. Fill one with biscuits, beans or stones.
Leave the other empty. Say: "This jar has many. This jar has
ዜሮ — zero." Then take things out of the full jar one by one,
counting down, until both jars hold zero.

## Count the house
Walk through your home counting out loud, touching each thing
once: doors, chairs, cups, shoes, people. Count one thing in each
room up to 20. Say the whole count in a sentence: "There are
fourteen cups."

## The Ge'ez line
On paper, write 1–10 in a row. Under each, write its Ge'ez
numeral: ፩ ፪ ፫ ፬ ፭ ፮ ፯ ፰ ፱ ፲. Read the line forwards, then
backwards, then point at any numeral and say its name.

## If it is hard
Work with real things, not only pictures: count stones, sticks
and pencils you can hold, and say which number is bigger and
which is smaller with the things in your hands (SNE toolkit
support, printed pp.30–31). Break the line into small steps —
1–5 first, then 0–10, then to 20 — and check each step before
the next one.
