# How Families Can Help with Amharic at Home

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the books ask of families

The Grade 7 and Grade 8 books open with useful advice to parents and guardians. The books are activity books, not lecture books: the child learns by doing — thinking deeply, analysing, summarising, asking questions, solving problems, deciding, communicating and judging their own work. The four skills — listening, speaking, reading, writing — grow together, and the home is half the school. A family that talks, reads and asks with the child is doing real teaching, even without a classroom.

## Sub lesson 2 — The eleven home activities

The prefaces give families eleven things to do at home: 1) ask about the day's topic; 2) read together, taking turns; 3) ask discussion questions — what kind of text is it, what is its topic, what do we learn, what is our own opinion, what else do we know or have heard or read, what do we want to know, what do we specially want to do; 4) talk about word meanings; 5) find other texts in books, newspapers and magazines; 6) encourage reading alone; 7) follow television, radio and web items on the topic; 8) look through the notebook together; 9) check writing and punctuation; 10) join homework such as interviews and speech practice; 11) encourage short letters and opinion pieces.

## Sub lesson 3 — What each strand practises

The prefaces also say what each strand of the book practises, so a family knows what it is helping with. Listening and speaking grow the ear and the tongue. Fluent reading (አቀላጥፎ ማንበብ) is reading smoothly, without dropping letters. Comprehension is understanding and summarising. Vocabulary is words in their sentences. Grammar at these grades covers singular and plural nouns, present and past verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and simple and compound sentences. Writing covers narrative, descriptive and comparative texts, with revision and spelling. When a child struggles, ask which strand the struggle is in — then practise that strand's home activity.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the key Amharic words of this lesson in your notebook, each with its meaning or its job.
2. Do the last sub lesson's task once more on your own, without looking, then check against the lesson.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, in your own words.
