# Lesson plan — Ready Hands — Before the Pencil

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Ready Hands — Before the Pencil
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a hand gets ready to write
before it writes — it holds, it scribbles freely, and it learns to
travel along a dotted line: straight, then wavy, then zigzag.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's
own world — a small child with a pencil for the first times. It is
the first lesson of the course, and the shared Pencil and Lines
course (the pencil grip itself, and lines of every kind) stands
next door, after it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Hold and scribble** — pick up the pencil, hold it, and make free scribble marks (መሞነጫጨር) in a big empty space: the hand moves, the pencil obeys, nothing can be wrong.
2. **Follow the dotted line** — join the dots along a line and travel dotted paths with the pencil: straight(ish) lines first, then wavy lines, then zigzag and wavy lines — always left to right, always slow.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — this is the first door of the
  course. The child's own play (drawing in sand, carrying,
  squeezing) is all the preparation there is.
- Next door, not before: the shared **Pencil and Lines** course
  teaches the pencil grip itself, in its own lessons, and lines
  of every kind. This lesson does not remake the grip — it sends
  the learner there for it (cross-reference, not a gate).
- After this lesson: The Fidel (Unit 2) — its letters are traced
  by joining dots, which is exactly the hand this lesson builds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Pick up a pencil and hold it ready to draw.
2. Fill a big space with free scribble lines, moving the pencil
   on purpose.
3. Join dots along a straight(ish) dotted path without lifting
   the pencil off the road.
4. Follow a dotted wavy line all the way, left to right.
5. Follow a dotted zigzag line, turning at each corner.

## What you need
A pencil and paper with big empty spaces and dotted paths on it.
Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner picks up the pencil and fills a
   big empty box with scribbles — any marks, any direction — for
   as long as it is fun; the lesson then names what the hand just
   did (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — holding the pencil and free scribbling;
   joining dots along a line; the wavy road; the zigzag road
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 7990] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of four dotted paths on warm cream paper — a straight dotted line, a wavy dotted line, a zigzag dotted line, and a soft curving dotted line — the student looks at each path and runs a finger along it before any pencil work.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 7988] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of a pencil travelling slowly along a dotted wavy line, left to right, its drawn line filling the dots behind it — the student watches the whole trip, then follows the same wavy road on paper.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 7989] in Sub lesson 2: a picture of a pencil travelling slowly along a dotted zigzag line, turning at each corner and finishing the road — the student watches, then follows the same zigzag road on paper.
3. **Say it to your companion** — tell your companion the road
   your pencil is taking ("straight… now wavy… up, down, up")
   while you draw it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     ready hand in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching,
   ordering, writing — served at the learner's level and
   teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md, including the fine-motor support
   activities in "If it is hard".
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw dotted roads for a toy car, scribble
   a sky, and make a zigzag road of your own (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- The hand tires or the fingers hurt → stop the pencil work and
  use the "If it is hard" activities in hands-on.md (spin and
  rotate, squeeze, palm-to-palm ball, textures): hand strength
  is built by play, and the support rule is to teach a comfortable
  hold BEFORE writing is demanded — never push through pain.
- The pencil keeps leaving the dotted road → make the road
  bigger (dots closer together, path wider on the page) and slow
  down; a finger travels the road first, the pencil second.
- The child presses far too hard or far too lightly → practise
  on different surfaces (sand tray, flour, a big sheet on the
  floor) where pressure shows; gentle-force play is in
  hands-on.md.
- Grip questions belong to the shared Pencil and Lines course —
  this lesson asks only for a hold that works, scribbles that
  flow, and a pencil that can follow a road.
