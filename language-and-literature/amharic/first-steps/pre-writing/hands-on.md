# Hands-on — Ready Hands — Before the Pencil

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Scribble in sand and flour
Spread sand, flour or fine soil in a tray or a flat box. Scribble
in it with a finger, then with a stick, then with the pencil's
blunt end. Wipe it smooth with your palm and go again. Marks
that can be wiped away are marks nobody is afraid to make.

## Roads for real things
Draw a dotted road on the floor with chalk, or lay a rope on the
ground as a wavy road. First a toy car travels it. Then walk it
yourself, heel to toe, slowly. Then let the pencil travel the
same shape on paper, small. Body first, paper second — the road
is the same road at every size.

## Dot-to-dot treasure
A grown-up dots a path across a page that ends at a drawn star,
a cup, or a small treat picture. The child joins the dots to
find where the road ends. New treasure, new road, every day —
the joining is the game, the treasure is the excuse.

## If it is hard
Fine-motor support activities — Ethiopia's own Special Needs
toolkit exercises for small hands, written as help, never as a
label on the child (SUPPORTING — SNE toolkit, printed pp.15–17).

- **Spin and rotate:** spin and turn things in the fingers — a
  ball, a pencil, coins — round and round, both hands.
- **Page turning:** turn the pages of a book often, one page at
  a time, with the fingertips. A whole book turned is a hand
  workout in disguise.
- **Squeeze:** stretch and squeeze the fingers; squeeze a ball,
  a sponge, soft fruit — and bounce a ball with the fingers of
  both hands.
- **Palm to palm:** pass a small ball from hand to hand,
  palm-to-palm, slowly, then faster; throw and catch small balls.
- **Textures:** explore things of different textures with the
  fingers — rough bark, smooth stone, soft cloth, grain, water —
  and say what each one feels like.
- **Real-life fastenings:** zipping, buttoning, socks and laces —
  real clothes, real practice, no worksheet needed.
- **Gentle force:** for a child who presses too hard or whose
  fingers cramp — draw on soft ground (sand, flour) where hard
  pressing fails, and on a big sheet on the floor where the whole
  arm can help the fingers.
- **Hold before writing:** a comfortable, correct hold is taught
  and settled BEFORE writing is demanded. Scribble and play
  first; letters later. That order is the toolkit's own rule.
