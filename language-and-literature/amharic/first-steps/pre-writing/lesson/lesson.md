# Ready Hands — Before the Pencil

## Do it first

Take a pencil. Here is a big empty box — a whole page of nothing. Fill it. Scribble! Round and round, up and down, fast lines, slow lines — መሞነጫጨር, free scribbling, where no mark can be wrong because the marks are yours.

Done? Look at your page. Your hand just did the first writing work there is: it held the pencil, and the pencil obeyed your hand. Everything else in this lesson is that same thing, made steadier.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hold and scribble

Hold the pencil the way that lets it move — pinched near its front, resting easy, so the tip can run. (The full grip lesson, with its own pictures and practice, lives in the shared **Pencil and Lines** course — this lesson only needs a hold that works.)

Now scribble again, but slower. Watch the tip. Make your lines go where YOU send them:

- Lines that run across the page, left to right.
- Lines that bounce up and down.
- A big round scribble, like a ball of string.
- A small tight scribble in one corner.

That steering — slow, on purpose, where you send it — is a ready hand being born. Scribble a little every day. A hand that scribbles freely today traces letters easily tomorrow.

## Sub lesson 2 — Follow the dotted line

Now the pencil gets a road: a line of dots. Your job is to travel it — start at the first dot, keep the tip on the dots, and do not lift the pencil until the road ends. Always travel left to right, the way reading and writing will go.

First, look at the roads. Run your finger along each one before the pencil tries it:

[Picture 7990 — dotted paths: straight, wavy, zigzag, curving]

**The straight(ish) road.** Join the dots along a line — the road from a megaphone's sound to the ear that hears it, the road from a watering can to the three flowers waiting for water. Straight roads are kind: keep the tip moving, dot to dot to dot.

**The wavy road.** Now a road that bends like a river — the ants' road, the trumpets' road. Watch a pencil travel it first:

[GIF 7988 — a pencil travels the dotted wavy line]

See how the pencil never rushes and never lifts? Your turn. Up the hill of the wave, down into its valley, up again. Slow is smooth, and smooth is the whole skill.

**The zigzag road.** Last, the road with corners — the toucan's road to its perch, the monkey's road to the banana. Watch:

[GIF 7989 — a pencil travels the dotted zigzag line]

At every corner the pencil stops for a breath, turns, and climbs the next side. Corners are not mistakes — they are the road. Travel them one side at a time, and finish the whole road.

When your pencil can travel all three roads — straight, wavy, zigzag — without lifting and without leaving the dots, your hand is ready. The fidel are waiting, and every letter in them is just a small road your pencil already knows how to travel.

## Say it to your companion

Draw a road while you tell it: "My pencil goes straight… now wavy, up, down, up… now a corner — turn! — and up again." Your companion listens and says the road back to you while you draw it a second time. If their words and your line disagree, one of you found a corner the other missed — look together.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a dotted road across a page for a toy car or a bottle cap to "drive" — then drive your pencil along it first to show the way.
2. Scribble a sky at the top of a page (round, cloudy scribbles), then draw one straight dotted road under it and travel it.
3. Make your own zigzag road: five corners, dots all the way. Travel it, then give it to someone at home and watch them travel it.
4. Every day this week: one box of free scribble, one wavy road, one zigzag road. Small and daily beats big and rare.
