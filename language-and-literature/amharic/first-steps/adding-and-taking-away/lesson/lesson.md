# Adding and Taking Away

## Do it first

Put 3 stones in front of you. Count them: 3. Now put 2 more stones
with them and count the whole pile again. The count grew. You have
just added.

Now take 2 stones away and count what is left. The count shrank.
You have just taken away.

This lesson is the first meeting with adding, taking away, and
the signs that compare two counts. The full version of all three
lives in the Maths course — this is where they begin.

## Sub lesson 1 — Adding — putting together

**መደመር (medemer)** means adding to what we have. Its sign is
**+**, and a sum says: this many, and this many more, makes how
many altogether?

The first sums: 1 + 1 = 2. Then 3 + 3 = _, and 5 + 1 = _ — count
the pictures and the count answers.

[Picture 8123 — 3 stones and 2 stones join: 3 + 2 = 5]

The way to answer is always the same: put the + sign between the
two sets, count the pictures altogether, and the last number is
the answer.

Then the sums grow in families. Making tens: 5 + 5, 6 + 4, 3 + 7 —
each one lands on 10. The three-family: 3 + 1, 3 + 2, 3 + 3,
3 + 4, 3 + 5, 3 + 6 — the first number stays, the answer climbs.

Adding also works with tally marks — little lines standing for
things: 3 + 3, 4 + 5, 2 + 2, 8 + 1, 3 + 7, counted line by line.

## Sub lesson 2 — Taking away

**መቀነስ (meqenes)** means taking away from what we have. Its sign
is **−**, the taking-away sign (የመቀነስ ምልክት).

The first example is a picture: 3 things, 1 taken away, 2 left —
3 − 1 = 2. Count what is left; never count what went away.

[Picture 8124 — 5 stones, 2 crossed out and gone: 5 − 2 = 3]

The sums take the smaller from the bigger: 3 − 2, 4 − 2, 5 − 4,
6 − 3, 7 − 6. And with tally marks, taking away crosses lines
out: // − / = / is 2 − 1 = 1; then 4 − 4, 3 − 2, 4 − 3, 5 − 2,
6 − 3, 7 − 4, 8 − 2, 9 − 5. When everything is taken away —
4 − 4 — what is left is ዜሮ, zero, the empty count from the
Counting lesson.

## Sub lesson 3 — Comparing — bigger, smaller, equal

Two counts can be compared, and the comparing signs
(የማወዳደሪያ ምልክቶች) say how:

- **>** — ይበልጣል (yibeltal): is greater. The first count is bigger.
- **<** — ያንሳል (yansal): is less. The first count is smaller.
- **=** — እኩል ይሆናል (ikul yihonal): is equal. The counts match.

[Picture 8125 — the balance three ways: 5 > 3, 2 < 4, 3 = 3]

The sign goes between two picture counts: count the left set,
count the right set, then choose. The open mouth of > and <
always faces the bigger count — 5 > 3 opens to the 5, and
2 < 4 opens to the 4. When the two sides balance like the
third pan — 3 against 3 — the sign is =.

Comparing rides on the sums too: after a tally addition, the
answer can be compared with another count using the same three
signs.

The same comparing eye works on numbers and sizes themselves.
Circle the BIGGEST number in each row — in a row holding 16, 17,
18, 19, 20, the biggest is 20 — and circle the SMALLEST number
in its row, even when the row is read sideways. Write the number
that comes BEFORE: _ 11, _ 13, _ 15, _ 17, _ 19 — the answers
are 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 — and the next number after the odd
climb: after 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 comes 11. Comparing words get written
for pictures too — ትንሽ (small) and ትልቅ (big) for tyres and
trees — and pictures are ordered from smallest to biggest and
marked in order. (The size words themselves live in the Size
and Quantity lesson; here they are the comparing tools.)

## Say it to your companion

Make a sum with stones: "3 stones, and 2 more — how many?"
Your companion counts and answers in a whole sentence: "3 + 2 = 5."
Then a taking-away: "5 stones, take away 2 — how many are left?"
Then hold stones in both hands, open them, and say the comparing
sentence: "6 is greater than 4 — 6 > 4." Swap turns.

## Do It Yourself

1. With stones or bottle tops, work these additions and write
   each sum: 3 + 2, 5 + 5, 3 + 6, 4 + 5.
2. Work these taking-aways and write each answer: 5 − 2, 7 − 6,
   4 − 4, 9 − 5.
3. Count two sets of things at home — cups and plates, shoes and
   socks — and write the comparing sign between their counts.
4. Draw a tally sum: draw 3 lines, add 4 lines, count and write
   3 + 4 = 7 in tallies and in numerals.
5. Tell someone at home the three comparing signs and what each
   one says: ይበልጣል, ያንሳል, እኩል ይሆናል.
