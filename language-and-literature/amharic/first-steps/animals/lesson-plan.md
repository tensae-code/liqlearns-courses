# Lesson plan — Animals

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Animals
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the names of the animals around the child — the animals at home, the wild animals, the walia of Ethiopia's mountains, and the farm animals with their homes and their young.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's own world — the animals the child has already seen at home, on the street, and in pictures. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Animals at home** — the animals that live with people — naming them, and the colour sets the child colours in.
2. **Wild animals** — the animals that live far from people, in the bush and the mountains.
3. **The walia — Ethiopia's own animal** — the walia ዋሊያ, the mountain animal of Ethiopia — its body parts, and how it is not like the animals at home.
4. **Farm animals, their homes and their young** — the farm animals and what they give us, where each animal sleeps, and the young animals' names.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing. This is a First Steps lesson — a child can start here. The home and family lessons next door help, because the first animals are the animals of the home. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the home animals: ድመት (dimet, cat), ውሻ (wusha, dog), ፈረስ (feres, horse), አህያ (ahya, donkey), ፍየል (fiyel, goat), በግ (beg, sheep), በሬ (bere, ox), the cow, ዶሮ (doro, hen), ግመል (gimel, camel).
2. Name the wild animals: giraffe, አንበሳ (anbesa, lion), hyena, ዝሆን (zihon, elephant), monkey, kangaroo, zebra, ነብር (nebr, tiger) — and sort any animal into home or wild.
3. Point to the walia ዋሊያ and name its parts: እግር (leg), ጭራ (tail), ቀንድ (horn), ፂም (beard).
4. Match the farm animals to their homes (ቆጥ, በረት, ጉሮኖ) and name the young animals (ጥጃ, ግልገል, ውርንጭላ, ቡችላ).
5. Say what the ox, the cow and the hen give us: farm work, milk and meat, eggs.
6. Tell apart the animals that live in water — ጉማሬ (hippopotamus), አዞ (crocodile), እንቁራሪት (frog) — from the animals that live on land.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes home and wild animals → ask one question: "Does this animal sleep near people, or far away?" Near people = home animal; far away = wild.
- Calls the walia a goat → they do look alike. The walia's horns are big and curved, it has a beard ፂም, and it lives high in the mountains where no goat of the home lives.
- Forgets the young animals' names → go back to the mothers: the cow's young is the calf ጥጃ, the dog's young is the puppy ቡችላ. Mother first, then young.
