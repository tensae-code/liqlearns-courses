# Animals

## Do it first

Look at the animals in this picture. Point at every animal you already know, and say its name out loud. Then count: how many animals are there?

[Picture 8060 — the home animals]

## Sub lesson 1 — Animals at home

Some animals live with people, at home and on the farm. Here they are, one by one. Say each name after you hear it.

- ድመት (dimet) — the cat. Small and soft, it sleeps in the house.
- ውሻ (wusha) — the dog. It guards the house and plays with children.
- ፈረስ (feres) — the horse. Big and strong, people ride it.
- አህያ (ahya) — the donkey. It carries heavy things.
- ፍየል (fiyel) — the goat. It climbs and it eats leaves.
- በግ (beg) — the sheep. Soft wool covers its body.
- በሬ (bere) — the ox. It pulls the plough on the farm.
- The cow — she gives us milk.
- ዶሮ (doro) — the hen. She gives us eggs.
- ግመል (gimel) — the camel. It walks far in dry places and carries loads.

Now colour them in your mind: the colour sets of the book ask you to colour the cow and the horse, then the dog and the cat. Which colours will you choose?

## Sub lesson 2 — Wild animals

Some animals do not live with people. They live far away, in the bush, in the forest and on the mountains. We see them in pictures, and we do not touch them.

[Picture 8061 — the wild animals]

- The giraffe — the tallest of all, with a very long neck.
- አንበሳ (anbesa) — the lion.
- The hyena — it laughs in the night, people say.
- ዝሆን (zihon) — the elephant. The biggest animal on land.
- ዝንጀሮ — the monkey. It climbs trees.
- The kangaroo — it jumps, and its baby rides in a pocket on its belly.
- The zebra — black and white stripes all over.
- ነብር (nebr) — the tiger.
- And one more, standing on the rock in the middle of the picture — the walia. The next part is all about the walia.

The test of this part: home, or wild? If the animal sleeps near people, it is a home animal. If it lives far from people, it is a wild animal.

## Sub lesson 3 — The walia — Ethiopia's own animal

[Picture 8062 — the walia]

This is the walia — ዋሊያ. The walia is Ethiopia's own animal. It lives high up in the big mountains, on the rocks, where the air is cold.

Look at its body, part by part:

- ቀንድ (horn) — two big horns, curved like moons, on its head.
- ፂም (beard) — a small beard under its chin.
- እግር (leg) — strong legs for climbing rocks.
- ጭራ (tail) — a short tail.

How is the walia different from us, and from the cat at home? The walia has horns — we do not, and the cat does not. The walia is big; the cat is small. The walia lives on the cold mountain rocks; the cat sleeps in the warm house.

Say the word the book's way: ዋ + ሊ + ያ — ዋሊያ, walia.

## Sub lesson 4 — Farm animals, their homes and their young

The farm animals work with the farmer, and each one gives us something.

The ox — በሬ. The ox is a home animal used for farm work. Most farmers in our country plough with oxen. Its meat is food, and its hide is used for clothes, shoes, belts and bags.

The cow — ላም. There is a song about the cow: the song says she carries milk in her udder, and we drink her milk and eat her meat and are healthy. It is a song — listen to it and sing along. The cow also gives us calves, and her hide is used for clothes, shoes, belts and bags, like the ox's.

[Picture 8066 — match the animal to its home]

The hen — ዶሮ. The hen is a farm animal. She gives eggs. Her meat and her eggs are food. She sits on her eggs and hatches chicks from them.

Where does each animal sleep? Every animal has its home:

- ቆጥ (qot') — the cattle pen, where the cattle rest.
- በረት (beret) — the barn.
- ጉሮኖ (gurono) — the chicken house, the hen's home.

And the young animals have their own names:

- ጥጃ (ṭija) — the calf, the cow's young.
- ግልገል (gilgel) — the horse's young.
- ውርንጭላ (wurnch'ila) — the cat's young.
- ቡችላ (buchila) — the puppy, the dog's young.

Last, the animals of the water. Some animals live in the water and on the land: ጉማሬ — the hippopotamus; አዞ — the crocodile; እንቁራሪት — the frog. The walia, the red fox ቀይ ቀበሮ, the cow and the hen live on the land.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: three animals that live at home, three wild animals, and one thing the walia has that a cat does not have. Then your companion names an animal, and you say: home, or wild?

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the animal you like best and colour it. Say its name in Amharic.
2. Join the dots to finish the ox በሬ and the goat ፍየል, like the book's dot-to-dot page.
3. At home, find one animal. Say: its name, where it sleeps, and what it gives us.
4. Write the animal names: አንበሳ, ድመት, በግ, ነብር, ውሻ, ዝሆን — copy each one slowly.
