# Money

## Do it first

Look at the pictures of money below. Point to the biggest number you can find. Point to the smallest. Money carries numbers — and every number has a name.

## Sub lesson 1 — Birr notes

Ethiopia's paper money is called **birr — ብር**. Here are the notes, drawn big so you can learn them. These are drawn teaching pictures that stand in for the fronts of the real notes.

[Picture 8163 — the birr notes]

- **10 — አስር ብር** (asir birr), the blue-ish note
- **50 — ሃምሳ ብር** (hamsa birr)
- **100 — መቶ ብር** (meto birr)
- **200 — ሁለት መቶ ብር** (hulet meto birr)

And the smaller notes: **1 — አንድ ብር** and **5 — አምስት ብር**.

The bigger the number, the bigger the money. 200 birr is more than 100 birr. 100 birr is more than 10 birr.

## Sub lesson 2 — Santim coins

The small, round, hard money is called **santim — ሳንቲም**.

[Picture 8164 — the santim coins]

- **5 — አምስት ሳንቲም**
- **10 — አስር ሳንቲም**
- **25 — ሀያ አምስት ሳንቲም**
- **50 — ሃምሳ ሳንቲም**

And the smallest of all: **1 — አንድ ሳንቲም**.

Coins are round. Notes are paper. Santim are the small money; birr are the big money.

## Say it to your companion

Point to a note or a coin in the pictures. Your companion names it — how many birr, or how many santim. Then swap. Finish by naming all four notes in order: 10, 50, 100, 200.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name every note in Picture 8163, in order, smallest first.
2. Name every coin in Picture 8164, in order, smallest first.
3. Match game: say a name — አስር ብር — and point to the right money before a grown-up counts to five.
4. Which is more money: 50 birr or 10 birr? 50 santim or 5 santim?
