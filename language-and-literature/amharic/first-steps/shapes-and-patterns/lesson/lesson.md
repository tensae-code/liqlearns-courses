# Shapes and Patterns

## Do it first

Look around the room. Find something round, like a plate. Find
something with four straight sides, like a book or a window. Find
something with a point, like the corner of a slice of watermelon.
Everything you found has a SHAPE, and every shape has a name.
This lesson teaches the names — and then teaches you to read the
rows shapes make when they line up in patterns.

## Sub lesson 1 — The shapes

[Picture 8129 — the shape cards with their Amharic names]

- **ክብ (kib) — circle.** Perfectly round, no corners. A round
  bread is a circle.
- **ካሬ (kare) — square.** Four straight sides, all the same. A
  window is a square.
- **ጎነ ሶስት (gone sost) — triangle.** Three sides, three corners.
  A slice of watermelon is a triangle.
- **አራት ማዕዘን — rectangle.** Four sides — two long, two short.
  A TV is a rectangle.
- **ሞላላ (molala) — oval.** A circle stretched out. An egg is
  an oval.
- **ኮከብ — star.** The shape that shines in the sky.

Learn them the books' way: colour each shape. Trace each shape on
the dots — your pencil learns the shape by going round it:

[GIF 8131 — a pencil tracing round a circle, a square, a triangle]

Then match the shapes to the things you see around you — that is
where shapes really live: the triangle to the watermelon slice,
the oval to the egg, the circle to the round bread, the square
to the window, the rectangle to the TV, the star to the star.

Then work the shapes harder: colour the geometric shapes, make
them again, and join shapes together to build things — a house
is a square with a triangle roof. Count the shapes in a picture
and write how many. Draw the shapes. And write their names:
አራት ጎን, ክብ, ጎነ ሶስት, ካሬ, ሞላላ. Last, the test the other way:
circle the object that shows the shape named.

## Sub lesson 2 — Patterns — what comes next

Shapes and colours line up in rows, and a row with a rule is a
pattern (ንድፍ). Read the row like reading words, and the rule
tells you what comes next.

[Picture 8130 — three pattern rows, each with an empty box at the end]

First row: circle, square, circle, square, circle — what comes
next? The row takes turns, so a square comes next. Second row:
triangle, circle, triangle, circle, triangle — a circle comes
next. Third row: star, star, circle, star, star — a circle
comes next, because the rule is two stars, then one circle.

Patterns run on colours too: colour following the pattern — the
rows of fish swim in a colour pattern, and the last fish waits
uncoloured for you to give it the pattern's next colour. And
copy the pattern: colour-and-shape rows (circle-rectangle…,
triangle-heart…) copied into empty boxes, following the example,
until the new row matches the old one exactly.

The whole skill is one question, asked of every row: say the row
aloud, find its rule, and say what comes next — before you draw it.

## Say it to your companion

Name a shape — ክብ! — and your companion must find a thing in the
room with that shape and name the thing: "The bread — ክብ."
Then draw a pattern row of four shapes and stop. Your companion
says what comes next, and why. Swap turns.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the six shapes and write each one's name under it:
   ክብ, ካሬ, ጎነ ሶስት, አራት ማዕዘን, ሞላላ, ኮከብ.
2. Go shape-hunting at home: find one real thing for each shape
   and say its shape name.
3. Build a house or a car out of shapes only — squares, a
   triangle, circles — and count which shapes you used.
4. Make your own pattern row of six shapes, leave the seventh
   box empty, and ask someone at home: what comes next?
5. Trace a circle, a square and a triangle on dotted lines you
   draw yourself, slowly, the way the pencil in the GIF goes.
