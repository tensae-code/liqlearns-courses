# Caring for Books and Shared Things

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book-care message — የጥንቃቄ መልዕክት

[Picture 27905 — books on a shelf, one inside its protective cover with its ribbon]

Every Grade 7 and Grade 8 Amharic book opens with the same message, የጥንቃቄ መልዕክት — the book-care message. The book belongs to the school, not to one child. Ten rules keep it undamaged: cover it, keep it dry and clean, turn pages gently, never write in it — write in your notebook instead — carry it in a bag, keep it away from food and small children, shelve it upright, repair a small tear at once, never lend it carelessly, and hand it back whole at the year's end, so another student can learn from it after you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cover it, shelve it, pass it on

Do the three hand-jobs of care. First, cover the book — paper or plastic wrapped neatly round the cover protects it from rain and dust. Second, shelve it upright, not bent under heavy things. Third, when you hand it on, hand it whole: all its pages, its cover clean, nothing torn out. A book you cared for can teach many children after you; a ruined book teaches no one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Shared things beyond books

Books are not the only shared things. A classroom chair, a school water tap, a library globe, a shared football — each one serves many people, and each one lasts only if everyone treats it as their own and as everyone's. The rule is one: use it gently, leave it clean, report damage at once, and never take a shared thing as if it were yours alone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the key Amharic words of this lesson in your notebook, each with its meaning or its job.
2. Do the last sub lesson's task once more on your own, without looking, then check against the lesson.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, in your own words.
