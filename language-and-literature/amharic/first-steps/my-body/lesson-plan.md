# Lesson plan — My Body

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** My Body
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the names of the parts of my
body, and what the five senses do.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's
own world — pre-primary lessons stand on the child's own body, home
and family, which the child already knows by living.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The parts of my body** — the fifteen named parts on the labelled
   figure: ፀጉር (hair), ግንባር (forehead), ቅንድብ (eyebrow), አይን (eye),
   ጆሮ (ear), አፍንጫ (nose), ጥርስ (teeth), አንገት (neck), ትከሻ (shoulder),
   ደረት (chest), እጅ (hand), ሆድ (belly), ጭን (thigh), ጉልበት (knee),
   እግር (leg/foot).
2. **The five senses** — አይን ለማየት (the eye is for seeing), አፍንጫ
   ለማሽት (the nose for smelling), ምላስ ለመቅመስ (the tongue for
   tasting), ጆሮ ለመስማት (the ear for hearing), and the hand/skin
   for feeling.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the
gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — this is a first lesson of First
  Steps. Recommended, never forced.
- Feelings sits next door in this same unit and uses the same face.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Point to and name the fifteen body parts, in Amharic.
2. Say what each sense does: the eye sees, the nose smells, the
   tongue tastes, the ear hears, the hand feels.
3. Point to the right part on their own body when its name is said.

## What you need
Your own body. A mirror helps, but is not needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner touches and names the parts of
   their own body before any picture is shown (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the labelled figure part by part, then the
   five senses and their jobs (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8010]: a picture of a child figure with all
     fifteen parts labelled in Amharic and English — the student
     points to each part on the picture, then on their own body.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 21004]: a picture of a fat person and a thin person, and of a child with short hair and a child with long hair — the student points and says ወፍራም, ቀጭን, short hair, long hair.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 21005]: a picture of two children exchanging greetings (ሰላምታ ሲለዋወጡ), shaking hands — the student greets their companion the same way and says ሰላም.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8011]: a picture of the five senses in five
     panels — eye, nose, tongue, ear, hand — the student says what
     each one does.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 8012]: a pointing dot that travels to the eye,
     the ear, the nose, the tongue and the hand as each sense is
     named — the student points to the same part on their own body.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice naming the fifteen parts slowly, then the
     five sense sentences (አይን ለማየት …) — the student points and
     repeats. Described only in this build; no audio file is made.
   - ADD AUDIO: the "All about me" mezmur (SUPPORTING — Training
     module, Session 3.1 Activity 1, PDF pp. 58–61) — a sung song
     that counts body parts 1–10: hair 1, forehead 2, eye 3, nose 4,
     ear 5, cheek 6, chin 7, neck 8, hand 9, leg 10 — the student
     sings along and touches each part at its number. Described
     only in this build.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name your parts and your senses
   to your companion (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the body
     song and the senses in the super-simple voice (made later).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching,
   true-or-false, short-answer — served at the learner's level
   (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the mirror game and the senses walk
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes ear and eye → slow down to the senses GIF: one sense at a
  time, the child touches the part before the next name comes.
- Knows the parts but not the Amharic names → say the name, the
  child points; pointing first, naming after — never the other way.
- This lesson's next door: Feelings — the same face, now showing
  what we feel.
