# Cleanliness

## Do it first

Show me — without water, with your hands in the air — how you wash
your hands. Rub them the way you really do. Good. Now learn the
real order, the one that makes hands truly clean (ንጽሕና, cleanliness).

## Sub lesson 1 — Wash your hands

Washing has an order — የእጅ አስተጣጠብ ቅደም ተከተል, the hand-washing
order. Four steps:

**1. Apply soap** — ሳሙና መቀባት. Wet your hands and put soap on them.
**2. Rub well with soap** — rub the palms, the backs of the hands,
and between the fingers.
**3. Rinse with clean water** — let the water carry the soap and
the dirt away.
**4. Dry with a towel** — በፎጣ ማድረቅ.

[GIF 8015 — hand washing in order]

When do we wash? Before eating — always. After the toilet —
always. Soap, rub, rinse, dry.

## Sub lesson 2 — Brush your teeth

Teeth have their own routine — የጥርስ አፋፋቅ.

Brush **across** the teeth. Brush **up and down**. Brush the
**tongue** gently. Then take a cup of water, **rinse**, and spit.

[GIF 8016 — teeth brushing, across and up-and-down]

Brush every day — that is the rule. Clean teeth do not ache, and a
clean mouth smiles wide.

## Sub lesson 3 — Keep clean every day

Cleanliness is a whole day of small jobs: **wash your face** with
soap and water, **brush your teeth**, **wash your body** with water
and soap, **wash your feet**, **wash your hair**, **comb your
hair**. Eat good food and keep your surroundings clean too.

Keeping healthy has four pictures: brushing teeth, washing the
face, eating, bathing. Do all four and you stay ጤና — healthy.

The things that keep us clean have names: **ሳሙና** (samuna, soap),
**ጥፍር መቁረጫ** (nail cutter), **የጥርስ ቡርሽ** (toothbrush), **የጫማ ቡሩሽ**
(shoe brush), **ማበጠሪያ** (mabet'eriya, comb).

And when a sneeze comes? Cover your mouth and nose — with your
elbow, like this:

[GIF 8017 — a sneeze covered with the elbow]

Now hear about a girl who did all of this. Her name is **Meseret**
— ጎበዟ መሰረት, Gobez Meseret, Clever Meseret. She is a village girl of
ten. She never eats without washing her hands first. After the
toilet she washes with soap and water. She keeps her nails cut
short. She brushes her teeth. When she sneezes or coughs, she
covers her nose and mouth. She does it every day, not only some
days — and the whole community holds her up as the example for
their children. (This is a summary; the story itself lives in the
Story title.)

Be like Meseret.


## Sub lesson 4 — Grade 5: The Smile — Fatuma, Momina, and the dentist


### The Smile (ፈገግታ)

Fatuma brushes her teeth after breakfast and supper and smiles freely; Momina hides her smile behind her palm. In the science lesson on dental care — a pea-sized drop of toothpaste, brush every tooth, gently scrub the tongue, twice a day and after sweets — Momina is seized by toothache, is taken to the dentist, treated, and returns a month later for cleaning and instruction. Her smile comes back. The tooth-brushing steps themselves stand at sub lessons 2 and 3 of this lesson as KG picture steps; this part stocks the Grade 5 book's own story — the reason the steps matter, told whole.


## Say it to your companion

Teach your companion the four hand-washing steps in order:
"First — soap. Second — rub. Third — rinse. Fourth — dry."
Your companion mimes each step as you say it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Wash your hands for real at the next meal — all four steps, in
   order. Say each step's name as you do it.
2. Brush your teeth tonight the book's way: across, up-and-down,
   tongue, rinse.
3. Check your nails. If they are long, ask a grown-up to help you
   cut them — like Meseret.
4. Practise the elbow sneeze three times, so your arm is ready
   when a real sneeze comes.
