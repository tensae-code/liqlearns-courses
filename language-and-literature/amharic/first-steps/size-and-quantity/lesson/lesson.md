# Size and Quantity

## Do it first

Take two things, one in each hand — two stones, two balls, two
books. One takes up more of your hand than the other. One is
bigger. Now try to lift two boxes: one packed full, one empty.
One fights your arms; one floats up. Bigger, smaller, heavier,
lighter — this lesson teaches the words for all of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — How big — comparing size and how many

Comparing words come in pairs, and each pair looks at things a
different way.

**ትልቅ (tilq) — big · ትንሽ (tinish) — small.** How much room a
thing takes. A lion is big; a mouse is small. A big ball stands
beside a small ball, and even drawn thin and fat figures compare:
the fat figure is bigger around than the thin one.

[Picture 8132 — the big lion (ትልቅ) and the small mouse (ትንሽ)]

**ብዙ — many · ጥቂት — few.** How many things there are — this
pair is counting compared. Many mangoes in the basket against
few mangoes. And a jar can be full — **ሙሉ** — or empty —
**ባዶ**, the same empty as ዜሮ's jar in the Counting lesson, or
nearly empty, holding only a few.

[Picture 8133 — the full jar, the empty jar, and the jar with few]

Watch the pairs move: first a big ball beside a small ball, then
many stars beside few stars.

[GIF 21144 — big beside small, then many beside few]

**ሰፊ — wide · ጠባብ — narrow.** How wide a thing opens: a wide
vase against a narrow vase.

**ረዥም — long · አጭር — short.** How far a thing reaches: a tall,
long tree against a short tree.

Say the pairs on the books' own pictures: lion and mouse, full
and nearly-empty bead jars, wide and narrow vases, tall and
short trees. The word always answers: compared to what?

Now sort by size with Ethiopia's own animals. Here are three:
the **ዋሊያ** (walia), the mountain animal with the curved horns;
the **አጋዘን** (agazen), the gazelle; and the **ቀይ ቀበሮ** (qey
q'ebero), the red fox.

[Picture 21008 — the walia, the gazelle and the red fox, biggest to smallest]

Put them in size order. The walia is the biggest — ትልቅ. The
gazelle stands in the middle. The red fox is the smallest —
ትንሽ. Circle the biggest animal. Circle the smallest animal.
That circling is the comparing work itself: look, compare, and
mark the biggest and the smallest.

## Sub lesson 2 — How heavy

Size is what the eye sees. Weight is what the arms feel, and it
has its own pair:

**ከባድ (kebad) — heavy · ቀላል (qelal) — light.**

[GIF 8134 — the heavy box that barely lifts, and the light box lifted easily]

The heavy box fights you: you bend, you strain, it barely leaves
the ground. The light box floats up in one hand. A thing can be
big and light — a big empty box — or small and heavy — a small
stone. That is why weight needs its own words: ከባድ and ቀላል
describe what the lift tells you, not what the eye tells you.

Describe the things around you by weight: lift, feel, then say
the word.

## Say it to your companion

Point at two things. Your companion makes the comparing sentence,
in Amharic where they can: "ትልቅ — big. ትንሽ — small." Then two
jars: many or few? Then two boxes to lift: ከባድ or ቀላል? Swap
turns, and let your companion choose the things.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find the biggest and the smallest book or box in your home
   and say: ትልቅ, ትንሽ.
2. Fill one jar full (ሙሉ), leave one empty (ባዶ), and put only
   a few (ጥቂት) stones in a third. Say each jar's word.
3. Find a wide cup and a narrow cup; a long stick and a short
   stick. Say the four words: ሰፊ, ጠባብ, ረዥም, አጭር.
4. Lift-test: pack a box until it is ከባድ for you, then empty it
   until it is ቀላል. Say the word before and after.
5. Put three things in order from smallest to biggest, the way
   the Level Two book orders pictures by size — smallest first,
   biggest last — and say the comparing word for each step.
