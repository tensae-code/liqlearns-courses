# Lesson plan — Size and Quantity

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Size and Quantity
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — First Steps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the comparing words for size
and amount — ትልቅ/ትንሽ (big/small), ብዙ/ጥቂት (many/few), ሰፊ/ጠባብ
(wide/narrow), ረዥም/አጭር (long/short) — and for weight: ከባድ/ቀላል
(heavy/light).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Counting (this unit) —
many and few are counts — and on the child's own world: a lion and
a mouse, full and empty jars, a box too heavy to lift. It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **How big — comparing size and how many** — ትልቅ (tilq, big) / ትንሽ (tinish, small), ብዙ (many) / ጥቂት (few), ሰፊ (wide) / ጠባብ (narrow), ረዥም (long) / አጭር (short), on the books' own pictures: big and small balls, thin and fat figures, many and few mangoes, a lion against a mouse, full and nearly-empty jars, wide and narrow vases, tall and short trees.
2. **How heavy** — ከባድ (kebad, heavy) / ቀላል (qelal, light): describing quantity by weight — the box that will not lift and the box that lifts easily.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Counting (this unit) — many/few is a count
  compared. Recommended, never forced.
- Ordering things by size — smallest to biggest, and the
  comparing words written for pictures — is worked again in the
  Adding and taking away lesson's comparing-signs part and in
  this lesson's own hands-on.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say which of two things is ትልቅ (big) and which is ትንሽ (small)
   — a lion against a mouse, a big ball against a small ball.
2. Say whether a jar or a pile holds ብዙ (many) or ጥቂት (few), and
   whether a jar is full (ሙሉ) or empty (ባዶ).
3. Use ሰፊ/ጠባብ (wide/narrow) and ረዥም/አጭር (long/short) on real
   things: vases, trees, sticks.
4. Say which of two things is ከባድ (heavy) and which is ቀላል
   (light), by trying to lift them.
5. Put things in order by size and use the comparing words for
   pictures of tyres and trees.

## What you need
Two balls or two stones of clearly different sizes. Two jars or
cups. A heavy box and a light box (an empty one) to lift.

2. **The explanation** — size and amount first, in the books'
   pairs; then weight (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8132]: a picture of a big lion (ትልቅ) beside a small mouse (ትንሽ) — the student says which is big and which is small, using the Amharic words.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 8133]: a picture of three jars — one full (ሙሉ) with many stars, one empty (ባዶ), one with few stars (ጥቂት) — the student names each jar's amount.

   - ADD GIF [GIF 8134]: a picture of a child figure straining at a big heavy box (ከባድ) that barely moves, while a second figure lifts a small light box (ቀላል) easily — the student says which box is heavy and which is light.
3. **Say it to your companion** — point at two things; your

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "big" for everything that is more → separate the two ideas:
  big/small is how much room a thing takes; many/few is how many
  things there are. A jar can hold many small stones.
- Cannot feel heavy/light from pictures → the GIF is only the
  signpost; the real test is lifting. Give the child the two boxes.
- Mixes long/short with big/small → lay two sticks side by side:
  length runs along the ground; size is the whole thing.
- This lesson's next door: Position words (same unit) — where
  these compared things stand.
