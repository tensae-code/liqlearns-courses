# Amharic course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Built from the bottom, the same way as the English course: a
learner does not start at stories — they start with the hand,
the letters, and the world they can point at. The path climbs by
the size of the thing:

**First Steps → Fidel → Word → Sentence → Paragraph → Essay → Story**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead" path points
down this ladder to the bottom).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **First Steps** — the pre-primary level (Ethiopia's ECCE
   workbooks, ages 4–6): my body, my home, animals, colours,
   counting, playing, staying safe — the world a small child can
   point at, taught in Amharic, picture first. The hand's first
   readiness (pre-writing) also sits here, before the shared
   Pencil and Lines course.
2. **Fidel** (ፊደል) — the Amharic letters themselves: every
   family and its seven forms, the sounds, first reading, and
   building words from fidel. The alphabet and the phonics of
   Amharic in one title, because in Amharic the letter and its
   sound family are one thing.
3. **Word** — the word kinds and word work: nouns, verbs, and
   the rest; words that mean the same, words that mean the
   opposite; building words.
4. **Sentence** — one idea, whole and ended properly: first
   sentences, the marks that end them, questions and answers.
5. **Paragraph** — sentences together making one bigger idea.
6. **Essay** — paragraphs together making a whole piece.
7. **Story** — stories and reading passages: people, a place,
   things happening — and the songs (mezmur) Ethiopia's books
   teach by.

Conversation lessons sit inside the units where the books place
them (greetings and talk run through every grade's book).
