# Lesson plan — More Punctuation

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** More Punctuation
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the punctuation the higher books add to the first four marks — the exclamation mark and its seven feelings, quotation marks and single quotation marks, the colon and the slash, the dash, parentheses and ellipsis, and abbreviations closed with one dot.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Marks That Help Us Read (first-punctuation: ሁለት ነጥብ, the four dots, ነጠላ ሰረዝ, የጥያቄ ምልክት) and on The Sentence and Its Marks. Every mark here grows out of one of those first marks or works beside them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The exclamation mark and the seven feelings** — ትዕምርተአንክሮ (!) follows strong feeling, in seven kinds: ድንጋጤ shock, መገረም surprise, ደስታ joy, ትካዜ despair, ዛቻ threat, ማሞገስ admiration, ማበረታት encouragement — each with its own cries.
2. **Quotation marks, grown** — ትዕምርተጥቅስ " " holds another person's exact words; ነጠላ ትዕምርተጥቅስ ' ' holds a quotation inside a quotation.
3. **The colon and the slash** — ኹለት ነጥብ ከሰረዝ (፡-) announces a list of points inside a sentence; እዝባር (/) shortens compound words only, and separates dates and reference numbers.
4. **Dash, parentheses, ellipsis** — ሠረዝ (–) breaks a thought; ቅንፍ ( ) pockets a small explanation; ሦስት ነጠብጣቦች (…) leave words out or trail speech off.
5. **Abbreviations with one dot** — አንድ ነጥብ (.) shortens a word two ways — first two letters (መስ.) or first and last letters (ወሮ.) — and whole names by their initials (እ.ማ.ማ.ኮ); only common, accepted shortenings may be used.
6. **Quotation marks first met — and the Corona paragraph** — in the Grade 3 book, quotation marks “ ” are first introduced as the mark of speech taken from another person or book, and the learner punctuates a whole paragraph about ኮረና (corona/COVID-19) using ? ። ! ፣ and “ ” together.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Marks That Help Us Read — the first four marks, which this lesson grows from. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the feeling behind any exclamation mark — one of the seven — and read its cry with that feeling.
2. Put a speaker's exact words inside " ", and a quote inside a quote inside ' '.
3. Use the colon ፡- to announce a list of points before a sentence closes.
4. Use the slash correctly: compounds only (ት/ቤት), dates (18/11/2013), reference numbers — and never slash a single word.
5. Set an aside inside ቅንፍ, break a thought with ሠረዝ, and trail speech off with ሦስት ነጠብጣቦች.
6. Shorten a word with አንድ ነጥብ both ways, and a long name by its initials — using only accepted forms.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads every ! as a shout → walk the seven feelings on Picture 8728: a threat, a joy and a despair do not sound alike; the mark is the same, the feeling is the reading.
- Slashes every long word → the rule is compounds only: ትምህርትቤት is two words grown together (ትምህርት + ቤት), so it may split at the join; መጽሐፍ is one word and may not.
- Puts the small quote marks outside → nest them like boxes: the whole speech in the big box " ", the quoted words in the small box ' ' inside it.
- Invents abbreviations → only accepted, common shortenings: if the reader would not already know it, the word must be written whole.
