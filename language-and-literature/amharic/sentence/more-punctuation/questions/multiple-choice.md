# More Punctuation — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "አይዞህ! አትፍራ! በርታ!" — Take heart! Don't fear! Be strong! The feeling behind these exclamations is —
a) ዛቻ, threat  b) ማበረታት, encouragement  c) ትካዜ, despair
**Answer: b) ማበረታት** — the cries lift the hearer up; a threat would say ቆይ አሳይሃለሁ!

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** How many feelings does the book catalogue for the exclamation mark?
a) three  b) five  c) seven
**Answer: c) seven** — ድንጋጤ, መገረም, ደስታ, ትካዜ, ዛቻ, ማሞገስ, ማበረታት.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Quotation marks (ትዕምርተጥቅስ) show —
a) a list is coming  b) words taken directly from another person's speech or writing  c) a word has been shortened
**Answer: b)** — the words inside the marks are the speaker's own, exactly.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A quotation inside a quotation is fenced with —
a) the slash  b) the four dots  c) single quotation marks — ነጠላ ትዕምርተጥቅስ ' '
**Answer: c)** — big marks hold the speech; small marks hold the quote inside it.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The colon (ኹለት ነጥብ ከሰረዝ ፡-) in a sentence signals —
a) the sentence has ended  b) a list of points follows before the sentence's idea closes  c) speech is trailing off
**Answer: b)** — as in the three parts of a complete text: መግቢያ፣ ሀተታ፣ መደምደሚያ.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Which of these እዝባር uses is WRONG?
a) ትምህርትቤት → ት/ቤት  b) the date 18/11/2013  c) መጽሐፍ → መ/ጽሐፍ
**Answer: c)** — the slash abbreviates only compound words; መጽሐፍ is a single word and must never be slashed.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** "ጅብ የማያውቁት ሀገር ሄዶ…" — the three dots at the end are —
a) ሦስት ነጠብጣቦች, the ellipsis: words left out  b) ቅንፍ, parentheses  c) ሠረዝ, the dash
**Answer: a)** — the proverb's rest is known and left unsaid; the dots also mark broken, trailing speech.

**8. (Sub lesson 5)** ወይዘሮ shortened to ወሮ. uses which way of አንድ ነጥብ?
a) the first two letters  b) the first and last letters  c) the initials of a long name
**Answer: b) the first and last letters** — ወ…ሮ. The first-two-letters way gives መስተዳድር → መስ.

**9. (Sub lesson 6)** Quotation marks “ ” first mean what?
a) The sentence has ended  b) Speech taken from another person or book  c) A list follows
**Answer: b)** — the words inside are carried exactly from somebody else's mouth or page.

**10. (Sub lesson 6)** The Corona paragraph exercise uses which marks together?
a) Only ።  b) ? ። ! ፣ and “ ”  c) Only “ ”
**Answer: b)** — the full set, each mark where the reading-aloud ear puts it.

**11. (Sub lesson 6)** Words a paragraph quotes from another person are written how?
a) In bold  b) Fenced inside “ ”  c) Underlined twice
**Answer: b)** — quoted speech wears the quotation marks.

**12. (Sub lesson 7)** In the greetings-paragraph exercise, what do the dashes in the exercise show?
a) Where a punctuation mark must be supplied  b) Words to delete  c) New vocabulary
**Answer: a) Where a punctuation mark must be supplied** — complete the paragraph with the correct marks.

**13. (Sub lesson 7)** Jemal, in the paragraph to punctuate, is what?
a) A famous school footballer  b) A herder  c) A driver
**Answer: a) A famous school footballer** — the descriptive paragraph's subject.

**14. (Sub lesson 7)** The herders in the Grade 6 dialogue are named what?
a) Demle and Berihun  b) Asrat and Lubaba  c) Mulugeta and Azalu
**Answer: a) Demle and Berihun** — in new Buhe festival clothes.


**15. (Sub lesson 8)** In the Grade 4 note box, the colon (፡-) announces…?
a) The sentence has ended  b) More follows — a list or explanation is coming  c) A question
**Answer: b) More follows** — the colon promises; the list keeps the promise.

**16. (Sub lesson 8)** ሦስት ነጠብጣብ (…) shows…?
a) An abbreviation  b) Speech or feeling cut off, trailing away  c) A shortened compound word
**Answer: b) Speech or feeling cut off** — the three dots trail into silence; the single dot shortens, እዝባር cuts compounds.

**17. (Sub lesson 8)** እዝባር is the name of which mark?
a) (.)  b) (…)  c) (/)
**Answer: c) (/)** — እዝባር, the slash; (.) is the single dot, (…) the ሦስት ነጠብጣብ.

**18. (Sub lesson 9)** In the field-trip passage, the numbers in the text are —
a) times of day  b) placeholders for the punctuation marks you must supply  c) page numbers
**Answer: b) placeholders for the punctuation marks you must supply** — numbers stand where marks belong; sense decides each one.

**19. (Sub lesson 10)** The father's conclusion is —
a) ride always  b) whatever you do, you cannot please everyone  c) never travel with a donkey
**Answer: b) whatever you do, you cannot please everyone** — the fable's moral, spoken by the father at the passage's end.
