# Writing Sentences

## Do it first

Here are three loose words: **ልጅ** (a child), **ወደ** (to), **ሔደ** (went).

Try to join them into one whole saying before you read on. Say your
joining out loud. Keep it in your mind — the first sub lesson shows
the books' own way of doing exactly this.

## Sub lesson 1 — Build a sentence from the given words

A sentence is built, not found. You are given words, and you join
them so that together they say one whole thing.

Here is a model the books themselves give. The given word is **ሄደ**
(went). The built sentence is:

**ልጅ ወደ ትምህርት ቤት ሔደ።** — The child went to school.

[Picture 8510 — word cards joining into a sentence]

See how it is built: who went (ልጅ), where to (ወደ ትምህርት ቤት), and
the doing word at the end (ሔደ). Two more of the books' own models:

- Given **ዓይኖች** (eyes): **የልጅ ዓይኖች ትላልቅ ናቸው።** — The child's eyes are big.
- Given **ውኃ** (water): **ካምግብ በኋላ ውኃ እጠጣለሁ።** — After food, I drink water.

Your turn with the books' own given-word sets. In the hygiene
lessons the given words are ፀጉር (hair), መቍረጥ (cutting), ማበጠር
(combing), ጤናማ (healthy), ልብስ (clothes), ጥፍር (nails), ማጠብ
(washing). Join some of them into sentences about keeping clean —
one whole saying each time. Then build a sentence from words given
in class, carefully and readably, the way the Grade 3 guide asks:
words first, the model in mind, then your own joining.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dictation — hear it, write it

Dictation is writing with your ears. Someone reads; you write
exactly what you hear — then you read it back.

The books' way is always the same: listen first, in silence. Hear
the word a second time. Write it. At the end, read back everything
you wrote and check it fidel by fidel.

The practice list, from the books' own dictation pages:

**ተዘጋ · ተማረ · አወጣ · ጠላት · ኩረት**

[GIF 8511 — dictation words appearing one by one]

Write each word as it is read to you. Then the harder step the
Grade 3 book sets: the teacher reads whole words aloud, you write
them exactly, and then you read them back aloud — your own writing,
in your own voice. If a fidel is wrong, you have found the exact
place to practise. That is what dictation is for.

## Sub lesson 3 — Word order — putting the words in their places

Words have places. When every word stands in its place, a sentence
is easy to read. When the words are mixed up, even good words are
hard:

**ጨዋታ እወዳለሁ መጫወት** — mixed up, as the book prints it.

[Picture 8512 — mixed-up words and their numbered places]

Read it aloud. Hear where your voice trips? That trip tells you a
word is out of its place. The work of this sub lesson is the books'
own repair work: take the mixed words, ask of each one "where do
you belong?", and write the sentence whole again in its right
order, with its end mark (።) at the end.

The same repair is practised in the books on whole story sentences
(the monkey story's jumbled sentences) and on shuffled words that
must be unscrambled into full sentences. The rule never changes:
every word in its place, and the sentence says one whole thing.

## Say it to your companion

Read your companion the sentence you built in Sub lesson 1. Your
companion answers two questions: does it say one whole thing? Is
every word in its place? Then swap — dictate your five words to
your companion, and let them write.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build two sentences: one from the word **ውኃ**, one from words of your own choosing. Write them carefully and read them aloud.
2. Ask someone at home to dictate five words to you. Write them, read them back, and check every fidel together.
3. Write one good sentence. Cut it into words, mix the words, and give the mix to a friend to repair. Then repair theirs.
