# My First Sentences

## Do it first

Listen: "I planted a tree." That is a whole saying — it tells
you something complete. Now listen to just one word from it:
"tree." The word alone tells you almost nothing. A sentence is
words joined so they tell a whole thing. In this lesson you read
whole sentences first — one breath each — and at the end you
build your own sentences out of single words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading whole sentences

Here are your first reading sentences. Read each one whole, like
one breath:

- **ዛፍ ተከልኩ** (Zaf tekilku) — I planted a tree.
- **አበባ እወዳለሁ** (Abeba iwodalehu) — I like flowers.
- **ብርቱካን በላሁ** (Birtukan belahu) — I ate an orange.

[Picture 8213 — a child planting a tree: ዛፍ ተከልኩ]

Read them again. First whole. Then touch each word as you say
it: ዛፍ — ተከልኩ. Two words, one saying. That is how a sentence
works: the words hold hands.

Now the sentences of your own clean body — read each whole:

- I washed my face with soap.
- I brushed my teeth.
- I washed my body.
- I washed my feet.
- I washed my hair.
- I combed my hair.

These are also the cleanliness lesson's sentences — the same
sentences live in two homes: there they teach washing, here they
teach reading whole.

Sentences about water:

- The Abay dam was built.
- Water is useful for people.
- There is water in our bodies.
- We need water to keep clean.

Sentences about moving — carrying, pushing, pulling:

- I carried the carton.
- They pushed the tyre.
- We pulled the car.

Sentences about the sky:

- There are stars in the sky.
- The sun gives heat.
- Ethiopia launched a rocket.
- When I grow up I will be a researcher.

Sentences from the market:

- I went to the market.
- I bought bread.
- Its price is five… — this one is a starter: the book leaves it
  open for you to finish. "Its price is five birr" is one way.

Sentences about farm life:

- The farmer sows seed.
- The field is ploughed with oxen.
- Sheep's meat is eaten.
- The cow gives milk.
- The hen lays eggs.
- The shepherd guards the cattle.
- The cow gives birth to a calf.
- The farmer harvests what he sowed.

You have read thirty whole sayings. Every one of them stands on
its own feet. That is a sentence.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making my own sentences

Now turn it around. You are given one word — your job is to
build a whole sentence that carries it.

The first words:

- **ውሃ** (wuha) — water. One sentence is already yours: "We need
  water to keep clean." Now make a different one.
- **ሃይቅ** (hayq) — lake. Think: what is on a lake? Who goes
  there? Say it whole.
- **ዓባይ** (Abay) — the great river. Think: where does it run?
  What was built on it? Say it whole.

The word alone is not the answer. The whole saying is.

And words can join hands with each other. Here are four given
words:

- **በሬ** (bere) — ox
- **ሸጠ** (shete) — sold
- **ገበሬው** (geberew) — the farmer
- **ፈረስ** (feres) — horse

Put three of them in a row and read the row: ገበሬው በሬ ሸጠ — the
farmer sold an ox. Change one word: ገበሬው ፈረስ ሸጠ — the farmer
sold a horse. You made both sentences — the words were given,
the saying is yours.

## Say it to your companion

Take turns. One of you reads a sentence from Sub lesson 1 whole,
in one breath. The other says what it tells — who did what. Then
the reader names one word from that sentence, and the other must
answer with a brand-new whole sentence carrying that word. Five
rounds, then swap readers.

## Do It Yourself

1. Read the three first sentences aloud, whole, three times
   each. Each time, one breath.
2. Pick the farm set. Read it to someone at home and tell them
   which sentence you like best — and why, in a whole sentence.
3. Make one sentence each for ውሃ, ሃይቅ and ዓባይ. Say them
   aloud; write the one you like best.
4. Write your own joining of the four given words — use በሬ in
   one sentence and ፈረስ in another.
5. Finish the market starter in your own way: "Its price is
   five…" — finish it, then say what you bought.
