# Joining Words

## Do it first

Read these two sentences, with a full stop and a full breath between them:

**የሒሣብ ትምህርት እወዳለሁ። ጥሩ ውጤት አላመጣሁም።**
I like maths lessons. I did not get a good result.

Now read them as ONE sentence, with a joining word standing between them:

**የሒሣብ ትምህርት እወዳለሁ፤ ይሁን እንጂ ጥሩ ውጤት አላመጣሁም።**
I like maths lessons; however, I did not get a good result.

One breath changed. The second sentence now pushes against the first — and the joining word, **ይሁን እንጂ**, is what does the pushing. You met the first joining words in The Word Kinds — እና (and) and ወይም (or). This lesson is those words grown up: the full family of joiners, the leaning joiners that bind one clause to another, and the words that carry a reader across a whole text.

## Sub lesson 1 — Coordinating joiners: አያያዦች

A **coordinating joiner (አያያዥ)** ties **two simple sentences** together into one **compound sentence (ድርብ ዓረፍተነገር)**. Each side could stand alone; the joiner decides how they stand *together*. Here is the grown family:

[Picture 8725 — coordinating joiners]

- **እና** — and: it adds the second sentence beside the first.
- **ወይም** — or: it offers a choice between them.
- **ግን / ነገርግን** — but: the second sentence pushes against the first.
- **እንጂ** — but / except: it turns the first sentence back.
- **ይሁን እንጂ** — however: the push, said formally.
- **ቢሆንም** — although / even so.
- **ስለዚህ** — therefore: the second sentence is the result of the first.
- **በመሆኑም** — so, accordingly: the result again, in formal dress.
- **ምክንያቱም** — because: the second sentence gives the reason.

[GIF 8733 — two simple sentences join into a compound]

The work is in the choosing. Join the same pair with እና and both facts sit side by side; join them with ነገርግን and they argue; join them with ስለዚህ and one becomes the other's result. When you build a compound sentence, first ask: what is the second sentence doing to the first — adding, choosing, pushing, or following from it? The answer picks the joiner.

## Sub lesson 2 — Subordinate conjunctions: ጥገኛ መስተፃምር

Some joiners do not tie equals. A **subordinate conjunction (ጥገኛ መስተፃምር)** makes one clause **lean** on another: the leaning clause cannot stand alone, because its verb is bound by the joiner.

The leaning joiners are: **ስለ** (because), **እንደ** (as / like), **ከ** (if / from), **ለ** (for), **ቢ**, **ሲ** (when / while), **ሊ**.

Watch one lean:

**አህመድ ቶሎ ስለመጣ ደብተሩን አገኘ።** — Because Ahmed came early, he found his exercise book.

The ስለ rides inside the verb — ስለመጣ, *because he came* — and that half leans: *because Ahmed came early…* — early for what? The main half completes it: ደብተሩን አገኘ, *he found his exercise book*.

[Picture 8726 — subordinate conjunctions]

A leaning half can also stand waiting for you to finish it:

**ሱሰኛነት ሰዎችን ስለሚጎዳ …** — Since addiction harms people, …
Finished: **ሱሰኛነት ሰዎችን ስለሚጎዳ ከሱስ ነፃ እንሁን።** — Since addiction harms people, let us be free of addiction.

Feel the difference from Sub lesson 1: a coordinating joiner ties two standing sentences; a subordinate conjunction bends one clause down so it hangs on the other. The leaning clause is the reason, the time, or the condition; the main clause is what stands.

## Sub lesson 3 — Transitional words: መሸጋገሪያ ቃላት

Now lift the joiners out of the single sentence and into a whole piece of writing. A **transitional word (መሸጋገሪያ ቃል)** stands at a crossing — most often at the head of a new sentence — and carries the reader across from the idea before to the idea after:

[Picture 8727 — transitional words]

- **ስለዚህ** — therefore: what follows is the result. The reader crosses forward.
- **ይሁንእንጂ** — however: what follows pushes back. The reader crosses against the stream.
- **በመሆኑም / ስለሆነም** — accordingly / so: the result again, dressed formally.
- **ነገርግን** — but: the turn, at the start of a sentence.
- **ምክንያቱም** — because: what follows explains what came before.

Without them, a text is a row of separate stones: fact. fact. fact. With them, the stones become a path — the reader always knows whether the next idea follows from the last, argues with it, or explains it. When you write, ask at every crossing: should my reader walk forward, turn, or look back for the reason? Put the transitional word that walks them that way.

Notice what you now hold: the same family of words works at three sizes — inside one compound sentence (Sub lesson 1), bending a clause inside a complex sentence (Sub lesson 2), and carrying a whole text from sentence to sentence (Sub lesson 3). One family, three sizes of joining.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Grade 4 book's conjunction bench: ወይም, እና, አለበለዚያ, ስለሆነም

The Grade 4 book sets the same work at its own bench: complete each sentence with the right conjunction, choosing from **ወይም** (or), **እና** (and), **አለበለዚያ** (otherwise), **ስለሆነም** (therefore / that is why).

The choosing is the learning. ወይም offers a choice between two things. እና adds one thing to another. አለበለዚያ warns what follows if the first part does not happen. ስለሆነም carries the result out of its reason. Read the two halves first, ask what the second half does to the first — chooses, adds, warns, or follows — and the bank gives you exactly one word that fits.

## Say it to your companion

Join this pair three ways — with እና, with ነገርግን, with ስለዚህ — and say how the meaning moves each time: የሒሣብ ትምህርት እወዳለሁ + ጥሩ ውጤት አላመጣሁም. Then read your companion a leaning half — ሱሰኛነት ሰዎችን ስለሚጎዳ … — and let them finish it. Finish by reading a short paragraph of your own aloud and naming every transitional word you planted at its crossings.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five compound (ድርብ) sentences, each with a different coordinating joiner from Picture 8725.
2. Join the pair የሒሣብ ትምህርት እወዳለሁ / ጥሩ ውጤት አላመጣሁም in four ways — with ግን, with ይሁን እንጂ, with ስለዚህ, and with እና — and read all four aloud. Then try ወይም and say, in one sentence, why it does not fit this pair.
3. Write four sentences of your own with subordinate conjunctions — one with ስለ, one with ሲ, one with ከ, one with እንደ — and underline the leaning clause in each.
4. Finish these leaning halves with a main clause of your own: ሱሰኛነት ሰዎችን ስለሚጎዳ … / ተማሪው ሲመጣ …
5. Write a paragraph of five sentences on one topic, and plant a transitional word at the head of at least three sentences. Read it aloud: does the path hold?
