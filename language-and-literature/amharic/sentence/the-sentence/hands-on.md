# Hands-on — The Sentence and Its Marks

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Mark hunt
Take any page you can read — a book, a notice, a letter at home. Find five sentences. For each one, point to its end mark and say its name: ። telling, ? asking, ! strong feeling. Copy the shortest sentence onto paper, exactly, with its mark.

## Say it two ways
Write አባትህ መጣ on a card. Hold up a ። card and read it as a statement. Hold up a ? card and read the same words as a question. Swap with a partner: they hold the mark, you read; then you hold, they read.

## The joining game
On two strips of paper write two short sentences: one thing you want, one thing you do. On a small strip write ስለምፈልግ. Slide the three strips together into one long sentence and read it aloud — the same join as GIF 8390, with your own hands.

## Build a compound
Take two sentences that can each stand alone. Join them once with እና, once with ወይም, once with ነገርግን. Read all three aloud and say how the joining word changed the meaning each time.

## Two stands
Write a sentence on a long strip — ትልቁ ልጅ እየሮጠ መጣ። works. Stand at its start and say "ባለቤት!" over the subject side; walk to its end and say "ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ!" over the predicate side. A partner then reads a new sentence, and you walk it: start — subject; end — predicate. Swap after three sentences.

## Four voices
Four cards: ሀተታዊ, ጥያቄያዊ, ትዕዛዛዊ, አጋናኝ. A partner reads a sentence aloud in one of the four voices — stating, asking, ordering, exclaiming. Hold up the card that names its function. Then read one yourself in the voice your partner's card names.

## Break it with አል- … -ም
One partner says an affirmative sentence (ልጁ መጣ።). The other breaks it into its negative, wrapping the verb in አል- … -ም (ልጁ አልመጣም።). Swap roles — five sentences each. The listener checks: are both hands of the negative there, አል- before and -ም after?

## Added 2026-11-12 — punctuate aloud
Take three plain sentences, say each with its feeling — telling, asking, shouting — and write the mark its feeling asks for.
