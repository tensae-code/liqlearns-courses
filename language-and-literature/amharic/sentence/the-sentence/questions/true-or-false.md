# The Sentence and Its Marks — true or false

Say true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A single word like ዝናብ is already a complete sentence.
**Answer: False** — it names a thing but carries no complete idea yet.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "አባትህ መጣ።" and "አባትህ መጣ?" are read the same way.
**Answer: False** — the mark changes the delivery: telling with ።, asking with ?.

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** In a compound sentence, each simple sentence could stand alone.
**Answer: True** — the conjunction only ties them together.

**4. (Sub lesson 6)** "ነፍሴ ምንጥቅ! አለች" is an exclamatory (አጋናኝ) sentence — a strong feeling bursting out.
**Answer: True.**

**5. (Sub lesson 7)** An affirmative (አዎንታዊ) sentence carries a special marker on its verb.
**Answer: False** — it states the action unbroken, with no special marker; the marker አል- … -ም belongs to the negative.

**6. (Sub lesson 9)** ልጁ ከትምህርትቤት መጣ። is a complex sentence because its verb is bound.
**Answer: False** — it is ነጠላ, simple: one clause unit, one verb, a complete sense. (ሲመጣ would be bound; plain መጣ is not.)

**7. (Sub lesson 10)** In the analysis table, ታላቁ የህዳሴ ግድብ is the ባለቤት of its sentence, and የኤሌክትሪክ ሀይል ማመንጨት ጀመረ is its ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ.
**Answer: True** — the Grand Renaissance Dam is what the sentence is about; "began generating electricity" is its verb phrase.

**8. (Sub lesson 11)** A bound morpheme (ጥገኛ ምዕላድ) like -ኣቸው can stand alone as a word by itself.
**Answer: False** — bound morphemes cannot stand alone; they must lean on a free morpheme, as -ኣቸው leans in ላም + ኦች + ኣቸው.

**9. (Sub lesson 12)** Every negative sentence is made by wrapping a verb in አል- … -ም.
**Answer: False** — a being sentence negates with አይደለም (is not) and a having sentence with የላቸውም (they do not have); the wrap is for action verbs.

**10. (Sub lesson 16)** እነአበበ in the model marks Abebe with his companions — the plural of a named person.
**Answer: True**

**11. (Sub lesson 17)** The p.142 table asks two things of each sentence: person and tense.
**Answer: True**

**12. (Sub lesson 18)** Grade 8's wise-woman chapter asks for 3 simple and 3 compound sentences of your own.
**Answer: True**

**13. (Sub lesson 19)** The four functions are declarative, interrogative, imperative and exclamatory.
**Answer: True**

**14. (Sub lesson 20)** Grade 8 converts questions the other way — into affirmative and negative statements.
**Answer: True**

**15. (Sub lesson 21)** Subject–verb number agreement is one of the p.46 error kinds.
**Answer: True**

**16. (Sub lesson 22)** የስርዓተ ነጥብ ዓይነቶች is the heading for the punctuation types in the Grade 9 guide's note.
**Answer: True**

**17. (Sub lesson 23)** ሐረግ is the guide's term for a phrase.
**Answer: True**
