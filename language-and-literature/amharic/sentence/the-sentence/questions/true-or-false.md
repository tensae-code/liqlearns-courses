# The Sentence and Its Marks — true or false

Say true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A single word like ዝናብ is already a complete sentence.
**Answer: False** — it names a thing but carries no complete idea yet.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "አባትህ መጣ።" and "አባትህ መጣ?" are read the same way.
**Answer: False** — the mark changes the delivery: telling with ።, asking with ?.

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** In a compound sentence, each simple sentence could stand alone.
**Answer: True** — the conjunction only ties them together.

**4. (Sub lesson 6)** "ነፍሴ ምንጥቅ! አለች" is an exclamatory (አጋናኝ) sentence — a strong feeling bursting out.
**Answer: True.**

**5. (Sub lesson 7)** An affirmative (አዎንታዊ) sentence carries a special marker on its verb.
**Answer: False** — it states the action unbroken, with no special marker; the marker አል- … -ም belongs to the negative.

**6. (Sub lesson 9)** ልጁ ከትምህርትቤት መጣ። is a complex sentence because its verb is bound.
**Answer: False** — it is ነጠላ, simple: one clause unit, one verb, a complete sense. (ሲመጣ would be bound; plain መጣ is not.)
