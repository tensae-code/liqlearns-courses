# The Sentence and Its Marks

## Do it first

Read these words out loud: ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ

Now read them again with this mark at the end: ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።

The words did not change. The mark tells your voice: the idea is finished. Stop here. That finished group of words is a sentence.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a sentence is

A sentence — ዓረፍተነገር — carries **one complete idea**.

Look at the book's first example:

**ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።** — Heavy rain fell.

Nothing is missing. You know what happened, completely. A single word like ዝናብ (rain) is not a sentence yet — it names a thing, but it does not yet carry a whole idea. When the words together tell one whole thing, and the mark closes them, you have a sentence.

A sentence can also be spoken as a talk between two people: one person asks, the other answers in a full sentence. "For your country, what do you want to contribute?" — "I want to become a doctor and treat sick people." That answer is a sentence: one complete idea, spoken whole.

## Sub lesson 2 — The end marks — ። ? !

A sentence wears its ending on its face. There are three end marks to learn here.

[Picture 8391 — the end marks chart]

**። — the full stop (አራት ነጥብ).** Four little dots. This mark shows the sentence has ended: the idea is complete. ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።

**? — the question mark.** When a sentence carries a question feeling, it ends with ?. Watch the same words change:

አባትህ መጣ። — Your father came. (a statement — it tells)
አባትህ መጣ? — Did your father come? (a question — it asks)

The words are the same. The mark changes how you read them, and how your voice goes: a telling voice for ።, a questioning voice for ?.

**! — the exclamation mark.** When a sentence carries a strong feeling — surprise, a shout, a strong order — it ends with !. The three marks together — ። ? ! — are the trio you will use in your own writing: end every sentence with the mark its feeling needs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Joining two sentences with ስለ

Two short sentences can become one longer sentence. Listen to this talk:

— What place do you want to finish? (ስንትኛ መወጣት ትፈልጋለህ?)
— I want to finish first. (1ኛ መወጣት እፈልጋለሁ።)
— And to finish first, what must you do?
— Study all the time. (ሁልጊዜ አጠናለሁ።)
— Now tell me the two sentences as one!

And the answer joins them:

**1ኛ መወጣት ስለምፈልግ ሁልጊዜ አጠናለሁ።** — Because I want to finish first, I study all the time.

See the joining word: **ስለምፈልግ** — "because I want". It is ስለ (because) carrying the reason. The word ስለ joins a reason to what follows from it: one sentence leans on the other, and ስለ holds them together.

[GIF 8390 — two sentences join with ስለ]

Watch the two short sentences slide together, and ስለ light up between them. Now the one long sentence says both things at once: the wanting, and the studying it causes.

## Sub lesson 4 — Compound and complex sentences

**Compound sentences — ድርብ ዓረፍተነገር.** A compound sentence is **two simple sentences joined by a conjunction**. The joining words the books give are: ስለዚህ (therefore), ነገርግን (but / however), ወይም (or), እና (and), ምክንያቱም (because).

Each simple sentence could stand alone. The conjunction ties them into one:

- Take two standing sentences. Join them with እና — and both stand together in one line.
- Join them with ወይም — and the sentence offers a choice.
- Join them with ነገርግን — and the second sentence pushes against the first.
- Join them with ስለዚህ or ምክንያቱም — and one sentence gives the reason or the result of the other.

**Complex sentences — ውስብስብ ዓረፍተነገር.** A complex sentence is **a sentence containing two or more verbs**. Count the doing words — if there are two or more, the sentence is complex. The books' own examples:

- ፀሀይ ወጣ ይመስላል፣ ይህን እንጅ ዝናብ መዝነብ አይቀርም።
- በሚገባ ማጥናት አለብን፣ አለበለዚያ ጥሩ ውጤት አናመጣም።
- ዕውቀት ለኛረጎኝ ይገባል፣ ስለዚህ መማር አለብን።

In each one, more than one action lives inside a single sentence. That is the mark of ውስብስብ: two or more verbs under one roof.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: a sentence carries one complete idea, and it ends with ።, ? or !. Then take two short sentences of your own and join them with ስለ, the way the dialogue joins 1ኛ መወጣት እፈልጋለሁ and ሁልጊዜ አጠናለሁ. Your companion counts the verbs in your joined sentence: if there are two or more, they say "ውስብስብ!"

## Do It Yourself

1. Write one statement and end it with ።. Use the book's model: ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።
2. Write the same words as a question, like አባትህ መጣ? — and read both aloud, once telling, once asking.
3. Say two short sentences about yourself: one thing you want, one thing you do. Join them with ስለምፈልግ into one sentence.
4. Join two sentences with ወይም and two with ነገርግን. Read all four aloud.
5. Copy this sentence and count its verbs: ዕውቀት ለኛረጎኝ ይገባል፣ ስለዚህ መማር አለብን። Say whether it is complex, and why.
