# The Sentence and Its Marks

## Do it first

Read these words out loud: ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ

Now read them again with this mark at the end: ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።

The words did not change. The mark tells your voice: the idea is finished. Stop here. That finished group of words is a sentence.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a sentence is

A sentence — ዓረፍተነገር — carries **one complete idea**.

Look at the book's first example:

**ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።** — Heavy rain fell.

Nothing is missing. You know what happened, completely. A single word like ዝናብ (rain) is not a sentence yet — it names a thing, but it does not yet carry a whole idea. When the words together tell one whole thing, and the mark closes them, you have a sentence.

A sentence can also be spoken as a talk between two people: one person asks, the other answers in a full sentence. "For your country, what do you want to contribute?" — "I want to become a doctor and treat sick people." That answer is a sentence: one complete idea, spoken whole.

## Sub lesson 2 — The end marks — ። ? !

A sentence wears its ending on its face. There are three end marks to learn here.

[Picture 8391 — the end marks chart]

**። — the full stop (አራት ነጥብ).** Four little dots. This mark shows the sentence has ended: the idea is complete. ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።

**? — the question mark.** When a sentence carries a question feeling, it ends with ?. Watch the same words change:

አባትህ መጣ። — Your father came. (a statement — it tells)
አባትህ መጣ? — Did your father come? (a question — it asks)

The words are the same. The mark changes how you read them, and how your voice goes: a telling voice for ።, a questioning voice for ?.

**! — the exclamation mark.** When a sentence carries a strong feeling — surprise, a shout, a strong order — it ends with !. The three marks together — ። ? ! — are the trio you will use in your own writing: end every sentence with the mark its feeling needs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Joining two sentences with ስለ

Two short sentences can become one longer sentence. Listen to this talk:

— What place do you want to finish? (ስንትኛ መወጣት ትፈልጋለህ?)
— I want to finish first. (1ኛ መወጣት እፈልጋለሁ።)
— And to finish first, what must you do?
— Study all the time. (ሁልጊዜ አጠናለሁ።)
— Now tell me the two sentences as one!

And the answer joins them:

**1ኛ መወጣት ስለምፈልግ ሁልጊዜ አጠናለሁ።** — Because I want to finish first, I study all the time.

See the joining word: **ስለምፈልግ** — "because I want". It is ስለ (because) carrying the reason. The word ስለ joins a reason to what follows from it: one sentence leans on the other, and ስለ holds them together.

[GIF 8390 — two sentences join with ስለ]

Watch the two short sentences slide together, and ስለ light up between them. Now the one long sentence says both things at once: the wanting, and the studying it causes.

## Sub lesson 4 — Compound and complex sentences

**Compound sentences — ድርብ ዓረፍተነገር.** A compound sentence is **two simple sentences joined by a conjunction**. The joining words the books give are: ስለዚህ (therefore), ነገርግን (but / however), ወይም (or), እና (and), ምክንያቱም (because).

Each simple sentence could stand alone. The conjunction ties them into one:

- Take two standing sentences. Join them with እና — and both stand together in one line.
- Join them with ወይም — and the sentence offers a choice.
- Join them with ነገርግን — and the second sentence pushes against the first.
- Join them with ስለዚህ or ምክንያቱም — and one sentence gives the reason or the result of the other.

**Complex sentences — ውስብስብ ዓረፍተነገር.** A complex sentence is **a sentence containing two or more verbs**. Count the doing words — if there are two or more, the sentence is complex. The books' own examples:

- ፀሀይ ወጣ ይመስላል፣ ይህን እንጅ ዝናብ መዝነብ አይቀርም።
- በሚገባ ማጥናት አለብን፣ አለበለዚያ ጥሩ ውጤት አናመጣም።
- ዕውቀት ለኛረጎኝ ይገባል፣ ስለዚህ መማር አለብን።

In each one, more than one action lives inside a single sentence. That is the mark of ውስብስብ: two or more verbs under one roof.

## Sub lesson 5 — Subject and predicate: ባለቤት and ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ

Now take a sentence apart. A sentence needs **at least two structures**:

- The **subject — ባለቤት** — stands at the **start**: who or what the sentence is about.
- The **predicate — ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ** — stands at the **end**: what is said about the subject.

**ከድር ከትምህርትቤት መጣ።** — Kedir came from school. ከድር is the ባለቤት — the sentence is about Kedir. መጣ is the ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ — it says what Kedir did, and it closes the sentence.

[Picture 8720 — subject and predicate]

Each side can carry its own **attachments** — extra words that travel with it. Watch this sentence open into its two sides:

**ትልቁ ልጅ እየሮጠ መጣ።** — The big child came running.
- Subject side: ትልቁ (attachment — *big*) + ልጅ (subject — *child*)
- Predicate side: እየሮጠ (attachment — *running*) + መጣ (predicate — *came*)

To take any sentence apart, ask two questions: who or what is it about? — that is the ባለቤት, at the start. What is said about them? — that is the ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ, at the end.

## Sub lesson 6 — The four things a sentence can do

Sentences divide **by function** — by the work they do — into **four**:

[Picture 8721 — the four sentence functions]

1. **Declarative — ሀተታዊ.** It *states*. **ልጁ ብዙ ሥራ ሠራ።** — The child did much work.
2. **Interrogative — ጥያቄያዊ.** It *asks*. **ከሥራ ለምን ቀረሽ?** — Why were you absent from work?
3. **Imperative — ትዕዛዛዊ.** It *orders*. **የቤት ሥራችሁን ሥሩ።** — Do your house work.
4. **Exclamatory — አጋናኝ.** It *exclaims* — a strong feeling bursts out. **ነፍሴ ምንጥቅ! አለች።** — "My soul, how tight!" she said.

You already know their end marks from Sub lesson 2: the stating sentence closes with ።, the asking one with ?, the ordering and exclaiming ones often with !. Now you can name the work each sentence is doing, not only its mark.

## Sub lesson 7 — Affirmative and negative sentences

A sentence can say a thing stands — or break it.

[Picture 8722 — affirmative and negative]

**Affirmative — አዎንታዊ ዓረፍተነገር.** It states the subject's action **without breaking it**, and it carries **no special marker**. **ልጁ መጣ።** — The child came. Nothing is added; the action simply stands.

**Negative — አሉታዊ ዓረፍተነገር.** It **breaks or denies** an idea that is already known — and its verb carries the affixes **አል- … -ም** wrapped around it: **ልጁ አልመጣም።** — The child did not come. See አል- before the verb and -ም after it: the two hands of the negative, holding the verb between them.

When you sort sentences, these two stand beside the asking kind (መጠይቃዊ) in one table: does the sentence affirm, deny, or ask?

## Sub lesson 8 — The being-verb and its complement

Some sentences have no action at all — they say what a thing **is**.

**እነዚህ ዋልያዎች ናቸው።** — These are walia ibexes.

Take it apart:

[Picture 8723 — the being-verb and its complement]

- **ናቸው** — the **verb of being (መሆን ግስ)**: *are*. It does not show an action; it shows being.
- **ዋልያዎች** — its **complement (ስም መሙያ)**: the noun that completes the being-verb — *what* they are.
- **እነዚህ** — the **pointer**: it stands before the complement noun and points at it — *these*.

Word order matters: the scrambled ናቸው እነዚህ በሬዎች says nothing clean; put the pointer first, the complement next, the being-verb last — **እነዚህ በሬዎች ናቸው።** — These are oxen. That is the shape of an "is / are" sentence: pointer + noun complement + መሆን ግስ.

## Sub lesson 9 — Simple and complex sentences, grown

Sub lesson 4 gave you the first mark of a complex sentence: **two or more verbs**. Now the fuller picture.

**Simple — ነጠላ ዓረፍተነገር.** A simple sentence holds **one clause unit** — one subject, one predicate, one whole sense that stands alone: **ልጁ ከትምህርትቤት መጣ።** — The child came from school. One verb — መጣ — and the sense is complete.

**Complex — ውስብስብ ዓረፍተነገር.** Watch what happens when a verb is **bound**:

**ልጁ ከትምህርትቤት ሲመጣ አባቱ ወደቢሮ ሄደ።** — While the child was coming from school, his father went to the office.

[Picture 8724 — simple and complex sentences]

The verb ሲመጣ (*while coming*) is bound — said alone, it gives no full sense; it **waits** for a completer. ሄደ (*went*) is the **binding verb** that completes it. Two verbs, one bound and one binding — that is a complex sentence from the inside. But ልጁ ከትምህርትቤት መጣ, with plain መጣ, is simple: nothing waits, nothing is bound.

[GIF 8736 — a bound verb waits for its binder]

So the old test and the new test agree: count the verbs — two or more, and the sentence is ውስብስብ; then look closer and find which verb is bound and which one binds it.

## Sub lesson 10 — The sentence's constituents, grown: ባለቤት, ተሳቢ, ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ *(teacher guide — evidence)*

In Sub lesson 5 you took a sentence into two sides. Now the analysis goes one part deeper — a sentence is opened into **three constituents**:

- **ባለቤት** — the **subject**: who or what the sentence is about.
- **ተሳቢ** — the **object**: the one the action falls on, who receives what is done.
- **ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ** — the **verb phrase**: the doing part that binds the sentence together.

[Picture 9001 — opening a sentence: subject, object, verb phrase]

Work the analysis the way the table works it. First row: **ቶፊቅ ጻፈ።** — Tofiq wrote. The ባለቤት is **ቶፊቅ** — the sentence is about him. The ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ is **ጻፈ** — the verb phrase, *wrote*.

Now a long one: **ታላቁ የህዳሴ ግድብ የኤሌክትሪክ ሀይል ማመንጨት ጀመረ።** — The Grand Renaissance Dam began generating electricity. Do not let its length frighten you. Ask the same questions: who or what is it about? **ታላቁ የህዳሴ ግድብ** — the subject, the whole grand name together. What is its doing part? **የኤሌክትሪክ ሀይል ማመንጨት ጀመረ** — the verb phrase: *began generating electricity*.

Ten sentences are worked this way in the full table — subject on one side, verb phrase on the other, and the ተሳቢ named where the action lands on someone or something. Your habit is the table's habit: subject first, verb phrase last, object where the doing falls.

## Sub lesson 11 — Morphemes: the free and the bound *(teacher guide — evidence)*

Under the word there is one smaller floor: the **morpheme — ምዕላድ** — the smallest piece of a word that carries meaning. Morphemes come in two kinds.

[Picture 9002 — morphemes: free and bound]

**Free morphemes — ነፃ ምዕላድ.** A free morpheme can **stand alone** as a word by itself: **ላም** (a cow), **ሴት** (a woman), **ውይይት** (a discussion). Nothing holds it up; it stands.

**Bound morphemes — ጥገኛ ምዕላድ.** A bound morpheme **cannot stand alone** — it must lean on a free one. They come in two families:

- **Prefixes — ቅድመ አምድ ቅጥያ** — they enter **before** the stem: **ስለ-**, **ከ-**, **በ-**, **መ-**.
- **Suffixes — ድህረ አምድ ቅጥያ** — they enter **after** the stem: **-ኦች** (the plural you know), **-ኤ**, **-ኣቸው**, **-ዋ**.

Now split a built word into its pieces: **ላም + ኦች + ኣቸው** — the free stem ላም, the plural suffix -ኦች, and the suffix -ኣቸው riding last. And **ውይይት + ኣችን** — a free stem carrying one bound piece. You have done this before with plurals; now you can name every piece: free or bound, prefix or suffix.

Bound pieces also **build new words**. With the building suffixes **-አም, -ነት, -ኛ, -አዊ** the language grows: **ወተታም**, **ልጅነት** (childhood), **ዜግነት** (citizenship), **ሰማያዊ** (sky-blue / heavenly), **ዘመናዊ** (modern). A free morpheme is the wood; bound morphemes are the joints and the carving.

## Sub lesson 12 — Affirmative to negative, grown *(teacher guide — evidence)*

Sub lesson 7 taught you the negative's wrap: **አል- … -ም** around the verb. Now the conversion work is done in full, on the guide's own keyed pairs — and the verb itself changes shape inside the wrap:

**ገሰፀ → አልገሰፁም.** He rebuked → he did not rebuke. Look closely: አል- stands before, -ም after, and the verb between them is not quite the verb you started with — ገሰፀ has become ገሰፁ inside the negative. Learn the pair as the pair is given: affirmative on the left, its negative on the right.

[Picture 9003 — affirmative to negative, grown]

[GIF 9017 — an affirmative verb wrapped into its negative]

Watch አል- and -ም close around the verb from both sides. That closing is the conversion.

But not every negative is a wrapped verb. Two more shapes stand beside it:

- **አይደለም** — *is not*. Where a sentence says what a thing **is**, its negative does not wrap a verb — it says አይደለም.
- **የላቸውም** — *they do not have*. Where a sentence is about **having**, the negative for possession is የላቸውም.

So the converter's questions are three: is it an action? — wrap the verb in አል- … -ም. Is it a being? — አይደለም. Is it a having? — የላቸውም.

## Sub lesson 15 — Grade 4 review bench: compound sentences with በሌላ በኩል

The Grade 4 book returns to compound sentences — ድርብ ዓረፍተነገር — at its review bench, and asks you to write them with five joiners: **እና** (and), **ነገርግን** (but), **ወይም** (or), **ስለዚህ** (therefore) — and one more that Sub lesson 4 did not carry: **በሌላ በኩል** — on the other hand.

በሌላ በኩል turns the sentence around to look from the other side: the first half shows one face of a thing; በሌላ በኩል, the second half shows its other face. Build one compound sentence with each of the five joiners, and feel how the joint changes the meaning each time — adding, pushing against, choosing, following, and turning to the other side.

## Sub lesson 23 — Phrases — pick them out and build sentences from them: the Grade 10 guide (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 guide's Chapter 5 grammar part is one clean move, stocked as labelled evidence: the **phrase (ሐረግ)**.

Learners **pick phrases out** of standing text — finding the word-groups that hang together — and then **build complete sentences from them**: the phrase is the brick, the sentence the wall. Chapter 5's materials line up behind the work: short and long fiction, poems, articles, research articles — real text to quarry phrases from.

This lesson's verb phrase (ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ) stands under other citations; the general phrase work — pick out, then build — is this guide's page, kept with its own citation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: a sentence carries one complete idea, and it ends with ።, ? or !. Then take two short sentences of your own and join them with ስለ, the way the dialogue joins 1ኛ መወጣት እፈልጋለሁ and ሁልጊዜ አጠናለሁ. Your companion counts the verbs in your joined sentence: if there are two or more, they say "ውስብስብ!"

Then go further: take the sentence ትልቁ ልጅ እየሮጠ መጣ and name its ባለቤት and its ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ for your companion. Say one sentence of each of the four functions — stating, asking, ordering, exclaiming. Say መጣ as an affirmative, then break it into its negative with አል- … -ም. Finish with an "is" sentence on the model እነዚህ ዋልያዎች ናቸው, and let your companion name the pointer, the complement and the being-verb.

Go deeper still: open ታላቁ የህዳሴ ግድብ የኤሌክትሪክ ሀይል ማመንጨት ጀመረ into its ባለቤት and its ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ for your companion. Split ላም + ኦች + ኣቸው aloud, naming each piece free or bound. Then convert: say ገሰፀ, and let your companion answer with its negative — አልገሰፁም — and name the two shapes አይደለም and የላቸውም and what each one negates.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write one statement and end it with ።. Use the book's model: ኃይለኛ ዝናብ ዘነበ።
2. Write the same words as a question, like አባትህ መጣ? — and read both aloud, once telling, once asking.
3. Say two short sentences about yourself: one thing you want, one thing you do. Join them with ስለምፈልግ into one sentence.
4. Join two sentences with ወይም and two with ነገርግን. Read all four aloud.
5. Copy this sentence and count its verbs: ዕውቀት ለኛረጎኝ ይገባል፣ ስለዚህ መማር አለብን። Say whether it is complex, and why.
6. Take apart ከድር ከትምህርትቤት መጣ።: point to the ባለቤት, then to the ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ. Then take apart ትልቁ ልጅ እየሮጠ መጣ። and name each side's attachment too.
7. Write four sentences — one stating (ሀተታዊ), one asking (ጥያቄያዊ), one ordering (ትዕዛዛዊ), one exclaiming (አጋናኝ) — and end each with its right mark.
8. Write ልጁ መጣ። as a negative sentence, wrapping its verb in አል- … -ም.
9. Unscramble ናቸው እነዚህ በሬዎች into the right order, then write two "is / are" sentences of your own with a noun complement.
10. Write one ነጠላ (simple) sentence. Then grow it into a ውስብስብ (complex) one by binding its verb with ሲ- and adding a binding verb, on the model ልጁ ከትምህርትቤት ሲመጣ አባቱ ወደቢሮ ሄደ።
11. Open these two sentences into ባለቤት and ማሰሪያ አንቀጽ: ቶፊቅ ጻፈ። and ታላቁ የህዳሴ ግድብ የኤሌክትሪክ ሀይል ማመንጨት ጀመረ። Say where a ተሳቢ would stand — the one the action falls on.
12. Split these words into their morphemes and name each piece free or bound: ላምኦችኣቸው (ላም + ኦች + ኣቸው), ውይይትኣችን. Then build one new word with -ነት and one with -አዊ, on the models ልጅነት and ሰማያዊ.
13. Convert ገሰፀ into its negative, exactly as the keyed pair gives it. Then write the negative shape for "is not" (አይደለም) and for "they do not have" (የላቸውም).
