# Lesson plan — The Marks That Help Us Read

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** The Marks That Help Us Read
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Sentence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** four little marks — the
two dots (ሁለት ነጥብ), the four dots, the comma (ነጠላ ሰረዝ) and
the question mark (የጥያቄ ምልክት) — and what each one tells a
reader to do.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the sentences the
learner can already read (My First Sentences); the marks only
make those same sentences easier to read aloud. It needs nothing
else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short,
single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: My First Sentences — the learner reads whole
  sentences; the marks are shown working inside those same
  sentences. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Point to each of the four marks and say its name: ሁለት ነጥብ
   (two dots), the four dots, ነጠላ ሰረዝ (comma), የጥያቄ ምልክት
   (question mark).
2. Say what each mark does: the two dots open a list; the four
   dots close a saying; the comma is a small resting place; the
   question mark shows the sentence is asking.
3. Read a sentence aloud and lift the voice where the question
   mark stands.
4. Write the names of the marks, as the book's own work asks.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

   each named, each seen working inside a tiny sentence

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the two dots and the four dots → count the dots out loud
  with a finger: two… the list-opener; four… the full stop that
  closes the saying.
- Reads a question flat → say the same sentence as a telling,
  then as an asking, and let the ear hear the voice climb at the
  end; the mark is the picture of that climb.
- Forgets the comma's job → clap once where the comma stands: a
  comma is one small clap of rest inside the sentence.
