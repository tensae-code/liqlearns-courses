# Hands-on — The Marks That Help Us Read

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Marks in the air
Draw each mark big in the air with your whole arm: two dots —
poke, poke. Four dots — poke four corners of a square. The comma
— one small swoop. The question mark — a hook and a dot. Say the
name while your arm draws. Big movements first make the small
page marks easy.

## Dot stones
Lay out stones or bottle tops as the marks on the ground: two
for the two dots, four for the four dots. A partner reads a tiny
sentence; you build the mark it needs at its end or inside it.
Swap roles after four sentences.

## The mark hunt
Take any page of writing at home — a book, a letter, a packet.
Hunt for the four marks. Every time you find one, point, name
it, and say its job: opens a list, closes the saying, a small
rest, asks a question. Count how many of each you find.

## If it is hard
Work with the picture card 8214 open in front of you and only
two marks at a time — the two dots against the four dots first
(count them with a finger), the comma and the question mark
next. Picture, name and job together for every mark, in small
steps (SNE toolkit — SUPPORTING; see sources.md).
