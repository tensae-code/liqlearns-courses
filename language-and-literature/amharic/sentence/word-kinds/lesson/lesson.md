# The Word Kinds

## Do it first

Read this sentence: የዋህ ልጅ ቶሎ ሄደ።

Four words. Each word does a different job. One names a person. One describes him. One tells how he went. One is the going itself — and it stands at the end. This lesson names the six kinds of words, in the order the books teach them.

[Picture 8392 — the word kinds chart]

Keep the chart near you. We climb it one kind at a time: ስም → ግስ → ቅጽል → አያያዥ ቃላት → ተውሳከግስ → መስተዋድድ.

## Sub lesson 1 — Noun — ስም

A **noun (ስም)** is a word that **names a thing**.

The book's own examples: **ሰው** (a person), **ዛፍ** (a tree), **ድመት** (a cat).

If the word gives the name of a person, an animal, a place or a thing, it is a ስም. To test a word, ask: what does it name? If it names something, it is a noun.

## Sub lesson 2 — Verb — ግስ

A **verb (ግስ)** is the word that **shows the action** — and in Amharic it **comes at the end of the sentence**.

The book's own examples: **ተማረ** (learned / studied), **ሄደ** (went), **ተኛ** (slept / lay down).

Here is the test the books use: spot the noun and the verb in a sentence — the naming word is the ስም; the word at the end showing what was done is the ግስ. A verb can also be known this way: every word that can stand at a sentence's end and take person markers like -ህ, -ሽ, -ሁ is a verb.

**Past-tense verbs — የኃላፊ ጊዜ ግስ.** A past-tense verb shows an action **completed in past time**. The books' examples: **ገደለ** (he killed), **ሰበረች** (she broke). The doing is finished — that finished-ness is what makes the verb past-tense.

## Sub lesson 3 — Adjective — ቅጽል

An **adjective (ቅጽል)** is a word that **shows a noun's kind, quality or size**.

The book's own examples: **የዋህ ልጅ** (a gentle child — quality), **ቀይ በሬ** (a red ox — kind/colour), **ወፍራም በግ** (a fat sheep — size).

The adjective never stands alone in its work: it leans on a noun and tells you more about it — what kind it is, what it is like, how big it is. In the teacher guide the same words are also called **አይነግሮች** — describing words — and learners pick them out of sentences and use them with things.

## Sub lesson 4 — Conjunction — አያያዥ ቃላት

A **conjunction (አያያዥ ቃላት)** is a word that **joins** — word to word, phrase to phrase, sentence to sentence.

The book's own examples: **ስለዚህ** (therefore), **ይሁንእንጂ**, **በመሆኑም**, **ነገርግን** (but).

The teacher guide gives these joining words a name — **መስታጽምር** — and sorts them into **two types**:

1. **Coordinating (ወደረኛ)** — joins equals: noun with noun, adjective with adjective, verb with verb. Its words: **እና** (and) and **ወይም** (or). Examples: ህፃኑ እና ወንድሙ (the child and his brother), ወፍራም እና ቀጭኑ (the fat one and the thin one), ሽንኩርት ወይም ድንች (onion or potato).
2. **Subordinating (ጥገኛ)** — one verb depends on another. Its words: **ስለ** (because) and **ከ** (if / from). Example: ከመጣ አይሄድም — if he comes, he will not go: the going depends on the coming.

A conjunction cannot give meaning alone — its whole work is the joining.

## Sub lesson 5 — Adverb — ተውሳከግስ

An **adverb (ተውሳከግስ)** is a word that shows **when, where and how** an action is done.

The book's own examples: **ቶሎ** (quickly — how), **ሁልጊዜ** (always — when), **ትናንት** (yesterday — when), **ክፉኛ** (badly — how).

To find the adverb, ask the verb three questions: when was it done? where? how? The word that answers one of those questions is the ተውሳከግስ. More of the books' adverbs to meet in sentences: ዘንድሮ, ነገ, በድንገት, ዘወትር, አሁን.

## Sub lesson 6 — Preposition — መስተዋድድ

A **preposition (መስተዋድድ)** is a word that **comes before a noun** and shows **time, place, instrument, reason or purpose**.

The book's own list: **ስለ** (about / because of), **ወደ** (to / towards), **በ** (by / with / in), **ከ** (from), **እንደ** (like / as), **ለ** (for / to).

More prepositions the market lesson uses to locate things: ላይ (on), ፊትለፊት (in front of), አጠገብ (beside), ታች (under / below). A preposition never stands at the end doing the action — it stands before its noun and points: where, when, with what, why, for whom.

## Sub lesson 7 — Common and proper nouns

Inside the noun family there is a split. Some nouns name **any one of a kind**:

- **ደም** (blood), **መምህር** (teacher), **ዛፍ** (tree) — any blood, any teacher, any tree.

These are **common nouns — ወል ስም**.

Other nouns name **one named one**:

- **ጣና** (Tana — this lake), **ተከዜ** (Tekeze — this river), **ጎንደር** (Gondar — this town), **ማህሌት** (Mahlet — this person).

These are **proper nouns — የተጸውኦ ስም**.

[Picture 8670 — common nouns and proper nouns]

The test: point. If the word fits every one of its kind, it is ወል ስም. If it belongs to one named one only, it is የተጸውኦ ስም.

## Sub lesson 8 — Collective nouns — ጥቅል ስም

Some nouns name a **whole group in one word**. These are **collective nouns — ጥቅል ስም**.

The clearest one is **መንጋ** — a herd, a flock: many animals, one word.

[GIF 8673 — gathering into one መንጋ]

Watch them gather: many single animals, and when the group stands together, one word covers them all — መንጋ. More of the family, from the review lists: **ሕዝብ** (a people, a crowd), **ደን** (a forest — many trees, one word), **እጽዋት** (plants, vegetation), **ከተማ** (a town — many houses and people, one word).

The collective noun looks singular — it is one word — but a whole group lives inside it.

## Sub lesson 9 — Pronouns and their persons — ተውላጠስም, መደብ

A **pronoun (ተውላጠስም)** is a word that **stands in a noun's place**, so we do not have to keep saying the name. The books use them inside sentences from the start: **እኔ, አንቺ, እነሡ, እሱ, እኛ, እሷ** — and in a sentence like ያ ልጅ ጓደኛዬ ነው the little word ያ does the standing-in work.

Pronouns divide into **three persons — መደብ**:

[Picture 8671 — the three pronoun persons]

1. **First person** — the speaker: **እኔ** (I), **እኛ** (we).
2. **Second person** — the one spoken to: **አንተ** (you, to a boy/man), **አንቺ** (you, to a girl/woman), **እናንተ** (you all).
3. **Third person** — the one spoken about: **እሱ** (he), **እሷ** (she), **እነሱ / እነርሱ** (they).

To find a pronoun's person, ask: is it the one talking, the one listening, or the one talked about?

## Sub lesson 10 — Pointing pronouns and question pronouns

**Pointing pronouns — demonstratives (ጠቋሚ)** — point at a thing, near or far from the speaker and the hearer:

- **Near**: **ይህ** (this, m), **ይቺ** (this, f), **እነዚህ** (these).
- **Far from the speaker, near the hearer**: **እሱ, እሷ, እነሱ**.
- **Far from both**: **ያ** (that, m), **ያቺ** (that, f), **እነዚያ** (those).

The forms change with number and gender — singular masculine ይህ / እሱ / ያ, singular feminine ይቺ / እሷ / ያቺ, plural እነዚህ / እነዚያ. One more near form the books use in sentences: **ይህች**.

**Question pronouns — interrogatives (መጠይቅ ተውላጠስም)** — each one asks about a different thing:

- **ማን** — asks about a **person** (who?)
- **ምን** — asks about a **thing** (what?)
- **ወዴት** — asks about a **place** (where?)
- **መቼ** — asks about a **time** (when?)
- **እንዴት** — asks about a **manner** (how?)
- **ለምን** — asks about a **reason** (why?)

Hear the question word and you already know what kind of answer is coming.

## Sub lesson 11 — Countable and uncountable nouns

One last split inside the noun family. Some nouns can be **counted** — their number can be stated:

**የሚቆጠሩ ስሞች** (countable nouns): **ሰው** (person), **እንስሳ** (animal), **ቤት** (house), **ተማሪ** (student), **በሬ** (ox). One በሬ, two በሬ, three በሬ — the number states itself.

Others cannot be counted that way — no number is stated for them:

**የማይቆጠሩ ስሞች** (uncountable nouns): **ስኳር** (sugar), **ዘይት** (oil), **አሸዋ** (sand), **ዉኃ** (water), **ቡና** (coffee), **ብርሃን** (light), **ጨለማ** (darkness), **ኃይል** (strength).

[Picture 8672 — countable and uncountable nouns]

The test is in the name: try to say its number. If the number can be stated, the noun is የሚቆጠሩ. If it cannot, it is የማይቆጠሩ.

## Sub lesson 12 — Personal pronouns and person, grown *(teacher guide — evidence)*

Sub lesson 9 gave you the pronouns and their three persons (መደብ) as a table. Now person is learned the way it is taught in the room — **by standing in it**.

Three learners stand up. One **speaks**. One **listens**. One stands apart as the **topic** — the one being talked about. Now the persons are not rows in a chart; they are places you can stand in:

[Picture 9005 — pronouns and person, staged]

- Stand in the **speaker's** place and every word you say for yourself is **first person**: እኔ (I), እኛ (we).
- Stand facing the **listener** and the word for them is **second person**: አንተ, አንቺ, እናንተ (you).
- Point at the one **talked about** and the word is **third person**: እሱ, እሷ, እነሱ (he, she, they).

Swap places. Speak again. The pronoun changes because **you** changed places — that is what መደብ is: not a label stuck on a word, but the place the word's person is standing in.

Then the written work: **write at least five sentences in the first person, and at least five in the second person** — sentences of your own, drawn from what you have read. እኔ sentences from the speaker's place; አንተ / አንቺ sentences to the listener's face. Count them: five and five.

## Sub lesson 13 — Adjectives, grown *(teacher guide — evidence)*

Sub lesson 3 met the adjective (ቅጽል) beside its noun. Now the teaching goes further, in two steps.

**Step one — the modifier before the noun.** A noun's modifiers — ስም ገላጭ, the words that show the noun — **stand before the noun** and describe its **kind, size, type or origin**. The worked pairs:

- **ረጅም ቀሚስ** — a long dress (size)
- **አዲስ መኪና** — a new car (kind/state)
- **ጎጂ ልማድ** — a harmful habit (kind)

In every pair the describing word walks first and the noun follows it. Find the noun, then look just before it: that is where its ገላጭ stands.

[Picture 9006 — adjectives, grown]

**Step two — pick the adjectives out of sentences.** In noun phrases (ቅጽል እና ስም together), the work is to spot the adjective hiding in a full sentence and lift it out. The keyed adjectives, the ones the answers name: **ትላልቅ** (big), **አዲስ** (new), **ጎበዝ** (clever), **ወፍራም** (thick), **ሰፊ** (wide), **አጭሩ** (the short one), **ሩቅ** (far), **ትኩስ** (hot / fresh), **ጥቁር** (black), **ረዥም** (long).

Read a sentence, find the noun, look before it, and ask: what word here tells the noun's kind, size, type or origin? That word is the ቅጽል. Lift it out and name it.


## Sub lesson 14 — Grade 5 and 6: adverbs whole — native and derived, the four kinds, and adverbs at work in sentences and a story


### The Adverb (ተውሳከግስ) — native and derived

The adverb defined the Grade 5 book's way: the word-part that adds place (where), time (when), manner (how) or reason (why) to the verb's action — the old scholars called it "addition to the verb", modern scholars "describer of the verb". Two families: native adverbs (ነባር: ዛሬ today, አሁን now, ቶሎ quickly, ገና yet) and derived adverbs (ምሥርት: ክፉኛ badly, from the adjective ክፉ; በፍጥነት, ግምኛ, ጅልኛ). Pick the adverbs out of sentences and say which family each belongs to. The adverb's definition stands in sub lesson 5 from the Grade 3 book (when/where/how only); the native/derived distinction is stocked here, at its own pages.

### Building sentences with adverbs — the Grade 5 practice set

Build complete sentences using the adverbs ክፉኛ, ቶሎ, ገና, ዛሬ, ትናንት — one sentence each, the adverb doing its work on the verb. Adverbs in sentences stand in sub lesson 5 from the Grade 3 book; this practice set is the Grade 5 book's own, at its own page.

### Adverbs and their kinds — the Grade 6 classification

Identify adverbs in sentences (አዘንኩ, ደነገጠ, ጠጣ, ቶሎ መጣች…) and classify them into the Grade 6 book's four kinds: የጊዜ (time), የቦታ (place), የሁኔታ (manner), የመጠን (degree/quantity). The review completes sentences choosing the right adverb: እንዴት, ትንሽ, ዘወትር, ዛሬ, እዚህ, ክፉኛ, ውስጥ, ብዙ.

### Building sentences with time adverbs — the Grade 6 set

Build sentences with the time adverbs among a mixed list: ዛሬ, አምና, ማታ, ወዲያው, ድሮ, ቅድም, አሁን, ዘወትር — each sentence must let its time word do the timing. Time words stand in sub lesson 5 from the Grade 3 book; this set is the Grade 6 book's own, at its own page.

### Adverb kinds in a story — the table and the night cloze

First sort adverbs into the table of kinds (time / place / quantity / manner: ቶሎ, በጣም, ክፉኛ, ወደዚያው, ወዲያው, ሌሊት…). Then complete a night-time mini-story choosing adverbs: waking at an unusual hour, a sound from the kitchen, a dishevelled man eating, tomato-red eyes, fear, backing into a wall. No adverb-kind sorting stood in this lesson before; this part stocks the Grade 6 book's own review at its own pages.

## Sub lesson 16 — Grade 7: the adjective/adverb sorting table

Grade 7's Haddis Alemayehu chapter sorts phrases into two columns: adjective + noun against adverb + verb. The rule: an adjective (ቅጽል) describes a NOUN — ቀይ ቦርሳ, ቀይ describes the bag's colour. An adverb (ተወሳከግስ) describes a VERB — ቶሎ መጣ, ቶሎ describes መጣ by manner. The sorting set: ጥቁር አንበሣ, ግምኛ ሄደች, ክፉ ሰው, ክፉኛ ተራገመች, ረጅም ልጅ, ጥሩ እናት, ቶሎ አልተናገረችም, ወፍራም ጎማ, ገና አልተንቀሳቀሰም, ነጭ ጃኬት. Ask of each describing word: WHAT does it describe — a thing, or an action? The answer is its column.

## Sub lesson 17 — Grade 7+8: adverb types, persons, and picking adverbs out

Three more adverb works. Grade 7 (p.59): fill a table from given sentences with the pronoun, its person, and the ADVERB'S TYPE — of time (የጊዜ), of place (የቦታ), of manner (የሁኔታ). Grade 8 again (pp.103–104): pick out the adverbial words in sentences — the model: ዘርፌ ትናንት ደስተኛ ነበረች, the adverb is ትናንት; the items carry ነገ, ገና, ክፉኛ, ቶሎ, እንደገና, ዛሬ. Picking an adverb out is the reverse of building with one: find the verb, then find what leans on it.

## Sub lesson 18 — Grade 8: words that describe nouns and verbs

Grade 8's Goze Mosque chapter picks out modifiers of both kinds in sentences: የስም ገላጭ (noun-modifiers) and የግስ ገላጭ (verb-modifiers), on the model ጎበዟ ልጅ ነገ ትመጣለች — ጎበዟ modifies the noun ልጅ; the time and manner words modify the verb. Then write 3 own sentences containing BOTH a noun-modifier and a verb-modifier. One sentence, two leanings: find the noun's describer and the verb's describer separately, or the sentence stays half-read.

## Sub lesson 19 — Three-letter words: writing them, and nouns against verbs (teacher guide — evidence)

This part is teacher-guide evidence, and it carries a sorting the course needs. First the writing: write fidel and three-letter words on the line — ወፈረ, ገበረ, ከበረ. Then sort printed three-letter words into two families: ስሞች (nouns — naming words) such as በቀሎ and ሚላት, against ግሶች (verbs — doing words) such as ሰበረ, ከበበ and ዘመረ. Say each word, ask 'does it name a thing, or does it do something?', and place it. A noun names; a verb does — that one question sorts the whole set.
## Sub lesson 20 — Common nouns and proper nouns — ወንዝ or አባይ? (teacher guide — evidence)

## Sub lesson 22 — Word classes at work in sentences — the Grade 10 guide (teacher guide — evidence)

Word Kinds teaches the classes by definition, and the Grade 9 guide's in-context adjective work stands in this lesson already. Two Grade 10 guide parts take the classes *into* sentences, and they are stocked here as labelled evidence.

**A word changes its class (Chapter 5).** Learners **build sentences with given complex words** — and the chapter's stated outcome is to **show how a word's word class changes when it enters a sentence**. A word is not born into its class forever; the sentence it stands in can re-make it.

**Classifying from context (Chapter 7).** Learners classify the word class of **bold-underlined words from the context they occur in** — recalling the six-class list of Chapter 2 — and write sentences that obey grammatical rules. The guide's key is printed: **verb, adverb, verb, noun, adjective, verb, pronoun**. The class is read off the word's work in its sentence, never off the word alone.

## Say it to your companion

Name the six kinds in the books' order — ስም, ግስ, ቅጽል, አያያዥ ቃላት, ተውሳከግስ, መስተዋድድ — and give one example of each from this lesson: ሰው, ሄደ, ቀይ, እና, ቶሎ, ወደ. Then let your companion test you: they read a word, you name its kind and say its job. Then go deeper: let them name a noun — ደም, ጎንደር, መንጋ, ዉኃ — and you say which noun family it belongs to: ወል ስም or የተጸውኦ ስም, ጥቅል ስም, የሚቆጠሩ or የማይቆጠሩ. Last, point at yourself, at your companion, and at someone far away, and say the pronoun for each person as you point: እኔ, አንተ / አንቺ, እሱ / እሷ.

Then stand in the places: you speak (first person), your companion listens (second person), and together you talk about a third person who is not listening (third person). Swap places twice and say the pronouns again each time. Finish with the adjective pairs: say ረጅም ቀሚስ, አዲስ መኪና and ጎጂ ልማድ, and let your companion name the adjective and the noun in each pair — and what the adjective tells: kind, size, type or origin.
