# Lesson plan — Speaking and Performing

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Speaking and Performing
**Place in the curriculum:** the Conversation strand — the speaking (መናገር) work of the Story and Sentence units (CURRICULUM.md's titles list Conversation as its own strand).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** speaking as performance: the two storytelling roles (ተራች the teller, መላሽ the responder), role play, debate, and reading aloud well — accuracy, speed and expression, in pairs, groups and echo — grown in Band 3 into the formal spoken forms: the full debate protocol (ክርክር) with debaters' and judges' rules, the panel discussion (ውይይት) with its moderator and participants, oral poetry performed aloud (ቃላዊ ግጥም), and a first meeting with qene (ቅኔ).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Stories and the reading lessons — the learner can hear, read and retell stories; this lesson puts the voice on stage, the way Ethiopia's books run their speaking slots.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Storytelling roles — ተራች and መላሽ** — Ethiopia's own way: a tale is a two-person performance — the teller tells, the responder answers; tale openings and closings; groups role-play the two roles.
2. **Role play** — take a part and speak as the part: the mule and the car, the litigant and the judge, a telephone call — in pairs and groups, each in character.
3. **Debate** — the books' debate craft: a claim is made, one group supports and one opposes, each defending its side from prepared notes.
4. **Reading aloud well** — the fluency trio — ትክክለኛነት accuracy, ፍጥነት speed, ገላጭነት expression (teacher guide — evidence) — practised in the three modes: paired reading, group reading, echo reading (teacher guide — evidence), with the student book's own fluency rules.
5. **Debate, the full protocol (ክርክር)** — the formal debate whole: an ordered argument between two people/groups before appointed judges; the debaters' rules (prepare, greet, introduce, points in order, note the opponent for rebuttal, evidence, time, eye contact, thanks) and the judges' rules (greet, introduce left/right debaters and topic, remind of time, quiet following, summarise, announce the winner). Grade 7 student book.
6. **Panel discussion (ውይይት)** — the two roles: አወያይ the moderator and ተወያይ the participants; the full protocol — grasp the topic and gather information, start on time with thanks, introduce topic and purpose, opening idea, main ideas clearly and in order, a loud orderly voice, sufficient reasons, respect and thanks, finish in time, summarise and thank. Grade 7 student book.
7. **Oral poetry, performed aloud (ቃላዊ ግጥም)** (teacher guide — evidence) — the teacher performs the harvest work-poem first; learners listen for what makes it a poem, then chant it; then compose their own oral poems with seasonal words and read them aloud with a raised voice, expressively. Grade 6 teacher guide.
8. **A first meeting with qene (ቅኔ)** (teacher guide — evidence) — qene poems carry a surface meaning and a hidden second meaning; learners interpret them by the methods of qene interpretation (የቅኔ አፈታት ዘዴዎች), practised inside the reading work; a set, examined skill. Grade 8 teacher guide.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Stories (story/first-stories) and Writing About Stories (story/writing-about-stories). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play both storytelling roles — ተራች and መላሽ — with a partner, with an opening and a closing.
2. Role-play a scene in character with a partner or group.
3. Take a side in a debate and defend it from prepared notes.
4. Read aloud by the trio — accurate, at a good speed, with expression — in paired, group and echo reading.
5. Run a formal debate (ክርክር) by the full protocol — every debater rule and every judge rule, in order.
6. Sit a panel discussion (ውይይት) in its two roles — አወያይ moderating, ተወያይ participating — following the protocol from gathering information to the closing summary.
7. Hear an oral poem performed, chant it, and perform an oral poem of your own aloud with a raised voice.
8. Meet qene (ቅኔ): state a qene poem's surface meaning and hunt the hidden meaning under it, by a method of interpretation.

## What you need
A partner or a small group. A short text to read aloud. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- The responder sits silent → the responder's part is real: answer, react, ask. A tale with a silent መላሽ is a lecture, not a telling.
- Role play becomes reading the book aloud in a funny voice → put the book down: know your part's want (the judge wants the truth; the mule wants respect) and speak from it.
- Debate becomes shouting → notes first, turns always; you answer the other side's last point, not their loudest one.
- Reading aloud is raced → the trio has three dials, not one: if the words go wrong, speed was too high; slow to where accuracy and expression live.
- A debate's rebuttal is empty → the debater skipped the noting rule: listen exactly, write the opponent's points down, and build the answer from their own words.
- One voice eats the whole panel → that is the አወያይ's job to fix: the moderator holds the order, gives the turns, and brings the quiet participants in.
- Qene feels like a riddle with no key → go back to the surface: say the plain meaning first, in full; the hidden meaning hangs on the poem's own words, and the methods of interpretation (የቅኔ አፈታት ዘዴዎች) are practised poem by poem, not guessed.
