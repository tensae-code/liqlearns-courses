# Lesson plan — Speaking and Performing

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Speaking and Performing
**Place in the curriculum:** the Conversation strand — the speaking (መናገር) work of the Story and Sentence units (CURRICULUM.md's titles list Conversation as its own strand).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** speaking as performance: the two storytelling roles (ተራች the teller, መላሽ the responder), role play, debate, and reading aloud well — accuracy, speed and expression, in pairs, groups and echo.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on First Stories and the reading lessons — the learner can hear, read and retell stories; this lesson puts the voice on stage, the way Ethiopia's books run their speaking slots.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Storytelling roles — ተራች and መላሽ** — Ethiopia's own way: a tale is a two-person performance — the teller tells, the responder answers; tale openings and closings; groups role-play the two roles.
2. **Role play** — take a part and speak as the part: the mule and the car, the litigant and the judge, a telephone call — in pairs and groups, each in character.
3. **Debate** — the books' debate craft: a claim is made, one group supports and one opposes, each defending its side from prepared notes.
4. **Reading aloud well** — the fluency trio — ትክክለኛነት accuracy, ፍጥነት speed, ገላጭነት expression (teacher guide — evidence) — practised in the three modes: paired reading, group reading, echo reading (teacher guide — evidence), with the student book's own fluency rules.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is
the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First Stories (story/first-stories) and Writing About Stories (story/writing-about-stories). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play both storytelling roles — ተራች and መላሽ — with a partner, with an opening and a closing.
2. Role-play a scene in character with a partner or group.
3. Take a side in a debate and defend it from prepared notes.
4. Read aloud by the trio — accurate, at a good speed, with expression — in paired, group and echo reading.

## What you need
A partner or a small group. A short text to read aloud. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- The responder sits silent → the responder's part is real: answer, react, ask. A tale with a silent መላሽ is a lecture, not a telling.
- Role play becomes reading the book aloud in a funny voice → put the book down: know your part's want (the judge wants the truth; the mule wants respect) and speak from it.
- Debate becomes shouting → notes first, turns always; you answer the other side's last point, not their loudest one.
- Reading aloud is raced → the trio has three dials, not one: if the words go wrong, speed was too high; slow to where accuracy and expression live.
