# Speaking and Performing — true or false

Say whether each sentence is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A tale told with a silent responder is still a full two-person telling.
**Answer: False** — the responder answers; a silent መላሽ makes it a lecture, not a telling.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** In the judge role play, each speaker stays in character — judge, plaintiff, defendant.
**Answer: True.**

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** If your reading-aloud words keep going wrong, the trio says your speed is right and accuracy must catch up.
**Answer: False** — the trio balances all three; words going wrong means speed was too high for accuracy.

**4. (Sub lesson 5)** A good debater may ignore the other side's points and only repeat their own.
**Answer: False** — the debaters' rules say the opposite: listen exactly, note the opponent's points, and build the rebuttal from them.

**5. (Sub lesson 6)** The moderator's work includes introducing the topic and its purpose at the start, and summarising at the end.
**Answer: True.**

**6. (Sub lesson 7)** In the oral-poetry work, learners first read the poem silently, and only later hear it performed.
**Answer: False** — the teacher performs the poem aloud first; learners listen for what makes it a poem, then chant it.

**7. (Sub lesson 8)** A qene poem carries only the meaning on its surface.
**Answer: False** — a qene poem carries two meanings: the surface meaning and a hidden second meaning under it.
