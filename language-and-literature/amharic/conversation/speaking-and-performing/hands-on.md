# Hands-on — Speaking and Performing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Tale night at home
Tell a tale at home the two-role way: you are ተራች, a family
member is መላሽ. Use a real opening and a real closing. Then
swap roles and tell a different tale. The responder must answer
at least five times — count together.

## The market scene
In pairs, role-play seller and buyer at the market (the books'
own scene). The buyer must ask the price, bargain politely, and
decide. Then swap. A third person judges: did each stay in
character to the end?

## Kitchen debate
At home or in class, debate a books' motion — "traditional sport
entertains people more than modern sport." Each side writes
three notes first. Speak in turns of one minute. The listeners
say which side answered the other's points better — not which
side was louder.

## The trio check
Read one paragraph aloud three ways: once in a pair (taking
turns by sentence), once in a group together, once as echo
after a leader. After each, the listeners score the trio on
their fingers: accuracy? speed? expression?

## A protocol debate at the table
Three people: two debaters, one judge. The judge keeps the
judges' rules on a paper checklist — greet, introduce, time,
quiet, summary, winner — and ticks each one as it happens.
The debaters each keep a notebook open: no rebuttal may be
spoken that is not built from a noted point.

## Threshing-floor poetry
Outside, with sticks and a pile of anything threshable (or
just the rhythm on a table): one person leads an oral poem
aloud, everyone chants the refrain back. Then each person
adds one line of their own, in the season's words, voice
raised — the poem grows the way work-poems grow.
