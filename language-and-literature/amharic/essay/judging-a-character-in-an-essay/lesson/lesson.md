# Judging a Character in an Essay

## Do it first

A son hid his own mother from his friends. Judge him in one sentence. Keep the sentence — Sub lesson 1 shows how the book makes you EARN that judgement across four paragraphs first.

## Sub lesson 1 — ቀበጡን ልጅ — judging the spoilt son

After the story of the mother and the fire, Grade 8 sets an evaluative essay on ቀበጡን ልጅ — the son who was ashamed of his scarred mother. Its hint bigar has four parts, and the essay has four paragraphs to match: 1) children and parents in general; 2) children who do not want to part from their parents; 3) children who do not want to be seen with their parents; 4) your judgement on both. The shape is the lesson: an evaluative essay does not shout its verdict first. It lays out the general ground, sets both cases fairly, and only then judges — with the story's evidence (the fire, the blanket, the photo, the tears) as its proof.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.
