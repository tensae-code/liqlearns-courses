# Problem-and-Solution Essays

## Do it first

A hillside loses its trees. Write what happens next, and next — three steps. Then keep your chain: Sub lesson 1 shows the book's own chain for the same hillside.

## Sub lesson 1 — Problem chain → solution chain

Grade 8's problem-and-solution essay is built on a bigar with two chains. The problem chain: forest felling → land stripping → soil erosion → drought → poverty and exile. The solution chain answers it link for link: planting seedlings → forest expansion → the land wearing green → sufficient rain → greenness and soil enrichment → development and prosperity. Three paragraphs carry it: the problem and how it grows, the turn, and the solution and what it grows. The craft rule: every link in your solution chain must answer a link in your problem chain — a solution that answers nothing is decoration.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.
