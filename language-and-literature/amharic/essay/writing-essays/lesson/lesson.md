# Writing Essays

## Do it first

Explain, in writing, how coffee is brewed — የቡና አፈላል.
Use four short paragraphs: getting the materials ready; lighting
the fire; the washing, pounding and brewing; serving.

Write it before you read on. When you finish, look at your first
paragraph and ask: is there ONE sentence in it that carries the
whole paragraph's idea? Keep the page — Sub lesson 1 names what
you have just tried to build.

## Sub lesson 1 — The expository essay and its topic sentence

An **expository essay (አስረጂ ድርሰት)** explains a thing: its
**nature**, how it **works**, or how it is **applied**. It does
not tell a story and it does not argue — it makes a thing clear.

[Picture 8840 — writing to explain: brewing coffee]

The worked essay is the one you attempted: **"የቡና አፈላል"
(brewing coffee), in four paragraphs**, built from an organiser:

1. prepare the materials;
2. lighting the fire;
3. from washing to pounding-grinding;
4. serving and concluding.

One organiser part, one paragraph. That is the expository shape:
divide the explanation into its true parts, and give each part
its own paragraph.

Every paragraph in such an essay stands on one sentence — its
**topic sentence (መንደርደሪያ ዓረፍተነገር)**, the sentence that
carries the paragraph's whole idea. The other sentences explain,
detail and support it.

[Picture 8841 — the topic sentence carries the paragraph]

The set work after building the coffee essay is exactly this:
**pick out each paragraph's topic sentence.** Practise it on
your Do-it-first page: underline the one sentence each of your
paragraphs stands on. If a paragraph has no such sentence, it
does not have its spine yet — write the sentence, and the
paragraph stands.

The same shape then goes free: a four-paragraph expository essay
on a topic of your own choice, following the same note — correct
its punctuation first alone, then in a group.

## Sub lesson 2 — The narrative essay

A **narrative essay (ተራኪ ድርሰት)** recounts actions and chained
facts **keeping their time sequence** — "this happened, then
this was done." The order is the essay's skeleton: what came
first is told first.

[Picture 8842 — a narrative essay keeps the time order]

The narrator — the one telling — can stand in three places:

- a **participant** in the events (I was there, I did it);
- an **observer** (I watched it happen);
- one who **heard it from another** (it was told to me).

Choose your place before you write, and keep it: a participant
says "I", an observer watches the actors, and a hearer keeps
clear whose doing is being retold.

The set task: **write a narrative essay taking a river in your
area as your topic** — follow the river's events in their time
order, from where it rises to where it goes.

## Sub lesson 3 — The cause-and-effect essay

A cause-and-effect essay is built from key sentences that join a
**cause** to its **effect**. The worked organiser is
**"የበጎፈቃደኖች መኖር" — volunteers exist**:

[Picture 8843 — cause and effect: volunteers exist]

- it helped raise children without caretakers;
- it kept the elderly from living in hardship;
- it lightened the community's problem.

One cause, three effects — and the set task is a **three-
paragraph essay** from that organiser, checking that **each
paragraph's key sentences are built as cause and effect**.

The same craft first appears as repair work: a model paragraph
on the causes of climate disturbance — deforestation and smoky
industries — is rewritten and improved, following the worked
example: plants take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen;
felled forests and industrial smoke pollute the air and disturb
the climate. Notice what the improvement does: it makes every
"because" true. Test each of your own key sentences the same
way — did this cause MAKE that effect happen?

## Sub lesson 4 — The argumentative paragraph and essay

An argumentative piece takes a position and defends it. The
craft arrives first as the **argumentative paragraph
(ኤከራካሪ አንቀጽ)** — one paragraph that states a side and holds
it with reasons.

It then grows to the whole essay: starting from a given **model
of how an argumentative essay (አካራካሪ/አከራካሪ ድርሰት) is
written**, each learner writes a **three-paragraph essay
(ባለ ሦስት አንቀፅ ድርሰት)** on the set topic.

Work it the model's way:

1. Read the model first — find where its position is stated.
2. Find the reasons, in their order.
3. Then write your own three paragraphs in the same shape:
   position first, reasons in order, and a close that holds the
   position.

An argumentative essay is not a quarrel on paper: the other
side is answered with reasons, not with noise.

## Sub lesson 5 — The organised essay built from a web

The biggest essay in this lesson is not planned in a list — it
is planned on **one web**. Everything about the topic goes onto
the web first: **ኤች አይ ቪ/ኤድስ** at the centre, and six
branches around it:

[Picture 8844 — one web holds the whole essay]

- **ምንነት** — what it is;
- **አመጣጥ** — origin;
- **አሁን ያለበት ሁኔታ** — its present state;
- **ምልክቶች** — signs;
- **መተላለፊያ መንገዶች** — the ways it passes;
- **ህክምና** — treatment.

Then the task: **write a complete essay (ድርሰት) from the web** —
one branch at a time, in the web's order, until every branch has
become writing. The review lesson then **edits and rewrites**
that essay: the web holds the plan, the first writing fills it,
and the rewrite makes it clean.

[GIF 8845 — web branches feeding the essay]

This is the organiser craft at full size: if it is not on the
web, it does not go in the essay; if it is on the web, the essay
must find it a place.

## Sub lesson 6 — The explanatory essay, grown up

The explanatory essay does not retire after Sub lesson 1 — the
higher classes return to it with a working order around it.

**First, review the craft.** Before writing, go back over
essay-writing techniques, and study **the types of essay
(የድርሰት ዓይነቶች)** as a study note: know the kinds of essay
there are before you choose the one your topic needs.

[Picture 9050 — the explanatory essay: how the work goes]

**Then write — from prompts, together.** Three prompts are
given; the writing is done **in pairs or groups**. The
explaining is yours; the topic is set, so all your strength
goes into making the thing clear.

[GIF 9051 — the essay growing paragraph by paragraph from a prompt]

**The same craft at paragraph size** is the **explanatory
paragraph (አስረጅ አንቀጽ)**: one worked paragraph explains the
mediation process itself, step by step, with note-taking as
part of the task — and another explains a title of your
choosing the same way. Paragraph or essay, the job is one:
make the thing clear to a reader who does not yet know it.

**Explaining things a reader may never have seen** is taught
with a model bank of everyday technology and what each thing
is for:

- **computer** — writing, reading, film, internet;
- **photocopier**; **iron**; **laundry**;
- **hair-dryer (ካስክ)**; **combine harvester**; **tractor**;
  **fridge**.

The bank exists because many readers may not know these
products — and that is the explanatory writer's whole lesson:
explain for the reader you actually have, naming the thing
and what it is for, in order, until it is clear.

## Say it to your companion

Your companion names three writing jobs: explaining how a jebena
is made; telling what happened at the river last week; defending
the position that school gardens should grow food. For each one,
name the essay kind you would choose — expository, narrative,
cause-and-effect or argumentative — and say why in one sentence.
Then swap: your companion must challenge one of your choices
with a reason.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a four-paragraph expository essay on how something near home is made or done. Underline each paragraph's topic sentence (መንደርደሪያ ዓረፍተነገር).
2. Write a short narrative essay (ተራኪ ድርሰት) about a river, a road or a journey near home, keeping the time order; write your narrator's place (participant, observer, hearer) at the top.
3. Build a cause-and-effect organiser with one cause and at least two effects, and write the essay from it; mark each key sentence C → E.
4. Choose a position you can defend, and write a three-paragraph argumentative essay (ባለ ሦስት አንቀፅ ድርሰት) following the model's shape.
5. Build one web around a big topic, count its branches, and write a complete essay from it. Edit and rewrite it once, as the review does.
6. Do the grown-up order before your next explanatory essay: review the essay types (የድርሰት ዓይነቶች) from your notes, set yourself three topics, take one, and write the essay with a partner. Then swap pages and check each other's explanations: is every thing named, and what it is for?
