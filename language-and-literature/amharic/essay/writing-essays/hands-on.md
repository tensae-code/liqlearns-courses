# Hands-on — Writing Essays

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Brew it, then write it
Watch (or help with) a real coffee brewing at home. Note the
four parts as they happen — materials, fire, washing and
pounding, serving — then write the expository essay from your
notes, not from memory. Underline each topic sentence.

## Walk the river
Walk along a stream, ditch or river near home. Note five things
in the order you meet them. That order is your narrative essay's
order — write it at home without moving a single event.

## Cause hunt at home
Find three real cause-and-effect pairs at home or on the street
(rain → mud; fire → smoke; study → a passed test). Say each pair
aloud with "because" between them, then write them as key
sentences for a short essay.

## The web on the wall
Draw one big web on a sheet for a topic your household knows —
a crop, a trade, a feast. Everyone adds one branch word. Then
each person takes one branch and writes its paragraph; read the
essay aloud together, branch by branch.
