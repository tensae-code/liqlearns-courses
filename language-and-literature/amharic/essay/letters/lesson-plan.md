# Lesson plan — Letters

**Course:** Amharic · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Letters
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Essay (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every letter kind the books teach, and the shape each one keeps — the personal letter, the greeting letter and its named parts, the congratulatory letter, official and request letters, the appreciation letter, the invitation letter, and the application letter.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Descriptive Paragraphs and on Summary and News — the learner can already build a paragraph around one idea and write to a frame; a letter is paragraphs with an address: the same writing, wearing the shape its kind demands.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The personal letter (የግል ደብዳቤ)** — the Grade 5 model letter's form: sender's name, town and P.O. box (የመ.ሣ.ቁ) at top, the date, the recipient, the greeting, the body, the close and signature (ፊርማ) — and what its model carries: health enquiries, school news, a promise to talk when they meet.
2. **The greeting letter (የሰላምታ ደብዳቤ) and its parts** — the Grade 7 model with its parts labelled: the greeting part (የሰላምታ ክፍል), the body part (የጉዳይ ክፍል), the closing part (የመዝጊያ ክፍል), and the signature.
3. **The congratulatory letter (የደስታ መግለጫ ደብዳቤ)** — the Grade 6 official model: heading, number, date, addressee, subject line ጉዳዩ, body, closing ከሠላምታ ጋር, signature — a prime minister's office congratulating Olympic athletes; the learner's task mirrors it as a school principal.
4. **Official and request letters** — the official letter frame (letterhead, abbreviations, reference number), the Grade 6 request letter's two-sided letterhead with its subject code and file number, and the Grade 7 office letter's full form: letterhead stamp (የራስጌ ማህተም), number, date, to, subject, detailed idea (ዝርዝር ሐሳብ), copy (ግልባጭ), closing, the office head's signature, name and title, round stamp.
5. **The appreciation letter (የአድናቆት ደብዳቤ)** — the Grade 6 model: a parent writes to the principal after the school celebration, greeting, body and warm closing; learners write their own to a parent, teacher, friend or club coordinator, then edit and read it aloud.
6. **The invitation letter (የግብዣ ጥሪ ደብዳቤ)** — letters that vary with the occasion (wedding, baptism ክርስትና, tsebel ፀበል, memorial ተዝካር); the wedding invitation form with its blanks, filled, exchanged and corrected.
7. **The application letter (የማመልከቻ ደብዳቤ)** — the Grade 7 model: a personal letter that enters an office, so it is written in formal words; date, office, subject line, the humble request in the body, closing ከሠላምታ ጋር, signature and name.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write a personal letter (የግል ደብዳቤ) in the model's form, sender to signature.
2. Write a greeting letter naming and filling its three parts: የሰላምታ ክፍል, የጉዳይ ክፍል, የመዝጊያ ክፍል.
3. Set out a congratulatory letter with every official part, from heading to signature.
4. Build an official or request letter on its frame — letterhead zones, number, subject code, ጉዳዩ — and name the office letter's full list of parts.
5. Write an appreciation letter (የአድናቆት ደብዳቤ) on the model, edit it and read it aloud.
6. Fill the wedding invitation form's blanks and write an invitation for another occasion.
7. Write an application letter (የማመልከቻ ደብዳቤ) with its subject line and humble request, in formal words.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. An envelope if you have one, to see where the finished letter goes.

## If the learner is stuck
- The parts come out in a jumble → lay the shape picture beside the page and write part by part, checking each one off; a letter is built top to bottom, never from the middle.
- A personal letter is written like an office letter (or the other way round) → ask who reads it: a friend reads warm, plain words; an office reads formal words, a number and a subject line — the reader chooses the shape.
- The ጉዳዩ line is missing → stop: an official or application letter without its subject line is a letter that hides its own errand; write the errand in one line first.
- An invitation's blanks stay empty → read the form aloud slowly; each blank asks one plain question — to whom? whose wedding? which day? with whom?
