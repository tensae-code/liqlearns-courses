# Hands-on — Life Stories

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Interview an elder
Sit with a grandparent or an older neighbour. Ask the seven
points, one by one, and write the answers in a notebook — dates
on the left, events on the right, like the table. Thank them;
read the life back to them and fix what you heard wrong.

## The family timeline
Draw one long line on a big sheet: the years of your family.
Each person adds one event at its place — a birth, a move, a
school, a wedding. The sheet is a table and a timeline at once;
tell the family's story along the line, in order.

## Run the story
Mark a start line and a finish line in a yard. One person tells
Abebe Bikila's life while another walks the course slowly: at
Rome, stop and say "2:15:16.2"; at Tokyo, stop and say
"2:12:11.2". The times must be exact before the finish line is
crossed.

## Record a life, play it twice
With permission, record an elder telling one part of their
life on a phone — five minutes is enough. Play it back
twice: the first time, write only the main points; the
second, add the details under them. Then tell the life to
the elder from your notes and let them correct it — the
recording method, at home.

## A name that sums a life
Dr Catherine Hamlin is called "Mother of Ethiopia's Mothers"
because of what she did. Talk at home: is there a person your
family calls by a name like that — a name earned by deeds?
Gather their life by the seven points, with permission.
