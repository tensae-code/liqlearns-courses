# Compare-and-Contrast Essays

## Do it first

Try the first task in Sub lesson 1 before you read any explanation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Own country vs another country

The form opens with a comparison bigar: 'One's Own Country and Another's Country' (የራስ ሀገርና የሰው ሀገር). The own-country column holds living, working, wealth-making, full citizenship, pride and equality; the other country's column holds second citizenship, shame and inferiority. The essay is built from the table: first what stands on each side, then the judgement the table supports. A compare-and-contrast essay is never two descriptions pasted together — every paragraph must carry BOTH things, compared point by point.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bat vs Hen, Lamp vs Generator

The book practises the form again and again. 'The Bat and the Hen' (የሌሊትወፍና ዶሮ): both have wings; the bat is a mammal, active by night, sleeping by day, inciting trouble, ending ashamed; the hen lays eggs, is active by day, preaches peace, and ends proud. 'Lamp and Generator' (አምፖልና ጀነሬተር): the youth like the lamp that shines, his mother like the unseen generator that makes the shining possible. Notice the shape both times: a shared point first (wings; light), then the differences that carry the meaning.

## Sub lesson 3 — The comparative essay 'ተማሪ' and good vs bad neighbours

Two more tasks complete the set. 'ተማሪ' (The Student) compares students of low learning-desire with students of high learning-desire, from a two-column bigar: teacher as source vs starting-point of knowledge, leaving class vs never absent, asking little vs asking much. And 'Good Neighbour and Bad Neighbour' (መልካምና ክፉ ጎረቤት): the bad neighbour thinks ill, obstructs work, defames names and gives no help; the good neighbour thinks good, cooperates, arrives quickly in bad times and good, and keeps the area's peace; shared by both — area resident, found nearby, member of the iddir, sharing the area's bad and good. The last task of the chapter set asks for a four-paragraph comparative essay on a title you choose yourself, after completing bigar tables of describing words and roles.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.

2. Do the last sub lesson's task once more on your own, without looking, then check against the lesson.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, in your own words.
