# Writing One Essay Together as a Group

## Do it first

You and a friend each wrote about the same topic. Read both drafts. Point to the ONE sentence in the other draft that is better than yours on the same point. That pointing is the start of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — From my essay to our essay

Grade 8's climate chapter ends its writing with a group task: the essays on the causes and effects of man-made climate change were first written individually — now, in groups, improve them into one group essay. The method: lay the group's drafts side by side; take the clearest statement of each point, wherever it stands; settle disagreements with evidence, not volume; rewrite the joined essay in one voice; and revise the result against the ten criteria before it is finished. The group essay is judged as one essay — one title, one flow, one voice — however many hands wrote it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the main thing each sub lesson taught, in your own words, in order. Your companion asks one question about each part, and you answer from the lesson. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the key Amharic words of this lesson in your notebook, each with its meaning or its job.
2. Do the last sub lesson's task once more on your own, without looking, then check against the lesson.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, in your own words.
