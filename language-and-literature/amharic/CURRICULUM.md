# Amharic course — CURRICULUM (the path)

Amharic is Ethiopia's own language, and in the Language and
Literature structure it stands as its own course inside that
folder (his architecture ruling, 2026-11-11). The path climbs by
the size of the thing — **First Steps → Fidel → Word → Sentence →
Paragraph → Essay → Story** (see TITLES.md).

**Levels, not grades:** a learner climbs when they are ready,
not when a school calendar says so. The school grades the books
come from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance),
never as the course's organisation. The pre-primary books are
this course's earliest level — First Steps — not a separate
course.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from Ethiopia's own government books for learning
Amharic — the pre-primary workbooks of the Addis Ababa City
Administration Education Bureau and, as the higher levels are
built, the Amharic student textbooks and teacher guides named in
each lesson's sources. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a
tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## Unit 1 — First Steps (the pre-primary level)

1. **Ready Hands — Before the Pencil** (`first-steps/pre-writing`)
   — holding, scribbling, following the dotted line. The grip
   itself is taught in the shared Pencil and Lines course; this
   lesson is the hand's readiness before it.
2. **My Body** (`first-steps/my-body`) — the parts of the body;
   the five senses.
3. **Feelings** (`first-steps/feelings`) — happy, surprised, sad,
   angry — naming and matching faces.
4. **Cleanliness** (`first-steps/cleanliness`) — washing hands;
   brushing teeth; staying clean every day.
5. **Family and Me** (`first-steps/family-and-me`) — all about
   me; my family.
6. **My Home** (`first-steps/my-home`) — the rooms; the things in
   them.
7. **School Things** (`first-steps/school-things`) — the things
   a learner carries and uses.
8. **Manners and Living Together**
   (`first-steps/manners-and-living-together`) — greetings, care
   and living side by side.
9. **Animals** (`first-steps/animals`) — animals at home; wild
   animals; the walia; farm animals, their homes and their young.
10. **Plants** (`first-steps/plants`) — the parts of a plant;
    caring for plants and watching them grow.
11. **Food** (`first-steps/food`) — fruits; vegetables; food
    that keeps us healthy.
12. **Water** (`first-steps/water`) — water from nature; water
    people bring.
13. **Weather and Seasons** (`first-steps/weather-and-seasons`)
    — today's weather; Ethiopia's seasons.
14. **Clothes** (`first-steps/clothes`) — the clothes we wear;
    clothes for the season.
15. **Nature and Man-Made**
    (`first-steps/nature-and-man-made`) — made by people or not;
    living or not.
16. **Counting** (`first-steps/counting`) — 1 to 9; zero and on
    to 10 (zero taught first, the books' own order); to 20 and
    the number names; the Ge'ez numerals ፩–፲. First steps only —
    the Maths course owns the full number course.
17. **Adding and Taking Away**
    (`first-steps/adding-and-taking-away`) — adding; taking away;
    comparing (more, less, the same). First steps only, as above.
18. **Colours** (`first-steps/colours`) — the first colours;
    mixing colours (red+yellow = ብርቱካናማ, yellow+blue = green,
    blue+red = ወይንጠጅ).
19. **Shapes and Patterns**
    (`first-steps/shapes-and-patterns`) — the shapes; patterns.
20. **Size and Quantity** (`first-steps/size-and-quantity`) —
    how big, how many; how heavy.
21. **Position Words** (`first-steps/position-words`) — in, on,
    under, beside; in front, behind, left, right.
22. **Money** (`first-steps/money`) — the birr notes; the santim
    coins (drawn teaching pictures, named as such in the lesson).
23. **Time** (`first-steps/time`) — the seven days of the week;
    the thirteen months of the Ethiopian year.
24. **Transport** (`first-steps/transport`) — ways we travel;
    long ago and now.
25. **Technology** (`first-steps/technology`) — the machines
    and tools of today, named.
26. **Professions** (`first-steps/professions`) — the people who
    help us and the work they do.
27. **Farming** (`first-steps/farming`) — farming then and now;
    tools and grains (the second part rests on the ECCE training
    module, labelled SUPPORTING in the lesson's sources).
28. **Play and Moving** (`first-steps/play-and-moving`) —
    pushing, pulling, balancing, moving the body.
29. **Road Safety** (`first-steps/road-safety`) — the traffic
    light; crossing on green.
30. **Safety** (`first-steps/safety`) — things that can hurt us;
    staying safe when the earth or weather turns.
31. **Ethiopian Culture** (`first-steps/ethiopian-culture`) —
    the instruments (masenqo, washint, krar, kebero, begena,
    tsenatsil); celebrations and traditional food; the dances of
    the peoples.
32. **Basic Needs** (`first-steps/basic-needs`) — food, clothes,
    shelter: what everyone needs.

## Unit 2 — Fidel

1. **The Fidel** (`fidel/the-fidel`) — the full chart; the first
   letters (KG1's sixteen first letters, traced and copied); all
   33 families and their seven forms; first sounds, matching and
   the odd one out; first reading; a word for every form;
   building words. The sounds that get mixed up (ለ/ረ/ጠ/ተ) are
   carried in the lesson's support material from the special-
   needs toolkit, labelled SUPPORTING.

## Unit 3 — Word

1. **Words That Go Together** (`word/same-and-opposite-words`)
   — words that mean the same; words that mean the opposite.

## Unit 4 — Sentence

1. **My First Sentences** (`sentence/first-sentences`) — reading
   whole sentences; making sentences of your own.
2. **The Marks That Help Us Read**
   (`sentence/first-punctuation`) — ሁለት ነጥብ (colon), the
   four-dot mark, ነጠላ ሰረዝ (comma), የጥያቄ ምልክት (question
   mark).

## Unit 7 — Story

1. **First Stories** (`story/first-stories`) — Butiye and the
   flower; Ali and Hawa; Mamo and Gobeze; Gobez Meseret
   (SUPPORTING book). Stories are told as summaries and acted —
   hear it, say it, act it — never copied out of the books.

Units 5 (Paragraph) and 6 (Essay) open with the G1–G4 band,
where Ethiopia's books first teach them.
