# About this course — Amharic

This file carries the course's provenance: what the course is,
which books it is built from, how Ethiopia's own books teach the
language, and what is honestly missing. Lesson pages carry none
of this — a learner opens a lesson and goes straight to the
doing. The detail lives here, once, on purpose.

## What the course is

Amharic is Ethiopia's own language, and this course teaches it
the way Ethiopia's own government books teach it: the pre-primary
workbooks of the Addis Ababa City Administration Education
Bureau, then the Amharic student textbooks and teacher guides of
the school grades. The course climbs by the size of the thing —
First Steps → Fidel → Word → Sentence → Paragraph → Essay →
Story (with Conversation as the speaking strand) — and by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they are ready and
have proved the level before, never because a calendar says so.
The school grades the books come from appear only in each
lesson's sources, never as the course's organisation.

Everything taught traces to a book and a printed page, named in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`. Teacher-guide material is
always labelled **TG-EVIDENCE**; supporting books are labelled
**SUPPORTING**. Neither ever counts as pupil-taught. Where a
lesson rests only on teacher-guide evidence, its sources say so
and its acceptance count is 0 countries.

## How Amharic is taught in Ethiopia — the method

From the Grade 1 teacher guide's front matter (TG-EVIDENCE,
printed pp. IV–VI), and carried through every guide:

**The unit cycle.** Every unit (ምዕራፍ) runs **18 periods**
(18 ክፍለ ጊዜ) through the same strand cycle:

1. **ማዳመጥ (Listening)** — the teacher reads a story/text aloud
   (the texts are printed in full in the guides); before, during
   and after questions, with a printed prediction question.
2. **አዳምጦ መናገር (Listening & Speaking)** — retell and discuss
   the heard text in your own words; sequence its events.
3. **ተዛውታሪ ቃላት (Related words)** — a word-web of theme words.
4. **የጽሑፌት ጽንሰ ሃሳብ (Concepts of print)** — book handling,
   print direction, letter/word awareness.
5. **ተተኻሪ ፊደል (Changing letters)** — the unit's new fidel set:
   shapes known, written on slate, board and in the air, found
   inside words.
6. **ፊደል ቅርጽ መለየት (Letter-shape sorting)** — fidel sorted by
   "legs": ባለ አንድ እግር (one-legged), ባለ ሁለት እግር
   (two-legged), ባለ ሦስት እግር (three-legged), እግር አልባ
   (legless) — Ethiopia's own way of teaching fidel shapes.
7. **ቃላት (Words)** — first/last sounds, syllable counting by
   clapping, rhyming sets.
8. **ማባመርና መነጠል (Blending & segmenting)** — blend fidel into
   words (ገ+በ+ረ = ገበረ); segment words into fidel.
9. **ንባብ / ማንበብ (Reading)** — a short reading text, read and
   re-read toward fluency; fluency is judged in the higher
   guides on ትክክለኛነት (accuracy), ፍጥነት (speed) and ገላጭነት
   (expression), and practised in pairs, in groups (choral) and
   by echo.
10. **ጽሑፍ (Writing)** — copy fidel on the standard line
    (መደበኛ መስመር); write words, then sentences, then texts.
11. **የምዕራፉ ማጠቃለያ (Unit summary/assessment)** — retell the
    unit's main story from memory; write the unit's new fidel
    and words from dictation; the teacher's observation
    checklist.

The weekly student-book shape is the same cycle in miniature:
ማዳመጥ (listening) → ማንበብ (reading) → መጻፍ (writing) → ክለሳ
(review), with መናገር (speaking) slots joining in the higher
grades.

**The five principles** (መርህ 1–5, Grade 1 guide, printed p. V):

1. Learners arrive with knowledge and experience from home and
   community — build new learning on it.
2. Start from the whole story/text, then its parts.
3. Teach new words inside meaningful sentences and texts, not
   in isolation.
4. Practise letters inside words learners know.
5. Let learners use new words in speech and writing; assess
   language knowledge through use.

The "to the teacher" pages add: learners retell stories in
sequence; use learners' own stories and experiences; use the
learner book's pictures for talk; give repeated chances to read
familiar print; praise effort.

education (የሥርዓተ-ያታና የእኩል ትምህርት); the full Grade 5

**How the guides themselves are built.** The Grade 4 guide is
a SCRIPTED guide: full lesson scripts in coloured boxes,
objectives+contents openers, 18 periods per unit, full
listening texts, scripted dialogues, definition boxes, answer
keys and yellow summary pages (G4-0-1). The Grade 5 and 6
guides are TASK guides: task-by-task prose keyed to the
learner book — objectives+contents openers, 18-period plans,
per-part approach bullets, numbered tasks with printed answer
keys, a named teaching aid (የትምህርት መርኛ መሳሪያ) and an
assessment line (ምዘና) per part, full texts printed; Grade 6
adds the full affix system, the argumentative paragraph and
the end-of-unit trial test (የሙክራ ፈተና) (G5-0-1, G6-0-1). The
method both state (የማስተማር መርሆዎች, G5 TG PDF p. 5 and the
G6 front matter): the four skills plus vocabulary and grammar
are taught unseparated; comprehension is built in three
stages — ቅድመ (before), ሐይት/ሂደት (during), ድኅረ (after);
speaking and writing grow through many chances to express plus
timely feedback (ወቅታዊ ግብረመልስ); vocabulary is taught from
contexts, with the strategy of guessing a word's meaning from
the text; grammar follows the structures the syllabus selects
for the level (G5-0-2, G6-0-2).

**The upper guides' apparatus.** The Grade 7 guide exists so
learners listen, speak, read and write competently and build
