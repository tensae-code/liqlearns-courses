# Fidel Shapes — Letters With Legs

## Do it first

Look at these four letters: **ቀ ለ ጠ መ**. They do not stand the same way. One stands on one leg. One stands on two. One stands on three. One has no legs at all — it sits round on the line.

Point to the one you think has no legs. Point to the one with three legs. You are already doing this lesson: **sorting fidel by their shapes.** In Ethiopia's classrooms this is how children learn to tell the letters apart — by their legs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sorting by legs

Fidel shapes are sorted into four groups:

- **ባለ አንድ እግር — one leg:** ቀ ተ ቸ ኘ ሸ የ ገ
- **ባለ ሁለት እግር — two legs:** ለ ሐ ሸ በ ኡ ኸ ዘ ዠ ጀ ጸ ኈ
- **ባለ ሦስት እግር — three legs:** ጠ ጨ ሐ
- **እግር አልባ — no legs:** ሠ መ ወ ዐ ዑ ሀ ፈ ረ

[Picture 8255 — the four leg groups]

Look closely. A letter's *family* can visit more than one group, because the seven forms of a family do not all stand the same way: ሸ stands in the one-leg row and in the two-leg row, and ሐ stands in the two-leg row and the three-leg row. It is the **shape in front of you** that you sort — not the family's name.

Watch the cards sort themselves — each card walks to its own group:

[GIF 8257 — fidel cards sorting into the four leg groups]

Now sort with your eyes. I say a letter, you say its group: ቀ — one leg. ለ — two legs. ጠ — three legs. መ — no legs. Again, faster: ተ — one. በ — two. ጨ — three. ወ — none. Your eye is learning the shapes the way your hand learns a road: by walking it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Look-alike fidel (ሞክሸ ፊደላት)

Some fidel are tricksters: they look almost the same, and only one small stroke tells them apart. These are the **ሞክሸ ፊደላት** — the look-alike fidel. The way to beat them is to look at them side by side, inside words, and to learn each family's **keyword name** — the name the family is called by:

[Picture 8256 — look-alike families and their keyword names]

- ሀ — **ሀሌታው**
- ሐ — **ሐመሩ**
- ኘ — **ብዝኘኙ**
- ሠ — **ኛንው**
- ሰ — **እሳቱ**
- አ — **አልፋው**
- ዐ — **ዐይኑ**
- ጸ — **ጸሎቱ**
- ፀ — **ፃፍቁ**

Say the pairs slowly and look at what changes: ሀ and ሐ. ሰ and ሠ. አ and ዐ. ጸ and ፀ. The sound changes when the shape changes — that small stroke is the whole lesson. When you meet one of these letters inside a word, stop, look at the stroke, and name the family by its keyword name before you read on.

**Compound fidel (ውሰብሰብ ፊደላት).** Some fidel are built from two parts joined — the compound fidel, like the -ዋ forms and the derived families you met at the end of The Fidel (ሏ, ቋ and their friends). Read the compound fidel in order, then take any four of them, build a word with each one, and say what your word means. A word that holds a compound fidel can still be blended and split like any other word — the compound fidel counts as one piece.

## Say it to your companion

Name a letter; your companion says its leg group. Then name a look-alike pair — ሀ and ሐ — and your companion says each family's keyword name. Swap: your companion names, you answer. Finish by each building one word with a compound fidel and telling its meaning.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the four groups as four houses on paper: one leg, two legs, three legs, no legs. Write each letter from the lesson into its house.
2. Hunt look-alikes at home: find ሀ and ሐ on a book cover, a calendar or a shop sign. Say each one's keyword family name.
3. Take four compound fidel (ሏ, ቷ, ቋ, ጐ or your own picks). Build a word with each and say what it means.
4. Teach the leg groups to someone at home: show them ቀ, ለ, ጠ and መ, and let them sort the next four letters themselves.
