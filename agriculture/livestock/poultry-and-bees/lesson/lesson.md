# Poultry — layers and broilers; the bee colony

## Do it first
Two chickens, two jobs: one is kept for the eggs she lays month after month; the other is meat in under two months. And in a beehive, one large female rules, thousands of females work, and the males cannot even sting. Sort the jobs before the names come.

## Sub lesson 1 — Poultry — layers and broilers
**Poultry** includes **chickens, ducks, guinea fowl, turkeys and geese**. Chickens divide by job: **layers**, kept **for eggs**, and **broilers**, kept **for meat, usually slaughtered at 6–8 weeks**.

The split is the whole management: a layer is fed and housed for a long laying life; a broiler is fed for fast growth to an early table weight. Confuse the two jobs and both fail.

## Sub lesson 2 — The bee colony — queen, workers, drones
A honey-bee colony has **three castes** with separate duties. **Workers and the queen are female** — the **queen reproductive and larger**; the workers do the colony's work. **Drones are male, stingless, with much larger compound eyes**.

Bees live on **pollen and nectar**, and they give **pollination as well as honey** — the colony works the farm's crops while it feeds itself. Ethiopia's roughly 7 million beehives from the numbers lesson are colonies exactly like this one.
