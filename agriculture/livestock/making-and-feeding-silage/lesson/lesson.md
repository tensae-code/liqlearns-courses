# Making and feeding silage

## Do it first
Hay dries feed. But there is a way to keep feed juicy for months — sealed away from air, quietly fermenting. Safe if the steps are right, spoiled if air leaks in. And not every animal may eat it. Learn the steps and the rules before judging a silo.

## Sub lesson 1 — Making silage — the airtight method
**Silage is green crop preserved by controlled fermentation without air (ensilage)**, keeping succulent feed for scarce times; **grasses and grass–legume mixtures make very good silage**. The checklist: **harvest at the flowering stage (35–40% dry matter)**, **wilt to about 30% dry matter without over-wilting**, **chop to a size that packs well**, **pack and seal airtight** — with **protective clothing and prepared materials**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Feeding silage — the rules
Feed carefully: **to ruminants only**, **never on an empty rumen**, **not to late-pregnant cows, calves under six months, or sick, hungry and weak animals**. **Start a bit at a time and increase gradually** so the rumen adjusts. The rules protect the animal — silage is strong feed, introduced with respect.
