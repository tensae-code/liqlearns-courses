# Enemies of the hive

## Do it first
A hive has more enemies than a fortress: diseases in the brood, beetles in the corners, moths in the comb, ants on the march. Name the enemy before you can fight it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diseases of bees
The threats list opens with **diseases, including chalk brood, nosematosis and amoeba disease**. Disease strikes the colony's brood and bees themselves — the beekeeper's vigilance is the first medicine.

[Picture 26663 — a wax moth larva in damaged comb]

## Sub lesson 2 — Pests and predators
Then **pests and predators**: the **small hive beetle (*Aethina tumida*), bee lice, ants (*Dorylus fulvus*), wax moth (*Galleria mellonella*), mice, spiders and snakes**. Each enemy has its damage: comb destroyed, stores raided, bees eaten, colonies driven out. Knowing which enemy made which damage is the lesson's field skill.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, using one real example from your own area for each sub lesson. Your companion asks one question about each part — answer from the lesson, not from memory of other lessons.

## Do It Yourself
1. Make enemy cards: name, damage, first sign in the hive.
2. From damage pictures, diagnose which enemy struck.
3. Ask a beekeeper which enemy costs them most locally.
