# Why we house animals — comfort and welfare

## Do it first
The best feed, the best bull — and a muddy, freezing pen. Where does the profit leak? Before the lesson names it, list what a bad house steals from good feeding and breeding.

## Sub lesson 1 — The house decides the payoff
**Good housing protects animals from health problems and bad weather and lets feeding and breeding pay off — good feeding and breeding will not give maximum production in a bad house.** A **well-designed house makes feeding, watering, breeding, egg collection, milking and cleaning easier** and **protects workers too**.

[Picture 26017 — a cow resting comfortably in a clean, airy barn]

## Sub lesson 2 — Welfare — the standard
**An animal is in a good state of welfare if it is healthy, comfortable, well nourished, safe, able to express innate behaviour and free of pain, fear and distress** — requiring **disease prevention and veterinary treatment, appropriate shelter, management, nutrition and humane handling and slaughter**. Welfare is the test every house in this unit must pass.
