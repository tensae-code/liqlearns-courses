# Lesson plan — The dairy business — where the profit is

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Livestock · **Lesson:** The dairy business — where the profit is
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Livestock title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** why dairying pays in Ethiopia — profitable margins and rising demand — and how profit is protected: year-round on-farm forage against the 60-70% feed cost, plus hygienic production and marketing
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — A market that is growing: **Milk production, processing and marketing have profitable margins in Ethiopia** — the business pays today.
- Sub lesson 2 — Protecting the profit — the forage lever: Profit is **protected by management**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the whole dairy unit, breeds to diseases. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why the outlook is good: milk production, processing and marketing have profitable margins in Ethiopia, and demand will rise with the economy and the urban middle class.
2. Name what rising demand creates: investment and employment opportunities.
3. State the biggest profit lever: since feed is 60-70% of cost, year-round on-farm forage production, alongside hygienic production and marketing.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks profit comes from price alone → the biggest lever is cost-side: on-farm forage against the 60-70% feed share.
- Forgets hygiene's money role → hygienic production protects the milk's price and market.
