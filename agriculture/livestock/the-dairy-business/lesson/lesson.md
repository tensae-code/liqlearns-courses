# The dairy business — where the profit is

## Do it first
Two dairy farmers sell the same milk at the same price. One ends the year with money; one with debts. Same cows, same market — what did the profitable one grow in his own fields? Guess the lever.

## Sub lesson 1 — A market that is growing
**Milk production, processing and marketing have profitable margins in Ethiopia** — the business pays today. And the road ahead widens it: **demand will rise with the economy and the urban middle class**.

Rising demand creates **investment and employment opportunities** — new farms, processing, transport and selling work. The dairy enterprise is not only a farm skill; it is a growing trade.

## Sub lesson 2 — Protecting the profit — the forage lever
Profit is **protected by management**. The arithmetic from the feeding lesson rules: since **feed is 60-70% of cost**, the biggest lever is **year-round on-farm forage production — good grasses and legumes grown on the farm** — shrinking the bought-feed bill in every season.

Beside it stand **hygienic production and marketing**: clean milk keeps its price, and well-marketed milk finds the rising demand. Grow the feed, guard the hygiene, work the market — that is where the profit is.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion the two halves: why the market rewards dairying, and the three profit protectors — on-farm forage, hygiene, marketing.
