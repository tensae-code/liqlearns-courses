# Keeping disease out — hygiene and biosecurity

## Do it first
A farm is a small country. Disease crosses its border in boots, buckets, buyers and new animals. Design the border: what gets washed, who gets stopped, where the sick go? Draft your defences before the three legs are named.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hygiene and management
Control rests on **proper hygiene and sanitation — washing feed and water troughs, separating sick from healthy animals, burning or burying dead animals** — and **good management: feeding, vaccination, treatment and clean housing**. Daily cleanliness does quietly what medicine does loudly.

[Picture 26026 — a farm gate with a footbath and protective clothing, sick animals penned apart]

## Sub lesson 2 — Biosecurity — guarding the gate
**Biosecurity measures include controlling entry to the farm and using protective clothing.** Every visitor, vehicle and new animal is a possible road for pathogens (the spread routes of the sickness lesson); the gate is where those roads are closed. Suspect disease still goes to the veterinarian — biosecurity prevents, it does not replace treatment.

Two words to keep apart — the teacher guide states the pair cleanly: **prevention is measures applied before a disease occurs, to stop it occurring; control is measures applied after a disease has occurred, to stop it spreading from infected to healthy animals.** Everything in this lesson is prevention; treatment and separation after an outbreak are control.

## Say it to your companion
