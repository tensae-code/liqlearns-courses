# Lesson plan — Breed introduction — gains and risks

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Livestock · **Lesson:** Breed introduction — gains and risks
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Livestock title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** breed introduction: outside blood can raise productivity quickly and crossbreds may outperform locals if they adapt — against new diseases, poor adaptation and never reaching full productivity
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — The gain — quick lift: **Introducing a breed from elsewhere can raise productivity quickly**, and **crossbreds may outperform local breeds if they adapt**.
- Sub lesson 2 — The risks — disease and adaptation: The disadvantages are plain: **introduced animals can bring new diseases and pests**, **exotic breeds may struggle to adapt**, and **they may never reach full productivity in the new environment**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Genes, heredity and why breeding matters. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the gain: productivity can rise quickly; crossbreds may outperform local breeds if they adapt.
2. Name the risks: new diseases and pests, adaptation struggle.
3. State the third risk: introduced animals may never reach full productivity in the new environment.
