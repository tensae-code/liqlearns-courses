# Choosing a dairy cow

## Do it first
At market, two cows cost the same. One will milk in two months, one in a year. One eats twice as much. The price tag tells you none of this. What do you ask before money is even mentioned?

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing the breed
Choice of breed weighs four things first: the **production objective** (what milk, for what market), **adaptability** (will this breed thrive here?), the **feeding system** (can you feed what it demands?) and **maturity** — when production starts.

Maturity has numbers: **small breeds such as Jersey and Guernsey come into production soon after 24 months; large breeds not until 28-30 months; local breeds later still**. A farmer wanting milk early chooses accordingly. **Price is the last consideration**, within reasonable limits — a cheap cow that milks late and eats dear is no bargain.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the individual
Breed chosen, the second level begins: **choice of the individual animal** — judging **the particular cow against the breed's promise**. Not every Holstein is a good Holstein; not every local cow is a good local cow.

The buyer judges the one animal in front of them — her body, udder, legs and health — because it is the individual, not the breed average, that will stand in the byre and fill the can.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion the four breed-choice factors, the three maturity figures, and why price comes last. Then say what individual choice adds.
