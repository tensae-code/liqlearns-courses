# From udder to market — milk hygiene and processing

## Do it first
Milk leaves the udder warm. By market time it can be sweet — or spoiled and dangerous. What two defences decide which? Guess: one is a temperature, one is a habit.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cold and clean — the two defences
Milk must be kept **cold — 4 °C or below** — by **mechanical refrigeration or cooling tanks**, from collection onward. Warm milk is an invitation to spoilage; the cold chain is the first defence.

The second defence is **hygiene guarded at every stage** of collection and processing. Milk travels in **cans or bulk tanks** — every surface it touches is either protected by cleanliness or is a contamination route.

## Sub lesson 2 — Processing — turning milk into more
**Processing converts raw milk into higher-value products**: **pasteurised liquid milk, cream, yoghurt, cheese, butter and ghee**.

Each product is milk made more durable or more valuable: pasteurising makes liquid milk safer; cream, butter and ghee concentrate its fat; yoghurt and cheese preserve it by fermentation and curdling. The next lesson makes two of them by hand.
