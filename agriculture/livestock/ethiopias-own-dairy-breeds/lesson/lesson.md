# Ethiopia's own dairy breeds

## Do it first
Look at the cattle nearest your home. Big, small, humped, many colours — yet they thrive on local feed and weather that would defeat a pampered foreign cow. What is their secret? Guess before the lesson answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Local zebu breeds, many and varied
Ethiopia has **many local cattle breeds**, varying in **size and colour**. All are **indigenous zebu types — *Bos indicus*** — the humped cattle of hot countries, shaped over centuries by Ethiopia's own highlands, lowlands, rains and dry seasons.

The book's tables name representative breeds; Ethiopian breeds stand alongside regional zebu breeds such as **Sahiwal, Gir and Kenana**. The point is not the list — it is the type: local cattle are **adapted to local conditions and harsh environments**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Slower to mature, harder to kill
Local breeds are generally **slower to reach maturity** than exotics — a local cow starts her producing life later. That is the honest cost.

The honest gain is the other side: local breeds **survive and produce where exotics struggle** — on rough feed, in heat, through disease pressure and dry seasons. A breed is not 'better' in the abstract; it is better **for a place and a feeding system**. That judgement is the next lessons' work.
