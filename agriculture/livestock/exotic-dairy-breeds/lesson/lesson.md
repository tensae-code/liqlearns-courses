# Exotic dairy breeds — the Holstein and others

## Do it first
Two cows: one huge black-and-white, one small golden-brown. One gives a river of milk and eats like a horse. One gives less, but richer. Which would you buy — before you know what each costs to feed?

## Sub lesson 1 — The Holstein Friesian — the outstanding producer
The **Holstein Friesian** comes from the **Netherlands**. It is **black-and-white or red-and-white**, **large and angular** — built like a milk factory. It **needs more feed than other breeds**, and in return it is the **outstanding producer: over 6,600 litres per lactation**.

The bargain is plain: the Holstein converts generous feeding into unmatched milk. Where feed is scarce, the same appetite becomes its weakness — which is why breed choice always asks about the feeding system first.

## Sub lesson 2 — The other exotics, and rich milk
The book profiles six more: **Jersey, Brown Swiss, Guernsey, Ayrshire, Milking Shorthorn and Red Danish** — each with its own size, colour and yield in the book's comparison figures.

Volume is not the whole story: **milk fat and protein** differ by breed. **Jersey-type milk is richer** — around **3.6% fat and 3.2% protein** in the figures quoted — so a smaller yield of rich milk can beat a larger yield of thin milk where butter and cheese are the goal.
