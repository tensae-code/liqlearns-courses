# Crop residues and forage crops

## Do it first
After harvest a field is full of stalks and straw. Rubbish to burn, or a feed store for the dry months? And what if a farmer planted a field ONLY to feed animals? Decide what each pile is worth before the lesson sorts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Crop residues — the harvest's second gift
**Crop residues — straws and stovers left after harvest — are the second great feed resource**, supplementing pasture **especially in the dry season**, when grass has stopped growing but stored stalks remain. They are bulky and plain, yet they carry herds through the gap months.

## Sub lesson 2 — Forage crops — grown to be eaten
**Forage crops are grown deliberately for their edible vegetative parts**, fed **fresh or preserved**. **Grasses** (Rhodes grass, Sudan grass and others) give the **cheapest bulk** for ruminants and **more yield per area**; **legume forages** give **less bulk but more protein and less fibre**. A wise forage plot mixes the two strengths.
