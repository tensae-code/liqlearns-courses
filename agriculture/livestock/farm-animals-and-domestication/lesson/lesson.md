# Farm animals — what domestication made

## Do it first
Six numbers describe Ethiopia's animal wealth: cattle, sheep, goats, camels, chickens, beehives — tens of millions of each. Before you see them, rank the six from most to fewest. The ranking will surprise you twice.

## Sub lesson 1 — Domestication — animals join the farm
**Farm animals are raised for food or work.** **Domestication** — **selectively breeding wild animals to live alongside humans** — followed settled farming, probably **between 8000 and 2500 BC**.

The pattern mirrors the crops: people chose the animals that tolerated people, bred from them, and over generations made herds and flocks that belong with human life — giving food and work in return for feed, shelter and protection.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's herds in numbers
Ethiopia's estimated populations (**CSA 2021**) anchor the unit: about **70.3 million cattle (97.4% local breeds)**, **42.9 million sheep**, **52.5 million goats**, **8.1 million camels**, **57.0 million chickens** and about **7.0 million beehives**.

Read the character inside the numbers: this is an **overwhelmingly indigenous herd** — local breeds, fitted by centuries to local land and disease, are nearly the whole of it. Every livestock lesson after this one is about these animals.
