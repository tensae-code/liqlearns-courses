# Kinds of bee colony

## Do it first
Open two hives: one boils with bees across every frame; the other whispers on two. Same apiary, same day. A beekeeper must name what they see before touching anything.

## Sub lesson 1 — Colony types described
The **types of honey bee colony** are described — the colony forms and conditions a beekeeper must **recognise before managing them**. A colony is not just 'bees': it has a state, and the state is the diagnosis.

[Picture 26659 — a strong colony and a weak colony opened side by side]

## Sub lesson 2 — Recognition before management
Strong and weak, queenright and failing — colony kinds are read from bee numbers, brood, stores and behaviour. The lesson's skill is the eye: name the colony's kind correctly, and the management of the next lessons follows from the name.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, using one real example from your own area for each sub lesson. Your companion asks one question about each part — answer from the lesson, not from memory of other lessons.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write colony-kind cards: strong, weak, and the signs of each.
2. From pictures, judge five colonies and defend each verdict.
3. Ask a beekeeper how they judge a colony in the first minute of opening.
