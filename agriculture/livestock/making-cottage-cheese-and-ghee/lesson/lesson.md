# Making cottage cheese and ghee

## Do it first
Two transformations, no factory needed: sour milk becomes a white cheese you can slice; butter becomes a golden oil that keeps for months without a fridge. What does each change remove — water, whey, or both? Hold your answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Making cottage cheese
The workshop starts with **protective clothing first** — the hygiene rule worn on the body. The apparatus: **whole fresh or sour milk and skim milk, a muslin cheese sieve, a weighing balance, salt, pails, and a moulder with presser**.

No rennet? The book's stand-ins coagulate the milk instead: **lemon juice, or lactic or acetic acid, or acid whey** can stand in **for rennet or starter culture**. The curd is then **pressed in the muslin** and shaped in the moulder — cottage cheese made with what a home or school actually has.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making ghee — butter clarified
**Ghee** begins as butter and one careful heating: butter is **heated to 110-120 °C until the water evaporates**. What remains is **almost entirely butterfat** — clear, golden, and keeping: ghee **keeps without refrigeration and stores more easily than butter**.

Safety holds the lesson: a pan at 110-120 °C is **adult work** — hot fat burns badly, the pan is never left, and water never meets the hot fat. Butter and the unit's other products follow the same practical pattern: simple apparatus, exact temperatures, clean hands.

## Say it to your companion
Talk your companion through both practicals: cheese apparatus and acid stand-ins, then ghee's temperature and why ghee keeps. Include the safety rule unprompted.
