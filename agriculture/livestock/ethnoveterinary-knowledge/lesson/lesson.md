# Ethnoveterinary knowledge — learning from local farmers

## Do it first
Before the vet clinic existed, grandmothers treated cattle with leaves, roots and bark — and herds survived. That knowledge still walks your village, untested by laboratories but tested by generations. How do you record it before it is lost? Design the questions.

## Sub lesson 1 — Knowledge from elders
**In Ethiopia, plant-based remedies are the most important, sometimes the only, way many farmers manage livestock disease — an inexpensive alternative to science-based treatment, with knowledge passed down from elders.** Ethnoveterinary knowledge is indigenous knowledge applied to animal health — the animal-side twin of this course's crop-side indigenous lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — The field interview
The unit sends pupils to **interview local farmers: which plants and parts are used, how remedies are prepared, how the dose is decided and how they are given.** Record exactly, credit the knowledge-holder, and treat remedies as knowledge to document — a sick animal still needs proven care, and an unrecorded remedy dies with its elder.
