# Lesson plan — Four kinds of animal housing

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Livestock · **Lesson:** Four kinds of animal housing
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Livestock title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four housing types: conventional barns (incl. deep-litter), loose housing, free-range, and modern farms — how each houses, feeds and exposes its animals
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Conventional and loose housing: **Conventional barns/houses include deep-litter poultry and pig housing, where straw or sawdust bedding is spread and fresh layers added as it soils.
- Sub lesson 2 — Free-range and modern farms: The **free-range system: stock on large natural or cultivated pasture with watering points and shelters, headquarters at the centre, an attendant moving with the animals — cheap but exposed to predators and low in productivity**, with **sheep and goats often ranged by day and barned at night**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Why we house animals — comfort and welfare. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four housing types.
2. Describe deep-litter: bedding spread, fresh layers added as it soils.
3. Contrast free-range (cheap, predator-exposed, low productivity) with modern farms (separate pens, prepared feed).
