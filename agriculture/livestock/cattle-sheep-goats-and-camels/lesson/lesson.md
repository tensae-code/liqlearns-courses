# Cattle, sheep, goats and camels

## Do it first
Four grazers, four mouths. One crop of grass feeds the first; the fourth eats thorn bushes that would tear your hands. Line the four up — cattle, sheep, goats, camels — and guess which is the browser, which the grazer, and which owns the toughest lips.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cattle and sheep
**Cattle** belong to the family **Bovidae**, genus ***Bos*** — over **1,000 breeds worldwide**. They give **meat, milk, draft power, hides for leather and manure**, and they are **ruminant herbivores feeding mainly on pasture**.

**Sheep** stand next in the book's order — the wool-and-meat grazer of the flock, sharing the pasture life and the ruminant stomach the digestion lesson will open up.

## Sub lesson 2 — Goats and camels — browsers of the dry land
**Goats** — over **300 breeds** — give **milk, meat and skins**; the **Angora** gives **mohair**. Goats are **browsers**: they feed on **shoots and shrubs**, reaching up where grazers cannot.

**Camels** complete the four: their **thick, tough lips** let them feed on **thorny bushes in dry country** — a mouth built for the desert's menu. Grazer, grazer, browser, thorn-eater: the four mouths divide the dry land's food between them.

## Say it to your companion
Introduce the four species to your companion as four mouths: what each eats and what each gives. End with the camel's lips and the goat's browsing.
