# Camel milk and meat — a slow herd's products

## Do it first
A cow herd can grow fast; a camel herd cannot. If you take too many camels for meat today, whose milk is missing in five years? Hold that question.

## Sub lesson 1 — Markets, and the hard limit
Camel **meat markets and consumption are not well developed in Eastern Africa outside Sudan**, though **Egypt and Libya offer encouraging export opportunities**. The limiting biology is plain: **camels have a low reproductive rate, making them inefficient meat producers, and an offtake of just 3–5% might already stress the camel population**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Milk — the product that pays year-round
Against slow meat stands **camel milk production and processing** — the pastoral product that **does pay year-round**. The wise camel economy milks the herd daily and slaughters rarely: the living herd is the capital, milk is its interest, and meat offtake stays inside the 3–5% the population can bear.
