# Lesson plan — What holds livestock back — and the answers

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Livestock · **Lesson:** What holds livestock back — and the answers
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Livestock title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the constraints on animal production and their mitigation — feed shortage first (pasture and residues over 90% of feed at only 3–6% crude protein, with dry-season gaps), disease and parasites, the traditional system, and weak infrastructure and extension
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — The major constraint — feed shortage: **Feed shortage is the major constraint.
- Sub lesson 2 — Disease, tradition and weak services — and their answers: **Disease and parasites** are answered by **feeding, vaccination, isolating and treating sick animals, and clean housing management** — the constraint list carries its mitigations with it.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What farm animals give us. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name feed shortage as the major constraint, with its figures: over 90% of feed from pasture and residues, at 3–6% crude protein, with severe dry-season gaps.
2. Pair disease and parasites with their answers: feeding, vaccination, isolating and treating sick animals, clean housing.
3. Name the remaining constraints: the traditional production system itself, poor infrastructure and weak extension services.
