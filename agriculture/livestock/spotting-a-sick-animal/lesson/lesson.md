# Spotting a sick animal; how disease spreads

## Do it first
One cow lags, head low, muzzle dry, coat standing up. Yesterday she was first to the trough. How many alarms can you count on her body — and how could her sickness reach the rest? Count first, learn names after.

## Sub lesson 1 — Health, disease and the roads between
**Health is an animal's state of wellbeing; disease is any departure from it, and the best returns come when disease is minimised.** **Pathogens spread by routes including direct contact, ingestion, inhalation, the urogenital tract, the placenta and vectors.** Every route is a road the keeper can block.

## Sub lesson 2 — The sick animal's signs
A sick animal shows signs such as **loss of appetite, changes at the body openings, sunken eyes, raised hair coat, rough skin, dry muzzle, swollen joints and abnormal temperature, pulse and respiration** — and **disease may end in death**. Read the signs early: the first day of signs is the cheapest day to act.
