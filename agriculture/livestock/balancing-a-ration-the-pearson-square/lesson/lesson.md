# Balancing a ration — the Pearson Square

## Do it first
You must mix a feed at exactly 16% protein from a low-protein grain and a high-protein meal. Guesswork wastes money both ways. Try a mix on paper — then learn the square that gets it right every time.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rations — maintenance and production
A **ration is the feed given in a day**. A **maintenance ration only keeps the animal as it is**; a **production ration adds what growth, milk or eggs require**. A **good ration is balanced, palatable, cheap and available** — all four, not one.

[Picture 26006 — the Pearson Square diagram with maize and soybean meal entered]

The teacher guide's good-ration checklist adds the working detail behind "good": a good ration **meets the dry-matter requirement by weight**; is **highly digestible, with enough minerals and vitamins**; is **palatable — improved with molasses or salt**; is **fairly bulky, for satiety and for the expulsion of undigested material**; is **properly prepared, for example ground or crushed**; and is **fresh and free of weeds and dust**. (Worked answers in the guide include a Pearson Square solution making 2,000 kg of broiler feed at 23% crude protein, with 929.40 kg of groundnut meal in the stated mix.)

## Sub lesson 2 — The Pearson Square
Formulation methods include **trial and error, substitution, computer-assisted** methods — and the **Pearson Square**, worked step by step: write the target in the centre, the two feeds at the left corners, subtract diagonally, and the differences give the parts of each feed. **To make 100 kg of a 16% crude-protein ration from maize and soybean meal, the square gives 80 kg maize to 20 kg soybean meal.** Check: the parts sum to the batch, and the protein lands on target.
