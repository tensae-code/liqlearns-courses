# Making hay

## Do it first
Fresh grass is mostly water. Leave it wet in a pile and it rots. Spread it right and it keeps for months. What must leave the grass before it can be stored? Feel two handfuls — fresh and cured — and judge.

## Sub lesson 1 — Conservation — banking the grass
**Conservation stores feed against shortage and evens out the seasons**. **Hay is forage cut in the growing period and preserved by drying** — the green surplus of the rains becomes the ration of the dry months.

[Picture 26007 — cut grass drying in the field, then bales and a tripod stack]

## Sub lesson 2 — Curing and storing
**Curing takes moisture from 70–90% down to 15–20%** so hay **keeps without spoiling**. Turn the cut grass as it dries so all of it cures evenly. Storage options include **bales (small squares and larger forms)** and the **tripod system**, which lifts hay off damp ground.
