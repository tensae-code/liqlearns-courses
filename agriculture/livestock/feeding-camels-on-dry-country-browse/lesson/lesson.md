# Feeding camels on dry-country browse

## Do it first
Where grass fails, the camel still eats — thorns, salt, dry shrubs that other animals refuse. Watch a camel feed in your mind and list its menu.

## Sub lesson 1 — Browsing the dry country
Camel feeding is **browsing**: the thorny, salty forage of dry country, taken high and low as the herd moves. The camel's mouth, neck and digestion are built for plants other livestock cannot use — its feed base is the land itself, not a trough.

## Sub lesson 2 — Growth and the long drink
The unit's feeding figures quote a **daily gain of 1.4 kg** in the growth discussion — growth is possible on browse when it is managed. And the camel is managed as an animal **built to go long between drinks**: feeding and watering rhythms follow the pastoral round, not the farmyard clock.

## Do It Yourself
1. List ten dry-country plants near you and mark which a camel could browse.
2. Work out a week's gain at 1.4 kg a day and write the total.
3. Ask a camel keeper how far the herd travels between waters in the dry season.
