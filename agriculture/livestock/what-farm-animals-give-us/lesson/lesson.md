# What farm animals give us

## Do it first
Stand beside one cow and count her gifts. Milk in the morning — one. The plough she pulls — two. Keep counting: the money, the leather, the dung on the field. Most people stop at two. This lesson counts them all.

## Sub lesson 1 — Food, work and livelihood
Animal husbandry gives **food — meat, milk, eggs, honey** — and **work: the draught power of oxen, horses, donkeys and camels**.

Then the scale widens: livestock is **livelihood and economy**. It supports about **65% of Ethiopia's population**, contributed **45–48% of agricultural GDP since 2018** and **16–19% of export earnings**. The herd is not a side activity; for most of the country it is the economy itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Materials, residues and manure
Animals give **raw materials**: **skins and wool for cottage industry, bones and hooves for adhesives, fats for candles and soap, organ extracts for pharmaceuticals**.

Even the leftovers work: **slaughter residues are recycled as livestock feed**, and **manure feeds the farm ecosystem** — the dung returns fertility to the fields that fed the animals. Nothing in the animal is a single gift; the wise farmer counts the whole animal.

## Say it to your companion
Count the gifts for your companion on one animal: food, work, income, materials, manure — with the three national percentages for the income gift.
