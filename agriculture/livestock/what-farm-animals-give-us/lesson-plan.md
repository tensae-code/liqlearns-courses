# Lesson plan — What farm animals give us

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Livestock · **Lesson:** What farm animals give us
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Livestock title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the importance of animal production — food, draught work, livelihood (about 65% of Ethiopia's population; 45–48% of agricultural GDP; 16–19% of export earnings), raw materials, recycled residues and manure
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Food, work and livelihood: Animal husbandry gives **food — meat, milk, eggs, honey** — and **work: the draught power of oxen, horses, donkeys and camels**.
- Sub lesson 2 — Materials, residues and manure: Animals give **raw materials**: **skins and wool for cottage industry, bones and hooves for adhesives, fats for candles and soap, organ extracts for pharmaceuticals**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Farm animals — what domestication made. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List what animal husbandry gives: food, work, livelihood and economy, raw materials, recycled residues and manure.
2. State the livelihood figures: about 65% of the population supported, 45–48% of agricultural GDP, 16–19% of export earnings.
3. Give raw-material examples: skins and wool, bones and hooves for adhesives, fats for candles and soap, organ extracts for pharmaceuticals.
