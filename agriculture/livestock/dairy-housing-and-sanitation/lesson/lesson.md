# Dairy housing and farm sanitation

## Do it first
A spotless cow in a filthy byre still gives risky milk. Walk a byre in your mind at dawn: floor, bedding, walls, pails, hands. Which five dirt-points would you attack first?

## Sub lesson 1 — Two houses for the dairy herd
Dairy cattle house successfully under conditions **from close confinement to near-freedom — except at milking**, when the herd comes in to be milked. Freedom between milkings is not neglect; it is one proven end of the housing range.

The **two common houses** are **loose housing** and the **conventional barn** — the same pair met in the housing unit. The choice follows the farm: land, labour and climate pick the house; the milk does not care, as long as sanitation does its work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sanitation is absolute
Sanitation is **absolute**: **regular cleaning of floors, bedding, walls and feeding surfaces** — the byre itself is washed as routine, not as rescue.

And every **contamination route is guarded**: **equipment, milk-contact surfaces, handlers, storage and packaging**. Guarding them **prevents the infections that debilitate calves and cows** — sanitation is disease control done daily, before any medicine is needed.
