## Sub lesson 1 — 305 days of milk, two months dry
A **normal lactation lasts about 10 months — 305 days** — followed by about **two dry months**, the **drying off** period when milking stops and the cow rests and prepares for the next calf.

The **first milk after calving is colostrum** — a special, rich milk made **for the calf**, loaded with what the newborn needs in its first days. The farmer's plan respects the order: colostrum to the calf first, then the saleable lactation.

## Sub lesson 2 — The lactation curve
**Yield rises in the first months after calving**, reaches its height, then **declines steadily through late lactation** — the shape called the **lactation curve**.

The farmer **plans feeding and breeding around the curve**: feed follows the rise, breeding is timed so the next calving renews the curve, and drying off closes the cycle. Milk is also the farm's nutrient gift — carrying **calcium, iodine, potassium and phosphorus** among its nutrients.
