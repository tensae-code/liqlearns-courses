# Dairy cattle diseases — the control table

## Do it first
A cow's face is swollen, her nose runs, she struggles to breathe. Is the danger only in her — or also in her feed trough and bedding? The control table answers in three columns. Guess what they are.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading the control table
The unit closes its health work with a **summary table of the dairy herd's diseases**, each entry giving **symptoms, transmission, and prevention/control**. The table is a tool: see the signs, name the road it travels, cut the road.

Work the pattern on **chronic respiratory disease**: cause **viral**; signs — **swollen face, nasal discharge, difficulty breathing**; spread — **through contaminated feed, water and bedding**; answer — **vaccination, sanitation and ventilation**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Named entries — TB and mastitis
Among the table's entries stands **bovine tuberculosis** — a herd disease the dairy farmer must know by name and control with the herd's health programme.

And carried through the unit is **mastitis, the udder infection** — the **dairy-specific threat**: it attacks the very organ the enterprise sells from, so udder health, milking hygiene and early spotting guard the farm's income directly.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion the table's three columns, work chronic respiratory disease through them, and finish with why mastitis is the dairy-specific threat.

## Do It Yourself
1. Build a blank control table (disease, symptoms, transmission, prevention/control) and fill the chronic respiratory disease row from memory.
2. Write why contaminated bedding is a disease road, in two lines.
3. Explain mastitis's special danger to a dairy farm in one sentence.
