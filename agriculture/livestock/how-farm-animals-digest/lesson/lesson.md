# How farm animals digest — ruminant vs monogastric

## Do it first
A cow eats grass — tough, fibrous stuff no single stomach could master. Her answer is not one stomach but four, in a row. A chicken swallows grain whole and grinds it later in a muscular mill. Guess the order of the cow's four chambers before they are named.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ruminant plan — four chambers
Digestion starts at the **mouth — tongue and teeth gather and break feed** — and the **oesophagus** carries feed to the stomach. **Ruminants (cattle, sheep, goats)** have a **four-chambered stomach: rumen (first and largest), reticulum, omasum, abomasum**.

Feed travels chamber to chamber, worked and reworked — the system that lets ruminants live on pasture and roughage. Beside them stand the **pseudo-ruminants (camels) with three chambers** — a ruminant-like plan in three parts, as the book teaches it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Monogastrics — and the chicken's crop and gizzard
**Monogastrics** have a **single stomach** and rely on **easily digested feed** — one chamber cannot do the rumen's slow work, so the feed must arrive ready. The horse's system and the chicken's are traced separately in the book.

The **chicken** stores feed in its **crop for up to 12 hours** before it trickles into the stomach and the **gizzard**, where grinding happens. No teeth, no problem: the crop is the pantry, the gizzard is the mill.
