# Hatching eggs — the incubator and its hygiene

## Do it first
An egg in an incubator needs what a hen gives without thinking: warmth, moist air, fresh air — and to be turned. Take away any one and the chick dies in the shell.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the incubator must supply
The incubator must supply **heat**, **humidity (water to moisten the air)** and **ventilation (fresh air in, stale air out)** — the hen's body, rebuilt as a machine. Eggs are also **turned**, as the hen turns them, so the embryo develops rightly. Miss one supply and the hatch fails.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hatchery sanitation and egg care
**Hatchery sanitation breaks the disease cycle**: cleaning and disinfection **after each hatch and before the next cycle**. And hatching eggs are **collected and stored under the section's rules** so they stay fertile and clean — the hatch begins at collection, not at the incubator door.
