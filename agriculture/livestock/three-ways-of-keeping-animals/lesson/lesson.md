# Three ways of keeping animals — extensive, semi-intensive, intensive

## Do it first
The same ten cattle in three lives: roaming a huge dry range; grazing by day and yarded by night; standing in a clean barn, fed by hand three times a day. Which life grows them slowest? Which gives most milk? Which costs most to run? Decide before the systems are named.

## Sub lesson 1 — Extensive and semi-intensive — the grazing ways
Systems are classified by **how much land, labour and bought feed they use**. **Extensive** keeping grazes animals over **large areas of low productivity**: **pastoralism**; **ranching**, its modern, fenced, profit-oriented alternative; and **mixed crop–livestock farming** in the same family. Extensive is **low input, slow growth, late maturity — but cheap**.

**Semi-intensive** is the **middle way: part grazing, part housed feeding** — some of the range's cheapness, some of the barn's control.

## Sub lesson 2 — Intensive — housed, fed, highest rate
**Intensive** keeping houses the animals and feeds them **grown forage by cut-and-carry plus industrial by-products and balanced rations supplemented with protein, minerals and vitamins**.

Its named forms: **pig, broiler, feedlot and commercial dairy production**. The cost is the highest — land, labour and bought feed all pour in — and so is the return: **meat, milk and eggs at the highest rate**. The three systems are one ladder of input against output; the farmer climbs as far as feed, money and market allow.

## Say it to your companion
Climb the ladder for your companion: extensive, semi-intensive, intensive — inputs rising, output rising — and place pastoralism, ranching, feedlot and commercial dairy on the rungs.
