# Lesson plan — Growing maize

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Crops · **Lesson:** Growing maize
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Crops title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** growing maize — ploughing two or three times, row-planting at 75 × 25 cm and 5–7 cm deep at the start of the rains, fertilising, weeding and harvesting Ethiopia's most productive cereal
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Planting maize — rows, spacing, depth: Maize management follows the standard crop pattern.
- Sub lesson 2 — Feeding, weeding, harvesting maize: **Fertilise**: farmers commonly apply about **41 kg nitrogen and 20 kg phosphorus per hectare** — though **rates should follow the soil's level**, not a fixed habit.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Growing teff. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give maize spacing exactly: 25 cm between seeds, 75 cm between rows, 5–7 cm deep, planted March–April at the start of the rains.
2. State the common fertiliser practice — about 41 kg nitrogen and 20 kg phosphorus per hectare — and that rates should follow the soil's level.
3. Say maize's nutritional place and that it is Ethiopia's most productive cereal per hectare.

## What you need
A measuring string or tape for the 75 × 25 cm spacing; the lesson picture.

## If the learner is stuck
- Swaps the spacing → rows are the wide gap (75 cm), seeds the close gap (25 cm).
- Plants too deep → 5–7 cm; deeper buries the shoot's strength.
