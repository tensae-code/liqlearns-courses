# Growing maize

## Do it first
Stretch a string 75 cm — that is the gap between maize rows. Now 25 cm — the gap between seeds in the row. A seed buried 5 cm wakes; buried 20 cm may never rise. Guess the right depth before the lesson gives it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Planting maize — rows, spacing, depth
Maize management follows the standard crop pattern. **Plough two or three times.** Then **row-plant at 25 cm between seeds and 75 cm between rows, 5–7 cm deep**, at the **start of the rains (March–April)**.

The numbers are the lesson: rows wide enough to walk and weed (75 cm), seeds close enough to fill the row (25 cm), depth shallow enough for the shoot to reach light (5–7 cm). Every figure comes from the field's needs, not from habit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Feeding, weeding, harvesting maize
**Fertilise**: farmers commonly apply about **41 kg nitrogen and 20 kg phosphorus per hectare** — though **rates should follow the soil's level**, not a fixed habit. Then **weed and manage pests** through the season, and **harvest at maturity**.

Maize feeds the effort back: it is an important source of **carbohydrates, protein and fat**, and it is **Ethiopia's most productive cereal per hectare** — the high-yield cereal of this unit.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion maize's five numbers from memory: ploughings, row gap, seed gap, depth, planting months — then the fertiliser pair, 41 and 20.
