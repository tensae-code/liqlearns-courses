# What a vegetable crop feels — temperature, light, humidity, soil

## Do it first
Stand outside in the morning and again at midday. Feel how temperature and light changed. A vegetable plant feels those changes all day, every day.

## Sub lesson 1 — Temperature and light — the switches
Some crops need a cold spell — **vernalisation** — before they switch from vegetative growth to reproductive growth and flower; this is true of biennials and some cool-season crops. Light matters two ways: **photoperiod**, the day length — flowering and seed production answer to the length of the night — and light quality, which varies by season.

## Sub lesson 2 — Humidity, soil and water
Crops differ in the humidity they need: cabbage and lettuce need high humidity, while peppers, carrots, onion, watermelon and sweet potato need low humidity. In the soil, waterlogging suffocates roots, and seedlings in compacted soil emerge slowly — and slow emergence leaves them longer vulnerable to disease, insects and weeds. The soil is also the plant's nutrient provider.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort six vegetables you know into high-humidity and low-humidity lists using the lesson's examples.
2. Watch one plant from morning to evening and note what changes around it: light, heat, shade.
3. Press a handful of garden soil: if it stays in a hard lump, say why seedlings might emerge slowly from it.
