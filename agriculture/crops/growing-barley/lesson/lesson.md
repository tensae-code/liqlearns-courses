# Growing barley

## Do it first
One field of barley, two buyers: a food miller, and a maltster making malt for drinks. The farmer pours on nitrogen for a huge yield — and one buyer walks away. Which one, and why would feeding the crop spoil it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Barley — wheat's companion crop
Barley shares **most of wheat's weeds, diseases and controls** — the same broad-leaf weed fight, the same fungal defences: **resistant varieties, rotation, and planting-time shifts**.

Its own insect enemies are named: **armyworm, grasshopper and barley shoot fly**. Barley **matures in three to four months**, judged — like wheat — by **drying and yellowing**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Food and malt — the nitrogen warning
Barley serves two tables: **food** and **malt**. That double service carries a barley-specific warning: **heavy nitrogen application lowers malt quality**.

So barley feeding is a balance: enough nitrogen for the crop to yield, not so much that the malting use is spoiled. The end use decides the feeding — a lesson that returns in every crop: grow for the buyer you actually have.
