# Lesson plan — Industrial crops — and growing sesame

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Crops · **Lesson:** Industrial crops — and growing sesame
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Crops title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what industrial crops are — oil, fibre and sugar crops grown as raw material for industry — and growing sesame, harvested when about a third of leaves, stems and pods have yellowed, before the pods shatter
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Industrial crops — grown for industry: **Industrial crops** are grown **mainly as raw material for industry**.
- Sub lesson 2 — Sesame — harvest at one-third yellow: **Sesame**, believed **African in origin**, is grown for its **oil**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Sorting crops — grains, oils, fibres, forage. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define industrial crops and name their three groups: oil, fibre and sugar crops.
2. Place sesame: believed African in origin, grown for oil, maturing in up to five months.
3. Give sesame's harvest signal — about a third of leaves, stems and pods yellowed — and why delay loses seed to shattering.

## If the learner is stuck
- Waits for full yellow → at about a third yellowed, harvest; delay shatters pods and loses seed.
- Calls sesame a grain crop for food first → it is an industrial oil crop, grown mainly as industry raw material.
