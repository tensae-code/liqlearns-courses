# Industrial crops — and growing sesame

## Do it first
A sesame pod is a small box that springs open when ripe and throws its seed away. Harvest early: seed unripe. Harvest late: seed on the ground. The farmer needs a signal between. What fraction of yellow might it be?

## Sub lesson 1 — Industrial crops — grown for industry
**Industrial crops** are grown **mainly as raw material for industry**. They split into three groups: **oil crops, fibre crops and sugar crops**. This unit walks them in that order — sesame and noug (oil), cotton and sisal (fibre), sugarcane (sugar).

The sorting from the earlier lesson returns with a purpose: these crops leave the farm gate for a factory — an oil press, a gin, a mill — and their farming is timed by what the factory needs and what the plant will bear.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sesame — harvest at one-third yellow
**Sesame**, believed **African in origin**, is grown for its **oil**. It **matures in up to five months** and must be harvested **when about a third of leaves, stems and pods have yellowed**.

The timing is the lesson: **delay lets the pods shatter and seed is lost**. Sesame's average national yield is about **0.74 t/ha** — and part of what is lost nationally is lost in those last waiting days, seed scattered before the harvester arrives.
