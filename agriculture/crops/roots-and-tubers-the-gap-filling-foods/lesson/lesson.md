# Roots and tubers — the gap-filling foods

## Do it first
Picture the weeks before the main harvest, when last year's grain runs low. What is still in the ground, ready to dig? That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Food that fills the gap
Roots and tubers provide **vitamins, carbohydrates and other nutrients** — and their special role is timing: they **fill food gaps that exist until other harvests come in**. A crop that can be dug when the store is empty is food security you can hold in your hand.

[Picture 26613 — a household calendar with the hungry gap bridged by sweet potato and cassava]

## Sub lesson 2 — Prospects against problems
Ethiopia's prospects for expanding these crops are real — but they stand against the **problems of their production**. The unit weighs both: what could be grown, and what holds growers back, so expansion is planned with open eyes.

## Say it to your companion

1. Mark the hungry-gap weeks on your household's year calendar and write which root crop could fill them.
2. Ask an elder which crop saved the family in a lean year.
3. Write one prospect and one problem for expanding cassava or sweet potato near you.
