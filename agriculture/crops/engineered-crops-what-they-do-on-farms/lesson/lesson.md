# Engineered crops — what they do on farms

## Do it first
The last lesson moved single genes. Now walk onto a farm: what do those moved genes actually change for the crop standing in the field?

## Sub lesson 1 — Engineered traits at work
The applications of genetic engineering in crop production put engineered traits into real crops: a gene moved for a purpose becomes a plant character — the crop **does** something it could not do before, or withstands something that used to destroy it.

[Picture 26625 — an engineered crop standing green in a pest-attacked field]

## Sub lesson 2 — What changes for the farmer
On the farm, engineered traits change **yield, protection and quality**: harvests protected where pests or conditions once took them, and produce of a quality the market rewards. The unit surveys these applications as the working end of biotechnology — the gene's journey finishes in a farmer's field, not in the laboratory.
