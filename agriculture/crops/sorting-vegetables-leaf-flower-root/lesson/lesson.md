# Sorting vegetables — leaf, flower, root; cool season, warm season

## Do it first
Picture a market stall full of vegetables. First sort them in your mind by which part you eat. Then sort the same pile again by the season they grow in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sorting by the part eaten
Vegetables are classified by the part used. **Leafy vegetables** — cabbage, Swiss chard, celery, lettuce and asparagus among them — are eaten for their leaves and stems. **Flower vegetables** are eaten for their flower parts, and the other part-groups of the classification sort the rest by root, fruit, bulb or seed — the part on the plate names the group.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting by season
By season, **cool-season (temperate) vegetables** are often small and shallow-rooted, and they respond more readily to nitrogen fertiliser. **Warm-season crops** need the warm part of the year. The same vegetable sits in one part-group and one season-group at the same time — the two sortings cross, like rows and columns of one table.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, using one real example from your own area for each sub lesson. Your companion asks one question about each part — answer from the lesson, not from memory of other lessons.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take ten vegetables from a market or kitchen and sort them by part eaten.
2. Sort the same ten by season and write both labels for each.
3. Explain to someone at home why one vegetable can be both 'leafy' and 'cool-season'.
