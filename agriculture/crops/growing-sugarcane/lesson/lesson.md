# Growing sugarcane for the factories

## Do it first
Every crop so far began from seed. This one begins from a piece of stem, chopped from the top of a young plant. Why would a farmer plant stem, not seed — and how long should each piece be? Measure your guess on a real stem.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sugarcane — planted from setts, not seed
**Sugar crops** — **sugarcane and sugar beet** — are raw material for sugar; in Ethiopia **sugarcane dominates**. Sugarcane is **hungry: on poor soils it needs heavy fertilisation**.

It is planted **not from seed but from stem cuttings called 'setts' — about 30 cm long, taken from the top of young, disease- and pest-free plants** — at about **1.6 m between rows**. The sett's health is the crop's health: a diseased sett plants the disease with the crop.

## Sub lesson 2 — The factories sugarcane feeds
The industry context: **eight state-owned sugar factories in 2022** — **Wonji-Shoa, Metahara, Finchaa, Kessem, Tendaho, Arjo, Omo Kuraz II and III** — producing about **400,000 tonnes a year**.

That output was **still below national demand in 2023**, so production must **rise toward self-sufficiency and export**. Every sett planted well is a step on that road: field practice and national supply are one story.
