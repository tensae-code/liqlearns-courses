# Cropping systems — mono, mixed, intercrop, relay, rotation

## Do it first
One field, one season. You could plant maize alone. Maize with beans between the rows. Beans first, maize later. Or this year maize, next year beans. Five farmers, five answers — all on the same land. Which is which? Sort the five ideas before their names are given.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sharing land — mono, mixed, intercrop
A **cropping system** is the types, sequences and practices used to grow crops. **Mono-cropping** puts **one crop on a plot** — typical of large commercial farms. **Mixed cropping** grows more than one crop together on the same land. **Intercropping** grows **more than one crop in regular rows at the same time** — classically **maize with a legume**.

Intercropping earns its keep: **better soil cover, less erosion, and slower disease spread**, because the rows shelter the ground and a disease meets a mixed stand, not a single feast. Each system carries its own advantages and management needs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sharing time — relay and rotation
**Relay planting** starts time-sharing: a **second crop is planted after the first has grown alone for a while**, so the two overlap for part of the season. **Crop rotation** shares across seasons: the **crop on a plot changes in sequence** — this year a cereal, next a legume, then a cereal again.

Rotation rests and feeds the soil in turn — a legume year leaves nitrogen, a cereal year spends it — and breaks the build-up of one crop's pests and diseases. Land-sharing (mono, mixed, intercrop) and time-sharing (relay, rotation) together make the five systems.
