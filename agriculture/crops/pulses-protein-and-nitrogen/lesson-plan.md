# Lesson plan — Pulses — protein and nitrogen from one crop

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Crops · **Lesson:** Pulses — protein and nitrogen from one crop
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Crops title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what pulses are — the edible dry seeds of legumes — and their double gift: protein for people and nitrogen fixed through root nodules for the crops that follow
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — What pulses are: **Pulses** are the **edible dry seeds of legumes**.
- Sub lesson 2 — The nitrogen gift — why pulses rotate with cereals: Being **legumes**, many pulses **fix atmospheric nitrogen through root nodules** — those bumps on the roots — turning air's nitrogen into a form plants can use, and **leaving nitrogen for following crops**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Field crops — cereals and pulses; choosing the site. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define pulses as the edible dry seeds of legumes, also eaten as immature pods and used as feed.
2. Say pulses are dicots whose seeds carry two cotyledons.
3. Explain nitrogen fixing through root nodules and why pulses belong in rotations and mixtures with cereals.

## What you need
A legume plant's roots to wash and inspect, or the lesson picture of nodules; a bean seed.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls any seed a pulse → pulses are the dry edible seeds of legumes specifically.
- Thinks nodules are disease → nodules are the nitrogen-fixing organs; they are the plant's gift, not its illness.
