# Pulses — protein and nitrogen from one crop

## Do it first
Pull up a bean plant gently and wash its roots. Among the roots sit small round bumps. They are not eggs, not disease. They are factories. Guess what they make — something cereals hunger for and cannot make themselves.

## Sub lesson 1 — What pulses are
**Pulses** are the **edible dry seeds of legumes**. They are also eaten as **immature pods** and used as **feed**. Being **dicots**, their seeds carry **two cotyledons** — the two fat halves you split in the Sorting Crops lesson.

Faba bean, field pea and haricot bean are this unit's pulses, taken one by one next. First, the family gift that makes all of them special.

## Sub lesson 2 — The nitrogen gift — why pulses rotate with cereals
Being **legumes**, many pulses **fix atmospheric nitrogen through root nodules** — those bumps on the roots — turning air's nitrogen into a form plants can use, and **leaving nitrogen for following crops**.

That is why **pulses belong in rotations and mixtures with cereals**: the cereal spends nitrogen, the pulse restores it. A field that alternates or mixes the two feeds itself part of the way — protein for the family above ground, nitrogen for the soil below.
