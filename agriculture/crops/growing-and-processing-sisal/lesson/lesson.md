# Growing and processing sisal

## Do it first
A sisal leaf looks like a green sword — thick, spiked, full of water. Hidden inside it are long white fibres strong enough for rope. How would you free them? And why would a farmer plant this crop with metres of space between plants?

## Sub lesson 1 — Sisal — the hard-fibre plant
**Sisal**, from **Central America** and widely grown in the tropics, yields **hard fibres** used for **ropes, sacks and carpets**, and also in **paper-making and cloth polishing**.

It is planted **wide-spaced: 2.5 m between rows and 80 cm between plants** — the widest spacing in this course so far, because the plant itself is large and its long leaves need room to spread and to be harvested.

## Sub lesson 2 — From leaf to market — extract, grade, pack
After harvest the leaves are **processed**: the **fibres are extracted** from the leaf, **untangled from the wavy mass**, **graded by length and colour**, and **packed for market as the last step**.

Grading is where the money is sorted: long, clean-coloured fibre earns more than short or stained fibre. The processing line — extract, untangle, grade, pack — turns a spiked leaf into a bale a buyer can trust.
