# Growing haricot bean — and safe storage

## Do it first
The field work ends — and the crop can still be lost in the store. Tiny weevils drill dry beans month after harvest. The defence is a number: a moisture percentage. Guess the number, and how dry 'dry enough' really is.

## Sub lesson 1 — Haricot bean — the travelled bean
Haricot (common) bean **came from Mexico**. It grows at **1,400–2,000 m** in **most soil types**, **alone or mixed with maize and sorghum, in rotation**. Its pods are eaten **green as a vegetable**, or the seed **dry**.

Weed control needs a **clean seedbed and hand weeding within four weeks**. The crop **matures in three to four months**; harvest when **leaves fall and pods yellow and dry** — or green, for vegetable use.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keeping the harvest safe — the 12% rule
Haricot bean's special risk waits **in store: weevils attack the grain**. The control is exact: **dry the grain below 12% moisture**, with **chemical treatment** where used. Grain stored wetter feeds the weevil; grain dried below the line keeps.

Storage is farming's last field. The harvest signal (leaves fallen, pods yellow and dry) gets the crop in; the **12% rule** keeps it yours until it is eaten or sold.
