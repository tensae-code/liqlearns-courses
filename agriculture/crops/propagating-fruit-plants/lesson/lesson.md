# Propagating fruit plants

## Do it first
Hold a dry seed. It looks asleep — because it is. Think about what would have to happen for it to wake up and grow on time, together with all its brothers and sisters.

## Sub lesson 1 — From seed — and beating dormancy
A mature seed germinates when it meets a **favourable environment**. But many seeds carry **dormancy**: they will not wake even in good conditions until it is broken. **Pre-sowing treatments** overcome dormancy to ensure rapid, uniform, timely germination for seedling production — and they are applied **immediately before sowing**, not weeks ahead.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vegetative propagation — cuttings and grafting
Fruit plants are also propagated **vegetatively**: **cuttings** — pieces of stem that root into new plants — and **grafting** — joining a piece of one plant onto another so they grow as one. Vegetative plants carry the mother plant's exact character, which is why the arts matter for fruit quality. The coffee unit later applies the same arts in detail.
