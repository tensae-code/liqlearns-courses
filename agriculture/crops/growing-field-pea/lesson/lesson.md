# Growing field pea

## Do it first
Two highland pulses in two hands: big flat beans in one, smaller round peas in the other. Same mountains, same season — but the plants climb and sprawl differently, and a buyer pays for the right one. Learn the pair by telling them apart.

## Sub lesson 1 — Field pea — the second highland pulse
Field pea is produced for its **edible seed** and taught in the **same crop pattern** as the other field crops: its **place among Ethiopia's highland pulses**, its **growth requirements**, its **field management** and its **harvest**.

It stands beside faba bean as the highland pair: both are legumes, both fix nitrogen, both feed highland families protein — and the farmer must never confuse their seed, their plant or their market.

## Sub lesson 2 — Telling field pea from faba bean
The book pictures the two **side by side so they can be told apart by plant and seed**. Work the comparison: the **plant** — faba bean stands upright and sturdy; field pea is the climbing, sprawling plant. The **seed** — faba's is the large, flat bean; field pea's is the smaller, rounder pea.

Telling the pair apart is the lesson's doing-skill: at sowing, at weeding, at harvest and at market, the right name gets the right handling and the right price.

## Say it to your companion
Hold the two seeds (or picture them) and teach your companion the tell-apart: plant habit and seed shape for faba bean against field pea.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw the two plants and two seeds side by side, labelled, from the lesson picture.
2. Write field pea's crop pattern in four headings: place, requirements, management, harvest.
3. At a market or store, find both seeds and note how sellers keep them apart.
