# Managing vegetables — choose the site, choose the seed

## Do it first
Before planting anything, stand on an empty plot and decide: would you plant vegetables here? List everything you would want to know first.

## Sub lesson 1 — First: the site
Management starts with **site selection**: weigh the site's history (what grew and what sickened there before), the **soil analysis results**, the techniques you will be able to use, and **market information** — sales, demand and prices. A good site is chosen on evidence, not habit: soil tested, market checked, history known.

## Sub lesson 2 — Then: the planting material, and the standing crop
Planting material is chosen for **adaptation to local conditions**, **resistance to pests and diseases**, and **fit with consumer demand** — seed that suits the place, survives its enemies, and gives what buyers want. After planting, the standing crop is managed through the season by the remaining principles of the section: feeding, watering, protecting and tending until harvest.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, using one real example from your own area for each sub lesson. Your companion asks one question about each part — answer from the lesson, not from memory of other lessons.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do a site check on one real plot: write its history, what a soil test would tell you, and who would buy the crop.
2. Choose seed for that plot and defend the choice with the three tests.
3. Write a one-page standing-crop care plan from planting to harvest.
