# Sorting crops — grains, oils, fibres, forage

## Do it first
Split a bean in two — it falls into two fat halves. Try the same with a maize grain — no such halves. The seed itself is telling you its family. Guess what the two halves store, then read how all crops sort.

## Sub lesson 1 — Monocots and dicots — where the food sleeps
Crops divide first by the seed. **Monocots** store their food in the **endosperm** — mostly **starch**. **Dicots**, such as legumes and oilseeds, store food in the **cotyledons** — rich in **protein and oil**.

One caution rides with the dicots: **legume protein lacks some essential amino acids**, so a diet of legumes alone is not complete protein. The seed tells the family: one food-store leaf's worth in the endosperm, or two fat cotyledons you can split apart in your hand.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting by use — grains, oils, fibres, forage
By **use**, crops sort into four working groups. **Grain crops** feed us their seed. **Oil crops** give oils — sources of **essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamins**. **Fibre crops** give fibre: **cotton lint** is the most important, with **flax, jute and hemp** and other fibres from stems and barks. **Forage crops** are grown **to feed animals**.

One plant can serve twice — a grain fed to cattle is forage that day — but the sorting names the crop's main gift: food, oil, fibre, or feed.

## Say it to your companion
Hold up a bean and a maize grain. Explain to your companion where each stores its food, then sort six crops your family knows into grains, oils, fibres and forage.

## Do It Yourself
1. Split three seeds at home and sort them: monocot or dicot? Write the clue for each.
2. Make the four-basket list for your home: one grain, one oil crop, one fibre crop, one forage crop.
3. Write why a legume-only diet misses something, in one sentence.
