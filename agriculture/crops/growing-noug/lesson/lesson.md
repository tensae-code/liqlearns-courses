# Growing noug

## Do it first
One oilseed in this unit was born in Ethiopia itself. Its yellow flowers cover mid-altitude fields, its dark seed presses into the oil in most Ethiopian kitchens. Name it before you read — then check its yield figure against sesame's.

## Sub lesson 1 — Noug — Ethiopia's own oilseed
**Noug originated in Ethiopia** — this oilseed is the country's own. It **prefers mid-altitude areas** and is grown and managed in the **standard oil-crop pattern**: **land preparation, sowing, weeding through the first weeks, and harvest at maturity**.

The pattern should feel familiar — it is the field-crop rhythm of Unit 2 carried into Unit 3: prepare well, sow in season, weed early while the crop is weak, and take the harvest at its signal.

## Sub lesson 2 — Noug's oil, cake and yield
Noug's **oil is mainly for local consumption** — the kitchen oil of the country that bred the crop. Ethiopian oil crops generally also serve **paint and soap**, and the **residue cake** left after pressing is used as **animal feed** — nothing of the seed is wasted.

Noug's average national yield is about **0.8 t/ha** — a little above sesame's 0.74, in the same modest band as Ethiopia's other oilseeds.
