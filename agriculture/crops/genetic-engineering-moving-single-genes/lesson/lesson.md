# Genetic engineering — moving single genes

## Do it first
A breeder wants one trait — say, pest resistance — without dragging a plant's faults along with it. Crossing mixes everything. Is there a way to move just the one thing?

## Sub lesson 1 — The limits of conventional breeding
**Conventional breeding** has hard limits: it has **little or no guarantee of any particular gene combination** from millions of crosses, it **may transfer undesirable genes along with desirable ones**, and it **takes a long time**. Crossing shuffles the whole deck and hopes for the hand wanted.

[Picture 26624 — two paths to a better plant — a field of crosses vs one gene moved by a pipette]

## Sub lesson 2 — Engineering — one or a few genes, directly
**Genetic engineering (modification)** instead **transfers one or just a few genes directly**, between **closely or distantly related organisms**. The difference is precision: breeding shuffles and selects; engineering selects the gene first and moves only it.
