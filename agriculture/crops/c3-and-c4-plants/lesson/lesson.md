# C3 and C4 plants

## Do it first
Two fields in the same hot, dry week: maize stands green and pushing; barley wilts. Same sun, same soil. Something inside the leaf works differently. Guess what number — three or four — might be hiding in the difference.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two ways of fixing carbon
Crop plants are also classed by their **photosynthetic pathway** — how the leaf first fixes carbon dioxide. In **C3 plants**, the **first stable product** of carbon-dioxide fixing has **three carbons**. In **C4 plants**, the first stable product has **four carbons**.

The names are pure counting: C3 = three-carbon first product; C4 = four-carbon first product. One more C3 mark: **photosynthesis in C3 plants rises with more carbon dioxide** in the air.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the pathway chooses the climate
The two groups **differ in how they respond to light, temperature and water** — which is why **different crops suit different climates**. The hot, bright, dry-land cereals — **sorghum, maize and sugarcane** — are **C4** plants, built for strong light and heat. **Barley**, the cool-highland cereal, is **C3**.

So the pathway is a planting clue: match the crop's inner chemistry to the field's light, heat and water, and the crop works with the climate instead of against it.
