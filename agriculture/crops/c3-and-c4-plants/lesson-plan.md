# Lesson plan — C3 and C4 plants

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Crops · **Lesson:** C3 and C4 plants
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Crops title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two photosynthetic pathways — C3 plants, whose first fixed product has three carbons, and C4 plants, whose first product has four — and why the difference suits crops to different climates
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Two ways of fixing carbon: Crop plants are also classed by their **photosynthetic pathway** — how the leaf first fixes carbon dioxide.
- Sub lesson 2 — Why the pathway chooses the climate: The two groups **differ in how they respond to light, temperature and water** — which is why **different crops suit different climates**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Sorting crops — grains, oils, fibres, forage. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define C3 and C4 by the carbon count of the first stable product of carbon-dioxide fixing.
2. Say how C3 photosynthesis responds to more carbon dioxide.
3. Place sorghum, maize and sugarcane as C4, and barley as C3, and say why the groups suit different climates.

## If the learner is stuck
- Swaps the numbers → C3 first product has 3 carbons; C4 has 4. The number IS the name.
- Places barley with the C4 group → barley is the cool-highland C3 example; sorghum, maize, sugarcane are C4.
