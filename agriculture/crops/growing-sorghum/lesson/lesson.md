# Growing sorghum

## Do it first
Wind flattens a tall crop like a hand pressing grass. Sorghum stands tall in dry country where the wind works hard. Farmers answer with a small heap of soil at each stem. Guess what that heap does before the lesson names it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The drought-land cereal and its sowing
Sorghum suits **lowland and moisture-deficit areas** — it is the cereal of dry country. The soil is **cultivated two or three times**; seed is **broadcast or row-sown at 0.5–1 m between rows and about 40 cm between plants, about 2.5 cm deep, from March to May**.

Compare maize: sorghum stands **wider apart** (its rows can be twice maize's gap) and **shallower** (2.5 cm against maize's 5–7 cm). The spacing fits the land: fewer plants drinking from dry soil.

## Sub lesson 2 — Earthing up, and feeding sorghum
About **a month after planting**, the farmer **earths up** — heaps soil around the stems. The heap **helps the plant resist lodging in wind**: the stem stands in a firmer base, anchored against being laid flat.

Fertiliser commonly runs about **32 kg nitrogen and 10 kg phosphorus per hectare**, **set by the soil's level** — the same soil-first rule maize taught, in sorghum's smaller doses.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion sorghum's sowing numbers and compare them with maize's, then act out earthing up and say what lodging it prevents.
