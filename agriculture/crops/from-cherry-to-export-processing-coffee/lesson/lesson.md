# From cherry to export — processing coffee

## Do it first
A red cherry cannot be shipped as coffee. Between the tree and the export sack, the cherry must lose its coverings. Follow one cherry through.

## Sub lesson 1 — Harvest, then the mill
After harvest, coffee is **processed** and, before export, **milled**. Milling's key step is hulling — and hulling differs by how the coffee was processed, because wet and dry processing leave different coverings on the bean.

[Picture 26620 — dried cherries entering a huller and green beans coming out]

## Sub lesson 2 — Hulling — parchment or whole husk; polishing
For **wet-processed** coffee, hulling removes the **parchment layer (endocarp)**. For **dry-processed** coffee, hulling removes the **whole dried husk** — the mesocarp and endocarp coverings of the dried cherry. **Polishing** is an **optional finishing step** after hulling. Tea harvesting and processing are covered in the same section of the book.
