# Lifting and storing roots and tubers

## Do it first
A fork goes into the soil — and through a potato. That one cut costs food. How should underground crops be lifted so the harvest arrives whole?

## Sub lesson 1 — Lifting without damage
Harvest timing and careful lifting open the chain: underground crops are lifted **without the mechanical damage** counted as the first cause of post-harvest loss. The fork, the hand and the basket are quality tools — every cut and bruise at lifting becomes rot in the store.

[Picture 26615 — potatoes being fork-lifted carefully into a shaded store]

## Sub lesson 2 — Storage against sprouting, water loss and rot
After lifting, the handling chain continues into **storage**, guarded against **sprouting, water loss and rot**. A shaded, careful store keeps the underground harvest sound from harvest day to eating day — the last management job of the unit, and the one that decides how much of the crop is actually eaten.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write a careful-lifting rule list for one crop: tool, depth, handling, basket.
2. Check a store weekly for a month and log sprouting, drying and rot.
3. Compare two stored batches — one handled carefully, one roughly — after two weeks.
