# Growing faba bean

## Do it first
A faba bean pod ripens through colours: green, then dark, then black. Pick too early and the seed is soft; too late and the pod spills. Which colour says 'now'? And weeds race this crop too — how many weedings, and by when?

## Sub lesson 1 — Controlling weeds and trouble — integrated control
Faba bean is produced for its **edible seed**, and its control is **integrated** — several methods woven together: **hand weeding twice within six weeks of planting**, **rotation**, a **clean seedbed**, and **chemicals where needed**.

No single method carries the crop. The clean seedbed starts it ahead, the two early weedings keep it ahead, rotation starves its diseases, and chemicals stand as the reserve — used where needed, not by habit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Harvest — when pods turn black
**Harvest comes when leaves fall and pods dry and turn from green to black.** That colour change is the field's own signal. Picked **green**, the immature pods are eaten **as a vegetable** — the same plant, two foods, two harvest moments.

Faba bean's average national yield is about **1.9 t/ha** — the highest figure among this unit's pulses.
