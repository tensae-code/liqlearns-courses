# Vegetables in Ethiopia — small plots, big potential

## Do it first
List every vegetable your household ate this week. For each one, say where it grew: a home plot, a neighbour's garden, or a market farm.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a vegetable is, and who grows it
A **vegetable** is a plant whose fruit, seeds, roots, tubers, bulbs, stems, leaves or flower parts are eaten. Smallholders grow vegetables mainly for home consumption; commercial private farms grow them solely for the market. Over **6.7 million households** were estimated in 2018 to make a living involving vegetables — a huge number of families on mostly small pieces of land.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rich food, small land, real problems
Leafy and leguminous vegetables are rich in protein, and beta-carotene in vegetables protects the visual process — its prolonged deficiency leads to blindness. Against huge production potential, the land actually used is extremely small. The problems holding vegetables back are poor soil fertility, pests, drought, postharvest losses, and the high price of fuel for pumping irrigation water.
