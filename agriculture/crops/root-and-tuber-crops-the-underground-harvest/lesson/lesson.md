# Root and tuber crops — the underground harvest

## Do it first
Dig in your mind under a garden: potatoes clustered, a carrot pointing down, cassava roots spreading. The harvest you cannot see is still a harvest.

## Sub lesson 1 — The words, and the basket of crops
The unit's terms name the underground crops and their parts. Ethiopia's root-and-tuber basket holds **potato, sweet potato, beet root, carrot, cassava** and their companions — crops whose eaten part grows below the soil surface, as a swollen root or tuber.

[Picture 26612 — a soil cross-section with potato, carrot, beet and cassava growing at their depths]

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting them by their use
The crops are **classified by their use** — what the household or market takes them for. Classification by use tells the grower which crop fills which need, and it is the frame for the unit's management sections: each use carries its own planting, feeding and harvest practice.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, using one real example from your own area for each sub lesson. Your companion asks one question about each part — answer from the lesson, not from memory of other lessons.

## Do It Yourself
1. List every root or tuber crop in your kitchen and name its underground part.
2. Sort your list by use: daily food, gap food, market crop.
3. Draw a soil cross-section with three crops at their real depths.
