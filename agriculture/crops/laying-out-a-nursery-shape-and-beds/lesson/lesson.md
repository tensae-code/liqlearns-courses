# Laying out a nursery — shape and beds

## Do it first
Fencing is dear. Two nurseries hold the same area: one long and crooked, one square. Which eats more fence? Geometry is money here.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shape — square or rectangular
Design and layout begins with shape: the nursery should be **square or rectangular** — an **irregular shape increases boundary lines and fencing**, and **the square is ideal because it minimises them**. The cheapest fence for a given area is the square's.

## Sub lesson 2 — Beds — orientation and work flow
The **orientation and location of the nursery beds** are important considerations, laid out for **water, access and work flow**. Beds are placed so water reaches them, workers move between them, and seedlings travel the shortest road to the gate.

1. Draw two nurseries of equal area — square and irregular — and count fence lengths.
2. Lay out beds on paper for a real plot: mark water point, paths and gate.
3. Peg a square on the ground and walk its boundary against an irregular shape's.
