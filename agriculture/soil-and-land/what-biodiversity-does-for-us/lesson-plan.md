# Lesson plan — What biodiversity does for us

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Soil and Land · **Lesson:** What biodiversity does for us
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Soil and Land title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the values and services of biodiversity — it underpins food and farming (the genetic resources crops and livestock are bred from), ecosystem services, materials and the national economy — so conservation reads as self-interest, not charity
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — The breeding library: **Biodiversity underpins food and farming**: it holds **the genetic resources crops and livestock are bred from**.
- Sub lesson 2 — Services, materials, economy: Beyond the farm, biodiversity delivers **ecosystem services** (the working of living systems people depend on), **materials**, and strength to **the national economy** — the roles are enumerated so the conclusion lands plainly.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Biodiversity — the variety of life. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say biodiversity underpins food and farming as the genetic resources crops and livestock are bred from.
2. Name its wider work: ecosystem services, materials and the national economy.
3. Draw the lesson's conclusion: conservation is self-interest, not charity.
