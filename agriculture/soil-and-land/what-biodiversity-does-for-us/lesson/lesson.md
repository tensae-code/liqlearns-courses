# What biodiversity does for us

## Do it first
Your dinner's injera, stew and greens descend from wild plants chosen thousands of years ago — and breeders still return to those wild cousins when a crop meets a new disease. What would farming do if the cousins were gone? Hold that thought.

## Sub lesson 1 — The breeding library
**Biodiversity underpins food and farming**: it holds **the genetic resources crops and livestock are bred from**. Every improved variety began as genes found in some landrace, wild relative or local breed.

Lose the variety and the library burns unread: the gene that would have resisted tomorrow's rust, drought or pest may exist in exactly one wild population. The breeds lessons of this course — local zebu adaptation, exotic yield — are both chapters of this one library.

## Sub lesson 2 — Services, materials, economy
Beyond the farm, biodiversity delivers **ecosystem services** (the working of living systems people depend on), **materials**, and strength to **the national economy** — the roles are enumerated so the conclusion lands plainly.

That conclusion is the lesson's point: **conservation reads as self-interest, not charity**. Protecting variety is not a favour done for nature; it is a household and a nation keeping its own tools sharp.

## Say it to your companion
Make the self-interest case to your companion: the breeding library first, then services, materials and economy — ending with 'not charity'.
