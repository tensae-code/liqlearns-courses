# Ethiopia's water — eight river basins

## Do it first
Rain falls on a highland roof. A week later, where is that water — in a local well, a river two valleys away, or a sea in another country? Ethiopia's water mostly travels. How far? Hold the question.

## Sub lesson 1 — The water cycle, simplified
Water moves in a circle: it **evaporates** from seas, lakes and land, gathers as **cloud**, falls as **rain**, and runs back through **rivers** — the **water cycle** in its simplified form. No water is made or lost; it is only ever somewhere on the circle.

Farming lives on one arc of the circle — the rain over the field. Everything about farm water management later in this course is an attempt to slow the circle down where the farm needs it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Eight river basins — the water tower
Ethiopia's endowment is set out basin by basin: **eight river basins and one lake basin**, tabled in the book with their **sources and flow directions**.

The sources sit in Ethiopia's highlands and the rivers flow outward to the lowlands and beyond — which is why **Ethiopia is called the water tower of Africa**: a highland roof from which a continent's rivers descend. The gift carries the duty of the management units: a tower's water is everybody's business.
