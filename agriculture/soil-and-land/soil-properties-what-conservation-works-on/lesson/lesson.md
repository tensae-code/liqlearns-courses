# Soil properties — what conservation works on

## Do it first
Take two handfuls of soil from different fields. One runs through your fingers; one sticks like dough. Before conserving either, you must know which is which — and why it matters.

## Sub lesson 1 — Physical and chemical character
**Soil properties** open the conservation unit: the **physical and chemical character** of a soil — its texture, structure and chemistry — is the ground truth every later decision stands on. Conservation works on a patient whose body must first be understood.

[Picture 26673 — hands holding two soils — sandy and clay — compared]

## Sub lesson 2 — Properties decide risk and remedy
A soil's character decides both its **erosion risk** and **which conservation practice fits it**. The same rain is a drink to one soil and a thief to another; the same bund saves one field and fails on the next. Know the soil, then choose the practice.

## Do It Yourself
1. Compare two soils by hand: texture, stickiness, colour; write the property list for each.
2. Predict which of your two soils erodes faster on a slope, and why.
3. Ask a farmer how they judge a soil before working it.
