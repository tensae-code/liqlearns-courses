# Lesson plan — What forests give us

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Soil and Land · **Lesson:** What forests give us
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Soil and Land title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the gifts of trees — firewood, fruits, medicines, timber and food, construction materials, shade and wind protection, and aesthetic and spiritual value — and the long relationship between humans and forests
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — The product gifts: A forest pays in things you can carry: **firewood** for the hearth, **fruits** and **food**, **medicines** from bark, leaf and root, **timber** and **construction materials** for houses and tools.
- Sub lesson 2 — The service gifts — and the long relationship: The forest also pays in services no one carries: **shade** for people, animals and crops; **wind protection** for fields and homes; and **aesthetic and spiritual value** — beauty, and the sacred groves and church forests where a community meets its deepest meanings.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural resources — renewable and not. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the forest's product gifts: firewood, fruits, medicines, timber and food, construction materials.
2. List its service gifts: shade and wind protection, aesthetic and spiritual value.
3. Say that the human-forest relationship is long — forests have carried communities for generations.

## What you need
One real tree to stand under; the lesson picture of a great tree with its gifts.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists only firewood and timber → add the services: shade, wind protection, aesthetic and spiritual value.
