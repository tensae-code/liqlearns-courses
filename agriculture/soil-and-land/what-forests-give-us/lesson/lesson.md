# What forests give us

## Do it first
Stand by the biggest tree you know. Count what it gives without being cut: shade, wind shelter, fruit, beauty. Then what it gives if cut. Which list is longer — and which list keeps giving?

## Sub lesson 1 — The product gifts
A forest pays in things you can carry: **firewood** for the hearth, **fruits** and **food**, **medicines** from bark, leaf and root, **timber** and **construction materials** for houses and tools.

These are the visible gifts — the ones with a price at market. Every village economy runs partly on this list, whether money changes hands or not.

## Sub lesson 2 — The service gifts — and the long relationship
The forest also pays in services no one carries: **shade** for people, animals and crops; **wind protection** for fields and homes; and **aesthetic and spiritual value** — beauty, and the sacred groves and church forests where a community meets its deepest meanings.

The unit traces the **long relationship between humans and forests**: we have always lived from them. The lesson hidden in the list is simple — a standing forest pays every year; a felled one pays once.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion around one great tree, naming a gift at each side: products first, then the services — shade, wind, beauty, spirit.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the forest's gift list in two columns: carried away, and enjoyed standing.
2. Interview an elder: what did the forest give your family when you were a child? Write three answers.
3. Argue in three lines: a tree standing is worth more than a tree felled — or the opposite, for one named need.
