# Conservation practices — structures and conservation farming

## Do it first
Two farmers fight erosion differently: one builds stone lines across the slope; the other barely disturbs the soil and keeps it dressed in old stalks. Both are conserving. Learn both weapons.

## Sub lesson 1 — Structures — bunds and terraces
The practice families include the **physical structures — bunds, terraces** and their kin — built lines that slow water, hold soil and step a slope into workable ground. Structures are conservation you can see from across the valley.

[Picture 26676 — bunds and terraces on a slope beside a residue-covered no-till plot]

## Sub lesson 2 — Conservation farming — three principles
Beside them stands **conservation tillage / conservation farming**: practices conserving soil, water and soil moisture, enhancing fertiliser and seed use and saving time and money — under **three principles: minimum soil disturbance; crop residue management (leaving residues on the surface); and rotation/diversification**. One family builds; the other disturbs less and covers more. Most farms need both.
