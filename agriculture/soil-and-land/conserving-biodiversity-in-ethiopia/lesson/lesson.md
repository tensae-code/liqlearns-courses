# Conserving biodiversity in Ethiopia

## Do it first
A seed can be saved two ways: guarded in the field where it evolved, or locked in a store far away. Each way fails differently. Which failures? Decide before the lesson keeps life in both places at once.

## Sub lesson 1 — Protecting habitats and genetic resources
**Conservation measures answer the threats** — the unit pairs them deliberately: habitat destruction is answered by **protecting habitats**, and the loss of genetic resources by **protecting genetic resources** themselves.

Protection is active, not a fence and a wish: the threats list (clearing, fire, illegal harvest, invasives, failed regeneration) is the checklist conservation patrols against, site by site.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two keeping-places, and Ethiopia's status
Life is kept in **two keeping-places: in place** — in the habitats and farms where it lives and adapts — **and in collections**, where seeds, genes and specimens are held against disaster. Each covers the other's failure: a store cannot adapt; a field can burn.

The unit closes by reporting **the status of biodiversity conservation in Ethiopia**, whose **forests and species are under the pressures of the previous lesson**. The honest status is the starting line: measures exist, pressures persist, and the work of this whole title — managing soil, water, forest and the whole environment — is what conservation looks like at farm scale.
