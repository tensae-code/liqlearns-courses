# Lesson plan — Indigenous knowledge — caring for land the local way

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Soil and Land · **Lesson:** Indigenous knowledge — caring for land the local way
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Soil and Land title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous knowledge in natural resource management — Ethiopia's diverse altitudes and vegetation carried in local practice, as North Shewa farmers' indigenous soil and water conservation shows — learned by surveying your own surroundings (a field-work lesson)
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Local knowledge conserves real diversity: Ethiopia's **diverse altitudes and climates carry diverse natural vegetation** — and **indigenous knowledge contributes directly to conserving it**.
- Sub lesson 2 — The survey — your surroundings as the text: This is a **field-work lesson**: pupils **survey their own surroundings** — **listing local water and forest resources, the management in use, and judging the community's practice**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Managing the whole environment. This is a field-work lesson. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say indigenous knowledge contributes directly to conserving Ethiopia's diverse natural vegetation across its altitudes and climates.
2. Give the book's example: farmers in North Shewa apply indigenous soil and water conservation practices against land degradation.
3. Do the field task: survey your surroundings — list local water and forest resources, the management in use, and judge the community's practice.

## What you need
A walk through your own surroundings; a notebook for the survey; the lesson picture of North Shewa stone bunds.
