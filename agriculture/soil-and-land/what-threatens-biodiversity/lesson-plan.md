# Lesson plan — What threatens biodiversity

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Soil and Land · **Lesson:** What threatens biodiversity
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Soil and Land title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the threats worked through one by one — land-use change, pollution, population growth raising demand, invasive species — and in Ethiopia: deforestation, habitat destruction, poor regeneration, clearing forest for agriculture, forest fires and illegal tree harvest threatening many tree species
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — The general threats: The threats are worked **one by one**.
- Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's named pressures: In Ethiopia specifically, the list is concrete: **deforestation, habitat destruction, poor regeneration, clearing forest for agriculture, forest fires and illegal tree harvest** — which **together threaten many tree species**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What biodiversity does for us. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the general threats: land-use change, pollution, population growth raising demand, invasive species.
2. Explain two mechanisms: converting forests, grasslands and woodlands to cultivation destroys habitats; fertiliser-rich eroded soil pollutes water and harms aquatic animals such as fish, to extinction.
3. List Ethiopia's specific pressures: deforestation, habitat destruction, poor regeneration, clearing forest for agriculture, forest fires and illegal tree harvest.
