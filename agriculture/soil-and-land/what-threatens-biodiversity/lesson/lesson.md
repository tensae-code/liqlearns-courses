# What threatens biodiversity

## Do it first
A forest becomes a field (a family eats). A river fills with rich mud (fish die). A new plant arrives and eats a hillside. A town doubles and doubles its hunger. Four different threats — or one? Sort them before the lesson names them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The general threats
The threats are worked **one by one**. **Land-use change**: **converting forests, grasslands and woodlands to cultivation destroys habitats** — the habitat-loss engine from the degradation lessons, now counted against variety itself.

**Pollution**: **fertiliser-rich eroded soil pollutes water and harms aquatic animals such as fish — to extinction**. **Population growth** raises **demand for food and services**, pressing every other threat harder. And **invasive species spread into new ground**, crowding out the natives that evolved there.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's named pressures
In Ethiopia specifically, the list is concrete: **deforestation, habitat destruction, poor regeneration, clearing forest for agriculture, forest fires and illegal tree harvest** — which **together threaten many tree species**.

Note **poor regeneration** among them: a forest can be lost not only by cutting but by failing to regrow — grazed seedlings, burned regrowth. The conservation lesson answers threat by threat; a threat unnamed is a threat unmanaged.
