# How water gets polluted

## Do it first
A river runs clear past a hill farm, then past a factory, then past sprayed fields, then into a village's pots. At which bend did it stop being the same river? What entered it — and could you see it all?

## Sub lesson 1 — What pollution is
**Water pollution is the presence of harmful substances at unacceptable levels, spoiling water quality.** Note the measure: not any trace, but levels that make water unfit — for drinking, for fish, for irrigation.

Pollution is judged by what the water must do. A river can carry a load and still serve; past the unacceptable level, every user downstream pays for what entered upstream.

## Sub lesson 2 — The sources — factory and farm
The book names the sources plainly. **Industrial waste water** carries **acids and alkalis** and other harmful substances from factories into streams.

And farming's own hand appears: **pesticides and fertilisers washing from farms** — rain carries field chemicals off the land they were meant for, into the water everyone shares. The lesson is not blame; it is aim: what is applied to land must be kept on land, at the right rate and time, or the river collects it.

## Say it to your companion
Define pollution with its measure ('unacceptable levels') for your companion, then name the industrial and farm sources without softening either.

## Do It Yourself
1. Trace your nearest stream on paper: mark every place something could enter it — factory, field, washing place — and the users downstream.
2. Write why 'a little poison' is not the definition: what makes pollution pollution?
3. Give one practice that keeps fertiliser on the field instead of in the river.
