# Why resources must be managed

## Do it first
One pile of firewood, two families. One burns as if the forest were endless and is cold by mid-winter. One measures every stick and is still warm at spring. Same pile — what differed? Name it before the lesson calls it management.

## Sub lesson 1 — The finite fact
Management begins from one hard fact: **many resources — especially non-renewables such as coal, natural gas and mineral ores — exist in finite quantities**. The pile is only so big; the seam only so deep.

**Failure to use them wisely depletes them in a short time** — not in some distant century, but within a working lifetime. The resources unit showed the stock and its wounds; this unit exists because the stock does not manage itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Concepts and objectives first
Before any practice, the unit sets out **the concepts and objectives that the management practices serve** — the why before the how. Every technique that follows — terraces, rainwater tanks, woodlots, whole-environment care — is an answer to this lesson's question: how do we draw on a finite stock without emptying it?

Hold the objectives in mind as aims to judge practices by: a practice manages a resource only if the stock lasts — for this generation's use and the next generation's inheritance.
