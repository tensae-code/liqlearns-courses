# Lesson plan — Climate change — what the words mean

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Soil and Land · **Lesson:** Climate change — what the words mean
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Soil and Land title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the difference between climate change (a long-term shift in temperatures and weather patterns) and climate variability (the shorter swing), and the mainly human causes of the change
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Change over decades, swing over seasons: **Climate change is a long-term change — over decades — in temperatures and weather patterns.
- Sub lesson 2 — Mainly human causes: The causes of today's climate change are **mainly human**: **burning fossil fuels**, **agriculture** itself, and **land-use change** — clearing forests and changing how land is used.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural resources and soil/water lessons of this course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell climate change (decades-long shift) from climate variability (shorter swing) in your own words.
2. Name extremes that become more frequent: floods, long droughts, wildfires, unusual snowfalls, intense hurricanes, global warming.
3. Name the mainly human causes: fossil-fuel burning, agriculture and land-use change.

## What you need
A notebook; local weather memories from elders — a dry year, a flood year.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks one hot week is climate change → change is measured over decades; a week is weather, a season's swing is variability.
- Thinks climate change is only natural → today's change is mainly human-caused: fuels, farming, land-use change.
