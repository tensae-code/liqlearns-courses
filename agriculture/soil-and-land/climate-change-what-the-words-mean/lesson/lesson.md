# Climate change — what the words mean

## Do it first
Think of the weather your grandparents describe and the weather you know. Is one hot week 'climate change'? Is thirty years of hotter, stranger seasons the same thing? Sort these before the words are named: a single storm, a decade of failing rains, one cold morning.

## Sub lesson 1 — Change over decades, swing over seasons
**Climate change is a long-term change — over decades — in temperatures and weather patterns.** It makes extremes more frequent: **unusual floods, long droughts, wildfires, unusual snowfalls, intense hurricanes and the warming of the Earth**.

**Climate variability is the shorter-term swing** around the pattern — a wet year and a dry year inside a climate that may itself be shifting. One strange season is variability; the pattern itself moving, decade by decade, is change.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mainly human causes
The causes of today's climate change are **mainly human**: **burning fossil fuels**, **agriculture** itself, and **land-use change** — clearing forests and changing how land is used.

That is why a farming course teaches it: farming feels climate change first, and farming is also one of its causes — so farming is part of the answer, as the next lessons show.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the two words apart — change over decades, variability as the swing — and the three human causes.
