# Biodiversity — the variety of life

## Do it first
Stand in one small patch of grassland and count kinds: grasses, herbs, insects, birds, things too small to see. Ten kinds? Fifty? You are counting something with a long name. What is being counted — individuals, or kinds?

## Sub lesson 1 — What biodiversity is
**Biodiversity is the range of all forms of life on Earth — plants, animals, humans, bacteria and other organisms.** It is counted not in individuals but in variety: **species numbers, sub-species and communities in a habitat**.

A field with forty kinds of living things is richer than a field with four, even if the second holds more individual plants. Variety itself is the treasure — the library of forms life has invented.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its scope and scales
The scope is wide: **plant genetic resources, animal genetic resources — including farm-animal and wildlife genetic resources — and microbial genetic resources, plus habitat diversity**. The genes inside one teff landrace are biodiversity as surely as a forest's species list.

And it **can be assessed at site, region, country or global scale, depending on the objective** — a farmer counts the site, a nation counts its heritage, the world counts the whole. Same variety, four magnifications.

## Say it to your companion
Define biodiversity for your companion, say how it is counted (kinds, not heads), then give the scope's four parts and the four scales.

## Do It Yourself
1. Census one small patch for 10 minutes: list every kind you can distinguish. That list is a site-scale biodiversity count.
2. Write why a teff landrace's genes count as biodiversity.
3. Choose an objective (a farm plan, a national report) and say which scale fits it, and why.
