# Soil degradation — erosion and its cousins

## Do it first
Two fields, same rain. One ends the storm muddy but whole; the other ends it scarred with a new gully, its dark top gone down the stream. Same theft — or are there other ways a soil can be ruined without moving at all? List your guesses.

## Sub lesson 1 — What degradation is, and erosion
**Soil degradation is a long-term fall in soil productivity** — the resource quietly worth less each year. Its first and most visible form is **erosion: soil carried away by water and wind**.

Water erosion carves sheet wash, rills and finally **gullies through farmland**; wind erosion lifts the fine, fertile fraction from bare dry ground. Erosion steals the profile's best layer first — the dark, feeding topsoil.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chemical and physical degradation
Soil can also be ruined **without moving**. **Chemical degradation** changes its chemistry for the worse: **acidification** (growing too acid), **salinisation** (salt building up) and **pollution** (poisons entering the soil).

**Physical degradation** damages its structure — such as **compaction**, where pressure squeezes the soil until roots and water can barely enter. Three families, one result: a soil that yields less. The management lesson answers each: cover and structures against erosion, organic matter and care against chemical decline, and loosening and protection against compaction.
