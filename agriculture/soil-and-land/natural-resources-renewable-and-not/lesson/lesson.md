# Natural resources — renewable and not

## Do it first
Look around you and list everything you did not make and could not make: sun, rain, soil, trees, stone, grazing animals. Which of them grow back when used — and which, once gone, are gone? Sort before the lesson names the divide.

## Sub lesson 1 — What natural resources are
**Natural resources** are **objects, materials, creatures or energy found in nature that humans use but cannot produce**. The soil under the field, the rain over it, the forest, the grazing land, the minerals below — none of them is manufactured; all of them are borrowed from nature's stock.

Farming is the art of living off this stock without emptying it. The rest of this title walks the stock item by item — forest, soil, water — and then asks how each is lost and how each is cared for.

## Sub lesson 2 — Renewable and non-renewable
The stock divides in two. **Renewable resources are able to regenerate themselves** — a forest regrows, rain returns, grass renews — if they are used at a pace nature can replace.

**Non-renewable resources are fixed in supply** — minerals and fossil stores do not regrow on any human timescale. **Use them wisely, or survival — ours and the coming generations' — is in question.** Even renewables obey the same warning in practice: cut a forest faster than it regrows and it behaves like a mine emptying.

## Say it to your companion
Define natural resources for your companion, give one renewable and one non-renewable example from home, and finish with the coming-generations warning.

## Do It Yourself
1. List ten natural resources your household uses in a week; mark each R (renewable) or N (non-renewable).
2. Write one way a renewable resource can be made to behave like a non-renewable one.
3. In two lines: why do the coming generations appear in this lesson?
