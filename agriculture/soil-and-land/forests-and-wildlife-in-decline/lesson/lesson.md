# Forests and wildlife in decline

## Do it first
An elder says: 'When I was young, the forest began at that rock.' Today it begins a valley away. What walked it backwards, year by year? And what walked away with it? List the suspects before the lesson counts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Forests degraded — the drivers
The scale first: **over half the world's tropical forests have been degraded since the 1960s**. Degradation here means the forest thins, sickens and loses its gifts — not always cleared outright, but diminished.

The drivers are named: **increasing fires and disease**, **logging — clearing for firewood and timber** — and **climate change** pressing on all of it. Each driver alone wounds; together they retreat the forest edge year by year.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wildlife declines with its home
**Wildlife degradation shows itself in declining animal populations** — fewer animals, fewer kinds, in the places they were common.

The two declines are one story: **habitat loss drives both forest and wildlife decline together**. An animal does not live in 'nature' in general; it lives in a particular forest, grassland or wetland. Shrink the habitat and the population follows it down — which is why the management lesson protects habitats, not only individual animals.
