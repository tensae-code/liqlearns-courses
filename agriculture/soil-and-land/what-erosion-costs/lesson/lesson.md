# What erosion costs

## Do it first
No thief sends a bill. Erosion's bill arrives quietly: thinner soil, smaller harvests, muddy water. Read the bill in full before deciding conservation is expensive.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lost topsoil and fertility
Erosion's costs begin with **lost topsoil and fertility**: the richest layer, built over centuries, is the layer erosion takes first. What leaves is exactly what the crop most needs — the soil's stored wealth and its power to feed a plant.

[Picture 26675 — a field's yield shrinking year by year as its topsoil thins]

## Sub lesson 2 — Falling yields and sedimented water
Then **falling yields** year by year, and **sedimented water** — soil choking streams and reservoirs downstream — among the other negative impacts the section counts. The bill is paid by the farmer uphill and the water-user downhill alike: that bill is what makes conservation pay.
