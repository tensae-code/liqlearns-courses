# Managing soil — structure and fertility

## Do it first
Pour the same jug of water on a hard farm track and on a loose garden bed. One puddles and runs away; one drinks. A compacted field is the track. What two jobs must management do — hold the soil, and do what else?

## Sub lesson 1 — The problem: compaction and runoff
**Repeated cultivation compacts soil, so rain runs off instead of entering.** The field's own traffic and tillage squeeze the sponge; the storm's water — which the crop needed — leaves for the river carrying topsoil with it.

So management answers with **erosion control and fertility building together**: **physical structures and cover** hold the soil where it lies and slow the water until it enters, instead of letting it escape.

## Sub lesson 2 — Feeding the soil — organic first, inorganic's trade
Fertility is raised by **raising organic matter**: **including legumes in cereal plantings** (the pulse lesson's nitrogen gift, used as management), **applying manure, compost and other organic fertilisers** — feeding the soil's life and structure, not only the crop.

**Inorganic fertiliser** is added for **a faster but shorter-lived fertility lift**: quick feed, briefly held. The wise manager combines them — organic matter as the soil's lasting wealth, inorganic as the season's quick cash — and never mistakes the second for the first.
