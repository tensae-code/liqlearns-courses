# The Gedeo way — tradition protecting nature

## Do it first
Can a custom protect a forest better than a fence? Think of one rule in your own community that protects a tree, a spring or a hillside — who keeps it, and what happens to the place it guards? Hold that beside the Gedeo example.

## Sub lesson 1 — A culture that protects
The **Gedeo community of southern Ethiopia** holds a **rich culture of environmental protection**. In their tradition, **nature — mountains, trees, hillsides and riversides — is respected and protected**.

Protection here is not a project with a start and an end; it is how the community lives, taught with the culture itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mitigation built over centuries
The climate unit closes with Gedeo because the tradition is **a working mitigation system built over hundreds of years**: protected trees and hillsides are carbon sinks that were never cut, and protected riversides hold their soils.

The course's running lesson lands again: **local knowledge is a real strategy** — not a decoration on the science, but one of its working tools.
