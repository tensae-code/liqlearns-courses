# Lesson plan — Managing forests and wildlife

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Soil and Land · **Lesson:** Managing forests and wildlife
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Soil and Land title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** forest management — including preventing wild forest fires — for sustainable fuelwood, productive vegetation use, rural employment and income, biodiversity conservation and less deforestation, desert expansion and climate vulnerability — with wildlife management as the animal half of the same duty
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Why forests must be managed: **Forest management — including preventing wild forest fires** — is needed for a whole list of reasons, and the list is the lesson: to **supply fuelwood sustainably**; to **use forest vegetation more productively**; to **create rural employment and income**; to **conserve biodiversity**; to **reduce deforestation and desert expansion**; and to **cut vulnerability to climate change**.
- Sub lesson 2 — Wildlife — the animal half of the duty: **Wildlife management is taught alongside forest management as the animal half of the same duty.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Forests and wildlife in decline. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List what forest management is needed for: sustainable fuelwood, productive use of forest vegetation, rural employment and income, biodiversity conservation, reduced deforestation and desert expansion, less climate vulnerability.
2. Name fire prevention as a named part of forest management.
3. Say wildlife management is taught alongside as the animal half of the same duty.
