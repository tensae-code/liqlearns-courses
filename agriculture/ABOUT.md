# About the Agriculture course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this part is built
from Ethiopia's own Agriculture books for Grades 11 and 12,
read in full, page by page, with the teacher guides folded in
as labelled evidence. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**:
a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips
are ours; the lessons are the books'.

This About files what the bands handed over for it: the two
books' own course shapes, and the Grade 11 student book's
figure-reference list. The teacher guides' apparatus stands
in the lessons themselves, as labelled evidence rows and
evidence lessons — it is not re-filed here.

## Ethiopia, Agriculture Grade 11 — the book's own shape (student evidence)

**The four arcs** (Grade 11 student book, placement 1): the
course runs crops (Units 1–3), animals (Units 4–9), land and
climate (Units 10–14) and food (Units 15–16); every unit opens
with Brainstorming from local life and closes with Summary +
Review Exercise, and the book returns throughout to Ethiopian
indigenous knowledge — Konso terracing, ethnoveterinary
remedies, Gedeo protection, chuko and enset/kocho. The book's
named course-intro picture (a four-arc road map) and intro
video are course-level material held here. The Grade 11
guide's Minimum Learning Competency list and Syllabus stand
both as lessons (in Farm Business and Technology, on the
0-countries evidence line) and as the record here.

### Figure references — where the Grade 11 book's pictures came from

The Grade 11 student book closes by sourcing its figures
(student book, printed p. 261, placement 107): breeding
research via **wur.nl** and **mdpi.com**; sisal and cotton
industry images; the **Markos Tibbo** animal-science research
behind **Figure 4.2**; the **Tolossa et al.** rainwater-
harvesting study behind **Figure 11.3**; and others the page
names. This provenance habit is the one the course build
mirrors in its own About pages and per-lesson sources files.

## Ethiopia, Agriculture Grade 12 — the book's own shape (student evidence)

**A year of seventeen enterprises** (Grade 12 student book,
placement 1): the book's own map of its seventeen enterprise
units — vegetables → fruits (propagation, nursery, orchard)
→ roots and tubers → coffee, tea and spices → plant
biotechnology → beef → sheep and goats → camels → poultry →
fish → bees → nursery and plantation technology →
agro-forestry → soil and water conservation → gender and
human nutrition → safe food and postharvest → ICT in
agriculture — the frame Band E built against, enterprise by
enterprise in the book's order. Each animal enterprise
repeats the book's five-beat rhythm as its lesson set:
breeds/selection → feeds → housing → diseases → products.
Its Ethiopian anchors stand in their own lessons: 6.7M+
households in vegetables; beef at 110 kg/head against a
world 212 kg/head; Nile tilapia and the catfish "Ambaza";
the Ethiopian Commodity Exchange (ECX); and mobile
subscriptions rising from 1.2M (2007) to ~54.3M. Its named
course-intro picture (a road map of seventeen enterprise
stalls) and intro video are course-level material held
here. The Grade 12 guide's Minimum Learning Competency
list and Syllabus stand both as lessons (in Farm Business
and Technology, on the 0-countries evidence line) and as
the record here.
