# Empowering women farmers — resources, income, leadership, time

## Do it first
She grew it. Can she decide its price, keep its income, lead the meeting about it, and still have hours left in her day? Four questions — four domains of power.

## Sub lesson 1 — Resources and income
Empowerment is broken into its domains. First: **access to and decision-making power about productive resources** (autonomy in production). Second: **control over the use of income** — sole or joint control over income and expenditures. A farmer empowered in the field but not over the money is half empowered.

## Sub lesson 2 — Leadership and time
Third: **leadership in the community**. Fourth: **time allocation**, judged from a log of different activities. Group work in the book asks pupils to explain the importance of empowering women in food production and consumption — because all four domains together are the difference between working in agriculture and standing in it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, using one real example from your own area for each sub lesson. Your companion asks one question about each part — answer from the lesson, not from memory of other lessons.

## Do It Yourself
1. Build a one-day time log for yourself and judge your own time domain.
2. Interview a woman farmer gently about the four domains; write which is strongest for her.
3. Do the book's group work: explain the importance of empowering women in food production and consumption.
