# Lesson plan — Mechanisation — power, gains and costs

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Farm Business and Technology · **Lesson:** Mechanisation — power, gains and costs
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Farm Business and Technology title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what mechanised farming is, what it gains, what it costs — including the communal work parties it can weaken — and its low, fair and high levels
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Machines take over the power: **Mechanisation replaces human or animal labour with machinery** across **land development, crop production, harvesting, storage, animal rearing and farm-gate processing**.
- Sub lesson 2 — The costs, and the three levels: The costs are named honestly: mechanisation **can hamper farmers' social capital such as *debo* and *guza* — the communal work parties**; it **reduces the manual work available for labourers**; and it **needs continuous fuel and electricity**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Crops and Livestock titles of this course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define mechanisation and name its six fields of work.
2. Name three costs: weakened communal work parties (debo, guza), less manual work for labourers, continuous fuel and electricity needs.
3. State the three levels and the fair level's animal-power share: about a third to two-thirds.

## What you need
A hand tool from home — a hoe or sickle — to hold while the levels are compared.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks mechanisation is only tractors in fields → it runs from land development to farm-gate processing, including animal rearing and storage.
- Thinks machines have no social cost → debo and guza, the communal work parties, can be hampered.
