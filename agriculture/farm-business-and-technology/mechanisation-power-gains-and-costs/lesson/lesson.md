# Mechanisation — power, gains and costs

## Do it first
Three farmers clear the same field: one with a hoe, one with oxen, one with a tractor. Who finishes first? Who needs fuel? Whose neighbours used to help — and what happens to that helping when the machine comes? Guess the gains and the costs before they are named.

## Sub lesson 1 — Machines take over the power
**Mechanisation replaces human or animal labour with machinery** across **land development, crop production, harvesting, storage, animal rearing and farm-gate processing**.

It **raises what a farm can do** — more land worked, work done on time, heavier jobs made possible.

## Sub lesson 2 — The costs, and the three levels
The costs are named honestly: mechanisation **can hamper farmers' social capital such as *debo* and *guza* — the communal work parties**; it **reduces the manual work available for labourers**; and it **needs continuous fuel and electricity**.

Farms are classed **low, fair and high** in mechanisation by the share of animal versus machine power — at the **fair level, animal power still supplies about a third to two-thirds** of the power.
