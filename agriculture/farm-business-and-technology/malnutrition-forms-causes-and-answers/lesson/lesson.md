# Malnutrition — forms, causes and answers

## Do it first
Malnutrition is not one thing. A child can be too short for their age, too thin for their height, or fed enough bulk and still missing one invisible nutrient. Before the forms are named, guess how many different shapes 'bad nutrition' can take — and who it strikes hardest.

## Sub lesson 1 — Five forms, three levels of cause
Malnutrition's sub-forms are **stunting, wasting, underweight, overweight and micronutrient deficiency** — too little growth, too little weight, and hidden hunger for single nutrients all count.

Its causes are classed as **immediate, underlying and basic** — from the food and illness of the day, down through household conditions, to the deepest structures. In Ethiopia the problem is tabled in the book — the trend among **children under five** — and it hits **children and pregnant and lactating women hardest**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring it, and the answers
The **Body Mass Index (BMI)** is introduced as a measure of weight against height, read from its chart — a number that sorts healthy weight from its neighbours.

Malnutrition's impacts run **from failure to perform daily work to lost productivity at individual, society and national level**. The solutions **begin with educating communities** — knowledge first, because so many causes sit in daily practice.
