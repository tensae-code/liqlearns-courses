# Sprayers, incubators and hand tools

## Do it first
Three tools, three jobs: one sprays, one hatches, one cuts. Before they are named in full, say what each must do well — the sprayer must cover evenly and safely, the incubator must hold warmth for weeks, the shears must cut clean. Then check your list against the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The sprayer and the shears — tools that apply and cut
The **sprayer applies insecticides, herbicides, pesticides or water to crops** — and is also used to **spray animals against external parasites**. Spray only as the product label directs, in protective clothing, never toward people, animals or water sources, and wash yourself and the equipment afterwards — the chemical is a tool, and it is handled like one.

**Pruning shears are heavy-duty scissors for cutting tree branches and plant stems** — the clean cut that keeps the plant healthy.

## Sub lesson 2 — The incubator — a machine hen
The **incubator keeps fertile eggs warm through 21 days of incubation**, with a **fan and heater** holding the warmth even, the way a sitting hen does.

Around these three stands **the rest of the unit's hand tools and animal-production equipment**, each taught by its use: name the job, and the tool follows.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the sprayer's two jobs and its safety rules, the incubator's 21 days, and what the shears cut.
