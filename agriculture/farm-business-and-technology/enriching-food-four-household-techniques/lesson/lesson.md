# Enriching food — four household techniques

## Do it first
A handful of plain grain enters four kitchens. In one it is mixed with a nutrient powder, in one it sprouts, in one it ferments, in one it roasts. The grain leaves each kitchen worth more than it entered. Guess what changed inside it in each kitchen before the four techniques are named.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fortification and germination
**Nutrient enrichment (modification) raises a food's nutritive value and the bioavailability of its micronutrients**, at commercial or household level, **aiming to cut micronutrient deficiency**.

**Fortification adds vitamins and minerals to foods** — the factory or mill does it for whole towns. **Germination is sprouting grain** — the household's own low-cost technique, waking the seed so its nutrients become easier to use.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fermentation and roasting
**Fermentation is micro-organisms breaking food down — yeast in bread, bacteria in yoghurt.** Ethiopia's fermented foods include **injera and milk curd** — while **kita is not fermented**.

**Roasting** completes the four: dry heat that changes flavour and value together. Four kitchens, four ways to make the same grain worth more.
