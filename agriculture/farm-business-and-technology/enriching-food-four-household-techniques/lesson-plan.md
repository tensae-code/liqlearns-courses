# Lesson plan — Enriching food — four household techniques

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Farm Business and Technology · **Lesson:** Enriching food — four household techniques
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Farm Business and Technology title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** four techniques that raise a food's nutritive value — fortification, germination, fermentation and roasting — at commercial or household level, against micronutrient deficiency
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Fortification and germination: **Nutrient enrichment (modification) raises a food's nutritive value and the bioavailability of its micronutrients**, at commercial or household level, **aiming to cut micronutrient deficiency**.
- Sub lesson 2 — Fermentation and roasting: **Fermentation is micro-organisms breaking food down — yeast in bread, bacteria in yoghurt.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Strategies for a diverse diet. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four techniques: fortification, germination, fermentation, roasting.
2. Define each in the lesson's words, including fermentation's micro-organisms and germination as sprouting grain.
3. State the aim: raising nutritive value and micronutrient bioavailability to cut micronutrient deficiency; and the trap — kita is not fermented.

## What you need
A handful of grain; a jar, water and cloth for the germination practice in hands-on.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls kita fermented → the book names injera and milk curd as fermented; kita is not.
- Thinks fortification is a home technique only → it works at commercial or household level; germination is the household's low-cost one.
