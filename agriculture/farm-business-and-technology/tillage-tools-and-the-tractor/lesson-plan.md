# Lesson plan — Tillage tools and the tractor

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Farm Business and Technology · **Lesson:** Tillage tools and the tractor
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Farm Business and Technology title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the tillage family in order — mouldboard plough and tillers for primary cultivation, disk harrow, cultivator and cultipacker for the seedbed — and the tractor that powers them
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Primary cultivation — opening the ground: The **tractor is the most popular and essential power source** of mechanised farming — the engine the tools hang on.
- Sub lesson 2 — Secondary tillage — making the seedbed: **Secondary tillage** prepares the seedbed: **disk harrows** break the opened ground; **cultivators remove weeds and pulverise and stir the soil before planting, often with rotary motion**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Mechanisation — power, gains and costs. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the tractor's place: the most popular and essential power source.
2. Separate primary cultivation (mouldboard ploughs, tillers) from secondary tillage (disk harrow, cultivator, cultipacker).
3. State each secondary tool's job, ending with the cultipacker's smooth, firm seedbed.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the baler among tillage tools → the baler is a harvesting machine.
- Thinks the cultipacker loosens soil → it does the opposite: crushes clods, presses stones, removes air pockets, firms the bed.
