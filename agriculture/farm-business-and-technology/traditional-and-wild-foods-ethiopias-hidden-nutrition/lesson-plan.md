# Lesson plan — Traditional and wild foods — Ethiopia's hidden nutrition

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Farm Business and Technology · **Lesson:** Traditional and wild foods — Ethiopia's hidden nutrition
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Farm Business and Technology title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous knowledge in nutrition-sensitive agriculture — mixed crop/livestock farming, dense traditional foods such as chuko and kocho, and mineral-rich wild foods worth bringing into cultivation
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — The farming Ethiopians already practise: Ethiopian farmers already practise **mixed crop/livestock farming, producing plant and animal foods for home and market**, with **organic vegetable and fruit production spreading** — nutrition-sensitive agriculture has local roots, not only new teaching.
- Sub lesson 2 — Wild foods — the gathered harvest: Wild foods add more: **bamboo shoots, stinging nettle and wild mushrooms of the north-west, central highlands and south-west are rich in minerals**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Enriching food — four household techniques; The Gedeo way. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the farming pattern: mixed crop/livestock farming for home and market, with organic vegetable and fruit production spreading.
2. Name chuko (barley flour with concentrated butter, Oromia) and kocho (enset, the south) and what they do in the diet.
3. Name three wild foods, their regions and their gift: mineral-rich; and the idea of domesticating edible wild plants.

## What you need
A notebook for the week's food diary in hands-on.
