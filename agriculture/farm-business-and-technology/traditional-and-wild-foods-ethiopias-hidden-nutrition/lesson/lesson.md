# Traditional and wild foods — Ethiopia's hidden nutrition

## Do it first
Some of Ethiopia's most nourishing foods never appear in a shop: a barley-and-butter food carried for journeys, enset in its many forms, greens gathered wild. Before they are named, list the traditional and wild foods of your own area — and guess what the shop-bought diet misses without them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The farming Ethiopians already practise
Ethiopian farmers already practise **mixed crop/livestock farming, producing plant and animal foods for home and market**, with **organic vegetable and fruit production spreading** — nutrition-sensitive agriculture has local roots, not only new teaching.

**Area-specific traditional foods carry dense nutrition: chuko — barley flour mixed with concentrated butter, common in Oromia — and enset in its many forms such as kocho in the south** complement staples and serve at ceremonies.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wild foods — the gathered harvest
Wild foods add more: **bamboo shoots, stinging nettle and wild mushrooms of the north-west, central highlands and south-west are rich in minerals**.

**Domesticating such edible wild plants in suitable agro-ecologies could widen diets further** — the gathered harvest brought into the garden. Only gather and eat wild foods your community knows well and identifies with certainty; the lesson's point is their value, and unknown plants are never food.

## Say it to your companion
Introduce chuko and kocho to your companion, then the three wild foods and where they grow.
