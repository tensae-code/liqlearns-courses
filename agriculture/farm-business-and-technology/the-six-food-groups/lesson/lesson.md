# The six food groups

## Do it first
Take yesterday's foods and sort them into piles by what each gives most. How many piles do you make? The book makes six — guess what holds the biggest pile in most Ethiopian homes before the groups are named.

## Sub lesson 1 — Grouped by what they carry most
**Foods are grouped by the nutrient they carry in abundance, into six groups**, tabled with their functions and Ethiopian examples.

**Staples** — cereal grains such as **sorghum, maize, barley and oats** — are **the base most people, especially the poor, eat most**. The staple pile is the biggest because energy must come first.

[Picture 26411 — a round plate divided into the six groups with Ethiopian foods in each]

## Sub lesson 2 — Fats and oils, and the other groups
**Fats and oils** — oilseeds such as **soybean, sesame, linseed and groundnut**, cooking oil and margarine — give **energy, essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamins**.

Beside them stand the **protein, legume, vegetable/fruit and animal-source groups** of the table — each group named for what it carries in abundance, so a day's eating can be checked group by group.

## Say it to your companion
Name the six groups for your companion, with one Ethiopian example for staples and three oilseeds for fats and oils.
