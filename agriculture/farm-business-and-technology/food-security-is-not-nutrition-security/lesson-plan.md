# Lesson plan — Food security is not nutrition security

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Farm Business and Technology · **Lesson:** Food security is not nutrition security
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Farm Business and Technology title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the careful difference between food security (enough food) and nutrition security (sufficient, safe and nutritious food, safe water and sanitation, health care and sound caring knowledge) — a household can have the first and lack the second
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — Enough food is not the whole test: **Food security in quantity alone is not enough.
- Sub lesson 2 — What nutrition security adds: **Nutrition security means access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food, safe water and adequate sanitation, the ability to reach health care, and knowledge of sound household and caring practices.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The six food groups. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State why quantity alone fails: a household can be food-secure and still malnourished.
2. List nutrition security's parts: sufficient, safe, nutritious food; safe water and sanitation; reachable health care; knowledge of sound household and caring practices.
3. Apply the difference to a real or imagined household.

## What you need
Two imaginary households to compare — same grain store, different meals.
