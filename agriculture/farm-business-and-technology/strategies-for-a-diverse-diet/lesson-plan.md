# Lesson plan — Strategies for a diverse diet

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** Farm Business and Technology · **Lesson:** Strategies for a diverse diet
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the Farm Business and Technology title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** dietary diversity and how to raise it — mixed cropping, integrated farming, new nutrient-rich crops, poultry and fish, vegetables and fruit — and how a diet itself may be modified
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — What dietary diversity is, and its Ethiopian gap: **Dietary diversity is the number of different foods or food groups eaten over a period.
- Sub lesson 2 — The strategies, and modifying the diet: The named strategies: **mixed cropping, integrated farming systems, introducing new crops of high nutritional importance, poultry and fish farming, and vegetable and fruit production**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Diversified food production — why variety matters. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define dietary diversity: the number of different foods or food groups eaten over a period.
2. Name five production strategies: mixed cropping, integrated farming, new nutrient-rich crops, poultry and fish, vegetables and fruit.
3. State four reasons a diet may be modified.

## What you need
A week's memory of your own meals.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks diversity counts only food groups in one meal → it counts different foods or groups over a period.
- Thinks staples make a diet diverse → Ethiopian diets rest on staples and diversity is low.
