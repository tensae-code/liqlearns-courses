# Food safety hazards — chemical, biological, physical

## Do it first
On one plate: a mould's poison you cannot see, a spray residue you cannot taste, a stone you can break a tooth on. Three families of danger — learn their surnames.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chemical hazards in detail
Hazards are classed, with **chemical hazards detailed**: **naturally occurring chemicals such as mycotoxins**; **intentionally added chemicals such as the preservative sodium nitrate**; and **unintentionally added chemicals such as pesticides** — pictured by a tractor spraying pesticide that can inadvertently find its way into the food we consume. A table of chemical hazard examples completes the family portrait.

[Picture 26682 — three hazard symbols over a plate of food]

## Sub lesson 2 — Biological and physical hazards
**Biological hazards** (living dangers — the germs) and **physical hazards** (objects that should not be in food) complete the scheme. Every food danger a handler meets belongs to one of the three families: chemical, biological, physical. Naming the family is the first step of blocking it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, using one real example from your own area for each sub lesson. Your companion asks one question about each part — answer from the lesson, not from memory of other lessons.

## Do It Yourself
1. Classify ten food dangers from your kitchen into the three families.
2. Draw the spraying tractor scene and trace the pesticide's unintended path to a plate.
3. Write why 'unintentionally added' is the hardest chemical group to see.
