## Sub lesson 1 — The eras, and Ethiopia's picture
Production moved through **eras**: hand work was given first to **draft animals**, then came **machinery (the Mechanical Era)**, then **agrochemicals (the Chemical Era)**, and today **irrigation, biotechnology and plant breeding**. Richer regions adopt the advanced technologies fastest — the eras did not arrive everywhere at once.

Ethiopia's farming is complex across many agro-ecologies. About **51 million hectares are arable**, but only about **a fifth is cultivated**, mostly by **smallholders**. Five crops — **teff, wheat, maize, sorghum and barley** — occupy about **75% of the cultivated area** and supply about **64% of calories**.

The weight behind those numbers: agriculture is the backbone of the country's economy — **over 40% of GDP, over 90% of foreign exchange and over 80% of employment** — and it determines the growth of all other sectors.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why yields lag
Yields have **risen over the decades** — the long record from 1961 to 2019 climbs — but they **stay low**. The reasons are plain: **soil degradation**, **traditional low-technology farming**, **variable rainfall**, **very little irrigation**, and **low use of improved seed, fertiliser and pesticides**.

Read the two halves together: the land is there, the crops are there, the yields rise — and the gap between what the land could give and what it gives is the work of this whole course: soil, water, seed and care.
