# Words for farming — shifting, settled, subsistence, commercial

## Do it first
Look at the fields nearest your home. Some are farmed every year. Some were farmed, then left. Some feed only one family. Some feed a town. Before any word is named, sort the fields you know into groups — by how the land is used, and by who eats the harvest.

## Sub lesson 1 — From hunting to settled farming
Long ago, people lived by **hunting and gathering** — taking what wild land gave. That life still survives where land and climate do not allow farming. **Agriculture** began when people settled and started the deliberate growing of crops and rearing of animals on purpose, season after season, in one place.

**Shifting cultivation** is the oldest farming step: a family clears a piece of land, farms it for a few seasons, then — as the yield falls — abandons it and moves on to clear a new piece. The old plot rests and recovers while nobody farms it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Settled, subsistence and commercial farming
**Settled agriculture** is the opposite habit: the **same plot is used continuously**, and the farmer raises its yield with practices such as manure, fertiliser, soil bunds and careful water use, instead of moving on.

Farming also sorts by who eats. **Subsistence farming** produces **only for the household** — any small surplus is stored or sold. **Commercial farming** produces **for the market**: the harvest is grown to be sold. One farm can lean either way; the words name the purpose, not the size of the family.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the four words to your companion using one real field for each: shifting, settled, subsistence, commercial. Your companion asks: what happens to the plot, and who eats the harvest?

## Do It Yourself
1. Walk your area and list four fields, one for each farming type you can find; write the clue that told you.
2. Ask an elder how one nearby plot was used thirty years ago. Was it shifting or settled then? Write what changed.
3. Write two sentences: one harvest that is subsistence, one that is commercial, from your own family's life.
