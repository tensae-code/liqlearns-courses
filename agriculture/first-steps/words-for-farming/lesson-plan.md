# Lesson plan — Words for farming — shifting, settled, subsistence, commercial

**Course:** Agriculture · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Words for farming — shifting, settled, subsistence, commercial
**Place in the curriculum:** Agriculture — the First Steps title (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four farming types — shifting cultivation, settled agriculture, subsistence farming and commercial farming — and how each one uses land
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 11 depth — it stands on the learner's own farm and fields, and on the course's earlier titles where named below.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
- Sub lesson 1 — From hunting to settled farming: Long ago, people lived by **hunting and gathering** — taking what wild land gave.
- Sub lesson 2 — Settled, subsistence and commercial farming: **Settled agriculture** is the opposite habit: the **same plot is used continuously**, and the farmer raises its yield with practices such as manure, fertiliser, soil bunds and careful water use, instead of moving on.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Nothing — this lesson opens the course; it stands on the learner's own home fields.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define agriculture as the deliberate growing of crops and rearing of animals.
2. Tell shifting cultivation from settled agriculture by what happens to the plot.
3. Tell subsistence farming from commercial farming by who the harvest is for.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes shifting with settled → ask one question: does the farmer move on, or stay and feed the same plot? Moving on is shifting; staying and feeding is settled.
- Mixes subsistence with commercial → ask who the harvest is for: the household's own pot, or the market's buyer?
