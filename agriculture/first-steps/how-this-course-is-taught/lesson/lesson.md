# How this course is taught

## Do it first
This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only. Read it as the course's teaching method made visible: how a Grade 12 Agriculture lesson is built, run and checked — before any single enterprise lesson begins.

## Sub lesson 1 — Student-centered learning, the teacher redeployed
The guide's doctrine: **student-centered learning does not diminish the teacher — it redeploys the teacher's expertise** into designing activities, organising groups and judging learning, with **whole-class lesson presentation used deliberately** rather than by default. The guide exists to help teachers plan, organise and deliver the course's activities.

## Sub lesson 2 — The lesson skeleton — competencies to self-check
Each unit guide follows one skeleton: **Competencies first**; the content overview; a teaching-learning process built on **brainstorming openings, group work (buzz groups, pair work), field and farm visits and presentations**; **'Answer to the Activities' keys**; and a closing **'Can you: Yes/No' self-check list** per lesson or unit, judged continuously.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 11 guide's way — the same skeleton, and the class keeps leaving the classroom (teacher guide — evidence)
The Grade 11 guide scripts every lesson the same way: each of its 16 units opens with a **Unit Introduction**, then treats every student-book section as a lesson on one skeleton — **Learning Competencies; Contents; an Overview restating the science; a Teaching-Learning Process** (suggested methods — brainstorming, question-and-answer, group discussion, presentation — then **Pre-Lesson Preparation, gathering materials from the school pedagogical centre**, then the lesson itself: **a) an introduction by brainstorming questions, b) the body, with assessment, additional questions and answer keys, including worked answers to the student book's exercises**); and a **Unit Assessment** at the unit's end, judged continuously against the competencies. Field work is structural, not decorative: pupils are sent to **nearby farms, farmers, markets, agricultural offices, veterinary clinics and processing factories** and must return with **reports shared with the whole class**, and the **school backyard is treated as a practice farm**.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Grade 11 methods library (teacher guide — evidence)
The Grade 11 guide's front matter defines the shared toolkit its unit lessons then invoke: **suggested instructional materials** (laboratories, farms, pictures, YouTube videos, ICT facilities), chosen against school facilities, material availability, time and teacher preference; **discussion built on challenging, argumentative questions with everyone participating**; **presentation tied to group and field work**; **guest speakers**, who make lessons interactive with answers no textbook or video gives; **group work capped at seven students, mixed fast/medium/slow learners**; teaching pupils to **take notes from the textbook and references**; **feedback on field and group work given as presentations**; and **assessment of both knowledge/understanding and underpinning skills, used to improve teaching, applied continuously**.
