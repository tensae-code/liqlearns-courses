# What Physical Education Is and Where It Came From

## Sub lesson 1 — PE defined — and ancient Greece

**Physical education** is an education field, practised in theory and activity in school, that **develops the whole person**. Learning it serves **health, work ability, mental development and effectiveness** — and it asks two things of the learner: strength of spirit and persistent participation.

In ancient history, **Greece holds the biggest place**: PE was given for military service, to build strength, and sport competitions were held regularly as part of religious festivals.

## Sub lesson 2 — "Modern" PE — from the feudal age to today

"Modern" physical education begins at the **transition from the feudal age to the capitalist age, at the end of the 16th and the start of the 17th century**, and runs to today. Across those centuries it changed in content and method as its military role grew — and it is **still being revised to fit pupils**.

That last line matters: the subject you are learning is not finished. It is fitted, again and again, to the learners in front of it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Four words, four growths (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 5 teacher guide takes the subject's name apart, word by word:

1. **ጤና** — health; 2. **ሰውነት** — the body, the person; 3. **ማጎልመሻ** — development, building up; 4. **ትምህርት** — education. HPE is **the education that develops the whole person through movement**.

And it names the **four growths** the subject gives: **physical growth** (strong muscles and bones; heart and lungs working well), **mental growth** (thinking and deciding through games), **social growth** (playing and competing with others builds relationships), and **motor and skill development** — the book's own English gloss — nerves and muscles working together in skilled movement.

## Sub lesson 4 — The four purposes — the Grade 6 pass

The Grade 6 book splits the subject's aims in four: **balanced physical development, skill development, mental development, and social-relations development** — building a healthy, strong citizen, with recreation and competition, and producing famous athletes along the way.

The Grade 6 teacher guide states the same four as purposes, each defined: the **physical** purpose strengthens muscles so the body's parts work well; the **mental** purpose grows mental capacity through fitness; the **social** purpose develops relationship with others; the **nerve-and-skill** purpose grows nerve–muscle coordination through varied activity.
