# The Ball Games and Their Stories — Football, Volleyball, Basketball, Handball

## Do it first

Four ball games fill the school field: football, volleyball, basketball, handball. Which is oldest? Which reached Ethiopia first? Guess now — the lesson answers in each game's own three scopes: world, Africa, Ethiopia.

## Sub lesson 1 — Football's story — world, Africa, Ethiopia

**World:** ancient games came first — Japan's game, Greek **Episkyros** and Roman **Harpastum** on a rectangular marked area — and the modern game grew from **England and school soccer**. **FIFA was founded on 21 May 1904** and runs the World Cup. The guide dates the game's proper shape to **England, 1863**, with John Charles Thring's first organised rules.

**Africa:** the **first Africa Cup of Nations was played in February 1957 in Khartoum** — Sudan, Egypt and Ethiopia (South Africa disqualified) — and CAF was founded alongside it. The guide adds **Ghana's Cape Coast Excelsior (1903), Africa's oldest surviving football club**.

**Ethiopia:** clubs played at **Teferi Mekonnen School as early as 1927**. Ethiopia **beat Tunisia and the United Arab Republic (Egypt) to become African champions in 1962 — its only title — with goal-scorer Mengistu Worku**. The federation line runs: **EFF founded 1943, FIFA membership 1953, CAF 1957; Saint George FC the leading club, with 29 titles** (guide).

## Sub lesson 2 — Volleyball's story — Mintonette to Ethiopia 1943

**World:** volleyball was **created by William G. Morgan, a YMCA instructor in Holyoke, Massachusetts, in 1895**, as **'Mintonette'** — a recreation game for his classes of businessmen; in 1896 it was renamed **volleyball**, because the object is to volley the ball back and forth over a net. Olympic programme from **1964**.

**Africa:** volleyball entered through **Egypt in 1915** — a founding member of the FIVB in 1947; the African Volleyball Confederation (CAVB) followed in 1972.

**Ethiopia:** introduced **around 1943 by foreign teachers**, played as recreation in schools, universities and the military; the **Ethiopian Volleyball Federation was established in 1954**, and Ethiopia joined the FIVB in 1964.

## Sub lesson 3 — Basketball's story — peach baskets to Addis Ababa 1950

**World:** basketball was begun by **Dr James Naismith, PE director at Springfield, Massachusetts** — played first with a **soccer ball** and **peach baskets, used until 1906**, then metal hoops with backboards. Dribbling was not part of the original game — passing was the only ball movement — and the now-standard **orange ball arrived only in the late 1950s**, introduced by Tony Hinkle so players and spectators could see it better.

**Africa:** the African Basketball Federation (AFABA) was founded in Egypt, with Ethiopia among the countries involved in its establishment.

**Ethiopia:** introduced in **1946–47 by PE teachers who came from Canada**; **Ethiopia joined FIBA in 1949** and holds **Sub-Saharan Africa's longest basketball tradition**; from **1950 basketball grew famous in Addis Ababa primary and secondary schools**, with inter-school games.

## Sub lesson 4 — Handball's story — ancient games to the IHF

**World:** handball-style games reach back to antiquity — Greek **'Urania'** and Roman **'Harpaston'**. Its modern roots are German, Danish and Swedish: Haandbold, Hazena and Torball evolved side by side in the late 19th century. Field handball appeared at the **Berlin 1936 Olympics**; the modern indoor game debuted at **Munich 1972**. The governing body formed in 1928 was renamed the **IHF in Copenhagen, 1946** — today 209 member federations.

**Africa:** the **African Handball Confederation (CAHB) was founded on 15 January 1973** in Lagos, Nigeria, its motto **'let's build African handball together'**.

**Ethiopia:** handball was **introduced in 1960 E.C. by Addis Ababa University instructors**, spreading first among the police, military and other organised sections; its men's premier league plays today.
