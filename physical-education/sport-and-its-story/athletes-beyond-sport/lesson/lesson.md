# Athletes Beyond Sport — What Champions Give Back

## Do it first

A champion wins a medal. Then what? Fame and money can sit still — or they can build hotels, open hospitals, train children and create jobs. The books follow Ethiopia's champions *after* the finish line. This lesson does too.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kenenisa's and the athletes' wider roles — the Grade 6 tasks

The Grade 6 book does not stop at races. For **Kenenisa Bekele**, its tasks ask learners to report on his **investment and social participation** beyond promoting Ethiopia; for **Meseret Defar**, the chapter activity asks the same — her **results and her social role**.

The question itself is the lesson: a champion's story has a second half, and the book sends you to research it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bikila and Dibaba — what their fame gave back (teacher guide — evidence)

**Abebe Bikila.** After his barefoot marathon victory at **Rome 1960**, his fame brought an agreement with the sportswear maker **Adidas** (the guide's spelling: "addidas"), bringing goods and support to Ethiopian athletes; and his story — told even in film — **made athletes**: the country's children followed his example into sport.

**Tirunesh Dibaba.** Her sport brought her into athletics itself and the **hotel business**; she **created jobs for more than 300 workers**, pays high taxes that support the economy, and after her wins in China the **"Tirunesh Dibaba Hospital"** was built in her name in **Kaliti sub-city**, fully funded. She also **opened an athletics training centre in her birth area** and helps young athletes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Athletes invest, employ and serve — the Grade 7 account

The Grade 7 book states the pattern plainly: **most Olympic athletes do charity and help work**. Its §1.5 teaches that athletes **invest fame and money in development — hotels, trade, farming, industry, import/export, sports centres — creating jobs**.

Its tasks send you out: research **local sportspeople's charity**, and national athletes' **investments, jobs created and wealth**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Sport legends at work — Haile, Derartu, Mengistu (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's method is printed as an instruction to the teacher: **focus on the legends' job creation, social and political affairs rather than their achievements in sport competition**.

**Haile Gebreslasie (1973– )** of Asela, Arsi: an entrepreneur who "believes in action"; **thousands of jobs** in hotel tourism, import and export trade, coffee farming and mining; one of the country's large taxpayers; founder of the **Great Ethiopian Run**; president of the Ethiopian Athletics Federation; UN goodwill ambassador; member of the national council of elders.

**Derartu Tulu (1972– )** of Bekoji: the first Ethiopian athlete and first black African woman to win Olympic gold, at 10,000 m; **thousands of jobs** in hotel tourism and other sectors; president of the EAF and member of the National Olympic Committee; UN goodwill ambassador for the empowerment of women; a meaningful role in peace building.

**Mengistu Worku (1940–2010)**: among Ethiopia's best footballers in history, famous for the **1962 African Nations Cup final**; Saint-George from 1957 for his whole career; afterwards he **coached the national team and different clubs** and took part in social and political life.

## Sub lesson 5 — Sport and money — athletes who invest and employ

Sport is defined in the Grade 9 book as **an organised competitive form of play** — and it builds economies: events benefit the **kebele, the woreda, the city and the nation**. The book pictures athlete investors: **Kenenisa Bekele and Tirunesh Dibaba**, investing in the **hotel industry and others**; **Naomi Osaka**, Sweetgreen's youngest investor (May 2021); and **Didier Drogba**, in Phoenix Rising and banks.

The Grade 9 teacher guide works the same truth at street level: at an event, **taxi owners earn from travellers, hotels earn from visiting teams and fans, organisers and sponsors gain too** — and top athletes earn foreign currency and invest it at home, **creating jobs**.

## Sub lesson 6 — Athletes who give back — schools, hospitals and peace

The Grade 10 book profiles humanitarian champions: **Sadio Mané**, who **financed schools and a hospital in his native Senegal** and donated football kits to poor children; **Didier Drogba**, who served on **Côte d'Ivoire's 11-member truth and reconciliation commission** after the 2011 civil war and founded the **Didier Drogba Foundation (2007)**; **Manuel Neuer**, whose kids' foundation works for child welfare and rights; and **Marcus Rashford**, whose charity fed vulnerable children and families during COVID-19.

The Grade 10 teacher guide adds **George Weah** of Liberia — a football star who helped end a long civil war, then entered politics and served as president — and Ethiopia's own: the **Great Ethiopian Run, funded by Haile Gebrselassie**, donating to charity every year, and the **two schools Haile founded**.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the Grade 7 research task: one local sportsperson's charity or help work — who, what, for whom.
2. Make a two-column card for one champion: races on the left, works on the right. Which column is longer?
3. Count jobs: the guide says Dibaba created jobs for more than 300 workers. What workplaces near you employ 300 people?
