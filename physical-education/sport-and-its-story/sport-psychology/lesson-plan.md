# Lesson plan — Sport Psychology

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Sport and Its Story · **Lesson:** Sport Psychology
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8 — Sport and Its Story (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sport psychology: mind, motivation, emotions, anxiety and arousal — and how they shape performance — as Samoa's Years 11–12 teacher guide teaches them: personality, motivation, goal setting, anxiety and arousal, relaxation and visualisation, and stress management.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Mind and performance** — sports psychology's first pass: personality, motivation, goal setting, anxiety and arousal.
2. **Calming and motivating yourself** — relaxation and visualisation, stress management, and what motivates regular participation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Sport Is — and How It Differs from PE; How Training Works. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the guide's mind-and-performance topics: personality, motivation, goal setting, anxiety and arousal.
2. Name the guide's calming tools: relaxation and visualisation, with stress management.
3. Say the guide's participation question: what motivates students to take part in regular physical activity.

## If the learner is stuck
- Anxiety and arousal blur together → the guide names them together: arousal is how worked-up you are; anxiety is the worry that can ride on it.
- Visualisation sounds like daydreaming → anchor it in the guide: it is a taught tool, paired with relaxation, for managing stress and preparing performance.
