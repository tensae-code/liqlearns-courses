# Sport for Everyone

## Do it first

A champion sprinter uses a wheelchair. A swimmer cannot see the pool wall. What stops a disabled person from doing sport — their body, or the world around them? This lesson teaches Cyprus's answer: sport is for everyone.

## Sub lesson 1 — What disability is — the WHO view and two models

The Cyprus book opens by asking **what disability is**, and teaches it in the framing of the **World Health Organization (WHO)**.

It then sets two ways of seeing disability side by side. In the **medical model**, disability is placed in the person's body — a condition to be treated or fixed. In the **social model**, disability is placed in the world around the person — in attitudes, barriers and arrangements that shut the person out. The same person, the same body: the two models point at two different problems, and they call for two different answers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stereotypes, prejudice and the right to equal sport

The book names the social barriers directly: **stereotypes** — fixed ideas about what disabled people can and cannot do — and **prejudice** — judging a person by those ideas before knowing them. Stereotypes and prejudice are barriers to sport just as steps and locked doors are.

Against them the book sets a right: **disabled people have the right to equal sport** — to take part in physical education and sport on an equal footing. That right is the ground of **inclusive sport** and of **Paralympic sport**, where athletes with disabilities compete at the highest level.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the difference between the medical and social models in one sentence each, then state the right this lesson ends on: disabled people's right to equal sport.
