# Lesson plan — What Physical Fitness Is

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Sport and Its Story · **Lesson:** What Physical Fitness Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 8 — Sport and Its Story (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the knowledge that opens the fitness line: physical fitness is the body's capacity foundation, in four named parts — heart and blood-vessel working, muscle strength, stretching and flexibility, and agility — and what it protects: heart, lungs, blood vessels, body shape and healthy weight.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's own body and school day. It is a knowledge lesson — the movement lessons of Fitness and Stretching are its neighbours, not its parts.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The four parts of fitness (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 1 teacher guide's fitness chapter opening: fitness is the body's capacity foundation for this subject; its four components named; because children sit much of the school day in class, fitness activity must be built into their play.
2. **What fitness protects (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 3 teacher guide's fitness chapter introduction: fitness activity protects the child's health — the five printed benefits.

3. **What fitness is — the Grade 5 definition** — the Grade 5 student book's concept section: fitness is a person's ability to carry out daily tasks competently — the knowledge base the whole fitness chapter builds on.
4. **Two kinds of fitness — health and skill** — the Grade 6 student book's framing: health-related fitness, which keeps the body healthy and supports daily work, and skill-related fitness, which prepares a competitor for events.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: none — this is an opening knowledge lesson of Sport and Its Story. The fitness movement lessons sit in Fitness and Stretching.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four components of physical fitness the guides name.
2. Say why fitness activity must be built into children's play: children spend much of the school day sitting in class.
3. List the five health benefits the Grade 3 guide prints: a protected heart, healthy lungs and breathing organs, healthy blood vessels, a well-proportioned body, healthy body weight.

4. Define fitness in the Grade 5 student book's own words — this lesson now stands on pupil-taught text as well as teacher-guide evidence.
5. Tell health-related fitness from skill-related fitness and say whom each is for.

## What you need
Nothing but your own body and a school day to think about.

## If the learner is stuck
- Fitness read as 'sport' → return to the guide's word: capacity. Sport uses fitness; fitness is the body's ability underneath.
- Components blur together → one gesture each: hand on heart (heart/blood), flexed arm (muscle), reach (stretch), quick side-step (agility).
