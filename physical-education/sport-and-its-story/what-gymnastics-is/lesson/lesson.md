# What Gymnastics Is and Why We Do It

## Do it first

Roll on the mat. Balance on one leg. Jump and stretch in the air. Three different movements — but one family holds them all. What is that family? And why do schools teach it to every child? This short lesson answers before the Gymnastics lessons begin.

**What gymnastics is.** In these books, the gymnastics chapter (ጅምናስቲክስ) teaches the body's basic trained movements: the **positions** (standing, sitting, lying), **rolls**, **balances**, and **jumps and turns** — done in order, with control, most of them on a mat. The Grade 3 book gives the gymnastics line its first **knowledge section**: what gymnastics is, and what its movements give the body, before the movements themselves.

**Why we do it.** The chapters that teach the movements state their gifts as they go: the basic movements **keep body and mind healthy**; **balance** is controlling the body standing, lying, walking and running — and its benefit is **not falling and breaking a bone**; the calisthenics sets develop the **arm, shoulder and chest muscles**. Gymnastics is the body learning to govern itself in every position.

**What comes next.** First the basic gymnastics movements, then **number gymnastics — የቁጥር ጅምናስቲክስ** — sequences done to counts. Those are movement lessons, in the Gymnastics title. This lesson is their door.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what gymnastics is and one thing its movements give the body. Then name the lesson family that teaches the movements themselves.

## Do It Yourself

1. Try the door's three shapes safely on a mat: a sideways roll, a one-leg balance with arms out, a stretched jump. Which needs most control?
2. Watch any gymnastics you can — a class, a picture strip — and sort what you see: position, roll, balance, jump, turn.
3. Write the chapter's road in order: the knowledge lesson, basic movements, number gymnastics.
