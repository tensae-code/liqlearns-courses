# Lesson plan — Physical Literacy and Lifelong Activity

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Sport and Its Story · **Lesson:** Physical Literacy and Lifelong Activity
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 8 — Sport and Its Story (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Physical literacy and lifelong activity — the motivation, confidence, competence and understanding to be active for life, and why physical activity matters.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Physical literacy** — the Namibia guide: physical literacy as motivation, confidence, competence and understanding for lifelong activity.
2. **Why physical activity matters** — the Namibia guide: the importance of physical activity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Physical Education Is and Where It Came From; What Physical Fitness Is. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what physical literacy is: motivation, confidence, competence and understanding for lifelong activity.
2. Name the four parts of physical literacy — motivation, confidence, competence and understanding.
3. Say why physical activity matters, in the guide's own framing: it is the foundation of being active for life.

## What you need
Nothing — a knowledge lesson.

1. **Do it first** — the learner names one activity they feel confident doing and one they avoid, and guesses what makes the difference, before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).

3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson to a companion in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- The four parts blur → anchor each one: want to (motivation), dare to (confidence), can do (competence), know why (understanding).
- Lifelong activity sounds abstract → name one activity a person can keep doing at every age.
