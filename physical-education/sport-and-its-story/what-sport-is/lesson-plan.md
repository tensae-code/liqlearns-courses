# Lesson plan — What Sport Is — and How It Differs from PE

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Sport and Its Story · **Lesson:** What Sport Is — and How It Differs from PE
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 8 — Sport and Its Story (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what sport is — organised competitive play with its own rules, where a winner and a loser are decided — and how it differs from physical education, which is an educational process for fitness, not necessarily for competition.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What Physical Education Is and Where It Came From in this title.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sport defined — competitive play with its own rules** — the Grade 6 student book: sport is competitive play with national and world rules and its own organisation; winner and loser are decided; sport sits inside PE.
2. **Sport's kinds and what sport does (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 6 teacher guide's list of sport's kinds and its account of what sporting activity does for the person and the country.
3. **PE and sport — same or different?** — the Grade 7 books compare them: different but closely linked, compared by participants, venue, aim and organisation; the teacher guide's Butcher definition of PE, and sport's three marks.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Physical Education Is and Where It Came From. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define sport in the books' words: competitive play done through physical activity, having its own rules and order.
2. Name sport's kinds as the books list them: football, volleyball, handball, basketball, throwing games, swimming, cycling and running races.
3. Say what sporting activity does for a person: keeps them growing, uses free time healthily, produces athletes who represent the country, and creates jobs.
4. Tell PE from sport: PE is instruction in exercises and games in schools, solely for physical and health fitness; sport is an activity requiring prowess or skill, often competitive.

## If the learner is stuck
- Everything looks like sport → apply the three marks: rules? organisation? a decided winner? All three, or it is play or PE.
- Everything looks like PE → ask for the aim: fitness and learning, or a competitive result?
