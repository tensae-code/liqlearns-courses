# Lesson plan — The Olympic Games, Ancient and Modern

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Sport and Its Story · **Lesson:** The Olympic Games, Ancient and Modern
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 8 — Sport and Its Story (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Olympic Games from Olympia in 776 BC to Athens 1896 and today: their founding, their flag of five rings, their motto — Faster, Higher, Stronger — the three bodies that run them, and their principles of peace, friendship and unity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What Sport Is in this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **776 BC to Athens 1896 — the founding story** — the Grade 5 book's origin and growth of the Games, with the Grade 5 teacher guide's Olympia-to-Athens account (teacher guide — evidence for its part).
2. **The flag, the rings and the motto** — the Grade 6 book's flag, emblem and motto: a white flag, five interlaced rings in blue, yellow, black, green and red, standing for the five participating continents; the motto Citius–Altius–Fortius.
3. **The three bodies that run the Games (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 6 teacher guide: the Olympic movement's three bodies — the IOC, the international federations (IFs) and the national Olympic committees (NOCs).
4. **The Olympic principles — peace, friendship, unity** — the Grade 7 book's philosophy and principles, with the Grade 7 teacher guide's six designed principles: non-discrimination, sustainability, humanism, universality, solidarity, and linking sport with culture and education.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Sport Is — and How It Differs from PE. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the founding story: Games at Olympia from 776 BC, every four years, five days, honouring Zeus; the name from Olympia.
2. Name the modern founder, Baron Pierre de Coubertin; the IOC founded 16 June 1894; the first modern Games at Athens in 1896.
3. Draw the Olympic flag and say what the five rings stand for; say the motto in its own words: Citius–Altius–Fortius, Faster–Higher–Stronger.
4. Name the movement's three bodies — IOC, IFs, NOCs — and the six Olympic principles from the Grade 7 guide.

## What you need
Paper and colours for the flag. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Dates tangle → use the five-sentence arc in Say It; dates hang on the story, not the reverse.
