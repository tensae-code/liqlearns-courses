## Sub lesson 1 — Timed running — Grade 5: beating your own time

Three markers stand at 5-metre spacing. Run 20 metres against the clock, using different start methods, and run the 20–40 metre speed run. The main aim is not beating someone else — it is improving the time over the distance you run. Note your time, rest, and run again to beat it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Running to the clock — middle distance (teacher guide — evidence)

Middle-distance running keeps an even pace to the clock. Run slowly for 5 to 6 minutes: breathing even, pace steady, body upright, running in pairs first — one runs, one watches the form and corrects it — then in threes, for 6 to 8 minutes. Running is a natural movement done everywhere, so learn to run well on varied ground: mind the surface, your posture, your direction, and your balance.

## Sub lesson 3 — Distance running — Grade 6: 1500 and 2000 metres

Build distance by mixing paces. Run 1500 metres by alternating: 500 metres at a medium pace, with 20-metre marching breaks between the parts. Then run 2000 metres at a steady medium pace from start to finish — the distance runner's skill is holding the pace, not sprinting the start.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
