# Lesson plan — Throwing for Distance

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Athletics · **Lesson:** Throwing for Distance
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Athletics (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** throwing for distance in its safe taught forms — small ball and stick, ring and javelin-style, shot put, javelin and discus — always into a clear throwing area, never toward people.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own body and the movements they already do in play; the parts climb from the youngest book's teaching to the oldest book's teaching in this band (Grades 5–7 books; see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Throwing a small ball and a stick — Grade 5** — overarm throwing of a small ball for distance, and the standing stick throw — step, turn, throw, follow through, into a clear area.
2. **Ring and javelin-style throwing — Grade 6** — ring throwing from spread fingers in an open palm with a pre-throw turn, and javelin-style throwing — run-up, gathering the run's force, the spear pulled back then thrown forward past the line.
3. **Shot put — Grade 7: the five stages** — the shot put in five stages — grip and stance, pre-throw arm movement, glide or turn, throw, and recovery with balance.
4. **Javelin — Grade 7** — the javelin throw — grip, run-up, throw, and a balanced finish without crossing the line; a throw that does not stick point-first shows the technique was wrong.
5. **Discus — Grade 7** — the discus throw in its stages — grip and basic stance, pre-throw arm swing, turn, throw — beginning with the standing throw.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Open flat ground, a mat for any roll or floor work, and the equipment named in the parts (a ball, a rope, rings or cones where the part says so). Modest sports clothes. A partner or a small group for the partner parts.

## If the learner is stuck
- The movement breaks down → go back to the youngest part and do it slowly, on the spot, before travelling or adding equipment.
- A roll or balance feels unsafe → stop, use a mat, work beside a partner or teacher, and never copy a movement that hurts.
- The learner cannot name the movement → say the name aloud together while doing it slowly; the name and the movement are learned together.
