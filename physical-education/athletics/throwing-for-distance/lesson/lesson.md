# Throwing for Distance

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Throwing a small ball for distance develops your throwing skill — throw overarm: step, turn, throw, and follow through, sending the ball far into the throwing area. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Throwing a small ball and a stick — Grade 5

Throwing a small ball for distance develops your throwing skill — throw overarm: step, turn, throw, and follow through, sending the ball far into the throwing area. Then throw the stick (ዘንግ መወርወር), the javelin-form throw, beginning standing still (በመቆም ዘንግ መወርወር): hold the stick ready, throw, and follow through. Strict safety: throw only on the signal, only into the clear throwing area, and collect only when everyone has thrown.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ring and javelin-style throwing — Grade 6

The ring (quoits) throw: hold the ring on spread fingers in an open palm, swing the arm and turn before the throw, the way a discus turns. Javelin-style throwing: run up, gathering the run's force; the spear is pulled back, then thrown forward; finish past the throwing line without crossing it, balanced.

## Sub lesson 3 — Shot put — Grade 7: the five stages

The shot put has five stages: 1. grip and stance, the shot held at the neck; 2. the pre-throw arm movement; 3. the glide or turn across the circle — the side glide is the pictured form; 4. the throw; 5. recovery, keeping your balance and staying in the circle. Practise the stages in order, then put them together in a class throwing competition. Shot training builds the upper body's throwing strength.

## Sub lesson 4 — Javelin — Grade 7

The javelin (ጦር) is an individual event needing upper-body and arm strength, like the shot. Take the grip, run up carrying the javelin, throw, and keep your balance without crossing the line. The javelin tells you the truth: if it does not land sticking point-first, the technique was wrong — practise the part that failed and correct it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Discus — Grade 7

The discus stages: 1. grip and basic stance, the discus held in the spread fingers; 2. the pre-throw arm swing; 3. the turn or spin; 4. the throw. Begin with the standing throw, with no turn, until the grip and the release are right; then add the turn.

## Sub lesson 6 — Shot put — the glide — Grade 8

Throwing is one branch of athletics — shot, discus, javelin and hammer are its family. The shot is put with the linear, backward-glide technique (መስመራዊ ስልት): stand with feet apart and the non-throwing arm raised; glide one step back on the left leg, leaning the trunk forward; then turn and put the shot away from your neck with a straightening arm. Practise the glide first without the shot, then with a light one, always inside the circle and toward an empty field.

## Sub lesson 7 — Discus — Grade 8

The discus throw is one unbroken movement, taught in stages. Learn the grip and the basic stance first. Then the arm swing that gathers force — first mimed without the discus, then holding it — growing into the turning throw and the release. The throw asks for strength, balance, coordination and speed together, and it builds all four. Throw only on the teacher's signal, from the circle, and fetch the discus together when everyone has thrown.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
