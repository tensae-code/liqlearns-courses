# Jumping for Distance and Height

## Sub lesson 1 — Long jump — Grade 5: run-up, take-off, flight, landing

The long jump is taught in its real parts. The run-up (መንደርደር): run at high speed to the take-off line. Take off on one foot. In the air, move the arms and legs to win a better distance. Land on two feet in the pit. Practise the parts, then jump from the full run-up, and organise the jump in competition form.

## Sub lesson 2 — Triple jump — Grade 6: hop, step, jump

The triple jump (የስሉስ ዝላይ) is hop, step, jump. Build it slowly: from standing, jump forward on one leg and land on the same leg — that is the hop. The step lands on the opposite leg. The last part is the jump, landing on two feet. Practise one-leg horizontal jumps first, then join hop, step and jump in order.

## Sub lesson 3 — High jump beginnings (teacher guide — evidence)

Jumping, like running and throwing, is a natural movement, used to pass obstacles, and the skill feeds many sports. The high jump's parts are: the run-up, taking off (on one foot), clearing the obstacle, and landing. Warm up first; practise jumping up with no obstacle; then clear a rope held at 50 to 70 centimetres, raising the height as your clearing and your landing control grow; land on soft ground.

## Sub lesson 4 — High jump — Grade 7: the scissors jump

High-jump movements help many sports and daily life. The scissors jump (የመቀስ ዝላይ) is taught fully: begin standing — jump and clear the bar with the legs crossing like scissors, one leg over, then the other; then take three run-up steps and jump the scissors at speed, clearing the bar and landing in control.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
