# Lesson plan — Controlling My Body

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Moving Basics · **Lesson:** Controlling My Body
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Moving Basics (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Controlling my body: moving body parts independently, controlling and stopping movements, and turning and spinning.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Controlling and stopping movements** — the El Salvador guide: controlling movement and holding one body segment still while others move.
2. **Different movements at the same time** — the El Salvador guide: moving left and right arms, hands, legs and feet differently at the same time.
3. **Bilateral efficiency** — the El Salvador guide: holding, moving, hitting, catching, throwing and driving implements — balls, hoops, batons, wheels, rackets — with both hands and feet.
4. **Independence of every part** — the El Salvador guide: independence of lower limbs from upper limbs in physical activity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Knowing My Body and How It Moves; Body and Space Awareness. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what controlling my body means: moving parts independently, and controlling and stopping movements.
2. Show the Grade 1 skill: hold one body segment still while the others move.
3. Say what Grade 2 adds: left and right sides moving differently at the same time.
4. Say what Grade 3 adds: implements worked with both hands and feet, and lower limbs independent of upper limbs.

## What you need
Nothing — your own body and a space to move in, as the guide teaches it.

## If the learner is stuck
- One side copies the other → slow right down — control is built slowly first, then speed comes.
- Stopping feels harder than starting → that is the lesson: a controlled movement is one you can stop, not only start.
