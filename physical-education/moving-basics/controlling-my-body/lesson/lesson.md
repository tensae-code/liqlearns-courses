# Controlling My Body

## Do it first

Move both arms together. Now move one arm and hold the other still. That difference — moving parts independently, and stopping when you choose — is the thing this lesson teaches. Read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Controlling and stopping movements

El Salvador's Grade 1 guide (Unit 1 Lesson 5, *Muevo mi cabeza, tronco y extremidades, y controlo mi cuerpo*) teaches **controlling movement**: move your head, trunk and limbs — and **hold one body segment still while the others move**. Control is not only starting a movement; it is stopping it and keeping part of you still on purpose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Different movements at the same time

In Grade 2 (Unit 1 Lesson 6, *Al mismo tiempo, diferentes movimientos*) the parts separate further: **left and right arms, hands, legs and feet move differently at the same time** — one side does one thing while the other side does another.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bilateral efficiency

In Grade 3 (Unit 1 Lesson 3, *¡Eficiencia bilateral!*) control reaches the implements: **holding, moving, hitting, catching, throwing and driving** them — **balls, hoops, batons, wheels, rackets** — **with both hands and feet**, not only the favourite side.

## Sub lesson 4 — Independence of every part

Grade 3 completes the line (Unit 1 Lesson 6, *¡En cada parte de mi cuerpo, maravillosa independencia!*): the **independence of the lower limbs from the upper limbs** in physical activity — legs and arms each doing their own work inside one activity.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the one thing this lesson teaches — controlling my body: moving body parts independently, controlling and stopping movements, and turning and spinning. — and name each sub lesson's part of it in order.
