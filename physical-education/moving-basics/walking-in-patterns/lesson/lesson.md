# Walking in Patterns

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Walk hand in hand with a partner, then in a group. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

[Picture 13500 — children walking hand in hand along a zigzag line]

## Sub lesson 1 — Walking hand in hand

Walk hand in hand with a partner, then in a group. Keep the hold. Your hands, legs and eyes work together — this is a travelling movement, a movement that carries your body from place to place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Walking the lines and shapes

Walk on a straight line. Walk round a rectangle. Walk round a circle. Walk on a zigzag line. Stay on the line and keep your balance.

## Sub lesson 3 — Walking while rolling a ball

Walk and roll a ball by hand at the same time. The ball stays close and your feet keep walking.

## Sub lesson 4 — Walking the line in balance (teacher guide — evidence)

Walk heel to toe along a straight line, arms out, and do not step off the line. This balance walk comes from the Grade 4 teacher guide.

## Sub lesson 5 — Walking tall — Grade 8 (teacher guide — evidence)

Walk tall, the way the Grade 8 guide teaches it. Keep your head up, with your chin parallel to the ground and your ears above your shoulders. Lengthen your back. Keep your shoulders down and back. Hold your middle firm, swing your arms, and step from heel to toe. Warm up and stretch for 5–10 minutes first, and cool down the same way after. Before any activity, say if you have a health problem — the guide asks this every time.

[Picture 13915 — walking tall — head up, shoulders down and back, heel-to-toe step]

## Sub lesson 6 — Walking drills that stretch you as you go — Grade 9 (teacher guide — evidence)

These walking drills are dynamic stretching — you stretch by moving. High knees: take an exaggerated step, drive the knee high and push up on the toes of the other foot; your arms stay bent at 90 degrees, hands rising to chin level and back past the rear pocket. Straight-leg kicks: kick up and touch your toes with the opposite hand — the first set at about three-quarter effort, the second set full. Then walk a forward lunge, and a side lunge, from one end of the ground to the other. Stretch only when your muscles are warm.

## Sub lesson 7 — Selame Selame — greet, pass the cloth, change places (teacher guide — evidence)

Movement first has directions: ወደፊትና ወደ ኋላ (forward and back), ወደ ቀኝና ወደ ግራ (to the right and to the left), and sliding to the side. The game that practises them is Selame Selame — ሰላሜ ሰላሜ: walk around in a circle, greet each other as you pass, and a scarf or cloth (ጨርቅ/ፎጣ) travels hand to hand around the ring. On the signal — change places! Everyone moves, greets, and settles somewhere new, and the cloth keeps travelling.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
