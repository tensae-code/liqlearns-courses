# Combined Travelling and On-the-Spot Movements

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: There are three families of movement: movements done in place, movements that carry the body from place to place, and movements done with objects. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three kinds of movement (teacher guide — evidence)

There are three families of movement: movements done in place, movements that carry the body from place to place, and movements done with objects. Show a movement without leaving your spot — that is movement in place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Combined travelling movements

Chain travelling skills together: pass through a ring, pass a ball backwards between the legs in a line, jump into a sack, jump forward in it, step out and run.

## Sub lesson 3 — Combined movements on the spot

Stay in your place and combine: bend one leg and hold the stretched leg with both hands; reach sideways to touch a stretched leg; run between numbers on the ground, put your foot on each number, and return.

## Sub lesson 4 — Travelling and on-the-spot, chained together

Chain the two families in one sequence: travel, stop and work on the spot, then travel again. In Grade 4 this grows to rope jumping while walking, and holding a ball in two hands while jumping along a rectangle line beside a partner.

## Sub lesson 5 — What fundamental movement skills are — Grade 3 (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 3 chapter is called መሠረታዊ የእንቅስቃሴ ክህሎት — fundamental movement skills. A fundamental movement skill is the most-used capacity in a child's daily life: you use it at home, at school, and in social activities with others. Practising it bears two fruits: strong body parts, and social and psychological growth — growing with other people, and growing in your own mind.

The chapter has three parts, and you have met all three in this lesson: combined fundamental skills, skills done in place, and skills done with objects. By the end of the chapter you can show the main movements in games and in place, join movements together into one action, and use each body part as the task demands.

## Sub lesson 6 — How far can our team step? Hunter and Hunted (teacher guide — evidence)

**Distance stepping — ለርቀት ጠብ ጠብ ማለት.** Your team steps as far as it can, one learner at a time. The first learner steps out as far as they can and stops. The next learner starts exactly where the first one stopped, and steps on from there. Step by step, your team's total distance grows — no one's step is wasted, because every step begins on the last one's mark.

**Hunter and Hunted — አዳኝ እና ታዳኝ ጨዋታ.** One group hunts; the other group flees, inside the marked field only. When a hunter catches you, you do not sit out — you join the hunters, and the hunting group grows. The game ends when one free learner is left, and that last free learner is praised.

## Sub lesson 7 — First movement skills — the Grade 1 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)

Fundamental movement skills — መሠረታዊ የእንቅስቃሴ ክህሎት — are the base laid in childhood. Control of your body position, and the basics of health, are built by joining these movements in varied sport and social activities. The chapter's promises are three: show movement skills in varied places, perform movements in place, and move the body's parts in varied directions. Every later part of this lesson grows from this base.

## Sub lesson 8 — Moving in place — bend, stretch, keep your balance (teacher guide — evidence)

Some movements never leave your spot. Bend your body forward and down to touch your toes, then come back up slowly. Move one body part at a time — an arm, a leg, your head — while your feet stay planted, and keep your balance the whole time. The guide's picture shows children stretching toward the ground in a park: straight legs, long arms, slow and controlled.

## Sub lesson 9 — Moving my body well — the Grade 2 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)

In Grade 2 the same chapter opens wider: fundamental skills use the whole body's capacity — walking, running, jumping and movements with objects. The chapter's three skills are movement in place, movement that travels, and movement with objects. Its promises: move fast with a change of direction without falling, move in place inside a small space, and use your body parts together with partners.

## Sub lesson 10 — Moving in my own space — the Grade 2 in-place skills (teacher guide — evidence)

In-place movement skill — በቦታ ላይ በመሆን የመንቀሳቀስ ክህሎት. Stand inside a small marked space and move the small parts of your body: hands, feet, head. Everything is slow and controlled, and nothing travels — your spot is your whole field. This is how a crowded class can all move at once, safely.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
