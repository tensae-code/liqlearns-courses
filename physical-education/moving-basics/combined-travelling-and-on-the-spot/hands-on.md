# Hands-on — Combined Travelling and On-the-Spot Movements

The lesson with your body, on real ground, not only your eyes.

## Do the parts in order
Do each part of the lesson in order, slowly first: three kinds of movement (teacher guide — evidence), combined travelling movements, combined movements on the spot, travelling and on-the-spot, chained together.

## Teach it on
Pick one part. Name it, show it slowly, and teach it to a partner. Your partner does it; you watch for safety and for the right shape.

## Count and check
Do the main movement ten times, counting aloud. Stop if anything hurts. Rest, breathe, and say what the movement trained.

## If it is hard
Do the youngest part only, on the spot, at walking speed, beside a partner or a wall for balance. Smaller and slower is still the lesson.

## Teacher-guide parts (added depth)
- **First movement skills — the Grade 1 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence):** do it slowly first — fundamental movement skills are the base laid in childhood — control of body position and health basics built by joining movements in varied activities. Stop if anything hurts; work beside a partner or teacher.
- **Moving in place — bend, stretch, keep your balance (teacher guide — evidence):** do it slowly first — bending forward to touch the toes and moving body parts without travelling, keeping balance. Stop if anything hurts; work beside a partner or teacher.
- **Moving my body well — the Grade 2 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence):** do it slowly first — fundamental skills using the whole body — moving fast with a direction change without falling, moving in a small space, and working with partners. Stop if anything hurts; work beside a partner or teacher.
- **Moving in my own space — the Grade 2 in-place skills (teacher guide — evidence):** do it slowly first — moving the hands, feet and head slowly and with control inside a small marked space, without travelling. Stop if anything hurts; work beside a partner or teacher.


## The Grade 3 teacher-guide parts

**The team distance chain.** In a line on open ground, the first learner steps out as far as they can and freezes. The next starts on that exact mark. Measure the team's total with a rope or counted paces, then try to beat it.

**Hunter and Hunted.** Mark a field with cones or stones. One small group hunts; everyone caught joins the hunters. Play until one free learner is left — and praise that learner, then swap the starting hunters and play again.
