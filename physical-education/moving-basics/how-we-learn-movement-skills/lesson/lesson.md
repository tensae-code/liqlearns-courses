# How We Learn Movement Skills

## Do it first

Think of a skill you practised until it got better — a throw, a stroke, a step. What actually made it better: something about how your body moved, something about your equipment, or something someone (or your own body) told you? Say your answer first — then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Biomechanics and moving well

Samoa's Years 11–12 teacher guide opens its human-movement unit with the **factors affecting movement and performance**. Two of them are about the movement itself and its tools: **biomechanical efficiency** — moving in the way that wastes the least effort, as in the guide's example of **the swimmer's arm recovery** — and **effective equipment**, equipment that helps the movement instead of fighting it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Feedback in skill learning

The same unit teaches **feedback in skill learning**, in three kinds: **intrinsic** feedback (what you sense from your own movement), **extrinsic** feedback (what comes from outside — a teacher, a result, a recording) and **kinaesthetic** feedback (what your body feels as it moves). A learner improves a skill by using this feedback, not by repeating the movement blindly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two factors from the first sub lesson and the three kinds of feedback from the second, as Samoa's guide teaches them.
