# Lesson plan — How We Learn Movement Skills

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Moving Basics · **Lesson:** How We Learn Movement Skills
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Moving Basics (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** How we learn movement skills: the factors that affect movement and performance — biomechanical efficiency and effective equipment — and the feedback (intrinsic, extrinsic and kinaesthetic) that skill learning runs on, as Samoa's Years 11–12 teacher guide teaches them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Biomechanics and moving well** — the guide's Unit 2A pass: biomechanical efficiency (the swimmer's arm recovery) and effective equipment as factors affecting movement and performance.
2. **Feedback in skill learning** — the guide's three kinds of feedback — intrinsic, extrinsic and kinaesthetic — in skill learning.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Running Basics; Combined Travelling and On-the-Spot Movements. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name two factors affecting movement and performance from the Samoa guide: biomechanical efficiency and effective equipment.
2. Give the guide's biomechanics example: the swimmer's arm recovery.
3. Name the three kinds of feedback in skill learning: intrinsic, extrinsic and kinaesthetic.

## What you need
No equipment is needed. Think of one skill you are learning — a swim stroke, a throw, a dance step — to test the ideas against.

## If the learner is stuck
- Biomechanics sounds big → start with the guide's own example: a swimmer's arm recovering over the water can waste effort or save it; that saving is biomechanical efficiency.
- The three feedbacks blur together → ask where the information comes from: inside you (intrinsic), outside you (extrinsic), or from the feel of the moving body itself (kinaesthetic).
