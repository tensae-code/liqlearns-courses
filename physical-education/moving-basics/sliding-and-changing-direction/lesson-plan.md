# Lesson plan — Sliding and Changing Direction

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Moving Basics · **Lesson:** Sliding and Changing Direction
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Moving Basics (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sliding sideways, and running fast while changing direction at markers and shapes.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own body and the movements they already do in play; the parts climb from the youngest book's teaching to the oldest book's teaching in this band (Grades 1–4 books; see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sliding sideways** — sliding sideways — step, close, step — left and right.
2. **Changing direction on shapes** — running the marked numbers, letters and rectangle and changing direction at the corners.
3. **Changing direction at speed, combined** — running fast, combined and correctly, changing direction without stopping.
4. **Turning at the cones (teacher guide — evidence)** — sprinting between cones and turning sharply at each cone.
5. **Change direction, roll to the target, obstacle walking (teacher guide — evidence)** — combined fundamental movement skill: changing direction at a signal without losing balance (wheelchair adaptation included), stepping and rolling to a target, and walking an obstacle line.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier titles on the ladder (Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games). Recommended, never forced — a learner may jump in here directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Do and explain: sliding sideways — step, close, step — left and right.
2. Do and explain: running the marked numbers, letters and rectangle and changing direction at the corners.
3. Do and explain: running fast, combined and correctly, changing direction without stopping.
4. Do and explain: sprinting between cones and turning sharply at each cone.
5. Change direction at a signal without losing balance, roll to a target on a mat, and walk an obstacle line.

## What you need
Open flat ground, a mat for any roll or floor work, and the equipment named in the parts (a ball, a rope, rings or cones where the part says so). Modest sports clothes. A partner or a small group for the partner parts.

## If the learner is stuck
- The movement breaks down → go back to the youngest part and do it slowly, on the spot, before travelling or adding equipment.
- A roll or balance feels unsafe → stop, use a mat, work beside a partner or teacher, and never copy a movement that hurts.
- The learner cannot name the movement → say the name aloud together while doing it slowly; the name and the movement are learned together.
