# Moving to a Beat — Rhythmic Movement

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Move in sequence to a beat, at low intensity. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

[Picture 13521 — children clapping overhead on the count]

[GIF 13522 — the counted clap sequence — 1-2-3-4 — keeping the beat]

## Sub lesson 1 — Simple sequences to a beat

Move in sequence to a beat, at low intensity. Clap overhead with right and left palms in turn, counting 1-2-3-4. Sit, beat the ground with both hands, then clap overhead. Face a partner hand in hand and fold and stretch the arms. March on the spot and touch foot to foot.

## Sub lesson 2 — Continuous sequences to music

Keep moving without stopping, at higher intensity, to music: stretch arm and leg to the side and return, hold your waist and stretch to the side, twist to different directions, jump on two feet forward and back, and jump left and right with feet together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Progressive sequences and equipment to music

Build a sequence step by step — first step, added step, full sequence — keeping the beat. Then time equipment work to the music: the ball, hoop or rope movement lands exactly on the beat.

## Sub lesson 4 — Equipment to music, and creating your own

Match the beat to the equipment: when you dribble, the ball's bounce is timed to the beat you hear; jump through circles on two feet to the music. Then create: creativity is making a new thing — invent your own beat movements, practise them, show them, and name your movement.

## Sub lesson 5 — Rhythmic gymnastics — Grade 5: a hoop to music

Rhythmic gymnastics (ሪትሚክ ጅምናስቲክስ) is a creative individual performance form. One pictured activity combines a ring or hoop (ቀለበት) with different music beats: the hoop's movements flow from one to the next, on the beat. Listen first, move second — the music leads and the body follows.

## Sub lesson 6 — Rhythmic gymnastics — Grade 6: ball, hoop and rope

Rhythmic gymnastics now works with three tools: a ball, a hoop (ቀለበት), and a rope or ribbon. It trains attention, the coordination of the body parts, enjoyment, and working together. To close the practice, perform hoop routines individually and in groups for the class.

## Sub lesson 7 — Movements that flow together — the Grade 1 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)

A young child's movement should not be broken or interrupted. Sequential movement — ተከታታይነት ያለው የእንቅስቃሴ ክህሎት — joins single movements together with music and children's games, so one movement flows into the next. The chapter has two parts: moving the body parts in sequence, and whole sequential movement. Its promise: join movements inside children's games and in social and cultural activities, without stopping.

## Sub lesson 8 — Marching to the beat, and the school song (teacher guide — evidence)

March in place to a steady beat, arms swinging — the guide's picture (ስዕል 2.1) names it አርምጪ, marching. While you march, the class sings the printed song "እንሒድ ትምህርት ቤት" — "Let's go to school". The song is a marching song: its beat carries the steps, and it teaches marching together, in time, on the way to school.

## Sub lesson 9 — The circle game — round dance in a ring (teacher guide — evidence)

Stand in a circle and hold the game together. A circle formation is sequential group movement: the ring turns, the play passes from child to child around the ring, and nobody's part comes alone — each move answers the one before it. Keep the circle round, keep the rhythm, keep everyone in.

## Sub lesson 10 — Movements in a chain — the Grade 2 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)

Sequential movements — ተከታታይነት ያላቸው እንቅስቃሴዎች — are unbroken chains: one movement follows another, to music and rhythm, without stopping. The Grade 2 chapter's parts are rhythmic sequential movement, changing sequential movement, and growth tasks that build step by step. Its promises: perform sequential movement to music, pass smoothly from one movement to the next, and keep the beat all the way through.

## Sub lesson 11 — Move and freeze; side bends (teacher guide — evidence)

Rhythmic repetition — መደበኛ የሆነ የምት ድግግሞሽ: the same movement again and again, to a beat. The Green-Green game — አረንጓዴ, አረንጓዴ: at the colour signal you move; at the stop signal you freeze in place, holding your body still and controlled — then free play with the same game. And side bends — ወደ ጎን መታጠፍ: both arms up, bend your trunk to the right with the left arm over, come back to the middle, bend to the left — in rhythm.

[Picture 34804 — stand, squat, stand under the red, yellow and green circles of the colour-call games]

## Sub lesson 12 — Counting catches; knee drives (teacher guide — evidence)

Rhythmic flow — ፍሰት ያለው የምት አማራጭ: choosing movements that fit a changing rhythm. The Number game — የቁጥር ጨዋታ: stand in two lines one metre apart; throw a ball so your partner catches it, and count the throws and catches together — the number is the score you build as a pair. Knee drives — ጉልበት ማንሳት (the guide's gloss: Knee-drive): lift one knee high, set it down, lift the other, in rhythm, travelling forward as you go.

## Sub lesson 13 — The circuit course; slow and controlled movements (teacher guide — evidence)

Progressive rhythm — ዕድገት ያለው የምት ድረጀ: tasks that grow harder in stages, each built on the last. The guide names the three kinds: መደበኛ (regular), ፍሰት ያለው (flowing) and ዕድገት ያለው (progressive) sequential movement. The Circuit — ዑደታዊ ጨዋታ: stations stand two metres apart; do the set movement at the first station, move to the second, then the third, arms and legs working together in balance. And the four control movements — አራቱ 'መ' በቁጥጥር: arms to the sides and down, legs opening and closing with the arms, turning the body on the spot — all slow, accurate, and to command.

## Sub lesson 14 — Dance, aerobics and movement chains — Grade 3 (teacher guide — evidence)

Sequential movement means performing varied movements one after another, without breaking, to music and rhythm. The chapter names its homes: ዳንስ — dance, አየሮዊ እንቅስቃሴ — aerobics, and ጅምናስቲክ — gymnastics. The beat of the music sets the movement. Sequential skill grows the way this lesson grows: start from simple movements, and join them.

By the chapter's end you can perform sequential movements accurately and show agility inside them — quick and light, without losing the chain.

## Sub lesson 15 — To the beat: in-place rhythm and arm-leg coordination (teacher guide — evidence)

This is ተከታታይ እድገት ያለው የምት ድረጀ — the progressive rhythmic sequence. Build it step by step: move each body part — head, arms, legs, trunk — in time; join two movements; then join more, keeping your posture and control all the way through. The practice balls are small balls of varied colours — ትናንሽ መጠንና የተለያዩ ቀለም ያላቸው ኳሶች.

**In-place rhythmic movements — በቦታ ላይ እየተንቀሳቀሱ የሚሠሩ እንቅስቃሴዎች.** Step in place to the beat. Move your arms with the beat. Turn your trunk left and right with the music's beat. Then combine them.

[GIF 34911 — in-place stepping with arm swings on the beat]

**Arm-and-leg coordination — እጅ እና እግር በማቀናጀት የሚሠሩ ተግባራት.** Opposite arm and leg move together to the beat — right arm with left leg, left arm with right leg. When the beat changes, the pattern changes with it.

[Picture 34910 — opposite arm and leg moving together to the beat]

## Sub lesson 16 — Follow the rhythm leader; bounce on the beat (teacher guide — evidence)

This is ተከታታይነት ያለው መሪ ምት — the leading rhythm sequence. One learner leads the rhythm, and the class follows: the leader's movements are copied exactly, and in time.

**Bounce on the beat — ኳስ ከሙዚቃ ምት ጋር ማንጠር.** Bounce a ball in time with the music's beat: low, controlled, landing on the beat — and move with your bounce.

[Picture 34912 — bouncing a ball exactly on the beat]

**Circulating balls — ኳሶችን በእጅ እየዞሩ የመመላስ ጨዋታ.** Balls pass hand to hand around the group, in rhythm. A dropped ball is picked up — and the rhythm continues. The chain does not stop for a drop.

## Sub lesson 17 — Moving with a partner; Selame Selame and clap-and-bounce (teacher guide — evidence)

Partner work must fit: partners of similar size work together and help each other.

**Selame Selame — ሰላሜ ሰላሜ ጨዋታ.** Partners and groups move in dance steps in a circle, greeting each other and changing partners in time. Group dance work is done in movement groups of five to ten, with beanbags (ቢያንስ) in the practice.

[Picture 34913 — the Selame Selame circle — greeting and changing partners]

**Clap and bounce — ትናንሽ ኳሶችን ከእጅ ጭብጨባ ጋር አቀናጅቶ መጨወት.** Clap. Bounce or pass the small ball. Clap again. One joined sequence, repeated to the beat: clap — bounce — clap.

[GIF 34914 — the clap-bounce-clap sequence]

## Sub lesson 18 — Movement chains and cultural dance — Grade 4 (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 4 chapter opens here: sequential movements are the movements seen in cultural dance and in sport — movement performed in a chain, to a beat. The chapter names three kinds: rope-jumping sequences, the game's own rhythm, and cultural and traditional movement.

By the chapter's end you can show sequential movement skills, create a sequential movement of your own, compose rhythm with others, and perform in a group.

[Picture 34915 — make a movement chain — step, turn, clap, jump]

## Sub lesson 19 — Stepping in sequence to the drum (teacher guide — evidence)

These are መሸሸል የሚታይበት ተከታታይነት ያላቸው የእንቅስቃሌዎች — sequential movements that show rhythm. The tools of the work: pictures, the rhythm stick (የክብር መምቻ ዱላ — ራትምስቲክ), mats, charts — and the drum (ክበሮ).

**Stepping in sequence — ተከታታይነት ያለው እንቅስቃሴ በአርማጂ.** March and walk in sequence to the drum's beat: step patterns repeated exactly, arms swinging, turning at the end of the phrase. The book shows the walk as a strip of six steps — your line of learners does the same: one pattern, repeated truly.

## Sub lesson 20 — Making my own movement chain; pair rope jumping (teacher guide — evidence)

Now create: build your own movement chains from movements you already know — this is ተከታታይነት ያለው እንቅስቃሴ ፈጠራ, creating sequential movement. Rope jumping is the chapter's worked example.

**Pair rope jumping — በጥንድ የገመድ ዝላይ.** Two partners jump a single rope together, in time. The rope game has its own call-and-response chant — a playful song about a thief and the police, and playing today — sung while you jump; the chant carries the beat, and the pair jumps inside it.

[Picture 34918 — two partners jumping one rope together]

[GIF 34919 — pair rope jumping — two jump as one]

## Sub lesson 21 — Long rope with clapping; skipping that travels (teacher guide — evidence)

**Long rope with clapping — በጭብጨባ የታጀበ የገመድ ዝላይ.** Two turners turn a very long rope. The class claps the beat while each jumper enters, jumps, and leaves — enter on the rhythm, leave on the rhythm.

[Picture 34920 — entering the turning long rope while the class claps]

**Skipping that travels — በግል የገመድ ዝላይ.** Skip with your own rope while travelling: forward and backward across a 20-metre space, turning at the ends, for four to five rounds. The rope never stops; the travelling is yours.

## Sub lesson 22 — One chain, whole group: cultural dance movement (teacher guide — evidence)

The whole group performs one chain together. The worked example is cultural dance movement: the group's movements must match the cultural music's beat, everyone keeps the same step, and the group's combined performance is what is assessed. The book's picture shows three traditional dancers with sticks, in cultural dress, doing one step as one body — that is the standard: many dancers, one chain.

[Picture 34921 — a line of dancers doing one stick-dance step together]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
