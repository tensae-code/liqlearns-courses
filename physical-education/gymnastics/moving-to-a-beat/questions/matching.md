What each teaches:
a) clapping overhead counting 1-2-3-4, marching on the spot, foot to foot
b) continuous sequences — side stretches, twists and two-foot jumps without stopping
c) sequences that build step by step, and equipment movements landing on the beat
d) timing the dribble bounce to the beat, and inventing your own rhythmic movement
e) rhythmic gymnastics as a creative individual performance — moving a ring or hoop in time with different music beats
f) rhythmic gymnastics with a ball, with a hoop and with a rope — training attention and body-part coordination, performed alone and in groups
g) sequential movement — joining single movements with music and games so the movement is never broken
h) marching in place to a beat, arms swinging, singing the marching song "እንሒድ ትምህርት ቤት" (Let's go to school)
i) circle-formation play — a round dance game held by the whole ring, each move answering the one before
j) unbroken movement chains to music — passing smoothly from one movement to the next and keeping the beat
k) moving and freezing on a colour signal, and side bending right and left with the arms overhead, in rhythm
l) throwing and catching in pairs while counting the catches together, and driving alternate knees high in rhythm while travelling forward
m) a three-station circuit two metres apart, and four slow controlled arm, leg and turn movements done to command
n) sequential movement to music and rhythm — dance, aerobics and gymnastics — growing from simple movements joined together
o) the progressive rhythmic sequence — body parts in time, joined step by step, and opposite arm-and-leg coordination
p) copying a rhythm leader exactly in time, bouncing a ball on the beat, and circulating balls hand to hand in rhythm
q) partner sequences, the Selame Selame circle dance game, and the clap-bounce-clap sequence
r) the Grade 4 chapter opener — movement chains to a beat in cultural dance and sport; creating and performing in a group
s) marching and walking in sequence to the drum, step patterns repeated exactly, turning at the phrase end
t) creating your own movement chains, and two partners jumping one rope together in time
u) entering, jumping and leaving a turning long rope to clapped rhythm, and solo skipping travelling over 20 metres
v) the whole group performing one cultural-dance chain together, everyone keeping the same step on the beat
