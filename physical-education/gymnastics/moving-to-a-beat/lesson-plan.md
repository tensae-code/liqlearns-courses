# Lesson plan — Moving to a Beat — Rhythmic Movement

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Gymnastics · **Lesson:** Moving to a Beat — Rhythmic Movement
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Gymnastics (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** moving in sequence to a beat — simple sequences first, then continuous, progressive, with equipment, and finally creating your own.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own body and the movements they already do in play; the parts climb from the youngest book's teaching to the oldest book's teaching in this band (Grades 1–4 books; see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Simple sequences to a beat** — clapping overhead counting 1-2-3-4, marching on the spot, foot to foot.
2. **Continuous sequences to music** — continuous sequences — side stretches, twists and two-foot jumps without stopping.
3. **Progressive sequences and equipment to music** — sequences that build step by step, and equipment movements landing on the beat.
4. **Equipment to music, and creating your own** — timing the dribble bounce to the beat, and inventing your own rhythmic movement.
5. **Rhythmic gymnastics — Grade 5: a hoop to music** — rhythmic gymnastics as a creative individual performance — moving a ring or hoop in time with different music beats.
6. **Rhythmic gymnastics — Grade 6: ball, hoop and rope** — rhythmic gymnastics with a ball, with a hoop and with a rope — training attention and body-part coordination, performed alone and in groups.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Open flat ground, a mat for any roll or floor work, and the equipment named in the parts (a ball, a rope, rings or cones where the part says so). Modest sports clothes. A partner or a small group for the partner parts.

## If the learner is stuck
- The movement breaks down → go back to the youngest part and do it slowly, on the spot, before travelling or adding equipment.
- A roll or balance feels unsafe → stop, use a mat, work beside a partner or teacher, and never copy a movement that hurts.
- The learner cannot name the movement → say the name aloud together while doing it slowly; the name and the movement are learned together.
