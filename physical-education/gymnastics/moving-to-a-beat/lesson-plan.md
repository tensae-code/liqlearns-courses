# Lesson plan — Moving to a Beat — Rhythmic Movement

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Gymnastics · **Lesson:** Moving to a Beat — Rhythmic Movement
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Gymnastics (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** moving in sequence to a beat — simple sequences first, then continuous, progressive, with equipment, and finally creating your own.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own body and the movements they already do in play; the parts climb from the youngest book's teaching to the oldest book's teaching in this band (Grades 1–4 books; see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Simple sequences to a beat** — clapping overhead counting 1-2-3-4, marching on the spot, foot to foot.
2. **Continuous sequences to music** — continuous sequences — side stretches, twists and two-foot jumps without stopping.
3. **Progressive sequences and equipment to music** — sequences that build step by step, and equipment movements landing on the beat.
4. **Equipment to music, and creating your own** — timing the dribble bounce to the beat, and inventing your own rhythmic movement.
5. **Rhythmic gymnastics — Grade 5: a hoop to music** — rhythmic gymnastics as a creative individual performance — moving a ring or hoop in time with different music beats.
6. **Rhythmic gymnastics — Grade 6: ball, hoop and rope** — rhythmic gymnastics with a ball, with a hoop and with a rope — training attention and body-part coordination, performed alone and in groups.
7. **Movements that flow together — the Grade 1 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)** — sequential movement — joining single movements with music and games so the movement is never broken.
8. **Marching to the beat, and the school song (teacher guide — evidence)** — marching in place to a beat, arms swinging, singing the marching song "እንሒድ ትምህርት ቤት" (Let's go to school).
9. **The circle game — round dance in a ring (teacher guide — evidence)** — circle-formation play — a round dance game held by the whole ring, each move answering the one before.
10. **Movements in a chain — the Grade 2 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)** — unbroken movement chains to music — passing smoothly from one movement to the next and keeping the beat.
11. **Move and freeze; side bends (teacher guide — evidence)** — moving and freezing on a colour signal, and side bending right and left with the arms overhead, in rhythm.
12. **Counting catches; knee drives (teacher guide — evidence)** — throwing and catching in pairs while counting the catches together, and driving alternate knees high in rhythm while travelling forward.
13. **The circuit course; slow and controlled movements (teacher guide — evidence)** — a three-station circuit two metres apart, and four slow controlled arm, leg and turn movements done to command.
14. **Dance, aerobics and movement chains — Grade 3 (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 3 chapter opener: sequential movement to music and rhythm, growing from simple movements joined together.
15. **To the beat: in-place rhythm and arm-leg coordination (teacher guide — evidence)** — the progressive rhythmic sequence: body parts in time, joined step by step; opposite arm and leg together.
16. **Follow the rhythm leader; bounce on the beat (teacher guide — evidence)** — copying a leader exactly in time; bouncing a ball on the music's beat; circulating balls hand to hand in rhythm.
17. **Moving with a partner; Selame Selame and clap-and-bounce (teacher guide — evidence)** — partner sequences of similar size; the Selame Selame circle dance game; the clap-bounce-clap sequence.
18. **Movement chains and cultural dance — Grade 4 (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 4 chapter opener: chains to a beat in cultural dance and sport; creating and performing sequences.
19. **Stepping in sequence to the drum (teacher guide — evidence)** — marching and walking in sequence to the drum (ክበሮ), step patterns repeated exactly, turning at the phrase end.
20. **Making my own movement chain; pair rope jumping (teacher guide — evidence)** — creating your own chains from known movements; two partners jumping one rope together to the rope chant (summary only).
21. **Long rope with clapping; skipping that travels (teacher guide — evidence)** — entering, jumping and leaving a turning long rope to clapped rhythm; solo skipping travelling forward and backward over 20 metres.
22. **One chain, whole group: cultural dance movement (teacher guide — evidence)** — the whole group performing one chain together to cultural music, everyone keeping the same step.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- The movement breaks down → go back to the youngest part and do it slowly, on the spot, before travelling or adding equipment.
- A roll or balance feels unsafe → stop, use a mat, work beside a partner or teacher, and never copy a movement that hurts.
- The learner cannot name the movement → say the name aloud together while doing it slowly; the name and the movement are learned together.
