# Balance and Calisthenics

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Balance is controlling your body's balance when standing, lying, walking and running. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — What balance is

Balance is controlling your body's balance when standing, lying, walking and running. Its benefit is not falling and breaking a bone. Practise walking imitating a duck, a giraffe and a bear, and balancing with one leg stretched forward, then back.

## Sub lesson 2 — Calisthenics — arms and shoulders

Put your hands on your shoulders, then stretch your arms to the side, up and forward, and fold them back. Circle both arms forward, then backward. Alternately touch your head with each arm. This set develops arm, shoulder and chest muscles.

## Sub lesson 3 — Calisthenics — legs

Sit and raise your two legs alternately, in rhythm. Support yourself on both hands and one knee on the ground, in the leg and arm support positions.

## Sub lesson 4 — Combined gymnastics movements

Raise one arm with the same-side leg and arm moving together. Lead with the same-side leg, hands on waist, and move in a straight line. Crouch, put your arms back and jump straight up. Crouch, rise, and straighten leg and arm in the air.

## Sub lesson 5 — Free gymnastics and balance — Grade 5

Free gymnastics (ነፃ ጅምናስቲክስ) is gymnastics with no apparatus. Keeping balance (ሚዛንን መጠበቅ) is a named element of it: move into the balance position and hold it steady. Form is checked, not just done — the book warns against a classic error: forgetting to turn the hand fully to the opposite leg. Do the movement correctly, not only quickly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
