# Gymnastics — Rules and Competition Types

## Do it first

Name every kind of gymnastics you have ever seen — on a mat, on apparatus, with music, in a group routine. This lesson sorts them: first the laws every gymnast competes under, then the kinds of competition there are.

## Sub lesson 1 — The basic laws of gymnastics

Gymnastics is not free movement. It has **basic laws** — the rules that say how its movements are performed and competed, the same for everyone who steps onto the floor. The laws are what turn rolls, balances, jumps and apparatus work into a **competition**: the same movements, judged under the same laws, wherever the competition is held.

Learn the movements in the Gymnastics lessons beside this one; here, learn that they stand under laws — and that knowing the laws is part of knowing the sport.

## Sub lesson 2 — At the Olympics — artistic and rhythmic

At the **Olympic Games**, gymnastics competes in kinds. **Artistic gymnastics** is the apparatus kind — its events are pictured with the **men's events** at the front of the chapter. **Rhythmic gymnastics** is the kind performed **for women only** — movement joined with apparatus and music, judged as a routine.

Same family, two faces: the artistic gymnast's strength and control on apparatus, and the rhythmic gymnast's routine performed to its beat.

## Sub lesson 3 — At the world championships — and sports aerobics

Gymnastics also competes at the **world championships**, and the championship family is wider. Among its kinds is **sports aerobics** — gymnastics movements performed as aerobic routines in competition.

So the map is: **basic laws** underneath everything; **artistic and rhythmic** at the Olympics; the **world championships** holding the wider family, **sports aerobics** among them.
