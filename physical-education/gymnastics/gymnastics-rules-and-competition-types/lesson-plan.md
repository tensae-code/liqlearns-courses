# Lesson plan — Gymnastics — Rules and Competition Types

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Gymnastics · **Lesson:** Gymnastics — Rules and Competition Types
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Gymnastics (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** gymnastics has basic laws, and its competitions come in kinds — artistic and rhythmic at the Olympics, and a wider championship family that includes sports aerobics.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Gymnastics movement lessons beside it; a learner may jump in here directly — the movements themselves are taught there, the laws and kinds here.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The basic laws of gymnastics** — gymnastics movements stand under basic laws, the same for every competitor.
2. **At the Olympics — artistic and rhythmic** — artistic gymnastics (men's events pictured) and rhythmic gymnastics, for women only.
3. **At the world championships — and sports aerobics** — the wider championship family, sports aerobics among its kinds.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Gymnastics Is and Why We Do It (Sport and Its Story) and the Gymnastics movement lessons. Recommended, never forced — a learner may jump in here directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the basic laws of gymnastics do: they govern how the movements are performed and competed, the same for everyone.
2. Name the two Olympic kinds in this lesson and say how they differ.
3. Name where sports aerobics competes in this lesson's map: the world championships.

## What you need
No equipment — this is a knowledge lesson. A mat and the movement lessons beside it if the learner wants to try the movements named.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names every kind of gymnastics they have seen, before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 35107]: a picture of artistic apparatus and a rhythmic routine side by side — the student names which side is which kind, and says one thing that tells them.
   - ADD AUDIO: the competition kinds (artistic, rhythmic, sports aerobics) read aloud — the student says them along with the voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name the three parts of the map (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- The kinds blur together → return to the picture: apparatus and strength on one side, routine and music on the other; then place sports aerobics in the championship family.
- Laws read as "only for judges" → the laws are what make a competition the same competition for everyone; without them there is no fair contest to judge.
