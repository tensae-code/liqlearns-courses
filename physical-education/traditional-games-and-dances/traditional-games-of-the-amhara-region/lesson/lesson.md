# Traditional Games of the Amhara Region

## Do it first

Stand at a line. Jump as far as you can and mark where you land. The next player jumps *from your mark*. The line of jumps grows across the ground. You have just played the heart of two of this lesson's games.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gudib jump, Suzi and ball-throwing-out

**Gudib jump — የጉድብ ዝላይ.** Jumping in different open places, pictured in the book.

**The Suzi game — የሱዚ ጨዋታ.** A traditional game that develops jumping, pictured in the book (Fig. 7.11). Its jumping pattern is the game's own — learned by playing it where it is known.

**Ball-throwing-out — ኳስ ማስጣል.** A traditional Ethiopian game of **two opposing teams**, throwing the other side out with the ball.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gebeta and Gena

**Gebeta — ገበጣ.** A board game of hollows and stones: a **thinking and social game**. The book traces its history to the **6th–7th centuries BCE in Ethiopia** and to the era of **Emperor Tewodros**. Wherever it is played locally, its basic playing orders must be identified and kept — the local orders are the game's law.

**Gena — የገና ጨዋታ.** The traditional Christmas hockey-like game shepherds played **from Tahsas to Tir, after the harvest, on a wide field in the afternoon** — curved sticks and a wooden ball. Its rules vary by locality; the book teaches it as a game whose local form is its own.

## Sub lesson 3 — Traditional wrestling and horse racing — the region's ruled games

**Traditional wrestling — የትግል ባህላዊ ጨዋታ.** The region's traditional wrestling game, taught in its own section of the book. Children study it here; wrestling itself is practised only under its own local teaching and care.

**Horse racing — የፈረስ ሸርጥ ጨዋታ.** Horses were the fastest messengers before phones and radio. The game's printed frame: a prepared **400 or 600 metre** course; **1-metre start flags** and **2-metre marker flags every 100 metres**; an **adult** category, both sexes, **riders over 18 and under 60**; **at most six riders at once**, front feet on the start line.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the games of this lesson with one fact for each: Suzi's jumping, Gebeta's hollows and stones, Gena's season, horse racing's six-rider limit.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a gebeta board and mark the hollows. Ask at home how many stones start in each hollow where you live — record the local order.
2. Write Gena's frame in four lines: who played it, when, where, and with what.
3. Measure 100 metres on a path near home: that is the distance between horse racing's marker flags.
