# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of the Amhara Region

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of the Amhara Region
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the named traditional games of the Amhara Region — Gudib jump, Suzi, ball-throwing-out, Gebeta and Gena, traditional wrestling and horse racing — each kept in its book's own frame: name, home and how it is played, never invented steps.
**This lesson's own level:** the games survey widens to the whole region; it stands on Traditional Games of Our Zone.

## Sub lessons
1. **Gudib jump, Suzi and ball-throwing-out** — the Grade 5 book's named Amhara games: Gudib jump (የጉድብ ዝላይ), the Suzi game (የሱዚ ጨዋታ) and ball-throwing-out (ኳስ ማስጣል), a two-team traditional game.
2. **Gebeta and Gena** — the Grade 6 book's Gebeta (ገበጣ) — traced to the 6th–7th centuries BCE and the era of Emperor Tewodros — and Gena (የገና), the shepherds' Christmas game played from Tahsas to Tir after the harvest.
3. **Traditional wrestling and horse racing — the region's ruled games** — the Grade 6 book's traditional wrestling (የትግል ባህላዊ ጨዋታ) and horse racing game (የፈረስ ሸርጥ ጨዋታ) with its printed course, flags, ages and field rules.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Traditional Dance and Games Are. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the region's games taught here and the home or season of each.
2. Play the Suzi jumping game and the Gudib jump as the book frames them.
3. Say what Gebeta is: a thinking and social game played on a board of hollows with stones.
4. Say when Gena is played: after the harvest, from Tahsas to Tir, on a wide field in the afternoon.
5. State horse racing's printed rules: a prepared 400/600 m course, flagged starts and markers, riders over 18 and under 60, at most six at once.

## What you need
Stones for Gebeta, open ground, a wooden ball and curved sticks for Gena where the class has them. Horse racing and wrestling are studied here, not staged by children.

## If the learner is stuck
- Nobody knows the local gebeta orders → that is a finding, not a gap to fill by invention: record it and ask an elder before playing.
- A learner wants to stage the horse race → the book's frame is adults, over 18: study the course and flags on foot instead.
