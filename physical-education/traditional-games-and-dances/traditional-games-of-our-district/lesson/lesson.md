# Traditional Games of Our District

## Do it first

Five small pebbles in one hand. Toss — and while they are in the air, your hand darts to the ground and gathers. Down they come — catch! That heartbeat of hand and eye is Qilmosh, and it has been played in your district longer than anyone can remember.

## Sub lesson 1 — Qilmosh and the named pebble and hand games

**Qilmosh — የጠጠር ቅልሞሽ.** The pebble game: **five small round pebbles, played in turns**. Toss and gather, gather and catch — the hand and the eye trained by five stones.

**Shimel Qindiyosh — የሽመል ቅንድዮሽ** (Fig. 6.2.ለ). A named game of the district survey.

**Qinchifit — ቅንጭፍት.** Another of the survey's named games.

The district's players hold each game's full rules; the book names the games and pictures them, and the class presents each one the way its home plays it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Zagol, Yaekob, the Hen Fight — and what games give to health

**Zagol — ዛጎል (ቁልፍ) መጣል** (key throwing). Small circles are marked; the piece is **thrown from a set distance** at them. Aim, distance, and a steady hand.

**Yaekob — ያቆብ** (Fig. 6.2.ሠ). The named game that also appears in the Grade 1 decision games — the district survey carries it on.

**The Hen Fight — የደሮ ጥል.** The last named game of the survey.

And the chapter's closing exercise turns the whole survey into one question, which ends this lesson too: **name traditional games — and list the benefit traditional games give to health.**

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the six games of the district survey, how Qilmosh is played, and one benefit traditional games give to health.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play Qilmosh with five pebbles, in turns, keeping score of clean catches.
2. Set Zagol: mark small circles, fix the throwing distance, and throw in turns.
3. Write the closing exercise's answer: name four traditional games and list their health benefits.
