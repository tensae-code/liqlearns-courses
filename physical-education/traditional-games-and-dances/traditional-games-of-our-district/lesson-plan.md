# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of Our District

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of Our District
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the named games of the district survey — Qilmosh of the five pebbles, Shimel Qindiyosh, Qinchifit, Zagol key-throwing, Yaekob, and the Hen Fight — and the closing exercise's question: the health benefit of playing them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the area games lessons next door in this title — the same survey, one ring wider.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Qilmosh and the named pebble and hand games** — the pebble game Qilmosh (five small round pebbles, played in turns); Shimel Qindiyosh; Qinchifit.
2. **Zagol, Yaekob, the Hen Fight — and what games give to health** — Zagol/key throwing — small circles marked, the piece thrown from a set distance; Yaekob; the Hen Fight game; the closing exercise: naming traditional games and listing the health benefit of playing them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Games of Our Area (Joint). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the district games: Qilmosh, Shimel Qindiyosh, Qinchifit, Zagol, Yaekob, the Hen Fight.
2. Play a turn of Qilmosh — five small round pebbles, played in turns.
3. List the benefit traditional games give to health, as the book's closing exercise asks.

## What you need
Five small round pebbles for Qilmosh, and whatever your area's forms of the other games use. Nothing bought.

## If the learner is stuck
- Pebbles keep dropping → begin with two pebbles and grow to five; the game itself is the ladder.
- Rules of a named game are disputed → the game's home area settles its rules; record, never invent.
