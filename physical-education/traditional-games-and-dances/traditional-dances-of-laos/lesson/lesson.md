# Traditional Dances of Laos

## Do it first

Name one game or dance from your own childhood that everybody knew without a book. Games and dances handed down like that are this lesson's subject — now read how Laos's books teach their own.

## Sub lesson 1 — The fon strand in the primary guides

In the 2007–2009 primary series the year has four strands — formation drill, gymnastics postures, gymnastics with music, and **dance/games (ການຟ້ອນ)** — and the dance strand is built from named Lao folk dances, each taught step by step as its own lesson. The 2020 revised Grade 1 edition replaced this strand with playground games; the dances stocked here come from the 2007–2009 guides of Grades 1, 2, 3 and 5 and the Grade 4 guide of 2009.

## Sub lesson 2 — Named dances — festivals, places and animals

The guides name their dances one by one: **Kan Bin**, **Yat Phaen Bin**, **Daen Kap Seua** and **Songkran** — the dance for the new-year festival — and **Ngan Kin Thang** in Grade 4 (2009); **Leu Lai**, **Thang Viset**, **Yat Duang**, **Ka Chap Lak** and **Yaeo Luk Ka Kai** in Grade 1 (2007), with Thang Viset taught again in Grade 5 (2009); **Lam Yan Khwa**, **Pa Kin Lao**, **Chat Tum**, **Yat Nam** and **Rak Sa San Tung** in Grade 3 (2009); and **Ka Don** and **Mae Yat Bon** in Grade 2 (2007).

## Sub lesson 3 — Partner, circle and formation dances

Other dances are built on partners and formations: **sending and receiving** (Grade 4 2009 and Grade 3 2009) and **receiving and sending** (Grade 1, 2007), **dancing around the post** (Grade 4, 2009), **circling the post** and **going around the post** and **guarding the victory post** (Grade 5, 2009), the **long-route folk dance** and **jumping over the rope** (Grade 4, 2009), the **duck circle dance** and **turn left, turn right** and **the cloth tied at the waist** (Grade 1, 2007). Each dance's steps are the ones its own lesson teaches — this lesson names the dances and their kinds, and invents no steps.
