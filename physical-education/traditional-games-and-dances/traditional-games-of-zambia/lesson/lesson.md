# Traditional Games of Zambia

## Do it first

Name one game from your own community — a game the older people around you played as children. Guess which family it belongs to: a chasing game, a breakthrough game, a rhythmic game, a knowledge game, or a manipulative/calculative game. Then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Research and perform your own community's games

Zambia's module does not hand learners a fixed list of games. It sets them a task: **learners research and perform the indigenous Zambian traditional games of their own communities**. The games live in the community; the learner goes to find them, learns them, and brings them back by playing them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The named games and why they matter

The module names the families by example. **Chain tag** is the **chasing** game. **Sheep-Sheep Come All** is the **breakthrough** game. **Musical chairs** is the **rhythmic** game. **Who Am I?** is the **knowledge** game. And there are **manipulative/calculative games** — games of handling and counting. The module teaches these games together with **why they matter**: they are the community's own games, carried by playing them.

Game rules are taught only as the placements summarise them; no rules have been invented here for any game the module leaves community-taught.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what Zambia's module asks learners to do — research and perform — then name the four named games and the family each one belongs to.
