# Traditional Games of DR Congo

## Do it first

What games do children play where you live that your grandparents also played? Say one — then read on and check yourself against DR Congo's books.

## Sub lesson 1 — Wrestling initiation games

DR Congo's books open traditional play with **wrestling initiation**: the Grade 1 book teaches **standing opposition games, scarf-pull and push games, with greeting and respect**; the Grade 2 book keeps it safe — **standing opposition, unbalancing and scarf-grab, under respect rules**; the Grades 3–4 book adapts the **Matadi wrestling games for safe duelling** inside its team-games chapter.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marelle (Kebo)

**Marelle, called Kebo**, is the hopping court game: **throw a puck, then hop the court without touching the lines**. The Grade 2 book practises the same game for **balance — puck throw and hopping course, keeping steady**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Zango and Nzango

**Zango** is a **rhythmic foot-and-hand agility game, played with clapping and song**. The older book's **Nzango** is adapted **for rhythm and leg power**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Adapting other local games

The Grade 1 book closes by **adapting other local games for its class — spinning-top and running games from Kivu, Tshopo and other provinces**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how each game in this lesson is played, as the books give them.
