# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of DR Congo

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of DR Congo
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Traditional games of DR Congo: Congolese traditional games, including wrestling initiation games and Marelle (Kebo).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

## Sub lessons
1. **Wrestling initiation games** — standing opposition games, scarf-pull and push games, greeting and respect; safe standing opposition, unbalancing and scarf-grab; Matadi wrestling games for safe duelling.
2. **Marelle (Kebo)** — throwing a puck then hopping the court without touching the lines, keeping balance.
3. **Zango and Nzango** — rhythmic foot-and-hand agility sequences with clapping and song; Nzango played for rhythm and leg power.
4. **Adapting other local games** — spinning-top and running games from Kivu, Tshopo and other provinces adapted for the youngest class.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Traditional Dance and Games Are. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the wrestling initiation games and the respect they begin with.
2. Say how Marelle (Kebo) is played — the puck throw and the hopping course.
3. Say what Zango and Nzango ask of the feet, the hands and the rhythm.
4. Name the provinces whose local games the Grade 1 book adapts.

## What you need
A scarf, a puck and a chalked marelle (Kebo) court, as the books' games use them; a clear space for the opposition games.

## If the learner is stuck
- The wrestling games feel rough → go back to the books' shape: standing opposition only, greeting and respect first, and stop at the scarf.
- Kebo lines get touched → slow the hopping down and keep balance first, as the Grade 2 book practises it.
