# Traditional Dances of Our Area (Joint)

## Do it first

The circle has everyone in it — girls and boys together. One step, one rhythm, one circle. If one dancer breaks the rhythm, the whole circle feels it. These are the dances the whole community dances as one body.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Grade 1 joint dances

The Grade 1 book pictures **mixed traditional dancing** — girls and boys dancing together — and sets the task: **identify the joint dances, bring them, dance them jointly, and explain them**.

The explaining matters: a joint dance belongs to everyone, so everyone should be able to say what it is and where it lives.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Grade 2 joint dances

The Grade 2 book pictures girls and boys dancing traditional dances **together**, inside the same chapter, with the chapter task on the joint dancing: the area's dances, danced by the whole class as one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Turning with a partner — the Embushe Gela dance (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide teaches a partner form: **pairs hold hands and turn and dance together** — በጥንድ መሸከርከር.

Its chant is **"Embushe Gela" — እምቡሼ ገላ**: a traditional circle/partner dance chant (the guide prints it; here its theme is honoured and summarised, not transcribed — a call to the dance, sung while turning).

And the guide explains why it is taught: **pair turning games build social bonds**. To turn with a partner, you must hold, trust, and move as one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how a joint circle dance works, and what a partner must do in the turning dance so the turn does not break.

## Do It Yourself

1. Dance your area's joint dance in a full circle. Count one full round without a break.
2. Partner turning: hold hands, turn slowly together, then at the dance's rhythm. Swap partners and turn again.
3. Explain one joint dance to a younger child — its name, its area, its step — in three sentences.
