# Traditional Games of Greece

## Do it first

What games did children play in your place long before you were born — games still played today? Say one — then read on and check yourself against Greece's books.

## Sub lesson 1 — Skipping rope — the world's game

The Grades 1-2 book calls the class to play: **skipping rope, an old traditional game children all over the world still play — two turn the rope, the rest jump, with a skipping song**. The Grades 3-4 book practises it properly: **skipping on the spot and while travelling, swinging the rope right-left, quick bounces without touching it, partner and group rope games** — and names the abilities skipping asks for.

## Sub lesson 2 — Circle and leapfrog games

**Gyro Gyro Oloi (All around in a circle)** is the Greek circle singing game, shown in Savvidis' 1905 painting and still played today, with its song. **Varelakia (The little barrels)** is leapfrog: **children bend in a line holding their knees, head tucked in, and the last one jumps over them one by one**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Elastic games

The Grades 3-4 book's elastic games use **a tied elastic band: stretching it into geometric shapes and playing jumping patterns over it**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Seasonal customs

Three customs close the lesson, each tied to its season. **The Chelidona (The swallow):** on 1 March children carried a flower-decked wooden swallow from house to house singing the spring carols (chelidonismata). **Perperouna:** the old rain-calling custom — a girl dressed in leaves and green branches went round the village with other children singing for rain, and was sprinkled with water. **The Rogatsia:** the Macedonian carnival custom — dancers in their best costumes or masked, dancing for the good of the year.

Tell your companion how each game and custom in this lesson is played, as the books give them.
