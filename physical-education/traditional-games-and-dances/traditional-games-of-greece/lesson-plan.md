## Sub lessons
1. **Skipping rope — the world's game** — skipping rope, an old traditional game children all over the world still play: two turn the rope, the rest jump, with a skipping song; and skipping on the spot and while travelling, partner and group rope games.
2. **Circle and leapfrog games** — Gyro Gyro Oloi, the Greek circle singing game, and Varelakia, the leapfrog game over a line of bent backs.
3. **Elastic games** — games with a tied elastic band: stretching it into geometric shapes and playing jumping patterns over it.
4. **Seasonal customs** — the Chelidona spring swallow custom, the Perperouna rain-calling custom, and the Rogatsia Macedonian carnival custom.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Traditional Dance and Games Are; Traditional Dances of Greece. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how skipping rope is played in the books and why it is called a game of the whole world.
2. Describe Gyro Gyro Oloi and Varelakia as the books give them.
3. Say what children do with the elastic band in the elastic games.
4. Tell the three seasonal customs — Chelidona, Perperouna and Rogatsia — and the season or hope each one carries.

## What you need
A skipping rope and a tied elastic band, as the books' games use them; a clear space for the circle and leapfrog games.

## If the learner is stuck
- The customs blur together → anchor each in its season: Chelidona calls the spring (1 March), Perperouna calls the rain, Rogatsia dances at carnival for the good of the year.
- Leapfrog feels unsafe → go back to the book's shape: bend in a line holding your knees, head tucked in, and jump one by one only over a steady back.
