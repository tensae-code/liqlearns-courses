# Traditional Games of Laos

## Do it first

Name one game or dance from your own childhood that everybody knew without a book. Games and dances handed down like that are this lesson's subject — now read how Laos's books teach their own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Animal chase games

The Lao primary guides teach chase games with animal roles, one named game at a time: **the bear carries rice** and **snatch the monkey's tail** (Grade 3, 2021); **the tiger chases the pig**, **the tiger and the lion** and **catch the bird** (Grade 1, 2020); **catch the rabbit**, **catch the fish** and **Mr Tiger** (Grade 2, 2020); and in the 2007–2009 guides the tiger-and-lion dance-game, catch the rabbit, **the cat catches the mouse** and **the rabbit and the hunter**. Each game is a full teacher-script lesson with objectives, warm-up, main activity and assessment.

## Sub lesson 2 — Circle, water, weather and hiding games

A second family is played in circles and with hiding and passing: **Nang Mon hides the cloth** (Grade 1, 2020), **flowing water and whirlpool** (Grade 2, 2020, and as a 2007 dance-game), **lightning, thunder and rain** (Grade 1, 2020), **pass and receive the treasure** (Grade 2, 2020), **the rice-field dance** (Grade 1, 2020), **the tortoise lays eggs** (Grade 1 2020; Grade 1, Grade 2 and Grade 3 guides of 2007–2009) and **monkeys snatch the ball** (Grade 2, 2020).

## Sub lesson 3 — Target, race and ball games

The third family aims, races and runs: **kick the ball at the target** and the **zigzag running game** (Grade 4, 2009), **race to snatch a chair** (Grade 2, 2020), and the 2007–2009 dance-games **change places**, **who reaches first**, **the running hand relay**, **jumping over the waves**, **little birds pecking** and **herding the ducks into the pen**. The rules of each game are the ones its own lesson prints — this lesson names the games and their kinds, and invents no rules.
