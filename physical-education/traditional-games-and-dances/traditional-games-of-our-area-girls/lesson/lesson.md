# Traditional Games of Our Area (Girls)

## Do it first

This lesson starts with an empty page — on purpose. The book pictures no game here and names none. The games are **yours**: the girls' games of your own area, carried in your memory and your grandmother's memory. Bring one. That bringing *is* the lesson's beginning.

Here is the whole of the book's teaching for this lesson, and it is a task, not a text:

**Bring the girls' games of your area. Play them with your classmates. Explain which body part each game works most.**

No game is pictured. No game is named. That is not a gap in the book — it is the book's design. Ethiopia's areas hold hundreds of girls' games, and the book refuses to pick one area's games over another's. The course keeps the same honesty: this lesson is the **frame** — name the game, tell where it belongs, play it by its rules, and study which body part it works most.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of a girls' game of your area, where it belongs, and which body part it works most.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ask at home for a girls' game of your area — its name and rules — and write them down before you forget.
2. Present the game to your class: name, home area, rules, demonstration.
3. Play it fully. Afterwards, everyone says which body part worked most.
