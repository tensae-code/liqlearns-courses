# Lesson plan — Traditional Dances of Our Area (Boys)

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Dances of Our Area (Boys)
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the boys' traditional dances of the pupils' own local area — opening with "Buhe Belu" — brought by the class, danced, and studied for the body part each moves most.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the rhythmic movement skills — moving to a beat. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The Grade 1 dance — "Buhe Belu"** — the Grade 1 opening picture "Buhe Belu"; the task: bring and dance the boys' dances of your area; say which body part each moves most.
2. **The Grade 2 dances — boys' dances of the locality** — the Grade 2 picture/task treatment, parallel to the girls', for boys' local dances.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Dances of Our Area (Girls), its neighbour in this title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Dance the boys' dances of their area, opening with "Buhe Belu".
2. Say which body part each dance moves most.
3. Bring a dance of their area to class and present it to classmates.

## What you need
Sticks if your area's dance uses them (as Buhe does), and a drum or song beat. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sticks become weapons in play → stop, lay sticks down, restate: sticks are danced with, never swung at a person. Resume only when safe.
- Nobody knows a boys' local dance → ask elders first; the locality supplies the dance.
