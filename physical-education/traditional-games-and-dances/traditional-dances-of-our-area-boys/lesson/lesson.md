# Traditional Dances of Our Area (Boys)

## Do it first

Sticks in hand, the song rises — **Buhe Belu!** The boys dance. Feet, shoulders, arms — which part dances hardest? The dances in this lesson are your own area's, and your class brings them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Grade 1 dance — "Buhe Belu"

The Grade 1 book's opening picture for the boys is **"Buhe Belu" — ቡሄ በሉ** — boys dancing with sticks at Buhe.

The task is the same honest frame as the girls' lesson: **bring the boys' dances of your area, dance them, and say which body part each moves most.** The book pictures a beginning; your area supplies the dances.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Grade 2 dances — boys' dances of the locality

The Grade 2 book treats the boys' dances the same way as the girls': picture and task, **the boys' local dances**, danced by the class, each studied for the body part it works most.

Bring the dance. Dance it together. Study it: which body part works most, and what does that tell you about the life the dance comes from?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about Buhe Belu: what the dancers hold, and which body part the dance moves most. Then name another boys' dance of your area.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ask at home which boys' dances belong to your area; write the names and bring them.
2. Dance the boys' dance with classmates, with sticks if the dance uses them — held safely, danced, never swung at anyone.
3. Say for each dance danced today: the body part it moves most.
