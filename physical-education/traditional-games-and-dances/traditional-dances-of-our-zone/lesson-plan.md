# Lesson plan — Traditional Dances of Our Zone

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Dances of Our Zone
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).

## Sub lessons
1. **Wedding and festival dances** — girls' traditional wedding dances; girls' festival dances (the book recalls from Grade 3 that girls dance at yearly, cultural and religious festivals); men's festival dances, with "Hoya Hoye" named.
2. **Work dances and victory dances** — men's work dances; victory dances — including a development victory dance ("Dub Dub Ale") and a sport victory dance.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Dances of Our District. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the kinds of zone dance the book names: girls' wedding, girls' festival, men's festival ("Hoya Hoye"), men's work, victory dances.
2. Say what the book frames dances as developing: communication, coordination and fitness.
3. Tell the two victory dances apart: the development victory dance ("Dub Dub Ale") and the sport victory dance.

## What you need
Your zone's dance music or drum, and the dress of the occasion if you have it. Nothing bought.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner sorts five dance names by occasion — wedding, festival, work, victory — before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Occasions get mixed → sort by the question 'when is it danced?' — the occasion IS the classification the book uses.
- A learner presents a dance without a home or occasion → it waits outside the survey until its home and occasion are known.
