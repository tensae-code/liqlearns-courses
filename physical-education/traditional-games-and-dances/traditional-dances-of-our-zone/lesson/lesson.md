# Traditional Dances of Our Zone

## Do it first

A wedding. A festival. A day's work done well. A victory. In the zone's dances, every great occasion has its own dance — and the dance tells you the occasion before a word is spoken.

## Sub lesson 1 — Wedding and festival dances

The Grade 4 book widens the survey to the **zone — ዞን** — and names the dances by their **occasions**:

**Girls' traditional wedding dances — የሴቶች ባህላዊ የሰርግ ጭፈራዎች.** The dance belongs to the wedding.

**Girls' festival dances — የሴቶች የበዓላት ባህላዊ ጭፈራዎች.** And the book recalls its Grade 3 teaching: girls dance at **yearly, cultural and religious festivals**.

**Men's festival dances — የወንድ የበዓላት ባህላዊ ጭፈራዎች** — with **"Hoya Hoye" — ሆያ ሆዬ** named among them.

The book frames the whole chapter first: dances develop **communication, coordination and fitness**. A festival dance is a community talking to itself — in rhythm.

## Sub lesson 2 — Work dances and victory dances

**Men's work dances — የወንዶች ባህላዊ የስራ ጭፈራዎች** (Fig. 6.1.ሠ). The zone's heir of the district's fukera: work praised in dance.

**Victory dances — የድል ጭፈራዎች.** And the book pictures two kinds:

- A **development victory dance** — "ዱብ ዱብ አለ" (Fig. 6.1.ረ-1): victory in the work of building and growing.
- A **sport victory dance** (Fig. 6.1.ረ-3): victory in the game itself.

Wedding, festival, work, victory: four occasions, four families of dance, one zone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four occasions of the zone dances, one named dance, and the three things the book says dances develop.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort your class's collected dances by occasion: wedding, festival, work, victory. Which occasion has the most dances in your zone?
2. Learn the main movement of Hoya Hoye as your locality dances it, and perform it with the group.
3. Dance a victory: after a class game, the winners dance the sport victory dance — and the other team joins the rhythm.
