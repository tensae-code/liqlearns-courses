# Traditional Games of Fiji — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. The parts run from the youngest book's teaching to the oldest, as the placements stack them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 5 Traditional Games: team and chasing games
Pass mark: Say what this part teaches: Chapter 5 Traditional Games: team and chasing games — Veibuka (beach goal-touch game, "seva" in Lau), Veisole (hidden carried player guessed by touch), Veileveleveti (tree-climbing leg-pull game), Veitara (tag with a "tibi" safe sign) and Veisabiliga (hand-slap chase game).

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 5 Traditional Games: target games
Pass mark: Say what this part teaches: Chapter 5 Traditional Games: target games — Veicibi and Veilavo (knocking "cibi" pieces out with a stone or coconut-shell disc on the ivayaga mat), Vakadovu (spearing hidden sugar cane, from Kabara Island, Lau) and Vakatalolovo (bow-and-arrow shooting at food hung on a thatched house).

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 8 Traditional Games: field and reed games
Pass mark: Say what this part teaches: Chapter 8 Traditional Games: field and reed games — Veitavi/Peleirauni (batting and fielding game where hitting the captain puts the whole team out), Veidia (spearing a standing reed), Veivana/Vanavana (breadfruit-branch pop-gun game), Veibo (wrestling without fists or kicks) and Toqacewa (throwing cane leaves for distance).

## Sub lesson 4 — Chapter 8 Traditional Games: lemon games
Pass mark: Say what this part teaches: Chapter 8 Traditional Games: lemon games — Veiteqe (rolling lemons to topple stone piles), Tafue (long-rope skipping to a counting song), Veiviritaki Moli (underwater lemon relay in water), Tavusasa (lemon targeting game) and Veimoli (girls' lemon-juggling game with its song from Nukubalavu, Savusavu).

## Sub lesson 5 — Chapter 6 Traditional Games: I-Taukei Games
Pass mark: Say what this part teaches: Chapter 6 Traditional Games: I-Taukei Games — Labolabo (leapfrog relay over children on the ground), Veiteqe Vutu/Niu/Vatu (toppling game recorded in Kadavu in 1865), the Simolo lap-slapping song game, Veisole Vatu (guessing which grass heap hides the stone), Veiboliga (arm wrestling) and Veividi (flicking gasau reeds, whose name changes by region).

## Sub lesson 6 — Chapter 3: Traditional Games
Pass mark: Say what this part teaches: Chapter 3: Traditional Games — Veitiqa: a throwing game made from the heart-shaped fruit of the cibi plant on a 1.5 m reed handle, thrown for distance, and making a tiqa or ulutoa from the environment.

## Sub lesson 7 — Chapter 3: Traditional Fijian Games
Pass mark: Say what this part teaches: Chapter 3: Traditional Fijian Games — Veitiqa Cori (reed throw retrieved by a bark string), Cokataki Moto/Zevlin (spear throwing on the seashore) and Veri (team hiding game).
