# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of Fiji

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of Fiji
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Traditional games of Fiji: Fijian team, chasing and target games such as Veibuka, Veisole and Veicibi. — taught here through Fiji's way: its *PE is Fun* books.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Chapter 5 Traditional Games: team and chasing games** — Chapter 5 Traditional Games: team and chasing games — Veibuka (beach goal-touch game, "seva" in Lau), Veisole (hidden carried player guessed by touch), Veileveleveti (tree-climbing leg-pull game), Veitara (tag with a "tibi" safe sign) and Veisabiliga (hand-slap chase game)
2. **Chapter 5 Traditional Games: target games** — Chapter 5 Traditional Games: target games — Veicibi and Veilavo (knocking "cibi" pieces out with a stone or coconut-shell disc on the ivayaga mat), Vakadovu (spearing hidden sugar cane, from Kabara Island, Lau) and Vakatalolovo (bow-and-arrow shooting at food hung on a thatched house)
3. **Chapter 8 Traditional Games: field and reed games** — Chapter 8 Traditional Games: field and reed games — Veitavi/Peleirauni (batting and fielding game where hitting the captain puts the whole team out), Veidia (spearing a standing reed), Veivana/Vanavana (breadfruit-branch pop-gun game), Veibo (wrestling without fists or kicks) and Toqacewa (throwing cane leaves for distance)
4. **Chapter 8 Traditional Games: lemon games** — Chapter 8 Traditional Games: lemon games — Veiteqe (rolling lemons to topple stone piles), Tafue (long-rope skipping to a counting song), Veiviritaki Moli (underwater lemon relay in water), Tavusasa (lemon targeting game) and Veimoli (girls' lemon-juggling game with its song from Nukubalavu, Savusavu)
5. **Chapter 6 Traditional Games: I-Taukei Games** — Chapter 6 Traditional Games: I-Taukei Games — Labolabo (leapfrog relay over children on the ground), Veiteqe Vutu/Niu/Vatu (toppling game recorded in Kadavu in 1865), the Simolo lap-slapping song game, Veisole Vatu (guessing which grass heap hides the stone), Veiboliga (arm wrestling) and Veividi (flicking gasau reeds, whose name changes by region)
6. **Chapter 3: Traditional Games** — Chapter 3: Traditional Games — Veitiqa: a throwing game made from the heart-shaped fruit of the cibi plant on a 1.5 m reed handle, thrown for distance, and making a tiqa or ulutoa from the environment
7. **Chapter 3: Traditional Fijian Games** — Chapter 3: Traditional Fijian Games — Veitiqa Cori (reed throw retrieved by a bark string), Cokataki Moto/Zevlin (spear throwing on the seashore) and Veri (team hiding game)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
