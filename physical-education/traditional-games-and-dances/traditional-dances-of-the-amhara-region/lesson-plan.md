# Lesson plan — Traditional Dances of the Amhara Region

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Dances of the Amhara Region
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the named traditional dances of the Amhara Region — Gondar, North Shewa, Wollo and Wag Himra — each with its home area, and the book's own question for every dance: which body parts above the waist does it develop?
**This lesson's own level:** the survey widens past area, district and zone to the whole region; it stands on Traditional Dances of Our Zone.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The Gondar traditional dance** — the Gondar dance (የጎንደር ባህላዊ ጭፈራ), pictured and taught in both the Grade 5 and Grade 6 books, with the book's body-parts task.
2. **The North Shewa traditional dance** — the North Shewa (Amhara) dance (የሰሜን ሽዋ ባህላዊ ጭፈራ) of the Grade 5 book — its shoulder movement in rhythm is the pictured motion of this sub lesson.
3. **The Wollo and Wag Himra traditional dances** — the Grade 6 book's second regional set: the Wollo dance and the Wag Himra dance, each pictured with the same develop-the-body-parts activity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Traditional Dance and Games Are — know what a traditional dance is before surveying a region's. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the region's dances taught here: Gondar, North Shewa, Wollo and Wag Himra, each with its home area.
2. For each dance, answer the book's task: which above-the-waist body parts can it develop?
3. Dance the shoulder movement of the North Shewa dance in rhythm, as the books picture it.

## What you need
Open ground and, where the class has them, traditional dress for dancing. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands and moves their shoulders to a steady beat — up, down, in rhythm — before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Shoulders will not keep the beat → slow the beat down; rhythm first, speed later.
- A learner's home dance differs from the book's picture → both stand: the book's frame is its home area's, and the learner's is theirs; name the difference, never erase it.
