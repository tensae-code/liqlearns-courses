# Traditional Dances of the Amhara Region

## Do it first

Stand up. Now move only your shoulders to a steady beat — up, down, forward, back, in rhythm. Hard to keep going, isn't it? In the Amhara Region, whole dances are built on that movement. This lesson names them, region by region.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Gondar traditional dance

**The Gondar traditional dance — የጎንደር ባህላዊ ጭፈራ.** Gondar's own dance, pictured in the books in traditional dress. It opens the region's survey in both the Grade 5 and the Grade 6 books.

The books set the same task for it as for every pictured dance in this lesson: **name the upper and lower body parts it develops, and its link to physical education — in practice, by dancing it.** Dance first; then answer from your own moving body.

## Sub lesson 2 — The North Shewa traditional dance

**The North Shewa traditional dance — የሰሜን ሽዋ ባህላዊ ጭፈራ.** North Shewa's own dance in the Amhara Region's survey.

Its pictured movement is the **shoulder movement in rhythm** — the shoulders working to the beat while the dance goes on. Practise it slowly first: shoulders up, down and circling, exactly on the beat, before the full dance.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Wollo and Wag Himra traditional dances

**The Wollo traditional dance** and **the Wag Himra traditional dance** — the Grade 6 book's second set of the region's dances, each pictured, each with the same activity: **name the body parts it develops, in practice**.

The region is bigger than one town's dance. Gondar, North Shewa, Wollo, Wag Himra — one region, many homes, each dance carrying its own place's identity.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four dances of this lesson and the home of each. Then show the North Shewa shoulder movement, on the beat.

## Do It Yourself

1. Dance one dance from this lesson for your family and ask: is it danced the same way here? Note any difference — do not correct the book or the elder; record both.
2. For the Gondar dance, write the body-parts answer: above the waist and below the waist, from your own dancing.
3. Find out whether your own locality has a dance inside this region's survey. Name it and its home.
