# What Traditional Dance and Games Are

## Do it first

Think of a festival or a holiday at home. Was there dancing? A game everybody knew without being taught? That dance and that game have a name, a home and an occasion. This lesson says what kind of thing they are.

## Sub lesson 1 — What traditional dance is

**Traditional dance** is a community's own cultural movement. The Grade 7 book says it plainly: it is **performed at festivals** and **expresses that community's identity**, inside its own cultural and environmental setting.

And what is culture? The Grade 5 book defines it in the same framing: **culture is a society's identity — its dress, and the rest of its way of life**. A traditional dance is that identity, moving.

## Sub lesson 2 — What traditional games are — and the games with laws

**Traditional games** are the games a community has played for generations — learned at home and at festivals, not from a rule book. The Grade 7 book opens its games chapter with what they are and why they matter, then shows the games that now have written laws.

The **Ethiopian Cultural Sport Federation** has given laws/rules to a pictured set of traditional games: **Korbo (ቆርቦ)**, **horse racing (የፈረስ ሸርጥ)**, **Gena (የገና)**, **Gebeta (የገበጣ)** and **Bub (የቡብ)**. A game with laws is still a traditional game — the laws protect it, so it can be played the same way in every place.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what culture means in the books' words, and name one traditional dance and one traditional game from your own area, with their occasions.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ask an elder: what game did you play as a child that children still play? Write its name, its home and when it is played.
2. From the five federated games, pick one and find out: is it played in your area? By whom, and when?
3. Name one way a traditional dance shows a community's identity — dress, occasion or movement.
