# Lesson plan — Traditional Dances of Our District

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Dances of Our District
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the survey widens from our area to our district (ወረዳ): dances performed with neck, shoulder, waist, arm and leg, their ornaments and props — and the men's work praise-dance, fukera, at the threshing floor.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the area dance lessons next door in this title — the same survey, one ring wider.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The district survey — body parts and props** — dances found at district (ወረዳ) level; performed with neck, shoulder, waist, arm and leg; ornaments/props named: ዘንግ stick, ዱላ, ሎሚ lemon.
2. **The men's work fukera — praise-dance at the threshing floor** — a pictured men's work praise-dance (የወንዶች የስራ ባህላዊ ፉከራ) — threshing grain.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the three Traditional Dances of Our Area lessons. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how the survey widens in Grade 3: from our area to the district (ወረዳ).
2. Name the body parts the district dances use: neck, shoulder, waist, arm and leg — and props named in the book: the ዘንግ stick, ዱላ and ሎሚ lemon.
3. Describe the men's work fukera dance and the work it praises.

## What you need
Your district's dance music or drum, and the props your dances use. Nothing bought.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tries the fukera shoulder movement in rhythm before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 13613]: a picture of men performing the work fukera dance while threshing grain, shoulders moving in rhythm — the student says what work the fukera accompanies and which body parts it moves.
   - ADD AUDIO: the district's dance music or a threshing-work rhythm → the student moves the shoulders on the rhythm.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson to a companion in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — made later, when videos are made.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Learner names dances the book does not → return to honesty: the survey is the district's own; a dance is recorded with its home, never invented.
- Fukera read as 'just work' → separate the two beats: the threshing rhythm, then the praise the shoulders add.
