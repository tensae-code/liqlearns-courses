# Traditional Dances of Our District

## Do it first

Your area was the first ring. Now the ring widens: the **district — ወረዳ**. Dances travel, villages visit, and the survey grows. Shoulders roll at a threshing floor: men are threshing grain, and the work itself becomes a dance of praise.

## Sub lesson 1 — The district survey — body parts and props

In Grade 3 the survey widens — from "our area" (Grades 1–2) to the **district, ወረዳ**.

The book describes the dances by their means: they are performed with **neck, shoulder, waist, arm and leg**, using ornaments and props it names — **ዘንግ** (a stick), **ዱላ**, and **ሎሚ** (lemon) among them.

Apart from the one pictured dance in the next sub lesson, the book's text does not name individual district dances — the survey is the district's own, and this lesson invents none.

## Sub lesson 2 — The men's work fukera — praise-dance at the threshing floor

"**Fukera — ፉከራ** is praise performed. The book pictures a **men's work praise-dance — የወንዶች የስራ ባህላዊ ፉከራ** — danced **while threshing grain** (Fig. 6.1.ሠ).

Work and dance are one act here: the rhythm that threshes is the rhythm that dances, and the dance praises the work and the workers. When you study it, watch the shoulders — the fukera's shoulder movement carries the praise.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how the survey widened in Grade 3, the five body parts of the district dances, and what the fukera praises.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ask in your district: which dances belong to the district beyond your own area? Add their names to your class record.
2. Move like the fukera: shoulders in rhythm, as if threshing. Feel the work in the dance.
3. Name a prop from the book's list and show how a dancer would hold it.
