# Lesson plan — Traditional Dances of Greece

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Dances of Greece
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Traditional dances of Greece: Greek folk dance — where it lives (festivals, weddings, home) and dancing it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

## Sub lessons
1. **Dance begins at home** — dance begins at home: babies are danced in arms with lullaby "tachtarismata", taught through a grandmother's photo album.
2. **Festivals, holds and costumes** — village festivals (panigiria) at feasts, weddings and christenings, everyone dancing in the square; the ways dancers hold each other; children joining the end of the dance circle; the festive costumes.
3. **Dance in myth, story and ancient Greece** — the Kouretes danced so Kronos would not hear the baby Zeus cry; the ancient Greeks honoured dance; the folk tale of the shepherd who danced with the kalikantzaroi.
4. **Dance travels through time and place** — dancing in 17th-century Corfu, under Ottoman rule, and the Cretan dance; dance as a whole-person activity tied to local customs; why Greece's varied terrain made many local dances and few Panhellenic ones.
5. **The dance band, improvisation and mimetic dance** — most Greek traditional dances leave room for the lead dancer to improvise; mimetic dances mime work and everyday life; the dance-song To Piperi; the zygies and kompanies that play for the dance.
6. **The named dances, dance by dance** — zonaradikos, tsamikos, syrtos-kalamatianos, tik, enteka and pentozalis, each with its region, as the Grades 5-6 book teaches them.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Traditional Dance and Games Are; Traditional Games of Greece. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say where Greek traditional dance lives: at home, at village festivals, weddings and christenings, in the square.
2. Name the ways dancers hold each other and say how a child joins the dance.
3. Tell how dance appears in Greek myth and ancient Greece, as the books tell it.
4. Say why Greece has many local dances and few Panhellenic ones.
5. Name the six named dances of the Grades 5-6 chapter and the region or hold each one carries, as the book gives them.

## What you need
A clear space to stand in a circle or line, as the books' dances use them.

## If the learner is stuck
- The dances blur together → anchor each one in the book's own words: zonaradikos is the Thracian belt-hold dance, pentozalis is the Cretan "five steps" war dance.
- "Traditional dance" feels like a show → the books teach it as home and festival life first: danced in arms as a baby, joined at the end of the circle as a child.
