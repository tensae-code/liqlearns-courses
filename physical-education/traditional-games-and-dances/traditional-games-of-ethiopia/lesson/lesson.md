# Traditional Games of Ethiopia

## Do it first

Take the stones from one hollow. Now drop them — one, one, one — into the hollows that follow, all the way round. You have made Gebeta's great move. This lesson gives Ethiopia's games their laws.

## Sub lesson 1 — The federated games — the five with laws

The Grade 7 book pictures the games **the Ethiopian Cultural Sport Federation has given laws/rules**: **Korbo (ቆርቦ)**, **horse racing (የፈረስ ሸርጥ)**, **Gena (የገና)**, **Gebeta (የገበጣ)** and **Bub (የቡብ)**.

Laws do not change what these games are — they let the same game be played the same way everywhere, and they name what happens when a game's laws are broken or not known: the book's Gena tasks ask exactly that — *the harm of not knowing or breaking a game's laws*.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gena — the rules pass

**Gena — የገና.** The book's tasks for Gena are the lesson: the **festivals** it is played at; **how it differs** from other traditional games; **when it began**; the **field's length and width**; its **kinds of play**; what its figures show; and its **value for productivity, friendship and culture**.

Work through the tasks as a class research set: what your locality knows, it teaches; what it does not, stays an honest open question.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gebeta — the rules pass: twelve holes and eighteen

**Gebeta — የገበጣ.** The book's board has **12 holes**: each player moves stones from **their six houses** in turn, sowing them round the board. Stone-count variants are named: **one-stone, two-stone and three-stone (ቅንጭቦሽ)** ways of playing. And there is a bigger board: the **18-hole gebeta (ስሉስ)**, pictured in the book.

Same game, more houses: the thinking deepens with the board.

## Sub lesson 4 — Horse racing and Gugs, and the Shah game

**Horse racing — የፈረስ ሽርጥ** stands in the book as a **ruled traditional sport**. Beside it, **Gugs — የፈረስ ጉግስ**, the horse game, is pictured with its own tasks.

**Shah — የሻህ ባህላዊ ጨዋታ.** The book's whole instruction for Shah is the right one: **play it with your friends**. Its form is learned in the playing, where it is known.

## Sub lesson 5 — Traditional wrestling — laws and practice

**Traditional wrestling (ትግል / ግብግብ)**: in ancient Ethiopia, **a person's strength was measured in wrestling, and its winners won great fame** — there is no firm record of when or where it began. Its equipment is named: **a bell, a whistle and a results board**. The book teaches **non-injurious wrestling types, including holding the waist with one hand**, then its **main laws** and its practice. Strength here is ruled strength: hold fairly, never to injure.

## Sub lesson 6 — Korbo — the ring and the spear

**Korbo (ኩርቦ)**: one player **rolls the Korbo ring along the prepared stick/track**, while the second player, **standing and moving inside the throwing zone, spears the ring's middle to stop it — and outscores the opponent.** The book gives Korbo's types, its main laws, the making of the stick and the ring as a task, and practice in pairs.

## Sub lesson 7 — Bub — the board of twenty-five stations

**Bub (ቡብ)** is a board game: **the Bub board has 24 stone stations plus one start station — 25 stations in all — with a diamond shape at the board's centre.** The book gives its main laws and its practice: stones move round the stations of a board you can draw and make yourself.

[Picture 14007 — Bub — a board of 25 stations]

## Sub lesson 8 — Archery — the traditional bow game

**Archery (ቀስት)** is the traditional bow game. The book gives its **main laws**, lists its **equipment in detail** as a task, and sets its practice — always in the book's safety framing: a traditional skill, practised at a target, supervised, never aimed at a person.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the five federated games. Then explain the 12-hole gebeta board: whose are the six houses, and where do the stones travel?

## Do It Yourself

1. Sow practice: on a 12-hole board, take the stones of one house and sow them perfectly three turns in a row.
2. Answer the book's Gena task in writing: the harm of not knowing or breaking a game's laws — one real example.
3. Play Shah with your friends, as the book directs. Afterwards, write how its form was taught to you.
