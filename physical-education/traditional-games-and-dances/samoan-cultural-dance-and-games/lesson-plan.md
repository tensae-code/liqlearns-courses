# Lesson plan — Samoan Cultural Dance and Games

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Samoan Cultural Dance and Games
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Samoan cultural dance and games: Samoa's own cultural practices expressed through movement in dance — work actions such as preparing coconut cream shown in dance — and lape, the Samoan hand game, as Samoa's Years 1–8 curriculum statement teaches them in its Year 3 movement work.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Samoan cultural dance** — cultural practices expressed through movement in dance — preparing coconut cream and other work actions shown in dance.
2. **Lape — the Samoan hand game** — lape named as a Samoan hand game.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Traditional Dance and Games Are; Moving to a Beat — Rhythmic Movement. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how Samoa's curriculum teaches cultural practices in movement: expressed through dance.
2. Give the book's dance example: preparing coconut cream and other work actions shown in dance.
3. Name Samoa's hand game from the curriculum: lape.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cultural dance feels like only performance → anchor it in the book: the dance shows work actions — preparing coconut cream — so the movement is the culture's daily life, danced.
- Lape's rules are not in the book's placed pages → name the game as the book names it; do not invent rules.
