# Lesson plan — Traditional Dances of Macedonia

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Dances of Macedonia
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Traditional dances of Macedonia: the Macedonian folk dances (ora) — holding hands, chains and steps, danced at feasts and weddings. — taught here through North Macedonia's Grade 9 and Grade 11 books, which teach the Macedonian folk ora as the country's own dances.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Macedonian folk dances (народни ора)** — North Macedonia's Grade 9 book teaches the Macedonian folk dances (народни ора): performing three ora of the pupils' and teacher's choice (named: Lesnoto, Teškoto, Kalajdžisko, Nevestinsko, Tresenica, Egejsko); how to hold hands and move in space, body carriage and steps; ritual and festive ora, danced in a semicircle, a closed circle, a snake form or in pairs.
2. **Macedonian folk dances (народни ора) — the gymnasium telling** — North Macedonia's Grade 11 book teaches the oro as cultural heritage handed down the generations, danced at feasts, weddings and gatherings, in open or closed chains moving to the right, dancers holding hands, shoulders or belts to music from gajda, tapan, zurla and kaval; its movements (turning, kneeling, hopping, shaking) run in slow and fast rhythms, above all the asymmetric Balkan metres 7/8, 9/8, 11/8, 7/16 and 13/16; the best known ora include Pravoto, Pajduško, Eleno mome and Čupurlika.
