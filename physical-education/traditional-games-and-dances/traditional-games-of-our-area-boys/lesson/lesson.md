# Traditional Games of Our Area (Boys)

## Do it first

The page is empty again — and again, on purpose. The boys' games of your area are not in this book, because they are in **your village, your neighbourhood, your elders' memory**. Bring one, and the lesson begins.

The book's teaching is the same task-only frame as the girls' lesson, in the same shape:

**Bring the boys' games of your area. Play them with your classmates. Explain which body part each game works most.**

No game is pictured and none is named in the book — the locality supplies the games. The lesson is the presentation frame: name, home area, rules, demonstration — then a full game, played properly, and the body-part study that closes every traditional-games lesson in these books.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of a boys' game of your area, and which body part it works most.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ask at home for a boys' game of your area — name and rules — and write them down.
2. Present the game to your class: name, home area, rules, demonstration.
3. Play a full game. Close with the body-part study.
