# Lesson plan — African Games — Bayoka, Mbube Mbube and Mamba

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** African Games — Bayoka, Mbube Mbube and Mamba
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** three traditional games of Africa, taught in the book's own game-card shape — age, players, equipment, time, aim and how to play: Bayoka the snake race (Zambia/Zaire), Mbube Mbube (South Africa) and Mamba.
**This lesson's own level:** it widens the traditional-games survey past Ethiopia to Africa; it stands on Traditional Games of Ethiopia.

## Sub lessons
1. **Bayoka — the snake race (Zambia / Zaire)** — the book's game card: outdoor, large groups of 12+, teams seated in a line holding waists with legs alternating, wriggling side to side round every obstacle to the finish; snakes must stay joined; 5–10 minutes.
2. **Mbube, Mbube — the lion and the impala (South Africa)** — the book's game card: all ages, 6 or more players, two blindfolds; the circle sings "mbube, mbube" faster and louder as the lion nears the impala, softer and slower as it strays; 2–3 minutes a round.
3. **Mamba — the last one caught (across Africa)** — the book's game card: a favourite children's game across African countries; all ages, groups of 10+, no equipment, 5–10 minutes a round; the aim is to be the last player caught by the snake.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Games of Ethiopia — the country's games first, then the continent's. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give each game's card: its country, players, equipment, time and aim.
2. Play Bayoka: a seated snake of 12 or more, holding waists, legs alternating, wriggling round every obstacle, staying joined.
3. Play Mbube Mbube: a blindfold lion hunting a blindfold impala while the circle sings it closer or farther.
4. Play Mamba: be the last player caught by the growing snake.

## What you need
Obstacles for Bayoka (chairs, boxes or tyres), two blindfolds for Mbube Mbube, nothing at all for Mamba.

## If the learner is stuck
- The snake keeps breaking → shorten the line and slow down; a joined slow snake beats a broken fast one.
- A learner fears the blindfold → they join the singing circle first; the circle is a full playing part.
