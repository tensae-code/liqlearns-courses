# African Games — Bayoka, Mbube Mbube and Mamba

## Do it first

Sit in a line on the ground. Hold the waist of the person in front of you. Now — wriggle sideways, all together, without letting go. You are a snake. Africa plays this game, and two more in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bayoka — the snake race (Zambia / Zaire)

**Bayoka (ባንዮካ) — Zambia / Zaire.** The book's card:

- **Where:** outdoors, among stones and trees — or built obstacles: chairs, boxes, tyres.
- **Players:** large groups, **12 or more**.
- **Time:** **5–10 minutes**.
- **How to play:** each team sits in a line, **holding waists**, legs alternating. The snake **wriggles side to side round every obstacle** to the finish line. **Snakes must stay joined** — a broken snake is not a snake. **First snake across wins.**

## Sub lesson 2 — Mbube, Mbube — the lion and the impala (South Africa)

**Mbube, Mbube (ምቡቤ) — South Africa.** The book's card:

- **Players:** all ages, **6 or more**; **equipment: two blindfolds**.
- **Time:** **2–3 minutes** a round.
- **How to play:** a blindfold **lion** hunts a blindfold **impala** inside a big circle. The circle sings **"mbube, mbube"** — **faster and louder as the lion nears, softer and slower as it strays**. The song is the lion's ears. If there is no catch in **1–2 minutes**, a new lion is chosen.

It is a sound-led game: listening *is* the skill.

[Picture 13811 — a blindfold lion and impala inside a singing circle]

## Sub lesson 3 — Mamba — the last one caught (across Africa)

**Mamba (ማማ) — played across African countries.** The book's card:

- **Aim:** to be the **last player caught by the snake**.
- **Players:** all ages, groups of **10 or more**; **equipment: none**.
- **Time:** **5–10 minutes** a round.

Caught players join the snake, and the snake grows — the mamba chain in the picture has already caught four. The last free player wins the round.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three games' cards: country, players, time, aim. Which game needs no equipment at all?

## Do It Yourself

1. Play Bayoka with chairs as obstacles: three wriggles round, staying joined. Time your snake.
2. Be the circle for Mbube: practise the song first — louder/faster, softer/slower — before any blindfold is tied.
3. Play Mamba at break. Count how many the snake has caught when you are caught.
