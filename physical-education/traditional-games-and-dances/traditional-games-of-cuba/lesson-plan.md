# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of Cuba

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of Cuba
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Traditional games of Cuba: Cuba's rhythmic and traditional games. — taught here through Cuba's way: its MINED national programmes and teacher Orientaciones Metodológicas.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Rhythmic and traditional games** — Cuba's Grade 2 programme teaches its rhythmic and traditional games inside the Rhythmic Activities unit — rhythmic and traditional games as the vehicle for expression and group norms; its Grade 2 teacher book works them out as named rhythmic and traditional games with organisation and rules.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

3. Say what the teacher book adds: named games with organisation and rules.
