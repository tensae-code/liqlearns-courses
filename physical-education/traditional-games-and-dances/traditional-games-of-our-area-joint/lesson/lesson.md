# Traditional Games of Our Area (Joint)

## Do it first

Hold on to the child in front of you. They hold on to the next. The chain moves — if one hold breaks, the game breaks. Joint games are the community in miniature: hold together, or fall apart.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Grade 1 named joint games and the chain game

Unlike the girls' and boys' sections, the joint section of the Grade 1 book **pictures and names** its games:

**Enchaewet Lijoch — እንጫወት ልጆች** ("let's play, children"). The invitation itself is the name.

**Endebeq Ke-wenberu Sir — እንደበቅ ከወንበሩ ስር.**

**Libe Libe Berta — ልቤ ልቤ በርታ.**

**The chain game — እርስበርስ ተያይዘን** (holding each other): children hold each other in a chain and move as one chain. Break the hold, break the game.

The chapter closes with its task on these games — play them, and explain them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Grade 2 games — Ayajibbo–Jibbo and Enchaewet Zare, and what games are

The Grade 2 book pictures two named joint games:

**Ayajibbo–Jibbo — አያጅቦ-ጅቦ.**

**Enchaewet Zare — እንጫወት ዛሬ** ("let's play today").

And this book says, in prose, what a traditional game *is*: games are **where a community works together, solves problems together, and reaches results together**. That sentence is the heart of this whole title — the chain game, the circle dance, and the named games are all that sentence, played.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the names of the joint games — three from Grade 1, two from Grade 2 — and the Grade 2 book's sentence about what games are.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play the chain game: a full round of movement with no broken hold.
2. Present Enchaewet Zare or Ayajibbo–Jibbo to a younger class: name, home, rules, demonstration.
3. After playing, each player says one problem the group solved together inside the game.
