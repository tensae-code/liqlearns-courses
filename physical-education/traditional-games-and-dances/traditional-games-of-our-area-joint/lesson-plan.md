# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of Our Area (Joint)

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of Our Area (Joint)
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the joint traditional games of the local area — the Grade 1 book's three named games and its chain game, and the Grade 2 book's Ayajibbo–Jibbo and Enchaewet Zare, with the books' teaching that games are where a community works and solves problems together.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the girls' and boys' games lessons next door in this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The Grade 1 named joint games and the chain game** — Enchaewet Lijoch ("let's play, children"), Endebeq Ke-wenberu Sir, Libe Libe Berta, and holding each other in a chain.
2. **The Grade 2 games — Ayajibbo–Jibbo and Enchaewet Zare, and what games are** — Ayajibbo–Jibbo and "Enchaewet Zare" (let's play today) pictured; the book's prose: games are where a community works together, solves problems together and reaches results together.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Games of Our Area (Girls) and (Boys). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name and play the Grade 1 named joint games: Enchaewet Lijoch, Endebeq Ke-wenberu Sir, Libe Libe Berta — and the chain game.
2. Name the Grade 2 games: Ayajibbo–Jibbo and Enchaewet Zare.
3. Say what the Grade 2 book teaches games are: where a community works together, solves problems together, and reaches results together.

## What you need
The games' own things as your area plays them, and a group. Nothing bought.

## If the learner is stuck
- Chain keeps breaking → slow down and shorten the chain; one unbroken short round beats a long broken one.
- A named game's rules are disputed → the locality's elders settle the rules; the course records the dispute in the lesson's presentation, never invents a rule.
