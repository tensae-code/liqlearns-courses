# Lesson plan — Tug of War

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Tug of War
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Tug of war: two teams pulling a rope to drag the other across the line — strength, coordination and teamwork. — taught here through Afghanistan's way: Afghanistan's Grade 7 teacher guide (Dari).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Tug of war (risman kashi) — a traditional Afghan game for holidays** — Tug of war — risman kashi — is a traditional Afghan game played for holidays, as the Grade 7 guide teaches it. Two equal teams pull a rope of 20 m or more.
2. **Pulling across the centre line — strength and coordination** — Each team pulls to drag the other team across the centre line. The game is played to build strength and coordination.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
