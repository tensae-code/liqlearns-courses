# Lesson plan — Traditional Dances of Our Area (Girls)

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Dances of Our Area (Girls)
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the girls' traditional dances of the pupils' own local area — brought by the class, danced together, and studied for which body part each dance moves most.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the rhythmic movement skills — moving to a beat. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The Grade 1 dances — opening with "Abeba Ayesh Wey"** — the Grade 1 picture series of girls' local traditional dances, opening with "Abeba Ayesh Wey"; the task: name which are done in your area, dance them, say which body part each moves most.
2. **The Grade 2 dances — your locality again, a year deeper** — the Grade 2 picture series of girls dancing traditional dances of their own locality, with the same task: dance them; say which body part each works most.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the rhythmic movement lessons in Moving Basics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name which of the pictured dances are done in their own area, starting with "Abeba Ayesh Wey".
2. Dance the area's girls' dances with classmates.
3. Say which body part each dance moves most.

## What you need
Music or a drum beat if your area's dances use them, and the traditional dress of your area if you have it. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner watches or joins one local girls' dance and names the body part that moves most, before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 13607]: a picture of girls dancing a local traditional dance in traditional dress, shoulders and necks moving to the rhythm — the student names the dance's home area if they know one, and says which body part moves most.

3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson to a companion in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — made later, when videos are made.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Nobody knows a local dance → ask elders and family first; the book's method is that the locality supplies the dance, never the page.
- A learner copies a far-away dance from a video instead → return to the task: name the dances done in YOUR area first.
- Shy dancers → begin with the body-part study: watch, name the moving part, then join for one short round.
