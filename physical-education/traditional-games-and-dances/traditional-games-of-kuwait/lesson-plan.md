# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of Kuwait

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of Kuwait
**Place in the curriculum:** Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** traditional games of Kuwait: the folk games Kuwaiti children played, such as Al-Hila hopscotch and Taq Taq Taqiya — taught here through Kuwait's way: a whole separate folk-games guide that teaches each game with its type, its definition, its rules and its song words, as national-heritage teaching.

## Sub lessons
1. **Al-Hila — hopscotch** — Al-Hila (hopscotch): hopping on one foot through six to eight squares chalked on the ground, tossing and picking up the small "rabaza" marker — a balance game girls played on sandy ground.
2. **Taq Taq Taqiya** — Taq Taq Taqiya: players sit in a circle while one walks behind them singing and secretly drops a cap or cloth; the chosen player must notice and chase the dropper round the circle.
3. **Tabaq Hanna Tabaq Mash** — Tabaq Hanna Tabaq Mash: a two-girl strength game — the partners stand back to back, lock arms, and take turns bending forward to lift each other onto their backs while chanting the call and answer.
4. **Haybala wa Haybala** — Haybala wa Haybala: a singing circle game — a leader stands in the middle, jumps to one of the girls at the song's cue naming a kind of food, and at "turn, my sisters" the whole circle turns with expressive movements.
5. **Ghazala Bazala — the choosing song** — Ghazala Bazala: the choosing song — the pivot of most Kuwaiti girls' games, a sung way of picking who plays which part, performed standing in a half-circle with pointing at each player.
6. **Shawt Shawt Ya Batta** — Shawt Shawt Ya Batta ("Kick, kick, O duck"): a role-play singing game — a mother, her daughter and the boy who wants to join the girls' play — that teaches Kuwaiti customs and values.
7. **Ana Al-Dheeb Bakalkum — the wolf game** — Ana Al-Dheeb Bakalkum ("I am the wolf and I will eat you"): a dramatic singing chase game — the mother stands guard before her line of daughters while the wolf prowls, makes frightening sounds and tries to snatch one.
8. **Amakor Tah Bil-Tannour — blindfold tag** — Amakor Tah Bil-Tannour ("Amakor fell in the oven"): blindfold tag for ages 6–15 — the blindfolded Amakor tries to catch the players who touch him, dodge away and keep chanting the rhyme.
9. **Al-Anbar — the seven stones** — Al-Anbar: a two-team target game — knock down the stack of seven stones ("the seven") with a cloth ball thrown from the "shish" line seven metres away, then rebuild the stack.
10. **Al-Muhaibis — the ring game** — Al-Muhaibis: the ring-guessing game — one team hides a small ring in one player's closed fist and the other team must find which hand holds it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the ten games of Kuwait's folk-games guide and say how each is played, as the guide teaches it.
2. Say where these games were played: in the sandy lanes (الفرجان), with palm-fronds, ropes and pebbles.
3. Say how the guide teaches each game: with its type, its definition, its rules and its song words.
