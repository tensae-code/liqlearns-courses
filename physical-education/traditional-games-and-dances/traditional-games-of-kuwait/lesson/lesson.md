# Traditional Games of Kuwait

## Do it first

Say what you already think this lesson is about, before any explaining — then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Al-Hila — hopscotch

Al-Hila (hopscotch): hopping on one foot through six to eight squares chalked on the ground, tossing and picking up the small "rabaza" marker — a balance game girls played on sandy ground.

## Sub lesson 2 — Taq Taq Taqiya

Taq Taq Taqiya: players sit in a circle while one walks behind them singing and secretly drops a cap or cloth; the chosen player must notice and chase the dropper round the circle.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tabaq Hanna Tabaq Mash

Tabaq Hanna Tabaq Mash: a two-girl strength game — the partners stand back to back, lock arms, and take turns bending forward to lift each other onto their backs while chanting the call and answer.

## Sub lesson 4 — Haybala wa Haybala

Haybala wa Haybala: a singing circle game — a leader stands in the middle, jumps to one of the girls at the song's cue naming a kind of food, and at "turn, my sisters" the whole circle turns with expressive movements.

## Sub lesson 5 — Ghazala Bazala — the choosing song

Ghazala Bazala: the choosing song — the pivot of most Kuwaiti girls' games, a sung way of picking who plays which part, performed standing in a half-circle with pointing at each player.

## Sub lesson 6 — Shawt Shawt Ya Batta

Shawt Shawt Ya Batta ("Kick, kick, O duck"): a role-play singing game — a mother, her daughter and the boy who wants to join the girls' play — that teaches Kuwaiti customs and values.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ana Al-Dheeb Bakalkum — the wolf game

Ana Al-Dheeb Bakalkum ("I am the wolf and I will eat you"): a dramatic singing chase game — the mother stands guard before her line of daughters while the wolf prowls, makes frightening sounds and tries to snatch one.

## Sub lesson 8 — Amakor Tah Bil-Tannour — blindfold tag

Amakor Tah Bil-Tannour ("Amakor fell in the oven"): blindfold tag for ages 6–15 — the blindfolded Amakor tries to catch the players who touch him, dodge away and keep chanting the rhyme.

## Sub lesson 9 — Al-Anbar — the seven stones

Al-Anbar: a two-team target game — knock down the stack of seven stones ("the seven") with a cloth ball thrown from the "shish" line seven metres away, then rebuild the stack.

## Sub lesson 10 — Al-Muhaibis — the ring game

Al-Muhaibis: the ring-guessing game — one team hides a small ring in one player's closed fist and the other team must find which hand holds it.
