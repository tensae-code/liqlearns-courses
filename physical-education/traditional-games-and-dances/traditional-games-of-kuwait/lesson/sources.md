| Kuwait — Taq Taq Taqiya: players sit in a circle while one walks behind them singing and secretly drops a cap or cloth; the chosen player must notice and chase the dropper round the circle (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 16 |
| Kuwait — Tabaq Hanna Tabaq Mash: a two-girl strength game — the partners stand back to back, lock arms, and take turns bending forward to lift each other onto their backs while chanting the call and answer (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 18 |
| Kuwait — Haybala wa Haybala: a singing circle game — a leader stands in the middle, jumps to one of the girls at the song's cue naming a kind of food, and at "turn, my sisters" the whole circle turns with expressive movements (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 20 |
| Kuwait — Ghazala Bazala: the choosing song — the pivot of most Kuwaiti girls' games, a sung way of picking who plays which part, performed standing in a half-circle with pointing at each player (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 22 |
| Kuwait — Shawt Shawt Ya Batta ("Kick, kick, O duck"): a role-play singing game — a mother, her daughter and the boy who wants to join the girls' play — that teaches Kuwaiti customs and values (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 24 |
| Kuwait — Ana Al-Dheeb Bakalkum ("I am the wolf and I will eat you"): a dramatic singing chase game — the mother stands guard before her line of daughters while the wolf prowls, makes frightening sounds and tries to snatch one (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 26 |
| Kuwait — Amakor Tah Bil-Tannour ("Amakor fell in the oven"): blindfold tag for ages 6–15 — the blindfolded Amakor tries to catch the players who touch him, dodge away and keep chanting the rhyme (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 28 |
| Kuwait — Al-Anbar: a two-team target game — knock down the stack of seven stones ("the seven") with a cloth ball thrown from the "shish" line seven metres away, then rebuild the stack (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 30 |
| Kuwait — Al-Muhaibis: the ring-guessing game — one team hides a small ring in one player's closed fist and the other team must find which hand holds it (teacher guide — evidence) (original target `traditional-games-and-dances/traditional-games-of-kuwait`) | Kuwait Girls Folk Games Guide (2nd ed., primary), printed p. 32 |
