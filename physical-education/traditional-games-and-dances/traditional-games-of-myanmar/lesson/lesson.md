# Traditional Games of Myanmar

## Do it first

Name one chasing game from your own childhood that everybody knew without a book. Games handed down like that are this lesson's subject — now read how Myanmar's books teach their own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Htok-See-Toe — the line-crossing tag game

Myanmar's Grade 6 and Grade 7 books teach **Htok-See-Toe**, the country's traditional line-crossing tag game, with its full rules: a court marked in zones, **runners** who must cross the zones, and **taggers** who guard the lines and try to stop them. The Grade 7 book gives the game its own section (§2-5-4) with the court zones, the runners and taggers, and the full rules; the Grade 6 book prints the court and the full rules in its games chapter.

## Sub lesson 2 — "What Time Is It, Great Jasmine Flower?"

The Grade 2 book names a traditional call-and-chase game of Myanmar playgrounds: **"What Time Is It, Great Jasmine Flower?"** The class asks the time and crosses the flower's ground; when the call turns, the flower chases. The game is printed with its full rules.

## Sub lesson 3 — White Rope, Black Rope

The second named Grade 2 game is **White Rope, Black Rope**: two rope teams crouch in the lanes; at the caller's word — white! black! — one side chases the other home. This game too is printed with its full rules.
