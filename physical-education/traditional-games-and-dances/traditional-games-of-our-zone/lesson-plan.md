# Lesson plan — Traditional Games of Our Zone

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Traditional Games and Dances · **Lesson:** Traditional Games of Our Zone
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 7 — Traditional Games and Dances (CURRICULUM.md path: … → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the zone survey's games — a wrestling-type game, the spinning-target stick throw, circle ball games and genna, the dama board game that trains thinking, and team running and chasing games.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Traditional Games of Our District next door — the same survey, one ring wider.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Strength, aim and the circle — wrestling, the spinning target, and genna** — a traditional wrestling-type game (a game of individual fitness: throw your opponent by the back, side or seat — a traditional sport kind); hitting a spinning round object by throwing a stick (ዘንግ) from a set distance; hitting a ball out of a circle with a ball, between two teams; genna-type ball striking into a circle.
2. **Dama — the thinking game — and team running games** — the dama game: its foot-soldier and tank-soldier pieces named; the book notes it improves thinking, attention, decision-making and patience; and team running/chasing games (እግረኛና ታንከኛ named in the body). The closing exercise: listing at least four traditional games of your area.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Traditional Games of Our District. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the wrestling-type game and the spinning-target stick throw as the book describes them (it names neither).
2. Play the genna strike: the ball struck with the genna stick into a circle.
3. Say what the book says dama improves: thinking, attention, decision-making and patience — and name its two kinds of pieces.

## What you need
A stick (ዘንግ) and a ball for genna and the target game, a marked circle, a dama board and pieces if your area plays it. Nothing bought.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stick games drift toward people → the safety line is absolute: targets only, a set distance, nobody beyond the target, ever.
- Dama's rules are disputed → as with every traditional game here, the game's home settles its rules; the book names pieces and benefits, not a rulebook.
