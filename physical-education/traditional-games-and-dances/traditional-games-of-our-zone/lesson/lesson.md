# Traditional Games of Our Zone

## Do it first

A stick swings — crack! — and the ball rolls toward the circle. In, or out? The zone's games are games of aim, strength, and patience: a wrestler's grip, a thrower's eye, and a board where the slowest player with the clearest mind can beat everyone.

## Sub lesson 1 — Strength, aim and the circle — wrestling, the spinning target, and genna

**The wrestling-type game.** The book describes it without a proper name: a game of **individual fitness** in which you **throw your opponent by the back, side or seat** — a traditional sport kind. (The lesson keeps the book's description and invents no name for it.)

**The spinning-target game.** Its aim: **hitting a spinning round object** — of tin, wood, carton or plastic — **by throwing a stick (ዘንግ) from a set distance**.

**The circle ball game.** Hitting a ball **out of a circle with a ball**, played between **two teams**.

**Genna — ኳስን በገና በመምታት ክብ ውስጥ ማስገባት** (Fig. 6.2.መ). Striking the ball **with the genna stick, into a circle**. Aim and power, judged by one question: did it go in?

## Sub lesson 2 — Dama — the thinking game — and team running games

**Dama — የዳማ ጨዋታ** (Fig. 6.2.ሠ). The board game of the survey. The book names its pieces — the **foot-soldier** and the **tank-soldier** — and states plainly what the game improves: **thinking, attention, decision-making and patience**. Not every traditional game trains the legs; dama trains the mind that leads them.

**Team running and chasing games.** The survey closes with the running games — **እግረኛና ታንከኛ** named in the body — teams running and chasing, the oldest form of all.

And the chapter's closing exercise is this lesson's too: **list at least four traditional games of your area.**

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how genna is scored, what the spinning-target game aims at, and the four things dama improves.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play genna: mark the circle, set the striking place, and strike in turns. Count balls in.
2. Set the spinning target safely — a round object of carton or plastic, a set distance, nobody beyond the target. Throw the stick in turns.
3. Do the closing exercise: write at least four traditional games of your area, with one line on each.
