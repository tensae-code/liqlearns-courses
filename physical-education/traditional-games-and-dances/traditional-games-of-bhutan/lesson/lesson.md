# Traditional Games of Bhutan

## Do it first

Name one game or dance from your own childhood that everybody knew without a book. Games handed down like that are this lesson's subject — now read how Bhutan's books teach their own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fun with Hopscotch

Bhutan's *PP–I Suggested Activities* student book teaches this as its own lesson: **hopscotch played with hopping and sliding technique points and balance variations (bean bag on head/hand/shoulder)**. The teaching is cited to the book and printed page in lesson/sources.md, and this lesson names it as its subject without adding to it.

## Sub lesson 2 — What Time Is It, Charo?

Bhutan's *PP–I Suggested Activities* student book teaches this as its own lesson: **the Bhutanese listening-and-chase game: children step forward by the called hour until "lunch time" starts the chase (charo = friend)**. The teaching is cited to the book and printed page in lesson/sources.md, and this lesson names it as its subject without adding to it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Zeko

Bhutan's *Class IV Suggested Activities* student book teaches this as its own lesson: **the Bhutanese ring game: throw the zeko (ring) over a pole from 1, 2 or 3 metres for points, with team roles of thrower, retriever, scorer and pole guard**. The teaching is cited to the book and printed page in lesson/sources.md, and this lesson names it as its subject without adding to it.
