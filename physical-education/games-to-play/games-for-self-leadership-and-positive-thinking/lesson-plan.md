# Lesson plan — Games for Self-Leadership and Positive Thinking

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Games to Play · **Lesson:** Games for Self-Leadership and Positive Thinking
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 6 — Games to Play (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Grade 4's renamed first games family — leading yourself, thinking positively, keeping your word, working patiently, resting, and respecting every player's difference.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Games for Self-Leadership next door — the same family, a year deeper.

GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Copying two movements in order** — the Grade 4 book's play: following a partner's movement and doing two movements in imitation — you do the two movements your partner shows.
2. **Managing myself — my word, my patience, my rest (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 4 teacher guide's definition lesson: self-management is controlling your behaviour, feelings and actions by yourself; its parts are keeping your word, working with patience, and taking rest.
3. **Good attitudes and respecting difference (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 4 teacher guide: positive attitudes trained by playing — being a good sportsperson, and accepting and respecting diversity; everyone is included.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Games for Self-Leadership. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Copy a partner's two movements in order, without a miss.
2. Say what self-management is and name its three parts: keeping your word, working with patience, taking rest.
3. Show a positive attitude in play: being a good sportsperson, and accepting and respecting diversity.
4. Say why rest belongs inside training, not outside it.

## What you need
A partner and open space. A mat helps for rest. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Forgets the first of two movements → the partner shows them one at a time with a pause; join them only when both are held.
- Sore loser → return to the guide's own fruit: a good sportsperson accepts results. Practise the words: 'Well played.'
- Skips rest → frame it as the guide does: rest is one of the three parts of managing yourself, not a reward for finishing.
