# Games for Self-Leadership and Positive Thinking

## Do it first

Your partner shows you TWO movements, one after the other. Now you do both — in order, without a miss. Your memory leads, your body follows, and your temper stays calm when you drop one. That is this lesson: leading yourself, and thinking well of the game and of the other players while you do it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Copying two movements in order

The Grade 4 book renames the family — **self-leadership and positive thinking** — and its pictured play is the copying game, grown: you do **the two movements your partner shows**, in imitation and in order.

One movement was Grade 3. Two, in order, is Grade 4: your memory must hold the first while your eyes take the second. Lead yourself through both.

## Sub lesson 2 — Managing myself — my word, my patience, my rest (teacher guide — evidence)

**Self-management — ራስን መመራት.** The guide's taught answer: managing yourself means **controlling your behaviour, feelings and actions by yourself**, in activities and games. And it has three parts:

**Keeping your word — ቃልን መጠበቅ.** Doing for others what you promised.

**Working with patience — በትእግሥት መሰራት.** Not abandoning a task.

**Taking rest — ዕረፍት ማድረግ.** After work, resting so the body and the mind recover.

The guide frames the whole person in the same chapter: **body, mind and psychological state together** — and by doing varied physical activities, learners build their social and psychological health.

## Sub lesson 3 — Good attitudes and respecting difference (teacher guide — evidence)

Attitude — አመለካከት — toward yourself and toward others is **trained by playing**. The guide names its two fruits:

**Being a good sportsperson — ጥሩ አድማዊ መሆን.** Accepting results, and accepting others.

**Accepting and respecting diversity — ብዝህነትን መቀበልና ማክበር.** Others' abilities, bodies and views differ — and are respected.

The activities are the games themselves, **played so everyone is included**. A positive thought is not only said; it is played.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three parts of self-management, and which one is hardest for you. Then say what a good sportsperson does when they lose.

## Do It Yourself

1. Two-movement copying with a partner, five rounds — then let your partner make it three movements and try.
2. Keep your word in a game: promise your partner one thing before you play (a turn, a pass, a job) — and do exactly that.
3. After hard play, take real rest: lie down, breathe slowly, and notice your body recovering. Rest is part of the training.
