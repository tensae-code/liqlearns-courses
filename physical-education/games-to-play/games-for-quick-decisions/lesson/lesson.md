# Games for Quick Decisions

## Do it first

A mouse runs around a circle of children, and a cat runs after it. The mouse can slip through a gap in the circle — the cat cannot. In one heartbeat the mouse must decide: through, or around? These games are all that heartbeat: decide fast, decide well.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Grade 1 games — Cat and Mouse, Homeless, Yaekob

These games force a **fast decision** — think too long and the moment is gone.

**Cat and Mouse — አይጥና ድመት.** The mouse runs around the circle of children; the cat chases. The circle is the mouse's friend: gaps open for the mouse.

**"Have you seen my handkerchief?" — ማሀረቤን ያያችሁ.** The asking game: a question is asked, and the play turns on the answer.

**Homeless — ቤት የለሽ.** Players need a home place; one player has none. When the moment comes, everyone moves — will the homeless player find a place?

**Yaekob — ያዕቆብ.** Another named game of the book's decision family.

**The circle-turning game — የክብ መዞር.** The circle itself turns; your decision turns with it.

The chapter's closing task is the book's own: explain the benefit of taking part in the various games.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Grade 2 games — Nekahuny and the handkerchief race

**Nekahuny — ነካሁኝ** ("you touched me"). A touch decides the play — the name of the game is the call itself.

**The handkerchief race.** Two children start from **equal distances**. Between them lies a handkerchief. At the signal both race: snatch the handkerchief and run — or be caught. Equal distances mean nobody can complain: the decision and the speed are yours.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 3 game — throw before the runner leaves

Now the decision carries an aim. A runner waits on a line. Throwers hold small balls. The play of the book: **throw at a target below the waist before the runner leaves the line**.

Aim too long, and the runner is gone. Throw wildly, and you miss. Decide: *now* — and throw true, below the waist, the way the game is set.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Grade 4 games — follow the change, and find the ball blindfolded

**Follow the change.** The leader changes the movement; you change with it — the instant you see it. Your decision is your eyes.

**Find the ball blindfolded.** In pairs, inside a marked circle, one partner's eyes are covered (ዓይንን ተሸፍኖ ኳስ ፈልጎ በእግር መምታት — with the eyes covered, finding a ball and kicking it with the foot). The searcher feels and listens; the partner guides. Safety first, always: a marked circle, a guiding partner, slow steps — nobody runs blindfolded.

## Sub lesson 5 — Home-less, chasing games, Who Do You Want?, and Let Me In Today (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guides print these games with their rules — and the rules are part of the decision.

**The Home-less game — ቤት አልባው.** A marked centre spot is home. Learners move around it; at the signal, the one without a home must find a place. The printed rules: you may leave your home for another — **and no pushing**.

**Run-and-escape — ሮጦ ማምለጥ.** Two groups face each other. At the signal one group runs and the other chases, catching by a touch. Caught learners join the other side. Inside the marked area only, no pushing.

**Gesh-Gedo — ጌሽ-ጌዶ.** A chasing game on a 10-metre path: one chases the other along the line; at the end, the roles swap; the rest watch and take turns fairly.

**Relay handovers and moving safely — ቅብብሎሽ / ከአደጋ መጠንቅቅ.** Rows run with an object to the marker, return, and hand over — the team that finishes accurately wins, and waiting learners encourage in an orderly way. And in the "watch out for danger" game, children move inside a hoop area and **stand still at the signal, without colliding**.

**Who Do You Want? — ማንን ትፈልጋላችሁ?** Two groups face each other. One calls "who do you want?"; the other names a learner, who must cross by breaking the callers' held-hands line, under strict safety rules: holding by the arm, no tripping.

**Let Me In Today — አስገብኝ ዛሬ.** A circle game: the outside learner must decide the moment and the place to break into the circle, while the circle decides together how to close the gaps.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how Cat and Mouse works, and the one decision the mouse must make in a heartbeat. Then tell them the two safety rules of Who Do You Want?

## Do It Yourself

1. Play Cat and Mouse with a circle of friends. Swap the cat and the mouse every round.
2. Set the handkerchief race: two runners, equal distances, one handkerchief. Best of five — keep the distances exactly equal.
3. Play Let Me In Today: one outsider, one circle. The outsider feints left, darts right. The circle closes gaps together.
