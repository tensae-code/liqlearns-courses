# Lesson plan — Games for Quick Decisions

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Games to Play · **Lesson:** Games for Quick Decisions
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 6 — Games to Play (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** games that force a fast decision — chase, choose, aim, or break in — where waiting too long loses the game.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the first movement skills and on Games for Self-Control. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The Grade 1 games — Cat and Mouse, Homeless, Yaekob** — Cat and Mouse; "Have you seen my handkerchief?"; Homeless; Yaekob; and the circle-turning game.
2. **The Grade 2 games — Nekahuny and the handkerchief race** — Nekahuny ("you touched me") and racing from equal distances to snatch a handkerchief and run.
3. **The Grade 3 game — throw before the runner leaves** — throwing small balls at a target below the waist before a runner leaves the line.
4. **The Grade 4 games — follow the change, and find the ball blindfolded** — changing the movement when the leader changes it, and — blindfolded, in pairs inside a marked circle — finding a placed ball and kicking it.
5. **Home-less, chasing games, Who Do You Want?, and Let Me In Today (teacher guide — evidence)** — the teacher guides' decision games with their printed rules: the Home-less game; run-and-escape and Gesh-Gedo chasing; relay handovers and moving safely among others; Who Do You Want?; and Asgebiny Zare, the circle-entry game.

6. **Games for responsible decisions** — the Grade 5 book's decision family: games with printed procedures and rule-sets stressing cooperation and helping each other, each pupil carrying out their given responsibility.
7. **Deciding in the moment — and solving problems in competition (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 5 teacher guide: the ball-to-goal game (pass left, pass right or shoot — before the defenders close) and the ball-receiving game; then the solver steps for problems in competition and the 4-against-4 worked example.
8. **Decision games — below-the-knee dodgeball and the waist chain** — the Grade 6 book's decision games: thrower lines hitting the middle group below the knee while they jump to dodge, and chains of five holding waists, scoring on the rival chain's last player.
9. **Ilama — and choosing your own challenge** — the Grade 7 book's Ilama (ኢላማ) target game and its reflection tasks on winning, losing and the rules — with the Grade 7 guide's "What a decision!" shooting game, where each learner selects their own shooting distance.

10. **Two Houses and the Kangaroo Race — the Grade 8 decision games** — the Grade 8 student book's §§2.3.1–2.3.2: Two Houses on its 20–30 m line with 1 m house-circles, and the Kangaroo Race run hopping with tied legs.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Games for Self-Control and Games for Playing Together, its neighbours in this title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play Cat and Mouse, Homeless, Yaekob and the circle-turning game from the Grade 1 book.
2. Play Nekahuny and the handkerchief snatch race from the Grade 2 book.
3. Aim and throw at a target below the waist before a runner leaves the line (Grade 3 form).
4. Play the Home-less game, the chasing games, Who Do You Want?, and Asgebiny Zare from the teacher guides — by their fairness and safety rules.

5. Make the pass-or-shoot decision at speed, within the moment, and walk the five solver steps on a real problem.
6. Choose your own challenge distance and evaluate the decision honestly afterwards.

## What you need
A handkerchief, small balls, a marked area and a marked circle. Lines on the ground for the chasing paths. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Decides too slowly → shorten the game: a smaller circle, a shorter path. Fast decisions grow from small games.
- Chasing turns to pushing → stop and restate the printed rules: catch by touch, no pushing, inside the marked area only.
- Blindfolded searcher is frightened → swap roles, let them guide first, and keep the circle small and the steps slow.
