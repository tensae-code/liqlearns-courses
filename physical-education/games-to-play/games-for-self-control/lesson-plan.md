# Lesson plan — Games for Self-Control

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Games to Play · **Lesson:** Games for Self-Control
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 6 — Games to Play (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** games that train a child to control their own body and temper — moving in turn, in line, and by the rules.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the first movement skills — walking, running and handling a ball. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The first games — rows, over and under, lines and zigzags** — the Grade 1 games: passing a ball in two rows; throwing and catching a ball over and under; the line-crossing game; zigzag (in-and-out) running.
2. **Knowing my strengths and valuing my own ability (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 1 teacher guide's self-awareness teaching: knowing your own strength, the ball-into-basket aiming game, and building self-respect — abilities differ and every child's ability is valued.
3. **The Grade 2 games — holding on, Dirchi Dirchi, passing backwards** — the Grade 2 games: pairs and groups crouching, holding each other and jumping forward together; the Dirchi Dirchi game; kneeling and passing a ball backwards.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Moving Basics lessons — the learner can walk, run and pass a ball. Recommended, never forced.
- The other Games to Play lessons are next door in this same title.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play the row-passing, over-and-under and line-crossing games in turn, without leaving their place.
2. Run the zigzag (in-and-out) run keeping control of their speed.
3. Say what self-control in a game is: working while respecting the rules.
4. Show one thing they can do well, honestly, and value another child's different ability.

## What you need
A ball, a line or rope on the ground, and a group of players. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Leaves their place or rushes → shrink the game: a shorter row, a slower ball. Control is built small first.
- Says self-control is 'being quiet' → show the basket game: it is checking yourself against a target, not silence.
- Falls in the partner jump → the pair counts aloud together before each jump; the count is the shared control.
