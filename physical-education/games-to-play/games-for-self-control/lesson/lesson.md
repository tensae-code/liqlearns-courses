# Games for Self-Control

## Do it first

Stand in a row with your friends. The first child passes the ball to the next, and the next, all the way to the end of the row. Nobody runs. Nobody shouts over another. The ball must visit every pair of hands. Did it? That waiting, that holding yourself in your place — that is what these games train.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first games — rows, over and under, lines and zigzags

These are the first games of this family, and every one of them asks the same thing: hold yourself steady while the game moves around you.

**Passing a ball in two rows.** Two rows face the game together. The ball goes from hand to hand down the row. Your job is not to be fast — your job is to be ready, and to pass cleanly to the next child.

**Throwing and catching, over and under.** The ball travels over heads and under arms. You watch where it is coming from and you send it on the way it must go.

**The line-crossing game.** A line is crossed only the way the game says — not when you feel like it.

**Zigzag running (in-and-out).** You run in and out, in and out, changing direction again and again — and you keep your speed under your own control, so you never crash and never fall.

Play each game, then do what the book's players do: perform it, and explain what it gave you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Knowing my strengths and valuing my own ability (teacher guide — evidence)

Games also teach you about **yourself**.

**Knowing my strength — ጥንካሬን መረዳት.** In play you find out what you can do well. Show it honestly: not bigger than it is, not smaller.

**The aiming game.** A girl throws a ball into a basket — ኳስ ወደ ቅርጫት ውስጥ ማስገባት. You set yourself a target, you try, and you check yourself: did it go in? That checking is how you learn what you can do.

**Building self-respect — የራስን ክብር ማዳበር.** Abilities differ from person to person. Your friend may throw better; you may run better. Every child's ability is to be valued — yours too.

Playing and living with others starts here: feelings, respect and working together are trained inside games.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 2 games — holding on, Dirchi Dirchi, passing backwards

A year on, the games ask for more control, because now your body is tied to a partner's body too.

**Holding each other and jumping forward together.** Pairs and groups crouch, hold each other, and jump forward together. If one child rushes, both fall. Control is shared now.

**Dirchi Dirchi — ድርቺ ድርቺ.** The named game of this family in the Grade 2 book: play it the way your teacher shows it, keeping its rule from start to finish. Self-control includes working while respecting rules — and its benefit is that you can tell your own task apart from others'.

**Kneeling and passing a ball backwards.** You kneel, and the ball goes backwards over the line of players. You cannot see where it goes — you must trust your hands and your control.

## Sub lesson 4 — Self-control — waiting, turns and the judge's word (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 6 teacher guide completes its social chapter with the third fruit: **self-control**.

In games, the learner **controls impulses, waits for turns, and accepts the judge's and the game's rules**. The chapter's summary names the three skill types built across the whole chapter: **togetherness, communication, and standing for self and others** — and responsible decision-making plays the key role in all of them.

Self-control here is not silence. It is the impulse felt — and governed — in the middle of a game you want to win.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: name one game from this lesson. Say what the player must control — their hands, their speed, or their temper — and what happens if they do not.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play the two-rows passing game with your family or friends. Count how many clean passes your row makes before a drop.
2. Set a basket or box as a target. Throw a ball into it ten times and check yourself honestly: how many went in?
3. Play the hold-and-jump game with a partner: crouch, hold, and jump forward together five times without falling.
