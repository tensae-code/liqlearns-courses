# Lesson plan — Games for Self-Leadership

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Games to Play · **Lesson:** Games for Self-Leadership
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 6 — Games to Play (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** games that train leading yourself — holding your place, copying exactly, managing your feelings, keeping rules unwatched, and beating the clock fairly.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Games for Self-Control and Games for Playing Together, its neighbours in this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Holding a place and copying a partner** — the Grade 3 book's self-leadership games: taking and holding a place, and following/copying a partner's movement.
2. **Managing my feelings — Akukulu and the ball-return game (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 3 teacher guide on self-management in games, taught through the Akukulu game and the ball-return game against the clock.
3. **Self-discipline and Pipali Kol (teacher guide — evidence)** — self-discipline defined through the body's government — keeping rules without being watched; social experiences named from play; and the Pipali Kol game of strict turns.
4. **Beating the clock fairly — timed rope jumps and bicycle legs (teacher guide — evidence)** — giving time limits in games — a boundary of time, space and turns, and keeping it; responsibility defined; rope jumping inside a time limit; the cycling movement.

5. **The cloth-snatch game — leading yourself at speed** — the Grade 5 book's self-leadership games: each pupil stands with a cloth tucked at the back of their belt and, on the "start" command, tries to snatch others' cloths while guarding their own.
6. **Self-knowledge games — hoop race, handkerchief-hand, rooster fight** — the Grade 6 book's games for self-knowledge, opening its social-and-emotional chapter: knowing your strengths, weaknesses and limits, and controlling your actions, feelings and thoughts to reach a goal.
7. **Managing myself and thinking positively (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 5 teacher guide on self-management: the ability to carry out your daily tasks with courage, without being pushed by others; personal conduct improved through the subject; and positive thinking — seeing the good path, finishing what you start, accepting the result and trying again.
8. **Confidence, control and calm breathing (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 7 teacher guide's self-awareness lesson: self-confidence, self-control and self-awareness defined; neck and shoulder movements; and active breathing — breathing out on a chosen word, such as "peace".

9. **Rescue from Danger and Change of Place — the Grade 8 self-knowledge games** — the Grade 8 student book's Chapter 2 opening games (§§2.1.1–2.1.2): Rescue from Danger on its 20–30 m parallel-line field, and Change of Place, closing with the book's reflection task.
10. **Rolling in Admiration and mindful minutes (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 8 teacher guide's §2.1: the Rolling in Admiration dice game, the self-awareness and self-management definitions, and resilience through mindful meditation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Games for Self-Control — self-leadership grows out of self-control. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Hold a place and copy a partner's movement exactly, without delay.
2. Play Akukulu and the ball-return game, and manage their feelings in play: not leaving in anger, waiting their turn, accepting the result.
3. Say what self-discipline is — keeping rules without being watched — and play Pipali Kol by strict turns.
4. Work inside a time limit fairly: timed rope jumps and the bicycle-legs movement.

5. Play the cloth-snatch game inside its rules and name what self-knowledge each Grade 6 game trains.
6. Use active breathing to calm down, and do the neck and shoulder movements as the guide prints them.

## What you need
A ball, a rope, a cloth for Akukulu, a mat for bicycle legs, and a way to count time (a stopwatch or a counted count). Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Leaves a game in anger → name the feeling out loud, let them breathe, and bring them back for the next round; the guide's model is calm behaviour.
- Breaks Pipali Kol turns → slow the exchange down; turns first, speed later.
- Cannot pedal and count together → count aloud for them until the legs know the rhythm.
