# Games for Self-Leadership

## Do it first

Face a partner. They move — an arm up, a step to the side. Now you: the same, exactly, without delay. Hard, isn't it? Leading yourself means your body obeys *you* — your eyes, your patience, your temper. These games train that.

## Sub lesson 1 — Holding a place and copying a partner

**Taking and holding a place — ቦታ መያዝ.** Your place in the game is yours to hold. Take it, and keep it, whatever moves around you.

**Copying a partner's movement — የጓደኛን እንቅስቃሴ ተከትሎ መስራት.** Your partner shows a movement; you follow and do it — the same movement, in the same way. Leading yourself starts with watching truly and obeying your own eyes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Managing my feelings — Akukulu and the ball-return game (teacher guide — evidence)

**Self-management** is controlling your own behaviour and feelings while playing with others: **not leaving the game in anger, waiting your turn, accepting the result**. The teacher's own behaviour is the model — the guide says so.

**Akukulu — አኩኩሉ ጨዋታ.** One learner starts at the centre; the others take their places around. The seeker calls; the others answer and move without being caught — under the game's printed rules of fair chasing.

**The ball-return game — የኳስ ምልስ ጨዋታ.** A ball is thrown out or kicked away. The named learner must bring it back **within 2–3 minutes**, while the class counts and waits **in order**. The waiting is part of the game: your feelings, held, while another child runs.

## Sub lesson 3 — Self-discipline and Pipali Kol (teacher guide — evidence)

**Self-discipline — ተግሣፅ.** The guide defines it through a picture: a person controls themselves the way a government controls a country. In games it shows as one thing: **keeping rules without being watched**.

**Social experiences — ማህበራዊ ልምዶች.** What we learn from living and playing with others: helping, sharing, greeting, resolving quarrels. They are identified and named from the games you played — name yours.

**Pipali Kol — የፒፓሊ ኮል ጨዋታ.** Two groups. One group's learner calls and throws; the other group answers and moves. The exchange continues by **strict turns** — the side that breaks the turn order loses the round. A discipline-and-listening game.

## Sub lesson 4 — Beating the clock fairly — timed rope jumps and bicycle legs (teacher guide — evidence)

**Giving a time limit — ውስኔ መስጠት** — means setting a boundary: of **time, of space, and of turns** — and keeping it. And **responsibility** (ኃላፊነት) is defined in the same pages: doing what is yours to do, for yourself and for others.

**Rope jumping inside a time limit.** How many jumps are possible in the set time? The clock does not care who you are — beat it fairly, by jumping, not by arguing.

**The cycling movement — ሳይክል.** Lying on your back, legs pedalling in the air in turns, hands supporting — for the set count or the set time. Your legs are the wheels; the limit is the road.

## Sub lesson 5 — The cloth-snatch game — leading yourself at speed

**The cloth-snatch game.** Each pupil stands with a **handkerchief or cloth tucked at the back of their belt**. On the **"start" command**, everyone tries to **snatch others' cloths** — while guarding their own. The Grade 5 book prints the game with its full procedure and its "systems to follow" (rules).

Leading yourself is tested at speed here: attack and defence in the same second, inside the rules, without losing your temper when your own cloth is taken.

## Sub lesson 6 — Self-knowledge games — hoop race, handkerchief-hand, rooster fight

The Grade 6 book frames this family first: **self-knowledge and self-direction** mean knowing your **strengths, weaknesses and limits**, and controlling your actions, feelings and thoughts to reach a goal. Three games train it.

**Hoop on-and-off race — የቀለበት የማጥለቅና የማውለቅ እሽቅድድም.** Each team passes a hoop **over the head, down the body and off at the feet** to the next member; the first team finished wins.

**Handkerchief-hand — እጅ ከፍንጅ.** A circle passes a small ball right and left; on the **"enough"** command, whoever holds it is out — dropping it to the ground *before* the command saves you. The last two win.

**Rooster fight — የደሮ ጥል.** Pairs hop facing each other, **arms folded on the chest**, pushing **palm to palm**; losing balance loses; **first to 10 points wins**.

## Sub lesson 7 — Managing myself and thinking positively (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 5 teacher guide defines **self-management**: your internal ability to control yourself — **carrying out your daily tasks with courage in hard situations, without being pushed by others**.

Through this subject, personal conduct improves: **giving up lying, not wanting what is not yours, not cheating, keeping the order of home rules, and entering physical activity with cleanliness of body and character**.

**Positive thinking — አምንታዊ አስተሳሰብ** is seeing the good path in a situation instead of only its difficulty. Build it the guide's way: **begin tasks expecting to succeed, face problems as solvable, finish what you start, accept the result and try again** — and carry it from games into study and life.

## Sub lesson 8 — Confidence, control and calm breathing (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 7 teacher guide names the inner skills. **Self-confidence**: a feeling of self-assurance from appreciating your own abilities — confident movers experience joy and grow competence. **Self-control**: regulating your responses to avoid undesirable behaviours, increase desirable ones and reach long-term goals. **Self-awareness**: knowing your thoughts, behaviours, values, goals, strengths and challenges. **Self-management**: regulating emotions and behaviours so you and others feel free.

Two movement practices train awareness of the body: **neck movement** — seated, legs stretched, rotate the neck slowly without jerk, five times each way — and **shoulder movement** — kneeling, lifting both shoulders toward the ears and releasing, 3 sets of 5.

And for calming: **active breathing**. When upset, worried or over-excited: sit or stand comfortably; choose a relaxing word — the guide's example is **"peace"** — breathe in, and softly say the word as the air leaves. Use it any time you need to calm down.

## Sub lesson 9 — Rescue from Danger and Change of Place — the Grade 8 self-knowledge games

**Rescue from Danger (ከአደጋ ማዳን)** is played on a field marked by **two parallel lines 20–30 metres apart**, with its full play and rules printed in the book — a self-knowledge and self-direction game: you learn what you do when danger comes.

**Change of Place (ቦታ ለውጥ)** is an enjoyable game to build the same two strengths — self-knowledge and self-direction. After the Chapter 2 games, the book asks the reflection question that makes them self-leadership games: **name one strength, and one thing to correct, that the games showed you about yourself.**

## Sub lesson 10 — Rolling in Admiration and mindful minutes (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 8 teacher guide's self-awareness game is **Rolling in Admiration** — it works like Catch the Compliment: at least three players sit in a circle; one rolls the dice, and **says that many positive characteristics about the person next to them**; then the next person takes the dice.

Self-awareness means **conscious knowledge of your own character and feelings**; self-management is **regulating your emotions, thoughts and behaviour in different situations**. Resilience — recovering from setbacks and keeping going — is practised with **mindful meditation**: get comfortable in a quiet place, consider a timer, focus on breathing, notice your thoughts, give yourself a break.

## Sub lesson 11 — Managing myself on purpose — Capture the Flag, Three Legs, Trust (teacher guide — evidence)

Self-management begins with **self-knowledge** — accepting responsibility for your own thinking, and building the desire to play by your own activity. A person who is **fit for a purpose** shows **reliability, duty, courage, honesty, careful thought and predictability**, and openly shows their ideas and values. What blocks self-management: not guiding yourself, not building guidance early, and company that pulls you away from managing yourself.

**Capture the Flag — the markers raid.** Two teams on a **20 m × 20 m** field, split by a middle line. Each team guards its **markers** (cloths) at its own back line. Raid the other team's markers and bring them home — but if you are **tagged** after crossing into their half, you are **out**. Your team must plan its own special skills for the raid, and guard its own cloth while it raids.

[Picture 35104 — a capture-the-flag field with markers — two halves, a marker flag at each end]

**Three Legs — the two-legs-tied race.** Pairs run with their **adjacent legs tied together**, in lanes. The tied legs must **step together**, carefully and in order — a pair that does not move as one falls.

**Trust games.** Trust is taught through play: **holding hands in a circle and leaning together**, and **partner trust falls and leans** — trusting a person, showing trust, and earning trust, inside the group.

## Sub lesson 12 — Knowing yourself through games — Grade 7

Play shows you yourself. In games, your **ability**, your **character** and the **unknown self** — the parts of you that you did not know were there — all come out where you can see them. Social interaction, emotion and games are linked: how you play is how you meet other people and how you meet your own feelings, and games train **emotional control** as they reveal it.

So use what play shows you: **strengthen the good** that the game reveals in you, and **improve the weak**. That is this book's own way of knowing yourself — not a label stuck on you, but a thing you watch in your own play and then work on.

## Sub lesson 13 — Growing inside through games — the Grade 8 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)

**Social and emotional learning** grows a learner's capacity to bring skills, attitudes and behaviours together to deal **effectively and ethically with daily tasks and challenges** — and a physically active class is the ideal place to grow it. A personal best in the mile run, playing on a successful team, or **dropping a catch during a key play** — each one is a moment where this learning comes into play.

A growing child's **mental, intellectual, emotional and social** development is closely related to their **physical** development. A physically fit person possesses a **well-balanced personality — mentally sharp, emotionally stable, socially well-adjusted**. The chapter this opens works toward three things: making a practice plan for a skill you choose yourself, showing communication and leadership that build the team, and analysing situations to solve problems.

## Sub lesson 14 — Managing myself and getting along — what the Grade 3 chapter builds (teacher guide — evidence)

In Grades 1 and 2 you learned small movement skills. The Grade 3 chapter takes those same skills and builds a person with them — the social and psychological person: managing yourself, knowing your own behaviour, respecting others, and solving problems together.

The chapter builds six things, and you have already practised most of them in this lesson:

1. **Movement that builds self-management** — ራስን የማስተዳደር ክህሎትን የሚያዳብር የሰውነት እንቅስቃሴ.
2. **Practising your own conduct** — የራስን ስነ ምግባር መለማመድ.
3. **Identifying social experiences** — ማህበራዊ ልምዶችን መለየት: naming what playing with others teaches you.
4. **Solving problems in games** — በጨዋታዎች ችግሮችን መፍታት.
5. **Practising giving time limits** — ጊዜ ውስኔ መስጠትን መለማመድ.
6. **Practising giving notice** — ማስጠን መለማመድ: a clear signal before a change, so others can get ready.

By the chapter's end you can show self-management skill in varied activities, identify social experiences, solve problems in games, give time limits, and focus your attention — the whole of this lesson, in one view.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what self-management looks like in a game — the three signs. Then tell them what self-discipline adds: the rule kept with nobody watching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Copy-the-partner: your partner shows two movements; repeat both, in order, three times without a miss.
2. Ball-return at home: a ball is thrown out; one runner brings it back against a counted 2 minutes while everyone else waits in order. Swap.
3. Timed rope jumps: set one minute. Jump, count honestly, write your number. Rest. Try again and beat the clock fairly.
