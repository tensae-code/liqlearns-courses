# Games for Thinking Carefully

## Do it first

Stand in a circle. The leader calls: "Raise your hand!" Did you move? The command had no name in front of it. In the games of this lesson, moving at the wrong moment loses — thinking comes *before* the movement.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Yaekob game — move only when Yaekob is said

**The Yaekob game.** The class stands in a circle. The leader gives commands: "Yaekob, raise your hand!" "Yaekob, fold your leg!" "Yaekob, sit!" You do the movement **only when the word "Yaekob" is said first**. A command without Yaekob is a trap: freeze.

The game's printed rules include this one: **deceiving others in the game is forbidden** — the leader plays fair, and so do you. Deep thinking starts here: hear the whole command, judge it, *then* move.

## Sub lesson 2 — Thinking games — the narrow goal and group-by-number

**Critical thinking** is named in the Grade 6 book: understanding, analysing, combining and judging when a task is complex. Two games train it.

**Ball through a narrow goal — ኳስ በጠባብ ጎል ማሳለፍ.** The goal is only **1 metre** wide. From **10–15 metres**, pass or shoot with the **inside of the foot**, one touch per player. Points are recorded, and the winning team is praised. A narrow goal punishes rushing: aim first.

**Group by number — በቁጥር መያያዝ.** The circle runs to the centre on command. A number is called — group in **exactly that number**. Anyone left without a group is out; the last two win. Counting, scanning and choosing partners, all at speed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Special football — winning by thinking, talking and respect

**Deep thinking through play** is the Grade 7 book's framing: thinking deeply is what distinguishes humans, and in games it shows as **reading an opponent's weakness and changing your tactics**.

**Special football — ልዩ እግር ኳስ.** Girls and boys play together, with modified rules; girls get special care and priority. Winning needs **talking, respect, communication and tactics** based on the opponents' strengths and weaknesses — **not emotion**. After the game, the book asks the hard question: *were the winners really the better thinkers?* Answer it honestly.

## Sub lesson 4 — Deep thinking in games — plan, notice, correct (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guides give deep thinking its definition: it is **the higher thought process by which, inside an activity, you weigh your decisions and your responsibilities** — thinking carefully about a matter from many sides before acting.

In practice it runs in a circle: **plan the practice, notice the results, correct**. Children meet new movement problems and inner questions inside games and solve them — that solving *is* the training. A game you only rush through teaches your legs; a game you think through teaches your head as well.

## Sub lesson 5 — Circle Target — the Grade 8 deep-thinking game

**Circle Target (የክብ ኢላማ)** is a deep-thinking game: you **throw flat stones at a circle target**. One rule sharpens the thinking — **a burnt stone scores nothing, for the thrower or for the team.** The game is played for a set time or a set number of rounds, and the book closes with the thinking question: **did decision-making decide this game?** Aim is only half of it; choosing the throw is the other half.

## Sub lesson 6 — Deciding well — SWOT and the six problem-solving steps (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 8 teacher guide teaches decision-making as a skill with tools. **Responsible decision making** weighs safety concerns, social norms, the realistic consequences of each action, and the wellbeing of yourself and others.

**SWOT** analyses a situation in four boxes: **Strengths and Weaknesses (inside you or your team), Opportunities and Threats (outside)** — helpful: strengths and opportunities; harmful: weaknesses and threats.

**Problem solving** runs in six steps: **define the problem; list all possible solutions; evaluate the options; select the best solution; make an implementation plan; communicate your solution.** The guide practises it with a **Scavenger Hunt**: teams race through a list of tasks, from simple to hard, and the team that completes them all first wins.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the Yaekob rule and why deceiving is forbidden. Then tell them what deep thinking means in a game, in your own words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play Yaekob with your family: five commands, only three with the name. Anyone who moves on a nameless command sits out one round.
2. Make a 1-metre goal from two stones. From 10 metres, ten inside-foot passes: count how many pass through.
3. After your next game of any kind, write the three deep-thinking steps: what you planned, what you noticed, what you corrected.
