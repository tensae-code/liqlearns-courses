# Lesson plan — Games for Communication and Cooperation

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Games to Play · **Lesson:** Games for Communication and Cooperation
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 6 — Games to Play (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the second new Grade 3 family — games won by talking, signalling and working together: the Fishing game, and the signal games of the teacher guide.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the other Games to Play lessons, especially Games for Playing Together.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The Fishing game (Grade 3)** — the Grade 3 book's pictured Fishing game — the founding game of the communication and cooperation family.
2. **The Fishing game and the ball in the circle (Grade 4)** — the Grade 4 book pictures the Fishing game again, and adds the ball-inside-the-circle game.
3. **Signals before changes and the four-beat clapping game (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 3 teacher guide: giving notice before a change — a whistle, count or call — so others can get ready; the Gebeta game named among signal/turn games; and the four-time beat game.

4. **The jumping relay — cooperation in fours** — the Grade 5 book's cooperation games: groups of four take turns to jump from a set place and hand the jump over in a relay to cover a long distance; every group member must take part.
5. **Cooperation games — hand tug, baton relay, overhead relay** — the Grade 6 book's cooperation games, with its definition of cooperation: working to a shared goal — clear communication, listening, owning mistakes, respecting diversity.
6. **The bottle target — communication, awareness and applause** — the Grade 7 book's target game: two teams throw from their own ground to knock down a bottle 10 m away; most knock-downs in 3 minutes wins — and the losers must applaud and embrace the winners.
7. **Fair play, compassion and getting along (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 7 teacher guide's relationship lesson: fair play and its five basic principles, compassion, social awareness and relationship skills — practised in the Turn Back game and in role play.

8. **Pair Hunting and the Rope Tug — the Grade 8 social games** — the Grade 8 student book's §§2.2.1–2.2.2: Pair Hunting (last uncaught pupil wins) and the 3-against-3 Rope Tug.
9. **Ball in a Box and Special Basketball — the Grade 8 cooperation games** — the Grade 8 student book's §§2.5.1–2.5.2: Ball in a Box (pass down the line, last pupil runs, repeat until all have run) and Special Basketball in four equal teams; GIF 14017 shows the pass-and-run.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Games for Playing Together. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play the Fishing game in a group, in both its Grade 3 and Grade 4 pictured forms.
2. Play the ball-inside-the-circle game.
3. Give and answer signals before changes — a whistle, a count, a call — and clap the four-beat pattern together, joining exactly on the beat.
4. Name the Gebeta game as one of the signal and turn games the guide names.

5. Play the Grade 6 cooperation games by their printed frames and complete the bottle game's sportsmanship ending.
6. State fair play's five principles and show compassion the guide's way: cheering for, not against.

## What you need
A ball, a whistle or a clear voice for signals, and a group. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Nobody calls or answers → make the rule explicit: a play without a call does not count. Communication becomes the game's law.
- Claps drift off the beat → shrink to one row, slow the beat, count aloud; add the second row when the first is solid.
- Signals are ignored → the signal-giver also freezes the game until the signal is answered; readiness is the point.
