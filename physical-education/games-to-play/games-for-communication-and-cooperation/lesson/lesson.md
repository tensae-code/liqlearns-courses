# Games for Communication and Cooperation

## Do it first

Some games cannot be won alone, and cannot even be *played* alone. The fisher casts — but who catches? Who calls? Who answers? These games run on talking, signalling, and pulling together.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Fishing game (Grade 3)

**The Fishing game — አሳ ማጥመድ.** The Grade 3 book pictures it (Fig. 3.5.ሀ) as the game of this family: a fishing game played together, casting and catching in a group. A cast that nobody answers catches nothing — the game is the **communication** between the caster and the catchers, and the **cooperation** of the whole group playing it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Fishing game and the ball in the circle (Grade 4)

The Grade 4 book pictures **the Fishing game — አሳ ማጥመድ** again (Fig. 3.5.ሀ), a year deeper, and adds its partner game:

**The ball-inside-the-circle game.** A ball inside a circle of players — kept, moved and answered by the circle together. The circle is a team only if it talks: who has it, who wants it, who is free.

## Sub lesson 3 — Signals before changes and the four-beat clapping game (teacher guide — evidence)

**Giving notice — ማስጠን.** Before a game changes, you give a **clear signal** — a whistle, a count, or a call — so others can get ready. A change without a signal is a trap; a change with a signal is communication. The guide names the **Gebeta game — ገበጣ ጨዋታ** among its signal and turn games.

**The four-time beat game — የአራት ጊዜ ምት ጨዋታ.** Learners stand in **two rows** and clap the four-beat pattern. One learner's clap starts; the others **join exactly on the beat**. After 5 rounds, the beat changes — and the rows must hear the change and follow it together. Cooperation you can hear.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why a cast that nobody answers catches nothing, and what a signal does for a game. Then clap the four-beat pattern and let them join exactly on the beat.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play the Fishing game with your group — every cast must be answered by name.
2. Signal practice: lead a small game. Before every change, give your signal — whistle, count, or call. No signal, no change.
3. Four-beat clapping in two rows: start alone, let the rows join on the beat. After 5 rounds, change the beat and keep everyone together.
