# Games for Playing Together

## Do it first

Stand in a circle and hold the circle's shape. One player walks around the outside carrying a handkerchief. Where will it drop? Behind you? You must watch, wait, and be ready to run — and the whole circle plays with you. These are the games where the group is the player.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Grade 1 games — Monday–Tuesday, Akukulu Alnegam, and the handkerchief games

Pair, group and whole-class games build one thing: **helping each other**. These are the Grade 1 book's named games.

**The Monday–Tuesday game — ሰኞ-ማክሰኞ.** A named circle game of the class — play it as your teacher shows it, keeping together.

**Akukulu Alnegam — አኩኩሉ አልነጋም.** Another of the book's pictured games, played by the group together.

**The hand-on-cup game — እጅ ከፍንጅ.** Hands and a cup: quick hands, together.

**The picking game — ቆርኪ/ጠጠር/ኳስ/ለቀማ.** Cork, stone or ball — picking the right thing, in the group.

**The handkerchief-snatch game — መሀረብ ንጥቂያ.** A player circles outside with a handkerchief, drops it behind one seated player, and runs. The chosen player snatches it up and gives chase. The whole circle watches, ready.

The placements name these games and picture them; each locality's teacher holds the full rules, and the games are played the way the class is shown.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Grade 2 games — playing together grows

The Grade 2 book keeps the same family and carries it on: pair and group games, pictured through the chapter, that build **social interaction** — children playing together, helping each other through the game.

The teaching is in the pictures of the book: the group is the player, and a game is finished together or not at all. Play the games of this chapter the way your teacher shows them, and after each one, do the book's closing task: perform, and explain the benefit you got from playing together.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 3 games — the relay hand-over

**Handing over in a relay — ዕቃ መሸኘት/መቀባበል.** Your team runs in turn, and at the change the object passes from hand to hand. Drop it, and the whole team pays. Hold it clean, and the whole team flies.

The book states what such games build: **team spirit, response to sound, speed and accuracy**. Four things at once — your ears (the sound), your hands (the hand-over), your feet (the speed), and your team (the spirit).

## Sub lesson 4 — The Grade 4 games — five rounds around the circle

**Five rounds around the circle.** The ball must travel five full rounds around a circle of players, hand to hand, without a drop. The book states its aim in words: **building working together, and hand–eye coordination**.

**Collecting small balls.** The group's other pictured game: small balls are gathered in together — many hands, one job, done as a group.

By now the pattern of this whole lesson is clear: the older the book, the more the game belongs to the whole group at once.

## Sub lesson 5 — Understanding others, the three-legged game, and respecting others (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide teaches the same family from the inside — what playing together does to the players.

**Understanding other people's feelings — የሌሎችን ችግሮች መረዳት.** People differ — in feelings and in inclinations (ስሜትና ዝንባሌ). Games must include everyone.

**The three-legged game — የሦስት እግር ጨዋታ.** Pairs join their middle legs together and walk and run **holding hands**, in step. Cooperation you can feel in your own legs: your partner's step is your step. The guide prints the procedure step by step.

**Giving respect to others — ለሌሎች ክብር መስጠት.** Respect is shown in games, at school and in the community. Its game is **Abro Mechewet — አብሮ መጫወት** (playing together): 4–6 children pass a ball around a 5-metre circle, changing direction at the whistle. A dropped ball is retrieved and play continues — nobody is put out of the game for a drop.

## Sub lesson 6 — The Fisherman, Form a Family, Abo Yaeqob, and Pass It Back (teacher guide — evidence)

Four more games from the Grade 2 teacher guide.

**The Fisherman game — ዓሣ አጣማጅ.** One learner is the fisherman; the rest are fish inside the marked water. The fisherman catches by touch. Caught fish help catch the rest — one catcher becomes many. The last free learner wins.

**Form a Family — ቤተሰብ መመስረት.** Learners move freely. At the teacher's number call, everyone must quickly form groups — families — of that size, complete and settled. **No learner is left out**: the class counts and helps.

**Abo Yaeqob — አቦ ያዕቆብ.** A chain game: learners join hands in the order called and follow spoken body-part instructions in sequence, together.

**Pass It Back — ኳስ በእጅ ወደ ኋላ መቀበል.** Lines pass the ball back, hand to hand, to the end of the line. The receiver controls it and the team completes the pass without dropping.

## Sub lesson 7 — Follow the leader and tug-of-war (teacher guide — evidence)

**Working together in a row — እንቅስቃሴዎችን በጋራ መስራት.** A leader leads a line of children in a lunge and step movement, and the row follows **exactly**. Together means the same movement, at the same time, from everyone.

**Tug-of-war — ገመድ ጉተታ.** Two teams pull one rope. The team that pulls the other's marker across the line wins.

The guide ties the game to knowing yourself, in three parts: **knowing yourself** (ራስን ማወቅ ወይም መገንዘብ), **expressing yourself** (ራስን መግለፅ), and **accepting a partner's or group's opinion** (ከንደኛ ወይም ከቡድን አስተያየት መቀበል). A team that cannot accept each other's opinions cannot pull together.

## Sub lesson 8 — Myself and others — the Grade 2 chapter opener (teacher guide — evidence)

Social and psychological education — ማህበራዊና ስነ-ልቦናዊ ትምህርት — means knowing yourself, controlling your feelings, respecting other people's work, and building social relationships. In this society everyone has a share. The chapter's learning is carried by games in three ways: looking after yourself through games, building social relationships through games, and making responsible decisions through games. Its promises: show positive personal attitudes, respect others' feelings and views, value social relationships in cultural sport and games, and take responsibility for your decisions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about the handkerchief-snatch game: where the players stand, what the outside player carries, and what happens when the handkerchief drops. Then say what all these games build.

## Do It Yourself

1. Play the handkerchief-snatch game with a full circle. Everyone must be chosen once before anyone is chosen twice.
2. Form a Family: one caller calls a number — 3! 5! 2! — and everyone forms groups of that size. Count together: is anybody left out? Help them in.
3. Pass a ball five rounds around your circle without a drop. If it drops, start that round again — together.
