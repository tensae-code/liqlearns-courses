# Lesson plan — Games for Playing Together

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Games to Play · **Lesson:** Games for Playing Together
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 6 — Games to Play (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the pair, group and whole-class games that build helping each other — the social heart of the games family.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the first movement skills and on Games for Self-Control next door. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The Grade 1 games — Monday–Tuesday, Akukulu Alnegam, and the handkerchief games** — the Grade 1 named games: the Monday–Tuesday game, Akukulu Alnegam, the hand-on-cup game, the cork/stone/ball picking game, and the handkerchief-snatch game.
2. **The Grade 2 games — playing together grows** — the Grade 2 book's pair and group games that build social interaction, continuing the Grade 1 set.
3. **The Grade 3 games — the relay hand-over** — passing and handing over an object in a relay; the book states such games build team spirit, response to sound, speed and accuracy.
4. **The Grade 4 games — five rounds around the circle** — passing a ball five rounds around a circle (its aim: working together and hand–eye coordination) and collecting small balls.
5. **Understanding others, the three-legged game, and respecting others (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 1 teacher guide: understanding others' feelings, the three-legged partner game played step by step, and the Abro Mechewet circle-passing game of respect.
6. **The Fisherman, Form a Family, Abo Yaeqob, and Pass It Back (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 2 teacher guide's games: the Fisherman tag game, forming families at a number call, the Abo Yaeqob instruction chain, and backward team passing lines.
7. **Follow the leader and tug-of-war (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 4 teacher guide: performing movements together in a row following the leader exactly, and tug-of-war — with its self-knowledge points: knowing yourself, expressing yourself, accepting a partner's or group's opinion.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Games for Self-Control — the learner can already wait, hold a place, and keep a rule. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Play the Grade 1 named games — Monday–Tuesday, Akukulu Alnegam, hand-on-cup, cork/stone/ball picking, handkerchief-snatch.
2. Hand an object over in a relay at speed without dropping it, and pass a ball five rounds around a circle.
3. Play the teacher-guide games: the three-legged game, the Fisherman game, Form a Family, Abo Yaeqob, Pass It Back, tug-of-war.
4. Say what playing together builds: helping each other, respecting others' feelings, and team spirit.

## What you need
A ball, a handkerchief or scarf, and a group of players. A basket helps for collecting games. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- A child is left out of groups → use the guide's own rule: the class counts and helps; nobody is left out.
- Hand-overs keep dropping → slow the relay down and shorten the run; speed is added only after clean hands.
- Tug-of-war turns rough → stop, restate the three self-knowledge points, and pull again gently.
