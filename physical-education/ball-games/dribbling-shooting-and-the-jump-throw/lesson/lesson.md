# Dribbling, Shooting and the Jump Throw

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Dribbling and bounce-passing (በማንጠር) keep the ball moving under your hand. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dribbling and bounce-passing — Grade 5

Dribbling and bounce-passing (በማንጠር) keep the ball moving under your hand. Dribble while moving, change direction without losing the ball, and play the dribbling games the book sets — the bounce family closes the Grade 5 skill ladder.

## Sub lesson 2 — Basketball low dribble — Grade 6

Basketball dribbling (ኳስ ማንጠር) pushes the ball to the ground continuously, under control, the body slightly bent at the waist, ready to move in any direction. The low dribble stays below the knee: it shields the ball from opponents and escapes pressure. Practise it with figure-8 drills, dribbling the ball in a figure of eight between the legs.

## Sub lesson 3 — High dribble and dribbling games — Grade 6

The high dribble bounces the ball higher, for moving fast in open space. Dribble between obstacles without touching them. Then play the dribbling games in letter shapes, moving sideways, forwards and backwards — the dribble must survive every direction change.

## Sub lesson 4 — Basketball standing shot — Grade 7

First the grip: hold the ball correctly in the hands. The standing shot (በመቆም ወደ ቅርጫት መወርወር) shoots at the basket from standing, without a jump — aim, extend, and follow through. Then join the earlier skills in game-form practice, to revise and improve them together.

## Sub lesson 5 — Handball dribble and jump throw — Grade 7

Handball has been played for a long time, in the world and in Ethiopia. Carry the ball at the waist in both hands; dribble (ማንጠር) as the basketball parts taught, joining the movements together. Score with the jump throw: run, jump, and throw at the goal in the air, landing in balance.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
