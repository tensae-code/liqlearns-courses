# Dribbling, Shooting and the Jump Throw

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Dribbling and bounce-passing (በማንጠር) keep the ball moving under your hand. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dribbling and bounce-passing — Grade 5

Dribbling and bounce-passing (በማንጠር) keep the ball moving under your hand. Dribble while moving, change direction without losing the ball, and play the dribbling games the book sets — the bounce family closes the Grade 5 skill ladder.

## Sub lesson 2 — Basketball low dribble — Grade 6

Basketball dribbling (ኳስ ማንጠር) pushes the ball to the ground continuously, under control, the body slightly bent at the waist, ready to move in any direction. The low dribble stays below the knee: it shields the ball from opponents and escapes pressure. Practise it with figure-8 drills, dribbling the ball in a figure of eight between the legs.

## Sub lesson 3 — High dribble and dribbling games — Grade 6

The high dribble bounces the ball higher, for moving fast in open space. Dribble between obstacles without touching them. Then play the dribbling games in letter shapes, moving sideways, forwards and backwards — the dribble must survive every direction change.

## Sub lesson 4 — Basketball standing shot — Grade 7

First the grip: hold the ball correctly in the hands. The standing shot (በመቆም ወደ ቅርጫት መወርወር) shoots at the basket from standing, without a jump — aim, extend, and follow through. Then join the earlier skills in game-form practice, to revise and improve them together.

## Sub lesson 5 — Handball dribble and jump throw — Grade 7

Handball has been played for a long time, in the world and in Ethiopia. Carry the ball at the waist in both hands; dribble (ማንጠር) as the basketball parts taught, joining the movements together. Score with the jump throw: run, jump, and throw at the goal in the air, landing in balance.

## Sub lesson 6 — The basketball jump shot — Grade 8

Stand with feet slightly apart and knees slightly bent. Hold the ball, jump straight up, and release at the very top of the jump, aiming at the basket. Land balanced, on both feet, ready for what comes next. Practise the shape from your stance first, then shoot, then play it in 3-against-3 games.

## Sub lesson 7 — Lay-ups — Grade 9 underhand, Grade 10 extended-arm

The lay-up is basketball's most essential shot — you lay the ball up near the basket with one hand, off the backboard and in, for two points. The underhand lay-up, step by step: step toward the basket with the right foot, and shoot with the right arm as the right leg lifts — picture a string tying the arm and the leg together so they rise as one. Aim at the marked spot on the backboard, and practise until your muscles remember. The extended-arm (reverse) lay-up of Grade 10 extends the same shot around and under the defence — catch the ball landing on your foot, step forward, and lay it up on the far side; build a group checklist and check each other's lay-ups with it, right hand and left hand. Modified games make it everyone's: lower the rings, play half-court, and use a smaller ball (size 4 or 5, or a volleyball); finish with a dribbling-and-shooting relay race, and with mini basketball — two teams of five, all the skills together.

## Sub lesson 8 — Handball — the dive throw, goalkeeping and passing — Grades 8–10

Handball joins running, jumping, catching and throwing. The dive throw at goal (በመስፈንጠር ወደ ግብ መወርወር) — Grade 8's throw, beyond Grade 7's jump throw: hold the ball at waist height, throw at goal as you dive forward, let the throwing arm follow the ball through, and land safely. Goalkeeping — the keeper faces the shot directly: the ready posture is feet apart, knees slightly bent, arms bent at the elbows with palms at chest or head level and elbows sideways, so the hands can intercept at any height; the keeper shifts position and posture as five outfielders (two wings, three centres) pass the ball, saves with hands, legs and torso, and starts the counterattack with a fast, flat, precise throw-off. Passing: the lob over an opponent, and the over-, under-, lean and jump passes for shorter distances — practised one pair at a time, one shooting and one keeping goal inside the 6 m area, with the conditioning set: one-leg skipping, a vertical jump into half-splits on a signal, and goal-area leg swings.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
