# Lesson plan — The Rules of Tennis

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Ball Games · **Lesson:** The Rules of Tennis
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ball Games (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The rules of tennis: court, equipment, scoring, the serve and how a match runs. — taught here through Afghanistan's way: Afghanistan's Grade 10 teaching guide (Pashto).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Tennis court and equipment** — The Grade 10 guide sets out the tennis court and equipment: racket weights and the ball's size and weight, court dimensions for singles and doubles, and the net.
2. **Scoring, the serve, foot faults and lets** — The guide teaches scoring in points, games and sets, how the serve works, foot faults and lets — the rules a tennis match runs by.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the book teaches in sub lesson 1 — Tennis court and equipment.
2. Say what the book teaches in sub lesson 2 — Scoring, the serve, foot faults and lets.

Nothing is bought for this lesson — paper and a pencil for the hands-on tasks, and a safe space to move.
