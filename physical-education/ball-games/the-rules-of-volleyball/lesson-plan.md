# Lesson plan — The Rules of Volleyball

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Ball Games · **Lesson:** The Rules of Volleyball
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ball Games (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the rules that frame a volleyball match — the court and free zone, the net, the 12-player team with six on court, the rotational order fixed by the starting line-up, how a rally runs and when the ball is out.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the volleyball skills lessons (Volleyball Passing and Setting); it teaches the Grade 9 rules pass as its own depth and cross-references the Grade 10 pass taught there, without duplicating it (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The court, the free zone and the net** — the rectangular, symmetrical court, the free zone around it, and the net (1 m wide, 9.50–10 m long, 10 cm black mesh) on its posts.
2. **Twelve players, six on court, and the rotational order** — up to 12 players a team, always six on court; the starting line-up fixes the rotational order for the set.
3. **How a rally is played, and when the ball is out** — playing format and playing actions, and the calls that end a rally.
4. **Facilities, equipment and the referees (teacher guide — evidence)** — the difference between facilities and equipment, how referees lead matches, the five major skills as the frame, three hits in addition to the block contact.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Volleyball Passing and Setting. Recommended, never forced — a learner may jump in here directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name and draw the court, the free zone, the net (with its length and width) and the posts.
2. Say how many players a team may have, how many are on court, and what the starting line-up fixes.
3. Rotate correctly one position when the team wins the serve back.
4. Say when a ball is out and what ends a rally.
5. Tell facilities from equipment, name the referees' job, and state the three-hits rule with the block contact.

## What you need
A court or a marked-out space, a net or a rope on posts, and a volleyball. Modest sports clothes. A partner or a small group for the rotation and rally parts.

## If the learner is stuck
- The rotation tangles → stop the game, stand on the drawn court, and walk the one-position move slowly, naming each new position.
- In/out calls are argued → replay the rally slowly and let the player nearest the landing call it; honest calling is part of the lesson.
- Facilities and equipment blur → ask "is it built and lasting, or brought and taken away?" for each item.
