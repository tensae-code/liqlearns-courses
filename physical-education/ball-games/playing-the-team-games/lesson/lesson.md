# Playing the Team Games

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Small games (ትንንሽ ጨዋታዎች) are the proving ground: small-sided games that call for all the skills together — the forearm pass and the finger pass, setting, passing with the inside and outside of the foot, controlling with the chest, and dribbling. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Small ball games — Grade 5: putting the skills together

Small games (ትንንሽ ጨዋታዎች) are the proving ground: small-sided games that call for all the skills together — the forearm pass and the finger pass, setting, passing with the inside and outside of the foot, controlling with the chest, and dribbling. Set the game up, agree the rules, and rotate so every player uses every skill.

## Sub lesson 2 — Team games — Grade 6: volleyball, football, basketball, handball

Now play the team games themselves: a volleyball game, a football game, a basketball game and a handball game. The book's instruction is one line long and it is the whole point: apply the skills you have learned in a real game. Positions, rules and turns are shared so everyone plays.

## Sub lesson 3 — Football formations and team strategy — Grade 10

Strategy is a team's method for each play, every player accomplishing a role within the team and its zones. Formations group the outfield players — the goalkeeper is not counted in the numbers: 4-4-2, 4-5-1, 3-5-2 and 4-3-3 are the shapes pictured. Defending: take position between the opponent and your goal — a team without the ball is defending, every player protecting the goal, communicating, defending as a unit, holding the line and marking, with spare players covering attackers, until the ball is won back; and remember, offensive play is also another way of defence. Attacking: keep possession, spread out on the opponent's field, pass and move quickly to create space — never stand still — moving into open space closer to goal. The rules that govern the game ride with this part (Grade 9): the referee has full authority, a goal counts when the whole ball crosses the goal line, and a throw-in is awarded against the player who last touched the ball.

## Sub lesson 4 — Small-sided games — Grade 9: 2 against 1, 4 against 4, 5 against 5

Small games teach the big game. In handball: passing-and-catching positions 2 against 1; then 4 against 4; then 5 against 5 — and a player without the ball runs a longer run into open space to be ready for the next position. Basketball and volleyball ride the same way: modified games with lower rings, half courts and smaller balls, and a modified volleyball game where you use critical thinking to make the game competitive and enjoyable for everyone.

## Sub lesson 5 — Handball team defence and attack — Grade 10

On the full court — three times bigger than the small court — the game grows to 6 against 6, and individual advanced skills must join the team's whole tactic. Defence is a team concept: defenders shift as a unit, control and protect space, and step out together to meet the offence. Attack grows from two-line passing into passing-and-attacking drills. The rules that govern it ride with this part: goalkeepers may leave the goal area without the ball but then count as court players, substitutes may enter at any time once the replaced player has left, and a goal counts as soon as the referees award it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Football rules — the guide's own pass (teacher guide — evidence)

The rules of football are the same game everywhere: FIFA's rules govern the game in every country. For international competitions the playing area measures 110 m × 75 m, a team plays with eleven players, and a match is two equal halves of 45 minutes. The rules this pass teaches, one by one: the ball in and out of play — when the ball has wholly crossed a line it is out, and play restarts by the rule for that line; scoring — a goal when the whole ball crosses the goal line between the posts; offside — a player may not gain an advantage by waiting beyond the defence for the ball; the throw-in — the restart when the ball leaves over the side line, awarded against the player who touched it last; and the corner kick — the restart when a defender puts the ball out over their own goal line. The model official named for how all of this is run is the famous Italian referee Pierluigi Collina: watch how a referee positions, signals and decides, and you see the rules carried by a person, not only by a book.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
