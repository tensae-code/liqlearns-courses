# Playing the Team Games

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Small games (ትንንሽ ጨዋታዎች) are the proving ground: small-sided games that call for all the skills together — the forearm pass and the finger pass, setting, passing with the inside and outside of the foot, controlling with the chest, and dribbling. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Small ball games — Grade 5: putting the skills together

Small games (ትንንሽ ጨዋታዎች) are the proving ground: small-sided games that call for all the skills together — the forearm pass and the finger pass, setting, passing with the inside and outside of the foot, controlling with the chest, and dribbling. Set the game up, agree the rules, and rotate so every player uses every skill.

## Sub lesson 2 — Team games — Grade 6: volleyball, football, basketball, handball

Now play the team games themselves: a volleyball game, a football game, a basketball game and a handball game. The book's instruction is one line long and it is the whole point: apply the skills you have learned in a real game. Positions, rules and turns are shared so everyone plays.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
