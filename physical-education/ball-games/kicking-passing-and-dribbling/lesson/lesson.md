# Kicking, Passing and Dribbling

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Kick a ball standing still. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kicking and passing a ball

Kick a ball standing still. Kick forward after one or two steps. Pass a ball to a partner. Receive a pass and control it — trap it still.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ball control with the feet

Dribble a ball tied on a string, tapping it with the front of your foot. In pairs, kick a thrown ball in the air at close range. Kick a hanging ball standing still, and lie on your back and push a hanging ball upward with your feet.

## Sub lesson 3 — Equipment skills — balls, rings and targets

Combine equipment skills with travelling and stationary moves. Dribble a ball while moving in different directions and in a zigzag between cones, keeping it close. Throw a ball at a target. Turn step-rings by hand, go in and out of a ring, turn a ring at your waist, pass a ball round your waist and between your legs, and kick a ball between cones.

## Sub lesson 4 — Football dribbling and net ball (teacher guide — evidence)

Dribble a ball with your feet along the ground — control it with the inside of your foot, keep it close, then strike it toward the goal or target. In net ball, move and run with the ball and set and dig it with your hands over the net.

## Sub lesson 5 — Passing with the inside and outside of the foot — Grade 5

Pass and receive with the inside of the foot at graded distances — 4 metres, 6 metres, 8 metres, up to 10 metres: ankle locked, ball along the ground to your partner. Then the outside-of-foot pass, the counterpart skill, most useful once your ball skill has grown. Put both together in one-touch circle play: stand in a circle and play the ball with the inside and the outside of the foot in one touch, without stopping it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Football dribbling — Grade 6

Dribbling (ኳስ መንዳት) moves the ball with you, close to the feet. Dribble with the inside of the foot and with the outside of the foot; dribble 20 metres to a partner; dribble between cones, keeping the ball close at every turn; then play small team football games that use the dribble to beat space, not people.

## Sub lesson 7 — Kicking and controlling — Grade 7

Kick with the inside of the foot, and with the front of the foot, from a correct stance. Control the ball with the sole (በእግር መዳፍ): eye on the ball, trap it still under the sole — practise against a wall at 7 metres, then 5 metres, then 3 metres. Control with the front of the foot from 10 metres: meet the ball gently and take its speed away with the first touch.

## Sub lesson 8 — Shooting at goal — Grade 8

Shoot at goal in stages, exactly as the book builds it. First, run up without touching the ball. Next, run up and only brush it — no roll, no shot. Then shoot from 15 metres with no goalkeeper. Then shoot with a goalkeeper. Only then, shoot in the game. Each stage earns the next one.

## Sub lesson 9 — Chest control, first touch and heading — Grade 9

Control brings the ball under your command. Chest control: get your body behind the ball's flight, meet it on the chest and pull the surface back just as it touches, so the ball loses its sting and drops at your feet — the forward's skill, even with a marker behind you. Practise in a circle with a centre tosser, then in facing rows. First touch and scoring: lean forward over the ball, move your first touch into space, and accelerate away. Heading: meet the ball on the forehead, eyes on the ball the whole way — never close them — driving through it to pass, shoot or clear, standing, jumping or diving. Play head tennis over a net or rope to practise: head it back over, and never let it touch the ground. For chest control and heading practice, the guide's safety note stands: use soft or appropriate balls, supervise, and practise responsibly.

## Sub lesson 10 — Tackling, thigh control and goalkeeping — Grade 10

Three advanced football skills. Tackling: the block (front block) tackle — meet the attacker head-on and make contact forcibly with the inside of the tackling foot, putting your weight into it, practised first in pairs without a ball, then to a stationary ball, then against a slow dribble. The slide tackle is riskier — sliding can miss the ball and put you out of the game — and the guide's safety rule is absolute: practise it only on a soft or grass field; if not, do not practise it at all. Thigh control: for a ball arriving above the knee, place the thigh behind its flight, across its line — and drop the thigh the moment the ball touches, cushioning it dead; the mistakes are poor body position, forcing into the ball, and not dropping the thigh. Goalkeeping: stay around the goal area; for a ground dive, hold the basic position, eyes on the ball's direction, and push off to transfer your weight toward the ball; a punch clears with width (toward the sidelines, out of the centre) and distance (as far from goal as possible); and every goalkeeper must kick well — the goal kick is part of the job.

## Sub lesson 11 — Kicking a ball, and up-and-down steps (teacher guide — evidence)

Object-control basics — በብቃት የመሰረት ክህሎት — done accurately, with partners. Kicking a ball — ኳስ በእግር መምታት: stand beside the ball, swing your leg, and kick toward a partner or a target; feet planted in turn, left and right. Up-and-down movement — የመውጣት መውረድ እንቅስቃሴ: step up onto a low place and down again, in turn, one foot after the other, steady and controlled.

## Sub lesson 12 — Passing the football; hit the target (teacher guide — evidence)

The football passing game — የእግር ኳስ ቅብብሎሽ ጨዋታ: in pairs or groups, pass the ball with your feet along the ground across a five-metre space marked by two scarves; receive with one foot and pass it back. Hit the target — ዒላማ መምታት: throw or kick at a set target and score when you hit it. Accuracy is the game — a soft, true pass and a aimed shot beat a wild one every time.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
