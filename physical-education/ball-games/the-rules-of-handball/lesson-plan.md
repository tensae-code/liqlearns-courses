# Lesson plan — The Rules of Handball

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Ball Games · **Lesson:** The Rules of Handball
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ball Games (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the rules that build a handball game — the colour-banded court and goals, the ball, the team and its substitutes, playing time with the obligatory time-out, fouls and personal punishments, and the guide-depth rules for the goalkeeper, scoring and the throw-in.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the handball skills lessons (Dribbling, Shooting and the Jump Throw; Playing the Team Games). It grows the rules teaching from the Grade 9 student pass as the opening depth and cross-cites the Grade 10 student pass where it already lives, rather than duplicating it (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The court, the goals and the ball** — the marked court, goals and markings in two contrasting colour bands, the net that keeps a scored ball in the goal, the goal area and the ball.
2. **The team, playing time, the obligatory time-out and punishments** — substitutes entering at any time once the replaced player has left; the time-out that must be called at a 2-minute suspension or a disqualification; fouls and personal punishments, hardest for fouls aimed at the body.
3. **The rules in the guide's own pass: court, time, ball, team and fouls (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide's order under the international rules, with the court drawn in pairs, and the four basic skills named.
4. **The goalkeeper, scoring and the throw-in (teacher guide — evidence)** — why rules exist (safety, integrity, fairest competition); the keeper saving with any part of the body; scoring confirmed by the referee; the throw-in awarded when a rule is broken or the ball leaves play.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Dribbling, Shooting and the Jump Throw, and Playing the Team Games. Recommended, never forced — a learner may jump in here directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw the court and goals and say why the markings are colour-banded and the goal netted.
2. Bring a substitute on legally — only after the replaced player has left — at any time, repeatedly.
3. Say when a time-out is obligatory and what personal punishments punish hardest.
4. Draw the court from the guide's pass and name the four basic skills the rules protect.
5. Keep goal legally with any part of the body, state how a goal stands, and award a throw-in with its reason.

## What you need
A court or marked space with goals (posts, bags or a marked wall will do), a handball or a soft ball of similar size. Modest sports clothes. A partner or a small group for the small-sided game parts.

## If the learner is stuck
- The substitute rule tangles → freeze the game at the bench line and walk it: out fully, then in — never both on court together for the same place.
- Punishments feel abstract → act three fouls of rising danger and sort them together: which were aimed at the body, and what should each cost?
- The throw-in's reasons blur → every award in practice games is spoken aloud with its reason, as in the Do It Yourself block.
