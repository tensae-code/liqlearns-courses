# The Rules of Basketball

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: stand on a court — or in a space with a hoop, a target or a marked wall. Shoot once from close to the basket and once from far away. Which shot should be worth more, and why? Hold your answer. Then read on and learn the rules that run the game, from the first pass at Grade 9 to the deeper pass at Grade 10.

## Sub lesson 1 — The playing area, the equipment and the quarters

Basketball is played on a marked court with a basket at each end, and the equipment at its heart is the backboard and the basket fixed above it. A game is played in 4 quarters of 10 minutes each, with intervals between them. If the score is tied when the fourth quarter ends, the game does not stop at a draw: overtime is played until one team is ahead when the time runs out. Learn the frame first — court, backboard, quarters — because every other rule is timed or placed inside it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Timed violations, fouls and free throws

Some rules are counted in seconds. A player may not stay 3 seconds in the restricted area while their team has the ball; a closely guarded player holding the ball must play it within 5 seconds; and a free-throw shooter must not cross the free-throw line while the shot is in the air. Fouls are the game's illegal contacts and conduct. When a shooter is fouled, the shooter is awarded free throws — unopposed shots from the free-throw line. A technical foul is called for conduct outside normal play. The referees run all of this — and one habit is part of the rules themselves: players, coaches and referees must re-read the rules every year, because the rules change. Finish this part the way the game itself teaches it: watch a mini game and spot the violations as they happen.

## Sub lesson 3 — Scoring and the international court (teacher guide — evidence)

The team with the most points when the game ends wins. The guide fixes the values every player must know cold: a basket scored from inside the three-point line is worth 2 points, a basket from outside it is worth 3 points, and each successful free throw is worth 1 point. The court for international competitions measures 28 m by 15 m. Rules vary slightly by level and country — professional and college, international and the professional game in the USA — but most of them apply everywhere: the court, the equipment, the playing regulations, violations and fouls, and the referee are taught as one whole.

## Sub lesson 4 — Who may play, the players' roles and the coach's team — Grade 10

The second pass deepens the people side of the rule book. First, who is eligible to play: a player must be authorised for their team under the regulations before they can take part. Players carry individual roles and responsibilities inside the team, and because basketball is a contact game, fouls grow out of that contact — knowing your role is part of staying legal. The head coach does not work alone: the coaching staff is a team of professionals — a nutritionist, a physician, an assistant coach and others — sharing the duties of developing the players. And the clock has the last word: a quarter, an overtime or the game ends on the game-clock signal — and where the backboard carries red perimeter lighting, the lit light governs the end, not the sound alone.

## Sub lesson 5 — Teams of twelve, uniforms, and how the game is run (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's detailed pass names the team exactly: a maximum of twelve players, including the team captain, with the head coach and the assistant coach counted as the team's delegates. The uniform is the team's identity on the court: teams choose their own colours, the material is soft and non-abrasive, and shirt numbers run from 0 to 99. The captain is the player given the title for good leadership and communication skills. The playing regulations then run the match moment by moment: how a quarter begins and ends and how the ball is played; what a goal is; the throw-in, which puts the ball back into play; the time-out, a break a team may call; and substitution, changing a player for a team-mate. Learn these as one chain — team, uniform, captain, coach, quarter, throw-in, time-out, substitution — and you can follow any game from its first minute to its last.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: how long a quarter is, what a basket is worth from inside and outside the three-point line, and what a free throw is worth. Then tell them the three timed violations — 3 seconds, 5 seconds, and the free-throw line. Your companion says them back. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the court from memory and label it: the two baskets and backboards, the centre line and circle, the key, the free-throw line and the three-point arc. Write its international size beside it: 28 m by 15 m.
2. Score a make-believe game: your team scores four baskets from inside the arc, two from outside it, and three free throws. Work out the total, showing each value.
3. Play or watch a mini game. Keep a violation tally: every 3-second, 5-second and free-throw-line crossing you spot, mark it down and say the rule aloud.
4. Number six shirts from the range the rules allow — any numbers from 0 to 99 — and name your captain, saying why that player fits the title.
5. Say, in one sentence, what happens when the score is tied at the end of the fourth quarter.
