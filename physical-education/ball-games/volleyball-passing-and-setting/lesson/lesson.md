# Volleyball Passing and Setting

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: The forearm pass is volleyball's receiving skill: join the forearms into one flat platform, meet the ball on the platform, and rebound it up to a partner. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forearm pass — Grade 5

The forearm pass is volleyball's receiving skill: join the forearms into one flat platform, meet the ball on the platform, and rebound it up to a partner. Practise the pass first, then use it in games — for example, playing the ball with the forearm in a circle.

## Sub lesson 2 — The finger pass — Grade 5

The finger pass plays the ball overhead: hold the ball in two hands, throw it above your head, and tap it up again and again with the fingers — the fingertips touch the ball when you push it up above the head. Keep the ball above your head for as many clean taps as you can, then use the finger pass in its games.

## Sub lesson 3 — Setting the ball for a team-mate — Grade 5

Setting places the ball where a team-mate can play it. Stand in the middle of the circle and set the ball to each pupil in turn. Learn the backwards set too (ወደኋላ ኳስን በጣት ማመቻቸት): set the ball with the fingers backwards, over your own head, to the player behind you — without looking first.

## Sub lesson 4 — Underarm serve and forearm receive — Grade 6

The underarm serve (ልግ) is the easiest serve, the one beginners use: stand behind the line, swing the arm underarm, and serve over the net to a partner standing 3 to 6 metres away. Facing it, receive the serve on the forearms with the flat platform from the first part of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 5 — Side underarm serve and receiving stance — Grade 7

The side underarm serve (ኳስን በጎን ከታች ወደ ላይ መለጋት) turns the body side-on: take the grip and stance, serve to a partner without holding the ball in the air first, then serve over the net. Receiving on the forearms (በክንድ ልግን መቀበል) begins from the ready stance: feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, platform ready.

## Sub lesson 6 — The overhead serve — Grade 8

The overhead serve (ከላይ ወደታች መለጋት) is the serve that marks a complete volleyball player. Stand with the ball on your free palm. Toss it up, and as you strike, shift your weight from the back foot to the front, hitting the ball hard from above. Practise in stages: mime the serve with no ball; serve at a wall from 4 metres; then serve and receive in the game — the forearm reception you already know is revisited here, because every serve must be received by someone.

## Sub lesson 7 — The spike — Grade 9

Spiking is volleyball's main way to score, and it is built in four stages: the approach to the net, the take-off, the body movement in the air, and the hitting action. Stand relaxed 2–4 m off the net. The approach is usually four steps — right-left-right-left for right-handers, mirrored for left-handers — growing slow, faster, fastest and small, bigger, biggest, the last two steps the fastest, ending in a two-footed jump, arms swinging back then forward with the jump. At contact the hand is firm and hard, on top of the ball, striking its centre so it drops with topspin — and the ball stays in front of your hitting shoulder, watched from the setter's hands. Three kinds: the standing spike (one foot still on the floor — easiest to receive), the off-speed spike (less force, more control — the safe hit into open space), and the hard-driven spike (full force, through the block or to end the rally). Warm up, stretch and strengthen — continuous jumping injures players who do not.

## Sub lesson 8 — Blocking — Grade 10

Blocking is the defence at the net. Stay close to the net, hands at shoulder level with palms facing the net, follow the hitter and keep your eyes on them. Jump and reach over the net, then land ready in a defensive position. The blocking line is the court area your block covers — the outside blocker owns two primary areas. Work as one, two or three blockers: a single block covers least; a double block covers more; a triple block leaves the hitter little room; and the swing block swings the arms and steps into the jump for a little extra height. The game's interruptions ride with this part: the only regular interruptions are time-outs and substitutions — at most six substitutions a set — and the libero, in a different-coloured uniform, defends at the back and may not block or serve.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
