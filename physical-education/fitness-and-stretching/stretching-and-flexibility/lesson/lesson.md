# Stretching and Flexibility

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Bend forward to touch your toes and return. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Stretching and bending on the spot

Bend forward to touch your toes and return. Lean forward, back, left and right. Bend forward and touch the ground with your palms. Lie down and raise alternate arms. Raise both arms joined overhead and stretch. Sit with legs stretched and pull forward. These movements above the waist develop arm, shoulder, waist and chest muscles.

## Sub lesson 2 — What stretching and flexibility are

Stretching is increasing the muscles' elasticity. Flexibility is the joints' range of movement. Their benefit is preventing injury on the field. Stretch with neck tilts right and left, standing side-stretches, both arms stretched overhead, the sheberek forward pull, leaning forward and back, waist twists, and a seated forward pull to the toes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Stretching together and with a ball

Sit and raise your hands and return. Stand in a circle facing outward, hold hands, bend and return together. Sit supported on your back and lift your legs. Pass a ball backwards overhead with both hands to a partner.

## Sub lesson 4 — Side leans and waist stretches

Raise both arms up and lean right and left. Stretch the waist forward and back, with alternate hands supporting from behind. Stretch the legs — this reduces injury and load when the legs work.

## Sub lesson 5 — Stretching and flexibility — Grade 5

Flexibility work is done with the joints warmed. Lean the body left and right. Bring the knee to the chest and hold it. Then the bridge (ድልድይ መስራት): bend back from the waist into the bridge shape, hold it only as far as is comfortable, and come back down slowly and safely. The book notes that carrying much body fat often makes flexibility smaller — gentle, regular stretching is the answer, never bouncing.

## Sub lesson 6 — Flexibility — Grade 6 stretches

Bend and reach with arm raises. Sit and reach to touch the toes — this improves the hip and back joints. Bend to the side, left and right. Stand wide, lunge to the side and hold for 10 seconds. Two more stretches come with a helper's care (teacher guide — evidence): the seated inner-thigh stretch — sit with the soles together or the legs wide, lean forward gently, press the knees slowly toward the ground and hold, three or more times; and the hip-and-thigh stretch — one leg forward bent at 90 degrees, the other back straight with the knee toward the ground, hands on the front knee, press the hips down gently, hold, and change sides; this stretch also protects against back pain. Flexibility means moving the joints and muscles as far as they can go without pain — warm up first, always.

## Sub lesson 7 — Flexibility — Grade 7: sit-and-reach and the six-stretch circuit

Flexibility is the ability to move the joints and use the muscles through their full range of motion. The sit-and-reach (ቁጭ ብሎ ወደፊት መሣብ) stretches the lower back and the back of the thigh. Then work the six-stretch circuit, each stretch on slow breaths (teacher guide — evidence): 1. The runners stretch — step into a lunge, fingertips down; breathe in, and as you breathe out straighten the front leg; return; repeat 4 times; switch sides. 2. The standing side stretch — feet together, arms overhead, hands clasped with the pointer fingers extended; reach up on the in-breath, bend the upper body right on the out-breath; five slow breaths; return and repeat left. 3. The forward hang — feet hip-distance apart, knees a little bent, fingers interlaced behind the back; straighten the arms to open the chest, then fold at the waist with the hands stretching toward the head; hold five deep breaths. 4. The low lunge arch — the same lunge steps as the runners stretch. 5. The seated back twist — sit with the legs straight; bend the right knee and step the foot over the left leg; right hand behind you, left elbow against the right knee; sit tall on the in-breath and twist on the out-breath, looking over the right shoulder; hold five breaths; switch sides. 6. The bound angle — soles of the feet together, knees dropping toward the ground; hold the shins, lift the chest, then hinge forward from the hips without rounding the back; hold five slow breaths.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
