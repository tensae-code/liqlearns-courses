# Lesson plan — Biomechanics in Sport

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Fitness and Stretching · **Lesson:** Biomechanics in Sport
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Fitness and Stretching (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Biomechanics in sport: forces, levers, planes of movement, projectile flight, and analysing and correcting technique — taught here through the United Kingdom's GCSE way: how movement works mechanically — the lever systems in the body, and the planes and axes along which sporting movements happen.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Lever systems** — the United Kingdom book: the three lever classes in the body and mechanical advantage in sport.
2. **Planes and axes of movement** — the United Kingdom book: the planes and axes along which sporting movements happen.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Heart and Breathing Fitness; How Training Works. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say that the body moves through lever systems, and that there are three lever classes.
2. Say what mechanical advantage means in sport, as the book teaches it.
3. Say that sporting movements happen along planes and around axes, and name the book's topic for each.

## What you need
Nothing is bought for this lesson — paper and a pencil for the hands-on tasks, and a safe space to move.
