# Lesson plan — How Training Works

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Fitness and Stretching · **Lesson:** How Training Works
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Fitness and Stretching (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how training works — the four rules of training, and the methods you choose by goal.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the fitness lessons before it (heart and breathing fitness, muscle strength and endurance, stretching and flexibility); the parts climb from Grade 8 to Grade 10 (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Two kinds of fitness and the four rules of training** — specificity, progressive overload, reversibility, individual differences; acute responses vs chronic adaptation.
2. **Ways to train — aerobic, anaerobic and circuits — Grade 10** — aerobic vs anaerobic work, and circuit training station by station.
3. **The seven methods — choosing by goal (teacher guide — evidence)** — continuous, interval, fartlek, circuit, resistance, flexibility, plyometric — chosen by goal.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Heart and Breathing Fitness and Muscle Strength and Endurance. Recommended, never forced — a learner may jump in here directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name and explain the four rules of training, with an example of each.
2. Tell aerobic work from anaerobic work, and run a four-station circuit in order.
3. Name the seven training methods and choose the right family for strength and for heart-and-breathing fitness.

## What you need
Open flat ground, cones or markers for circuit stations, a stopwatch, and modest sports clothes. A partner for passive stretching and for checking each other's plans.

## If the learner is stuck
- The rules blur together → take one rule and one real example (a stamina run for specificity) and rebuild from there.
- A circuit station is too hard → shorten the station to 15 seconds and keep the order; order matters more than load.
- The learner cannot choose a method → state the goal first in one word — strength, or heart — then pick from that family.
