# Agility — Rising, Running and Quick Changes

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Physical fitness has parts: heart and blood-vessel working, muscle strength, stretching and flexibility, and agility. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — What physical fitness is (teacher guide — evidence)

Physical fitness has parts: heart and blood-vessel working, muscle strength, stretching and flexibility, and agility. The Grade 3 teacher guide adds that fitness protects health — a protected heart, healthy lungs and breathing organs, healthy blood vessels, a well-proportioned body and a healthy body weight.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rising and running

Sit with legs stretched — rise and run. Lie on your chest — rise and run. Lie on your back — rise and run. In pairs, touch foot to foot in turn, coordinating together. This quick rising and running is agility, a new fitness part in Grade 3.

## Sub lesson 3 — Line drills

Run, touch a line, and return. Run while jumping a line forward and back. Jump a line left and right on two feet. Sprint to touch a line and turn back — the shuttle turn.

## Sub lesson 4 — Agility — Grade 5: obstacles and group runs

Agility at this grade means changing direction fast and accurately while keeping your balance. Run between obstacles without losing your balance. In the group circle games, equal numbers from two groups are called and run when their number is called — listen first, then run at once, in control.

## Sub lesson 5 — Line-touch races and cone weaves (teacher guide — evidence)

The line-touch race: lines are set at 5 metres, then 4 metres, then 3 metres; sprint from the start, touch each line with your hand, and sprint back; in the second round, run around all the markers in one flow. Then the cone weave: sprint in and out of a line of cones at speed without touching them, keeping your balance through every turn.

## Sub lesson 6 — Agility — Grade 6: starting positions and direction changes

Agility is changing the body's direction quickly and accurately, and it needs the body and the mind working together. Practise sprint starts from varied positions: lying on your chest, and kneeling on one knee — on the command, rise and sprint 30 metres. Jump fast forward over 10 metres on two legs, then on one leg. Then run the marker course in four directions in one flow: from the first marker to the second fast forward, from the second to the third sideways, from the third to the fourth backward, and from the fourth back round to the first at speed; and sprint between markers, turning at each one and touching it with your hand before the next.

## Sub lesson 7 — Speed work — Grade 7: high knees, pursuit and the three sprint phases

High-knee running (ጉልበትን ከፍ በማድረግ መሮጥ) lifts the knees high as you run. Pursuit runs (ተከታትሎ መሮጥ) chase a partner ahead of you. The sprint itself has three phases: drive, transition, and top speed — if your step length and your step frequency grow, your speed grows too. Run 20 metres at average and high speed twice, resting 10 to 30 seconds; then the 30-metre and 40-metre runs the same way, and finish with a 40-metre run against a friend in competition form, keeping the basic body movement of the sprint.

## Sub lesson 8 — Speed and the rules of sprinting technique — Grade 8

Speed is a person's ability to move fast; speed joined with strength gives power. Practise the 30-metre sprint run: warm up in small groups, practise reaction drills — reaction time is how fast you answer a signal — and sprint from standing starts and crouch starts, alone and chasing a partner. The sprinting rules the guide prints: hands move from the hips to the chin, never side to side; elbows stay bent at 90 degrees and drive back; shoulders square, knees pulled straight up, never angling in; strike the ground directly below the hips; and run on the balls of your feet — quietly, never flat-footed. The 40-metre run follows the same procedure as the 30-metre run.

The student book's own speed work — 20–30 metre runs past a hoop and fast feet through ground ladder lines — is practised in Running Basics.

## Sub lesson 9 — Fartlek — speeding up and easing off — Grade 10 (teacher guide — evidence)

Fartlek is running that changes its own pace: jog, burst fast across open ground, ease off again, and repeat — over grass, paths and gentle hills. The Grade 10 guide names fartlek the aerobic exercise best for developing heart-and-breathing fitness. Change pace when you feel ready, not by the clock, and always finish by easing down to a walk.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
