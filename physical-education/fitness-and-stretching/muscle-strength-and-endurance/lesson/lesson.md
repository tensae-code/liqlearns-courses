# Muscle Strength and Endurance

## Do it first

Try it now, before you read: Muscle endurance is the leg, arm and stomach muscles working for a long time at a low load without tiring. Do it slowly once. Then read on and learn how the books teach it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — What muscle endurance is

Muscle endurance is the leg, arm and stomach muscles working for a long time at a low load without tiring. You use it for writing with pen and pencil, drawing and clapping. Train it with jumping up holding the waist, arm side-stretches, hand-in-hand two-foot jumping, and standing on one leg holding hands with the other leg stretched back.

## Sub lesson 2 — Muscle strength work

Build strength with alternating leg lifts touched by the hand, partner squat-and-rise holding a ball behind the back (sheberek), jumping up holding a ball and throwing it, back-to-back partner squats, and frog jumps — crouch, spring, land in a crouch, like a frog.

## Sub lesson 3 — Slow and continuous strength work

Stand and stretch both arms sideways, level with the shoulders, again and again. Do strength work slowly and continuously to get the change. Add seated leg stretch-and-fold in the air.

## Sub lesson 4 — Forward jumps and seated leg raises

Stand and move both arms in different directions, again and again. Jump forward and back on both feet from one spot, and jump forward progressively. Sit with legs stretched and raise them in turn.

## Sub lesson 5 — Muscle strength by body part — Grade 5

At this grade the strength work is organised by body part: the muscles above the waist, the stomach and back muscles, and the muscles below the waist. Above the waist: walk forward and back supporting yourself on your hands on the ground. Stomach and back: lie on your chest and raise the upper body from the waist, hold it, and lower it slowly. Work each part in its turn, and name the part before you start.

## Sub lesson 6 — Push-ups, side hops and curl-ups (teacher guide — evidence)

The knee push-up (knee push-up): kneel, hands under the shoulders, body straight from the knees; lower the chest toward the floor by bending the arms, then push back up. Practise in sets — ten at a time, three rounds, thirty in all — resting between sets, and grow toward the full push-up on hands and toes. The side hop (Side hop): stand with the feet open, hands on the hips; hop side to side over a line or a low marker, taking off and landing on both feet together with soft knees — five hops each way for two rounds, then eight each way over a raised marker. The curl-up (CURL-UP): lie on your back with the knees bent, curl up to build the stomach muscles — ten at a time, three rounds, thirty in all, with a minute's rest between rounds; a partner may hold your feet.

## Sub lesson 7 — Muscle strength — Grade 6 sets

The Grade 6 strength set: push-ups (with the knees-down variant), walking on the hands, pull-ups on a single bar with a shoulder-width grip, and sit-ups with the arms on the chest, at the ears, on the shoulders, or at the side. Then: lying trunk raises, held up to 30 seconds; the rabbit jump (የጥንቸል ዝላይ); the wall-sit (ግድግዳ ተደግፎ መቀመጥ) for the leg muscles; and stepping up and down on a box. Each movement names the muscles it builds before you do it.

## Sub lesson 8 — Muscular endurance — Grade 7

Muscular endurance is a muscle's ability to exert force consistently and repetitively over a period of time — think of it as stamina. Push-ups build the triceps, chest and shoulders: 4 sets of 10 repetitions, with 30 seconds' active rest between sets; if the full push-up is too hard, start on the knees. The squat jump improves the lower body and the core: stand with the feet shoulder-width, descend to a full squat, spring up off the floor with the legs fully extended, and land in control through the foot — toes, ball, arch, heel — straight into the next squat; 3 sets of 5, with 30 seconds' active rest. Pull-ups at this grade are modified: the bar is only a few feet off the ground and your feet still touch the ground, supporting part of your weight, so both boys and girls can train the back, biceps and forearms; use a wide grip, the full range of motion, and try to pull fully off the ground for 6 to 10 repetitions. Endurance work exposes the major muscles — follow the teacher's stop and continue instructions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
