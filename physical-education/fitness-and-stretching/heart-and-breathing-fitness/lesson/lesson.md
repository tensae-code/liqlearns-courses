# Heart and Breathing Fitness

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — What heart and breathing fitness is

Heart and breathing fitness is the heart and lungs' ability to work for a long time at low intensity. It is what lets you walk to school and back and run errands without tiring. Its movements include rope skipping, arm stretches and circling, running round small circles, and somsoma shuttle running.

## Sub lesson 2 — Heart and breathing fitness grows

Endurance means resisting a load and working for a long time. Train it with partner holds, alternating forward and back arm-and-leg swings, arms to the chest while the feet step forward and back, and throwing a ball one-handed into a basket.

## Sub lesson 3 — Fitness that protects the heart and breathing

This fitness protects against diseases that come with the heart and breathing system. Train it with group rope skipping — a long rope can be turned by others — jumping with arms and legs opening sideways and returning, and arm-and-leg stretch-and-return.

## Sub lesson 4 — The six-minute run and steady stepping

This work has great importance for preventing bodily pain and unnecessary body weight. Train it with group rope jumping, the six-minute run, on-the-spot somsoma heel-touching in turn, and stepping up and down with two feet at a steady pace.

## Sub lesson 5 — The step-up test and counting the heart (teacher guide — evidence)

Step up and down on a high step, 20 to 30 centimetres high, in rhythm — first for 40 seconds at one pace, then for one full minute, then for two minutes without stopping, resting between rounds (Step-up). Your heart is found with a stethoscope and its beats are counted before and after, and you compare the counts with a partner. The blood's main work is to carry food and oxygen to the body's parts and bring the used air back — that is why the heart beats faster when you work.

## Sub lesson 6 — Heart and breathing fitness — Grade 5 movements

Four movements build the heart and breathing. Walking (የእግር ጉዞ) is the first. Rope jumping (ገመድ ዝላይ) is the second. Jumping jacks — opening and joining the arms and legs together (እጅና እግርን መክፈትና መግጠም) — are the third. The fourth is the side-jump lunge from standing, which also strengthens the leg muscles to a high degree. Do each one in a steady rhythm, and say its purpose first: a stronger heart and stronger breathing.

## Sub lesson 7 — Heart and breathing fitness — Grade 6: pulse, skipping and stepping

First learn your pulse: feel it at the wrist with three fingers, or at the neck; count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4 — that is your heartbeats in one minute. The movements: marching and running drills, rope skipping on the spot (the rope is turned back-to-front at this grade), skipping while travelling forward, group skipping with a long rope turned by two, running on the spot lifting the knees, jumping jacks, and stepping up and down on a box 30 to 40 centimetres high for 30 to 40 seconds, then a minute of work and a minute of rest, then two minutes — checking the pulse before and after.

## Sub lesson 8 — Cardio fitness — Grade 7: jacks, hop squats and ropes

Cardiovascular endurance is the ability of the heart, blood vessels, blood and breathing system to supply fuel and oxygen to the muscles, and of the muscles to use that fuel to keep exercising. Aerobic exercise — running, cycling, football, rope jumping — makes the blood pump and the large muscles work; it strengthens circulation, grows heart and lung capacity, lowers health risk and helps with weight. The dose: do each exercise at least a minute, rest 30 seconds between exercises, and grow the time and the effort gradually. Jumping jacks: feet together, arms by your sides; jump the feet apart as the arms rise overhead, jump back, and continue. In-and-out hopping squats: feet together; jump the feet out to the side and squat there; jump back together and squat; continue. Rope jumping: rope behind the heels, turn it gently forward, and as it passes your shins spring from your toes with the knees a little bent, staying on the balls of your feet.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the name of each movement in this lesson, and what it trains. Your companion does each movement slowly while you name it. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do each part of the lesson once, slowly and safely, in order.
2. Do the first part again — this time at your full, careful speed.
3. Teach one part to a friend or a family member: name it, show it, and watch them do it.
4. Say, in one sentence, what this lesson trained in your body.
