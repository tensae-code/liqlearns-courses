# Lesson plan — The Body in Exercise

**Course:** Physical Education · **Title:** Fitness and Stretching · **Lesson:** The Body in Exercise
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Fitness and Stretching (CURRICULUM.md path: Moving Basics → Fitness and Stretching → Gymnastics → Athletics → Ball Games → Games to Play → Traditional Games and Dances → Sport and Its Story).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The body in exercise: bones, joints, muscles and the body systems that produce movement — taught here through the United Kingdom's GCSE way: bones, joints, muscles and how they produce movement — the musculo-skeletal system the United Kingdom GCSE book opens with.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Bones and the skeleton** — the United Kingdom book: the bones of the musculo-skeletal system and what the skeleton does.
2. **Joints** — the United Kingdom book: the joints — where bones meet and movement becomes possible.
3. **Muscles and how they produce movement** — the United Kingdom book: the muscles and how bones, joints and muscles together produce movement.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Heart and Breathing Fitness; How Training Works. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three parts of the musculo-skeletal system as the book teaches them: bones, joints and muscles.
2. Say what each part does in the system.
3. Say how the three parts work together to produce movement.

## What you need
Nothing is bought for this lesson — paper and a pencil for the hands-on tasks, and a safe space to move.
