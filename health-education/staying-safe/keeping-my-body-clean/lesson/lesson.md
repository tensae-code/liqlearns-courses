# Keeping My Body Clean

## Do it first

How many times do you wash in one day? Say what you think first — then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Morning washing routine

Kiribati's Year 3 big book, *Stay Clean and Healthy*, teaches early health as a first-person picture story — one child's whole day told as a chain of washing moments. The day begins with the **morning washing routine**: washing the face on waking, brushing teeth and bathing, then putting on a clean dress and combing the hair.

## Sub lesson 2 — Washing hands again and again through the day

The story does not stop at the morning. Through the day, hands are **washed again and again** — before breakfast, after playing at school and before lunch. The clean day ends with eating healthy food and sleeping on a clean bed and pillow. In Kiribati's own way, hygiene is learned as a daily routine rather than as rules.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hygiene in puberty

Kiribati's Years 5–6 WASH guide teaches **hygiene in puberty**: in puberty the glands are more active, so there is more oil and stronger sweat. That is why the guide teaches bathing and changing clothes more often, and washing face, hands, feet, underarms, groin and under the fingernails with soap. (This part comes from a teacher guide — it is the teachers' book for the school programme.)
