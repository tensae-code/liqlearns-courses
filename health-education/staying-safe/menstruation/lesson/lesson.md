## Sub lesson 1 — What menstruation is

Kiribati's Years 5–6 WASH guide teaches **what menstruation is**: the **monthly cycle**, how long periods last, and the facts of menstruation. It also teaches what menstruation is **NOT** — not an illness, not shameful, and no reason to miss school.

## Sub lesson 2 — Menstruation and culture: Te Katekateka

In Kiribati's own way, menstrual health is taught through **Te Katekateka** — Kiribati's celebration of a girl's first menstruation. For three days a girl stays with a female elder, learning hygiene practices: warm-water bathing for stomach pain, making a sanitary pad from white soft cloth, washing cloths with soap and sun-drying them, and Taberanikai herbs. It closes with a family celebration. The school programme is deliberately aligned with that cultural teaching.

## Sub lesson 3 — Menstrual Hygiene Management in school

The guide teaches **Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) in school**: why MHM matters — without it girls face social exclusion, missed schooling and health risks — and what schools need: girl-friendly facilities with water and soap, safe disposal, and washing of cloth pads. Most girls in Kiribati use washable cloth pads, called **pasador**, rather than commercial pads, and the class activity is making sanitary pads. (This lesson comes from a teacher guide — it is the teachers' book for the school programme.)
