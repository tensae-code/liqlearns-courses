# Lesson plan — Healthy Habits Every Day

**Course:** Health Education · **Title:** Staying Safe · **Lesson:** Healthy Habits Every Day
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Staying Safe (Health Education course).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** healthy habits every day — the daily lifestyle — food, activity, hygiene, rest and check-ups — that keeps you healthy for life; taught here through Jordan's Grade 1 pass: following daily healthy habits, and telling healthy habits from unhealthy ones.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands in the Staying Safe title beside Hygiene for Sport.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **Healthy habits** — Jordan's Grade 1 teaching: following daily healthy habits, telling healthy from unhealthy pictures, and aiming a ball at the picture of a daily healthy habit.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Health Is. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say that the book teaches following daily healthy habits.
2. Tell a healthy habit from an unhealthy one, the way the book's pictures teach it.

## What you need
Nothing is bought for this lesson — paper and a pencil for the hands-on tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Healthy and unhealthy feel like words only → anchor them in the book's own way: the Grade 1 book teaches them with pictures — look at the picture, say whether the habit is healthy or unhealthy, then aim the ball at the healthy one.
