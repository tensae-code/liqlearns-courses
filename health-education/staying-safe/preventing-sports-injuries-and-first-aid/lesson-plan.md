# Lesson plan — Preventing Sports Injuries and First Aid

**Course:** Health Education · **Title:** Staying Safe · **Lesson:** Preventing Sports Injuries and First Aid
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Staying Safe (Health Education course).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how sports injuries happen and how to prevent them — no warm-up, tiredness, wrong kit and disobeyed instructions are the books' named causes — and what to do when one happens anyway: first aid, and RICE — Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation — with the guide's RICER adding Referral.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Staying Safe While Exercising in this title.

## Sub lessons
1. **Preventing sports injuries — the causes and the list** — the Grade 6 book's injury section (tagged [Both] in the placements; its content is purely health knowledge, so it is built here in Health Education): a sports injury defined, its causes diagrammed, and the prevention list.
2. **Sports injuries — the kinds** — the Grade 7 book's pictured injury kinds (fig. 1.1) and the guide's named list: sprains, dislocations, bone fractures and bruising.
3. **First aid and RICE** — the Grade 7 book's first aid: quick, careful help where the accident happened, until health-facility care arrives — and RICE pictured (fig. 1.3): Rest, Ice, Compression (ማሰር), Elevation (ከፍ ማድረግ).
4. **RICER and the first-aid kit (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 7 teacher guide's first aid in full: its purposes — prevent further injury, preserve life, promote recovery — the kit items, RICER for the first 72 hours, and the blood-safety note.

5. **First aid and injury management — the Grade 8 definitions (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 8 teacher guide's §1.5: first aid defined (emergency care before regular medical aid) and injury management defined (timely, safe, durable return).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Staying Safe While Exercising — prevention there, prevention and first aid here. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the books' injury causes and the prevention list: build fitness, increase speed gradually, easy-to-hard, match exercise to ability, sex and age, hard moves only with teacher support, equipment used properly.
2. Name the injury kinds the books list: sprains, dislocations, bone fractures and bruising.
3. Define first aid: the quick, careful help given where the accident happened, to save life until health-facility care arrives.
4. Apply RICE in order — and the guide's fifth step, Referral — and state the blood-safety rule: no blood contact when helping others.

## What you need
A school first-aid kit where available: gloves, gauze or clean clothing, splints, slings, scissors — the guide's named items. Ice or cold water, a bandage and a cushion for RICE practice on a pretend injury.

## If the learner is stuck
- RICE letters are forgotten → the word itself is the memory: say RICE, then unpack it letter by letter on the fingers.
- A learner plays doctor beyond first aid → the guide's limit is printed: first aid is initial care in a limited skill range; referral is a step, not an afterthought.
