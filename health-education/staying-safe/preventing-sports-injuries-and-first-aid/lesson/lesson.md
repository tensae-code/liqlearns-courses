# Preventing Sports Injuries and First Aid

## Do it first

A game is running. Your friend lands badly and their ankle swells. What do you do — first, second, third? Write your steps. This lesson gives you the books' steps, and the word that holds them: RICE.

## Sub lesson 1 — Preventing sports injuries — the causes and the list

A **sports injury** is harm done to the body in sport or exercise. The Grade 6 book diagrams its causes: **no proper warm-up, exercising while tired, no proper kit, and disobeying instructions**.

Its prevention list:

- **Build fitness**;
- **increase speed gradually**;
- go **easy-to-hard**;
- **match the exercise to ability, sex and age**;
- do hard and complex moves **only with teacher support**;
- **use equipment properly**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sports injuries — the kinds

The Grade 7 book pictures and lists the **kinds of sports injuries** (fig. 1.1), and its exercises test which harms are — and are not — sports injuries, bone injury and muscle injury among the options.

The teacher guide names the kinds plainly: **sprains, dislocations, bone fractures and bruising**. Knowing the kinds is what lets the first help fit the harm.

## Sub lesson 3 — First aid and RICE

**First aid** is the **quick, careful help given where the accident happened, to save life until health-facility care arrives**.

For a hurt limb, the book pictures **RICE** (fig. 1.3):

- **R — Rest**: stop; rest the injured part.
- **I — Ice**: cold on the injury.
- **C — Compression — ማሰር**: bandage it, firm and careful.
- **E — Elevation — ከፍ ማድረግ**: raise it up.

Four letters, four steps, in order.

## Sub lesson 4 — RICER and the first-aid kit (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide gives first aid its purposes: **prevent further injury, preserve life, promote recovery**. Its kit: **gloves** (plastic bags or dish gloves), **gauze** (clean clothing), **splints** (straight wood, plastic, cardboard or metal), **slings and scissors**.

And it extends RICE to **RICER**, for the **first 72 hours**: **R**est — remove the person from activity; **I**ce — 15–20 minutes of cold; **C**ompression — a firm, elastic, non-adhesive bandage; **E**levation — above heart level, to decrease blood flow; **R**eferral — see a doctor or physiotherapist for an accurate diagnosis.

One rule stands over all of it, printed in the guide: when you manage others' injuries, **take care not to have blood contact** — it prevents the transmission of HIV and other diseases.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion RICE letter by letter — then RICER — then the one rule about blood. Finish with the four injury causes and how each is prevented.

## Do It Yourself

1. Practise RICE on a pretend injury (a cushion for the ankle, a cloth for ice, a scarf for the bandage): say each step aloud as you do it.
2. Make the class first-aid kit list from the guide and check what your school actually has. Report the gaps to the teacher.
3. Write the prevention list as a poster: six lines, one for each prevention.
