# Lesson plan — Staying Safe on the Street — Self-Defence Principles and Resolving Conflict

**Course:** Health Education · **Title:** Staying Safe · **Lesson:** Staying Safe on the Street — Self-Defence Principles and Resolving Conflict
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Staying Safe (Health Education course).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** staying safe before any fight: what self-defence is for, its principles (alertness, decisiveness, speed, surprise), the street-safety rules, the ethics that govern it, and resolving conflict with words first.
**This lesson's own level:** it follows Staying Safe While Exercising in the Staying Safe title — safety in exercise first, then safety on the street.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **What self-defence is — and the ethics that govern it** — the Grade 9 student book's §8.1 and the Grade 10 books' ethics: self-defence as a basic human right, built on identification and avoidance first, under fairness, integrity, responsibility and respect — with the Grade 9 teacher guide's own framing stocked as labelled evidence (the body itself as the weapon; "no rule in street fighting" where life hangs in the balance; the right to respond for yourself, your family and your friends).
2. **The principles — alertness, speed, surprise** — the Grade 9 student book's §8.2 with its teacher guide: the six principles, the hand cautions, and why sporting rules do not apply when life is the stake.
3. **Street-safety rules — the three strategies** — the Grade 9 student book's §8.2 rules with its teacher guide: know your path, be expected, walk confidently, trust instincts, avoid routines, carry noise.
4. **Resolving conflict without fighting — and why learn self-defence** — the Grade 10 student book's §§8.1.2 and 8.2 with its teacher guide: training's widened options, starting ages, nonverbal communication, forgiveness, and sticking with the facts.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what self-defence is for, and the four ethics virtues that govern it.
2. List the six principles and explain Principle Five (Speed) in the book's words.
3. Recite the street-safety rules and the three strategies in order.
4. Name three conflict-resolution skills and two benefits of self-defence classes for high-school students.
