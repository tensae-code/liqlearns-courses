# Hands-on — Self-Defence Techniques — Stance, Escapes, Blocks and Strikes, Grappling

One line of framing: these are the books' own practice forms, done with a teacher, on mats, in proper sportswear — and the practice safety rules govern every activity: while practising strikes, no one creates a problem on the partner's body; correct each other on the spot; swap roles so everyone defends and attacks.

## Step into the stance
In rows, step from standing normally into the basic stance. Your partner checks the four fundamentals — feet, torso, arms, head — and corrects you on the spot. Swap roles.

## Escape practice in pairs
On mats, in pairs of attacker and defender: the attacker holds loosely — never around the neck — and the defender bends forward from the waist, turns in, breaks away and takes two running steps toward the "busy place" marked at the edge of the mats. Practise the bear-hug escape, then the hands-trapped and side-headlock escapes, slowly. Swap roles after each round.

## Blocks and strikes to a pad
In rows, follow the blocks in the air: inward, outward, like a windshield wiper, fist at eye level. Then, in pairs, one partner holds the pad and the other practises the heel palm strike and the elbow strike to the pad only — slowly, with the weight shifting forward for the elbow. The front kick is practised in the air and to the pad, never toward a partner.

## Grappling hold practice
On mats, in pairs: take a close hold, hold safely, and release at your partner's tap. Swap the defender and attacker roles. Start simple — the throws belong to the advanced lesson.
