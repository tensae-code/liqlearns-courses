# Lesson plan — Non-communicable Diseases

**Course:** Health Education · **Title:** Staying Safe · **Lesson:** Non-communicable Diseases
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Staying Safe (Health Education course).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** non-communicable diseases — diabetes, hypertension, asthma, heart disease, cancer and obesity — their causes, risks and prevention; taught here through Cuba's Grade 2 pass on obesity: what obesity is, how it is spotted with Cuba's national BMI percentile tables, why it must be avoided, and the teacher's prevention work.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands in the Staying Safe title beside Staying Safe While Exercising.

## Sub lessons
1. **What obesity is — and how Cuba spots it (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Cuba Grade 2 teacher guide's obesity section: obesity as a chronic disease of excess body fat, its conditioning factors (hypercaloric processed food, sedentary screen habits), and identifying overweight/obesity with Cuban BMI percentile tables (overweight 90–97th percentile, obesity above 97th).
2. **Preventing overweight and obesity — the teacher's work (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Cuba Grade 2 teacher guide's basic elements for managing overweight and obesity: why obesity must be avoided (cardiovascular risk, type 2 diabetes, orthopaedic and psychosocial harm) and the teacher's prevention work — early identification and weight monitoring, healthy-eating guidance to parents, more active play and less sedentary screen time.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what obesity is, in the book's terms: a chronic disease of excess body fat.
2. Name its conditioning factors as the book names them: hypercaloric processed food and sedentary screen habits.
3. State Cuba's percentile rule: overweight is the 90–97th percentile, obesity is above the 97th.
4. Name why obesity must be avoided: cardiovascular risk, type 2 diabetes, orthopaedic and psychosocial harm.
5. Name the teacher's prevention work: early identification and weight monitoring, healthy-eating guidance to parents, more active play and less sedentary screen time.
