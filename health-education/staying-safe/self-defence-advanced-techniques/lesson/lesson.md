# Self-Defence — Advanced Techniques

## Do it first

Think of the three escapes you already know. What would make an escape "advanced" — and what must never change, however advanced the technique gets? Hold your answer: this lesson's techniques are still escape-first, still practised on mats with matched partners, and still the last resort.

## Sub lesson 1 — Advanced escapes — and the safety regime for advanced practice

Advanced work begins with a fact about attackers: most attackers look for someone who appears weak and easily intimidated. Awareness and a confident bearing are therefore part of the advanced escape itself — the first act of defence is still avoidance, and the advanced escape techniques are practised with the unit's safety framing, building directly on the Grade 9 escapes.

[Picture 35314 — Self-defence — advanced practice: a mat class, partners of similar weight]

That safety regime is printed in full, and it governs every practical part of this lesson: take the class to a safe, well-arranged mat class; before warm-up, explain that students must respect their partner and classmate; warm up with selected relevant stretching; pair students with similar weights — safer and advisable; if possible, use protection; and the teacher keeps keen attention and continuous observation over the class the whole time. The techniques of this lesson — escape strategy, strike and grappling — need more time, more practice and more repetition than the basics.

The moving picture for the advanced escape is a described slot only: a picture of the advanced escape performed slowly with a cooperating partner on the mats, the defender breaking the hold and stepping away to safety — watched with the teacher before any practice.

## Sub lesson 2 — Advanced strikes — last-resort awareness only

This part is knowledge only. There is no practice for it, no drill, and no moving picture — by design. What you learn here is awareness: what the book names as the body's weapons, so that you understand what a last resort means, under the ethics that govern all of this — identification and avoidance first, skill without arrogance, and force only when there is no other way.

The book names them in its printed sequence. The knee: the legs hold some of the body's strongest muscles — and the book's own caution travels with it: do not kick above the groin shot, or the attacker can catch and control your leg. The groin can be struck with a knee, a punch or a kick, and a strike from below is less likely to be blocked than one straight on. The book also names a thumb gouge to the eyes. These are named here as the book names them — as last-resort awareness, in the book's sequence, and nothing in this part is practised.

## Sub lesson 3 — The roundhouse kick

The roundhouse kick starts from a fighting stance with the left leg forward. From that starting position: swing the right leg forward, bending the knee; roll the hip over; and snap the foot out.

[GIF 35303 — Self-defence — the roundhouse kick to a pad]

In practice the kick goes to a pad held by a partner, on the mats, inside the safety regime of sub lesson 1 — slowly first, with the partner respected and the teacher watching.

## Sub lesson 4 — Grappling throws

Grappling in hand-to-hand combat is seizing the attacker at close range to gain a physical advantage. The throw builds on the grappling you already know, and its mechanics are printed as a control sequence: keep controlling the attacker's right arm — it is grabbed with your left hand, with his hand trapped at your armpit; get your hip squared and under the attacker's hip; that position is what makes the proper throw possible.

The moving picture for the throw is a described slot only: a picture of the hip throw with the arm controlled, performed on mats with a cooperating partner under the teacher's eye. A throw puts a partner's whole body in motion, so it is learned live, on mats, with a teacher — never copied from a screen.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the safety regime for advanced practice, and why sub lesson 2 is knowledge only.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the safety regime from memory: mat class, respect, stretching warm-up, similar weights, protection, the teacher watching.
2. With a partner holding a pad, practise the roundhouse kick slowly: stance, swing with the knee bent, hip rolls over, foot snaps out — then back to the stance.
3. Write the options ladder once more — avoid, talk, escape, skills last — and mark where every technique in this lesson sits.
