# Lesson plan — First Aid for Emergencies

**Course:** Health Education · **Title:** Staying Safe · **Lesson:** First Aid for Emergencies
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Staying Safe (Health Education course).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** first response for a sick learner — examining the learner with the ThinkMD guide, referring fast to the health facility, and the front-line care a health teacher gives before referral — as Zambia's Health Teacher Training Manual teaches them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands in the Staying Safe title beside Knowing My Body.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **ThinkMD and sick learner examination** — Zambia's teaching: using the ThinkMD guide to examine a sick learner and decide the next step (Chapter 5: ThinkMD and Sick Learner Examination, printed p. 50).
2. **The child health referral system** — Zambia's teaching: when and how to refer a sick child fast to the health facility (Chapter 23: Child Health Referral System, printed p. 200).
3. **Front-line care theory** — Zambia's teaching: first-response care a health teacher gives before referral (Chapter 24: Front-Line Care Theory, printed p. 204).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lessons — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lessons' whole question set is the gate pool.
