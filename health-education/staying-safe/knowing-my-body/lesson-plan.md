# Lesson plan — Knowing My Body

**Course:** Health Education · **Title:** Staying Safe · **Lesson:** Knowing My Body
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Staying Safe (Health Education course).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** knowing my body — the parts and organs of the body and what each one does; taught here through Afghanistan's Grade 7 pass: the water inside the body — body water as 40–60% of body weight, water inside and outside the cells, where the body's water comes from (drinks, food, metabolic water), and a table of the water content of common foods (teacher guide — evidence).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands in the Staying Safe title beside The Body Systems.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **The water inside the body — how much, and where it sits** — Afghanistan's Grade 7 teaching (teacher guide — evidence): body water as 40–60% of body weight, and water inside and outside the cells.
2. **Where the body's water comes from — and the water in foods** — Afghanistan's Grade 7 teaching (teacher guide — evidence): the body's water comes from drinks, food and metabolic water, with a table of the water content of common foods.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lessons — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lessons' whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Staying Safe While Exercising. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how much of body weight is water in the guide's teaching: 40–60%.
2. Say where the body's water sits: inside and outside the cells.
3. Name the three places the body's water comes from: drinks, food and metabolic water.
4. Say that the guide gives a table of the water content of common foods.
