# About the Health Education course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — the first part from the
health-knowledge sections of Ethiopia's Health and Physical
Education books, read in full, page by page. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## Where the first part comes from — stated honestly

Ethiopia's HPE student books Grades 1–4 carry **no health chapter
at all**; the first true health sections are in Grade 5
(exercise precautions, §1.2; doping, §3.6). There is no hygiene,
nutrition or disease chapter in the Grade 5 book, and none has
been invented for this course. The cleanliness and body-care
teaching of the younger years stands in the Science course,
where Ethiopia's Environmental Science books carry it. Book
entries are named in full in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`;
teacher guides are evidence only, never counted as pupil-taught
(the G1–G8 guide scans held are truncated; G9 is complete, G10
substantively complete).

**Acceptance reckoning:** this course's first part is taught in
1 country (Ethiopia), except any lesson whose sources state it
stands on teacher-guide evidence only — counted nowhere, and
says so.

## A note on the books' front matter — the same HPE books

The health sections in this course do not come from separate
health books. They arrive inside the same Health and Physical
Education books that stock the Physical Education course — one
book set, split by strand. Those books' front matter is filed in
full in the Physical Education course's About, under "What the
books say before the lessons start". The facts of that front
matter, in brief, are these:

- The student books open with a book-care message and an
  introduction: Grade 1 Student Textbook, book-care printed
  p. ii and introduction printed pp. ii–iii; Grade 2, Grade 3 and
  Grade 4 Student Textbooks, book-care printed p. ii each and
  introduction printed pp. iv–v each; Grade 5 Student Textbook,
  book-care printed p. ii and introduction and how to use the
  book printed pp. v–vi. The care messages give the series
  book-care list (in Grade 1, in full: cover the book, keep it
  dry and clean, use a bookmark, never write in it or tear
  pages), and the introductions give the curriculum-renewal
  rationale, activity-based, learner-centred use, and messages
  to pupils and to parents/guardians.
- The Grade 5 Teacher Guide's introduction (printed p. iii)
  states the class-time ruling in words: about 40% of class time
  to theory-type activity and about 60% to practice, taught
  mixed — a ruling the Grade 6 Teacher Guide's introduction
  (printed pp. I–II) repeats.
- The Grade 7 Teacher Guide's introduction (printed p. iii)
  states the inclusion duty: motivate students with physical
  impairment or health problems to participate actively, create
  a safe learning environment for them, and organise special and
  adapted activities for them in advance; it also names the
  assessment areas (attendance and dressing, social,
  psychological and emotional development, skill, theory).

Those rulings govern the health lessons here as much as the
movement lessons: health knowledge in this course is taught
inside that same 40% theory / 60% practice mix, and the
inclusion duty for a learner with impairment or a health problem
applies to health participation too.
