# Doping — What It Is and What It Costs

## Do it first

Before a race, someone offers a runner a tablet. "No training needed. You will just be faster." Three questions: Is it fair? Is it safe? Is it allowed? By the end of this lesson you will answer all three the way the books do.

## Sub lesson 1 — What doping is — honest sport

**Doping** is the use of **sport performance-enhancing substances** — substances taken to boost performance in sport. The Grade 5 book places the definition where it belongs: as the closing section of the health-and-fitness chapter, after everything fitness honestly builds.

Set it beside Chapter 1's honest-sport values and the picture's rule holds: **clean sport — strength you earn yourself**. No substances are pictured in this lesson, exactly as the book's placement directs for children.

## Sub lesson 2 — Banned — and the fight against it (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide defines it fully: **doping substances are what an athlete takes — injected, in tablets or in drinks — to gain strength and energy unfairly**: strength **not earned by nature or training**. They are forbidden because they are **both cheating and harmful** — taking them damages the body.

The fight against doping, in the guide's history: in **1967** the IOC set up its medical commission on stimulants; in **1968, at Mexico**, the **first doping tests** in sport were held; in **1999** the world anti-doping agency **WADA** was founded; and **Ethiopia's national anti-doping office** began work under **proclamation 400/2009**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Doping's harms — the Grade 6 map

The Grade 6 book maps doping's harm across four areas: **health, social relations, sport and the economy** — and its chapter exercise asks you to list doping's effects on athletes.

The Grade 6 teacher guide walks the same map in three families. **Body:** a false, borrowed strength that endangers the athlete's health and life. **Mind:** dependence and craving; irritability, lost patience, anxiety and depression, despair. **Social life:** income stopped — heavy fines and lost earnings for the caught athlete — and **the country's place in sport and Olympic programmes put at risk**.

## Sub lesson 4 — What doping costs — the Grade 7 reckoning

The Grade 7 book develops the harm teaching: doping is **science-made performance substances**, and their effects are the lesson.

The teacher guide reckons the cost in **four consequence families**: **health** — long-term harm, and doping can trigger anxiety, obsessive disorder or psychosis; **social** — stigma in many forms; **financial** — prize money returned or a financial sanction paid, sponsorship contracts terminated; **sanction** — results disqualified and medals forfeited, and a **ban from all sport — competing, training or coaching — for up to four years, or even life** in repeat or most serious cases.

The borrowed strength is repaid — with health, name, money and career.

## Sub lesson 5 — Stopping doping — history, rules and bans (Grade 8)

The Grade 8 book gives doping its longest history yet. Its use is recorded **since 668 BCE — Greek athletes, and even their horses, were given stimulants**. Controls began in the **1920s**; the IAAF **banned stimulants from 1928**; cycling and football federations began testing in **1966**; the IOC **banned doping in 1967**; and **WADA was founded in 1999**.

Why banned? The book's reasons: doping **spoils sport's good image, protects athletes from health disorder, keeps competition equal and clean** — and sport is **a contest of athletes, not of doctors and drug factories**. Its sanctions, as printed: **first offence — warning, result cancelled, up to 1 year ban; second offence — 2 years; third offence — lifetime ban.**

## Sub lesson 6 — Consequences — health, sanctions and a ruined name (Grade 9)

Grade 9 teaches the consequences whole. Doping is **the deliberate or inadvertent use of drugs or procedures that enhance performance** — and **anyone who intentionally assists can be excluded too**. Most athletes are clean; doping is banned first because **it is cheating — it destroys the genuine challenge** — and under anti-doping rules, **not knowing is not an excuse**.

The consequences are twofold. **Health:** including the **infection risk from non-sterile or shared needles — hepatitis and HIV/AIDS** — and the pictured effects of steroids. **Sport and life:** sanctions, and **lasting reputation damage** — a ban, the book says, **is only the beginning**: the health, mental-health, social and financial results are far-reaching. The teacher guide adds the reckoning: **results disqualified, awards, points and prizes forfeited**, and the banned athlete **socially isolated** — set against clean pioneers **Abebe Bikila and Mamo Wolde**, who reached the top without doping.

## Sub lesson 7 — The Anti-Doping Rules — the WADA Code (Grade 10)

Grade 10 names the rulebook. A governing body controls and protects clean sport: **WADA revises its Code over time — the 2021 Code has 27 articles** — and **international federations and national anti-doping bodies apply it**. The violations include **administration, or attempted administration, of a prohibited substance or method to an athlete**.

The teacher guide gives the Code its meaning: WADA protects **athletes' fundamental right to take part in doping-free sport**, national programmes work through **education, deterrence and detection** — and its classroom line stands over the whole lesson: **the spirit of sport is expressed in how we play true, and doping is fundamentally contrary to the spirit of sport.** Prevention, the Grade 8 guide adds in three words: **Educate, Counsel and Communicate** with athletes.
