# Lesson plan — Doping — What It Is and What It Costs

**Course:** Health Education · **Title:** Fair Play and the Body · **Lesson:** Doping — What It Is and What It Costs
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Fair Play and the Body (Health Education course).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what doping is — prohibited substances an athlete takes to gain strength and energy unfairly, strength not earned by nature or training — why it is banned as both cheating and harm, the fight against it (1968 tests, the IOC's 1967 medical commission, WADA 1999, Ethiopia's anti-doping office), and what it costs: health, mind, name, income and career.
**This lesson's own level:** it opens the Fair Play and the Body title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs here are 2 seconds, drawn, and show the movement the way it really moves.

## Sub lessons
1. **What doping is — honest sport** — the Grade 5 student book's §3.6 — the only doping section in Grades 1–5: sport performance-enhancing substances (doping) defined, closing the health-and-fitness chapter.
2. **Banned — and the fight against it (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 5 teacher guide's doping section: substances injected, in tablets or drinks, giving strength not earned by nature or training; forbidden as cheating and harm; the history — 1967 IOC medical commission, 1968 Mexico tests, WADA 1999, Ethiopia's office under proclamation 400/2009.
3. **Doping's harms — the Grade 6 map** — the Grade 6 student book's harm diagram across health, social relations, sport and the economy, with the Grade 6 teacher guide's three harm families: body, mind and social life, income stopped and the country's place at risk.
4. **What doping costs — the Grade 7 reckoning** — the Grade 7 student book's effects pass, with the Grade 7 teacher guide's four consequence families: health, social, financial and sanction — disqualification, forfeited medals, bans of up to four years or life.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — this is the opening lesson of its title. The Olympic Games lesson in Physical Education is its companion.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define doping in the books' terms: performance-enhancing substances taken for an unnatural, forbidden advantage.
2. Tell the fight's history as the guide gives it: first doping tests at Mexico 1968; the IOC medical commission of 1967; WADA founded 1999; Ethiopia's national anti-doping office working under proclamation 400/2009.
3. Map doping's harms in the books' families: health, mind (psychological), social life, sport and the economy — and the sanctions: disqualification, returned prize money, lost sponsorship, bans up to four years or life.
4. Refuse the offered substance: say why, in the books' own reasons.

## What you need
Nothing — a knowledge lesson. The picture carries the clean-sport message; no substances are pictured for children, as the Grade 5 placement directs.

## If the learner is stuck
- "It is only cheating, not dangerous" → the guide's two reasons stand together: forbidden because it is both cheating and harmful; walk both lists.
- Dates of the fight blur → four stops, one per finger: commission, tests, WADA, Ethiopia.
