# Fair Play in Sport

## Do it first

What does fair play mean when you play a game? Say what you think first — then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The values carried by games and physical activities

Haiti's Grades 10–13 framework programme teaches fair play as **civic education**: a whole EPS theme is "adaptation to the environment, respect for self and adversary: **democratic culture, peace and non-violence**". The first step is **identifying the values carried by games and physical activities** — games carry values, and the learner learns to name them. (This lesson comes from a teacher framework programme — it is the teachers' book for the EPS course.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Managing conflict in and out of play

The framework teaches **managing conflict in and out of play** — conflict is handled, in the game and outside it, as part of the same democratic culture of peace and non-violence.

## Sub lesson 3 — Refereeing, the rules, and respect

The framework teaches **learning refereeing and applying the rules**, and **respecting authority and the adversary** — the referee's authority and the opponent across from you are both respected, and the rules are applied as they stand.

## Sub lesson 4 — Tournaments for peace and camaraderie

The framework ends the theme with **organising recreational tournaments for peace and camaraderie at school** — play organised on purpose so that peace and friendship are what the tournament builds.
