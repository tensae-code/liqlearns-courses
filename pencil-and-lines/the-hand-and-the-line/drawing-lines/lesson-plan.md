# Lesson plan — Drawing lines

**Course:** Pencil and Lines · **Title:** The hand and the line ·
**Lesson:** Drawing lines
**Place in the curriculum:** Before Level 1 of every language course —
the second lesson of the prerequisite course (English CURRICULUM.md,
"Before Level 1").
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two roads every letter is
made of — the wavy line (curved moves, like hills) and the zigzag
line (straight moves with sharp turns, like mountains).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books — this lesson's source is a
picture book (Egypt KG1 is image-only), so its Trace box enters this
plan exactly this way. On the website the real add-on is dropped into
that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.
Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with
its source).

## Before this lesson
- Holding a pencil (the lesson before this one): the five-step grip
  first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw a wavy line — up and down, smooth, no sharp corners — slow
   and controlled.
2. Draw a zigzag line — up, sharp turn, down, sharp turn — slow,
   with clean corners.
3. Say which of the two roads a letter is mostly made of (o, c, s —
   curved; z, w, N — straight with corners).

## What you need
A pencil and paper.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — with the five-step grip, draw one line across
   the page, any line, your way; look at it and keep it — it is the
   "before" line (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of one page with
     a single wobbly line across it — the student draws their own
     "before" line under it and keeps the page.
2. **The explanation** — a line is the pencil's road; every letter is
   the two moves mixed; the book's Trace box gives the two roads to
   practice, each starting where its arrow starts (lesson/
   lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE for each road: a picture of the wavy road — dotted
     hills with a small arrow at the start — and a picture of the
     zigzag road — dotted mountains with their arrow — the student
     follows each road in the air with a finger first, then on
     paper with the pencil, from the arrow, slowly.
   - ADD GIF at the wavy road: a picture that moves — a pencil
     travelling the hills without stopping, smooth all the way —
     the student watches where the pencil does NOT stop, then draws
     their own row without lifting.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the two kinds of lines and
   name one letter made mostly of each.
   - ADD VIDEO: the two roads drawn on camera, slow, by a real hand
     (made later, when videos are made; this course's lessons carry
     hands-on and levels files, no script file — the tracing is the
     script) — the student draws along, one road per watch.
4. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
5. **Levels practice** — levels.md, tied to the learner's
   skill number.
6. **Do It Yourself** — a row of wavy hills, slow and smooth; a row
   of zigzag mountains with clean corners; both rows again, a little
   faster, still controlled; compare with the "before" line and keep
   both pages.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of a "before" page, one wobbly line → the
  student draws their own before line and keeps it.
- IMAGE — a picture of the dotted wavy road with its start arrow →
  the student traces it in the air, then on paper.
- IMAGE — a picture of the dotted zigzag road with its start arrow →
  the student traces it the same way.
- GIF — a picture that moves: a pencil travelling the hills without
  stopping → the student draws a row without lifting the pencil.
- VIDEO — the two roads drawn slow on camera (made later) → the
  student draws along.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- The wavy line gets corners → the hand is stopping; slow down until
  "smooth" is possible — a line controlled at slow speed is yours,
  speed comes later by itself.
- The zigzag corners go round → sharp turns are the point of this
  road: stop, turn, go. Stopping is allowed here; it is the wavy
  road where it isn't.
