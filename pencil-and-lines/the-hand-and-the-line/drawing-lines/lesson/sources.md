Second book, same country (added 2026-10-25): Egypt KG2 Term 1 opens
with a "Remember With Me!" page — trace the wavy and zigzag lines
"start from the dot," then trace shapes (square, circle, triangle,
heart), book p.1. KG2 assumes KG1's hand work and refreshes it first
thing — the lines lesson is the doorway into the next book too.

Third book, same country (added 2026-10-25): Egypt Primary 1 Term 1
keeps the line-tracing going inside its phonics lessons — dotted
paths to trace on PDF p.10 (zigzag, stepped, wavy — animals to their
objects), p.14 (loop, wave, arch paths), p.16 (straight, curved, wavy
vertical paths), and p.23 (five paths — straight, zigzag, wavy, big
wave, small wave — each led by a bug). Unit 2 adds p.31 (straight,
stepped, zigzag, wavy — butterflies to flowers) and p.40 (wavy, arch,
zigzag-wave, zigzag, drawn by pencils). The hand work does not stop
at KG: Primary 1 still opens its letter lessons with lines.

Second country (added 2026-10-25): Libya — English for Libya
Primary 1, Activity Book (CERC, Ministry of Education, Libya;
verified on disk: %PDF, 110 pp). Libya practises the hand a
different way: **dot-to-dot** — numbered dots scattered on the page,
joined in number order with straight lines until a picture stands.
Unit 4 Lesson 7 (book p.38) and Unit 6 Lesson 7 (book p.58) join
dots 1–10; Unit 9 Lesson 4 (book p.85) joins 1–18. The move is the
straight line from one point to the next — the zigzag lesson's
straight-and-corner move, aimed at a target. The Teacher's Book
adds a second Libya line page the Activity extraction could not
show (it is all picture, no print): Activity Book p.18 (Unit 2
Lesson 7) is a **"Trace the lines"** page — lines with different
movements, traced with finger or pencil for pencil control and
left-to-right movement, the teacher reminding pupils how to hold
the pencil correctly (Teacher's Book, Unit 2 Lesson 7 notes).

Second Libya book (added 2026-10-25): Libya — English for Libya
Primary 2, Activity Book (CERC / Garnet, 2019; verified on disk:
%PDF, 94 pp). Unit 6 Lesson 6 (book p.53) is a **dot-to-dot 1–20**
— "What is it? Draw and colour." — the same join-the-dots move,
now run to 20: the line work grows with the counting.

Third Libya book (added 2026-10-25): Libya — English for Libya
Primary 3, Activity Book (CERC / Garnet, 2019; verified on disk:
%PDF, 146 pp). Unit 5 Lesson 7 (book p.84) is **"Look and draw
the shape"**: dots numbered 1–20 are joined in ranges — 1 to 5,
6 to 10, 11 to 15, 16 to 20 — and each run of the line draws one
side-set of a shape the child then names. Joining dots has become
drawing shapes: the line work now builds the shape words
(triangle, square, circle, rectangle) of the Pupils Book's
maths lesson.

Third country (added 2026-11-03): Sierra Leone — *Lesson plans
for PRIMARY Language ARTS*, Primary 1, Term 1 (Ministry of
Education, Science and Technology, Sierra Leone; teacher
lesson-plan book, no publication year printed; full read in
english/books/sierra-leone.md). Sierra Leone's hand work is
**pattern drawing**: Lesson 12, "Drawing patterns," has the
child draw patterns, including the patterns of the letters in
pupils' own names — the letter's line-moves practised before
the letter itself — and Lesson 35, "Drawing," reviews the
d-words and draws patterns: a circle, then three circles in
a row as a pattern. It is not Egypt's dotted-line tracing or
Libya's dot-to-dot; it is the same pre-letter hand work in a
third shape, the repeated pattern. (The letter lessons' fixed
routine also has every child trace and write each new letter
in both cases — that tracing is filed in the alphabet lesson,
whose move it is.)

Fourth country (added 2026-11-03): Iraq — *English for Iraq*,
Grade 1, Pupil and Activity Book (Garnet Publishing Ltd /
Garnet Education, for Iraq's Ministry of Education; filed in
english/books/iraq.md (Grade 1 — English for Iraq, 1st
Primary, Pupil and Activity Book (combined pupil + activity
book) section, FULLY READ + CITATION CHECK PASSED
2026-11-03)). Iraq's line work is Egypt's shape, not Libya's
dot-to-dot or Sierra Leone's patterns: the Activities half
of the Grade 1 book (printed pp.89–161) opens with

and the Grade 1 Teacher's Book's skills spine names the
same ground "pre-writing strokes" on the way to lower-case
formation. The tracing comes first in the workbook, before
copying whole words.

Fifth country (added 2026-11-03): Ethiopia — *English for

**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Egypt, 3 books; Libya,
3 books; Sierra Leone — pattern drawing, Primary 1 Term 1;
Iraq — dotted-line tracing opening the Grade 1 Activities,
printed pp.89ff; Ethiopia — "Trace the dotted lines" opening
the Grade 1 writing work, printed p.2; South Africa — tracing
only: letter-formation tracing in HL and FAL Grade 1, no free
line-drawing teaching; Syria — pre-writing tracing lines
opening the Grade 1 Workbook's first unit, printed pp.3–5,
and pencil-control tracing of lines, spirals and shapes in
its Handwriting Practice, printed pp.78–82).**
The "understand the lines" framing is his: the lines are understood,
not just traced — a wavy line is the curved move, a zigzag is the
straight-and-corner move, and letters are those two moves mixed.
Iraq joins with dotted lines traced at the very opening of
its first workbook.
Ethiopia joins with "Trace the dotted lines" (printed p.2)
opening its Grade 1 writing work, and dotted letters and
words traced across Unit 1.
Counts grow as other finished countries' first books are read.
