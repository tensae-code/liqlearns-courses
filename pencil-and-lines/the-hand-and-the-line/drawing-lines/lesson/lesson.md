# Drawing lines — the lesson

Course: **Pencil and Lines** — the prerequisite course before every
language course. Title: The hand and the line · Lesson: Drawing lines
Built from: Egypt, English KG1 Term 1, book p.1 (the Trace box).

## Do it first
With your pencil held the five-step way, draw one line across your
page — any line, your way. Look at it. Is it smooth? Does it go where
you told it? Keep that line; it is your "before."

## Sub lesson 1 — Drawing the wavy line

So a line is the pencil's road. And here is the secret of the whole
course: **every letter in every language is made of lines** — only two
kinds of moves, really. Curved moves, like a hill. And straight moves,
up, down, and across, like a mountain's sides.

The book gives you two roads to practice, in a box that says "Trace":

- **The wavy line** — up and down, up and down, smooth like small
  hills. No sharp corners. Your hand flows and does not stop. This is
  the move inside round letters: o, c, s, a.

## Sub lesson 2 — Drawing the zigzag line

- **The zigzag line** — up, sharp turn, down, sharp turn, like
  mountains in a row. This is the move inside straight letters with
  corners: z, w, v, N.

You follow each dotted line from where the arrow starts. Slow first.
A line you control at slow speed is yours; speed comes later by
itself, the way it always does when the hand understands.

Understand the two lines and you understand what letters are made of.
When a letter comes later and looks hard, you will be able to say:
"Ah — a hill and a mountain together." Nothing new. Just the two
roads, mixed.

## Say it to your companion
Explain to your companion: what are the two kinds of lines, and name
one letter made mostly of each. (Curved — o, c, s. Straight with
corners — z, w, N.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Trace or draw a row of wavy hills, slow and smooth. Then a row of
   zigzag mountains, slow, with clean corners.
2. Draw your two rows again, a little faster, still controlled.
3. Compare with your "before" line from the top of this lesson. Keep
   both — this page is the first proof your hand is learning.
