# Sources — Holding a pencil

Course: Pencil and Lines — the prerequisite course before every
language course (his ruling, 2026-10-25).

| Lesson part | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| "Let's learn how to hold a pencil correctly" — the 5 grip pictures | Egypt — English Language KG1 Term 1 (student book), book p.1 (PDF p.5 of English_KG1_Tr1.pdf; verified on disk 2026-10-25: %PDF, 8,212,061 B, 100 pp, image-only — page read as a picture) |

(The Trace box on the same page is the source of the Drawing lines
lesson, next door.)

Second country (added 2026-10-25): Libya — "21st Century English
for Libya" Primary 1, Teacher's Book (CERC / Garnet Education;
verified on disk: %PDF, 190 pp). Libya names holding the pencil as
a course aim outright: the Unit 2, 3 and 4 aims each include
"Learn how to hold a pencil correctly," and the lesson notes check
it again and again — "check that pupils are seated correctly and
have their books in a suitable position… Remind pupils how to hold
the pencil correctly" (Unit 2 Lesson 7 notes, on Activity Book
p.18's trace-the-lines page), "make sure they are sharpened and
pupils know how to hold them correctly" (Unit 6 Lesson 4 notes).
Libya teaches the grip by the teacher showing it, not by printed
steps — the printed five-step pictures remain Egypt's — but the
part itself, *learn to hold a pencil correctly before the letters*,
is taught in Libya too.

**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Egypt — printed steps;
Libya — named aim, taught by showing).**
This material began inside the English course (as Letter →
before-the-letters) and was moved here when he ruled it a normal
course of its own, prerequisite before every language — it is not
English-specific; the same hand writes every language's words.
Counts grow as other finished countries' first books are read.
