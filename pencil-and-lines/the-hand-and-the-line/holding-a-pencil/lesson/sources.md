Second country (added 2026-10-25): Libya — "21st Century English
for Libya" Primary 1, Teacher's Book (CERC / Garnet Education;
verified on disk: %PDF, 190 pp). Libya names holding the pencil as
a course aim outright: the Unit 2, 3 and 4 aims each include
"Learn how to hold a pencil correctly," and the lesson notes check
it again and again — "check that pupils are seated correctly and
have their books in a suitable position… Remind pupils how to hold
the pencil correctly" (Unit 2 Lesson 7 notes, on Activity Book
p.18's trace-the-lines page), "make sure they are sharpened and
pupils know how to hold them correctly" (Unit 6 Lesson 4 notes).
In Primary 1, Libya teaches the grip by the teacher showing it,
not by printed steps — the printed five-step pictures remain
Egypt's — but the part itself, *learn to hold a pencil correctly
before the letters*, is taught in Libya too.

Libya's printed steps arrive in Primary 2 (added 2026-10-25):
Libya — "21st Century English for Libya" Primary 2, Teacher's
Book (CERC / Garnet, 2019; verified on disk: %PDF, 154 pp),
Unit 1, in the Activity Book note to Lesson 2 — the three steps
in words, given as the preparation for tracing and writing:
**1 Sit correctly** — both feet on the floor, arms and hands on
the writing surface. **2 Hold the pencil correctly** — "between
the thumb and first finger and with the second finger acting as
support. The grip should be comfortable and relaxed. The free
hand should control and stabilize the paper." **3 Position
their book correctly** — turned about 30–40 degrees, to the
right or left by the child's writing hand; left-handers hold
the pencil farther from the point so they can see what they
have written, may use a higher chair, and need space on their
left side. The Introduction's Unit 1 aims name it too ("learn
how to hold a pencil correctly"), and the Unit 1 lesson notes
keep checking it ("check there are no problems holding the
pencil"). So Libya's way grows across the two years: shown by
the teacher in Grade 1, written as steps in Grade 2 — and its
steps cover more than the grip: sitting and the book's angle
come with it.

Third country (added 2026-11-03): Sierra Leone — *Lesson plans
for PRIMARY Language ARTS*, Primary 1, Term 1 (Ministry of
Education, Science and Technology, Sierra Leone; teacher
lesson-plan book, no publication year printed; full read in
english/books/sierra-leone.md). Lesson 17, "Different parts of
a book," teaches the child to use an exercise book correctly —
front cover from back cover, how to open and close it, and
**how to hold a pencil correctly**. Sierra Leone names the
grip inside book-handling, where Egypt prints the grip
pictures and Libya names it as a course aim: three countries,
three ways into the same first move — the hand is taught
before the letters in all three.

Fourth country (added 2026-11-03): Iraq — *English for Iraq*,
Grade 1, Teacher's Book (Garnet Publishing Ltd / Garnet
Education, for Iraq's Ministry of Education; filed in
english/books/iraq.md (Grade 1 — English for Iraq, 1st
Primary, Teacher's Book (teacher's guide) section, FULLY
READ + CITATION CHECK PASSED 2026-11-03)). Iraq's
Introduction names the same three "ready-to-write" steps
Libya prints — sit correctly, **hold the pencil correctly**,
position the book correctly — with a note for left-handers
(printed p.29). The steps are the teacher's preparation for
writing, named before the letters, exactly where Libya puts
them.
