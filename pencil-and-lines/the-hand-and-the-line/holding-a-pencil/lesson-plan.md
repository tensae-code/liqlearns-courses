# Lesson plan — Holding a pencil

**Course:** Pencil and Lines · **Title:** The hand and the line ·
**Lesson:** Holding a pencil
**Place in the curriculum:** Before Level 1 of every language course —
this course is the recommended prerequisite that comes first
(English CURRICULUM.md, "Before Level 1").
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the book's five-step grip —
the pencil resting, not squeezed, in a small chair made of fingers.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books — this lesson's source is a
picture book (Egypt KG1 is image-only), so its five pictures enter
this plan exactly this way. On the website the real add-on is dropped
into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's
guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real
art, with its source).

## Before this lesson
- Nothing. This is the first lesson of the first course — the hand
  comes before every letter, in every language.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Pick up a pencil and find the grip by themselves: thumb and first
   finger on top, pencil resting on the middle finger.
2. Tell squeezing from resting (white fingers = squeezing).
3. Put the pencil down and pick it up again into the same grip,
   five times.

## What you need
A pencil and a table. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner holds their pencil up their own way
   and takes a photo in their head of where their fingers are; then
   puts it down (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of a child's hand
     holding a pencil the book's way, close up — the student holds
     their own pencil beside it and says one way their fingers are
     different.
2. **The explanation** — the five pictures from Egypt KG1, page 1:
   hand comes down open; thumb and first finger take the pencil like
   a bird's beak; it rests on the middle finger; it tilts back into
   the soft place; you write (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE for each step: a picture of the hand at step 1, then
     step 2, and so on — five pictures in a row — the student copies
     each picture with their own hand before moving to the next.
   - ADD GIF at step 2: a picture that moves — two fingers closing
     on a pencil the way a bird's beak takes something small, again
     and again — the student copies the beak move until it is soft,
     not a squeeze.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain why every language starts
   here and not with A.
   - ADD VIDEO: the five steps filmed with a real hand and a real
     pencil, slow (made later, when videos are made; this course's
     lessons carry hands-on and levels files, no script file — the
     five steps are the script) — the student follows along, step by
     step, pausing the video at each step.
4. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
5. **Levels practice** — levels.md, tied to the learner's
   skill number.
6. **Do It Yourself** — hold the pencil the five-step way and check
   the fingers; put it down and pick it up five times until the hand
   finds the grip by itself; use this grip as the warm-up for every
   writing lesson from now on.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of the finished grip, close up → the student
  compares their own fingers and names one difference.
- IMAGE — a picture of each of the five steps, in a row → the
  student copies each step before moving on.
- GIF — a picture that moves: fingers closing like a bird's beak →
  the student copies the soft beak move.
- VIDEO — the five steps filmed slow (made later) → the student
  follows along, pausing at each step.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Fingers turn white → they are squeezing; shake the hand out and
  rest the pencil again — the pencil sits in a chair, it is not
  held up by force.
- The pencil slides out → check step 3: it must rest ON the middle
  finger underneath; the two top fingers only steady it.
