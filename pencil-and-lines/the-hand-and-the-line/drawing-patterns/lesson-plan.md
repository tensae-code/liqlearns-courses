# Lesson plan — Drawing patterns

**Course:** Pencil and Lines · **Title:** The hand and the line ·
**Lesson:** Drawing patterns
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Pencil and Lines, of the English path — and the recommended
prerequisite that comes first in every language course
(English CURRICULUM.md, Unit 1).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the hand copying patterns —
looking at a pattern of lines and shapes, and drawing the same
pattern yourself — and answering by drawing.

## Why this lesson exists
No Pencil and Lines lesson housed copying patterns. Holding a
pencil teaches the grip; Drawing lines teaches the wavy and the
zigzag. Sierra Leone's first plans put a third thing between
them: "Drawing patterns" — draw patterns of the letters in
their names (Primary 1 Term 1, Lesson 12), "Drawing" — draw
and copy different patterns (Lesson 35) — and a year later,
answering a text by drawing (Primary 3 Term 1, Lesson 1). The
2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked these NEW LESSON
NEEDED for this course. Under the everything-goes-in ruling
(2026-11-06) they get their own lesson. Sources: the Sierra
Leone plans named in lesson/sources.md — teacher evidence,
cited as such.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Copying patterns** — look at a pattern, say what repeats, and draw the same pattern: rows of lines, curves, corners and shapes, steady and in order.
2. **Drawing to answer** — drawing as an answer: read or hear a thing, and draw what it says — the hand answering before the words can.

Gate pool: none — this lesson has no questions/ files.

## Before this lesson
- Holding a pencil (its grip) — recommended, never forced: a
  learner can copy patterns with any grip, and the grip lesson
  stands next door as the "start here instead".

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Look at a pattern and say what repeats in it.
2. Copy a pattern of lines and shapes so a partner can see it
   is the same pattern.
3. Answer a short read or heard text with a drawing that shows
   its main thing.

## What you need
A pencil and paper. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner is shown a pattern for ten
   seconds, it is covered, and they draw what they remember
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of a row of
     patterns — lines, curves, corners, circles — the student
     points at each and says what repeats.
2. **Sub lesson 1** — copying patterns: say what repeats, then
   draw it, row by row.
   - ADD GIF at the copying step: a picture that moves — a
     hand copying a pattern slowly, one shape at a time — the
     student copies the move shape by shape.
3. **Sub lesson 2** — drawing to answer: hear a short text,
   draw its main thing.
4. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
5. **Do It Yourself** — copy a pattern from a cloth or a mat
   at home; draw the answer to a story someone reads you.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of a row of patterns → the student names
  what repeats in each.
- GIF — a picture that moves: a hand copying a pattern shape
  by shape → the student copies along.

## If the learner is stuck
- The copy drifts from the pattern → slow down to one shape at
  a time, and say each shape aloud before drawing it.
- The drawing-to-answer comes out as decoration → ask "what is
  the ONE thing the text said?" and draw only that.
