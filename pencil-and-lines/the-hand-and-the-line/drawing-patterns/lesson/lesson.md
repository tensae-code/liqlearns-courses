# Drawing patterns — the lesson

Course: **Pencil and Lines** — the prerequisite course that comes
before every language course (his ruling, 2026-10-25: learners have to
learn the words, and words are written — the hand comes first).
Title: The hand and the line · Lesson: Drawing patterns
Built from: Sierra Leone, Primary 1 and Primary 3 Language Arts lesson
plans — the drawing work the plans teach between the grip and the lines.

## Do it first
Look at this pattern for ten seconds:

∿ ∿ ∿ — / / / — ○ ○ ○ — ∿ ∿ ∿

Now it is covered. Draw what you remember.

Hard? Your eye saw it, but your hand needs a way in. This
lesson is that way in: look, say what repeats, copy.

## Sub lesson 1 — Copying patterns

A **pattern** is shapes that repeat in an order.

Copying one goes in three moves:

1. **Look** at the whole pattern.
2. **Say what repeats** — "wave, wave, wave — line, line,
   line — circle, circle, circle."
3. **Draw it**, one shape at a time, in the same order, the
   same size as you can.

The first patterns the plans teach are the patterns inside
the letters of your own name — curves and lines that will
soon be letters. Then patterns of every kind: rows of hooks,
steps, stars, zigzags, rings.

Copy this one: ∩ ∩ ∩ — √ √ √ — □ □ □. Say it first. Then
draw it. Check yours against it, shape by shape.

## Sub lesson 2 — Drawing to answer

Drawing is also a way to **answer**.

Someone reads: "A goat stands under a mango tree. A girl
carries a bucket to the well." Before you can write that,
you can **draw** it — the goat, the tree, the girl, the
bucket, the well — and your drawing shows you understood.

Draw the main thing, not everything. One drawing, one clear
answer: what happened, who was there, where it was.

Hear this: "The fisherman mends his net by the boat while
the sun goes down." Draw your answer.

## Do It Yourself

1. Copy a pattern you can see at home — from a cloth, a mat,
   a wall. Say what repeats before you draw.
2. Ask someone to read you a short story. Draw your answer:
   its main thing.
3. Make your own pattern of four shapes that repeat. A
   partner copies it and says what repeats.
