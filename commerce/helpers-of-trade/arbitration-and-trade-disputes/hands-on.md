# Hands-on — Arbitration and Trade Disputes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two traders agree a price for a lorry-load of grain. When the grain arrives, the buyer says it is poorer than promised and refuses to pay the full price. The seller says the grain is exactly as agreed. Who decides — and how fast, and at what cost? That deciding, without a long court fight, is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: when traders fall out over a bargain, the dispute can be settled by arbitration — a trusted decider chosen by the trade itself — and a commodity exchange enforces its contracts through the margin traders deposit, a guarantee fund standing behind every trade, and its own arbitration, so bargains hold and quarrels are decided quickly by people who know the trade. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
