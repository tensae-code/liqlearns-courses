# Hands-on — Tourism in Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A family from far away spends a week in your town. They pay for the bus, a room, meals, a guide and gifts to take home. Count how many local people earned from that one visit. That earning — visitors buying services — is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: tourism is a trade service: visitors pay for transport, lodging, food, guiding and sights, so a place earns from tourism as it earns from any other service in trade — and like other trade it can be developed and promoted on purpose, with real gains and real costs to weigh. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
