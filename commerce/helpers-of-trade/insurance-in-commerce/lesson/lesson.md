# Insurance in Commerce

## Do it first

Imagine a fire takes a trader's whole stock in one night. Alone, he is ruined. Now imagine a hundred traders each paid a small sum every year into one fund, and the fund rebuilds his shop. That pool — and its rules — is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — How insurance works — and its three principles

**Insurance as pooled protection.** Insurance is pooled group protection against loss, traced to merchants sharing cargo risk in ancient China. Learn its three working words: the premium — what you pay in; the claim — what you ask back when the loss happens; and the policy — the contract that binds both. And see where the money works: premiums are invested (in Samoa, through the NPF) to earn the income that pays claims and administration.

**The three principles.** Three principles guard the pool. Insurable interest: you may only insure what you stand to lose. Indemnity: a loss is made good, never profited from. Utmost good faith: tell the insurer the whole truth — false details void the claim. Break any one of the three and the protection breaks with it.

**Principles.** The history of insurance companies in Nigeria and the basic principles that apply to all classes of insurance (Nigeria's book.)

**Indemnity-principles.** The principles that apply to indemnity insurance alone (Nigeria's book.)

## Sub lesson 2 — The policies a trader can take

**The policy family, sorted three ways.** Samoa sorts its policies three ways. Personal and family: life, home, contents, travel, motor vehicle. Business protection: freight, marine, cargo, theft by employees, commercial vehicle, loss of income or rent. Social protection: accident compensation, national superannuation, medical, natural disaster. One family of promises, sorted by whose loss it covers.

**Life assurance in depth.** Life assurance comes as whole-life, term, endowment and combined life-and-endowment policies. It begins with the proposal form and the personal statement, which let the insurer assess the risk: higher premiums for higher risks, and lower ones for non-smokers. The price of the promise is set by the risk it covers.

**Funds, houses, cars — and the broker.** More of the family: superannuation through Samoa's National Provident Fund; medical insurance; Accident Compensation, funded by employer and government; property cover — houseowners against householders policies; and motor vehicle insurance with an excess, the first part of a loss the customer funds himself. And the middle person of this trade: insurance brokers, who arrange cover for their clients.

**Insurance of goods and trade debts.** The trade-facing covers inside the book's insurance table: trade credit insurance (against debtors not paying), goods in transit insurance, and marine insurance in its parts (hull, cargo, freight, shipowners' liability)

## Sub lesson 3 — Insurance companies serving trade

**Insurance companies as servants of trade.** Kenya's book shows insurance companies as financial institutions serving trade itself: savings through endowment and education policies; loans to policy holders at low interest; guarantees for policy holders borrowing elsewhere; advice on safeguarding property; finance to meet losses from insured risks; and underwriting new share issues. The insurer does more than pay claims — it finances, guarantees and advises the trading world around it.

**Aids to trade.** Auxiliaries to trade, 4 — insurance named among the helpers of trade, covering the risks of business activity (Nepal's book.)

**Insurance as a trade service.** The concept of insurance and the vocabulary of a life policy: the insured, the nominee (who receives the sum on the insured's death), the policy itself as the legal contract, the premium paid regularly for cover, the sum assured, policy term, death benefit, maturity benefit, savings plans, renewal premium, waiting period and health riders (Nepal's book.)

**Insurance as a trade service.** Essentials of an insurance contract: offer and acceptance, consideration, utmost good faith (both sides giving true and complete information, or the contract can be voided), insurable interest (a real financial interest in the insured thing or person), and indemnity (Nepal's book.)

**Insurance as a trade service.** Non-life insurance and its types: motor insurance (comprehensive and third-party), property insurance, marine insurance, health insurance and crop insurance; and reinsurance — insurers insuring their own risks, with Nepal's two reinsurance companies named as giving the market long-term stability and trust (Nepal's book.)

**Insurance as a trade service.** The process of issuing an insurance policy: submission of the proposal form, medical examination, underwriting (the insurer's team reviews the reports and assesses the risk), issuance of the policy under agreed terms and conditions, then premium payment and activation of the policy (Nepal's book.)

**Insurance as a trade service.** Life insurance and its policy types: whole-life policies (premiums for life, payment on death, with variants) and endowment-type (आवधिक) policies (ordinary, double-benefit and joint-life forms, paying on survival or death), and term policies (temporary, and renewable without a fresh medical test) (Nepal's book.)

**Insurance as a trade service.** The general principles of insurance: utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity (only the actual loss is paid — an insured vehicle worth a stated sum can never pay more than that sum; insurance compensates, it does not profit), contribution, subrogation, proximate cause (the fire, not the water used to fight it, is the cause of the loss), and mitigation of loss (the insured must still try to lessen the damage) (Nepal's book.)

**How insurance works.** Strand 5: insurance for e-business — insurance as the pooling or sharing of risks (premiums into a fund, compensation from the fund); insurers and the insured; insurance brokers and agents in PNG and their commissions; the principles of insurance (utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity); the steps to taking out insurance (proposal form, premium, cover note, policy); and making a claim (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Opening.** Insurance: its nature, insurance companies and underwriting, and insurable versus non-insurable risks (Nigeria's book.)

**Information.** The basic insurance problems of information asymmetry: adverse selection and moral hazard (Nigeria's book.)

**People-and-regulation.** Agents, brokers, loss adjusters, the contract of insurance and bancassurance; group insurance, reinsurance and the regulatory authority (Nigeria's book.)

**Types.** Types of insurance business: non-indemnity insurance and indemnity insurance, opening with marine insurance (Nigeria's book.)

**Risks.** The other forms of indemnity insurance, the categories of risk (pure versus speculative, fundamental versus particular), and the importance of insurance (Nigeria's book.)

**Insurance-introduction part.** Insurance and its importance for the existence of businesses (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Principles part.** The principles of insurance that govern its practical use (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Policy-types part.** The types of insurance policies covering the various risks of a business (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**The 2017 guide dropped this level; it is the pair's only teaching of the insurance market's parties.** The parties in the insurance market: the Sri Lanka Insurance Board (registration of companies and brokers, supervision, policy advice), insurance companies registered under Act No. 43 of 2003, insurance brokers as middlemen between insurer and insured (incl. reinsurance, earning brokerage), and insurance agents (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Insurance part.** The insurance service as an auxiliary service of trade (Sri Lanka's book.)

**Insurance part.** Insurance (காப்புறுதி) as an aid to trade, its kinds worked through the chapter (Sri Lanka's book.)
