# Hands-on — Warehousing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A farmer harvests more maize than the town can eat this month. Left in the open it rots, and it can be stolen; in a good store it waits safely until buyers come, month by month. That store — and the work of keeping goods in it — is warehousing. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a warehouse keeps goods safe and in condition between making and selling — taking goods in, holding what is needed and no more, grading and conditioning them, and sending them out — so trade never has to stop for want of stock, and stored goods can even stand as collateral for a loan. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
