This lesson is about one thing.
Banks are trade's money-servants: they take deposits, lend, keep valuables, move money at home and across the world, and stand behind trade with instruments — cheques, bills of exchange, drafts and cards — that let strangers trust each other's promises.

Kenya's system stands in three parts: the Central Bank of Kenya directing the system, the commercial banks, and the non-bank financial institutions such as ICDC, DFCK and HFCK.

The Central Bank is set up by government to manage and control the supply of and demand for money — monetary control, aimed at growth, employment, stable prices, balanced development and balance-of-payments equilibrium.

Commercial banks are trading businesses, making profit by financial intermediation: interest on loans and overdrafts — their largest asset; investment in government securities such as Treasury bills; and charges for services — ledger fees, standing orders, banker's cheques, credit transfers, salary processing.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at the market, in your street.
