# Hands-on — Banking Services for Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one payment: a trader in one town paying a supplier in another country. List everything that must happen for the money to arrive safely and provably. Every item on your list is a bank service. That list is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: banks are trade's money-servants: they take deposits, lend, keep valuables, move money at home and across the world, and stand behind trade with instruments — cheques, bills of exchange, drafts and cards — that let strangers trust each other's promises. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
