# Advertising and Sales Promotion

## Do it first

Think of the last thing you bought because something persuaded you. Was it a person talking, a free sample, a poster, or the shop's good name? Name which one it was. Sorting those four persuaders is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four forms of promotion

**The four forms compared.** Promotion comes in four forms, and Nigeria's book compares them: personal selling, public relations, sales promotion and advertising. The same chapter walks the stages a customer passes through on the way to buying — promotion meets the customer at each stage, in a different form each time.

**Personal selling and public relations.** Personal selling: know when it beats the other forms, and its strengths and its costs. Public relations: the methods and activities a business uses to be well thought of — from sponsorship to lobbying. One persuades a single customer face to face; the other persuades the whole public's opinion of the firm.

**Sales promotion and public relations.** Sales promotions compared in a table with costs and benefits: free samples, coupons, premiums/gifts, contests and sweepstakes, loyalty points, BOGO/BOGOF/BOGOHO, point-of-sale displays, rebates, bundling and loss leaders; and public relations (sponsorship, press releases, scholarships) as free publicity that builds a positive image

## Sub lesson 2 — Sales promotion — the short push

**Sales promotion, consumer and trade.** Sales promotion works in two directions. To consumers: samples, price packs, coupons, premiums, competitions and patronage rewards. To the trade itself: bonuses, free goods, cooperative advertising and training. The consumer forms pull goods off the shelf; the trade forms push them onto it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Advertising — the paid message

**Advertising — types, media, merits.** Advertising itself: informative, persuasive and competitive types; its roles for a business; the media available to carry it; and its merits and demerits for society, stated both ways. A good advertiser knows which type he is running and what it is for — telling, convincing, or answering a rival.

**Advertising in Samoa.** Samoa's book brings advertising home: word of mouth, radio, newspaper, television, sponsorship and roadside signs. And the test of it: effective advertising targets the likely buyers, and is eye-catching, informative and memorable. A message aimed at everyone reaches no one; a message aimed at the likely buyer, that the eye catches and the mind keeps, does the work.

**Advertising.** Advertising: paid communication through media, in its two forms — informative and persuasive — plus subliminal advertising and the psychosocial techniques marketers use

**Promoting goods.** Advertising techniques: the promotional mix made of advertising (paid use of mass media), promotions (a benefit, usually economic, offered for buying), public relations (building strategic links and alliances) and word of mouth (Ecuador's book.)

**Promoting goods.** The advertising and commercial plan inside the investment project: choosing the advertising and promotion tools and the commercial strategies that will meet the programmed sales — the plan generates the advertising that attracts customers to the point of sale (Ecuador's book.)

**Aids to trade.** Auxiliaries to trade, 6 — advertisement and promotion, named among the helpers of trade (Nepal's book.)
**Advertising and sales promotion.** The promotional mix and advertising: promotion informs, reminds and persuades customers and can lift sales volume at a given price or win a first trial; advertising is paid for by the sponsor through media — newspapers, television, magazines, direct mail, radio, e-mail, billboards and digital platforms — and advertisements are classified by medium (Tanzania's book.)

**Promotion mix part.** The elements of the promotion mix: advertising (informing the public about an idea, goods or services through a paid announcement by an identified sponsor, to promote sales) and sales promotion beside the other elements (Bhutan's book.)

**Promotion techniques.** Unit 10.1: marketing techniques compared by their advantages and disadvantages, and creating an advertisement (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Promotion.** Strand 3 Unit 1: promotion — its methods (display, cash discounts, giveaways, lucky draws, sponsorships, media advertising) and purposes; the promotional mix (advertising, publicity/PR/sponsorship, personal interaction, direct marketing, sales promotions); the costs of promotion and the factors in choosing media; low-cost promotion (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Opening.** Advertising: its meaning and nature, its roles, and its types (Nigeria's book.)

**Methods.** Direct versus indirect methods of advertising, sales promotion as indirect advertising, and other advertising forms (Nigeria's book.)

**Evaluation.** The advantages and disadvantages of advertisement (Nigeria's book.)
