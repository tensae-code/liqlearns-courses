# About the Commerce course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — the Business Studies
and Commerce books of Kenya, Malaysia, Nigeria and Samoa. The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'.

## How this course is organised

Commerce is ONE top-level course, one of the three courses of
the Business / Commerce / Accounting split (the split the books
themselves forced: the same school subject carries business
management, trade and bookkeeping side by side, and each part is
stocked in the course it belongs to). Inside the course the
organisation is by **units and levels, not grades**: a learner's
place is found by what they prove, and each lesson stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grade a book comes from appears only in each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four units**: First Steps (what
commerce is, retail, wholesale, buying and selling, credit),
Trade Across Distance (transport, foreign trade), Helpers of
Trade (documents, advertising, insurance, banking) and
Institutions and the Consumer (trade organisations, consumer
protection). **13 lessons, 76 placements stocked.**
