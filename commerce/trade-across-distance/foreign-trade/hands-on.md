# Hands-on — Foreign Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing your country sells to the world and one thing it must buy from the world. Write both down, then ask: what paper and what promises would let a seller trust a buyer across an ocean? That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: foreign trade is trade between countries, so each can obtain what it does not produce and sell its surplus — run on quoted-price terms, secured by documents such as the letter of credit, and shaped by the restrictions governments impose and the trends now loosening them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
