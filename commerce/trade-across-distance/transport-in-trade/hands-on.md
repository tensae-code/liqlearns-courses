# Hands-on — Transport in Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Follow one imported good into Samoa: a carton of tinned fish on a ship. Ask: what happens to the shop's shelves if the ship is late? Write the answer down. That answer is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: distribution is an aid to trade that lives or dies on transport: goods move direct, or through retailers and wholesalers — and where shipping is slow or missed, a shortage can force a trader onto expensive air-freight. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
