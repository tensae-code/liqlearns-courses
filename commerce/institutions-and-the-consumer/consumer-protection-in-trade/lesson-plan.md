# Lesson plan — Consumer Protection in Trade

**Course:** Commerce · **Title:** Institutions and the Consumer · **Lesson:** Consumer Protection in Trade
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit — Institutions and the Consumer (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the consumer at the end of the chain needs protecting — against deceptive advertising, fake brands, health hazards and short weight — and the protection stands in three layers: the consumer's own rights, the laws, and the bodies that enforce them.
**This lesson's own level:** Unit — Institutions and the Consumer, lesson 15 of 15 in the course ladder; it stands on Trade Organisations and Institutions, the lesson before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why consumers need protection — and their rights** — consumer protection.
2. **The laws and the measures behind them**
3. **The protection bodies — government and voluntary**; Fair-trading-and-consumer-protection; Bulk buying part; Sales and advertising part.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks consumer protection is only the government's job → the lesson's third layer is protection without government: consumer associations, producer and professional associations, and crafts unions policing their own members.
- Go back to the one thing: the consumer at the end of the chain needs protecting — against deceptive advertising, fake brands, health hazards and short weight — and the protection stands in three layers: the consumer's own rights, the laws, and the bodies that enforce them. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
