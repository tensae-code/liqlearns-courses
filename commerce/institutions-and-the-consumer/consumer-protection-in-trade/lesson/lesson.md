# Consumer Protection in Trade

## Do it first

Remember a time a bought thing was not what it promised — too light, fake, or unsafe. Ask: whose job was it to stop that, and where could the buyer complain? This lesson names the rights, the laws and the doors to knock on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why consumers need protection — and their rights

**Why consumers need protecting.** Nigeria's book opens with the harms: essential goods and price control; deceptive advertising; health hazards; fake brands; and short weight and measure. The consumer is the weakest party in the chain — the one who did not make, pack, label or weigh the goods. Protection exists because that weakness is real.

**Consumerism, rights and redress.** The answer begins with consumerism: the eight internationally accepted consumer rights, and the matching consumer responsibilities that stand beside them — rights are held by people who also carry duties. And when a right is broken, the book gives the five steps for seeking redress in Nigeria. Know your rights, carry your responsibilities, and know the steps before you need them.

**Consumer protection.** Why consumers need protection (copycat products, false advertising, collusion, unfair treatment, uninformed consumers) and how they are protected: laws, consumer organisations and government agencies; the rights of consumers (education, safety, information, choice, to be heard, redress); the Bureau of Standards and the Ombudsman, with the Caribbean acts named

**Protection.** Consumer protection: the protections a consumer can rely on (Fiji's book.)

## Sub lesson 2 — The laws and the measures behind them

**Nigeria's protective laws.** The laws in detail: the Hire Purchase Act 1965; the Sale of Goods Act's implied terms; the Trade Descriptions Acts; the Standards Organisation Act; the Weights and Measures Act; and the food and drugs legislation. Each law answers one of the harms of the first sub lesson — false description, short weight, unsafe food and drugs, unfair hire purchase.

**Regulatory measures behind the laws.** Laws need measures that bite day to day: vehicle roadworthiness rules; the licensing of professionals and trades; destination inspection of imports; and the Weights and Measures Unit at work. A law on paper protects no one until a measure carries it into the market.

## Sub lesson 3 — The protection bodies — government and voluntary

**The government protection bodies.** The bodies, and what each does: the Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON); NAFDAC, for food and drugs; the Consumer Protection Council (CPC); and the Federal Environmental Protection Agency (FEPA). Four doors to knock on, each with its own harm to answer — learn which door fits which complaint.

**Protection without government.** The last layer needs no government at all: consumer associations, Nigerian and international; producer and professional associations; and crafts unions policing their own members. Trade protects its own good name when its associations refuse to shelter the cheat — protection is also a habit of honest traders.


**Fair-trading-and-consumer-protection.** Fair competition's means — low prices, good quality, customer facilities such as returns and easy payment, good presentation and packaging, shop appearance, advertising, the trader's reputation and conduct — against unfair competition done by illegal and deceitful means; and the legislative and market-police measures that protect the consumer, including the ban on misleading advertising (Greece's book.)

**Bulk buying part.** Buying in bulk and when it is appropriate, worked through family cases: why a case of wine might be a good buy for one family but not a 10-litre can of detergent, why 500 grams of cheese is not a good buy for another — judging a bulk buy against the family's actual use (Samoa's book.)

**Sales and advertising part.** Sales and advertising for the wise buyer: a store holds a sale because it is not selling enough, or not fast enough, or needs cash quickly, or the goods will go out of style or spoil — so a careful shopper can find real bargains at a sale; and advertising studied from the buyer's side, including finding an advertisement you think is misleading (Samoa's book.)

**Protection part.** Consumer protection: its concept and need — protecting consumers from unscrupulous, unethical, unfair and deceptive practices of business entities, with appropriate redress, remedies or compensation for aggrieved consumers (Bhutan's book.)

**Rights part.** The rights and responsibilities of consumers set out as a pair (Bhutan's book.)

**Unfair practices part.** Misleading and false representation as an unfair trade practice: a practice adopted by a supplier, manufacturer or service provider to promote sales by misrepresenting the goods or services (Bhutan's book.)

**Redressal part.** The consumer grievance redressal mechanism: how an aggrieved consumer's complaint is taken up and resolved (Bhutan's book.)
