# Hands-on — Trade Organisations and Institutions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a map of your own region from memory. Mark the neighbouring countries, then ask: what would change for a trader if goods, workers and money could cross those borders freely? That question — and the bodies built to answer it — is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: traders and countries organise: world institutions steady money and lend for development, regions integrate their markets step by step, and West Africa's groupings join trade, rivers and money across borders. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
