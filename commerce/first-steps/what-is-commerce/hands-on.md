# Hands-on — What Is Commerce

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one thing you used today that was made far away. Write down: who made it, and every hand and service it passed through before it reached you. That journey — and everything that helped it — is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: commerce is the work of getting goods from the people who make them to the people who use them — distribution across the gap between producer and consumer, helped by the aids to trade: transport, communication, banking, insurance, warehousing and advertising. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
