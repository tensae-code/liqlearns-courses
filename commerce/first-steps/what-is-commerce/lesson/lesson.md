# What Is Commerce

## Do it first

Think of one thing you used today that was made far away. Write down: who made it, and every hand and service it passed through before it reached you. That journey — and everything that helped it — is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Distribution — bridging the gap between producer and consumer

**Distribution bridges the gap.** A producer makes goods in one place; the people who need them live somewhere else, and they need them in the right quantities, at the right price and at the right time. Distribution is the work that bridges that gap. The path goods follow from the producer to the final consumer is called a channel of distribution — learn to picture every good you use travelling along such a path.

**Choosing the channel.** A business does not pick its channel by habit; it weighs real things. The nature of the product comes first — perishable and bulky goods need short channels. Then the nature of the market, the services the middlemen offer, the firm's own resources and size, the channels competitors use, government policy, and the risks of marketing. A wise choice fits all of these together.

**Origins of trade and exchange.** How exchange began: specialisation produced surpluses, barter exchanged them, and barter's limits (double coincidence of wants, no agreed rate of exchange, indivisibility, inability to store wealth) drove the search for money

## Sub lesson 2 — The aids to trade

**The aids to trade.** Commerce does not work alone. Samoa's book names the tertiary service industries as the working parts of commerce: transportation, communication, trade itself, accounting, banking, insurance, warehousing and advertising. Each one removes a difficulty that would otherwise stop goods moving — distance, silence, payment, risk, time and not-knowing. The rest of this course takes these helpers one by one.

**Money in trade.** Money: what it is, its four functions (medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, means of deferred payment) and the characteristics good money needs (portable, divisible, acceptable, durable, scarce, homogeneous); token money and how money encourages specialisation

**Opening / the parts of business.** The three dimensions (models) of business: industry, commerce and service enterprise — the map of the whole subject (Nepal's book.)

**What commerce is.** Commerce defined: all the activities that help exchange by carrying goods and services from the place of production to the consumer; buying, selling and exchange in the market (Nepal's book.)
**What commerce is.** What commercial exchange is and why it matters: production and exchange are two branches of one root — exchange moves what production makes to where needs are satisfied; it grew out of specialisation and the division of labour, from self-sufficiency through barter to exchange through money, and Islam's own regulation of trade dealings (sales, mudaraba, agency, guarantee) is part of its history (Sudan's book.)

**What commerce is.** Money and where it came from: barter failed on the lack of a double coincidence of wants, no common measure of value, perishability of goods and disputes over repayment, so exchange moved through commodity money (portable, divisible, exchangeable, scarce) and metallic money to modern money — anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange, performing the functions of measure of value/unit of… (Tanzania's book.)

**What-trade-is.** Trade (εμπόριο) defined: the set of acts by which goods pass from producer to consumer; its nature and object, and why an intermediary trade stage exists between production and consumption at all (Greece's book.)

**Commerce part.** Commerce taught whole: trade classified as home trade and foreign trade (import, export, entrepot), and wholesale against retail; and the aids to trade that remove the hindrances of commerce, with logistics named (Bhutan's book.)

**Classification of trade.** Trade classified in one diagram: trade divides into home trade (wholesale trade and retail trade) and foreign trade (import trade and export trade) (Kenya's book.)
