# Wholesale Trade

## Do it first

Take one good — a sack of rice or a box of soap. Draw every hand it passes through from the farm or factory to the person who eats or uses it. Count the hands. This lesson is about the biggest hand in the middle: the wholesaler.

## Sub lesson 1 — Channels of distribution

**The channel patterns.** Channels run from the full chain — producer, agent, wholesaler, retailer, consumer — down to the producer selling direct to the consumer. The people in the middle are the middlemen, or intermediaries. Remember this cost rule: each middleman adds his own costs and profit, so the longer the chain, the higher the final price the consumer pays.

**The channels Kenyan goods follow.** Kenya's book follows real goods. Local agricultural produce moves through co-operatives and marketing boards, or direct. Locally manufactured goods move their own ways, including through government-appointed agents such as the Kenya National Trading Corporation for controlled commodities. Imported goods come through agents or the manufacturer's own representative. Same lesson, three goods, three paths.

**The chain drawn whole.** Samoa's younger book draws the whole chain in one line: the producer sells to the wholesaler; the wholesaler sells on to the food market, to shops or individual retailers, or to an agent or industry; and they sell to the consumer. When you can draw that line from memory, you hold the map of this whole unit.

**The taro chain — and the money flowing back.** Follow a taro: planter, then the Prime Minister's Department, then shipping, then the wholesaler, then the retailer and the consumer. And watch the money: it flows back up the chain, so that each party in the line benefits. Goods flow down; money flows up; every hand in the chain is paid by the hand below it.

**Middle people — and Samoa's shorter chain.** Middle people between producer and consumer make exchange more efficient, but each one adds a charge. Samoa's own way is shorter: the wholesaler is not prominent there — importers usually sell directly to retailers, and that keeps the consumer price lower. Fewer hands, lower price; more hands, more service. That trade-off is the heart of this lesson.

**Channels of distribution.** Distribution chains/channels: the typical chain producer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer, the functions and benefits of wholesalers (bulk buying, breaking bulk, storage, promotion, credit to retailers, transport, market feedback) and of retailers, and the four alternative channels (direct, via retailer only, via wholesaler only, via wholesaler and retailer)

## Sub lesson 2 — What the middlemen do

**What intermediaries actually do.** Intermediaries earn their place by work: they reduce the number of transactions between producers and consumers; they break bulk into usable sizes and accumulate bulk (the assemblers gather many small lots into one); they take risks; they provide finance; they pass information both ways; they promote products; they handle transport and storage; they provide variety; and they avail goods conveniently to consumers. A middleman who does none of this is only a cost.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why wholesalers need credit

**Why wholesalers need credit.** Wholesaling runs on credit. A wholesaler needs it to buy facilities and branches, to finance producers, to sell to retailers on credit in turn, to buy in bulk, and to hold stock while waiting for an expected price rise. Credit flows down the chain because the wholesaler both receives it and gives it.

**Home trade.** Home (internal) trade divided into wholesale and retail trade; wholesale trade as the link that buys in bulk between producer and retailer (Nepal's book.)

**Wholesale trade.** Wholesale trade inside the book's production chain: the wholesale business buys goods in large amounts — boxes, cartons, containers — and sells large-quantity goods to retail businesses (the book's example: the wholesaler buys sacks of sugar already packed in kilograms from the producer) (Uganda's book.)
**Wholesale trade.** Middlemen inside the marketing institutions: a middleman operates between producer and ultimate consumer or industrial user; merchant middlemen take title to the goods while performing marketing functions, agent middlemen do not (Tanzania's book.)

**Wholesale/retail distinction.** Trade distinguished by the quantity traded: wholesale trade, the sale of goods in large quantities from trader to trader (as the ΚΒΣ defines it), retail trade, and the in-between semi-wholesale form (Greece's book.)

**Distribution part.** The distribution functional department: its purpose (every product reaches the buyer), the manager choosing among four distribution channels by market needs and product type, and stock held in warehouses before delivery (Malaysia's book.)
