# Buying and Selling

## Do it first

Take a pretend purchase: 100 exercise books for a shop. Write a price, then ask three questions: Is there a discount? What does the price include? When must I pay? Those three questions are this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Discounts — trade, quantity and cash

**The three discounts of trade.** Trade runs on three discounts. A quantity discount rewards buying in larger quantities. A trade discount rewards the buyer who resells. Neither of these two is recorded in the books of account. The third is different: a cash discount is an allowance for paying a debt promptly, within the stated terms. Learn the three apart — who earns each one, and why.

**Cash discount against trade discount.** Nigeria's book sets the two side by side: know what each is deducted from, and know your trade discounts — a cumulative trade discount builds on what you have already bought, a non-cumulative one does not. The discount is part of the price, so a trader who cannot say which discount applies does not yet know what he will pay.

## Sub lesson 2 — Quoted prices and delivery terms

**Quoted prices in home trade.** A quoted price must say what it covers. Home trade builds its prices from the same cost idea as foreign trade: ex-warehouse (loco) — the price at the warehouse door; carriage forward — the buyer pays the carriage; free on rail; and carriage paid — the seller carries the delivery cost. Two traders quoting the same number may be offering different deals; the term tells you which.

## Sub lesson 3 — When payment is made

**The timing of payment.** When is cash paid? The terms name the moment: payment in advance, cash with order, cash on delivery, spot cash, prompt cash, net cash, a monthly account, or deferred payment on open account. Each term shifts the risk and the waiting between buyer and seller — read the term before you agree the price.

**Buying for the business.** Choosing suppliers: suppliers supply the goods and services the venture runs on; the best supplier is not always the cheapest — also judge who gives the best solutions and weigh their location (Nicaragua's book.)
**The-sale-and-its-parties.** Goods (εμπόρευμα) defined — a movable thing, material or immaterial, destined for resale (by extension also securities) — and the commercial sale (αγοραπωλησία): its definition and elements, and the obligations and rights of seller and buyer (payment of the price, preservation costs, risk passing on delivery, interest on late payment, retention-of-ownership clause, seller's liability for defects, sale on approval) (Greece's book.)

**Quantity-and-quality-terms.** Fixing the quantity and quality term of a sale: net weight against gross weight, and the tare (απόβαρο) in its four kinds — real, legal/customs, average and conventional — plus shrinkage (φύρα) allowed for in the quantity (Greece's book.)

**Price-term.** Fixing the price term of a sale: how the price of goods is determined and the factors that push prices up or down (Greece's book.)

**Packaging.** Packaging as a term and tool of the sale: what packaging must do for the goods and the buyer, including multiple packaging and family packaging (Greece's book.)

**Delivery-terms.** The delivery term of a sale: immediate delivery (within 15 days), future delivery and delivery by instalments, and the standard delivery terms that split cost and risk between seller and buyer — FAS "Free Alongside Ship" and FOB "Free on Board" (Greece's book.)

**Sales-channels.** Kinds of sales by channel: the direct sale and the indirect sale through commercial agents and brokers working on commission; and sale by tender or auction (διαγωνισμός / δημοπρασία), including international tenders open on equal terms across the EU (Greece's book.)

**Discounts.** Trade discount and cash discount worked as calculations: on a sh 10,000 price, trade discount of 10% (sh 1,000) is taken first, leaving sh 9,000 payable; cash discount is then calculated on that reduced amount (10% = sh 900, so sh 8,100 is paid; at 5% = sh 450, so sh 8,550 is paid) (Kenya's book.)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: buying and selling run on agreed terms — the discounts of trade, the quoted price and what it covers, and the moment payment is due — and the trader who knows the terms knows the real price. Give one real example from your own home, market or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: the three discounts of trade, cash discount against trade discount, quoted prices in home trade, the timing of payment. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
