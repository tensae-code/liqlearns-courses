# Hands-on — Credit and Hire Purchase

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a family buying a piece of furniture they cannot pay for all at once. The shop lets them take it home today and pay month by month. Write who lends, who owes, and what is promised. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: buying on credit means taking the goods now and paying over time: the seller becomes the creditor who lends, and the buyer becomes the debtor who owes — and the promise to pay is measured exactly in money. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
