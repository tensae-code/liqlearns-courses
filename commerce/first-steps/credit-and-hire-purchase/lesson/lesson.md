# Credit and Hire Purchase

## Do it first

Think of a family buying a piece of furniture they cannot pay for all at once. The shop lets them take it home today and pay month by month. Write who lends, who owes, and what is promised. That is this lesson.

## Buying on credit

Credit is a way of buying. The seller is also the creditor and lender: he hands over the goods now. The buyer, who promises to pay at a later date, is the debtor and borrower. Credit makes it possible to buy a car, a house and consumer goods such as furniture, with the payment made over a period of time extending into the future — and the promise is measured exactly in money. Two words to hold: creditor (the one who is owed) and debtor (the one who owes).

## Credit buying

Buying on terms inside the book's terms-of-sale teaching: credit (price split into payments over under a year, possession at once), hire purchase (down payment plus interest-bearing balance over 1–5 years, ownership only at the final payment) and layaway (seller holds the item while the buyer pays, no interest)

**Credit buying.** Trade credit and its instruments: the payment facilities suppliers give the project in goods or raw materials, repaid by agreement usually within under a year; (Palestine's book.)
**Credit and hire purchase.** Consumer credit, its two edges: consumer credit needs no collateral, so no valued asset is risked on default, and it meets emergencies (health, a wedding) without begging relatives; against this, it can lower the borrower's credit score and make future loans difficult or expensive, and it ties up future income in paying for past purchases (Tanzania's book.)

**Paying-cash-vs-on-credit.** The time and ways of paying the price: cash ways — deposit to the seller's bank account, bank remittance, payment against shipping documents, opening a letter of credit on a pro-forma invoice, sending a cheque — against credit ways, where payment is postponed to an agreed date and made in cash, by deposit, or by promissory note or bill of exchange (Greece's book.)

**What-credit-is.** Credit (πίστωση) defined: the transfer of movable things — goods or money — to another person on the trust that they will be returned on the agreed terms; its elements (a two-sided agreement between credit-giver and credit-taker, resting on trust in the taker's will and ability to repay) and its distinctions — short, medium and long-term and standing credits, and credits secured personally, on the borrower's standing, against credits secured on property (mortgage or pledge) (Greece's book.)

**Credit-instruments.** Credit instruments (πιστωτικοί τίτλοι) in general: documents that embody a property right, which can be exercised only by whoever holds the document, the document's own particulars fixing the nature and extent of the right and of the debt (Greece's book.)

**Bill-of-exchange.** The bill of exchange (συναλλαγματική): the order to pay a fixed sum at a fixed place and time, its legally required elements under Law 5325/1932 (the word "συναλλαγματική", a clean unconditional payment order, the sum in figures and words, the payer's name), acceptance, endorsement, the two-working-day grace after maturity, protest (διαμαρτυρικό) drawn by a notary when it is not paid, and discounting bills to raise working capital (Greece's book.)

**Promissory-note.** The promissory note (γραμμάτιο σε διαταγή): the maker's own written promise to pay, how it differs from a bill of exchange and how it circulates (Greece's book.)

**Hire-purchase part.** The Hire Purchase Act 1967 (Amendment 2010): hire purchase as hiring goods with an option to buy — the user is the hirer, the financing company is the owner, and ownership passes only after all instalments are paid (Malaysia's book.)

**Borrowing concept part.** Borrowing across cultures: the old Samoan language of borrowing built on "fa'amolemole" — the word showing humility, love, politeness and respect in asking for someone's kindness — contrasted with the Western (palagi) meaning of "borrow"; in commerce, borrowing is taking or obtaining (Samoa's book.)

**Credit part.** Borrowing money rather than saving up for needs and wants means: you can pay for goods later, you can have the goods immediately, you can avoid price rises in the future, and you can afford the price of a good that would have taken too long to save up for — and when you have borrowed, you have obtained credit (Samoa's book.)

**Buying.** Types of buying: the ways goods are bought, including buying on credit terms (Fiji's book.)


**Credit and hire purchase.** Buying by instalments and its cost: under an instalment loan the buyer repays part of the principal plus interest every month, so buying on credit costs more than paying once in full — the interest paid always exceeds what the same money would earn as a deposit. (Vietnam's book.)

**Credit and hire purchase.** Trade credit (tín dụng thương mại): credit between enterprises themselves, carried out as buying and selling on deferred payment — goods are delivered now and the buying enterprise repays principal and interest in money at the agreed date. (Vietnam's book.)
